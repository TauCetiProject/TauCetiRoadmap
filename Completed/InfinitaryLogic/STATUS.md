## Where this roadmap stands

**At a glance.** The v1 summit is proved: Scott's isomorphism theorem holds for countable structures in countable relational languages, with no counting hypothesis, and all four layers have every milestone the README names. Only one worked example is still missing: the Lω₁ω sentence for a fixed well-order type.

### Named results

- **Scott's isomorphism theorem** — in a countable relational language, every countable structure has an Lω₁ω sentence that holds in a countable structure exactly when that structure is isomorphic to it ([`scott_isomorphism`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Isomorphism.html#TauCeti.scott_isomorphism)). The same holds for the Scott sentence at any countable internally stabilizing level ([`realize_scottSentenceAt_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Isomorphism.html#TauCeti.realize_scottSentenceAt_iff)).
- **Karp's theorem** — relational structures of any cardinality are potentially isomorphic exactly when they are L∞ω-equivalent. It is proved at any common coded carrier ([`karp_theorem_at`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/Karp.html#TauCeti.karp_theorem_at)) and packaged over all carriers ([`karp_theorem`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/Karp.html#TauCeti.karp_theorem)). For countable structures it gives isomorphism exactly when they agree on Lω₁ω sentences ([`countable_infEquivAt_nat_iff_iso`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/Karp.html#TauCeti.countable_infEquivAt_nat_iff_iso)).
- **The characteristic property of Scott formulas** — in any structure of any cardinality, a tuple satisfies the level-α Scott formula of a tuple from a countable structure exactly when the two tuples are back-and-forth equivalent at level α ([`realize_scottFormula_iff_bfEquiv`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Formula.html#TauCeti.realize_scottFormula_iff_bfEquiv)).
- **Countable Scott rank** — the internal Scott rank of a countable structure is below ω₁ ([`internalScottRank_lt_omega_one`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Rank.html#TauCeti.internalScottRank_lt_omega_one)). It lies between the stabilization ordinal and that ordinal's successor, and the stabilization ordinal is the supremum of the orbit ranks ([`selfStabilizationOrdinal_eq_iSup_orbitRank`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Rank.html#TauCeti.selfStabilizationOrdinal_eq_iSup_orbitRank)).
- **Elementary equivalence is strictly weaker than infinitary equivalence** — the orders ℤ and ℤ + ℤ satisfy the [same first-order sentences](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/DiscreteOrder.html#TauCeti.elementarilyEquivalent_int_sumLex_int), but an [Lω₁ω sentence separates them](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/DiscreteOrder.html#TauCeti.not_infEquivAt_nat_int_sumLex_int). Above ℵ₀, [two dense linear orders of size ℵ₁](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/DenseLinearOrder.html#TauCeti.exists_dlo_aleph_one_potentialIso_isEmpty_equiv) are potentially isomorphic but not isomorphic.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **The fixed-carrier infinitary syntax** [`BoundedFormulaInf`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Syntax.html#FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormulaInf), with Lω₁ω as the case where the carrier is ℕ. With carrier codings and [`reindex`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Reindex.html#FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormulaInf.reindex), conjunctions indexed by the elements of a structure can be written at any coded carrier, which is how Karp and the Scott formulas are stated. A formula's [free-variable support](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/FreeVarSupport.html#FirstOrder.Language.BoundedFormulaInf.freeVarSupport) is countable at carrier ℕ, and realization depends only on it.
- **Ordinal back-and-forth equivalence** [`BFEquiv`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/Equivalence.html#TauCeti.BFEquiv) and [`PotentialIso`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/PotentialIso.html#TauCeti.PotentialIso). A tuple presentation links the two. Potential isomorphism is [an equivalence relation](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/PotentialIso.html#TauCeti.PotentialIso.trans), and level α of back-and-forth equivalence gives [agreement up to quantifier rank α](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/BackAndForth/Karp.html#TauCeti.BFEquiv.realize_iff_of_qrank_le).
- **Internal stabilization**: the back-and-forth refinement of a countable structure's own tuples [stabilizes below ω₁](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/Stabilization.html#TauCeti.exists_complete_self_stabilization). This is why the Scott theorem needs no extra hypothesis.

### Roadmap coverage

All four layers are done. Layer 0 is done with the free-variable support and its realization law. Layer 1 is done with transitivity of potential isomorphism. Layer 2 was already done, and Layer 3 is done with `realize_scottSentenceAt_iff` and `scott_isomorphism`. The worked examples cover finite structures (and, for a finite language, their first-order characteristic sentences), the countably-many-unary-predicates regression, pure sets, dense linear orders at ℵ₀ and ℵ₁, and K₂ ⊔ K₃ and a rigid graph. They also cover the [Rado graph](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/RandomGraph.html#TauCeti.nonempty_iso_radoGraph_iff) and [equivalence relations classified by how many classes of each size they have](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Scott/EquivalenceRelation.html#Setoid.nonempty_relIso_iff), and the ℤ versus ℤ + ℤ gap. The well-order example is the one gap.

## The frontier

- **The well-order example** — write the Lω₁ω sentence whose countable models are exactly the well-orders of a fixed countable order type, a class with no first-order axiomatization. It is the last worked example in the README.
- **Alignment with Mathlib's infinitary syntax** — the README asks the local syntax to follow the names and shapes in the open mathlib4#42758, and to be replaced by imports if Mathlib takes it up. The supplied material does not show whether that has happened.
- **Beyond v1** — relationalizing function and constant symbols, and the out-of-scope topics such as López–Escobar and Barwise compactness, belong to separate roadmaps.
