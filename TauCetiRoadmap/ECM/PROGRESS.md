## ECM: 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-09 (`454fefe` to `7ca783d`)

All five of the roadmap's end theorems arrived in this window. Montgomery's two x-only formulas are now proved correct on Mathlib's native point group of the monic model: [doubling](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Double.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.double_coordinates) computes the x-coordinate of `[2]P` for every point, and [differential addition](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/DiffAdd.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.diffAdd_coordinates) computes that of `P + Q` from `P`, `Q` and `P − Q`, with output zero exactly when the difference is infinity or the distinguished 2-torsion point `(0, 0)`; [other points of order two](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/DiffAdd.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.diffAdd_coordinates_some_ne_zero) are shown not to be exceptional. [Suyama's ellipticity criterion](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Suyama/Basic.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.isElliptic_model_suyama_iff) pins down exactly the five factors in `σ` at which Suyama's curve degenerates, and [stage-1 detection](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Detection.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.prime_dvd_gcd_of_card_dvd) shows that a prime `p ∣ n` divides `gcd(Z, n)` once a pair over `ZMod n` reduces to a multiple of a point by a multiple of the group order.

Underneath sit the [monic model](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Basic.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.model) with its discriminant and ellipticity criterion, raw coordinate pairs, and the projective x-coordinate, whose fibres are `{P, −P}` and whose image is [identified with the quotient by negation](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Coordinates.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.orbitRelQuotientEquivRangeProjectiveX). The stage-2 side got its [cross-difference criterion](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Coordinates.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.cross_coordinates_zsmul_eq_zero_iff): the pairs of `[m]Q` and `[n]Q` agree projectively exactly when `[m − n]Q` or `[m + n]Q` vanishes. A gcd lemma for [finite products of residues](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Data/ZMod/ProductGCD.html#List.prime_dvd_gcd_val_prod_iff) needs no elliptic-curve hypothesis. Further work made points, coordinates and the projective line compatible with field embeddings.

Not yet there: the description of the stage-1 multiplier `M(B₁)` as an lcm of prime powers.

## ECM: 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-19 (`7ca783d` to `b235000`)

The last open item of the roadmap, the stage-1 multiplier, is now covered, so every milestone the README lists is in place. For `Nat.lcmUpto n`, the lcm of `1, …, n`, the library now proves that [every positive `k ≤ n` divides it](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LcmUpto.html#Nat.dvd_lcmUpto), together with a [powersmoothness criterion](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LcmUpto.html#Nat.dvd_lcmUpto_iff): `d` divides it exactly when every prime-power divisor of `d` is at most `n`. This is the precise sense in which a group order must be "smooth" for stage 1 to catch it, which is stronger than having all prime divisors at most `n`, since `16 ∤ lcm(1, …, 10)`. The expansion as a product of maximal prime powers is not reproved here; the new module cites Mathlib's own (TauCeti#27388).

That pull request and three others landed earlier and are recorded here late. They gave Montgomery's ladder a [relation generated by binary ladder steps](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Ladder/Relations.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.LadderRel) and its [invariant](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Ladder/Relations.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.LadderRel.coordinates): any run of the ladder from a nonzero representative of `P` produces representatives of `[n]P` and `[n + 1]P`. They also settled the distinguished point `T₀` of order two, whose multiples [alternate with infinity](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Coordinates.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.coordinates_nsmul_twoTorsionPoint), with the odd ladder step [nonzero exactly when](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Ladder.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.diffAdd_nsmul_ne_zero_iff_of_cross_eq_zero) `P` is neither infinity nor `T₀`. Stage 2 got its batched form: if one cross difference in a batch is detected modulo `p` and none modulo another prime divisor, the [gcd of the batch product is a proper factor](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/StageTwo/Product.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.one_lt_gcd_prod_cross_and_lt_of_addOrderOf_dvd_mul).

The rest of the window restated [doubling](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/Double.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.double_coordinates_of_cross_eq_zero), [differential addition](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Montgomery/DiffAdd.html#TauCeti.Montgomery.diffAdd_cross_eq_zero_of_cross_eq_zero) and stage-2 detection for arbitrary representatives, the form the ladder actually produces, and added worked examples modulo `5` and `7`.
