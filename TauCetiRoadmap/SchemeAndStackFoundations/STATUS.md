## Where this roadmap stands

**At a glance.** Layer 0 is partial but large. It has the henselization of a pair, perfection of schemes, scheme Frobenius, normal schemes, and the Cohen–Macaulay and Nagata predicates. Every later layer is partial and has its first theorems: fpqc descent of sections, Leray acyclicity, the Nisnevich henselization criterion, degree laws for vector bundles, formal completion and cycle pushforward. None of the summit theorems (Keel–Mori, Grothendieck duality, Schlessinger, de Jong, Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch) has begun.

### Named results

- **The universal property of the henselization.** For any ring and ideal, the henselization is a henselian pair through which every map to a henselian pair factors uniquely ([`Henselization.existsUnique_lift`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/Henselization/Lift.html#TauCeti.Henselization.existsUnique_lift)).
- **The perfection of a scheme.** For `X` over `𝔽_p`, `X^perf` is perfect, maps to `X` by a universal homeomorphism, and every map from a perfect scheme to `X` lifts uniquely to it ([`Scheme.bijective_comp_fromPerfection`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Frobenius/Perfection.html#AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.bijective_comp_fromPerfection)).
- **The henselization criterion for Nisnevich coverings.** A family of étale maps is a Nisnevich covering exactly when, at each point, the spectrum of the henselized local ring lifts through one of them ([`isNisnevichCovering_iff_henselization`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Sites/Nisnevich/Henselization.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.isNisnevichCovering_iff_henselization)).
- **Leray acyclicity.** For a morphism of sites and an abelian sheaf with no higher direct images, the edge map `Hⁿ(C, f_* F) → Hⁿ(D, F)` is bijective in every degree ([`lerayEdge_sheafAdjunctionContinuous_bijective`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/CategoryTheory/Sites/SheafCohomology/Leray/Edge.html#CategoryTheory.Sheaf.H.lerayEdge_sheafAdjunctionContinuous_bijective)). It is proved by dimension shifting, without the spectral sequence.
- **Resolution of curves.** For `Y` integral, locally Noetherian, of dimension at most one, with finite normalization, that normalization is regular ([`isRegular_normalization_fromSpecStalk_genericPoint`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Normalization/Regular.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.isRegular_normalization_fromSpecStalk_genericPoint)). Finiteness of the normalization is still a hypothesis.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Chow groups** ([`Chow.ChowGroup`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Chow/Basic.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Chow.ChowGroup)) are `k`-cycles modulo rational equivalence on locally Noetherian schemes, compared with the divisor class group of an integral scheme of finite type over a field. The functorial [pushforward of cycles](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Chow/Pushforward.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Chow.cyclesMap) is the start of proper pushforward.
- **The formal completion** ([`Scheme.formalCompletion`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/AdicThickeningSystem/FormalCompletion.html#AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.formalCompletion)) of a scheme along a closed subscheme is an adic thickening system, and for `Spec R` it agrees with the formal spectrum of the completion. It is the object needed for the theorem on formal functions and for algebraization.
- **fpqc descent of sections** ([`isSheaf_bigPresheaf_fpqcTopology`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Sites/Big/Sheaf.html#AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.isSheaf_bigPresheaf_fpqcTopology)): for quasi-coherent `F`, `T ↦ Γ(T, h^*F)` is an fpqc sheaf on the big site. Together with the [faithfulness of the descent comparison](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Descent/QuasicoherentSheaf/Separated.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Descent.qcohPseudofunctor_faithful_toDescentData) for every fpqc covering, it is half of the stack property that §1.1 asks for.

Effective descent (§1.1), fppf descent (§1.2), algebraic spaces over a base (§1.4) and everything from §1.5 on are open. In Layer 2, §2.1 has cohomology pullback and higher direct images as sheafified cohomology presheaves, now with their module structure. §2.2 has the Leray edge maps and acyclicity but not the Leray or Čech spectral sequence. The Nisnevich topology and functorial torsor classes are also in place. Layer 3 has genus invariance under field extension and part of §3.3's degree laws. Layer 4 has thickenings, the coefficient category, formal completion, modifications and regular schemes. Layer 5 has Chow groups, the divisor class comparison and pushforward on cycles.

## The frontier

- **Effective fpqc descent of quasi-coherent modules (§1.1).** Morphisms descend uniquely, and they exist along affine faithfully flat maps. What remains is effective descent of objects and gluing to general fpqc coverings.
- **The Leray spectral sequence (§2.2).** The edge maps, acyclicity and degree-one injectivity are proved. What remains is the spectral sequence `H^p(D, R^q f_* F) ⇒ H^{p+q}(C, F)` itself, its relative form, and the Čech-to-cohomology spectral sequence.
- **Topological invariance of the étale site (§0.23a).** What remains is essential surjectivity of base change along a universal homeomorphism, then §0.24's equivalence of étale sites for the perfection.
- **Proper pushforward on Chow groups (§5.2).** Cycle pushforward exists. What remains is that it respects rational equivalence for proper maps, then flat pullback and the localisation sequence.
- **Torsor classes and `H¹` (§2.3).** What remains is pullback along morphisms of sites and the comparison `equivSheafH` with abelian sheaf cohomology.
