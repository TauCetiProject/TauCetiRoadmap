# Roadmap: Gröbner bases, affine semigroups and algebraic degenerations

**Summary.** Fix a field `k` and a monomial order on `k[x₁, …, xₙ]`, so that every nonzero
polynomial has a leading term, its largest term. A *Gröbner basis* of an ideal `I` is a subset of
`I` whose leading terms generate the ideal of leading terms of all elements of `I`; a *SAGBI basis*
of a subalgebra `A` is a subset of `A` whose leading terms generate the algebra of leading terms of
`A`. An *affine semigroup* is a finitely generated submonoid `S` of a finitely generated free
abelian group, with semigroup ring `k[S]`. The roadmap develops these as tools of algebraic
geometry (normal forms and elimination, normalization, orbits and degrees of semigroup rings,
subduction, and the flat families that degenerate an ideal or a subalgebra to its initial ideal or
algebra) and applies them to minors, invariant algebras, explicit surfaces and tree degenerations.
Division and normal forms for polynomials with unit leading coefficients, toric ideals and the
ideal-adic associated graded ring work over any commutative ring, and the identities among minors
over `ℤ`; reduced bases, completion and SAGBI theory are over a field, algebraically closed for
statements about points.

The headline theorems are these.

- *Buchberger's criterion and normal forms.* A set `G` whose nonzero members have unit leading
  coefficients is a Gröbner basis of the ideal it generates if and only if every S-polynomial of
  two members has remainder `0` on division by `G`. Over a field every ideal has exactly one reduced
  Gröbner basis, and the monomials outside its ideal of leading terms form a basis of `k[x]/I`. Tau
  Ceti proves these, and the roadmap builds on them.
- *Normality.* For an affine semigroup `S`, `k[S]` is integrally closed if and only if `S` is
  saturated in the group it generates, and the normalization of `k[S]` is the semigroup ring of the
  saturation.
- *SAGBI bases and degenerations.* A family of monic polynomials is a SAGBI basis of the algebra it
  generates if and only if each binomial relation among its leading monomials lifts, by a
  subduction with strictly smaller exponents, to a relation of the family; the lifted relations then
  generate all relations. For a weight `w` with values in `ℕ`, every ideal `I` is the fibre at
  `t = 1` of a flat family over `k[t]` whose fibre at `t = 0` is the ideal of `w`-initial forms of
  `I`; for a subalgebra the fibre at `t = 0` is the algebra of `w`-initial forms.
- *Minors and invariants.* The `2ⁿ − 1` flag minors of the generic `n × n` matrix form a SAGBI
  basis for a diagonal term order, with initial monoid the Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns with nonnegative
  entries. For the graded reverse lexicographic order, the leading terms of the Plücker relations
  among the maximal minors of a generic `n × d` matrix are generated by the products of incomparable
  pairs. For a group `G` of permutations of the variables, `k[x]^G` has a finite SAGBI basis for a
  given monomial order if and only if `G` is generated by the transpositions it contains.

Tau Ceti's Gröbner, toric-ideal and SAGBI libraries are the floor of Layers 1–3. The affine toric
schemes of the completed AnalyticToricGeometry roadmap are compared with semigroup rings in Layer 2.
SchemeAndStackFoundations supplies relative Proj, the cohomology of projective space and `K₀`, used
by the projective families of Layer 4 and the surface `F₁` of Layer 6. The Gelfand–Tsetlin patterns
of ClassicalGroups Layer 6 are the initial monoid of Layer 5's flag-minor algebra. SchubertGeometry
takes Layer 5's straightening, and DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra and
ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero take Layer 4's associated graded ring.

## How to read the build

Layer 1 is the theory of Gröbner bases: remainders, normal forms, Buchberger's criterion, reduced
bases, monomial orders, elimination, finite weight realization and weight-initial ideals. Most of
it is Tau Ceti's, and the layer names the pinned declarations and states what remains. Layer 2
studies the semigroup ring `k[S]` of an affine semigroup: its binomial presentation, its ring
structure, saturation and normalization, its integral model and torus orbits, its projective
spectrum, Hilbert function and degree. Layer 3 is the theory of initial algebras and SAGBI bases:
monic normalization, subduction, the SAGBI criterion, the presentation by lifted relations, nonmonic
families and completion. Layer 4 builds Rees algebras of filtrations, the ordinary associated graded
ring of an ideal, the flat weight-initial families of ideals and of subalgebras with finite SAGBI
bases, their projective versions under standard and nonstandard gradings, Hilbert series of graded
modules, graded sheafification on projective space and the comparison of fibre classes in `K₀` of
projective space. Layer 5 applies Layers 1–4 to minors of generic matrices, flag minors, `SL₂`
invariants and permutation invariants. Layer 6 carries out explicit calculations with all of
them.

Layer 2 needs Layer 1's elimination and Hilbert-function results; Layer 3 needs Layer 2's binomial
presentation; Layer 4 needs Layers 1–3; Layer 5 needs Layers 1–4; Layer 6 needs Layers 2–5. Other
roadmaps cite Layer `k` of this roadmap as `Gk`. Each section's *Needs:* line lists what its
statements and its route use, so that a target proved by another route is not blocked behind an
unrelated section.

## Conventions

1. **Rings and variables.** Rings are commutative. Polynomial rings are Mathlib's
   `MvPolynomial σ R`. The variable type is finite (`[Finite σ]`) whenever a statement asserts a
   finite Gröbner basis, a finite SAGBI basis, termination, Noetherianity or finite-type geometry;
   other statements allow any `σ`. Division with remainder is stated over a commutative ring for
   divisors whose nonzero members have unit leading coefficients, keeping Mathlib's more general
   coefficient hypotheses; over a field this is every nonzero divisor
   (`MonomialOrder.isUnit_leadingCoeff`). Normal forms need only a Gröbner basis whose nonzero
   members have unit leading coefficients; reduced bases, Buchberger completion and SAGBI theory are
   over a field `k`. Statements about geometric points and orbits assume `k` algebraically closed;
   the ring and scheme constructions precede that specialization and are made over an arbitrary
   field, or over `ℤ`.
2. **Leading terms.** A monomial order is Mathlib's `m : MonomialOrder σ`, and the initial term of
   a polynomial is its **largest** term: `m.degree`, `m.leadingCoeff` and `m.leadingTerm`, compared
   through the well-ordered synonym `m.toSyn`, written `a ≺[m] b`. Mathlib's `m.degree 0` is `0`;
   a statement about the leading exponent of `f` assumes `f ≠ 0`, Tau Ceti's remainders measure
   degrees by `MonomialOrder.withBotDegree`, which is `⊥` at `0`, and no strict inequality is ever
   asserted for the totalized degree of `0`. `m.Monic f` means leading coefficient `1`.
3. **Variable order.** Variables are ordered antitonely: `X i ≤ X j` in the monomial order if and
   only if `j ≤ i` in the index order (`Finsupp.Lex.single_lt_iff`,
   `MonomialOrder.degLex_single_le_iff`, `TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex_single_le_iff`), so that
   `X 0 > X 1 > ⋯` on `Fin n`. The entries `z_(i,j)` of a generic matrix are indexed by
   `Lex (Fin n × Fin n)`, so that `z_(0,0)` is the largest variable, then `z_(0,1)`, and so on
   row by row. Plücker variables `P_I` are indexed by the order dual of the lexicographic order on
   sorted row sets, so that `P_I < P_J` when `I` precedes `J` lexicographically.
4. **Weights.** A weight is `w : σ → ℕ`, with Mathlib's `Finsupp.weight w` on exponents. The
   `w`-initial form of `f` selects the terms of **maximal** weight; it may have several terms and is
   distinct from a leading term for a monomial order. Weight filtrations are increasing: the stage
   `F_j` consists of the polynomials all of whose terms have weight at most `j`.
5. **Semigroup rings.** All semigroup algebras are Mathlib's `AddMonoidAlgebra k S`, with additive
   exponents, and a generator `a` of `S` is sent to the monomial `AddMonoidAlgebra.single a 1`, with
   coefficient `1`. Compare with `MonoidAlgebra k (Multiplicative S)`, the carrier of Tau Ceti's
   toric coordinate rings, through `AddMonoidAlgebra.toMultiplicativeAlgEquiv`.
6. **Affine semigroups.** An affine semigroup is a finitely generated `S : AddSubmonoid L` of a
   finitely generated free abelian group `L`. Its generated group is `L_S = AddSubgroup.closure S`.
   Saturation, normalization, torus characters and lattice indices are taken in `L_S`, never in an
   oversized ambient lattice: the saturation of `S` is the set of elements of `L_S` with a positive
   multiple in `S`, that is, Tau Ceti's `S.nsmulSaturation ⊓ L_S`, and `S` is *saturated* when this
   is `S`. The ring `k[S]` may be nonnormal. Cone and fan geometry is that of Tau Ceti's toric
   library and is not recast as a private class of varieties.
7. **Degrees and counts.** The affine degree (multiplicity) and the projective degree are
   different invariants; *degree* alone is the degree of a projective scheme. Hilbert functions and
   their series and polynomials have values in `ℚ`, also when `k` has positive characteristic.
8. **Mathematics, not algorithms.** Gröbner and SAGBI statements are existence, uniqueness and
   termination theorems, and a *certificate* is a finite set of mathematical data, such as a
   standard representation or finitely many monomial comparisons. A tactic's internal Gröbner
   engine does not replace this API. The predicates `MonomialOrder.IsRemainder` and
   `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis` are Tau Ceti's and follow the design of the Mathlib proposals
   listed under References, with the division and remainder direction of the Zulip discussion
   listed there.
9. **Names.** The headline theorems and the few definitions they need are declared in
   `Suggested.lean` under `TauCetiRoadmap.GroebnerGeometry`; a target without a declaration there is
   described in prose.

## Layer 1: ideals, normal forms and elimination

Cox–Little–O'Shea, Chapters 2–4, and Bossinger, §2.2, for weight-initial ideals. Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/` and `TauCeti/Data/Finsupp/MonomialOrder/` prove most of
this layer; each section names the pinned declarations and states the targets that remain.

### 1.1 Gröbner bases and remainders

The ideal of leading terms of `I` is `Ideal.span (m.leadingTerm '' I)`. A Gröbner basis of `I` is
Tau Ceti's `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis m G I`: `G ⊆ I` and the leading terms of `G` span the
ideal of leading terms of `I`. A remainder is Tau Ceti's `MonomialOrder.IsRemainder m f B r`:
`f = ∑_b g_b · b + r` with `deg b + deg g_b ≤ deg f` for every `b ∈ B` (a bounded-degree division
representation), and no monomial of `r` is divisible by the leading monomial of a nonzero member
of `B`. The remainder condition concerns divisibility of monomials, not a comparison of total
degrees (Cox–Little–O'Shea, Chapter 2, §3). Remainders exist when every nonzero divisor has a
unit leading coefficient (`MonomialOrder.exists_isRemainder`, from Mathlib's
`MonomialOrder.div_set`). The pinned API includes `MonomialOrder.isGroebnerBasis_self`,
extension by redundant generators `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.mono`, zero removal
`MonomialOrder.isGroebnerBasis_sdiff_singleton_zero`, and generation
`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.ideal_eq_span`.

Prove the remaining API, separating ring statements with unit leading coefficients from field
statements:

- *Monic rescaling.* If `G` is a Gröbner basis of `I` and every nonzero `g ∈ G` has a unit leading
  coefficient, the set of the `C (m.leadingCoeff g)⁻¹ * g` for nonzero `g ∈ G` is a Gröbner basis
  of `I` consisting of monic polynomials, with the same leading monomials; over a field this holds
  for every Gröbner basis.
- *Transport.* For an injective `e : σ → τ` and monomial orders `m` on `σ` and `m'` on `τ` with
  `a ≼[m] b ↔ a.mapDomain e ≼[m'] b.mapDomain e`, renaming by `e` carries remainders to remainders
  and a Gröbner basis `G` of `I` to a Gröbner basis of `I.map (rename e)`. The converse direction,
  contraction to the variables `e τ`, is the elimination theorem of §1.7.
- *Subsets and spans.* A subset `G ⊆ I` is a Gröbner basis of `I` if and only if it is a Gröbner
  basis of `Ideal.span G` and `Ideal.span G = I`, when its nonzero members have unit leading
  coefficients.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis`, `MonomialOrder.IsRemainder`,
`MonomialOrder.exists_isRemainder`, the leading-data transport lemmas of
`TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/MonomialOrder.lean`; Mathlib `MonomialOrder.div_set`,
`MvPolynomial.rename`.

**Checks.**
- Over `ℚ[x, y]` with lex `x > y`, take `B = {x² + y}` and `f = y²`. Then `r = y²` is a remainder:
  no monomial of `r` is divisible by `x²`, and it is the only remainder, since the leading monomial
  `x²` of the divisor lies above `y²` and the degree bound forces the quotient to be `0`. A
  definition asking instead that no monomial of `r` have total degree at least that of a leading
  monomial would reject `r = y²`, and `f` would have no remainder at all.
- With `G = {x, x + y}` in `ℚ[x, y]`, lex `x > y`, the leading terms are both `x`, so `G` is not a
  Gröbner basis of `(x, y)`: `y` lies in the ideal, and its leading term is not in `(x)`.

### 1.2 Normal forms and the standard-monomial basis

The normal-form theory is Tau Ceti's, for a Gröbner basis `G` of `I` whose nonzero members have unit
leading coefficients. A remainder of `f` is zero exactly when `f ∈ I`
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.remainder_eq_zero_iff_mem`), and it is unique
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.existsUnique_isRemainder`). The standard monomials
`MonomialOrder.standardMonomials m I`, those outside the ideal of leading terms, give a basis
`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.basisQuotient` of `MvPolynomial σ R ⧸ I`. The normal form
`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.normalForm` is a linear map, so it satisfies the additive and scalar
equations; it is idempotent (`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.normalForm_normalForm`), has kernel `I`
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.ker_normalForm`), lifts the quotient map
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.mk_normalForm`) and is compatible with quotient multiplication, the
product reduction `NF(NF f · NF g) = NF(f g)`
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.normalForm_mul_normalForm`). This roadmap restates none of it.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/NormalForm.lean`.

**Checks.**
-  For `I = (x² − y)` in `ℚ[x, y]` with lex `x > y`, the leading-term ideal is `(x²)`, the standard
  monomials are the `yᵇ` and `x yᵇ`, and the normal form of `x³` is `x y`. With the order `y > x`
  instead, the leading monomial of `x² − y` is `y`, the standard monomials are the `xᵃ`, and the
  normal form of `x³` is `x³`: the basis depends on the order.

### 1.3 Homogeneous ideals

For a weight grading `w : σ → M` (total degree when `w = 1`) and an ideal homogeneous for it, the
graded versions are Tau Ceti's: a Gröbner basis of weighted homogeneous polynomials exists
(`MonomialOrder.exists_isGroebnerBasis_isWeightedHomogeneous`), the reduced basis is weighted
homogeneous (`MonomialOrder.IsReducedGroebnerBasis.isWeightedHomogeneous`), the normal form
preserves weighted homogeneous components, and each graded piece of the quotient is linearly
isomorphic to the corresponding piece for the ideal of leading terms
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.mapWeightedHomogeneousSubmoduleEquivSpanLeadingTerm`), no
compatibility of the monomial order with the grading being needed. *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/Homogeneous.lean`; Mathlib
`MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousSubmodule`.

### 1.4 S-polynomials, Buchberger's criterion and completion

Use Mathlib's `MonomialOrder.sPolynomial`, its lcm formula `MonomialOrder.sPolynomial_def`, the
leading-coefficient cancellation `MonomialOrder.coeff_sPolynomial_sup_eq_zero` and the degree drop
`MonomialOrder.degree_sPolynomial_lt_sup_degree`. Buchberger's criterion is Tau Ceti's
`MonomialOrder.isGroebnerBasis_span_iff_isRemainder_sPolynomial_zero`, through the standard
representations of `MonomialOrder.isGroebnerBasis_span_of_isRemainder_sPolynomial_zero`. Adjoining
a nonzero remainder of an element of `Ideal.span G` strictly enlarges the ideal of leading terms
(`MonomialOrder.IsRemainder.span_leadingTerm_lt`), so these adjunctions are well founded in
finitely many variables over a Noetherian ring (`MonomialOrder.wellFounded_remainderStep`), and
finite Gröbner bases exist (`MonomialOrder.exists_finset_isGroebnerBasis`,
`MonomialOrder.exists_finset_monic_isGroebnerBasis`).

Prove the termination of S-polynomial completion (Cox–Little–O'Shea, Chapter 2, §§6–7): over a
field and in finitely many variables, for every finite `G`, every sequence of finite sets
`G = G₀ ⊂ G₁ ⊂ ⋯` in which `G_(i+1)` adjoins to `G_i` a nonzero remainder, on division by `G_i`, of
an S-polynomial of two members of `G_i` is finite; and a sequence that cannot be extended, every
remainder of every S-polynomial of two members of its last set on division by that set being `0`,
ends at a Gröbner basis of `Ideal.span G`. No complexity bound is asserted.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `MonomialOrder.RemainderStep`, `MonomialOrder.wellFounded_remainderStep`,
`MonomialOrder.isGroebnerBasis_span_iff_isRemainder_sPolynomial_zero`; Mathlib
`MonomialOrder.sPolynomial`.

**Checks.**
- In `ℚ[x, y]` with lex `x > y`, `G = {x y − 1, y² − 1}` has S-polynomial
  `y (x y − 1) − x (y² − 1) = x − y`, whose leading monomial `x` is divisible by neither `x y` nor
  `y²`: `G` is not a Gröbner basis. Adjoining `x − y` gives a Gröbner basis, and since
  `x y − 1 = y (x − y) + (y² − 1)`, the reduced basis of `(x y − 1, y² − 1)` is `{x − y, y² − 1}`.

### 1.5 Reduced Gröbner bases and comparison of generating sets

Reduced bases are Tau Ceti's `MonomialOrder.IsReducedGroebnerBasis`, with existence and uniqueness
over a field (`MonomialOrder.existsUnique_isReducedGroebnerBasis`), finiteness in finitely many
variables and the comparison `MonomialOrder.IsReducedGroebnerBasis.eq_iff_ideal_eq`: two ideals are
equal if and only if their reduced Gröbner bases are. Prove the comparison of generating sets by
normal forms: over a field, for sets `s` and `t` and a Gröbner basis `G` of `Ideal.span t`,
`Ideal.span s ≤ Ideal.span t` if and only if every member of `s` has normal form `0` modulo `G`;
hence `s` and `t` generate the same ideal if and only if the normal forms vanish both ways, if and
only if the two ideals have the same reduced Gröbner basis. *Needs:* §1.2; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/Reduced.lean`.

### 1.6 Monomial orders and the variable convention

Use Mathlib's `MonomialOrder.lex` and `MonomialOrder.degLex` and Tau Ceti's graded reverse
lexicographic order `TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex`, with its comparison lemmas
`TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex_le_iff` and `TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex_lt_iff` and its
well-foundedness on the synonym `TauCeti.DegRevLex`. All three follow the antitone variable
convention of Convention 3. Layer 5 uses `degRevLex` on the Plücker index of Convention 3, so that
`P_I < P_J` when `I` precedes `J` lexicographically. *Needs:* Mathlib
`Mathlib/Data/Finsupp/MonomialOrder/DegLex.lean`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/Data/Finsupp/MonomialOrder/DegRevLex.lean`.

**Checks.**
- On `Fin 3`, `X 0 * X 2 < X 1 ^ 2` for `degRevLex` while `X 1 ^ 2 < X 0 * X 2` for `degLex`: in
  degree two the two orders differ. A variant that compared at the largest differing index but
  preferred the larger exponent there would put `X 1 ^ 2 < X 0 * X 2`, as `degLex` does.
- For `lex` on `Fin 2`, `X 1 ^ k < X 0` for every `k`: the largest variable dominates every power
  of a smaller one.

### 1.7 Elimination, intersection, saturation and colon ideals

The elimination theorem is Tau Ceti's: for an elimination order, the members of a Gröbner basis of
`I` in the variables `e τ` form a Gröbner basis of `I.comap (rename e)`
(`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.comap_rename`, `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.comap_rename_eq_span`,
and for lex `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.comap_rename_lex`). The added-variable comparisons are
Tau Ceti's: `Ideal.span_inf_span_eq_comap_rename_some` for intersections,
`Ideal.comap_rename_some_map_rename_some_sup_span` for the saturation by an element as a
contraction of `I + (f X_none − 1)`, with membership certificates
`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.remainder_eq_zero_iff_mem_inf` and
`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.remainder_eq_zero_iff_exists_pow_mul_mem`, the Gröbner basis of the
saturation `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.comap_map_algebraMap_away`, and the colon ideal by a
regular element `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.colon_singleton_eq_span`.

Prove the remaining computations (Cox–Little–O'Shea, Chapter 3, §1, and Chapter 4, §§3–4):

- *Intersection.* For an elimination order on `Option σ` eliminating the added variable, the
  members free of the added variable of a Gröbner basis of the auxiliary ideal of
  `Ideal.span_inf_span_eq_comap_rename_some` form a Gröbner basis of `I ∩ J`.
- *Colon ideals.* For ideals `I` and `J = (f₁, …, f_r)`, `(I : J) = ⋂_i (I : f_i)`, and each
  `(I : f_i)` with `f_i` regular (every nonzero `f_i` over a field) is computed from `I ∩ (f_i)` by
  the pinned statement, so a Gröbner basis of `(I : J)` comes from those of the `(I : f_i)` and
  the intersections above.
- *Ideal equality.* `I = J` if and only if their reduced Gröbner bases agree (§1.5), and
  `I ≤ J` if and only if the generators of `I` have zero remainder modulo a Gröbner basis of `J`.

*Needs:* §1.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/{Elimination,Intersection,Colon}.lean`
and `TauCeti/RingTheory/Ideal/Colon.lean`.

**Checks.**
- For `I = (x) ∩ (y)` in `ℚ[x, y]`, the auxiliary ideal is `(t x, (1 − t) y)` in `ℚ[t, x, y]`;
  for lex `t > x > y` its reduced Gröbner basis is `{t x, t y − y, x y}`, and the member free of
  `t` is `x y`, so `(x) ∩ (y) = (x y)`. The product `(x)(y)` agrees here, but for `I = J = (x)` the
  intersection is `(x)` and the product is `(x²)`.

### 1.8 Finite weight realization

Any finitely many comparisons in a monomial order are realized by one strictly positive integral
weight (`MonomialOrder.exists_pos_weight_lt`, `MonomialOrder.exists_pos_weight_lt_iff_lt`),
including all comparisons among exponents of bounded total degree in finitely many variables
(`MonomialOrder.exists_pos_weight_lt_iff_lt_of_degree_le`), through the finite linear-inequality
separation lemma `TauCeti.exists_nat_sum_nsmul_eq_zero_and_forall_coeff_add_dual_pos`. No single
weight represents every comparison of an arbitrary monomial order. *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/Data/Finsupp/MonomialOrder/Weight.lean`, `TauCeti/Geometry/Convex/Cone/Alternative.lean`.

**Checks.**
- For lex on `k[x, y]` with `x > y`, no weight `w` with `w(x), w(y) > 0` satisfies
  `k · w(y) < w(x)` for all `k`, so no weight realizes all the comparisons `yᵏ < x`; for the
  finitely many comparisons among exponents of total degree at most `d`, the weight `(d + 1, 1)`
  does.

### 1.9 Weight-initial ideals, refinement and Gröbner cones

Define `weightInitialForm w f`, the sum of the terms of `f` of maximal `w`-weight (Mathlib's
`MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousComponent` at `MvPolynomial.weightedTotalDegree w f`), and the
weight-initial ideal `weightInitialIdeal w I`, generated by the weight-initial forms of the elements
of `I` (Bossinger, §2.2, with the same maximal-weight convention).

- The weight-initial form of `0` is `0`, that of a weighted homogeneous polynomial is itself, and
  over a domain `in_w(f g) = in_w(f) · in_w(g)`.
- `weightInitialIdeal w I` is homogeneous for the grading by `w`, and it contains the
  weight-initial forms of all elements of `I`, not only of chosen generators.
- *Refinement.* For a weight `w` and a monomial order `m`, the order comparing exponents first by
  `w`-weight and then by `m` is a monomial order, the refinement of `w` by `m`. For every ideal
  `I`, the ideal of `m`-leading terms of `weightInitialIdeal w I` is the ideal of leading terms of
  `I` for the refinement; if `G` is a Gröbner basis of `I` for the refinement, the weight-initial
  forms of `G` are a Gröbner basis of `weightInitialIdeal w I` for `m`.
- *Gröbner cones.* Let `G` be a finite Gröbner basis of `I` for `m` whose nonzero members have unit
  leading coefficients, with the finite set of comparisons of
  `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.exists_finset_comparisons`. If a weight `w` satisfies all of these
  comparisons strictly, then `weightInitialIdeal w I` is the ideal of `m`-leading terms of `I`: the
  finite comparisons certify an unchanged initial ideal. The pinned
  `MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.exists_pos_weight_preserving` supplies such a strictly positive
  `w`.

*Needs:* §§1.1, 1.8; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/Groebner/Cone.lean`; Mathlib
`MvPolynomial.weightedHomogeneousComponent`, `MvPolynomial.weightedTotalDegree`.

**Checks.**
- For `f = x² − y` and `w = (1, 1)`, the weight-initial form is `x²`; for `w = (1, 3)` it is `−y`;
  for `w = (1, 2)` it is `x² − y`, with two terms, and no monomial order has a leading term with
  two terms. A definition selecting the terms of minimal weight would give `−y` for `w = (1, 1)`.
- For `I = (x − y², y)` and `w = (1, 1)`, the weight-initial forms of the two generators are `−y²`
  and `y`, which generate `(y)`; but `x = (x − y²) + y · y` lies in `I`, so
  `weightInitialIdeal w I = (x, y)`. Initial forms of chosen generators do not determine the
  weight-initial ideal.

### 1.10 Change of coefficients

`MonomialOrder.IsGroebnerBasis.comap_map_eq`), and over a field extension of scalars does not change
the ideal of leading terms (`MonomialOrder.span_leadingTerm_map`). Prove the same for reduced bases,

## Layer 2: semigroup presentations, normalization and degree

### 2.1 The exponent map and the binomial presentation

graph ideal (`TauCeti.toricIdeal_eq_comap_graph`), so §1.7 computes it. *Needs:* Tau Ceti

- *Dimension.* For a field `k` and `S` finitely generated, the Krull dimension of `k[S]` is the rank

- *Products.* `k[S × T] ≃ₐ[k] k[S] ⊗[k] k[T]`, as algebras, extending Mathlib's linear

- *Functoriality.* A monoid map `S → T` induces `k[S] → k[T]` (Mathlib

**Checks.**

### 2.3 Saturation and normalization

Tau Ceti proves that monomials of the saturation are integral over `k[S]`
(`AddMonoidAlgebra.isIntegralElem_single_one_iff`,
`AddMonoidAlgebra.isIntegral_mapDomainRingHom_inclusion_nsmulSaturation`) and that an integrally
closed `k[S]` forces `S` to be saturated in `L_S` (`AddSubmonoid.nsmulSaturation_inf_closure_eq`).
Prove the converse and the identification of the normalization (Miller–Sturmfels, Definition 7.24
and Proposition 7.25):

- The saturation of a finitely generated `S` is the set of points of `L_S` in the rational cone
  `ℝ≥0 S`, it is finitely generated (Gordan's lemma, Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Toric.IsToricCone.fg_dualSemigroup`
  for the dual cone), and `k[sat S]` is a finite `k[S]`-module.
- `isIntegrallyClosed_addMonoidAlgebra_of_saturated`: for a field `k` and `S` finitely generated
  and saturated in `L_S`, `k[S]` is integrally closed.
- `k[sat S]` is the integral closure of `k[S]` in its fraction field, which is the fraction field
  of `k[L_S]`; so `k[S]` is normal if and only if `S` is saturated in `L_S`. Over an integrally
  closed domain `R` of coefficients, `R[S]` is integrally closed if and only if `S` is saturated,
  and the hypotheses on the coefficients are stated explicitly in each statement.

The normalization is never taken in a larger fraction field by enlarging the lattice. *Needs:*
§2.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/Algebra/MonoidAlgebra/Saturation.lean`,
`TauCeti/Algebra/Group/Submonoid/NSMulSaturation.lean`, `TauCeti.Toric.IsToricCone.fg_dualSemigroup`;
Mathlib `IsIntegrallyClosed`, `integralClosure`, `AddSubmonoid.NSMulSaturated`.

**Checks.**

### 2.5 Faces, orbits and stabilizers

A *face* of `S` is a submonoid `F` such that `a + b ∈ F` with `a, b ∈ S` forces `a, b ∈ F`. For a
face `F`, construct its prime monomial ideal, spanned by the monomials of `S ∖ F`, the closed face
scheme it cuts out, and the orbit `Spec k[gp F]`, a localization of the face scheme. Over an
algebraically closed field prove (Sturmfels, *Equations defining toric varieties*, Proposition
1.3(a), over `ℂ`, for the first two statements):

- the geometric points of `Spec k[S]` are the disjoint union of the orbits, one for each face, and
  the orbit of `F` has dimension `rank (gp F)`;
- the closure of the orbit of `F` is the union of the orbits of the faces contained in `F`, so
  closure is the order of faces;
- the stabilizer of the orbit of `F` in the torus `Spec k[L_S]` is the diagonalizable group with
  character group `L_S ⧸ gp F`, the kernel of the restriction map `Spec k[L_S] → Spec k[gp F]`
  through which the torus acts on the orbit. The torsion subgroup `T` of `L_S ⧸ gp F`, of order the
  index of `gp F` in its saturation in `L_S`, gives the finite diagonalizable factor `Spec k[T]` of
  the stabilizer, isomorphic to `μ_(m₁) × ⋯ × μ_(m_r)` for `T ≅ ℤ/m₁ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ ℤ/m_r`; it is nonreduced
  exactly when the characteristic divides the order of `T`, and `T` need not be cyclic;
- the normalization map restricts on the orbit of each face of `sat S` to a map of tori, which need
  not be injective on geometric points of boundary orbits.

Supply the dense torus action as a native coaction of `k[L_S]` on `k[S]`, compatible with Tau Ceti's
torus coaction of `TauCeti/Geometry/Toric/Algebraic/TorusAction/`, and compare the faces of a toric
cone with the faces of its dual semigroup. *Needs:* §§2.2–2.4; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/Geometry/Toric/Algebraic/{FaceLocalization,DenseTorus,TorusAction}`.

**Checks.**
- In `L = ℤ³` let `S` be generated by `(2, 0, 0)`, `(0, 2, 0)`, `(0, 0, 1)`, `(1, 0, 1)` and
  `(0, 1, 1)`. Then `L_S = ℤ³`, since `(1, 0, 0) = (1, 0, 1) − (0, 0, 1)` and likewise for

  single `μ_m`; the index of `gp F` in its saturation `ℤ² × 0` is `4`.

## Layer 3: subalgebras, SAGBI bases and subduction

Bruns–Conca–Lembo, §2. Fields throughout.

### 3.1 Initial algebras and SAGBI bases

The carriers are Tau Ceti's. The initial algebra `MonomialOrder.initialAlgebra m A` is the
subalgebra generated by the leading terms of the elements of `A`; the leading exponents of the
nonzero elements of `A` form the additive submonoid `MonomialOrder.leadingExponents m A`, and
`MonomialOrder.initialAlgebraEquiv` identifies its monoid algebra with the initial algebra. A SAGBI
basis is `MonomialOrder.IsSAGBIBasis m B A`: `B ⊆ A` and the leading terms of `B` generate the
initial algebra. The definition applies to arbitrary `B`; monicity is no part of it. The pinned API
includes zero removal `MonomialOrder.isSAGBIBasis_sdiff_singleton_zero`, inclusion
`MonomialOrder.IsSAGBIBasis.mono`, the characterization by leading exponents
`MonomialOrder.isSAGBIBasis_iff_degree_mem_closure` and generation
`MonomialOrder.IsSAGBIBasis.adjoin_eq`. Prove the remaining API:

- *Extension of scalars.* For a field extension `k → K`, the image of a SAGBI basis of `A` is a
  SAGBI basis of the `K`-subalgebra generated by the image of `A`, whose initial algebra is generated
  by the image of the initial algebra of `A`.
- *Transport.* Renaming the variables by an injective map compatible with the monomial orders, as in
  §1.1, carries initial algebras to initial algebras and SAGBI bases to SAGBI bases.
- *Scalar generators.* A nonzero constant in `B` is a generator of degree `0`, with leading exponent
  `0` and the relation to `1` of §3.4; alternatively it is dropped from the family by comparing the algebras over the
  base field. Neither the zero polynomial nor a nonzero constant acquires a positive leading
  exponent.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/SAGBI/Basic.lean`; Layer 1 §1.10.

**Checks.**
- For `A = k[x², x³] ⊆ k[x]`, the leading exponents are `{0, 2, 3, 4, …}`, the initial algebra is
  `A`, and `{x², x³}` is a SAGBI basis; `{x²}` is not, since `3` is not a multiple of `2`.

### 3.2 Monic normalization

Over a field, for an indexed family `b`, first remove its zero members, and put
`ℓ_i = m.leadingCoeff b_i` and `c_i = C(ℓ_i⁻¹) * b_i`. Prove `ℓ_i ≠ 0`, `m.Monic c_i`,
`m.degree c_i = m.degree b_i` and `b_i = C(ℓ_i) * c_i`; that `b` and `c` generate the same
subalgebra; that the algebras generated by their actual leading terms are equal; and that `b` is a
SAGBI basis of a subalgebra `A` if and only if `c` is. These comparisons apply to infinite families;
the finite criteria below use finite indexed families (Bruns–Conca–Lembo, §2, on the reduction to
monic families). *Needs:* §3.1; Mathlib `MonomialOrder.Monic`, `MonomialOrder.leadingCoeff`.

### 3.3 Subduction

*Subduction* of `f` by `B` cancels the leading term of `f` against a scalar multiple of a product of
members of `B` with the same leading exponent, records that product in a polynomial expression in
variables `Y_b`, and recurses on the difference, which has a smaller leading exponent; the
recursion is well founded by the monomial order. Prove the subduction theorem: for a SAGBI basis
every element of `A` subducts to zero, with every product used having leading exponent at most that
of `f` (Tau Ceti `MonomialOrder.IsSAGBIBasis.mem_span`), so `B` generates `A`
(`MonomialOrder.IsSAGBIBasis.adjoin_eq`). For a general finite `B`, construct subduction as a
function retaining both the expression and the remainder: `f` equals the evaluated expression plus
a remainder whose leading exponent, if it is nonzero, is not in the monoid generated by the leading
exponents of `B`. A nonzero remainder of an element of `A` shows that `B` is not a SAGBI basis; it
does not show that an element lies outside `A` merely because the procedure stopped. *Needs:* §3.1.

### 3.4 The SAGBI criterion for a monic family

For a finite family `c` of nonzero monic polynomials, write `a_i = m.degree c_i` and
`A(u) = ∑_i u_i a_i` for `u ∈ ℕ^ι`. Compare Layer 2's coefficient-one presentation with the actual
homomorphisms `ψ₁(Y_i) = monomial a_i 1` and `φ_c(Y_i) = c_i` into `k[x]`: the semigroup algebra
embeds into `k[x]`, so the kernel of `ψ₁` is the toric ideal `TauCeti.toricIdeal k (fun i ↦ a_i)` of
§2.1. Choose finitely many nonzero binomials `β_j = Y^(u_j) − Y^(v_j)` generating it, with common
exponent `α_j = A(u_j) = A(v_j)`; an empty family of relations is allowed. A *bounded zero
subduction* of `φ_c(β_j)` is a polynomial `Q_j` in the `Y` variables with `φ_c(Q_j) = φ_c(β_j)` such
that every exponent `w` in the support of `Q_j` has `A(w) ≺[m] α_j`. The SAGBI criterion, that `c`
is a SAGBI basis of `k[c]` if and only if every `φ_c(β_j)` has a bounded zero subduction, is Tau
Ceti's `MonomialOrder.isSAGBIBasis_range_iff_forall_exists_aeval_eq` (Bruns–Conca–Lembo, §2,
Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 2.4).

The certificate is constructed by genuine leading-term subduction (§3.3). By monicity,
`φ_c(β_j) = 0` or `m.degree (φ_c(β_j)) ≺[m] α_j`; when `φ_c(β_j)` is nonzero, the products in a
zero subduction have exponent at most that of `φ_c(β_j)`, hence below `α_j`; when it is exactly
zero, `Q_j = 0`, with empty support. No strict inequality is asserted for Mathlib's totalized degree
of `0`, so that a relation of degree `0` such as `Y_i − 1` for a constant `c_i = 1` remains valid.
*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.2, 3.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/SAGBI/Criterion.lean`.

**Checks.**
- For `c = (x, 1)` in `k[x]`, the toric ideal of the leading exponents `1` and `0` is generated by
  `Y₂ − 1`, and `φ_c(Y₂ − 1) = 0`, so `Q = 0` is a bounded zero subduction and `c` is a SAGBI basis
  of `k[x]`. A criterion demanding `m.degree (φ_c(β)) ≺[m] α` would require `0 ≺ 0` and reject it.
- For the empty family, `k[c] = k`, the toric ideal of the empty family is `0`, generated by no
  binomial, and the criterion holds vacuously: `∅` is a SAGBI basis of `k`.
- For the redundant family `c = (x, x)`, the relation `Y₁ − Y₂` evaluates exactly to `0`, `Q = 0`,
  and `c` is a SAGBI basis of `k[x]`; `x` has the two expressions `Y₁` and `Y₂`.

### 3.5 The presentation by lifted relations

Set `F_j = β_j − Q_j`. Prove `φ_c(F_j) = 0` and that the top `A`-homogeneous part of `F_j`, the sum of
its terms of maximal `A`-exponent, is exactly `β_j`. Prove `ker_aeval_eq_span_sub_of_lifts`: the
`F_j` generate the kernel of `φ_c` (Bruns–Conca–Lembo, §2, Theorem 2.2). This holds for any family
`c` of monic polynomials and any family of binomials generating the toric ideal, finite or not, a
binomial with `u_j = v_j` included; Bruns–Conca–Lembo allow countable families, and the induction
uses no finiteness. Route: decrease the
maximal `A`-exponent of a polynomial expression in the kernel. Its cancelling top block lies in the
kernel of `ψ₁`; by the `A`-grading of that kernel the block is a combination of monomial multiples of
the `β_j`, all at that same exponent; replacing each `β_j` by `Q_j` lowers the maximal exponent. This
is a well-founded induction, not a list of relations whose substitutions happen to vanish. Export
these standard representations, the exact kernel maps and their finite-support data. Necessity in
§3.4 uses the subduction theorem with the same exponent bounds. Normal forms in the quotient of the
polynomial ring in the `Y` variables use this presentation; expressions in a redundant SAGBI family
need not be unique. *Needs:* §3.4; Tau Ceti `MonomialOrder.weightedHomogeneousComponent_mem_toricIdeal`.

### 3.6 Nonmonic families

Keep the coefficient changes in every certificate for the original nonmonic family `b` of §3.2.
Define the polynomial algebra automorphism `D_ℓ` of `k[Y]` by `D_ℓ(Y_i) = ℓ_i Y_i`, with inverse
`D_(ℓ⁻¹)`, and prove `φ_b = φ_c ∘ D_ℓ` and `ψ_b = ψ₁ ∘ D_ℓ`, where `ψ_b` sends `Y_i` to the actual
leading term of `b_i`. Hence the kernel of `ψ_b` is the preimage of the kernel of `ψ₁` under `D_ℓ`,
and the same transport carries the `F_j` to generators of the kernel of `φ_b`. Record the factor
`∏_i ℓ_i^(w_i)` on every monomial `Y^w` and its inverse on the transported relations and subduction
coefficients; clearing nonzero scalar denominators changes neither the ideal nor the exponent
bounds. This comparison keeps the original subalgebra and its presentation, rather than substituting
`b` into a coefficient-one relation. *Needs:* §§3.2, 3.5.

### 3.7 SAGBI completion

*Completion* adjoins to a finite family each nonzero subduction remainder (§3.3) of the images of
the binomial relations of §3.4, together with its expression in the previous generators, divides it
by its nonzero leading coefficient and records that scalar in the presentation maps of §3.6. Prove
that if the initial algebra of `A` is finitely generated, every fair completion, one that processes
every relation of every stage, started from a finite family generating `A`, ends after finitely many
steps at a finite SAGBI basis of `A` (Bruns–Conca–Lembo, §2, p. 5, the completion following
Proposition 2.4); without that hypothesis completion may run forever, even for a finitely generated
`A`. *Needs:* §§3.3–3.6.

### Examples

As a regression over `ℚ[x, y]` with lex `x > y`, take `b = (x, x + y, 2x + y)`. All leading
exponents are that of `x`. The coefficient-one relations `U − W` and `V − W`, evaluated on the raw
family, give `−(x + y)` and `−x`, each with a one-step zero subduction by a member of `b`, although
`y = b₂ − b₁` lies in `ℚ[b]` and not in `ℚ[x]`, the algebra of the actual leading terms. For the
normalized family `c = (x, x + y, x + y/2)` the relations evaluate to `−y/2` and `y/2`: their leading
exponent is below that of `x`, but no product of members of `c` with exponent below that of `x` is
other than a constant, so they have no bounded zero subduction. The criterion of §3.4 correctly
rejects this family. The nonzero scalar generator, the empty family, the exact-zero relation and the
redundant monic generators of the Checks of §3.4 complete the regression set.

## Layer 4: Rees algebras and flat initial families

### 4.1 Weight filtrations and their Rees algebras

For a weight `w` with values in `ℕ`, the increasing multiplicative filtration of a polynomial ring,
or of a subalgebra, by `w`-weight is an instance of Mathlib's `IsFiltration` and `IsRingFiltration`,
with the lower stages as the native `F_lt` family. Construct its Rees algebra `⊕_j F_j t^j` inside
`A[t]`, as Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.coeffSubalgebra` of the filtration. Give the comparison of the graded
sum with the subalgebra, the action of `t`, the associated graded ring `⊕_j F_j/F_(j−1)`, the fibre
at `t = 0` (the associated graded ring), the fibre at `t = 1` (`A`, Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.coeffSubalgebra.quotientSpanXSubAlgebraMapEquiv`), the generic localization
`R[t, t⁻¹] = A[t, t⁻¹]`, and naturality under filtered homomorphisms. In `k[t][x]` the Rees algebra
of a subalgebra `A` of `k[x]` is `weightReesFamily w A`, generated over `k[t]` by the weight
homogenizations `weightHomogenization w f` of the elements of `A` (§4.3); the map
`f t^j ↦ t^j f(t^(−w₁) x₁, …)` identifies the two. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsFiltration`,
`IsRingFiltration`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/CoeffSubalgebra/`.

### 4.2 Ideal-adic Rees algebras and the associated graded ring

For an ideal `I` of a commutative ring `R`, construct the associated graded ring
`gr_I R = ⊕_(n≥0) Iⁿ/Iⁿ⁺¹` on Mathlib's direct sums and quotients, with its graded multiplication
`[a]_r [b]_s = [a b]_(r+s)`, its unit, the homogeneous quotient-class maps, the degree-zero
identification with `R/I`, the generator map from `(R/I)[X_i]` for a finite generating family of
`I`, coefficient-change maps and functoriality in `(R, I)` with identity and composition. Construct
the extended Rees ring `R[I t, t⁻¹]` inside Mathlib's Laurent polynomials, prove that its
nonnegative graded part is Mathlib's `reesAlgebra I` and that its quotient by `t⁻¹` is `gr_I R`,
and prove these comparisons compatible with the generator and base-change maps. The increasing
`ℤ`-indexed `I`-adic filtration is `F_j = R` for `j ≥ 0` and `F_j = I^(−j)` for `j < 0`; its pieces
`F_j/F_(j−1)` identify the nonpositive indices `j` with the degrees `−j` of `gr_I R`. The ordinary
ideal Rees ring `reesAlgebra I` need not contain `t` as a scalar parameter; the extended Rees ring
contains `t⁻¹`. *Needs:* Mathlib `reesAlgebra`, `LaurentPolynomial`, `DirectSum`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/RingTheory/ReesAlgebra/Grading.lean`.

**Checks.**
- For `R = k[x]` and `I = (x)`, `gr_I R ≅ k[X]` with `X` the class of `x` in degree `1`; for
  `R = ℤ` and `I = (p)`, `gr_I R ≅ 𝔽_p[X]`. For `R = k[x]/(x²)` and `I = (x)`, `gr_I R ≅ k[X]/(X²)`.
- For `R = k[x]` and `I = (x)`, `R[I t, t⁻¹] = k[x t, t⁻¹]`, and its quotient by `t⁻¹` is the
  polynomial ring in the class of `x t`, which is `gr_I R`.

### 4.3 Homogenization and t-saturation

Define `weightHomogenization w f`, the polynomial of `k[t][x]` obtained from `f ∈ k[x]` by replacing
each term `c xᵉ` by `c t^(D − w(e)) xᵉ`, where `D` is the maximal weight of a term of `f`; its value
at `t = 1` is `f` and at `t = 0` is `weightInitialForm w f`. Define the ideal
`weightDegenerationIdeal w I`, generated by the homogenizations of **all** elements of `I`. Build
the homogenized polynomial presentation of the Rees algebra of `k[x]/I` for the weight filtration
and prove that it is `t`-saturated: `t g ∈ weightDegenerationIdeal w I` forces
`g ∈ weightDegenerationIdeal w I` (Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra*, §15.8; Bossinger, §2.2, where
the quotient is a free `k[t]`-module). Homogenizing only chosen generators can fail to be saturated,
and multiplying an equation by a power of `t` can create an unwanted component at `t = 0`. *Needs:*
§4.1; Layer 1 §1.9.

**Checks.**
- For `I = (x − y², y)` and `w = (1, 1)` (Layer 1 §1.9), the homogenized generators generate
  `(t x − y², y) = (t x, y)`, whose fibre at `t = 0` is `(y)`; `t · x` lies in this ideal and `x`
  does not. Its saturation is `(x, y) = weightDegenerationIdeal w I`, with fibre
  `(x, y) = weightInitialIdeal w I` at `t = 0`.

### 4.4 The flat weight-initial family of an ideal

Prove `weightDegeneration_flat_and_fibres`: for an ideal `I` of `k[x₁, …, xₙ]` (finitely generated,
since the variables are finite) and a weight `w`, the quotient of `k[t][x]` by
`weightDegenerationIdeal w I` is flat over `k[t]`, its fibre at `t = 0` is cut out by
`weightInitialIdeal w I`, and its fibre at `t = 1` by `I` (Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra*, §15.8,
Theorem 15.17; Bossinger, §2.2, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic `0`). Route: by
§4.3 the quotient is `t`-torsion free, it embeds in its localization at `t`, which is
`(k[x]/I)[t, t⁻¹]` after the substitution `x_i ↦ t^(w_i) x_i`, so it is torsion free over the
principal ideal domain `k[t]`, and torsion-free modules over a Dedekind domain are flat (Mathlib
`IsDedekindDomain.flat_iff_torsion_eq_bot`). Identify every fibre over `k` and every base change
along `k[t] → k[t]`. *Needs:* §4.3; Mathlib `IsDedekindDomain.flat_iff_torsion_eq_bot`,
`Polynomial.evalRingHom`.

**Checks.**
- For `I = (x² − y)` and `w = (1, 1)`, the family is `k[t][x, y]/(x² − t y)`: at `t = 1` it is
  `k[x, y]/(x² − y)`, at `t = 0` it is `k[x, y]/(x²)`, and the quotient is a free `k[t]`-module with
  basis the monomials `yᵇ` and `x yᵇ`.

### 4.5 The Rees family of a finite SAGBI basis

For a finite SAGBI basis, use Layer 3's nonzero monic family `c`, its binomial relations `β_j` and
strict certificates `Q_j` to construct the finite-type Rees family of the subalgebra, with fibre `A`
at `t = 1` and the initial algebra of `A` at `t = 0`. Choose a positive integral ambient weight `w`
from the following finitely many comparisons: every nonleading exponent `γ` of each `c_i` satisfies
`w(γ) < w(a_i)`, and every `v` in the support of `Q_j` satisfies `w(A(v)) < w(α_j)`. The equalities
`A(u_j) = A(v_j)` are kept exactly, not approximated by inequalities. Layer 1's finite weight
realization (§1.8) supplies `w`. The generator weights `δ_i = w(a_i)` are nonnegative; constant
generators have `δ_i = 0` and keep their relations of degree `0` to `1`. All comparisons are on
explicit finite supports, and an exact zero contributes no comparison.

Prove, again by the certificate induction of Layer 3 §3.5, that these data give
`in_w(A) = k[monomial a_i 1]`, and that the weight-initial ideal of the kernel of `φ_c` for the
weights `δ` is the kernel of `ψ₁`. A whole initial-ideal equality cannot be inferred from the
initial forms of chosen generators alone. Construct the Rees presentation `Y_i ↦ c_i t^(δ_i)`,
`t ↦ t`, with the lifted relations

    Y^(u_j) − Y^(v_j) − ∑_v q_(j,v) t^(w(α_j) − w(A(v))) Y^v,

where `Q_j = ∑_v q_(j,v) Y^v`. The correction exponents are strictly positive; at `t = 0` these
relations are the `β_j` and at `t = 1` they are the `F_j`. Prove that they generate the whole
kernel of the Rees presentation and that this kernel is `t`-saturated, from the bounded standard
representations of §3.5, and identify the algebra with the Rees algebra of the ambient increasing
weight filtration (§4.1); homogenized equations alone are not this identification. Transport the
presentation back through `D_(ℓ⁻¹)` (§3.6), keeping all scalar factors. These steps give the
finite-type family and its special-fibre maps without changing `A`. Prove flatness over `k[t]` from
torsion-freeness over a principal ideal domain, and identify every fibre and base change. For an
arbitrary subalgebra `A` and weight `w`, prove `weightReesFamily_specialFibre`: setting `t = 0` on
`weightReesFamily w A` has kernel generated by `t` and image the algebra generated by the
weight-initial forms of the elements of `A` (the SAGBI degeneration of Miller–Sturmfels, §14.3).
*Needs:* §§4.1, 4.4; Layer 1 §1.8; Layer 3 §§3.4–3.6.

### 4.6 Projective families: the standard-graded route

For homogeneous data, with a positive grading whose degree-zero part is `k`, construct the
projective flat family by relative Proj over `𝔸¹ = Spec k[t]` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.3, on
Tau Ceti's `CategoryTheory.CommMon.relativeProj`). The standard route applies when the whole family
has a fixed standard-graded presentation: a quotient of `k[t][Z_0, …, Z_N]` with each `Z_i` of
degree one, with presentation maps and fibre maps preserving this grading. In this route prove
that every graded-piece Hilbert function, the Hilbert polynomial, the projective dimension and the
degree of the fibres in the fixed `ℙᴺ_(k[t])` are constant. *Needs:* §§4.4–4.5;
SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.3; Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Proj`.

### 4.7 Nonstandard gradings and Veronese algebras

Nonstandard positive gradings are in scope. For a finitely generated graded Rees-family algebra `B`
over `k[t]`, construct a sufficiently divisible Veronese algebra `B^(r) = ⊕_(n≥0) B_(rn)` generated in
degree one (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.exists_grade_mul_eq_pow`), prove that the construction
and its fibre maps commute with base change, and give the canonical comparison `Proj B ≃ Proj B^(r)`
with `O_(Proj B^(r))(1) ≃ O_(Proj B)(r)` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.3, `relativeProj_veronese`).
Construct the fixed projective embedding from the degree-one generators of `B^(r)`, and prove the
standard-graded Hilbert-polynomial conclusions of §4.6 and the `K₀` conclusion of §4.11 there. This
is the projective-polynomial conclusion for a nonstandard grading: define the polynomial of the ample
Veronese polarization by `P_r(n) = χ(X, F ⊗ O_B(r)^(⊗n))`, and compare fibres in the new Veronese
degree `n`. A further Veronese refinement by `s ≥ 1` gives `B^(rs) = (B^(r))^(s)`; prove that it is
generated in degree one, by grouping degree-one generators into `s`-fold products, and construct the
comparison of the Proj schemes and the polarization `O_B(rs) ≃ O_(B^(r))(s) ≃ O_B(r)^(⊗s)` from the
degree-divisible localizations and their transition maps. Its Hilbert polynomial is
`P_(rs)(n) = P_r(s n)`, and its degree is `s^d` times the degree for `O_B(r)`, `d` the dimension.
This handles every chosen Veronese polarization without asserting an eventual polynomial in the
original component index. *Needs:* §4.6; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.3, 2.6; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Veronese.lean`.

**Checks.**
- For `B = k[s, u]` with `deg s = 1` and `deg u = 2`, `B^(2) = k[s², u]` is generated in degree one
  and `Proj B ≅ ℙ¹`. The component function `dim B_m = ⌊m/2⌋ + 1` is not eventually a polynomial,
  while `dim B^(2)_n = n + 1` is.

### 4.8 Polarizations and graded-piece ranks

A comparison with an ample invertible sheaf `L` is made only when a family-level isomorphism
`O_B(r) ≃ L^(⊗r)`, compatible with base change, is constructed. Define `P_L(m) = χ(X, F ⊗ L^(⊗m))`.
After that isomorphism and the comparison of sections in large degree, the Veronese polynomial is
`P_L(r n)`, and its degree in dimension `d` is `r^d deg_L(X)`. For a further refinement `s`, use the
tensor power of the same isomorphism to identify `O_B(rs) ≃ L^(⊗rs)` and obtain `P_L(rs n)` and
`(rs)^d deg_L(X)`. Ample invertibility of `O_B(1)` alone does not supply these identifications of
twists: the graded twists `O_B(m)` are not asserted to be powers of `O_B(1)` without constructing the
multiplication isomorphisms, and the component function of a nonstandard graded ring is not
identified with an eventual polynomial unless that is separately proved. For every original degree
`m`, keep the comparison of Hilbert functions at that graded piece: in a finite-type positively
graded flat family over `k[t]` whose degree-zero part is `k[t]`, each graded piece is a finite
`k[t]`-module and a direct summand of the flat total module, hence torsion free and so finite free;
its rank is constant in the fibres. These statements separate the preservation of all graded-piece
ranks from the existence of an eventual polynomial. *Needs:* §4.7; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.6.

**Checks.**
- For `B = k[s, u]` of §4.7, `O_B(1)` has the single section `s` and is trivial on `Proj B ≅ ℙ¹`,
  while `O_B(2)` has the sections `s², u` and is `O(1)`: so `O_B(2) ≇ O_B(1)^(⊗2)`.

### 4.9 Hilbert functions, Hilbert series and syzygies of graded modules

For every finitely generated `ℤ`-graded module `M` over a standard graded polynomial ring
`κ[x₀, …, x_N]` (bounded below, graded shifts allowed), define its Hilbert function `n ↦ dim_κ M_n`,
its formal Laurent Hilbert series and its eventual Hilbert polynomial. Prove the graded Hilbert
syzygy theorem: `M` has a finite graded free resolution
`0 → ⊕ S(−d)^(b_(N+1,d)) → ⋯ → ⊕ S(−d)^(b_(0,d)) → M → 0` of length at most `N + 1` (Eisenbud,
*Lectures on the Geometry of Syzygies*, Theorem 1.3). Prove rationality: the Hilbert series is
`(∑_d k_d T^d)/(1 − T)^(N+1)` with the alternating syzygy numerator `k_d = ∑_i (−1)^i b_(i,d)`
(Eisenbud, *Lectures on the Geometry of Syzygies*, Theorem 1.1 and its proof), and compare the
eventual polynomial with Mathlib's `Polynomial.hilbertPoly`. Compare with Layer 2's monoid-counting
functions. Here `κ` denotes the field `k`, in SchemeAndStackFoundations' fixed-field notation.
*Needs:* Layer 2 §2.6; Mathlib `Polynomial.hilbertPoly`,
`Polynomial.eq_hilbertPoly_of_forall_coeff_eq_eval`; Tau Ceti graded free resolutions
`TauCeti/Algebra/Category/GradedModuleCat/`.

**Checks.**
- For a conic `S/(q)` in `ℙ²`, the resolution is `0 → S(−2) → S`, the Hilbert series is
  `(1 − T²)/(1 − T)³ = (1 + T)/(1 − T)²` and the Hilbert polynomial is `2 n + 1`.

### 4.10 Graded sheafification and saturated presentations on projective space

Construct the graded-module sheafification API on `ℙᴺ_A` for any ring `A`: restriction and
localization charts, exactness of `M ↦ M~`, `S(−d)~ = O(−d)`, `(S/I)~ = i_* O_X` for the closed
subscheme `X` of a homogeneous ideal `I`, and, when `A` is Noetherian, the comparison of `(S/I)_m`
with `Γ(X, O_X(m))` for all sufficiently large `m`. Prove that every coherent sheaf on `ℙᴺ_κ` is
the sheafification of a finitely generated graded module (Stacks Project, Tag 0BXF), and sheafify
the graded free resolutions of §4.9 to locally free resolutions. For every closed subscheme `X`
of `ℙᴺ_A` over a Noetherian ring `A`, construct its saturated homogeneous ideal presentation, and
prove the comparison between closed subschemes and saturated presentations, its compatibility with
restriction, and base change of Proj.
For `X` flat over `A = κ[t]`, prove that the saturated graded quotient is torsion free over `κ[t]`,
using the projective affine charts and saturation, so that each graded piece is finite free;
identify the fibre schemes with Proj of the base-changed quotient, and deduce that the fibres have
the same Hilbert polynomial by the comparison of sections in large degree (Eisenbud, *Lectures on
the Geometry of Syzygies*, Theorem 1.5). The Hilbert functions in small degrees of the separately
saturated fibre presentations need not be constant. This supplies the general scheme-flat family
used in §4.11. *Needs:* §4.9; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.3, 2.6; Mathlib
`AlgebraicGeometry.Proj`.

**Checks.**
- For `X = V(x₀) ⊆ ℙ¹`, a point, `(S/(x₀))_m = κ` for every `m ≥ 0` and `Γ(X, O_X(m)) = κ`; the

### 4.11 Classes in K₀ of projective space

This roadmap owns the comparison on projective space. Consume `O(d)`, coherent cohomology, Serre
vanishing and proper pushforward on `G₀` from SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.3, 2.6 and 5.7, whose
`K₀` and `G₀` of a scheme are *Chow.K0* and *Chow.G0*. For `π : ℙᴺ_κ → Spec κ`, define the
Hilbert-polynomial map `[F] ↦ (m ↦ rank π_!([F] · [O(m)]))` and prove its additivity, polynomiality
and independence of representatives. Prove that `K₀(ℙᴺ_κ)` is free on the classes `[O(−i)]`,
`0 ≤ i ≤ N` (Weibel, *The K-book*, Theorem II.8.6), and that the Hilbert-polynomial map is
injective: for spanning, express the class of a coherent sheaf through the `O(d)` by the graded free
resolutions of §§4.9–4.10, prove that the exact Koszul complex of the coordinates gives
`(1 − [O(−1)])^(N+1) = 0`, and reduce all twists to the basis; for independence use the binomial
Euler polynomials `m ↦ C(m − i + N, N)` of the `O(m − i)`. For the standard-graded flat family of
§4.6 embedded in a fixed `ℙᴺ_(κ[t])`, prove `[i_(0*) O_(X₀)] = [i_(1*) O_(X₁)]` in *Chow.K0* of
`ℙᴺ_κ`: form the coherent pushforward classes in *Chow.G0*, transport them through
SchemeAndStackFoundations' *Chow.K0.toG0_bijective* for the regular ambient projective space
(Weibel, *The K-book*, Theorem II.8.2), identify the polynomial of each fibre class with its
preserved Hilbert polynomial, and conclude by injectivity. For a nonstandard family, use the fixed
projective embedding from the chosen Veronese algebra (§4.7) and state the equality in the `K₀` of
that embedding's ambient `ℙᴹ_κ`. For the standard-graded flat initial families, including those
regraded by a Veronese algebra, also prove the direct numerator formula
`[i_* O_X] = ∑_d k_d [O(−d)]` with `∑_d k_d T^d = HS_(S/I)(T) (1 − T)^(N+1)`; their preserved full
Hilbert function makes the numerators equal. The two classes lie in the one ambient group; no
identification of the intrinsic `K₀` groups or cohomology rings of the fibres is asserted. *Needs:*
§§4.6–4.10; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.3, 2.6, 5.7.

**Checks.**
- On `ℙ¹`, the Koszul complex `0 → O(−2) → O(−1)² → O → 0` gives

  `1`.
- The conic `x z − y²` and its weight degeneration `x z` (two lines) in `ℙ²` both have numerator
  `1 − T²`, so both classes are `[O] − [O(−2)]`, with Hilbert polynomial `2 m + 1`.

### 5.1 Generic minors and their diagonal leading terms

### 5.2 Maximal minors: Plücker relations and straightening

For `0 < d ≤ n`, let the Plücker variables `P_I` be indexed by `PluckerIndex n d`, the strictly
increasing `d`-tuples of `Fin n` ordered by the dual of the lexicographic order, with tuple
`pluckerTuple I`, and let `pluckerMap k n d` send `P_I` to the maximal minor of the generic `n × d`
matrix on the rows `I`. Give the polynomial presentation of the algebra of maximal minors by the
Plücker relations: the kernel of `pluckerMap` is a homogeneous prime ideal, generated by the quadratic
straightening relations, which are polynomial identities over `ℤ`. With `TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex`
on the Plücker index, so that `P_I < P_J` when `I` precedes `J` lexicographically, prove that the
straightening relations form a Gröbner basis and `span_leadingTerm_ker_pluckerMap`: the ideal of
leading terms is generated by the products `P_I P_J` of the pairs that are incomparable in the
componentwise order (Miller–Sturmfels, Theorem 14.6, restricted to subsets of one size; Borovik–Hibi,
§1, on the straightening order with variables increasing along the poset). Prove spanning of the
standard monomials, the products of chains `I₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ I_r`, by a well-founded straightening order, and
their independence by their distinct diagonal leading monomials (Miller–Sturmfels, Corollary 14.9).
Index standard monomials by Tau Ceti's bounded semistandard tableaux `TauCeti.BoundedSSYT`, with weak
rows and strict columns, rather than by a second tableau type. This is the theorem about the
polynomial algebra; identifications with schemes and sections are consumers'. *Needs:* §5.1; Layer 1
§§1.4–1.6; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MonomialOrder.degRevLex`, `TauCeti.BoundedSSYT`.

**Checks.**
- For `n = 4`, `d = 2`, the only incomparable pair is `{0, 3}`, `{1, 2}`, and the Plücker relation
  `P₀₁P₂₃ − P₀₂P₁₃ + P₀₃P₁₂` has `degRevLex` leading term `P₀₃P₁₂`: the monomial `P₀₁P₂₃` contains
  the smallest variable `P₀₁`, and `P₀₂P₁₃` the next one. For `lex`, in either orientation of the
  variables, the leading term is `P₀₁P₂₃`, a comparable pair, and the incomparable-pair description
  fails.
- For `n = 4`, `d = 2`, the degree-two piece has dimension `21 − 1 = 20`, the number of
  semistandard tableaux of shape `(2, 2)` with entries in `{0, 1, 2, 3}`.

### 5.3 Flag minors and the Gelfand–Tsetlin initial monoid

For an `n × n` generic matrix take the flag minors `flagMinorFamily k n`: for each nonempty row set
`I = {i₀ < ⋯ < i_(d−1)}`, the minor on the rows `I` and the first `d = #I` columns; there are
`2ⁿ − 1` of them, including the determinant, and the diagonal exponent of `I` is that of
`∏_(c<d) z_(i_c, c)`. Prove `flagMinorFamily_isSAGBIBasis`: the flag minors form a SAGBI basis for
the diagonal order (Miller–Sturmfels, Theorem 14.11; Kogan–Miller, Theorem 5, over `ℂ`), and their
standard monomials, indexed by semistandard tableaux, form a basis of the flag-minor algebra, with
its fundamental multigrading in which a minor of size `d` has degree the `d`-th fundamental weight.
Identify the initial exponent monoid with the nonnegative part of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.GTPattern n`:
construct the pointwise `AddCommMonoid` API on patterns, its nonnegative `AddSubmonoid`, and
`TauCeti.GTPattern.shift m` as addition of the constant pattern `m`. The map from a diagonal
exponent `e` to a pattern is `patternEntry e`, whose entry in column `c` and row `j` is
`∑_(r<j) e(r, c)` for `j ≤ n` and `0` for `n < j`. Prove `exists_leadingExponents_equiv_gtPattern`:
this map is a bijection from the leading exponents of the flag-minor algebra onto the patterns with
nonnegative entries, with inverse by successive differences `e(r, c) = P(c, r + 1) − P(c, r)`, and
it is additive (Miller–Sturmfels, Lemma 14.13, Proposition 14.22, Theorem 14.23 and Corollary 14.24,
transposed). Prove its interlacing and nonnegativity, the finite generation and saturation of the
monoid, the comparisons of patterns with tableaux and contents through `TauCeti.gtPatternEquivSSYT`,
and the Rees degeneration of Layer 4 §4.5 (Kogan–Miller, Theorem 5). The **initial** algebra is the
semigroup algebra of patterns; the flag algebra itself is not toric. *Needs:* §5.1; Layer 3
§§3.4–3.5; Layer 4 §4.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GTPattern`, `TauCeti.gtPatternEquivSSYT`,
`TauCeti.BoundedSSYT`.

**Checks.**
- The determinant of the `n × n` matrix has diagonal exponent `∑_c e_(c,c)`, and its pattern has
  entry `1` in every informative cell (`c < j ≤ n`): multiplication by the determinant is
  `GTPattern.shift 1`. The strict inequality `r < j` matters: summing over `r ≤ j` would give the
  `2 × 2` determinant the entry `1` in the cell `(1, 1)`, outside the triangle of a pattern.
- The flag minor on the row set `{1}` of a `2 × 2` matrix is `z_(1,0)`, with pattern entries
  `P(0, 1) = 0`, `P(0, 2) = 1` and `P(1, 2) = 0`, which are nonnegative and interlacing.

### 5.4 Inverting the determinant

Prove that inverting the full determinant corresponds to all integer patterns: in the localization
of the flag-minor algebra at the determinant, multiplication by the determinant acts on leading
exponents as `TauCeti.GTPattern.shift 1`, and the localization allows `TauCeti.GTPattern.shift c`
for every integer `c`, so its leading exponents are all of `TauCeti.GTPattern n`. Compare this
additive localization with Tau Ceti's shift and tableau API (`TauCeti.GTPattern.detShiftEquivBoundedSSYT`)
and with the rational-weight branching of ClassicalGroups Layer 6 (§6.1). *Needs:* §5.3; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/ClassicalGroups/GelfandTsetlin/Shift.lean`.

**Checks.**
- The top row `(1, 0, −1)` occurs after inverting the determinant and not before: it is the shift by
  `−1` of the top row `(2, 1, 0)`.

### 5.5 SL₂-invariants of n × 2 matrices

For generic `n × 2` matrices over a field `k`, with the right action `Z ↦ Z g`, prove
`equalizer_sl2Coaction_eq_adjoin_minors`: the `2 × 2` minors generate the invariant algebra of the
group scheme `SL₂`, the polynomials `f` whose coaction is `f ⊗ 1` (Hashimoto, Theorem 17, over every
commutative ring and for `n ≥ 4`, for the symplectic group of a plane, which is `SL₂`, whose
pairings are the `2 × 2` minors). For `n ≤ 3` the coaction preserves the degree in each row, so an
invariant of `n` rows is an invariant of `4` rows of degree `0` in the added rows, hence a
polynomial in the minors on the first `n` rows. The coaction is that of Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.SpecialLinear.coordinateHopfAlgebra k 2` on `MvPolynomial (Fin n × Fin 2) k`, with its
native base-change isomorphisms. Use `TauCeti.Comodule`, `TauCeti.SpecialLinear.standardComodule`
and `TauCeti.Comodule.fixedSubcomodule`. Fix the right coaction `sl2Coaction k n` by
`z_(i,r) ↦ ∑_s z_(i,s) ⊗ x_(s,r)`, where `x` is the image of
`TauCeti.GeneralLinear.genericMatrix k 2` under `TauCeti.SpecialLinear.coordinateMap k 2`, each row
being a copy of the standard comodule. Build the multiplicative map by `MvPolynomial.aeval`, prove
coassociativity and the counit law on generators, and give its fixed submodule the resulting
subalgebra structure. Prove the invariant theorem by straightening (§5.2), in every characteristic.
Compare with the invariants of the `k`-points over an infinite field `k`; invariants of the points
over a finite field need a separate comparison and are not substituted for group-scheme invariants.
*Needs:* §5.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti/Algebra/AlgebraicGroup/SpecialLinear/`,
`TauCeti/Algebra/Coalgebra/Comodule/`.

**Checks.**
- For `n = 1` the invariants are the constants, and there is no minor; for `n = 2` they are
  `k[det Z]`. Over `𝔽₂` and for `n = 1`, the polynomial `z_(0,0)² + z_(0,0) z_(0,1) + z_(0,1)²` is
  fixed by the six `𝔽₂`-points of `SL₂` acting on the row, but it is not a coaction invariant:
  invariance under the points of a finite field is weaker.

### 5.6 Permutation invariants

For a group `G` of permutations of the variables, construct the invariant subalgebra
`permInvariants k G` and its basis of orbit sums of monomials. Prove
`exists_finite_isSAGBIBasis_permInvariants_iff`: for every monomial order, the invariant algebra has
a finite SAGBI basis if and only if `G` is generated by the transpositions it contains, equivalently
`G` is the product of the full symmetric groups of its orbits (Göbel's conjecture: Reichstein,
Theorem 1.7; Thiéry–Thomassé, Theorem 1.2). The elementary symmetric polynomials of each orbit
(Mathlib `MvPolynomial.esymm`) supply the positive case; prove the other case through the monoid of
leading exponents of invariants. The statement concerns the given permutation coordinates, not every
linear change of variables. For the alternating group, in characteristic different from `2` and for
`n ≥ 2`, give the presentation of the invariant algebra by `k[e₁, …, eₙ, Δ]` with
`Δ = ∏_(i<j) (x_i − x_j)` and the relation `Δ² = disc(e₁, …, eₙ)`, the discriminant written in the
elementary symmetric polynomials (Mathlib `Matrix.det_vandermonde`, Tau Ceti `TauCeti.alternant`).
For `n ≥ 3` deduce from the permutation criterion that the invariants of the alternating group have
no finite SAGBI basis, for every order and in every characteristic. Treat `n = 1` separately: the
alternating group is trivial and its invariant algebra is `k[x]`. *Needs:* Layer 3 §§3.1, 3.3;
Mathlib `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra`, `MvPolynomial.esymmAlgEquiv`.

**Checks.**
- In three variables with lex `x > y > z` and characteristic different from `2`, with
  `e₁ = x + y + z` and `e₂ = x y + x z + y z`, the invariant `(e₁ e₂ − Δ)/2` of the alternating group
  equals `x² z + x y² + y z² + (3/2) x y z`, with leading monomial `x² z`. Its exponent `(2, 0, 1)` is
  outside the monoid generated by the leading exponents `(1, 0, 0)`, `(1, 1, 0)`, `(1, 1, 1)` and
  `(2, 1, 0)` of `e₁`, `e₂`, `e₃` and `Δ`, in which every element has `y`-exponent at least its
  `z`-exponent; so `{e₁, e₂, e₃, Δ}` is not a SAGBI basis.
- For `G` the subgroup of `S₃` generated by the transposition of `x₁` and `x₂`, the set
  `{x₁ + x₂, x₁ x₂, x₃}` is a finite SAGBI basis of the invariants.

### 5.7 Two linear actions beyond the permutation criterion

For actions of linear algebraic subgroups over `ℂ`, give two explicit boundaries to a
characterization of finite SAGBI bases by connected reductive groups. The scalar subgroup
`μ₂ ≤ GL₁` acts by `x ↦ −x`; prove that its invariant algebra is `ℂ[x²]`, with singleton SAGBI basis
`{x²}` for every monomial order, although `μ₂` is disconnected. For the lower unipotent subgroup
`𝔾_a ≤ GL₂`, use the contragredient coordinate coaction `x ↦ x`, `y ↦ y − t x`; prove that its
invariant algebra is `ℂ[x]`, by comparing the highest `t`-degree of `f(x, y − t x)` for a polynomial `f`
of positive `y`-degree, with singleton SAGBI basis `{x}` for every monomial order, although this
connected group is unipotent and not reductive. Use the algebraic coaction invariants, not finitely
many pointwise equations. Thus a finite SAGBI basis for a linear action implies neither connectedness
nor reductivity, and the permutation criterion of §5.6 keeps its exact scope. *Needs:* Layer 3 §3.1.

### 6.1 The cuspidal semigroup x²y = z³

Compute the affine semigroup with exponents `(3, 0), (0, 3), (2, 1)`: its ring is
`k[x, y, z]/(x² y − z³)`. Its generated lattice is the set of points with coordinate sum divisible by
`3`; its normalization adds `(1, 2)`, not all of `ℕ²`, and is the cubic Veronese monoid algebra.
Exhibit the integral element `z²/x`, of exponent `(1, 2)`, and its relations `z · (z²/x) = x y` and
`(z²/x)³ = x y²`. Compute its four affine face orbits, their closure order and their stabilizers. In
the grading `(a, b) ↦ (a + b)/3`, identify `Proj k[S]` with the plane cubic `x² y = z³` and its
normalization with `ℙ¹`, through the cubic Veronese map; compute the two projective boundary orbits and
the dense torus. *Needs:* Layer 2 §§2.1–2.7.

**Checks.**
- The face along `(3, 0)` has `gp F = 3ℤ × 0`, and `L_S / gp F ≅ ℤ` by `(a, b) ↦ b`: its stabilizer is
  connected, unlike the Whitney umbrella of §6.3.

### 6.2 The cubic scroll and the Hirzebruch surface F₁

For the five exponents `a = (2, 0, 0)`, `b = (1, 1, 0)`, `c = (1, 0, 1)`, `d = (0, 2, 0)`,
`e = (0, 1, 1)`, prove the presentation by `a d − b²`, `a e − b c`, `b e − c d`, normality and the
four extremal rays `a`, `c`, `d`, `e` (`b = (a + d)/2`). Compute the ten affine orbits, with the four
one-dimensional and four two-dimensional support patterns, and the nine projective orbits. Construct
the projective cubic scroll and identify it with the blowup `Bl_p ℙ²` of a point, embedded by
`|2H − E|`, with Tau Ceti's blowup `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.blowup`, its universal
property `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.blowupLift` and its affine charts. Define `F₁` as
SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.3's relative Proj over `ℙ¹` of Tau Ceti's graded symmetric algebra
`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.gradedSymmetricAlgebra (O ⊕ O(1))`, with the affine graded-ring charts
`CategoryTheory.CommMon.isLocalization_gradedSections_basicOpen` and
`CategoryTheory.CommMon.isBaseChange_restrictGradedSections` as its relative-Proj input; reach the
blowup through §0.3's relative-Proj comparison. In the lines convention this `F₁` is
SchemeAndStackFoundations §5.4's projective bundle of `O ⊕ O(−1)`, whose relative Proj is that of the
symmetric algebra of the dual. Consume its tautological `O_P(1)`, identify `F₁` with the blowup, and
compare the cubic polarization `O_P(1) ⊗ π^* O(1)` with `2H − E`. Prove
`π_* O_P(m) = Sym^m (O ⊕ O(1))` for `m ≥ 0` and the twist rule
`π_*(O_P(m) ⊗ π^* L) = Sym^m (O ⊕ O(1)) ⊗ L` for a line bundle `L`, so the cubic polarization has five
independent sections, using SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.6's computation of `H⁰(ℙ¹, O(d))`. Verify
the degree `3` in the generated difference lattice. *Needs:* Layer 2 §§2.1–2.7;
SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.3, 2.6, 5.4; Tau Ceti blowups and graded quasi-coherent algebras.

**Checks.**
- `a d = b² = (2, 2, 0)`, `a e = b c = (2, 1, 1)`, `b e = c d = (1, 2, 1)`.
- In the coordinates `(y, z)` of the plane `x + y + z = 2`, the polygon has vertices `(0, 0)`,
  `(2, 0)`, `(1, 1)`, `(0, 1)` and area `3/2` in the difference lattice, of normalized volume
  `2! · 3/2 = 3`, the degree; and `(2H − E)² = 4 − 1 = 3`.
- `π_*(O_P(1) ⊗ π^* O(1)) = O(1) ⊕ O(2)` has `2 + 3 = 5` sections, and `h⁰(2H − E) = 6 − 1 = 5`.

### 6.3 The Whitney umbrella

Compute the nonnormal semigroup `S = ⟨(1, 0), (1, 1), (0, 2)⟩`, with ring `k[u, v, w]/(v² − u² w)`,
generated group `ℤ²` and normalization `k[x, y]`. On the face along the `y`-axis, `gp F = 0 × 2ℤ`;
the stabilizer character group is `ℤ ⊕ ℤ/2`, so the stabilizer scheme is `𝔾_m × μ₂`. The
normalization restricts on that orbit to `y ↦ y²`: two-to-one on geometric points outside
characteristic `2`, and purely inseparable and bijective on points in characteristic `2`, where the
stabilizer `μ₂` is nonreduced. *Needs:* Layer 2 §§2.3, 2.5.

**Checks.**
- `v² = (2, 2) = u² w`, and `(0, 1) = (1, 1) − (1, 0)` lies in `L_S`, so `L_S = ℤ²`; `(0, 1)` has the
  multiple `(0, 2) ∈ S`, so it lies in the saturation but not in `S`.

### 6.4 Trivalent trees and path semigroups

For every finite trivalent tree with `n ≥ 3` labelled leaves, define the exponent of a leaf pair as
the indicator of the edges on the path between them, in the lattice of edges, with a combinatorial API
for the existence and uniqueness of paths. Prove the description of the generated lattice by parity
conditions, its full rank `2n − 3`, the nonnegative triangle inequalities at the internal vertices
describing the cone, and normality, by decomposing integral points into paths. Build the quartet
binomial ideal, one binomial for each four-leaf subtree, and prove that it is the whole presentation
ideal of the path semigroup (Speyer–Sturmfels, proof of Corollary 4.4). Normality also follows from
the squarefree initial ideal of that ideal found there (Sturmfels, *Equations defining toric
varieties*, Theorem 2.11). *Needs:* Layer 2 §§2.1–2.3.

### 6.5 Tree degenerations of the Plücker algebra of 2-planes

Let `P_ij`, `i < j`, be the variables of `k[P_ij : i < j]`, mapped to the sorted-row minor of the
generic `n × 2` matrix with diagonal leading term `z_(i,0) z_(j,1)`; equivalently, to the column-pair
minor of the transposed `2 × n` matrix. For a four-leaf split `12|34`, the maximal-weight initial form
of the Plücker relation is `P₁₃P₂₄ − P₁₄P₂₃`. Choose positive rational internal-edge lengths, scaled
to integers, and define `w(P_ij)` as the total internal-edge length of the path from `i` to `j`.
Lengths of leaf edges change the weights by a leaf grading and do not change the Plücker initial
ideal. Fix the labels by a planar embedding of the tree and its linearized circular leaf order `ρ`:
for `i < j` put `ε_ij = +1` if `ρ(i) < ρ(j)` and `−1` otherwise. Construct the involution of the
`k`-algebra `k[P_ij : i < j]` with `P_ij ↦ ε_ij P_ij`, and prove that for the path-length weights it
carries the toric ideal of §6.4 to the maximal-weight Plücker initial ideal (Speyer–Sturmfels, proof
of Theorem 3.4, identity (10)); the pure-difference
ideal itself appears when the circular order is `1, …, n`. Prove the whole equality of initial
ideals, not only the initial forms of the relations, and compare Hilbert bases and counts with
Layer 5. Construct the flat affine and projective families with this fibre (Layer 4 §§4.4, 4.6) and
their maps under scalars and relabelling. Compare the initial monoid of diagonal minors of Layer 5
with the path monoid of the comb tree, through the explicit map between path and diagonal exponents
and its additive inverse. *Needs:* §6.4; Layer 4 §§4.4, 4.6; Layer 5 §§5.1–5.2.

**Checks.**
- For the split `12|34` with unit internal-edge length, the parameter `t` of Layer 4 and the
  saturated relation `P₁₃P₂₄ − P₁₄P₂₃ − t² P₁₂P₃₄`: at `t = 1` it is minus the sorted-minor Plücker
  relation `P₁₂P₃₄ − P₁₃P₂₄ + P₁₄P₂₃`, and at `t = 0` it is the toric relation.
- For the split `13|24`, the Plücker initial form is `P₁₂P₃₄ + P₁₄P₂₃`, while the unsigned toric
  binomial is `P₁₂P₃₄ − P₁₄P₂₃`. A planar circular order compatible with the tree is `1, 3, 2, 4`, for
  which only `ε₂₃ = −1`, and the sign change `P₂₃ ↦ −P₂₃` reconciles the two in every
  characteristic.

### 6.6 Dimension and degree of the Plücker algebra of 2-planes

Prove `exists_hilbertPolynomial_pluckerMap_two`: for a field `k` and `n ≥ 2`, the dimension of the
degree-`m` piece of the image of `pluckerMap k n 2`, the Plücker algebra of `2`-planes in `kⁿ`, is
for all large `m` the value at `m` of a polynomial of degree `2n − 4` with leading coefficient
`catalan (n − 2)/(2n − 4)!` (Mathlib `catalan`). So the projective dimension is `2n − 4` and the
degree is `catalan (n − 2)` (Speyer–Sturmfels, proof of Theorem 3.4). Compute the Hilbert function
through the tree degeneration of §6.5 and Layer 2 §2.7. The case `n = 2`, where the Grassmannian is
a point, is included, with the polynomial `1`. *Needs:* §6.5; Layer 2 §2.7; Layer 4 §4.6; Layer 5
§5.2.

**Checks.**
- For `n = 4` the degree is `catalan 2 = 2` (a quadric in `ℙ⁵`), and the Hilbert function in degree
  `2` is `20` (§5.2); for `n = 5` the degree is `catalan 3 = 5`; for `n = 2` it is `catalan 0 = 1`.

### 6.7 Flag minors of size 2 and 3

For the `2 × 2` flag matrix, compute the flag-minor algebra `k[z₀₀, z₁₀, det Z]` and its free monoid
of diagonal exponents. For size `3`, name `a = z₀₀`, `b = z₁₀`, `c = z₂₀`, `d`, `e`, `f` the
`2 × 2` minors on the sorted row sets `{0, 1}`, `{0, 2}`, `{1, 2}` and the first two columns, and
`g = det Z`. Prove the presentation `k[a, b, c, d, e, f, g]/(a f − b e + c d)`, the initial
presentation with relation `a f − b e`, and the fundamental multigraded Hilbert series
`(1 − u v)/((1 − u)³ (1 − v)³ (1 − w))`. Compare the count `8` for the fixed top row `(2, 1, 0)` with
ClassicalGroups Layer 6's branching example. Identifications with Grassmannian and flag schemes are
outside this polynomial scope. *Needs:* Layer 5 §5.3; ClassicalGroups Layer 6.

**Checks.**
- Expanding, `a f − b e + c d = 0` is the Laplace expansion of a `3 × 3` determinant with a repeated
  column; the diagonals of `a f` and `b e` are both `z₀₀ z₁₀ z₂₁`, while that of `c d` is
  `z₀₀ z₁₁ z₂₀`, which is smaller.
- The coefficient of `u v` in the Hilbert series is `3 · 3 − 1 = 8`, the number of semistandard
  tableaux of shape `(2, 1)` with entries in `{0, 1, 2}` and the dimension of the Weyl module of
  `GL₃` of highest weight `(2, 1, 0)` in every characteristic; the simple module of that highest
  weight has dimension `7` in characteristic `3`.
