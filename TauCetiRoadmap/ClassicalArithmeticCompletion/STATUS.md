### Named results

- **The law of quartic reciprocity** — for primary π₁, π₂ in ℤ[i], (π₂/π₁)₄ = (π₁/π₂)₄ · (−1)^{((Nπ₁−1)/4)((Nπ₂−1)/4)}, with no primality or coprimality hypothesis ([`IsPrimaryGaussian.quartic_reciprocity`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/NumberField/Cyclotomic/Four/QuarticReciprocity/Primary.html#TauCeti.IsPrimaryGaussian.quartic_reciprocity)).
- **The Eisenstein reciprocity law** — for an odd prime p, a primary α in ℤ[ζ_p] and a rational integer a prime to p, (α/a)_p = (a/α)_p (`IsPrimitiveRoot.eisenstein_reciprocity`).
- **Christol's theorem** — over a finite field with q elements, a power series is algebraic over K[X] exactly when its coefficients are q-automatic ([`PowerSeries.isAutomatic_coeff_of_isAlgebraic`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Automatic.html#PowerSeries.isAutomatic_coeff_of_isAlgebraic), [`PowerSeries.isAlgebraic_of_isAutomatic_coeff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Christol.html#PowerSeries.isAlgebraic_of_isAutomatic_coeff)).
- **Cobham's theorem** — for multiplicatively independent k, l ≥ 2, a sequence that is both k-automatic and l-automatic is eventually periodic ([`isAutomatic_and_isAutomatic_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/AutomaticSequence/Cobham.html#TauCeti.isAutomatic_and_isAutomatic_iff)).
- **The Markoff tree** — the positive solutions of x² + y² + z² = 3xyz, joined by Vieta moves, form a tree in which every vertex has degree three, rooted at (1, 1, 1) ([`markoffGraph_isTree`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/Markoff/Tree.html#TauCeti.markoffGraph_isTree)). The coordinates of each triple are also [pairwise coprime](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/Markoff/Coprime.html#TauCeti.pairwise_isCoprime_of_mem_positiveMarkoffTriples).

## The frontier

- **The general power reciprocity law.** The local degree-n norm-residue symbol exists. The global degree-n Hilbert symbol and Hilbert's product formula are the missing inputs.
- **Symmetric numerical semigroups.** With Apéry sets in place, the Apéry-set criterion for symmetry, symmetry as least genus, and the symmetry of two-generator semigroups are next.
- **Explicit class groups.** Norms as Hermite pivots, followed by the class groups of ℚ(√−14) and ℚ(√−30), build on Minkowski's bound and relation lattices.
- **Locally free classes through ideles.** The next steps are rank-one lattices classified by idele double cosets and idele representatives for all of Cl(Λ). So far the second is known only when Λ is the base Dedekind ring. Fröhlich–Taylor is still far off.
- **Mahler measure and small heights.** Layer 6 has nothing beyond Pisot numbers. Salem numbers and Mahler measure come before Smyth's and Dimitrov's theorems.
