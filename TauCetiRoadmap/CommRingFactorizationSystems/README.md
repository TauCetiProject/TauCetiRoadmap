# Roadmap: generated factorization systems on commutative rings

## Overview

Several classical closure operations on a commutative ring are reflections: the localization of a
ring map at the elements it inverts, the integral closure of a ring in an extension, the largest
absolutely flat quotient of a ring, and the seminormalization and absolute weak normalization of a
ring. Each of them is the factorization of a single map in an **orthogonal factorization system**
on the category of commutative rings, whose left class is generated, under pushouts, coproducts and
transfinite composition, by finitely many explicit maps between finitely presented rings. This
roadmap builds those systems, identifies both classes of each of them by intrinsic ring theory,
identifies the rings the reflections land in, and separates the systems from one another by
examples.

The five systems, with their generators and the two intrinsic descriptions:

| system | generators | left class | right class | local objects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| localization | `ℤ[t] ⟶ ℤ[t, t⁻¹]` | localization maps | local homomorphisms | the zero ring |
| Zariski | that map and `ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ` | Zariski maps (ind-Zariski localizations) | local homomorphisms bijective on idempotents | the zero ring |
| integral closure | `ℤ[x] ⟶ ℤ, x ↦ 0` and the universal monic roots | integral maps | integrally closed extensions | the zero ring |
| absolutely flat | `ℤ[x] ⟶ ℤ × ℤ[x, x⁻¹], x ↦ (0, x)` | its cellular class, epimorphisms | maps reflecting regular decompositions | von Neumann regular rings |
| absolute weak normalization | the cusp `ℤ[x, y]/(x³ − y²) ⟶ ℤ[t]` and, for each prime `p`, `ℤ[x, y]/(pᵖx − yᵖ) ⟶ ℤ[t]` | universal homeomorphisms | maps reflecting cusp and `p`-th power data | absolutely weakly normal rings |
| seminormalization | the cusp alone | universal homeomorphisms with residue isomorphisms | maps reflecting cusp data | seminormal rings |

together with a sixth, the **monic system** generated by the unit generator and the universal
étale monic roots, whose right class contains the Henselian pairs of Mathlib's `HenselianRing` and
which is the boundary of this area.

Two theorems carry the roadmap. The **absolute weak normalization theorem** (Stacks
[0CNE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNE)) says that the left class generated by the cusp
and the `p`-th power generators is exactly the class of universal homeomorphisms, and its
seminormal companion ([0CND](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CND)) says that the cusp alone
generates the universal homeomorphisms inducing isomorphisms on residue fields. Both are proved by
an elementary-step argument: a universal homeomorphism that is not an isomorphism contains an
element `b` outside the image with `b²` and `b³` inside, or with `bᵖ` and `p·b` inside for a prime
`p`, and each such element is a pushout of a generator. The **Zariski theorem** says that the left
class generated by the unit and idempotent generators is the class of filtered colimits of
Bhatt–Scholze Zariski localizations `A ⟶ ∏_{j<n} A[1/f_j]`, and that a finitely presentable map in
it is such a localization on the nose.

The formal input is the `OrthogonalFactorization` roadmap
([`TauCetiRoadmap/OrthogonalFactorization/README.md`](../OrthogonalFactorization/README.md),
introduced in TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap#341): unique lifting, the orthogonal operators
`leftOrthogonal` and `rightOrthogonal`, orthogonal factorization systems, the codiagonal generators
and the small object argument for them, the cellular description of a generated left class, the
filtered-colimit stability of a right class with presentable generators, and local objects. That
roadmap is stated for an arbitrary category and names no ring; this one is its first instance, and
every statement below that is "formal" is formal *because* it is a specialization of a target there.

## What is missing (build here)

Everything that names a ring. The ring-level input to the generic machinery: local finite
presentability of `CommRingCat` and of `Under R`, the tensor-square form of the codiagonal, the
epimorphism test, and the packaged "small family of maps between finitely presented rings generates

(Milestones B–G). The Zariski theorem and the absolute weak normalization theorem (C4, F6). The
relationship of the Zariski and absolutely flat systems through Bhatt–Scholze's `A^Z` (C5). And an
example suite that gives every notion introduced a ground witness and a refutation and resolves
every inclusion between classes into a strict inclusion or an equality (Milestone H).

## Scope boundary

**In scope.** Orthogonal factorization systems on `CommRingCat` whose generators are maps between
finitely presented rings and whose two classes have elementary intrinsic descriptions: the
localization, Zariski, integral, absolutely flat, weak normalization and seminormalization systems,
and the monic system as far as its right class is described by Mathlib's `HenselianRing`. The rings
that are their local objects (von Neumann regular, seminormal, absolutely weakly normal, w-local)
and the reflections onto them. The example suite that separates all of these.

**Out of scope, and grounded elsewhere.** The generic theory of unique lifting is the
`OrthogonalFactorization` roadmap's and is only consumed. The system generated by *all* étale maps
between finitely presented rings, whose right class is the class of Henselian ring maps and whose
left class is the ind-étale maps; henselization, strict henselization, and the finite étale theory
of Henselian pairs; the pro-étale topology, weakly étale maps and w-contractible rings of
Bhatt–Scholze; and any statement about schemes or sites. The monic system stops exactly where these
begin: its right class is *compared* with the Henselian pairs of `HenselianRing` (G2), and nothing
is said about the étale-generated system.

The line is: a generator family in this roadmap is finite or indexed by the primes, its members are
written down as explicit ring maps, and both classes it generates are described by conditions on
elements, ideals and residue fields of the rings involved.

## Conventions, pinned up front

These are decisions, not recommendations.

**Naming.** Each system has a left class and a right class named `<system>Left` and
`<system>Right` when they are *defined* by orthogonality (`zariskiLeft`, `zariskiRight`,
`absFlatLeft`, `absFlatRight`, `awnLeft`, `awnRight`, `snLeft`, `snRight`, `monicLeft`,
`monicRight`), and intrinsic names when they are defined by ring theory (`localizationMap`,
`localHom`, `integral`, `integrallyClosed`, `regularHom`, `univHomeo`, `residueIso`, `awnHom`,
`snHom`). The theorem that a defined-by-orthogonality class equals an intrinsic one is
`<system>Right_eq_<intrinsic>` or `<system>Left_eq_<intrinsic>`, and it is the statement a
consumer applies. A generator family is `<system>Generators`, a single generator
`<name>Generator`. The absolutely flat system is named for its reflection (Olivier's *anneau
absolument plat universel*), not for the ring class; the ring class is `IsVonNeumannRegularRing`,
matching Mathlib's `Is…Ring` spelling for ring classes. Should Mathlib acquire a von Neumann
regular predicate, its name is adopted and this one deleted.

**Universes.** An explicit generator family lives in `CommRingCat.{0}`: its sources and targets are
quotients of `MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℤ` and localizations thereof, so they are in `Type`. Everything
*defined from a generator family* (`zariskiRight`, `absFlatLeft`, `awnRing A`, …) is therefore in
`CommRingCat.{0}` too. A class defined by ring theory (`localHom`, `integral`, `regularHom`,
`univHomeo`, …) is universe-polymorphic, `CommRingCat.{u}`, and every closure property of such a
class that does not go through a generator family is stated in `{u}`. An identification of the two
kinds (`absFlatRight = regularHom.{0}`) is stated at universe `0`, and the docstring says so. This
is the only honest arrangement: comparing `zariskiRight` with `localHom.{u}` for `u ≠ 0` would need
`ULift` on every generator, and nothing downstream wants that.

**Ring-homomorphism predicates first.** Whenever Mathlib has a predicate on `RingHom`, the
morphism property is `RingHom.toMorphismProperty` of it (`integral`, `surjective`), never a
re-definition, so that `RingHom.isStableUnderCobaseChange_toMorphismProperty_iff` and its
companions translate Mathlib's stability results. Where Mathlib has an unbundled class instead
(`IsLocalHom`, `IsLocalization`, `IsIntegrallyClosedIn`), the morphism property is defined by that
class with the algebra structure `f.hom.toAlgebra`, and the `_iff` lemma unfolding it to elements
is stated next to the definition.

**Integrally closed means `IsIntegrallyClosedIn`.** The right class of the integral system is
Mathlib's `IsIntegrallyClosedIn A B` for the algebra structure of the map: the map is injective and
every element of `B` integral over `A` lies in the image. It is not the class of maps with
integrally closed *target*.

**Codiagonals only where needed.** The absolutely flat and localization generators are
epimorphisms, so the H4 form of the generic theory applies and no codiagonal generators are formed.
The integral, Zariski, weak normalization and monic generators are not all epimorphisms (`ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ`
and the degree-`n ≥ 2` universal roots are not), and for these the augmented family
`orthogonalGenerators` is what the small object argument runs on. Every statement says which.

**Universal homeomorphisms are a ring-theoretic class.** `univHomeo f` is *integral, universally
injective, and surjective on spectra*, with universal injectivity in the field-valued-point form:
for every field `K`, precomposition with `f` is injective on `Hom(B, K)`. This is Stacks
[0CNE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNE)'s characterization taken as the definition. No
statement about morphisms of schemes is made or assumed.

**Seminormal and absolutely weakly normal are Stacks' notions.** `IsSeminormalRing` is
[0EUL](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EUL)(1): for `x³ = y²` there is a *unique* `a` with
`x = a²`, `y = a³`. `IsAbsolutelyWeaklyNormalRing` adds, for every prime `p` and `pᵖx = yᵖ`, a
unique `a` with `x = aᵖ`, `y = p·a`. Stacks' *weakly normal* ([0H3S](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3S))
is a different notion and no declaration is named `weaklyNormal`.

**"w-local" is Bhatt–Scholze's notion.** `IsWLocalRing A` means the closed points of `Spec A` form
a closed set and every connected component of `Spec A` contains exactly one closed point. Von
Neumann regular rings are "the local objects of the absolutely flat system", never "w-local".

**Every reflection is a factorization of the terminal map.** `A ⟶ A^af`, `A ⟶ A^sn`, `A ⟶ A^awn`
are the left factor of `A ⟶ 0` in the respective system, the universal property is the unique
lifting property against the right class, and the adjunction is packaged as an `Adjunction` into the
full subcategory of local objects. Idempotence (`A` local implies `A ⟶ A^…` is an isomorphism) is a
separate theorem, from `IsOrthogonalPair.inf_eq_isomorphisms`.

## Milestone A — Commutative rings as a locally finitely presentable category

The ring-level hypotheses of the generic small object argument, packaged once so that every later
milestone discharges them by naming its generator family.

### A1 — Finitely presentable objects and local finite presentability

A finitely presented ring is a finitely presentable object: maps out of it into a filtered colimit
factor through a stage, uniquely up to a later stage. Mathlib proves this for `Under R`; passing to
`CommRingCat` itself is the observation that `CommRingCat` is `Under ℤ`, and the polynomial ring
`ℤ[X]` is a finitely presentable strong generator, which makes `CommRingCat` locally finitely
presentable. The same argument over a base gives that `Under R` is finitely accessible, which is
what Mathlib's `ind_iff_exists` and `IsMultiplicative.ind_of_preIndSpreads` need.

- `CommRingCat.isFinitelyPresentable_of_finitePresentation_int`: a ring `R` with
  `(isInitial.to R).hom.FinitePresentation` is `IsFinitelyPresentable.{u}`, through
  `Under.equivalenceOfIsInitial` and Mathlib's `isFinitelyPresentable_under`; the algebra form
  `isFinitelyPresentable_of_algebra_finitePresentation` for `[Algebra.FinitePresentation ℤ R]`.
- `CommRingCat.isStrongGenerator_freeOne` and `isFinitelyPresentable_freeOne`: `ℤ[X]`, as
  `free.obj PUnit`, is a finitely presentable strong generator; hence the instance
  `IsLocallyFinitelyPresentable.{u} CommRingCat.{u}`.
- `CommRingCat.isStrongGenerator_underFreeOne`, `isFinitelyPresentable_underFreeOne`, and the
  instances `IsLocallyFinitelyPresentable.{u} (Under R)` and
  `IsFinitelyAccessibleCategory.{u} (Under R)`, for every `R : CommRingCat.{u}`.
- *Acceptance:* `ℤ[x₁, …, xₙ]/(f₁, …, fₘ)` is finitely presentable; a filtered colimit of rings
  receives a map from `ℤ[X]` only through a stage.

### A2 — The codiagonal, the epimorphism test, and the packaged generated system

The codiagonal of a ring map `i : A ⟶ B` is the multiplication map `B ⊗_A B ⟶ B`. So `i` is an
epimorphism exactly when that multiplication map is an isomorphism, and a ring map is formally
unramified as soon as it is an epimorphism. With A1 in hand, the generic small object argument is
available to any small family of maps between finitely presented rings, and the right class of the
generated system is stable under filtered colimits.

- `CommRingCat.pushoutSelfIsoTensorProduct` and `codiagonal_eq_lmul'`: `pushout i i ≅ B ⊗_A B`,
  under which `pushout.codiagonal i` is `Algebra.TensorProduct.lmul'`.
- `CommRingCat.epi_iff_isIso_lmul'`: `Epi i ↔ IsIso (lmul' : B ⊗_A B ⟶ B)`, from
  `pushout.isIso_codiagonal_iff` and the previous item.
- `Algebra.FormallyUnramified.of_isEpi` and
  `CommRingCat.not_epi_of_not_subsingleton_kaehlerDifferential`: an epimorphism of rings is
  formally unramified, so a nonzero module of Kähler differentials refutes epimorphicity. This is
  the tool that shows `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[X]` and the universal monic roots of degree `≥ 2` are not
  epimorphisms.
- `commRingCat_hasSmallObjectArgument`: a small `I` whose generator *sources* are finitely
  presentable has the small object argument; `commRingCat_hasSmallObjectArgument_orthogonalGenerators`:
  if both endpoints of every generator are finitely presented over `ℤ`, the augmented family
  `orthogonalGenerators I` has it. Both hypotheses are in the
  `∀ {A B} (i : A ⟶ B), I i → …` form, discharged by `rintro ⟨j⟩` at a concrete family.
- `commRingCat_generatedOrthogonalFactorizationSystem` and
  `commRingCat_generatedHasFunctorialFactorization`: the packaged conclusion,
  `IsOrthogonalFactorizationSystem I.rightOrthogonal.leftOrthogonal I.rightOrthogonal` with a
  functorial factorization.
- `commRingCat_rightOrthogonal_isStableUnderFilteredColimits`: for generators with finitely
  presentable endpoints, `I.rightOrthogonal` is stable under colimits of every essentially small
  filtered shape. This is the ring form of Stacks [0FWT](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FWT)
  and is what makes each later right class closed under filtered colimits with no further work.
- `CommRingCat.surjective_isStableUnderCobaseChange`: `MorphismProperty.surjective CommRingCat` is
  `RingHom.toMorphismProperty RingHom.surjective` and is stable under cobase change, from Mathlib's
  base-change stability.
- *Acceptance:* a family of two maps between polynomial rings generates an orthogonal factorization
  system by supplying two finite-presentation facts; `ℤ[X] ⟶ ℤ[X, X⁻¹]` passes the epimorphism test
  and `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[X]` fails it.

*Depends on:* Mathlib, and G2, G3, H4, I2 of `OrthogonalFactorization`.

## Milestone B — Localization and local homomorphisms

The one system whose factorization needs no small object argument: a ring map `f : A ⟶ B` inverts
the submonoid `S = f⁻¹(Bˣ)`, so it factors through `S⁻¹A`, and the second factor reflects units.
Both classes are universe-polymorphic, and the generator description is added afterwards.

### B1 — The classes and the explicit factorization

- `localHom : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{u}`, `f ↦ IsLocalHom f.hom`, with `IsMultiplicative`
  and `RespectsIso`. Defined directly, since Mathlib's `IsLocalHom` is a class on the map rather
  than a `RingHom` predicate.
- `invertedSubmonoid f := (IsUnit.submonoid B).comap f.hom`, `localizationObj f`,
  `toLocalizationObj f : A ⟶ localizationObj f`, `fromLocalizationObj f`, and
  `toLocalizationObj_comp_fromLocalizationObj`: the factorization of `f` through
  `Localization (invertedSubmonoid f)`.
- `localizationMap : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{u}`, `f ↦ IsLocalization (invertedSubmonoid f) B`
  for the algebra `f.hom.toAlgebra`, with `localizationMap_iff` (the three-clause elementwise form)
  and `localizationMap_iff_exists` (`f` is a localization at *some* submonoid). `RespectsIso`, and
  `localizationMap_of_isIso`.
- `localHom_fromLocalizationObj`, `localizationMap_toLocalizationObj`, and
  `localizationFactorization f : localizationMap.MapFactorizationData localHom f`.
- `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_toLocalization_of_localHom`: the first factor lifts uniquely against
  every local homomorphism, by `IsLocalization.lift` and `IsLocalization.ringHom_ext`.

### B2 — The identifications and the system

- `leftOrthogonal_localHom_eq : localHom.{u}.leftOrthogonal = localizationMap.{u}`. The inclusion
  `⊇` is B1; for `⊆`, factor a left-orthogonal `f` as `toLocalizationObj f ≫ fromLocalizationObj f`,
  cancel to put the second factor in both classes, and conclude it is an isomorphism by
  `IsOrthogonalPair.inf_eq_isomorphisms`.
- `rightOrthogonal_localizationMap_eq : localizationMap.{u}.rightOrthogonal = localHom.{u}`, with
  `localHom_of_retraction` as the lemma that a map with a retraction reflects units.
- `localizationFunctor : Arrow CommRingCat.{u} ⥤ CommRingCat.{u}` with the two natural
  transformations from `Arrow.leftFunc` and to `Arrow.rightFunc`, and
  `localizationHasFunctorialFactorization`: the factorization is functorial in the arrow, by
  `IsLocalization.map`. Built by hand, with no presentability hypothesis.
- `localizationLocalHomFactorizationSystem : IsOrthogonalFactorizationSystem localizationMap.{u} localHom.{u}`.
- The generic closure properties, instantiated: `localizationMap` is stable under cobase change,
  colimits of every shape, coproducts and retracts; `localHom` under base change, limits of every
  shape and retracts; and `localizationMap ⊓ localHom = isomorphisms`.
- *Acceptance:* `A ⟶ A[1/f]` is in `localizationMap` and `A ⟶ A/I` is in `localHom` exactly when
  `I ≤ jacobson ⊥`; the factorization of `ℤ ⟶ ℚ` has middle object `ℚ`, and that of
  `ℤ ⟶ ℤ/(p)` has middle object `ℤ_(p)`.

### B3 — The unit generator, Zariski pairs, and the cellular form

Maps out of `ℤ[t, t⁻¹]` are units, so the map `λ : ℤ[t] ⟶ ℤ[t, t⁻¹]` classifies "an element that
becomes a unit", and lifting against it is exactly reflecting units. Since `λ` is an epimorphism the
lift is automatically unique, and the generic H4 applies: the localization system is the system
generated by `λ`, with no codiagonal. Every localization is then a single pushout of a coproduct of
copies of `λ`, one per element of the submonoid.

- `unitGenerator : of (Polynomial ℤ) ⟶ of (Localization.Away X)` (in `CommRingCat.{0}`),
  `laurentHomEquiv R : (ℤ[t, t⁻¹] →+* R) ≃ Rˣ` with its naturality, and
  `epi_unitGenerator`.
- `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_unitGenerator_iff : HasUniqueLiftingProperty unitGenerator (ofHom f) ↔ IsLocalHom f`,
  together with `hasLiftingProperty_unitGenerator_iff` (the same for ordinary lifting) and the
  statement that the two agree because `λ` is an epimorphism.
- `unitGenerators := ofHoms (fun _ : Unit ↦ unitGenerator)` and
  `rightOrthogonal_unitGenerators_eq : unitGenerators.rightOrthogonal = localHom.{0}`.
- `leftOrthogonal_rightOrthogonal_unitGenerators_eq : unitGenerators.rightOrthogonal.leftOrthogonal = localizationMap.{0}`,
  and `localizationMap_eq_cellular_unitGenerators : localizationMap.{0} = cellular.{0} unitGenerators`,
  from H4 and B2.
- `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_unitGenerator_quotientMk_iff : HasUniqueLiftingProperty unitGenerator (ofHom (Ideal.Quotient.mk I)) ↔ I ≤ Ideal.jacobson ⊥`,

- `isPushout_coproduct_unitGenerator`: for a submonoid `M ⊆ A`, the map `A ⟶ Localization M` is

## Milestone C — The Zariski system

Adjoining to `λ` the map `δ : ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ`, which classifies idempotents, produces a system whose right
class consists of the local homomorphisms that are bijective on idempotents and whose left class is
the class of *Zariski maps* in the sense of Bhatt–Scholze: filtered colimits of maps
`A ⟶ ∏_{j<n} A[1/f_j]`. This is the system whose reflection of `A ⟶ A^af` produces the w-local ring
`A^Z` of *The pro-étale topology for schemes*, §2.2.

### C1 — The idempotent generator

- `idempotentGenerator : of ℤ ⟶ of (ℤ × ℤ)`, the cast; `prodIntHomEquiv R : ((ℤ × ℤ) →+* R) ≃ {e : R // IsIdempotentElem e}`
  sending `φ` to `φ (1, 0)`, with its naturality.
- `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_idempotentGenerator_iff`: unique lifting against `δ` is bijectivity of
  `f` on idempotents. Existence is surjectivity on idempotents and uniqueness is injectivity, and
  both halves are stated (`exists_isIdempotentElem_map_eq_of_…`, `eq_of_isIdempotentElem_of_map_eq_of_…`).
- `IsIdempotentElem.universalRing := AdjoinRoot (X² − X)` with `homEquiv`, and
  `prodIntEquivUniversalRing : ℤ × ℤ ≃+* universalRing`: the two presentations of the classifier
  of idempotents agree.
- `idempotentGenerator_source_finitePresentation`, `idempotentGenerator_target_finitePresentation`.
- *Acceptance:* `ℤ ⟶ ℤ/6` is not bijective on idempotents; a local homomorphism between local rings
  is; `A ⟶ A/nilradical A` is (Mathlib's `existsUnique_isIdempotentElem_eq_of_ker_isNilpotent`).

### C2 — The generators and the right class

- `Zariski.Index` with two constructors, `zariskiGenerators := ofHoms Index.hom`, its smallness, and
  `zariskiGenerators_eq_sup : zariskiGenerators = single idempotentGenerator ⊔ single unitGenerator`.
- `zariskiRight := zariskiGenerators.rightOrthogonal`, and the pinning lemma
  `zariskiRight_iff : zariskiRight (ofHom f) ↔ IsLocalHom f ∧ Function.Bijective (idempotent map of f)`.
- `zariskiRight` is multiplicative, respects isomorphisms, has the of-postcomp property, and is
  stable under base change, limits of every shape, and filtered colimits (A2).
- `zariskiRight_le_localHom : zariskiRight ≤ localHom.{0}` and `zariskiRight_lt_localHom`,
  separated by the diagonal `ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ`, which reflects units and is not surjective on idempotents.
- *Acceptance:* every field extension is in `zariskiRight`; `A ⟶ A^af` (E3) is not, for `A = ℤ`.

### C3 — The generated system

- `zariskiGenerators_source_finitePresentation`, `zariskiGenerators_target_finitePresentation`,
  and `zariskiGenerators_hasSmallObjectArgument : HasSmallObjectArgument.{0} (orthogonalGenerators zariskiGenerators)`.
  `δ` is not an epimorphism, so the codiagonal augmentation is genuinely used here.
- `zariskiLeft := zariskiRight.leftOrthogonal`, `zariskiOrthogonalFactorizationSystem`,
  `zariskiHasFunctorialFactorization`, and the instantiated closure properties: `zariskiLeft` is
  multiplicative, respects isomorphisms, is stable under cobase change, colimits of every shape and
  transfinite composition.
- `zariskiLeft_eq_cellular : zariskiLeft = cellular.{0} (orthogonalGenerators zariskiGenerators)`,
  from H3.
- `localizationMap_le_zariskiLeft : localizationMap.{0} ≤ zariskiLeft` (antitonicity from
  `unitGenerators ≤ zariskiGenerators`), and `localizationMap_lt_zariskiLeft`, separated by
  `A ⟶ A × A`, which is a Zariski map but not an epimorphism, while every localization map is one.
- *Acceptance:* `A ⟶ A[1/f]` and `A ⟶ A × A` are in `zariskiLeft`; `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[X]` is not.

### C4 — The Zariski theorem: Zariski maps are ind-(Zariski localizations)

A *Zariski localization* of `A` is a map `A ⟶ ∏_{j<n} A[1/f_j]` for a finite family `f : Fin n → A`
(Bhatt–Scholze, Definition 2.2.1; `n = 0` is allowed and gives `A ⟶ 0`). These are exactly the
finite cell attachments of the Zariski system: a coproduct of copies of `λ` pushed out along `A`
is `A ⟶ A[1/f]`, a copy of `δ` pushed out is `A ⟶ A × A`, and both operations preserve the shape
`A ⟶ ∏ A[1/f_j]`. The theorem is that the cellular class, which is `zariskiLeft` by C3, is the
class of filtered colimits of Zariski localizations in `Under A`, and that a finitely presentable
member of `zariskiLeft` is a Zariski localization on the nose.

- `RingHom.IsZariskiLocalization f`: `f` is isomorphic as an arrow to `A ⟶ ∏_{j<n} A[1/f_j]`;
  `zariskiLocalization : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{u}` from it, with `RespectsIso`,
  `isomorphisms ≤ zariskiLocalization`, membership of `A ⟶ S` for `[IsLocalization.Away g S]` and
  of the diagonal `A ⟶ ∏_{Fin n} A`, and `zariskiLocalization_iff_exists_splitting_away`.
- `zariskiLocalization` is multiplicative (a Zariski localization of a Zariski localization is one,
  by `A[1/f][1/g] = A[1/fg]` and distributing products), stable under cobase change, contained in
  `isFinitelyPresentable`, and satisfies `PreIndSpreads`: a Zariski localization of a filtered
  colimit is the cobase change of a Zariski localization of a stage, since finitely many `f_j`
  come from a stage.
- `indZariskiLocalization := ind.{u} zariskiLocalization`, multiplicative by Mathlib's
  `IsMultiplicative.ind_of_preIndSpreads` and A1's accessibility of `Under A`.
- `zariskiGenerators ≤ zariskiLocalization.{0}`, `codiagonalGenerators zariskiGenerators ≤ zariskiLocalization.{0}`
  (the codiagonal of `δ` is `ℤ × ℤ × ℤ × ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ`, a Zariski localization; that of `λ` is an
  isomorphism), and `zariskiLocalization ≤ zariskiLeft`.
- **`zariskiLeft_eq_indZariskiLocalization : zariskiLeft = indZariskiLocalization.{0}`.** The
  inclusion `⊇` is that `zariskiLeft` is stable under colimits in `Under A` (A2 of
  `OrthogonalFactorization`). The inclusion `⊆` is `cellular_le_ind`: every layer of the cellular
  class (coproducts, pushouts, transfinite composition) stays inside `ind` of a class of finitely
  presentable maps stable under cobase change and composition, which is Mathlib's `ind_ind`
  together with the closure of `ind` under filtered colimits of maps, coproducts and transfinite
  compositions.
- **`zariskiLocalization_eq_zariskiLeft_inf_isFinitelyPresentable`:** a finitely presentable map
  in `zariskiLeft` is a Zariski localization. Such a map is a filtered colimit of Zariski
  localizations `A ⟶ B_j`; by finite presentability it is a retract of some `B_j` over `A`; and a
  retract of a Zariski localization is a Zariski localization, because retracts of
  `A ⟶ ∏ A[1/f_j]` under `A` are given by an idempotent of `∏ A[1/f_j]`, hence by clopen subsets of
  the `Spec A[1/f_j]`, which are again finite unions of basic opens. This is the point that
  separates `zariskiLeft` from the Stacks Project's *ind-Zariski* maps, which are filtered colimits
  of the larger class of *all* local isomorphisms.
- `zariskiLocalization_lt_zariskiLeft`: the inclusion is strict, since `A ⟶ A^Z` (C5) is not
  finitely presented for `A = ℤ`. The other comparisons of `zariskiLeft` in this roadmap are
  `localizationMap < zariskiLeft` (C3) and `zariskiLeft < monicLeft` (G3).
- *Acceptance:* `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3]` is a Zariski localization; `A ⟶ A^Z` (C5) is in
  `indZariskiLocalization`; a finitely presentable map in `zariskiLeft` with connected target and
  domain a local ring is a localization at one element.

### C5 — The Zariski factor of the absolutely flat reflection, and w-local rings

Factoring `A ⟶ A^af` (E3) in the Zariski system gives `A ⟶ A^Z ⟶ A^af`. The second map is a local
homomorphism bijective on idempotents into a von Neumann regular ring, and that single property
forces a great deal: its kernel is the Jacobson radical of `A^Z`, it is surjective, the closed
points of `Spec A^Z` are a closed set homeomorphic to `Spec A^af`, every connected component of
`Spec A^Z` contains exactly one closed point, and `Spec A^Z ⟶ Spec A` is surjective. This is
Bhatt–Scholze's construction of the w-localization, §2.2, obtained here from two factorization
systems.

- `IsWLocalRing A`: `PrimeSpectrum.closedPoints A` is closed and every connected component of
  `Spec A` contains exactly one closed point; `closedPoints` as a definition.
- `isWLocalRing_iff_exists_zariskiRight_isVonNeumannRegularRing : IsWLocalRing R ↔ ∃ V, IsVonNeumannRegularRing V ∧ ∃ r : R →+* V, zariskiRight (ofHom r)`:
  the characterization of w-local rings through the Zariski right class.
- For `r : R →+* V` in `zariskiRight` with `V` von Neumann regular:
  `ker_eq_jacobson_bot_of_zariskiRight : ker r = jacobson ⊥`,
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_quotient_of_zariskiRight : IsVonNeumannRegularRing (R ⧸ jacobson ⊥)`,
  and, when `r` factors `A ⟶ A^af`, `surjective_of_zariskiRight` and the induced isomorphism
  `R ⧸ jacobson ⊥ ≃+* A^af`.
- `zLocalization A`, `zLocalizationMap : of A ⟶ zLocalization A`, `zLocalizationProj`, with
  `zariskiLeft_zLocalizationMap`, `zariskiRight_zLocalizationProj`, and uniqueness
  `zLocalization_unique` up to unique isomorphism (E2 of `OrthogonalFactorization`).
- `ker_zLocalizationProj`, `surjective_zLocalizationProj`,
  `zLocalizationQuotientEquiv : zLocalization A ⧸ jacobson ⊥ ≃+* absFlatRing A`.
- `closedPoints_zLocalization_eq : closedPoints (A^Z) = zeroLocus (jacobson ⊥)`,
  `isClosedEmbedding_comap_zLocalizationProj`,
  `closedPointsZLocalizationEquivPrimeSpectrumAbsFlatRing : closedPoints (A^Z) ≃ₜ Spec A^af`.
- `connectedComponentClosedPoint`: the unique closed point of the component of `x`, its continuity,
  and `connectedComponentsZLocalizationEquiv : ConnectedComponents (Spec A^Z) ≃ₜ Spec A^af`.
- `isWLocalRing_zLocalization : IsWLocalRing (zLocalization A)`, through the characterization
  above, and `surjective_comap_zLocalizationMap`: `Spec A^Z ⟶ Spec A` is surjective, every prime of
  `A` being the restriction of a maximal ideal of `A^Z`.
- `flat_zLocalizationMap`, `faithfullyFlat_zLocalizationMap`, and `Algebra.WeaklyEtale A (A^Z)`:
  the w-localization is faithfully flat and weakly étale, being a filtered colimit of Zariski
  localizations (C4).
- *Acceptance:* for `A` a field or a local ring `A ⟶ A^Z` is an isomorphism; for `A = ℤ`, `A^Z` is
  not `ℤ`, since `ℤ` is not w-local; `IsWLocalRing` holds for `LocallyConstant S k` (H1) and fails
  for `ℤ`.

*Depends on:* B, E3; and A2, E2, H3 of `OrthogonalFactorization`; Mathlib's `ind`.

## Milestone D — Integral closure

The integral closure of `A` in `B` factors `A ⟶ B` as an integral map followed by an integrally
closed extension, and the two classes are orthogonal: an integral element of `Y` over `X` has a
unique preimage along an integrally closed `X ⟶ Y`, so a map from an integral extension lifts
uniquely. The generators are the map killing a variable, which classifies "an element of the
kernel", and the universal monic root of each degree, which classifies "a root of a monic
polynomial".

### D1 — The classes and the explicit factorization

- `integral := RingHom.toMorphismProperty RingHom.IsIntegral` and
  `integrallyClosed f := IsIntegrallyClosedIn A B` for `f.hom.toAlgebra`, with
  `integrallyClosed_iff : integrallyClosed f ↔ Injective f ∧ ∀ y, f.IsIntegralElem y → ∃ x, f x = y`.
  Both in `CommRingCat.{u}`, both `RespectsIso`.
- `integralClosureObj f`, `toIntegralClosure f`, `fromIntegralClosure f`, the composition law,
  `toIntegralClosure_isIntegral`, `fromIntegralClosure_isIntegrallyClosed`, and
  `integralClosureFactorization f : integral.MapFactorizationData integrallyClosed f`.
- `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_of_integral_of_integrallyClosed`, hence
  `integral_le_leftOrthogonal_integrallyClosed` and `integrallyClosed_le_rightOrthogonal_integral`.
- `leftOrthogonal_integrallyClosed_eq : integrallyClosed.{u}.leftOrthogonal = integral.{u}`: by the
  collapse argument, in every universe.
- *Acceptance:* `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[i]` is integral; `ℤ ⟶ ℚ` is integrally closed; the factorization of
  `ℤ ⟶ ℚ(i)` has middle object `ℤ[i]`.

### D2 — The generators

  `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_kernelGenerator_iff : HasUniqueLiftingProperty kernelGenerator (ofHom f) ↔ Injective f`.
  `kernelGenerator_isIntegral`.
- `UniversalMonic.polynomial n := Xⁿ + Σ_{i<n} C (X i) X^i` over `MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℤ`, for

  and `universalMonicRootGenerator_isIntegral`.
- `BasicIntegralGeneratorIndex` (`kernel | monicRoot n`), `basicIntegralGenerators := ofHoms basicGenHom`,
  its smallness, `basicIntegralGenerators_isIntegral : basicIntegralGenerators ≤ integral`, and the

## Milestone E — Absolute flatness

A ring is *von Neumann regular* (absolutely flat) when every element `a` satisfies `a = a²b` for
some `b`; equivalently every principal ideal is generated by an idempotent, every module is flat,
or the ring is reduced of Krull dimension zero. The system is generated by the single map
`w : ℤ[x] ⟶ ℤ × ℤ[x, x⁻¹]`, `x ↦ (0, x)`, which classifies "an idempotent `e` with `ae = 0` and
`a` a unit modulo `e`", the *regular decomposition* of `a`. Since `w` is an epimorphism the
system has no codiagonals, unique lifting is ordinary lifting, and the left class consists of
epimorphisms. Its local objects are the von Neumann regular rings, and its reflection is Olivier's
universal absolutely flat ring `A ⟶ A^af`.

### E1 — Von Neumann regular rings

- `IsVonNeumannRegularRing A`: `∀ a, ∃ b, a = a * a * b`. Characterizations:
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_exists_idempotent` (every `a` has a regular decomposition),
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_forall_exists_isIdempotentElem_mul_isUnit` (`a = e·u`),
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_span_add_ann` (`(a) + Ann(a) = 1`),
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_forall_flat` (all modules in the ring's universe are flat, with
  `Module.Flat.of_isVonNeumannRegularRing` for modules in any universe),
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_isReduced_and_krullDimLE_zero`,
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_forall_isField_localization_atPrime`,
  `isVonNeumannRegularRing_iff_isReduced_and_t2Space`.
- The uniqueness `isIdempotentElem_unique_of_mul_eq_zero_of_isUnit_quotient` of the regular
  decomposition, and `Ideal.FG.exists_isIdempotentElem_span_eq` (finitely generated ideals are
  principal on an idempotent).
- Closure: fields, products, arbitrary `Pi`, quotients, localizations, direct limits, images of
  surjections, and `of_jointly_surjective`; `IsReduced`, `isField_iff_isDomain`,
  `isField_of_isLocalRing`, `isField_of_forall_isIdempotentElem` (no idempotents but `0, 1`);
  `jacobson_eq_bot`; `Ring.KrullDimLE 0`; `T2Space (Spec A)`; and the elementary
  `Ring.krullDimLE_zero_of_t2Space`.
- *Acceptance:* fields, `ℤ/6`, `LocallyConstant S k` are von Neumann regular; `ℤ` and `ℤ/4` are
  not; a domain is von Neumann regular exactly when it is a field.

### E2 — The generator and the right class

- `regularGeneratorHom : ℤ[x] →+* ℤ × ℤ[x, x⁻¹]`, `regularGenerator` in `CommRingCat.{0}`,
  `classifyingHomEquiv : (ℤ[x] →+* A) ≃ A`, and `hom_prod_classifies`: a map `ℤ × ℤ[x, x⁻¹] ⟶ A`
  under `ℤ[x]` is a regular decomposition of the image of `x`.
- `epi_regularGenerator`, through `Algebra.IsEpi.prod` of a surjection and a localization with
  zero tensor product (`ℤ ⊗_{ℤ[x]} ℤ[x, x⁻¹] = 0`), and `regularGenerators_le_epimorphisms`.
- `regularGenerator_fst = kernelGenerator` and `regularGenerator_snd = unitGenerator`: the two
  components of `w` are the kernel generator of D2 and the unit generator of B3.
- The base change formula: for `a : A`, `A ⊗_{ℤ[x]} (ℤ × ℤ[x, x⁻¹]) ≃ (A ⧸ (a)) × A[1/a]`
  (`baseChangeEquiv`), so a cell attachment along `w` at `a` is `A ⟶ A/(a) × A[1/a]`.
- `RegularDecomp a`, `regularHom : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{u}`, `f ↦ ∀ a, RegularDecomp (f a) → RegularDecomp a`
  (a map *reflects* regular decompositions), `reflects_regularDecomp_iff_compat` (the decomposition
  upstairs then maps to the one downstairs), multiplicativity and `RespectsIso`.

- `regularHom_terminal_iff : regularHom (terminal.from (of A)) ↔ IsVonNeumannRegularRing A`, in

- *Acceptance:* every map out of a von Neumann regular ring is in `regularHom`; `ℤ ⟶ ℚ` is not;
  `A ⟶ A ⧸ jacobson ⊥` is in `regularHom` when the quotient is von Neumann regular.

### E4 — The spectrum of the reflection, residue fields, and profinite spectra

`Spec A^af ⟶ Spec A` is a bijection, and the residue field of `A^af` at the prime over `p` is the
residue field of `A` at `p`: `A^af` is the ring obtained from `A` by making every residue field
into a factor. The spectrum of a von Neumann regular ring is profinite, its clopens are the
idempotents, and a map between such rings bijective on idempotents is a homeomorphism on spectra.
For a product of fields the spectrum is the Stone–Čech compactification of the index set.

- `residueFieldAt A p := FractionRing (A ⧸ p)`, `toResidueFieldAt`, `ker_toResidueFieldAt`,
  `absFlatResidueLift : absFlatRing A →+* residueFieldAt A p` by the universal property, and
  `residueFieldAtEquiv : residueFieldAt A p ≃+* absFlatRing A ⧸ q` for the prime `q` over `p`.
- `absFlatPrime A p : PrimeSpectrum (absFlatRing A)`, `comap_absFlatPrime`, and
  `bijective_comap_absFlatMap : Bijective (comap (absFlatMap A).hom)`.
- `homeomorphConstructibleComapAbsFlatMap`: the bijection is a homeomorphism from `Spec A^af`
  to `Spec A` with the constructible topology (Mathlib's `WithConstructibleTopology`). Its proof is that both are compact Hausdorff and the

## Milestone F — Weak normalization and seminormalization

A ring is *seminormal* when `x³ = y²` forces `x = a²`, `y = a³` for a unique `a`, and *absolutely
weakly normal* when moreover `pᵖx = yᵖ` forces `x = aᵖ`, `y = p·a` for a unique `a`, for every
prime `p` (Stacks [0EUL](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EUL)). Each condition is unique
lifting against one explicit map: the cusp `ℤ[x, y]/(x³ − y²) ⟶ ℤ[t]`, `x ↦ t²`, `y ↦ t³`, and the
`p`-th power map `ℤ[x, y]/(pᵖx − yᵖ) ⟶ ℤ[t]`, `x ↦ tᵖ`, `y ↦ p·t`. The generated systems have as
local objects exactly the seminormal, respectively absolutely weakly normal, rings, and their
reflections are the seminormalization and the absolute weak normalization of Stacks
[0EUR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EUR). The theorem of the milestone is the
identification of the left classes: the absolute one is the class of universal homeomorphisms
([0CNE](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNE)), the seminormal one the universal
homeomorphisms inducing isomorphisms on residue fields ([0CND](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CND)).

### F1 — Seminormal and absolutely weakly normal rings

- `IsSeminormalRing A` and `IsAbsolutelyWeaklyNormalRing A` (extending it), with the elementary
  API: `exists_sq_cube`, `eq_of_sq_of_cube`, `eq_of_sq_eq_of_cube_eq`, `eq_zero_of_isNilpotent`,
  the instance `IsSeminormalRing → IsReduced`, and the module documentation's remark that
  `ℤ[ε]/(ε²)` satisfies existence but not uniqueness, so reducedness needs the `∃!`.
- `IsSeminormalRing.of_isIntegrallyClosed` (a normal domain is seminormal), instances for fields,
  `ℤ`, and `k[X]`.
- Characteristic: `existsUnique_pth_of_isUnit` (the `p`-condition is automatic when `p` is a unit),
  `isAbsolutelyWeaklyNormalRing_of_algebra_rat` (a seminormal `ℚ`-algebra is absolutely weakly
  normal), `IsAbsolutelyWeaklyNormalRing.perfectRing` (in characteristic `p`, Frobenius is
  bijective) with the clause-level converse `existsUnique_pth_of_bijective_pow`, and for fields
  `isAbsolutelyWeaklyNormalRing_iff_perfectField`.
- *Acceptance:* the cusp `k[x, y]/(y² − x³)` is reduced and not seminormal; the node `k[x, y]/(xy)`
  is seminormal and not normal; `ℤ/4` is not seminormal; `𝔽_p(s)` is seminormal and not absolutely
  weakly normal (all in H1).

### F2 — Universal homeomorphisms and residue isomorphisms

- `RingHom.UniversallyInjective f`: for every field `K` in the universe, `g ↦ g ∘ f` is injective
  on `B →+* K`; identity, composition, outer cancellation, surjections, stability under base
  change; `RingHom.UniversallyInjective.isPurelyInseparable_residueField`: the residue field
  extensions are purely inseparable ([01S4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01S4)).
- `RingHom.ResidueIso f`: for every field `K` and `g : B →+* K`, the image of `g` lies in the
  subfield generated by the image of `g ∘ f`; `residueIso_iff_surjective_residueFieldMap`: this is
  surjectivity, hence bijectivity, of every residue field map `κ(f⁻¹q) ⟶ κ(q)`.
- `univHomeo : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{u}` (integral, universally injective, surjective on
  spectra) and `residueIso`, both multiplicative, respecting isomorphisms, stable under retracts,
  cobase change, filtered colimits, transfinite composition and coproducts.
  `univHomeo_of_surjective_of_isNilpotent_ker` ([0BR6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BR6)),
  `PrimeSpectrum.surjective_comap_iff_ker_le_nilradical` for integral maps,
  `RingHom.UniversallyInjective.injective_comap_of_isIntegral`.
- `univHomeo_codiagonal` (for `i` universally injective) and `residueIso_codiagonal` (for every
  `i`): the augmentation stays in the class.
- The subalgebra lemmas: `univHomeo_of_subalgebra`, `univHomeo_subtype_of_subalgebra`,
  `univHomeo_residueIso_of_subalgebra`, and their engine `RingHom.exists_extend`: a field-valued

  ([0CN7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CN7)).
- `univHomeo_le_integral : univHomeo.{u} ≤ integral.{u}`.
- *Acceptance:* `𝔽_p(tᵖ) ⟶ 𝔽_p(t)` is a universal homeomorphism and not a residue isomorphism;
  `A ⟶ A_red` is both; `ℤ[x] ⟶ ℤ` is neither, being a closed immersion onto `V(x)`; the
  necessity of the outer-map hypothesis in cancellation is witnessed by `𝔽₂ ⟶ 𝔽₄ ⟶ 0`.

  `hasUniqueLiftingProperty_pthGenerator_iff`.

### F5 — The elementary steps

The two operations that generate the left classes, and the proof that each is a left-class map.
The cusp step adjoins to `A ⊆ B` an element `b` with `b², b³ ∈ A` such that `A[b] = B`; the `p`-th
power step adjoins `b` with `bᵖ, p·b ∈ A`. Each is a cobase change of a generator followed by a
quotient with nilpotent kernel, and both are in `awnLeft`; the cusp step is in `snLeft`.

- `awnLeft_of_cusp_step`: for `f : A ⟶ B` injective with `B` generated over `A` by `b` and
  `f x = b²`, `f y = b³`, `awnLeft f`. Proved by a direct unique-lifting check: a map from `B` to a
  ring `X` over `A` is determined by the image of `b`, and two candidates differ by an element whose
  cube is zero (`cube_eq_zero_of_cusp_step`), which the right class kills
  (`isNilpotent_ker_eq_zero_of_awnHom`).
- `awnLeft_of_pth_step`: the same for `f x = bᵖ`, `f y = p·b`, `p` prime; two candidates differ by
  an element `z` with `p^{p−1} z = 0` and `z` nilpotent prime by prime, through the Frobenius
  congruence `sum_pow_sub_sum_pow_mem_span_natCast` and lying over.
- `snLeft_of_cusp_step`, the seminormal version.
- `elementaryStep : MorphismProperty CommRingCat.{0}` (injective, target generated by one `b`, and
  `b` a cusp or a `p`-th power step), `elementaryStep_le_awnLeft`, and the chain machinery
  `awnLeft_chainComp`, `awnLeft_ι_app_zero_of_elementaryStep`: a countable composite of elementary
  steps is in `awnLeft`.
- *Acceptance:* `k[t², t³] ⟶ k[t]` is a cusp step; `𝔽_p(tᵖ) ⟶ 𝔽_p(t)` is a `p`-th power step;
  `ℤ ⟶ ℤ[√2]` is neither.

### F6 — The absolute weak normalization theorem and its seminormal companion

Every universal homeomorphism is a transfinite composite of elementary steps. The construction half
is Stacks [0CNC](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNC): if `A ⊆ B` is a universal
homeomorphism with `A ≠ B`, there is `b ∈ B ∖ A` with `b², b³ ∈ A` or with `bᵖ, p·b ∈ A` for a
prime `p`. The argument reduces to `B` finite over `A` (a single element generates a finite
universal homeomorphism, [02JJ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02JJ)), takes a minimal prime
`𝔭` of the annihilator of `B/A`, the unique prime `𝔪'` over it, and splits on whether `𝔪'` lies in
the saturation of `A` at `𝔭`: if not, a rescaled element of `𝔪'` has two consecutive powers in `A`
and [0CNA](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNA) produces a cusp step; if so, the purely
inseparable residue extension produces a `p`-th power step. Under a residue isomorphism the second
branch is impossible ([0CNB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CNB)). The identification
theorem follows by Zorn's lemma over subalgebras, using that `awnLeft` is closed under directed
unions.

- `Nat.exists_add_mul_add_eq_of_sq_le` and `exists_sq_cube_mem_of_pow_pow_mem` ([0CNA]): if
  `b ∉ A`, `bⁿ, bⁿ⁺¹ ∈ A`, `n ≥ 2`, then some power `b' = bⁱ ∉ A` has `b'², b'³ ∈ A`.
- `univHomeo_finite_adjoin_of_univHomeo` ([02JJ]): `A[b]` is finite over `A` and the inclusion is a
  universal homeomorphism.
- `exists_minimalPrimes_annihilator_quotient_of_notMem_range`, `saturation B S : Subalgebra A B`
  with `saturation_ne_top`, `exists_pow_mul_mem_map_of_mem` (prime avoidance),
  `isMinimalPrime_map_of_liesOver_of_mem_minimalPrimes`, and
  `RingHom.UniversallyInjective.injective_comap_of_isIntegral`: the localization step, done with
  the saturation rather than by clearing denominators. `exists_clearDenominators` ([0CN9]) is
  stated and proved as well, since it is the form Stacks uses.
- `IsPurelyInseparable.exists_pow_notMem_range_pow_mem_range` and
  `exists_pth_step_of_subset_saturation`: the `p`-th power branch.
- **`exists_elementary_of_univHomeo'`** ([0CNC]) and **`exists_cusp_of_univHomeo_residueIso`**
  ([0CNB]), for `B` finite over `A`.
- `exists_lt_awnLeft_of_ne_top`, `sSup_mem_awnLeft_of_isChain` (with the directed union of a chain
  of subalgebras exhibited as a `CommRingCat` colimit, `chainCocone_isColimit`), and
  **`awnLeft_eq_univHomeo : awnLeft = univHomeo.{0}`** ([0CNE]).
- **`snLeft_eq_univHomeo_residueIso : snLeft = univHomeo.{0} ⊓ residueIso.{0}`** ([0CND]).
- `univHomeo_inf_awnRight_eq_isomorphisms` ([0H3H](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3H)) and
  `univHomeo_awnMap : univHomeo (awnMap A)`, `univHomeo_residueIso_snMap`: the reflections are
  universal homeomorphisms, and the seminormalization is a residue isomorphism, so that
  [0EUR](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0EUR)(1)–(4) hold in the form "terminal among
  universal homeomorphisms out of `A`" (`awnMap_final_among_univHomeo`, unconditional).
- *Acceptance:* `k[t², t³] ⟶ k[t]` is in `snLeft`; `𝔽_p(tᵖ) ⟶ 𝔽_p(t)` is in `awnLeft` and not in
  `snLeft`; `awnLeft < integral`, separated by `ℤ[x] ⟶ ℤ`.

### F7 — The relative form: the weak normalization of `A` in `B`

For a *dominant* map `A ⟶ B` (kernel inside the nilradical, equivalently dense image on spectra),
the subalgebras `C ⊆ B` with `A ⟶ C` a universal homeomorphism have a largest element `B'`, the
weak normalization of `A` in `B` ([0H3J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3J)), and it
commutes with localization ([0H3K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3K)). The factorization
system recovers it: for a dominant map, the middle object of the factorization in the absolute
system is `B'`.

- `RingHom.IsDominant f := ker f ≤ nilradical A`, `isDominant_iff_denseRange_comap`,
  `IsDominant.of_isLocalization`.
- `IsUnivHomeoSubalgebra C`, `univHomeoSubalgebra A B := adjoin A (⋃ such C)`,
  `le_univHomeoSubalgebra` (maximality, no dominance needed), `univHomeo_toUnivHomeoSubalgebra`
  (it is itself a universal homeomorphism, for dominant maps), closure under the elementary steps
  `mem_univHomeoSubalgebra_of_sq_of_cube` and `mem_univHomeoSubalgebra_of_pth`, and
  `awnRight_univHomeoSubalgebraHom : awnRight (B' ⟶ B)`.
- `awnFactorization_eq_univHomeoSubalgebra`: for dominant `A ⟶ B`, every factorization in the
  absolute system has middle object `B'`, uniquely.
- `localizedUnivHomeoSubalgebra_eq_univHomeoSubalgebra` ([0H3K]): `(B')_M = (B_M)'` for a
  submonoid `M ⊆ A`.
- The seminormal analogues `residueIsoSubalgebra A B`, its maximality and universal property, and
  `snFactorization_eq_residueIsoSubalgebra`, following [0H3L](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3L)
  and [0H3M](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0H3M).
- *Acceptance:* for `A ⊆ B` reduced with `B` integral over `A`, `B'` is the set of `b` with `b²,
  b³ ∈ A` iterated transfinitely; for `A ⟶ 𝔽_p`, non-dominant, the family of subalgebras is empty
  and the theorem does not apply.

*Depends on:* A, D1; and D, E, G, H3, H5, J of `OrthogonalFactorization`.

## Milestone G — The monic system

The unit generator together with the universal étale monic roots — for each `n`, the map from
`ℤ[a₀, …, aₙ₋₁]` to the ring `(ℤ[a][x]/(xⁿ + Σ aᵢxⁱ))[1/f']` inverting the derivative — generates a
system whose right class is, on a quotient map `A ⟶ A/I`, exactly the condition that `(A, I)` is a
Henselian pair in the sense of Mathlib's `HenselianRing`: `I` is in the Jacobson radical and simple
roots of monic polynomials lift uniquely. The system sits strictly between the Zariski system and
the system generated by all étale maps; only the comparison with the former is in scope.

### G1 — The generators and the system

- `MonicGenerators.universalMonic n : (MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℤ)[X]`, `coeffMap f` (the
  specialization to a given monic `f`), `monicGeneratorPair n : StandardEtalePair (MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℤ)`
  with `f := universalMonic n`, `g := derivative (universalMonic n)`; `MonicGenerators.Index`
  (`unit | root n`), `src`, `tgt`, `hom`, `monicGenerators := ofHoms hom`, and
  `hom_etale : RingHom.Etale (hom i).hom` for every `i`. `hom .unit = unitGenerator` definitionally.
- Finite presentation of all endpoints, `monicGenerators_hasSmallObjectArgument` (augmented),
  `monicRight := monicGenerators.rightOrthogonal`, `monicLeft`, `monicOrthogonalFactorizationSystem`,
  `monicHasFunctorialFactorization`, and `monicRight_isStableUnderFilteredColimitsOfShape`.
- `monicLeft_eq_cellular : monicLeft = cellular.{0} (orthogonalGenerators monicGenerators)`.
- *Acceptance:* `ℚ ⟶ ℚ[x]/(x² − 2)` is in `monicLeft`, as a pushout of the degree-two root
  generator along `(a₀, a₁) ↦ (−2, 0)`; `ℤ_p ⟶ 𝔽_p` is in `monicRight`.

### G2 — The right class and Henselian pairs

- `henselianRing_iff_uniqueRLP_monicGenerators : HenselianRing R I ↔ monicGenerators.rightOrthogonal (ofHom (Ideal.Quotient.mk I))`,
  through `quotient_hasUniqueLiftingProperty_unitGenerator_iff` (B3, the Jacobson condition) and
  `monicGeneratorPair_hasMap_iff` (a map out of the root generator's target is a simple root).
- `HenselianRing.existsUnique_root`: for `[HenselianRing R I]`, a monic `f` and `a₀` with
  `f a₀ ∈ I` and `f' a₀` a unit modulo `I`, there is a unique root `a ≡ a₀ mod I`. Mathlib supplies
  existence; uniqueness is `eq_of_isRoot_of_isRoot_of_sub_mem`.
- `monicRight_iff_henselianRing_ker_of_surjective : monicRight (ofHom p) ↔ HenselianRing R (ker p)`
  for surjective `p`, the pinning lemma of `monicRight`.
- Necessary conditions for an arbitrary map in `monicRight`: `isLocalHom_of_monicRight`,
  `ker_le_jacobson_bot_of_monicRight`, `henselianRing_ker_of_monicRight`, and
  `exists_isRoot_of_monicRight` (simple roots lift); the module documentation says the converse of
  the third fails without surjectivity.
- The sufficient criterion `monicRight_of_isLocalHom_of_forall_root`: a local homomorphism along
  which simple roots of monic polynomials lift uniquely is in `monicRight`.
- `monicRight_quotient_idempotent`: for a Henselian pair `(R, I)` and an idempotent `e`, the
  quotient by `I` in `R ⧸ (1 − e)` is in `monicRight`.
- *Acceptance:* `(ℤ_p, (p))` and `(k[[t]], (t))` are Henselian pairs and their quotient maps are in
  `monicRight`; `(ℤ_(p), (p))` is not, witnessed by `x² − 7` for `p = 3`; `ℚ ⟶ ℚ(√2)` is not in
  `monicRight`.

### G3 — Comparison with the Zariski system

- `unitGenerator_mem_monicGenerators`, `monicGenerators_le_monicLeft`, and
  `idempotentGenerator_eq_pushout_monicGenerator_two`: `δ : ℤ ⟶ ℤ × ℤ` is the pushout of the
  degree-two root generator along `(a₀, a₁) ↦ (0, −1)`, since `x² − x` with derivative `2x − 1`
  inverted is `ℤ × ℤ`. Hence `zariskiGenerators ≤ monicLeft`.
- `monicRight_le_zariskiRight` and `zariskiLeft_le_monicLeft`.
- `monicRight_lt_zariskiRight`, separated by `ℚ ⟶ ℚ(√2)`, which is a field extension (so in
  `zariskiRight`) along which the root `√2` of `x² − 2` does not lift.
- `zariskiLeft_lt_monicLeft`, separated by the same map: it is in `monicLeft` (G1) and in
  `zariskiRight` (C2) and is not an isomorphism, so by `IsOrthogonalPair.inf_eq_isomorphisms` it is
  not in `zariskiLeft`.
- `monicLeft_adjoinRoot_sub_two`, `zariskiRight_adjoinRoot_sub_two`,
  `not_monicRight_adjoinRoot_sub_two`, `not_zariskiLeft_adjoinRoot_sub_two`: the four facts about
  the separating map.
- *Acceptance:* the chain `monicRight < zariskiRight < localHom` and the chain
  `localizationMap < zariskiLeft < monicLeft`, all in `CommRingCat.{0}`, each strictness with a
  named witness.

*Depends on:* B3, C; and D, E, G, H3, I of `OrthogonalFactorization`; Mathlib's `HenselianRing`
and `StandardEtalePair`.

## Milestone H — Examples, separations, and coverage

The area introduces eleven classes of maps and five classes of rings. Each is given a ground witness
(a construction whose hypotheses do not mention the class) and a ground refutation, and every
inclusion proved between two classes is resolved into a strict inclusion, with a separating map, or
an equality. The point is that a definition with no witness and no refutation is indistinguishable
from a vacuous one, and a `≤` with no separation may be a `=` in disguise.

### H2 — Witnesses and refutations for every notion

For each of `localizationMap`, `localHom`, `zariskiLeft`, `zariskiRight`, `zariskiLocalization`,
