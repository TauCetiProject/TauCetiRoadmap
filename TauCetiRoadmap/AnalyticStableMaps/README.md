# Analytic stable maps and their implicit atlases

Construct the analytic moduli theory of stable pseudoholomorphic maps from compact nodal complex curves to a closed symplectic manifold with an arbitrary tame almost complex structure. Build the actual map groupoids and topology, general-genus compactness, finite equivariant obstruction reductions, uniform nodal gluing and topological local projection charts. Verify the complete oriented topological implicit-atlas axioms and all specified auxiliary-choice/refinement comparisons, then construct its rational dual-Čech virtual functional through ImplicitAtlases. Include compact parameter families and the actual evaluation, stabilization, clutching and joint normal-model interfaces used by Gromov–Witten theory.

The definitive specification is this README. `Suggested.lean` gives fixed-normalization node-matching interfaces and an integer dimension formula; it is not an exhaustive list of the analytic constructions.

### M0.1. Normalization of a nonconstant closed curve

Construct this early map-normalization theorem before M1's stable-map assembly and M2's simple-map regularity. Let (Σ,j) be a connected compact smooth Riemann surface without boundary, let X be a finite-dimensional Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold without boundary with a smooth almost complex structure J, and let u:Σ→X be smooth, nonconstant and J-holomorphic. The construction does not assume that u is an immersion, that its image is embedded, that J is integrable or generic, or that its ordinary homology class is primitive. M0's compact symplectic target is a specialization. For the present theorem on an arbitrary ambient X, construct the compact-image localization below; it supplies no global noncompact moduli compactness, no-escape, punctured or bordered theorem.

**Local analytic inputs and finite exceptional set.** Consume only [HF F2.2a–c and F2.3](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/0ea0ff861d0ca2cef582d8d3e9b43c675894e538/TauCetiRoadmap/HeegaardFloer/README.md): local finite-order vanishing/isolated preimages, the coincident-branch accumulation lemma, and finite-energy interior removal on an actual compact target-image neighborhood. Do not consume HF F2.6b's global sphere normalization, generic transversality, intersection positivity, M2 regularity, M3 stable-map compactness or a virtual engine. Construct the actual localized application on the compact set u(Σ): choose finitely many relatively compact target charts and the required uniform local bounds. At each exceptional image value choose a smaller chart whose constant standard closed two-form tames J, after a linear complex identification and shrinking; prove this taming by openness. These local forms and auxiliary metric comparisons supply the removal hypotheses there; a global taming form or compactness of X is not inferred. Use nonconstancy, local unique continuation and compactness of Σ to prove that Crit(u) and every fibre u⁻¹(x) are finite. Set B=u⁻¹(u(Crit(u))), the full preimage of the critical values, and prove B finite. It is not just Crit(u).

**Coincident-branch quotient.** On the connected punctured surface Σ∖B define Γ₀ by equality of local image germs, including the diagonal: (z,ζ) belongs when there are neighborhoods on which the two immersed branches differ by a holomorphic change of source coordinate. Prove equivalence with the local accumulation characterization using HF F2.2c; isolated intersections of different branches are excluded. Construct its equivalence-relation and holomorphic local-inverse properties, and prove Γ₀ closed in (Σ∖B)². Prove the first projection Γ₀→Σ∖B is a proper finite covering: a limiting partner over z∉B cannot lie in B because its image would be the noncritical value u(z). Its fibre cardinal d≥1 is constant. Prove Γ=cl(Γ₀) in Σ×Σ is an equivalence relation with finite classes, with B invariant. Construct positive local multiplicities e_z, equal1 off B, and prove Σ_(ζ∼z)e_ζ=d. Extend the local source-coordinate transitions across the exceptional points and construct the quotient holomorphic charts by the local branch-product coordinate. Prove their independence, transition holomorphy and local degree e_z. This is the general coincident-germ normalization construction of McDuff–Salamon, Proposition2.5.1; the quotient is not the image subset with its subspace topology.

**Factor surface and simple map.** Construct the connected compact Hausdorff second-countable smooth Riemann surface Σ₀=Σ/Γ and the surjective finite holomorphic branched cover π:Σ→Σ₀ of degree d, with π⁻¹(π(z)) exactly the Γ-class of z. The original map descends continuously to v:Σ₀→X with u=v∘π. Prove v is J-holomorphic off the finitely many exceptional values. In a local target metric use change of variables for π off its branch locus to prove finite Dirichlet energy near each exceptional point; use the local taming/compact-image data above and HF F2.3 to extend v smoothly and J-holomorphically. Prove that the coincident-germ multiplicity of v is1 by lifting germ coincidences to those of u and using their identification by the quotient. In the multiplicity-one argument retain regular points of B with a possibly critical partner; use finite fibres to discard a subsequence with constant partner before applying the precise accumulation lemma. The accumulation lemma then shows that the noninjective-point set {z | dv(z)=0 or #v⁻¹(v(z))>1} is at most countable and can accumulate only at critical points. In particular v has an injective regular point. This does not assert that v is globally injective or embedded.

Define a nonconstant closed curve to be simple when it does not factor through a finite holomorphic branched cover of degree greater than1. Prove that simplicity is equivalent to somewhere injectivity, and equivalent to d=1 in this construction. Use the actual finite-map degree-one biholomorphism, not just a set-theoretic bijection. If u=v₁∘π₁=v₂∘π₂ are two such factorizations with v₁,v₂ somewhere injective, construct the unique biholomorphism a:Σ₁→Σ₂ satisfying a∘π₁=π₂ and v₂∘a=v₁. Prove uniqueness by the quotient/germ characterization, including separate branches at self-intersection image values. Construct identity/composition laws under source biholomorphisms and target diffeomorphisms intertwining the almost complex structures. Equality of two image subsets alone is not a chosen comparison of their source data.

**Degree, class and genus.** Consume [FuchsianOrbifolds Layer5's independent compact-Riemann-surface degree theory](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/350e970ce49f707a327909beac0b7b7c782cc7ee/TauCetiRoadmap/FuchsianOrbifolds/README.md#layer-5-compact-surface-degree-theory-and-fuchsian-applications) for fibre-independent local degree, the degree-one biholomorphism and Riemann–Hurwitz. Compare π with that actual finite-map carrier and its complex orientations. Prove π_*[Σ]=d[Σ₀] and u_*[Σ]=d v_*[Σ₀] in integral ordinary homology, retaining torsion and the actual class of v rather than defining it by an ambiguous division by d. Prove the corresponding c₁ and, when a taming closed form ω is supplied, area identities c₁(u)=d c₁(v) and ∫Σu*ω=d∫Σ₀v*ω. With R=Σ_z(e_z−1), prove 2−2g(Σ)=d(2−2g(Σ₀))−R, or equivalently 2g(Σ)−2=d(2g(Σ₀)−2)+R. No analytic/cohomological genus comparison or Riemann–Roch theorem is a premise of this topological genus formula.

**Original domains, constants and later applications.** Constant maps remain separate and have no somewhere-injective factor; do not apply the nonconstant statement to them. For a disconnected compact source apply the theorem independently to its finitely many nonconstant connected components, retaining each constant component, original genus, labels and map. Distinct components with the same image are not merged. For M1 nodal maps apply it to each nonconstant smooth domain-normalization component only as additional factorization data: retain the original domain graph, node pairings, ordered marks, cover, degree, ramification and all map-automorphism arrows. Images of distinct marks or flags under π can coincide, so this construction does not automatically give a stable pointed curve or a morphism obtained by replacing the original stable map by v. Retain deck transformations and the original map automorphisms; no multiple-cover multiplicity is discarded.

On M2's simple locus the degree-one comparison identifies the factor surface with the original source, so M2's actual moving-domain index and regularity theorem apply with the original genus and class. For a covered map retain that original genus/class in the Fredholm complex; normalization does not make the covered operator regular. As a formal unmarked index check, put D(u)=(m−3)(2−2g(Σ))+2c₁(u) for dim_R X=2m and obtain D(u)=dD(v)−(m−3)R from the preceding identities. This is an index identity, not a dimension assertion for a multiple-cover moduli space. Construction under isomorphisms is not an assertion that factor surfaces or degrees form a smooth family through arbitrary degenerations; M1–M3 retain their original varying-domain and stable-map data. HF F2.6b consumes this general construction and proves its sphere-normalization and simple-sphere quotient application; it is not a second owner of the generic quotient construction.
