# Roadmap: p-adically closed fields

**Summary.** A *p-valuation of p-rank d* on a field `K` of characteristic `0` is a valuation whose
ring `O` has residue characteristic `p` and `O/pO` of dimension `d` over `𝔽_p`. Then `d = e·f`,
with `e` the number of values in `[|p|, 1)` and `f` the residue degree. `K` is *p-adically closed*
when no proper algebraic extension carries a p-valuation of the same p-rank extending it. This is
the p-adic analogue of a real closed field, with the p-valuation in place of the ordering, and
Prestel–Roquette's theory follows Artin–Schreier's step by step. A p-valued field is p-adically
closed exactly when it is henselian and its value group is elementarily equivalent to `ℤ`. It
has a p-adic closure, unique up to isomorphism exactly when its value group is elementarily
equivalent to `ℤ`. Algebraic extensions of the same p-rank of a henselian p-valued field are
generated by radicals, and two of them are isomorphic exactly when the same elements of the base
become `n`-th powers in each.

The model theory follows. The theory of p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` is model complete
and decidable. Two such fields are elementarily equivalent exactly when their relative algebraic
closures of `ℚ` are isomorphic, so every one is elementarily equivalent to a finite extension of
`ℚ_p` of degree `d`, and the completions of the theory correspond to these extensions. With constants for
an `𝔽_p`-basis of `O/pO` and predicates for the `n`-th powers, the theory eliminates quantifiers;
for `d = 1` this is Macintyre's theorem on `ℚ_p`. The p-adic Kochen operator `γ` plays the part of
the square: a valuation is a p-valuation of bounded ramification and residue degree exactly when
it is integral on the image of `γ`, the ring of a henselian unramified p-valuation is that image,
and the ring generated by the image of `γ` gives the p-adic analogues of Artin's theorem on
positive definite functions and of the real Nullstellensatz. Finally the absolute Galois group of
a p-adically closed field is that of a finite extension of `ℚ_p`.

The roadmap builds on AxKochenErshov, which supplies the one-sorted language of valued fields, the
interpretation of the residue field and value group, and the finitely ramified AKE theorem; on
SaturationAndQuantifierElimination, which supplies saturated extensions, model completeness,
quantifier elimination tests, the theory of `ℤ`-groups and decidability; and on
LocalFieldsRamificationPartII, which supplies henselization, coarsening and Krasner's lemma. It
feeds two planned roadmaps of the same series: GaloisCharacterizationOfPadicallyClosedFields,
which proves the converse of Layer 7, and CiFieldsAndArtinsConjecture. It is the general form of
the Macintyre elimination that LogicAndDefinabilityInNumberTheory states over a fixed finite
extension of `ℚ_p`.

Layer 0 sets up p-valuations and their behaviour in finite extensions. Layer 1 is the algebra of
p-adically closed fields and p-adic closures. Layer 2 is Prestel–Roquette's embedding theorem
into saturated p-adically closed fields. Layer 3 draws the model theory: model completeness,
elementary equivalence, completions and decidability. Layer 4 is quantifier elimination. Layer 5
is the Kochen operator and formally p-adic fields, Layer 6 function fields over p-adically closed
fields, and Layer 7 the absolute Galois group.

## Conventions

- **Valued fields.** As in AxKochenErshov: a valued field is a field `K` of characteristic `0`
  with a Mathlib `ValuationSubring K`, written `V` or `O`, maximal ideal `𝔪`, residue field
  `IsLocalRing.ResidueField V`, and value group `valueGroup V`, the units of Mathlib's
  `ValuationSubring.ValueGroup`, written multiplicatively with `|x| ≤ 1` on `V`. Additive
  statements of Prestel–Roquette are translated: `v(x) ≥ 0` becomes `|x| ≤ 1`. An extension of
  valued fields `(L, W)/(K, V)` has `W.comap (algebraMap K L) = V`. Henselian means
  `HenselianLocalRing V`.
- **p-rank.** `p` is a fixed prime. The p-rank of `V` is `d` when
  `Nat.card (V ⧸ Ideal.span {(p : V)}) = p ^ d` with `d ≥ 1`. This forces `p ∈ 𝔪`, so the residue
  characteristic is `p`, and `O/pO` finite. The ramification index `e` is the number of values `γ`
  with `|p| ≤ γ < 1`, and the residue degree `f` is `[k : 𝔽_p]`.
- **p-adically closed** is relative to the p-rank: `(K, V)` of p-rank `d` is p-adically closed
  when every algebraic extension `(L, W)` of p-rank `d` has `L = K`. Prestel–Roquette p.33.
- **`ℤ`-groups** are SaturationAndQuantifierElimination's `IsPresburgerGroup` (its 3.3): ordered
  abelian groups elementarily equivalent to `ℤ`. Prestel–Roquette's condition "`vK` is a
  `ℤ`-group" (p.85) is `IsPresburgerGroup (valueGroup V)`.
- **Languages.** The language of valued fields is AxKochenErshov's `valLang`: `Language.ring` with
  `x ∣ y :↔ ∃ a ∈ V, a * x = y`. Prestel–Roquette use a predicate for `O` (p.83); it is
  `1 ∣ x`, so the two languages have the same quantifier-free definable sets. The language of
  Layer 4, `pcLang d`, adds to `valLang` constants `u₁, …, u_d` and unary predicates `Pₙ` for
  `n ≥ 1`, read `Pₙ(x) :↔ ∃ y, yⁿ = x`.
- **Finite extensions of `ℚ_p`** are fields `F` with `[Algebra ℚ_[p] F] [FiniteDimensional ℚ_[p] F]`,
  with the valuation subring of the unique prolongation of `ℤ_p`: the `W` with
  `W.comap (algebraMap ℚ_[p] F) = padicValuationSubring p`.
- **The Kochen operator** of type `(e, f)` is
  `γ(x) = p⁻¹ · [(x^q − x) − (x^q − x)⁻¹]^(−e)`, `q = p^f` (Prestel–Roquette p.92). It has a pole
  where `(x^q − x)² = 1`. In Lean, where `0⁻¹ = 0`, `γ` takes the value `0` there, and statements
  that need `γ` to be finite carry the condition `(x^q − x)² ≠ 1` explicitly. Over a p-valued base
  `k` with prime element `π` the operator is `π⁻¹ · [(x^q − x) − (x^q − x)⁻¹]^(−e)` with
  `q = #k̄^f` (p.95).

## Layer 0: p-valuations

### 0.1 Definitions

`IsPValuation p d V`: `K` has characteristic `0` and `V` has p-rank `d ≥ 1`. `IsFormallyPadic p d K`:
some valuation subring of `K` is a p-valuation of p-rank `d` (p.92). A p-valuation is *of type
`(e, f)`* when its ramification index is at most `e` and its residue degree divides `f` (p.93).
API:

- `d = e·f` (p.15), and conversely a valued field of characteristic `0` with residue
  characteristic `p` and finite `e` and `f` is p-valued;
- the smallest value `< 1` exists, `π` with `|π|` that value is a prime element, and `𝔪 = πO`
  (pp.13–14);
- `IsPValuationOfType p e f V` is equivalent to: `|p| < 1`, at most `e` values in `[|p|, 1)`,
  and `x^(p^f) = x` on the residue field.

**Checks.**

- `ℚ_p` with `ℤ_p` has `e = f = d = 1`; `ℚ_p(√p)` has `e = 2`, `f = 1`, `d = 2`; the unramified
  quadratic extension of `ℚ_p` has `e = 1`, `f = 2`, `d = 2`.
- `ℚ` with `ℤ_(p)` is a p-valuation of p-rank `1`.
- The Gauss extension of `ℤ_p` to `ℚ_p(t)` has residue field `𝔽_p(t)`, so `O/pO` is infinite and
  it is not a p-valuation; `Nat.card` is `0`, which is no power `p ^ d` with `d ≥ 1`.

### 0.2 The coarse valuation and the core field

Let `Δ ≤ Γ` be the convex subgroup generated by `|p|`. The coarsening of `V` by `Δ`
(LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §5.1, as used in AxKochenErshov §5.6) is the *coarse valuation*
`V̇ = V[1/p]`, the smallest overring of `V` in which `p` is a unit (pp.25–27). Its residue field is
the *core field* `K⁰`, which carries the induced *core valuation* with value group `Δ ≅ ℤ`.

- `K⁰` is p-valued with the same `e`, `f` and `d` as `K`, of characteristic `0` (p.27);
- `V̇` is trivial if and only if `Γ = Δ`;
- the completion of `K⁰` for the core valuation is a finite extension of `ℚ_p` of degree `d`
  (Rem. 2.12, pp.31–32), so `K` has a place into such an extension; conversely a place of `K` into
  an extension of `ℚ_p` of degree `d` gives a p-valuation of p-rank dividing `d`.

### 0.3 Extensions

For an extension `(L, W)/(K, V)` of valued fields with `V` a p-valuation:

- `W` is a p-valuation exactly when the initial index `i[Γ_L : Γ_K]`, the index of the parts of
  the value groups inside `Δ`, and the residue degree are finite. Then `e_L = e_K · i[Γ_L : Γ_K]`
  and `d_L = i[Γ_L : Γ_K]·[k_L : k_K]·d_K`, which holds whenever `[L : K]` is finite
  (Lem. 2.7, p.24);
- `i[Γ_L : Γ_K]` is the ramification index of the core fields, and
  `[Γ_L : Γ_K] = [Γ̇_L : Γ̇_K] · i[Γ_L : Γ_K]` (Lem. 2.8, p.28);
- for `K` henselian and `L/K` finite, `[L : K] = [Γ_L : Γ_K]·[k_L : k_K]` (Lem. 2.9, p.29);
- *the basic criterion* (Cor. 2.10, pp.30–31): for `K` henselian and `L/K` finite, `K` and `L`
  have the same p-rank if and only if `[L : K] = [Γ̇_L : Γ̇_K]`. Then `Γ_L/Γ_K = Γ̇_L/Γ̇_K` and
  `[L : K] = [Γ_L : Γ_K]`, whose converse fails (Rem. 2.11, p.31).

The henselization of a p-valued field (LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §1.3) is an immediate
extension, so it is p-valued of the same p-rank.

**Checks.**

- `ℚ_p(√p)/ℚ_p` has `[L : K] = 2 = [Γ_L : Γ_K]` but `Γ̇` trivial on both sides, so the p-rank
  changes, from `1` to `2`, as Cor. 2.10 says.
- For `K = ℚ_p((t))` with the valuation composed of the `t`-adic and the p-adic one,
  `L = K(√t)` has `[Γ̇_L : Γ̇_K] = 2 = [L : K]` and the same p-rank `1`.

`HenselianLocalRing`, `Nat.card`, `Padic`; LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §1.3 and §5.1;
AxKochenErshov §5.6.

## Layer 1: p-adically closed fields

### 1.1 Characterization

`IsPadicallyClosed p d V`. *Characterization* (Thm. 3.1, p.34): a p-valued field is p-adically
closed if and only if it is henselian and its value group is a `ℤ`-group, equivalently the coarse
value group `Γ̇` is divisible.

- A finite extension of `ℚ_p` of degree `d` is p-adically closed of p-rank `d`: it is complete,
  hence henselian, with value group `ℤ`.
- The henselization of a number field at a prime over `p` is p-adically closed, of p-rank the
  local degree.
- Being p-adically closed of p-rank `d` is preserved by isomorphism of valued fields.

### 1.2 p-adic closures

A *p-adic closure* of a p-valued `K` is an algebraic extension of the same p-rank that is
p-adically closed; one exists by Zorn's lemma (p.33).

- *Uniqueness* (Thm. 3.2, p.37): if `Γ_K` is not a `ℤ`-group, `K` has non-isomorphic p-adic
  closures. If it is, the henselization `Kʰ` is p-adically closed by 1.1 and is the unique p-adic
  closure up to `K`-isomorphism.

**Checks.**

- `ℚ` with `ℤ_(p)` has a unique p-adic closure, its henselization, with value group `ℤ`.
- `ℚ_p((t))` with the composed valuation has value group `ℤ ×ₗ ℤ`, not a `ℤ`-group. It has a
  p-adic closure containing a square root of `t` and one containing a square root of `ut`, for
  `p` odd and `u ∈ ℤ` a non-square modulo `p`; none contains both, since `√u` would raise the
  residue degree, so the two are not `K`-isomorphic.

### 1.3 Relative algebraic closure

- *Algebraically closed subfields* (Thm. 3.4, p.38): if `L` is p-adically closed and `K ⊆ L` is a
  subfield, with the induced valuation, algebraically closed in `L`, then `K` is p-adically closed
  of the same p-rank. Conversely a p-adically closed subfield of the same p-rank is algebraically
  closed in `L`.
- The three steps of the proof are targets (Lem. 3.5, pp.39–44): for `L` henselian p-valued with
  residue field of order `q`, the roots of `X^q − X` are a set of representatives of the residue
  field, algebraic over every subfield; if `k_K = k_L`, `K` contains a prime element of `L` that
  is a root of an Eisenstein polynomial over `K`; and if `K` and `L` have the same p-rank, every
  `α ∈ Γ_L` with `αⁿ ∈ Γ_K` is the value of some `t ∈ L` with `tⁿ ∈ K`.
- For `L` henselian of the same p-rank as `K ⊆ L` and `|b| ∈ Γ_K`, `b = c·uⁿ` with `c ∈ K` and
  `u` a unit of `L` (Lem. 3.6, pp.45–46).
- An algebraic extension of the same p-rank of a henselian `K` with the same value group is `K`
  (Lem. 3.7, p.48).

### 1.4 Radical structure

*Radical Structure Theorem* (Thm. 3.8, pp.48–49): let `K` be henselian p-valued and `L/K`
algebraic of the same p-rank, and `T ⊆ Lˣ` the group of radicals, the `t` with `tⁿ ∈ K` for some
`n ≥ 1`. Then `L = K(T)`, `|T| = Γ_L`, the valuation induces `T/Kˣ ≅ Γ_L/Γ_K`, and
`[T : Kˣ] = [L : K]` when `L/K` is finite. For `L/K` finite, `L ≅ K[X₁, …, X_r]/(X_i^{n_i} − c_i)`
with `c_i ∈ K` and `∏ n_i = [L : K]` (Cor. 3.9, p.53).

### 1.5 Embeddings and isomorphisms of algebraic extensions

- *Algebraic Embedding Theorem* (Thm. 3.10, pp.53–54): let `L/K` be an algebraic extension of
  p-valued fields of the same p-rank with `L` henselian, and `L'` a henselian valued extension of
  `K`. Then `L` embeds into `L'` over `K` as a valued field if and only if `K ∩ Lⁿ ⊆ L'ⁿ` for all
  `n ≥ 1`.
- *Isomorphism Theorem* (Cor. 3.11, p.57): two henselian algebraic extensions `L`, `L'` of `K` of
  the p-rank of `K` are `K`-isomorphic as valued fields if and only if `K ∩ Lⁿ = K ∩ L'ⁿ` for all
  `n ≥ 1`. In particular this decides when two p-adic closures of `K` are isomorphic.
- *Subfield structure* (Thm. 3.12, p.59): for `K` henselian and `M` a p-adic closure of `K`,
  `L ↦ Γ_L/Γ_K` is a lattice isomorphism from the intermediate fields of `M/K` to the subgroups
  of `(ℚ/ℤ) ⊗ Γ̇_K`, with `[L : K] = #(Γ_L/Γ_K)` when `L/K` is finite. The lattice does not depend
  on `M`.

**Checks.**

- `ℚ_p(√p)` and `ℚ_p(√(up))`, for `p` odd and `u ∈ ℤ` a non-square modulo `p`, are henselian
  quadratic extensions of `ℚ_p` of p-rank `2`; `p` is a square in the first only, so by Cor. 3.11
  they are not isomorphic.
- For `K = ℚ_p`, `(ℚ/ℤ) ⊗ Γ̇_K = 0`, so `ℚ_p` is its own unique p-adic closure, as 1.1 says.

`IsPresburgerGroup` (3.3); LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §1.3; Layer 0.

## Layer 2: the general embedding theorem

Saturation is SaturationAndQuantifierElimination's `IsSaturated` (its Layer 0) in `valLang`.

- For `L/K` an extension of p-valued fields of the same p-rank with `L` henselian, `K` is
  algebraically closed in `L` if and only if `K` is henselian and `Γ_L/Γ_K` is torsion-free
  (Lem. 4.2, p.64).
- *Embedding Theorem for regular extensions* (Thm. 4.3, p.65): let `L/K` be an extension of
  p-valued fields of the same p-rank, `K` henselian and `Γ_L/Γ_K` torsion-free. Then `L` embeds
  over `K` into every p-adically closed extension `L'` of `K` that is `κ`-saturated for some
  `κ > #L`.
- *General Embedding Theorem* (Thm. 4.1, p.63): let `L/K` be an extension of p-valued fields of
  the same p-rank with `L` henselian, and `L'` a p-adically closed extension of `K` that is
  `κ`-saturated for some `κ > #L`. Then `L` embeds over `K` into `L'` if and only if
  `K ∩ Lⁿ ⊆ L'ⁿ` for all `n ≥ 1`. The condition involves only the relative algebraic closure of
  `K` in `L`, and is empty when `L/K` is regular.

The proof reduces Thm. 4.3 to transcendence degree `1` (Rems. 4.4–4.6), using that an extension
embeds once each finitely generated subextension does, by saturation, and treats the rational
case through Props. 4.10A–B. A henselian `L'` whose value group satisfies
`ℚ ⊗ Γ_{L'} = ℚ + Γ_{L'}` also serves (Rem. 4.12, p.82); a merely henselian `L'` does not.

### Dependencies

SaturationAndQuantifierElimination Layer 0; Layers 0–1.

## Layer 3: model theory

### 3.1 Axiomatization

In `valLang` the following are axiomatized, each by one sentence or one sentence for each `n`
(pp.83–86): p-rank `d` ("there are `u₁, …, u_d ∈ O` forming an `𝔽_p`-basis of `O/pO`");
henselianity (AxKochenErshov's `Theory.henselianValuedField`); the value group a `ℤ`-group ("for
every `a` there is `x` with `|a| = |πⁱxⁿ|` for some `0 ≤ i < n`", `π` a prime element). By 1.1
the p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` form an elementary class, axiomatized by a recursive
set `Theory.pAdicallyClosed p d` (a proposed name).

### 3.2 Model completeness

The theory of p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` is model complete (Thm. 5.1, p.86): every
`valLang`-embedding between p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` is elementary. The proof
applies Robinson's test (SaturationAndQuantifierElimination §2.5) with Thm. 4.3, using that a
p-adically closed field is algebraically closed in every p-valued extension of the same p-rank.

### 3.3 Elementary equivalence and completions

- *Elementary Equivalence Theorem* (Thm. 5.5, p.90): if `L`, `L'` are p-adically closed
  extensions of a p-valued `K` of the same p-rank and `K ∩ Lⁿ = K ∩ L'ⁿ` for all `n ≥ 1`, then
  `L ≡ L'` over `K`; the converse holds trivially. The proof identifies the relative algebraic
  closures of `K` by Cor. 3.11, which are p-adically closed by Thm. 3.4 and elementary in `L` and
  `L'` by Thm. 5.1.
- For `d = 1`, with `K = ℚ`: p-adically closed fields `L`, `L'` of p-rank `1` are elementarily
  equivalent if and only if `ℚ ∩ Lⁿ = ℚ ∩ L'ⁿ` for all `n ≥ 1`. For `d > 1` the theorem does not
  apply with `K = ℚ`, whose p-rank is `1`.
- The relative algebraic closure `L₀` of `ℚ` in a p-adically closed `L` of p-rank `d` is
  p-adically closed of p-rank `d`, a finite extension of the henselization of `ℚ` at `p`, hence
  the henselization of a number field `ℚ(x)` with `g(x) = 0`, `g ∈ ℚ[X]`, and `L₀ ≼ L` (proof of
  Thm. 5.2, pp.87–88).
- *Classification of completions*: p-adically closed fields `L`, `L'` of p-rank `d` are
  elementarily equivalent if and only if `L₀ ≅ L'₀` as valued fields. One direction is
  `L₀ ≼ L`; for the other, `L₀` is determined up to isomorphism by a sentence `∃ x, g(x) = 0`
  together with the axioms, `g` chosen close enough to a minimal polynomial of a generator for
  Krasner's lemma (LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §4.1) to apply (pp.87–89).
- Every p-adically closed field of p-rank `d` is elementarily equivalent to a finite extension
  `F` of `ℚ_p` of degree `d`, namely the completion of `L₀`, since `L₀ ≼ F` by the same argument;
  conversely a field elementarily equivalent in `valLang` to such an `F` is p-adically closed of
  p-rank `d`, by 3.1. Two finite extensions of `ℚ_p` of degree `d` are elementarily equivalent if
  and only if they are isomorphic. So the completions of `Theory.pAdicallyClosed p d` correspond to
  the isomorphism classes of extensions of `ℚ_p` of degree `d`, and there are finitely many
  (MassFormula's finiteness for the totally ramified part, Completed/LocalFieldsRamification for
  the unramified part).
- With AxKochenErshov §5.8: p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` are elementarily equivalent if
  and only if their residue rings `O/𝔪ⁿ`, which are finite, are isomorphic for every `n`; all
  `ℤ`-groups are elementarily equivalent (SaturationAndQuantifierElimination §3.3).

### 3.4 Decidability

- The theory of p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` is decidable (Thm. 5.2, p.87). It has
  finitely many completions (3.3), each axiomatized by the recursive axioms of 3.1 and one sentence
  `∃ x, g(x) = 0`, hence decidable by SaturationAndQuantifierElimination §4.3, and a sentence holds
  in every model exactly when it holds in each completion.
- For `F` the completion of a number field at a prime over `p`, of local degree `d`, `Th(F)` is
  axiomatized by `Theory.pAdicallyClosed p d` and one sentence `∃ x, g(x) = 0`, `g ∈ ℚ[X]`, and is
  decidable (Cor. 5.3, p.89).
- The theory of unramified p-adically closed fields of p-rank `d` is complete and decidable
  (Cor. 5.4, p.89), there being one unramified extension of `ℚ_p` of each degree.

**Checks.**

- For `d = 1`, Cor. 5.4 says the p-adically closed fields of p-rank `1` are those elementarily
  equivalent to `ℚ_p`, the axiomatization of AxKochenErshov §6.1.
- `ℚ_p` and `ℚ_p(√p)` are both p-adically closed but not elementarily equivalent: the p-rank is
  expressed by a sentence.
- The henselization `ℚʰ` of `ℚ` at `p` is an elementary substructure of `ℚ_p`.

## Layer 4: quantifier elimination

`pcLang d` is `valLang` with constants `u₁, …, u_d` and predicates `Pₙ`, `n ≥ 1`. A p-adically
closed field `K` of p-rank `d` is a `pcLang d`-structure once `u₁, …, u_d ∈ O` are chosen to form an
`𝔽_p`-basis of `O/pO` and `Pₙ` is read as the set of `n`-th powers. In this language every
substructure of a p-valued field of p-rank `d` is again p-valued of p-rank `d` (p.84).

- *Quantifier Elimination Theorem* (Thm. 5.6, p.91): the theory of p-adically closed fields of
  p-rank `d` in `pcLang d` eliminates quantifiers. Thm. 5.5 is substructure completeness, and the
  criterion `isQFEquivalent_iff_realize_iff_of_embeddings` of SaturationAndQuantifierElimination
  §2.2 turns it into elimination.
- *Macintyre's theorem* (Macintyre Thm. 1, p.606): for `d = 1` the constant `u₁ = 1` is already
  named, and the theory of `ℚ_p` eliminates quantifiers in `valLang` with the `Pₙ`.
- For p-adically closed fields of p-rank `1`, from Macintyre §2 (pp.608–609): every definable
  subset of `Kⁿ` is a finite union of sets `X ∩ Y` with `X` open and `Y` the zero set of a
  polynomial; an infinite definable subset of `K` has nonempty interior (Thm. 2); the
  model-theoretic algebraic closure of a set is its relative field-theoretic algebraic closure
  (Thm. 3); and the theory is algebraically bounded (Thm. 4).

**Checks.**

- The predicates `Pₙ` are needed: in `ℚ_p` the nonzero squares are infinite and coinfinite, while
  a quantifier-free `valLang` formula in one variable without `Pₙ` defines a finite Boolean
  combination of zero sets and discs. Macintyre's appendix (pp.609–610) shows more: in an
  `ℵ₁`-saturated model, `t²` and `k·t²`, `k` a non-square unit and `|t| < |p|ⁿ` for all `n`,
  satisfy the same formulas of the valued-field language quantifying only over the value group,
  yet only the first is a square.
- The constants are needed for `d > 1`: in `ℚ_p(√p)` the `valLang`-substructure generated by `∅`
  is `ℤ` with the induced divisibility, inside `ℚ` of p-rank `1`, so without the constants a
  substructure of a field of p-rank `2` need not have p-rank `2`.
- `{pⁿ : n ∈ ℤ} ⊆ ℚ_p` is infinite with empty interior, so it is not definable (Thm. 2): a
  cross-section is not definable (Macintyre §2.4).

## Layer 5: the Kochen operator and formally p-adic fields

Over a p-valued base `k` with prime element `π`, and `K/k` any extension, `O[γK]` is the subring of
`K` generated by the valuation ring `O` of `k` and the finite values of `γ`. The *γ-Kochen ring* is
`R = {b/(1 + πc) : b, c ∈ O[γK], 1 + πc ≠ 0}` (p.102).

- *Lem. 6.1* (p.93): a valuation of a field of characteristic `0` is a p-valuation of type
  `(e, f)` if and only if `|p| < 1` and `γ` has no pole and is integral on `K`; relative form over
  `k` (Lem. 6.2, p.95).
- *Characterization of formally p-adic fields* (Thm. 6.4, p.99): `K` is formally p-adic over `k`
  of relative type `(e, f)` if and only if `π` is not a unit of `O[γK]`, the analogue of "`−1` is
  not a sum of squares". A valuation of `K` is such a p-valuation exactly when it lies over
  `O[γK]` and is centred over `π` (Cor. 6.5, p.101).
- `Frac R = K` (Lem. 6.6, through Merckel's lemma, pp.102 and 153); if `π` is a unit of `O[γK]`
  then `R = K` (Lem. 6.7).
- *Ideals of the Kochen ring* (Thm. 6.8, p.103): if `K` is formally p-adic over `k` of relative type
  `(e, f)`, then `π` is not a unit of `R`, every maximal ideal of `R` contains `π`, every prime
  ideal containing `π` is maximal, and the p-valuations of relative type `(e, f)` are the
  valuations lying over `R` and centred at a maximal ideal. Their holomorphy ring is the integral
  closure of `R` (Cor. 6.9, p.105).
- *Spectral Structure Theorem* (Thm. 6.14, p.114): for `e = 1`, `R` is integrally closed and is the
  holomorphy ring of the p-valuations of relative type `(1, f)`; maximal ideals `P` of `R`
  correspond bijectively to them, with `O_v = R_P`. `R` is then a Prüfer ring (Cor. 6.16, p.117).
- *Uniqueness and definability* (Thm. 6.15, p.115): if `K` is henselian for a p-valuation `v` of
  relative type `(e, f)`, then `v` is the only p-valuation of `K`; if moreover `e = 1`, then
  `O_v = γK`, the image of `γ`. So the valuation ring of a henselian unramified p-valued field is
  existentially `∅`-definable in the ring language.
- *Principal Ideal Theorem* (Thm. 6.17, p.118): for a nonempty set of valuations of `K` with finite
  residue fields of bounded order, the holomorphy ring is a Bézout ring with fraction field `K`.

**Checks.**

- `γ_{1,1}` maps `ℚ_p` onto `ℤ_p` (Thm. 6.15).
- In `ℚ_p(√p)`, `|β(√p)| = |√p|` for `β(x) = [(x^p − x) − (x^p − x)⁻¹]⁻¹`, so `γ_{1,1}(√p)` has
  absolute value `|p|^(−1/2) > 1`, while `γ_{2,1}(√p)` is a unit: the type-`(1, 1)` operator does
  not serve ramification `2`, as Lem. 6.1 says.
- `ℚ` is formally p-adic and `ℂ` is not: a p-valuation has finite residue field, while every
  valuation of an algebraically closed field has an algebraically closed, hence infinite, residue
  field.

## Layer 6: function fields over p-adically closed fields

Let `k` be p-adically closed of p-rank `d` with valuation ring `O`, prime element `π` and residue
field of order `q`, and `γ` the type-`(1, 1)` `π`-adic Kochen operator over `k`; by Thm. 6.15
`O = γk`. Let `K/k` be a function field in `n` variables. A *rational place* of `K/k` is a place
over `k` with residue field `k`, given by a valuation subring of `K` containing `k` with residue
field `k`. For `u = (u₁, …, u_r)` and `z = (z₁, …, z_s)` in `K`, the *basic subset* `S_u^z` is the
set of rational places `P` with `u_j P ∈ O` and `z_i P ≠ ∞` (pp.124–125). `K` is formally p-adic
over `O[u]` when a p-valuation of p-rank `d` of `K` extends `O` and contains `u`. `R_u` is the
Kochen ring over `O[u]`.

- *Place Existence Theorem* (Thm. 7.2, pp.125–126): `S_u^z ≠ ∅` if and only if `K` is formally
  p-adic over `O[u]`. The proof uses model completeness (Thm. 5.1).
- *Holomorphy rings* (Thm. 7.4, p.136): the ring of functions holomorphic on a nonempty `S_u^z` is
  `R_u·k[z]`.
- *Nullstellensatz* (Thm. 7.6, p.143): for `g, f₁, …, f_m` holomorphic on a nonempty `S_u^z`, `g`
  vanishes at every common zero in `S_u^z` of the `f_i` if and only if `g^N = Σ A_i f_i` with `A_i`
  holomorphic on `S_u^z`.
- *Integral definite functions* (Thm. 7.7, p.144): `f ∈ K` takes values in `O` at every place of a
  nonempty `S_u^z` if and only if `f ∈ R_u`.
- *Simple points* (Thm. 7.8, p.145): for an affine variety `V` over `k` with function field `K`, if
  `V_u(k) = {a ∈ V(k) : u_j(a) ∈ O}` contains a simple point then `K` is formally p-adic over
  `O[u]`, and if `K` is formally p-adic over `O[u]` then the simple points of `V_u(k)` are Zariski
  dense in `V`. In particular `V` has a simple `k`-point if and only if `k(V)` is formally p-adic
  over `O`.
- *Polynomial Nullstellensatz* (Thm. 7.9, Cor. 7.10, pp.146–148; Jarden–Roquette): for
  `f₁, …, f_m, g ∈ k[X₁, …, X_n]`, `g` vanishes at every common zero of the `f_i` in `kⁿ` if and
  only if `g^N = Σ A_i f_i` with `A_i ∈ R·k[X]`, `R` the Kochen ring of `k(X)`.
- *Hilbert's seventeenth problem* (Thm. 7.11, p.149): if `f, g ∈ k[X₁, …, X_n]` and `f(a)/g(a) ∈ O`
  whenever `g(a) ≠ 0`, `a ∈ kⁿ`, then `f/g = t'/(1 + πt)` with `t, t' ∈ O[γk(X)]`. For a p-valued
  `k`, not necessarily p-adically closed, the same representation holds for every integral
  definite function of `k(X₁, …, X_n)` when `k` is dense in its p-adic closure; conversely, if it
  holds for every integral definite function of `k(X₁)`, then `k` is dense in its p-adic closure
  (Thm. 7.12, p.150).

**Checks.**

- `X² + Y² + 1 = 0` has a simple `ℚ_p`-point for `p` odd, so its function field over `ℚ_p` is
  formally p-adic; over `ℝ` the same curve has no real point, and its function field is not
  formally real.
- For `k = ℚ` with `ℤ_(p)`, dense in `ℚ_p`, Thm. 7.12 writes every integral definite function of
  `ℚ(X₁, …, X_n)` as `t'/(1 + pt)` with `t, t' ∈ ℤ_(p)[γℚ(X)]` (pp.151–152).

## Layer 7: absolute Galois groups

For `K` p-adically closed of p-rank `d`, let `K₀` be the relative algebraic closure of `ℚ` in `K`
and `F` the completion of `K₀`, a finite extension of `ℚ_p` of degree `d` (3.3). Then
`G_K ≅ G_{K₀} ≅ G_F` as profinite groups (Efrat Rem. 4.4, citing Prestel–Roquette). So the
absolute Galois groups of p-adically closed fields are exactly those of the finite extensions of
`ℚ_p`.

- `G_K → G_{K₀}` is surjective since `K₀` is algebraically closed in `K`, and injective since `K₀`
  has finitely many extensions `K₀(θ₁), …, K₀(θ_m)` of degree at most `n`, and the sentence
  "every monic polynomial of degree `n` has a root in some `K(θ_i)`" holds in `K₀`, hence in `K`
  since `K₀ ≼ K`; so `K̄ = K·ℚᵃˡᵍ`.
- `G_{K₀} ≅ G_F` is the decompletion of LocalFieldsRamificationPartII §4.1 for the henselian
  field `K₀` of height one.

**Checks.**

- For `K = ℚʰ`, the henselization of `ℚ` at `p`, `G_K ≅ G_{ℚ_p}`.
- Layer 7 does not reverse to "`G_K ≅ G_F` implies `K ≡ F`": Jarden and Ritter give
  non-isomorphic finite extensions `F`, `F'` of `ℚ_p` of the same degree with `G_F ≅ G_{F'}`, and
  by 3.3 they are not elementarily equivalent.
