# Roadmap: automorphic forms on reductive groups

**Summary.** Build the analytic and representation-theoretic interfaces for automorphic forms on a connected reductive group over a number field: smooth adelic functions and their growth, Harish-Chandra modules and their globalizations, the adelic Hecke module and its local factors, constant-term calculus, cohomological and coherent representations, integral coefficients, and classical dictionaries. The general reductive group is the main object; locally convex compact-finite density and its Banach comparison (`gFiniteSubmodule_dense`) are principal analytic milestones. Explicit calculations for tori, SL₂, GL₂ and GSp₄ fix the conventions used by applications to automorphic cohomology and modularity.

## Scope and ownership

The adelic group, arithmetic quotient, measures, heights and reduction theory come from AdelicAlgebraicGroups. SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups supplies the nonarchimedean representation categories, compact-open Hecke algebras and spherical results. RepresentationTheory/LieGroups and RepresentationTheory/LieHighestWeight supply Lie groups, Cartan decompositions, enveloping algebras and the Harish-Chandra parametrization of the centre for semisimple Lie algebras; its extension to reductive Lie algebras is built here (§2.2). RepresentationTheory/SmoothBanachRepresentations supplies Lie test functions, Banach smooth vectors, their derivative topology and differentiated actions. Sections 0.2 and 2.7 specialize those constructions and supply the adelic and reductive comparisons. These objects are used with their existing names.

ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces supplies the shared real-points comparison, compatible Lie/group pairs and relative cochains, automorphic forms, cusp forms and their discreteness, algebraic weights and Wigner's lemma. This roadmap extends those interfaces. In particular, it does not create a second automorphic-form carrier or a second Chevalley–Eilenberg complex. The smooth space without the compact-finiteness condition is distinguished from its compact-finite part throughout.

QuaternionicModularForms §§2.1 and 3.1 owns the abstract level-form carrier, evaluation/reconstruction, coefficient and level maps, trace and opposite Hecke action for arbitrary groups. The level and central-character algebraic modular forms in §§7.4–7.6 specialize that carrier. This roadmap supplies the inverse-action Hecke adapter, Gross's rational-covariance comparison, reductive arithmetic finiteness and archimedean realization. The topology-free prefix of §2.1 owns the common algebraic finite-orbit-span interface. Its analytic part constructs complete locally convex integration, projectors and density, comparing the Banach case with the compact-group density input of SmoothBanachRepresentations SB4; CompactGroups on main supplies Haar integration, finite-dimensional projectors and Peter–Weyl. Sections 2.3–2.4 construct general normalized real smooth induction and real-group classification. NoncompactQuantumSymmetry M4 supplies the normalized rank-one smooth models, their line bundles, Fréchet topologies, normalized intertwiners and endpoint comparisons. Section 2.3 identifies the SL₂ specialization of general induction with these supplied models by inversion. NoncompactQuantumSymmetry also owns invariant Hilbert forms and completions, SL₂ unitary-dual exhaustion, Plancherel, tensor products and little-group applications.

Section 4.5 owns the classical `MaassCuspForm` carrier, its normalized level-one Hecke-eigenform subtype and the Fourier, Hecke, reflection-parity and adelization comparisons. Its adelic image uses the supplied ALS.F.6 cusp-form carrier. Standard and adjoint L-series, local factors, completions, functional equations and L-value norm identities belong to the L-function consumer.

The new real representation theory includes admissibility, principal series, Casselman's embedding, Langlands classification, discrete series, archimedean parameters for GLₙ, Casselman–Wallach globalization and their compatibility with the global Hecke action. The coherent-cohomology part uses an explicit Hermitian group datum, rather than requiring a Shimura variety. It includes the Lie-theoretic Hodge stabilizer, its central quotient, root coordinates and component transport. Geometric comparison with coherent sheaf cohomology, toroidal compactifications, endoscopic transfer, trace formulas, completed cohomology and Galois representations belong to their respective consumers.

A discrete automorphic occurrence means a concrete irreducible closed subrepresentation of a central-character Hilbert space, together with its occurrence map. The small amount of discrete-space language needed below is defined directly from those subspaces; it does not assume a later spectral decomposition or a multiplicity formula. General automorphic representations are admissible subquotients, and need not be cuspidal, unitary or generic.

## Conventions

Let `F` be a number field, `𝔸=𝔸_F`, `G` a connected reductive `F`-group, `G∞=G(F⊗ℚℝ)`, and `Gf=G(𝔸_f)`. Fix a maximal compact `K⊂G∞`, including its components, and set `𝔤=Lie(G∞)⊗ℝℂ`, `𝔨=Lie(K)⊗ℝℂ`. Write `U=U(𝔤)` and `Z=Z(U)`. All representation spaces in the analytic theory are complex. Algebraic and integral coefficient fields are specified separately.

Right translation is `R(y)f(g)=f(gy)`, so `R(x)R(y)=R(xy)`. The derived operator is `R(X)f(g)=d/dt|₀ f(g exp(tX))`. For smoothing write `L_Xa(y)=d/dt|₀ a(exp(tX)y)`; this geometric left differentiation fixes the minus sign under integration by parts. Convolution is `(a*b)(g)=∫a(x)b(x⁻¹g)dx`, and its integrated action is `R(a)f(g)=∫a(y)f(gy)dy`. Fix Haar measures once; a compact-open idempotent is `e_J=vol(J)⁻¹1_J`. Quotient measures on unipotent arithmetic quotients have total mass one.

Choose the proper dual-augmented algebraic matrix height `H≥1` of AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.5, with `H(xy)≤H(x)H(y)` and the supplied polynomial inversion bound. This height is used for every fixed-exponent translation, Fréchet-representation and finite-Hecke assertion. Polynomially equivalent heights define the same unions of growth spaces, with changes of exponent; no equality of individual exponent pieces is implied. A matrix realization used to obtain a proper archimedean height must have closed image in the endomorphism space, or be enlarged by inverse/dual coordinates. Closedness merely inside GLₙ is insufficient.

Let `𝐆=Res_{F/ℚ}G`, let `A_𝐆` be its maximal ℚ-split central torus, and put `A∞=A_𝐆(ℝ)°`, with Lie algebra `𝔞`. This differs from the entire real split centre when restriction of scalars is involved. A fixed central character is taken on its specified central subgroup; Hilbert norms integrate over that subgroup's quotient, never over a noncompact central direction on which a nonzero unitary character has constant absolute value. A nonunitary central character is handled by a specified positive norm twist, which also specifies its weighted Hilbert norm.

A `(𝔤,K)`-module has a locally finite smooth `K`-action, the actual differentiated inclusion `Lie K→𝔤`, and adjoint covariance. Relative cochains use the full `K`. Cohomology for `K°` carries a `K/K°`-action, and taking invariants gives the full-`K` answer in characteristic zero. A split-centre-quotiented pair is written `(𝔤/𝔞,K°)`, equivalently `(𝔤,K°A∞)`, only after the tensor coefficient has trivial `𝔞`-action.

For a positive system, the Harish-Chandra parameter is the linear Weyl orbit of `λ+ρ` for a highest weight `λ`; the centre's unshifted highest-weight character instead uses the dot action. For GLₙ, `ρ_i=(n+1)/2−i` with `1≤i≤n`. The dual coefficient has highest weight `−w₀λ`. Integral character lattices come from the algebraic group, not merely its Lie algebra.

For SL₂ and GL₂ use `r_θ=((cosθ,sinθ),(-sinθ,cosθ))`, so holomorphic weight `k` gives the right character `e^{ikθ}`. Write `D_k(μ)=D_k(0)⊗|det|^{μ/2}`; the scalar matrix infinitesimal action is `μ`, and `D_k(0)` is unitary. The Casimir convention is `Ω=(H²+2XY+2YX)/4`, where `[H,X]=2X`, `[H,Y]=−2Y`, `[X,Y]=H`.

Layer 0 supplies functions, growth and smoothing. Layer 1 compares the supplied Lie cochains with differentiable group cohomology. Layer 2 develops real representations, with Langlands classification preceding and supplying inputs to the globalization theorem. Layer 3 constructs the global Hecke representation and its local factorization. Layer 4 gives constant-term compatibility and cuspidal examples. Construct the topology-free prefix of §2.1 independently of Layers 0–1 and before any analytic compact-finiteness theorem. The arithmetic objects imported above can be used from the beginning. A dependence on their foundational layer does not require the later automorphic comparisons of ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces; those comparisons consume the real and global representation theory developed here. The Lean companion states milestone theorems and light definitions expressible on existing library carriers, with proved examples distinguishing their hypotheses. The analytic spaces and representation categories specified in the prose are mathematical construction targets.

## Layer 0: Smooth adelic functions, growth and convolution

### 0.1 Smooth functions with a finite level

Construct `AdelicSmoothFunction(G)` as the union over compact open `J⊂Gf` of functions `f:G(𝔸)→ℂ` satisfying `f(gj)=f(g)` and whose restrictions to every archimedean slice are `C∞`. Use the actual smooth structure of the supplied real-points group. Global finite level is part of this carrier; separate local constancy at each point does not by itself supply a single `J`. The rational-left-invariant subspace is defined by `f(γg)=f(g)` for every `γ∈G(F)`. Evaluation and the inclusions between levels are injective, and right translation and all `U(𝔤)` derivatives are defined on these function spaces. (Getz, §5.1, pp.22–23; Borel–Jacquet, §4.1, p.194.)

The API includes `eval`, `ofLevel`, `level_mono`, `rightTranslate`, `rightTranslate_mul`, `derivedAction` and `leftInvariant`. If `f` is right `J`-invariant, `R(y)f` is right `y_fJy_f⁻¹`-invariant. The compact subgroup is conjugated in that direction because `(x y_f j y_f⁻¹)y_f=x y_f j`. Rational left invariance is preserved by right translation. Arbitrary left adelic translation does not act on the rational quotient. The smooth functions form a ℂ-algebra under pointwise multiplication; `X↦R(X)` is a Lie-algebra action of `𝔤` that commutes with every `R(y_f)`, `y_f∈Gf`, and `R(y∞)R(X)R(y∞)⁻¹=R(Ad(y∞)X)`. The finite topology is the restricted-product topology of `Gf` supplied by AdelicAlgebraicGroups Layer 1.

**Checks.**

- The constant function is smooth at every level and rational-left-invariant.
- On GL₁/ℚ, `|x∞|^s 1_{Ẑ×}(x_f)` is a smooth finite-level function. It need not be rational-left-invariant.
- The continuous p-adic function `x↦|x−1|_p` on `ℚ_p×` is not locally constant at `1`, and does not qualify.
- The archimedean function `x↦|log|x||` fails smoothness at `x=1`.
- In `S₃`, conjugating the subgroup generated by `(01)` by `(012)` gives the subgroup generated by `(12)`, whereas inverse conjugation gives that generated by `(02)`. This finite two-direction calculation also tests the level-translation formula.

The elementary predicates also make sense on any abstract group: `IsLeftInvariant(Γ,f)` means `f(γg)=f(g)` for `γ∈Γ`, and `IsLevelInvariant(J,f)` means `f(gu)=f(g)` for `u∈J`. Their API is `subgroup_mono`, `add`, `scalar_mul` and `rightTranslate`; only the latter changes the right subgroup, by the conjugation above. Define the real-bodied function `rightTranslate(y,f)(g)=f(gy)` on all functions, with `eval`, `one`, `mul` and the two invariance compatibilities. Smoothness and arithmetic diagonal embeddings enter only in the specialization.

**Checks.**

- For either predicate, constants satisfy every subgroup condition; the trivial subgroup imposes no condition on an arbitrary function.
- Full left invariance forces `f(g)=f(1)`; full right invariance forces the same conclusion. Neither predicate at the trivial subgroup forces constancy.
- `rightTranslate(1,f)=f`, and evaluation at `(g,y)` is exactly `f(gy)`. The noncommuting `S₃` calculation above distinguishes this from translation by `y⁻¹` and fixes the order in `R(y)R(z)=R(yz)`.
- On `GL₁/ℚ`, a function that is `C∞` in `x∞` and invariant under the kernel of `Ẑ×→(ℤ/N)×` is smooth: that kernel is a compact open subgroup.
- For an odd prime `p`, the characteristic function of the finite subgroup `μ_{p−1}⊂ℤ_p×`, pulled back to `GL₁(𝔸_ℚ)` through the `p`-component, is invariant under no open subgroup of `ℤ_p×`, so it is not smooth; local constancy fails at every point of `μ_{p−1}`.
- With `f(g)=g(2)` on `S₃`, `J` the stabilizer of `2` and `y=(0 1 2)`, `j=(0 1)`: `R(y)f(1)=0`, while `R(y)f(y⁻¹jy)=2`. So `R(y)f` is invariant under `yJy⁻¹` and not under `y⁻¹Jy`.

### 0.2 Test functions and their topology

Construct `AdelicTestFunction(G)=C_c∞(G∞)⊗ℂ C_c^{lc}(Gf)`, using SmoothBanachRepresentations SB1's `LieTestFunction` for `C_c∞(G∞)`, and identify it with compactly supported smooth finite-level functions on `G(𝔸)`. Finite compact support permits a common compact-open level; archimedean support is measured on the Lie group. Use SB1's fixed-support derivative seminorms and locally convex final topology for the archimedean factor. The finite factor is the inductive limit of finite-dimensional spaces of functions with prescribed compact support and a fixed bi-level. Give the tensor product its corresponding test-function topology and prove independence of cofinal support/level choices. Construct the adelic convolution and continuous integrated action from the supplied archimedean operations and finite-place operations, and involution `a*(g)=overline{a(g⁻¹)}` for the unimodular reductive group. (SmoothBanachRepresentations SB1; Getz, §§3.1–3.4, pp.16–19; Bernstein–Krötz, §§2.3–2.5, pp.7–12.)

Expose `toFunction`, `ext`, `tensor_eval`, `convolution`, `convolution_assoc`, `involution`, `support_convolution_subset` and `finiteIdempotent`. The support bound is `supp(a*b)⊂supp(a)supp(b)`. There is no archimedean delta function inside `C_c∞` when `dim G∞>0`; its action is approximated by test functions. Finite idempotents act as exact projections at the finite level. Choosing an integral model away from a finite set `S₀`, the finite factor is the directed union over finite `S⊇S₀` of `⊗_{v∈S}C_c^∞(G(F_v))⊗⊗_{v∉S}1_{G(O_v)}`, with the restricted-product Haar measure of AdelicAlgebraicGroups Layer 0. **Checks.**

- A pure tensor of an archimedean bump and `1_J` is a test function, and evaluating it gives the product of the two values.
- `e_J*e_J=e_J`, with the factor `vol(J)⁻¹`; the unnormalized characteristic function squares to `vol(J)1_J`.
- The constant function on a noncompact group has noncompact support and is excluded.
- For the trivial zero-dimensional group the test algebra is ℂ and does have a unit. Thus the no-unit statement requires positive archimedean dimension.
- With `vol(J)=2`, the coefficient of `1_J` in `e_J*e_J` is `(1/2)²·2=1/2`, whereas `1_J*1_J=2·1_J`. This detects the normalization even away from volume one.
- On `GL₁/ℚ`, the convolution square of `φ∞⊗1_{Ẑ×}` is `(φ∞*φ∞)⊗vol(Ẑ×)1_{Ẑ×}`; with `vol(Ẑ×)=1` the finite factor is unchanged.
- The zero function is a test function and acts by zero; a nonzero bump with a nonzero integral acts nontrivially on constants.

### 0.3 Schwartz functions on the group

Construct `AdelicSchwartzFunction(G)` from smooth functions of fixed compact finite support and fixed bi-level whose every left/right invariant derivative decays faster than every inverse power of `H`. For each enveloping pair `u,v` and natural `r`, require finiteness of `sup_g H(g)^r |L(u)R(v)f(g)|`. Take the projective topology over these seminorms, then the inductive union over finite supports and levels. Prove independence under polynomially comparable heights, inclusion of test functions, continuity of translation, differentiation, involution and convolution. This is the rapidly decreasing group Schwartz algebra, with the height convention fixed here; it is not automatically the larger Harish-Chandra `𝒞(G)` algebra. (Bernstein–Krötz, §2.5, pp.11–12; Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.2, p.17.)

The API `seminorm`, `toSmoothFunction`, `testFunction_inclusion`, `convolution`, `inversion` and `integrable_weighted` includes weighted integrability of derivatives. Its integrability input is the polynomial volume estimate below, which is a target of this layer. The involution includes the group inversion and complex conjugation. The test functions form a dense subalgebra of `AdelicSchwartzFunction(G)` (Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.2, p.17). A completed tensor-product identification requires the stated topology and is an additional comparison, not the algebraic tensor definition of §0.2.

At a common finite bi-level, convolution is a continuous bilinear map from the stages with compact finite supports `C₁,C₂` to the stage with support `C₁C₂`; inversion maps the `C` stage to the `C⁻¹` stage. An arbitrary fixed support stage is a topological vector space. The inductive union is a convolution algebra, and a stage supported in a compact open subgroup is closed under convolution.

Prove `integrable_height_rpow_neg`: for the archimedean group `G∞` with a Haar measure there is `r₀≥0` such that `∫_{G∞}H(g)^{−r}dg<∞` for every `r>r₀`; with a fixed compact finite support the same bound holds on `G(𝔸)`. In particular `vol{g∈G∞ : H(g)≤T}≤C·T^{r₀+1}` for `T≥1`, and conversely such a volume bound gives the integrability for `r>r₀+1`. Compare `H` with the norm of a faithful representation with closed image (Conventions), write `g=k₁ak₂` by the `KAK` decomposition (LieGroups Layer 9), bound the Haar density on the closed positive chamber `A⁺` by a constant times `e^{2ρ(log a)}`, and use the lower bound `H(a)≥c·e^{δ‖log a‖}` on `A⁺` for some `δ>0`. The weighted integrability of Schwartz functions and the smoothing estimates of §0.5 rest on this bound. (Bernstein–Krötz, §5.3.1, p.27, where such an exponent is fixed for the weighted spaces.)

**Checks.**

- On `ℝ×`, `t↦exp(−π(t²+t⁻²))` is Schwartz for the symmetric height and is not compactly supported; its maximum is `exp(−2π)` at `t=±1`.
- Constants are excluded when the archimedean group is noncompact.
- If the archimedean group is compact, smooth functions at a fixed compact finite support and bi-level are Schwartz; compactness makes all height weights bounded.
- Inversion interchanges `t^s` and `t^{−s}` on the positive torus, testing both growth directions in the height.
- For `ℝ_{>0}` with `dt/t` and `H(t)=max(t,t⁻¹)`, `∫H^{−r}dt/t=2/r` for every `r>0`, so `r₀=0`; at `r=0` the integral diverges.
- For `SL₂(ℝ)` with the operator-norm height, the Haar density along `diag(e^s,e^{−s})`, `s≥0`, is a constant times `sinh(2s)` and `H=e^s` there, so the integral converges exactly when `r>2`. The exponent `r=2` fails, which separates the true threshold from a dimension count.
- For compact `G∞` the height is bounded and every `r≥0` works.
- On `GL₁(𝔸_ℚ)`, `exp(−π(x∞²+x∞^{−2}))·1_{Ẑ×}(x_f)` is Schwartz and not compactly supported; its archimedean factor is the Gaussian above.
- For compact `G∞`, the Schwartz space at fixed compact finite support and bi-level coincides with the test-function space as a topological vector space; their unions over supports are topological convolution algebras.
- For the norm-one torus of `ℚ(i)/ℚ`, the real group is compact and the group at `5` is `ℚ₅×`. Choose a compact open `J⊂Gf` with `J₅=ℤ₅×` and `a∈Gf` with component `5` at `5` and identity elsewhere. Then `1_{aJ}*1_{aJ}=vol(J)1_{a²J}`. The two supports have valuations one and two at `5`, so the `aJ` stage is not closed under convolution. Tensoring with the constant function on the real group of Haar mass one gives the adelic example.

### 0.4 Moderate growth and uniform derivative bounds

For any real-valued function `H`, define the underlying predicate `HasModerateGrowth(H,f)` by the existence of `C≥0` and `N∈ℝ_{≥0}` with `|f(g)|≤C H(g)^N`. For the arithmetic applications require `H≥1` and the supplied polynomial height bounds; under those hypotheses rounding the exponent up gives the equivalent natural-exponent condition, and the predicate is invariant under polynomial change of height. Define `T_sm([G])` by rational left invariance, smoothness with a finite level, and one exponent `N` that works for all `R(u)f`, allowing a constant depending on `u`. Fixed-`J,N` pieces carry the derivative weighted-supremum seminorms; construct their complete locally convex topology and continuous inclusions on increasing the exponent or shrinking the level. Use the resulting final topology on their union. A claim of strictness of the exponent limit requires a separate closed-image theorem and is not built into the definition. (Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §§2.5.7–2.5.8, p.18.)

Expose `height_change`, `add`, `scalar_mul`, `rightTranslate`, `derivedAction`, `fixedLevelExponent`, `seminorm` and `kFinite_equiv`. For `T_sm`, the last map identifies its `K`-finite vectors with the supplied `T([G])`. Derivative estimates use `p_v(R(u)f)=p_{vu}(f)`, and translating an infinitesimal operator uses `Ad(y∞⁻¹)`. Right translation preserves uniform moderate growth, with a constant depending on `y` and the seminorm. Moderate growth without the uniform derivative condition is not differentiated without further assumptions. For the submultiplicative height fixed in Conventions and `y∈G(𝔸)`, the translated seminorm estimate is `p_{N,v}(R(y)φ)≤H(y)^N·p_{N,Ad(y∞)⁻¹v}(φ)`, with the conjugated finite level. On each fixed-`J,N` piece `(y∞,φ)↦R(y∞)φ` is jointly continuous, so that piece is a smooth Fréchet representation of `G∞` of moderate growth. For a merely polynomial multiplication bound `H(xy)≤C H(x)^aH(y)^b`, translation instead maps the exponent-`N` piece to the exponent-`aN` piece. (Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.8, p.18.)

**Checks.**

- For the bounded height `H=1` on ℝ, the function `x↦x` is not moderate; every permitted bound would be constant.
- Constants have exponent zero, and the zero function belongs to every nonnegative exponent piece.
- The torus character `t↦t^s` on `t>0` is moderate for `H(t)=max(t,t⁻¹)` with exponent `|Re s|`; its Euler derivatives use the same exponent.
- The bounded smooth function `sin(exp(t²))` on `t>0` is moderate but its Euler derivative has no polynomial height bound. Moderate growth alone does not imply uniform moderate growth.
- Every element of the augmentation ideal of `U(𝔤)` kills a constant; an operator of positive filtration degree such as `1+X` need not kill it.
- `exp(t)` is not moderate for the symmetric polynomial height on the positive torus. On ℝ with `H(x)=1+|x|`, the same obstruction is `exp_not_hasModerateGrowth`.
- Every bounded function has moderate growth with exponent zero.
- On `GLₙ(𝔸)` with `H(g)=∏_v max(1,‖g_v‖_v,‖g_v^{−1}‖_v)`, `|det g|^s` has moderate growth for every `s∈ℂ`, with exponent `n|Re s|`, since `|det g|^{±1}≤H(g)^n`.
- For a Hecke character `χ` of `GL₁/ℚ` with `|χ|=|·|^σ`, the derivative along the positive real line is `R(X)χ=s·χ` with `Re s=σ`, so `χ` lies in the fixed-exponent piece for every `N≥|σ|`, and `R(y)χ=χ(y)χ` shows that the exponent is attained.
- On `Gₘ²/ℚ`, put `t_i=|g_i|_𝔸`, `H₀=∏_v max(1,|g₁,v|_v,|g₁,v|_v⁻¹,|g₂,v|_v,|g₂,v|_v⁻¹)` and `H'=H₀+(t₁−t₂)²`. Then `H₀≤H'≤5H₀²`. For a smooth bump `η` supported in `(3/2,5/2)` with `η(2)=1`, every derivative of `φ=t₂²η(t₁/t₂)` is bounded by a constant times `H'`. Translating by infinity coordinates `(2,1)` gives `t²` at infinity coordinates `(t,t)` and finite coordinates one, where `H'=t` for `t≥1`. Thus the exponent-one piece for `H'` is not translation invariant. The finite translation with first coordinate `1/2` at `2` and all other coordinates one has the same effect on `φ`; its torus Hecke double coset has one right coset.

### 0.5 Smoothing and finite Hecke operators

Prove that if `φ` is Haar-measurable and has moderate growth and `a` is a Schwartz function, `R(a)φ` converges absolutely, is smooth, and has uniform moderate growth with a common derivative growth exponent, after fixing a finite support/level. Every specialization carries this measurability hypothesis; continuous automorphic functions satisfy it. If the height satisfies `H(xy)≤C H(x)H(y)`, this exponent is the original exponent `N`; for a bound `H(xy)≤C H(x)^aH(y)^b`, record the output exponent `aN`. Establish differentiation under the integral by a dominating weighted Schwartz seminorm. The first derivative moves to the test function as `−L_Xa`; iteration uses the principal anti-automorphism, including reversal of products. Fubini gives `R(a*b)=R(a)R(b)`. The integrability input is an actual bound `∫H(y)^{N−r}dy<∞` for sufficiently large `r`, on the specified finite support, rather than a formal assertion that decay implies integrability. (Bernstein–Krötz, §2.5, pp.11–12; Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.8, equation (2.5.8.6), p.18.)

Prove `R(f*h)=R(f)R(h)` for the finite Hecke algebra, preservation of the growth exponent for the fixed submultiplicative height, commutation with `R(X)` and `R(g∞)`, and compatibility with `e_{J'}` for `J'⊆J`. **Checks.**

- `R(a)1=(∫a)1`, and a mass-one bump fixes constants.
- On the additive real group, moving the derivative in `∫a(y)φ(x+y)dy` gives `−∫a'(y)φ(x+y)dy`; the plus sign fails integration by parts. With `a(y)=e^{−y²}` and `φ(y)=y`, the two integrals have opposite nonzero signs.
- For `GL₂(ℚ_p)` and `J=GL₂(ℤ_p)`, the double coset of `diag(p,1)` has `p+1` right cosets, so the operator sends `1` to `(p+1)1`.

This tests `R(a*b)=R(a)R(b)` on a noncommutative group. For derivative order, the anti-automorphism sends `XY` to `YX`: in the standard two-by-two matrices `XY=diag(1,0)` and `YX=diag(0,1)`.
- The bounded continuous function `min(1,|log t|)` on `ℝ_{>0}` is not smooth at `t=1`, but its convolution with a smooth compactly supported mass-one bump is smooth.
- `φ(t)=exp(t⁴+t^{−4})` on `ℝ_{>0}` is not of moderate growth, and its pairing with the Schwartz function `exp(−t²−t^{−2})` against `dt/t` diverges. The moderate-growth hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- A non-Haar-measurable subset `E` of a compact interval in `ℝ_{>0}` has bounded indicator. Multiplication by a smooth bump positive on that interval leaves it nonmeasurable, so its convolution at the identity is not supplied by a growth bound.

### 1.2 Smooth cochains and the continuous comparison

For a finite-dimensional real Lie group `L` with finitely many components and a finite-dimensional continuous real or complex `L`-module `V`, construct the smooth group-cochain subcomplex. ```text
(df)(g₁,…,g_{q+1}) = g₁ f(g₂,…,g_{q+1})
  + Σ_{i=1}^q (−1)^i f(g₁,…,g_i g_{i+1},…,g_{q+1})
  + (−1)^{q+1} f(g₁,…,g_q).

Define the essential versions by a twist by a positive real character of `G/[G,G]`, which is determined by its restriction to `A_G`. Expose `matrixCoefficient`, `unitaryTwist`, `contragredient`, `isSquareIntegrable_isTempered` and normalized-induction compatibility. For a connected real reductive group modulo its centre, prove existence of discrete series exactly when a Cartan is compact modulo `A_G`, equivalently `rank(𝔤_ℂ)−dim A_G=rank(𝔨_ℂ)`. Fix such a Cartan and a unitary central character. Regular parameters `λ` on the actual character lattice shifted by `ρ` give the discrete series, with infinitesimal character `χ_λ`; parameters are identified by `W_K`. Their lowest compact type has highest weight `λ+ρ_n−ρ_c`. For a singular chamber-dominant parameter on that shifted lattice, its limit is nonzero exactly when it pairs nontrivially with every root that is compact and simple in the full chamber system. The limit is nondegenerate exactly when the pairing with every compact root is nonzero, equivalently when the parameter is strictly dominant for the compact root subsystem. A nonzero limit can therefore be degenerate on a compact root that is not simple in the full system. Include the component action when extending to disconnected groups. (Pilloni, §5.1.6, p.22; Calegari–Geraghty, *Minimal modularity lifting for nonregular symplectic representations*, proof of Theorem 5.5, p.22; Harris, §3.3, pp.62–63; Carayol–Knapp, Introduction (0.2)–(0.4), pp.5612–5613.)

Prove `langlandsClassification`: every irreducible admissible module is the unique irreducible quotient of a standard normalized induction from an irreducible tempered Levi module with real exponent in the open positive chamber. The standard triple is unique up to compact conjugacy. Allow `P=G` and zero exponent for a tempered module. A general tempered module is an irreducible constituent of unitary induction from discrete-series or nondegenerate-limit data on a suitable Levi. The existence/uniqueness proof belongs before globalization, and is not obtained by applying the later Casselman–Wallach equivalence. (Langlands, *On the classification of irreducible representations of real algebraic groups*, Lemmas 3.13–3.14, pp.44–45, and Lemma 4.2, p.50; Knapp, Theorem 1, p.400, for GLₙ.)

Write `IrrAdmissible(G)` for the set of isomorphism classes of irreducible admissible `(𝔤,K)`-modules, equivalently of irreducible Harish-Chandra modules. Expose `infChar`, `infChar_finite_fibres`, `dual` (the contragredient, an involution) and `twist` by characters of `G/[G,G]`. ### 2.5 Weil groups and archimedean parameters

Construct `WeilGroupComplex=ℂ×` and `WeilGroupReal=ℂ×⊔jℂ×` as topological groups with `j²=−1` and `jzj⁻¹=conj z`. The map `norm:W_ℝ→ℝ×`, `z↦|z|²`, `j↦−1`, induces the abelianization. The last means relatively compact image, not merely bounded eigenvalues of individual matrices. (Knapp, §3, equations (3.1)–(3.3), pp.401–403.)

Construct `recGL` for `GL_n(ℝ)` and `GL_n(ℂ)`, `n≥1`, as a bijection between irreducible admissible Harish-Chandra-module isomorphism classes and semisimple continuous `n`-dimensional Weil representations. For `W_ℝ`, restriction to `ℂ×` gives the infinitesimal-character multiset `(p_i)`, with `(q_i)` its permutation; for complex groups the two multisets belong to the two factors of the complexified real Lie algebra. ### 2.7 Smooth Fréchet representations and globalization

Define `SFRepresentation(G)` as a smooth Fréchet representation of moderate growth: for each continuous seminorm `p` there are `q,C,N` with `p(π(g)v)≤C H(g)^N q(v)`. The action is jointly continuous and every vector is smooth. Define `SAFRepresentation` by requiring its compact-finite module to be Harish-Chandra. For a complex Banach representation with explicitly continuous orbit maps, use SmoothBanachRepresentations SB2's `smoothLieVectors` and its separate smooth-topology carrier; `smoothVectors` denotes that carrier with the supplied complete Fréchet topology and continuous inclusion. Construct the identity-on-vectors continuous linear equivalence `smoothVectorTopologyEquiv` with the enveloping/Sobolev topology here, intertwining the group action, inclusion, differentiated action and compact-finite functor. Extend the smooth-vector construction and seminorm estimates to complete Hausdorff locally convex representations, using all continuous seminorms; for a Fréchet representation the smooth topology is again complete and Fréchet. Expose `seminormEstimate`, `kFinite`, `continuousMorphism`, `closedSubrepresentation` and `quotient`. Nuclearity of an SAF space is a theorem derived from compact-type multiplicity bounds. (SmoothBanachRepresentations SB2–SB4; Bernstein–Krötz, §§2.1–2.5, pp.5–12, and Theorem 5.5, pp.25–26.)

For a real Lie basis `X₁,…,X_m`, compare SB2's finite sum `P_k(v)=Σ_{|w|≤k}‖dπ(X_w)v‖` over all words with the ordered-monomial Sobolev seminorm `Q_k(v)=(Σ_{|α|≤k}‖dπ(X₁^{α₁}⋯X_m^{α_m})v‖²)^{1/2}`: for each `k` there are positive constants `c_k,C_k` with `c_k Q_k≤P_k≤C_k Q_k`. Finite-dimensional changes of basis and the filtered enveloping relations give the same comparison for a complex basis of `𝔤=Lie(G)⊗ℝℂ`. Use SB3's real derived action and its complex-linear extension, so `dπ(iX)=i dπ(X)`. These comparisons define `smoothVectorTopologyEquiv` without putting the inherited Banach subspace topology on smooth vectors. SB3 writes `R_X^{SB}a(y)=d/dt|₀ a(exp(tX)y)`, which is `L_Xa` in the Conventions here; hence for the right-translation representation `R(X)R(a)=R(−L_Xa)`. SB4 supplies density in the Banach space and the comparison with native `StronglyContinuousSemigroup.smoothVectors` for the real additive group, after restriction of scalars and the half-line orbit comparison. SB4's compact-Lie density in the smooth topology is compared with §2.1's algebraic compact-finite predicate and locally convex projectors on the same vectors. (SmoothBanachRepresentations SB2–SB4; Bernstein–Krötz, §2.4.2–§2.4.3, p.10.)

A `GContinuousNorm` on a Harish-Chandra module means a norm whose Banach completion carries an actual continuous `G`-representation extending the given compact-finite differentiated action and recovering that module. Construct such norms using the embedding theorem. For a norm `p` and a basis `X₁,…,X_m` of `𝔤`, the `k`-th Sobolev norm is `p_k(v)=(Σ_{|α|≤k}p(X^αv)²)^{1/2}`; write `p≺q` when `p≤C·q_k` for some `k` and `C`. Prove any two `G`-continuous norms are Sobolev-equivalent (`p≺q` and `q≺p`), and that every `G`-continuous norm is Sobolev-equivalent to a `K`-invariant Hermitian one (Bernstein–Krötz, §1, pp.2–3, and §2.4.2, p.10). An arbitrary norm need not be `G`-continuous. For a linear real reductive group, use these results to construct `casselmanWallachGlobalization` and prove the compact-finite functor is an exact equivalence between SAF representations with continuous intertwining maps and Harish-Chandra modules. All intertwining maps have closed image, and every module morphism extends uniquely and continuously. The proof proceeds through principal series, extension stability, induction/deformation, Langlands classification and the square-integrable case. (Bernstein–Krötz, Theorem 1.1, p.3, and Theorem 10.6 with §§10.2–10.4, pp.44–45.)

Prove `dixmierMalliavin`: each vector in a smooth Fréchet representation is a finite sum `Σ_i π(a_i)v_i` with `a_i∈C_c∞(G)`. For an `SFRepresentation(G)`, the integrated Schwartz algebra acts continuously and nondegenerately as well. For an irreducible Harish-Chandra module the globalization is algebraically simple as a Schwartz-algebra module. Nondegeneracy means finite linear span of the images, not a claim that one fixed test function is an identity. For a smooth representation of `G(𝔸)`, a strict inductive limit over compact open `J⊂Gf` of smooth Fréchet representations of `G∞`, apply Bernstein–Krötz, Remark 2.19, p.12, on each fixed finite-level Fréchet space and insert the finite idempotent `e_J`. **Checks.**

- A finite-dimensional continuous representation is SF; a finite-dimensional algebraic one has polynomial moderate growth for a suitable algebraic height.
- `C∞(S¹)` with rotations is smooth Fréchet, but its full space is larger than its compact-finite module. Recovering only that module does not identify the two vector spaces.

- The sequence `0→D₂⁺⊕D₂⁻→I(0,1)→ℂ→0` of Harish-Chandra modules globalizes to an exact sequence of SAF representations.
- For `G=ℝ` acting on `S(ℝ)` by translation, every Schwartz function is a finite sum of convolutions `f*g` with `f∈C_c^∞(ℝ)` and `g∈S(ℝ)`.

### 3.1 Smooth automorphic vectors and classical components

Define `SmoothAutomorphicForm(G)` as the `Z`-finite part of `T_sm([G])`. Here `Z`-finite means that some cofinite ideal of the enveloping centre annihilates the vector. - Repeating a representative twice produces a diagonal compatibility condition, not an unrestricted direct sum. Distinct double cosets are necessary for the stated bijection.
- On `GL₁/ℚ` the archimedean compact group `{±1}` is finite, so `A∞(GL₁)=A(GL₁)`: the finite sums of Hecke characters times polynomials in `log|·|_𝔸`.
- For a weight-`k` cusp form on `GL₂/ℚ`, the smooth vectors of its cuspidal representation are the Casselman–Wallach globalization of `D_k` tensored with the finite part.
- At level `K₀(N)`, compact type `e^{ikθ}`, `k≥2`, and the central ideal generated by `Ω−k(k−2)/4` and the centre, the fixed piece contains `S_k(Γ₀(N))` and the weight-`k` holomorphic Eisenstein series and is finite-dimensional. At weight zero without a fixed central ideal it contains Maass forms of infinitely many Laplace eigenvalues and is infinite-dimensional.

### 3.2 The global algebra and automorphic representations

Construct the commuting `(𝔤,K)×Gf` action on the existing automorphic-form space. Full archimedean group translation acts on `A∞`; it need not preserve the compact-finite carrier `A`. The finite group action, the enveloping action and the compact action do preserve `A`. Use the resulting global idempotented Hecke module to define `IsAutomorphicRepresentation(π)` as being an irreducible admissible subquotient of `A`. The carrier of irreducible isomorphism classes must contain actual modules and equivalences, not an arbitrary type labeled “representations”. Construct `subquotientOccurrence`, `centralCharacter`, `twist`, `smoothAtInfinity` and `multiplicity`. The multiplicity is initially an extended dimension of `Hom(π,A)`; fixed-type finiteness makes it finite in this arithmetic space. The space `A` itself is a union of fixed-type pieces and is not asserted to be admissible as one module; an admissible global module has finite-dimensional `(σ,J_f)`-isotypic parts for every compact type `σ` and compact open `J_f`. (Borel–Jacquet, §4.6, p.196.)

For the smooth comparison use admissible finite-length representations on each fixed finite-level/central piece, closed submodules and Hausdorff quotients, followed by the globalization equivalence. Do not infer an unrestricted topological-subquotient theorem for any topology on the entire union. Prove `automorphic_iff_cuspidalInducedConstituent`: an irreducible admissible representation is automorphic precisely when it is a constituent of adelic parabolic induction from a cuspidal automorphic representation of a rational Levi, with its specified central twist. Construct the minimal analytic input `eisensteinFiniteJetRealization` here: at a fixed finite level and compact type, normalized cuspidal induction forms a finite-dimensional holomorphic family; multiply its meromorphic Eisenstein map by a local common denominator to obtain a holomorphic equivariant map. Prove that its constant terms detect the family on a nonempty open parameter set. The finite-jet map then has kernel contained in sections vanishing at the base parameter, for a sufficiently large jet order. This realizes every base-fibre constituent in a finite sum of automorphic-form spaces. The analytic targets include the meromorphic extension, local denominator and constant-term identities needed for these assertions; the spectral consumer can subsequently extend this interface to its full decomposition and functional equations. (Langlands, *On the notion of an automorphic representation*, Proposition 2, p.204, and Lemmas 7–9, pp.206–207.)

Define the underlying function predicate `HasCentralCharacter(Z,χ,f)` by `f(gz)=χ(z)f(g)` for a subgroup `Z` and a homomorphism `χ:Z→ℂ×`. The equation makes sense for every subgroup; its automorphic specialization requires the actual central subgroup and a character trivial on the rational intersection. The API is `add`, `scalar_mul`, `subspace`, `character_unique_of_nonzero`, and `rightTranslate` under centrality.

Prove `centralTranslationFinite` directly: every automorphic vector's central translates remain in one finite-level, finite-compact-type, cofinite-central-ideal space, which is finite-dimensional by the supplied finiteness theorem. The resulting finite-dimensional central representation decomposes into joint generalized characters. Ordinary eigenspaces do not suffice for all automorphic forms. (Borel–Jacquet, §4.3(iv), p.195.)

**Checks.**

- A constant has trivial central character; the zero function satisfies every character equation. If `f(g)≠0` and `f(gz)=f(g)`, its specified character has `χ(z)=1`; a nonzero value is essential.
- The one-dimensional holomorphic map `E(t)=t` vanishes at the base parameter but its first jet detects the base fibre. Its zero-jet does not; a family without generic injectivity such as `E(t)=0` gives no occurrence theorem.
- The trivial representation is automorphic and its invariant occurrence is the one-dimensional space of constants. Its multiplicity is one.
- A Hecke character defines an automorphic representation of GL₁. Character twists preserve automorphy when they are actual characters of the rational quotient.
- `log|g|_𝔸` on GL₁ is rational-left-invariant by the product formula; its central orbit is spanned by itself and `1`. Translation adds `log|z|_𝔸`, giving a nontrivial generalized-character extension.
- An infinite formal sum of unrelated central characters need not be `Z`-finite and is excluded.
- Multiplicity one is not part of the general definition. No GLₙ uniqueness theorem is imported to arbitrary reductive groups.
- For `g∞=diag(2,1)∈GL₂(ℝ)^+`, the translate `R(g∞)φ_Δ` is a smooth automorphic vector with infinitely many `SO(2)`-types, so it lies in `A∞` and not in `A`; translation by a central element preserves compact-finiteness.
- A Hecke character `χ` spans the module `ℂχ` on which `𝔸_f^×` acts by `χ_f` and the pair `(𝔤𝔩₁,O(1))` by the archimedean component; its central orbit is one-dimensional.
- For `G=SL₂×T` with `T` an `F`-anisotropic torus, the product of an automorphic `SL₂` form and an automorphic character of `T` is automorphic, with separate central and compact-type actions; no identification of `G` with a general linear group is used.
- Automorphic multiplicities can exceed one: for `SLₙ`, `n≥3`, there are automorphic representations occurring with multiplicity greater than one, so the multiplicity is a cardinal-valued invariant, not a Boolean. (Lapid, *Some results on multiplicities for SL(n)*, Introduction, pp.157–158.)

### 3.3 Archimedean Hecke distributions

Construct `archimedeanHeckeAlgebra(𝔤,K)` from the distributions supported on `K` that are finite under both compact translations. Distributions here are continuous functionals on `C∞(G∞)` with its compact-derivative topology, hence compactly supported; equivalently use the compact-support subspace in the test-function dual. Let `R(K)` be the compact-finite matrix-coefficient convolution algebra. Prove a vector-space identification
`H(𝔤,K)≃U(𝔤)⊗_{U(𝔨)}R(K)` with the actual differentiated inclusion and its balancing actions. Its multiplication is induced by distribution convolution and adjoint covariance, and is not the ordinary tensor-product multiplication. Construct `tensorEquiv`, `action_tmul`, `compactTypeIdempotent` and `moduleEquiv`. Nondegenerate modules are exactly compatible locally compact-finite modules. (Flath, §3, the discussion following Theorem 3, p.182.)

For a finite set `S` of irreducible compact types, `e_S=Σ_{σ∈S}e_σ` is an idempotent; `e_Se_T=e_S` when `S⊂T`. These form a directed local identity. They also give the global algebra together with the finite adelic Hecke algebra. Expose `finiteTypeCorner`, `cornerAction` and compatibility with the finite-level idempotents.

**Checks.**

- If `G∞=K`, the enveloping balance collapses the carrier to `R(K)`.
- On `U(1)`, Fourier projectors satisfy `e_m e_n=0` for `m≠n` and `e_n²=e_n`.
- No finite sum of circle projectors is an identity on all compact types. A local identity is not a global unit.
- For a two-dimensional compact irreducible, the projector density is twice its inverse character. Acting on that irreducible gives identity; reciprocal normalization would give one quarter of identity.

### 3.4 Restricted tensor products and finite corners

For a countable index set of places, complex vector spaces `W_v` and nonzero reference vectors `w_v⁰` outside a finite exceptional set, construct `RestrictedTensor(W,w⁰)` as the filtered colimit of finite tensor products, with transitions adjoining reference entries. Construct `ofFinite`, `tprod`, `map`, `rescale` and the finite-index equivalence. `tprod` accepts families equal to the reference outside a finite set; arbitrary infinite families have no constructor. Rescaling every reference by a nonzero scalar gives an isomorphism after the scalar choices are specified; it is not a distinguished isomorphism independent of those choices. (Flath, §2, pp.180–181; Getz, §7.1, pp.34–35.)

For algebras stabilized by idempotents construct the analogous nonunital algebra and its compatible module action. Multiplication is computed at a common finite tensor stage. The finite inclusions preserve multiplication because the stabilization entries are idempotent, not because they are necessarily units. Prove the finite adelic test algebra is the restricted tensor product of its local Hecke algebras after choosing an integral model away from a finite set.

Prove `finiteCornerFactorization`: a nonzero finite-dimensional simple module over `A₁⊗ℂ A₂`, for unital complex algebras, is `V₁⊗V₂` with finite-dimensional simple factors, unique up to isomorphism. For a nondegenerate simple module over an idempotented algebra, every nonzero corner `eV` is simple over `eAe`; admissibility makes it finite-dimensional in the present application. A nonzero corner submodule generates the whole simple module, and multiplying back by `e` proves equality; no complement of that submodule is assumed. Apply finite-dimensional density/Schur theory over ℂ, then glue factors across larger corners. (Flath, Theorem 1 and proof, pp.179–180.)

**Checks.**

- An empty finite tensor stage is ℂ. For a finite full index set the restricted tensor is the ordinary tensor product.
- Stabilizing `ℂ[X_i]` by `1` gives the polynomial ring in finitely supported monomials in countably many variables. A monomial using infinitely many variables is absent.
- If a reference vector is zero, transition maps can kill a nonzero tensor; the nonzero-reference hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- Standard modules for `M_r(ℂ)` and `M_s(ℂ)` tensor to dimension `rs`. Taking `A₂=ℂ` gives a one-dimensional second factor.
- The nonsimple `C₂×C₂` module `1⊗1⊕sgn⊗sgn` has a rank-two character matrix and is not an exterior tensor of two modules. Simplicity matters.
- For `A₁=M_r(ℂ)` and `A₂=M_s(ℂ)` the factors of the standard module of `A₁⊗A₂` have dimensions `r` and `s`.

### 3.5 Spherical lines and Flath factorization

Prove `finrank_spherical_le_one`. Let `v` be a finite place and `K_v=𝒢(O_v)` hyperspecial for a reductive model `𝒢` of `G` over `O_v` (ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.3). The spherical Hecke algebra `C_c^∞(G(F_v)//K_v)` is commutative, because the Satake transform embeds it in a commutative algebra (SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.4.1), so `(G(F_v),K_v)` is a Gelfand pair. For an irreducible admissible complex representation `π_v`, the fixed space `π_v^{K_v}` is zero or a simple module over the level algebra (SR.1.3), and it is finite-dimensional by admissibility. A finite-dimensional simple module over a commutative complex algebra is one-dimensional by Schur's lemma, so `dim π_v^{K_v}≤1`. The source prints the conclusion as an equality; the inequality is correct, since a ramified representation has no nonzero fixed vector. (Getz, §8, Proposition 8.6, p.39.) Expose `spherical_commutative`, `isGelfandPair` and `finrank_spherical_le_one`.

For the global choice, AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.1 supplies an admissible maximal compact subgroup `∏_vK_v` of `G(𝔸)` whose factors are hyperspecial outside a finite set `S`; it is built from the integral models and spreading out of AdelicAlgebraicGroups §1.1 together with RG2.3. Fix such a subgroup once; the spherical line is then available at every `v∉S`. Local unramified theory at a single place does not supply this global choice. (Flath, §3 and the number-field discussion following Theorem 3, pp.181–182; Getz, §§7.1–7.2, pp.34–36.)

Prove `flath`: every irreducible admissible global `(𝔤,K)×Gf` module is the restricted tensor product of irreducible admissible local modules, spherical at almost all finite places. The factor is unique up to isomorphism and the reference line up to scalar. At infinity use the archimedean Hecke distribution algebra, and at finite places use local identities. The proof proceeds through a nonzero finite-dimensional level/type corner, the factorization in §3.4 and compatible enlargement; it does not assume a finite tensor decomposition of the whole infinite-dimensional module. Construct `localFactor`, `tensorEquiv`, `unramifiedAlmostEverywhere` and `factor_unique`. A Hilbert tensor-product version is a separate realization for unitary modules. (Flath, Theorems 1–3, pp.179–182; Theorem 4, p.182.)

**Checks.**

- A Hecke character factors into its local characters, trivial on integral units almost everywhere.
- At a spherical place a chosen nonzero fixed vector spans the whole fixed space. A ramified representation has zero such fixed space, not a preferred zero reference vector.
- For a GL₂ newform of exact conductor `N`, the local factor is unramified precisely away from the prime divisors of `N`; changing to a smaller test level does not redefine the conductor.
- Rescaling a spherical vector changes the displayed tensor realization but not the local representation's isomorphism class.
- For the trivial group all factors and the global irreducible are one-dimensional; zero representations are excluded by irreducibility.
- For `GL₂(ℚ_p)` and `K=GL₂(ℤ_p)`, an irreducible unramified principal series has a one-dimensional `K`-fixed line and the Steinberg representation has none.
- For the Iwahori subgroup `I`, which is not hyperspecial, the Iwahori–Hecke algebra is noncommutative; `St^I` is one-dimensional while an irreducible unramified principal series has two-dimensional `I`-fixed space. The dimension bound needs hyperspecial level.

For a GL₂ cusp form, the compact-finite vectors admit the Flath tensor decomposition, whereas a smooth vector with infinitely many archimedean compact types belongs to its globalization. These are distinct, compatible realizations. On GL₁, logarithmic extensions show why central translation finiteness is a generalized-character statement even though irreducible representations are Hecke characters.

## Layer 4: constant terms, cuspidal realizations and Fourier tests

### 4.1 Constant-term compatibility and transitivity

Extend the supplied constant term by its smooth/topological and equivariance API. For a rational parabolic `P=M_PN_P` and a continuous rational-left-invariant `φ`, integrate `φ(ng)` over the compact probability quotient `N_P(F)\N_P(𝔸)`. Continuity supplies integrability on every fibre; ambient almost-everywhere local integrability alone does not. Prove `constantTerm_smooth`, `constantTerm_leftInvariant`, `constantTerm_rightTranslate`, `constantTerm_derivedAction`, `constantTerm_finiteHecke` and continuity on the specified weighted pieces. The output is left `M_P(F)N_P(𝔸)`-invariant and keeps its compact type and enveloping-centre annihilator. The height estimate for uniform growth uses the supplied reduction comparison; if an exponent changes, the target piece records that change. (Getz, Definition 6.13, p.31; Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.10, p.19.)

For `Q⊂P`, construct the quotient-measure disintegration from `N_Q=N_P⋊N_{Q∩M_P}` and prove `constantTerm_constantTerm` by Fubini: integrating first along `N_P`, then along the Levi unipotent, equals the `Q`-constant term. For rational `γ`, prove `φ_{γPγ⁻¹}(g)=φ_P(γ⁻¹g)`. Use these identities to reduce vanishing at every proper rational parabolic to the maximal standard ones, relative to a fixed minimal rational parabolic `P₀` (AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.1). Unipotent groups over `F` are split in characteristic zero; the quotient measures in stages of AdelicAlgebraicGroups §2.2 give the iterated integral along a normal series with vector-group quotients. The identity parabolic is allowed as an operator, and its constant term is identity; it is excluded from cuspidality tests. The light kernel predicate `IsCuspidalFor(CT,v)` means `CT_P(v)=0` for every index `P` in the specified family of linear constant-term maps. Its API is `zero`, `add`, `scalar_mul`, `family_mono` and `nonzero_constantTerm_not_cuspidal`. The arithmetic specialization must use all proper rational parabolics; it then agrees with the supplied cusp-form subspace. (Beuzart-Plessis–Chaudouard–Zydor, §2.5.10, p.19; Borel–Jacquet, §4.1, p.194.)

**Checks.**

- A constant `c` has constant term `c`, using total mass one. Integrating over the full noncompact `N_P(𝔸)` instead would fail even for `c=1`.
- For `GL₃`, the Borel constant term factors through the `(2,1)` parabolic and then the Borel of its Levi; the two probability integrations give `1` on constants.
- For `GL₂`, the adelization of `Σa_nq^n` has Borel constant term `a₀y^{k/2}` at `diag(y^{1/2},y^{−1/2})`. The Eisenstein form with `a₀=1` gives `y^{k/2}`, and a cusp form gives zero.
- For `P=G`, the unipotent is trivial and the operator is identity. On a torus there are no proper-parabolic tests.
- In the additive example `ℚ\𝔸_ℚ`, `Ẑ×[0,1)` is a measurable fundamental domain, not a product decomposition of topological groups. The same distinction prevents replacing a nonabelian unipotent quotient by an arbitrary product measure without disintegration.
- `GL₂` has a single standard proper parabolic, so its cuspidality is the single condition `φ_B=0`.
- For the upper unipotent `N⊂GL₃`, `N(F)\N(𝔸)` fibres over `(F\𝔸)²` with fibre `F\𝔸` and has total mass one; its finite-level real quotients are compact Heisenberg nilmanifolds, and the adelic quotient is compact.

For the kernel predicate, an empty parabolic family makes every vector satisfy it; a nonzero value of one indexed constant term excludes the vector; and a family consisting entirely of zero maps imposes no condition. The last case does not establish arithmetic cuspidality unless these are the actual complete family of constant terms.

### 4.2 Cuspidal globalization and actual discrete occurrences

Use the supplied cuspidal kernel subspace and its rapid-decay and discrete-spectrum theorems. Construct the adapters `smoothCuspidalForm`, `cuspidal_kFinite`, `cuspidalOccurrence`, `cuspidalTensorFactorization` and `cuspidalCentralTwist`. At a specified unitary central character, a cuspidal automorphic module has a closed irreducible unitary occurrence in the supplied cuspidal Hilbert space; its smooth compact-finite realization recovers the algebraic module. Conversely recover every such algebraic occurrence. Every cuspidal automorphic representation is automorphic and occurs as an irreducible submodule, with finite multiplicity `m_cusp(π)`; at a unitary central character the cuspidal module is semisimple, so its subquotients are submodules. In a function-space realization the chosen subspace must be invariant under both the Lie and the compact actions. The algebraic direct sum uses compact-finite, finite-level, centre-finite vectors. The unrestricted smooth compact-finite part of a Hilbert sum can contain vectors with infinitely many infinitesimal characters and is not identified with `A₀` without that condition. (Borel–Jacquet, §§4.4–4.6, pp.195–197; Getz, §11, pp.53–55.)

For coherent applications construct `DiscreteOccurrence(G,ω)` as an irreducible closed unitary subrepresentation, its Hilbert space, and an isometric occurrence map into the specified central-character `L²` space. A weighted occurrence for a nonunitary `ω` additionally records the positive norm twist making that map unitary. Define the discrete subspace as the closed span of such occurrence images. This small interface does not claim completeness of the automorphic spectrum. Expose `occurrenceMap`, `smoothVectors`, `kFinite`, `centralBalance` and `unitarize`. A comparison of cusp cohomology with a larger discrete space must be induced by these actual inclusions, not by assigning arbitrary coefficients the same dimension.

**Checks.**

- If the derived group is anisotropic over `F`, there are no proper rational parabolics and the cuspidal condition is vacuous. Constants on a definite quaternion example are cuspidal.
- For split `SL₂`, constants have Borel constant term one, so they are automorphic and square-integrable on the finite-volume quotient but not cuspidal.
- A nonunitary norm twist of a cusp form need not be in the unweighted `L²` space; its weighted occurrence uses the specified twist.
- A zero Hilbert space has no irreducible occurrence. A sum of two equivalent occurrence images records two maps and permits multiplicity greater than one.
- Rapid decay is measured on the central quotient and reduction sets. A nonzero unitary central eigenfunction cannot decay along a noncompact central direction before that quotient.
- For `f∈S_k(SL₂(ℤ))`, `y^{k/2}|f(x+iy)|` decays exponentially as `y→∞`; the constant function is not rapidly decreasing on a Siegel set of `SL₂`.
- Petersson norms of cusp forms on `Γ₀(N)` are finite (ModularForms Layer 3), while the unitary adelization of `E₄` grows like `y²` at the cusp and is not square-integrable.
- For `SL₁(D)`, `D` a quaternion division algebra over `ℚ`, the quotient is compact, the whole `L²` space is discrete and the trivial representation is cuspidal; for split `SL₂` the trivial representation is square-integrable and not cuspidal.
- `GL₂/ℚ` is not anisotropic modulo its centre: the diagonal torus modulo the centre is split.

### 4.3 Genericity and the cuspidal GLₙ Whittaker interface

Use the character foundations of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §2.7's scalar Fourier/Poisson prefix. Its `standardAddChar` is `ψ=ψ_ℚ∘tr_{F/ℚ}`, where `ψ_ℚ` equals `e^{2πix}` at the real place and `e^{−2πi{x}_p}` at each prime `p`, with `{x}_p` the `p`-adic fractional part. Its diagonal-triviality theorem uses `x≡Σ_p{x}_p mod ℤ` for `x∈ℚ`. The local self-duality map is `a↦(x↦ψ_v(ax))` into Mathlib's native `PontryaginDual(Multiplicative F_v)`, a topological isomorphism. The restricted-product comparison identifies `𝔸_F` with `PontryaginDual(Multiplicative 𝔸_F)` through the same pairing; at a finite place the annihilator of `O_v` is the inverse different, equal to `O_v` at almost all places. These supplied constructions have no automorphic hypotheses. (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §2.7, scalar character foundations; Kudla, *Tate's Thesis*, §3, pp.122–124, for the standard-character and measure conventions only.)

The scalar prefix's diagonal self-annihilator theorem states `F⊥={a∈𝔸_F : ∀ξ∈F, ψ(aξ)=1}=F`. The inclusion `F⊆F⊥` follows from diagonal triviality. For the reverse inclusion, adelic self-duality and compactness of `𝔸_F/F` make `F⊥` discrete; its quotient by `F` is then finite because it is closed in `𝔸_F/F`. That quotient is also an `F`-vector space, so it is zero. This arithmetic theorem, together with the dual of the quotient being the annihilator, supplies surjectivity of `addChar_equiv`: `ξ↦ψ(ξ·)` identifies the additive group of `F` with the continuous characters of `F\𝔸_F`. Injectivity uses nontriviality on `ξ𝔸_F` for `ξ≠0`. Construct this compact-quotient adapter here. (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §2.7, diagonal self-annihilator and restricted-product duality; Mathlib, `PontryaginDual`.)

With compact abelian Fourier theory (CompactGroups Layer 5), every `L²` function on `F\𝔸_F` has the expansion `Σ_{ξ∈F}c_ξψ(ξx)` with `c_ξ=∫f(x)ψ(ξx)^{−1}dx` for the probability measure; for a smooth function of fixed finite level the series converges absolutely and uniformly. Expose `addChar_equiv`, `fourierCoeff`, `fourier_expansion` and `fourierCoeff_translate` (translating by `a∈𝔸_F` multiplies `c_ξ` by `ψ(ξa)`). (CompactGroups Layer 5, compact abelian Fourier theory; Cogdell, §1.2, pp.10–11, for its use along unipotent subgroups.)

**Checks.**

- At `x=1/3` the real factor of `ψ_ℚ` is `e^{2πi/3}` and the `3`-adic factor is `e^{−2πi/3}`, so `ψ_ℚ(1/3)=1`. With the sign `e^{+2πi{x}_p}` the product would be `e^{4πi/3}≠1`, and the character would not descend to `ℚ\𝔸_ℚ`.
- For `F=ℚ`, the adele with real component `1/2` and all finite components zero is not in `ℚ⊥`: pairing with diagonal `1` gives `−1`. A diagonal rational adele lies in `ℚ⊥`.
- At `ℚ_p`, `ℤ_p⊥=ℤ_p`; at a ramified completion the formula is `O_v⊥=𝔡_v⁻¹`, so replacing every local annihilator by `O_v` loses the different.
- For `b∈𝔸_F×`, replacing the pairing by `ψ(bxy)` changes the diagonal annihilator to `b⁻¹F`. Discreteness and cocompactness of the diagonal alone do not fix this subgroup.
- `ξ=0` gives the trivial character, and `c_0` is the mean of `f`.
- The function `ψ(x)` itself has `c_1=1` and every other coefficient zero; its translate by `a` has `c_1=ψ(a)`.

For a specified nontrivial character `ψ` of an arithmetic maximal-unipotent quotient, construct `whittakerCoefficient(φ,g)=∫φ(ng)ψ(n)⁻¹dn`; for GLₙ use a nondegenerate character on every simple-root subgroup. Prove equivariance under unipotent translation and finite/right archimedean actions, and prove global cuspidal GLₙ genericity by successive Fourier expansion and cuspidal vanishing of the degenerate terms. This supplies the local genericity needed in the purity argument, without assuming a later theory of L-functions. Expose `whittakerCoefficient`, `whittaker_equivariant`, `cuspidalGLn_hasWhittaker` and `localFactor_generic`. The construction is for the actual arithmetic quotient with its supplied Fourier/character duality. (Cogdell, Theorem 1.1 and §1.2, pp.7–10, for the cuspidal Fourier expansion and Whittaker model; Jiang–Zhang, Appendix B, pp.85–86, for the local generic-unitary input.)

Prove `trivial_not_generic` on split `SL₂`: every nontrivial Fourier coefficient of a constant is zero by character orthogonality, while its constant term is nonzero. The assertion that all automorphic representations are generic is therefore false. **Checks.**

- On the circle, `∫₀¹e^{2πinx}dx=0` for nonzero integer `n`, and equals one for `n=0`. These give respectively the Whittaker and constant-term values of `1`.
- For the trivial representation of `SL₂(𝔸)`, the nondegenerate Whittaker functional is zero; for a GL₂ newform with first Fourier coefficient one it is nonzero.
- For GL₁ the unipotent is trivial, genericity is automatic, and the construction agrees with evaluating the character.
- The normalized weight-four Eisenstein form has nonzero first positive Fourier coefficient `240` and nonzero constant term `1`. It separates generic Fourier behavior from cuspidality.
- For `k≥4` even, the level-one Eisenstein series `E_k=1−(2k/B_k)Σ_{n≥1}σ_{k−1}(n)qⁿ` has every positive Fourier coefficient nonzero; at `k=4`, `−2k/B_k=240`.

### 4.4 Holomorphic SL₂ forms and a Fourier vanishing criterion

For totally real `F`, compact-open level `J⊂SL₂(𝔸_f)` and integral parallel weight `k`, define `HolomorphicSL2Form` by rational/level invariance, moderate growth, right `SO(2)` weight `k` at each real place, and annihilation by the corresponding lowering operators. Give `classicalEquiv`, `qExpansion`, `qExpansion_injective`, `rationalStructure` and `coefficientExtension`. The archimedean trivialization multiplies the adelic value by `∏y_v^{−k/2}` in the Iwasawa section. For the fractional ideal `I` dual to the translation lattice at that cusp, the Fourier index set is `{0} ∪ I_{>0}`, where `I_{>0}` consists of the totally positive elements. The zero coefficient is retained in `qExpansion`; cuspidality imposes its vanishing at every cusp. ### 5.1 Highest-weight characters and the two algebraicity conventions

Fix a field `E` of characteristic zero over which `Res_{F/ℚ}G` splits, with a Borel pair `(B,T)` over `E`. Then `X*(T)=⊕_σX*(T_σ)` over the embeddings `σ:F→E`; a weight is dominant when every `λ_σ` is, regular when every `λ_σ+ρ` is regular, and `V_λ=⊗_σV_{λ_σ}`. Construct the infinitesimal-character adapter for the supplied algebraic coefficient `V_λ`: its parameter is `λ+ρ`, and the dual has parameter `−(λ+ρ)` modulo the Weyl group. A regular infinitesimal character and a strictly dominant highest weight are different conditions; `λ=0` already gives the regular parameter `ρ`. Expose `algebraicCoefficient_infinitesimalCharacter`, `dual_infinitesimalCharacter`, `coefficient_unique` and transport along scalar extension and group isomorphisms. The coefficient uniqueness assertion is among irreducible algebraic coefficients with the prescribed group character lattice. It is not a uniqueness assertion among all finite-dimensional representations of a disconnected real point group. (Harder–Raghuram, §3.1.5, Proposition 3.11 and proof, pp.18–19; LieHighestWeight Layers 2 and 7.)

For an irreducible admissible real or complex module with parameter `μ`, define `IsCAlgebraic` by `μ−ρ∈X*(T)` up to Weyl action and `IsLAlgebraic` by `μ∈X*(T)`. For complex groups impose the corresponding condition for both embeddings. These definitions use the infinitesimal character; the full general-group local Langlands correspondence is not required just to define them. Prove independence of positive roots and embeddings, compatibility with products and duals, and the implication from cohomological to C-algebraic. If an integral twisting element `θ` with simple-coroot pairings one exists, then `θ−ρ` is central and the associated central norm twist converts the two conventions. Existence of such an element is an extra group condition. For GLₙ take the staircase weight and obtain the determinant shift by `(n−1)/2`. On parameter tuples of `GLₙ(ℝ)` the two conditions are `IsLAlgebraicGL` (integral entries) and `IsCAlgebraicGL` (entries in `(n−1)/2+ℤ`), with `isCAlgebraicGL_iff_twist`; these are the light predicates of the Lean companion. (Buzzard–Gee, Definitions 2.3.1 and 2.3.3, pp.12–13, and §5.2, pp.28–31.)

Record separately Chenevier–Taïbi's convention `IsAlgebraicCT`: a Harish-Chandra module of `GLₙ(ℝ)` is algebraic in their sense when every element of the centre `ℝ^×⊂W_ℝ` acts on its Langlands parameter (§2.5) by `±1`. This forces `ℝ_{>0}` to act trivially, so it fixes a normalization as well as integrality; for cuspidal representations of `PGLₙ` over `ℚ` it agrees with their algebraicity through Clozel's purity lemma (§5.5). A comparison with L-algebraicity after a norm twist takes the common weight and the sign condition as explicit hypotheses (`isAlgebraicCT_compat`); bare L-algebraicity is not equivalent. (Chenevier–Taïbi, §2.1, pp.12–13; Buzzard–Gee, Proposition 5.2.2, p.29, for the twisting element.)

- For GL₂, `ρ=(1/2,−1/2)`; the trivial coefficient has these two infinitesimal coordinates. The half determinant twist adds `1/2` to both to give the integral pair `(1,0)`. Adding it to only one coordinate gives `(1,−1/2)` and fails. This is a coordinate test of the C/L comparison, with twist direction stated above.
- `λ=0` gives the trivial representation. For `GL₃`, `V_(2,1,0)` has dual `V_(0,−1,−2)`. For `GL₂`, `(0,1)` is not dominant.

### 5.3 The defect and the tempered range

### 5.4 Vogan–Zuckerman modules in every degree

For a connected real reductive group in the supplied class, with maximal compact `K`, choose a fundamental Cartan `𝔟=𝔱⊕𝔞_b` and a `θ`-stable parabolic `𝔮=𝔩⊕𝔲` determined by an element of `i𝔱`. Construct the invariant positive Hermitian form and its unitary completion here; Theorem 5.3's type/character characterization identifies this induced module with `A_q(λ)`.

Every compact type of `A_q(λ)` has highest weight `λ|𝔱+2ρ(𝔲∩𝔭)+Σ_αn_αα` with `n_α≥0` and `α` a root of `𝔲∩𝔭` (Vogan–Zuckerman, Theorem 5.3(c), p.74). Expose `thetaStableParabolic`, `admissibleCharacter`, `cohomologicalInduction`, `lowestKType`, `infinitesimalCharacter` and `unitaryClassification`. Let `F` be a nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible representation of the actual connected real group, with compatible Lie and compact actions, and lowest weight `−γ` for the compatible positive system. Then cohomology of `A_q(λ)⊗F` vanishes unless `γ=λ|𝔟`; in the matching case, writing `R=dim(𝔲∩𝔭)`, it is
`Hom_{L∩K}(Λ^{i−R}(𝔩∩𝔭),ℂ)` in degree `i`, with negative exterior degrees zero. Every irreducible unitary representation with nonzero finite-dimensional algebraic-coefficient cohomology arises in this way, with the adapted coefficient/Levi data in Theorem 5.6. The datum need not be uniquely determined just by writing an arbitrary parabolic name. (Vogan–Zuckerman, Theorems 5.5–5.6, pp.74–75.) For a specified finite direct sum of irreducible coefficients, take the direct sum of their individual cohomology formulas, including repeated summands.

For the Hermitian datum of §6.1 and balanced centre, construct the Hodge decomposition. Put `R±=dim(𝔲∩𝔭±)`, and retain the nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible group coefficient `F` whose lowest weight is `−λ`. For each `p≥0`, prove `H^{p+R+,p+R−}(𝔤,𝔨;A_q(λ)⊗F)≃H^{p,p}(𝔩,𝔩∩𝔨;ℂ)≃Hom_{𝔩∩𝔨}(Λ^{2p}(𝔩∩𝔭),ℂ)`. Its dimension is the number of length-`p` representatives of `W(𝔩,𝔟)/W_{L∩K}`. A bidegree `(a,b)` with `a−b≠R+−R−`, or with either shifted index negative, contributes zero. The non-Hermitian theorem above remains independent of this specialization. (Vogan–Zuckerman, Proposition 6.19, p.84.)

**Checks.**

- For `q=g`, the nilradical is zero and `R=0`; the module is the unitary character, and the formula is the invariant exterior cohomology of `𝔭`. Vogan's range condition is vacuous, and induction preserves this unitary character.
- For the trivial group with `λ=0`, an irreducible coefficient is a line and has `H⁰=ℂ`. The reducible coefficient `ℂ²` has `H⁰=ℂ²`, the sum of two line formulas; it cannot be inserted into the single-irreducible formula.
- For `U(2,1)`, choose the holomorphic θ-stable parabolic with compact Levi and `𝔲∩𝔭=𝔭⁺`. Then `(R+,R−)=(2,0)`, and a matching discrete series/coefficient pair has `dim H^{2,0}=1` and `H^{1,1}=0`. The conjugate choice gives `(0,2)`. Thus total-degree agreement alone cannot test the Hodge convention.
- With equal ranks and connected `K`, compare the same module with Ichino–Prasanna §7.1, pp.40–41: their highest-weight-`γ` coefficient is dualized. Setting `γ=λ|𝔱` gives infinitesimal parameter `λ+ρ`, lowest compact weight `λ+2ρ(𝔲∩𝔭)` and the same shifted Hodge groups. Omitting the coefficient dual changes the match for a non-self-dual coefficient, for example a nonzero central weight on `U(2,1)`.
- For `SL₂`, the matching discrete-series coefficient has the single semisimple degree one. The trivial coefficient for the trivial module has degrees zero and two instead.
- A Lie character not integrating to `L` does not qualify. An integral group-character lattice condition cannot be replaced by a condition on the Lie algebra alone.
- A compact group has `𝔭=0` and only degree zero; a coefficient mismatch has zero invariants. Zero coefficients contribute nowhere.
- In a rank-one Hermitian example with `R+=1,R−=0`, the lowest Hodge bidegree is `(1,0)`; conjugation gives `(0,1)`. Their total degree agrees but their difference changes from `1` to `−1`.
- For `SL₂(ℝ)` the two θ-stable Borel subalgebras give `D₂^±`, each with one-dimensional `H¹`, of Hodge types `(1,0)` and `(0,1)`.
- The Hermitian formula needs the split centre removed: for the raw pair of `GL₂(ℝ)` the central direction contributes a second copy of the cohomology shifted up by one degree.

### 5.5 Cuspidal GLₙ purity and weight-zero components

Prove `clozelPurity` for cuspidal GLₙ automorphic representations algebraic in the fixed Clozel convention: every archimedean factor is essentially tempered. Write the raw Langlands parameter on `ℂ×` as `⊕z^{a_i}zbar^{b_i}`. Its twist by `|·|_ℂ^{(1−n)/2}` has integral exponents `p_i=a_i+(1−n)/2`, `q_i=b_i+(1−n)/2` and common sum `w_C∈ℤ`, independent of `i` and the archimedean place. Thus the raw sums are `w_raw=w_C+n−1`, and `π⊗|det|^{−w_raw/2}=π⊗|det|^{(1−n−w_C)/2}` is tempered at every archimedean place. A unitary raw parameter has common real weight zero; the integral Clozel weight is then `1−n`. The proof uses global cuspidal genericity from §4.3, the local generic unitary dual and the algebraic exponent restrictions, together with the archimedean purity argument. This is the algebraic cuspidal theorem, not Ramanujan for arbitrary Maass forms. (Clozel, Lemme 4.9, pp.144–145; compare Calegari–Geraghty, *Minimal modularity lifting for nonregular symplectic representations*, proof of Theorem 7.11, pp.39–40, and Chenevier–Taïbi, §2.1, pp.12–13.)

Define cohomological weight zero by `χ_π∞=χ_1`, equality with the infinitesimal character of the trivial representation. For a real GLₙ parameter tuple, `IsCohomologicalWeightZeroGL(μ)` means that `μ` is a permutation of `ρ`; it is distinct from the purity condition `w_raw=0`. Construct the classification of irreducible tempered `GL_n(ℝ)` modules with this infinitesimal character for every `n≥1`, using the trivial coefficient. For even `n` there is one full-group module, its restriction to the identity component has two irreducible constituents, and its `(𝔤𝔩_n,SO(n))` cohomology is free over `ℂ[O(n)/SO(n)]`. For odd `n` there are two connected-pair cohomological modules differing by sign, with component action respectively `+1` and `−1`; only the first has full-`O(n)` cohomology. Purity and genericity place cohomological-weight-zero cuspidal infinity types in this list. Central balance permits the split-centre quotient; full-`K` cohomology takes component invariants as in §5.2. (Clozel, Lemme 3.14, pp.114–120; Boxer–Calegari–Gee, Introduction and Remark 1.2, pp.1,3–4.)

**Checks.**

- For `D_k(μ)`, both parameter exponent sums are `μ`; unitary `D_k(0)` has common weight zero and the cohomological twist `D_k(2−k)` has common weight `2−k`. It is not always `k−1`.
- For `n=2,k=12`, the cohomological raw sum is `−10`, its integral Clozel weight is `−11`, and the tempered twist exponent is `5`: `−10+2·5=0`, whereas the inverse twist gives `−20`. For a unitary raw sum zero, `w_C=−1` and the twist is zero.
- At `n=1`, `1` and `sgn` have infinitesimal character zero and give the two component actions on connected-pair cohomology. The raw connected pair has degrees zero and one, and the central quotient only degree zero; full `O(1)` kills the sign case.
- At `n=2`, the module is `D₂`; its `SO(2)` semisimple degree-one cohomology is `ℂ[C₂]`, and the raw `gl₂` pair has an additional degree-two copy from the central direction.
- The unitary `D₁₂(0)` of Δ has `w_raw=0` and coefficient `Sym¹⁰⊗det⁻⁵`, but its infinitesimal parameter is `(11/2,−11/2)` and its Casimir is `30`. It fails `IsCohomologicalWeightZeroGL`; `D₂(0)` has parameter `(1/2,−1/2)` and Casimir zero and satisfies it.
- At level one and odd `n`, only the trivial central-character component can occur globally. The local sign choice is not automatically a second level-one automorphic occurrence.
- A nonalgebraic cuspidal Maass representation is outside the purity hypothesis; no spectral lower bound is deduced from it.

### 5.6 Local and finite-adelic stable lattices

For a finite extension `E/ℚ_p`, finite rank `n≥0` and compact subgroup `J⊂GL_n(E)`, use EtaleCohomologyFoundations F.4.0's `GaloisLattice.IntegralModel` for the jointly continuous tautological action of `J` on `E^n`. Its underlying submodule satisfies Mathlib's `Submodule.IsLattice E`; stability under all group elements implies setwise equality. Instantiate `ContinuousRep.exists_stable_lattice_iff` and `GaloisLattice.IntegralModel.nonempty` for existence and `exists_pow_le` for commensurability, rather than constructing another local carrier. The Lean companion states the underlying native lattice and stability Checks directly on submodules, as `CoefficientLattices.rankZero_full_stable`, `CoefficientLattices.rankOne_bot_not_full`, `CoefficientLattices.scale_full_stable` and `CoefficientLattices.scale_ne_self`.

Extend this integral-model interface to an equal-characteristic nonarchimedean local field `E`, its valuation ring `O_E` and a jointly continuous action of a compact group on a finite-dimensional `E`-space. Prove existence of a stable `Submodule.IsLattice E`: compact matrix entries put every translate of a standard lattice inside one uniformizer-scaled lattice, and Noetherianity makes their sum finitely generated. Clearing denominators proves commensurability; conjugation transports the action and its stable submodule. Specialize the supplied integral-model structure to these coefficients and construct its equivariant generic-fibre comparison. (EtaleCohomologyFoundations F.4.0 for characteristic zero; Darmon–Diamond–Taylor, [Fermat's Last Theorem](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/darmon/pub/Articles/Expository/05.DDT/paper.pdf), §2.1, p.53, for the compact-image argument, extended here by the local DVR proof; Ding, arXiv v2, §4.2.2, pp.71–72, for the coefficient-lattice setting.)

For an algebraic representation over a number field `E`, construct `StableAdelicLattice(J,V)` as a family of local integral lattices, equal to a fixed reference family almost everywhere and stable under the given compact-open finite adelic action. Over `E⊗ℚ_p` work factor by factor; global integral lattices need not be free over `O_E`. Prove existence by the local result and integral-model spreading, and prove two such families coincide after inverting one nonzero integer. Expose `local`, `restrictedFamily`, `exists`, `eqAfterInverting`, `restrictLevel`, `conjugate`, `reduction` and `componentLocalSystem`. On a component represented by `t`, transport the lattice so the actual arithmetic stabilizer preserves it. A global `G(F)`-stable integral lattice is not asserted. (Ding, arXiv v2, §4.2.2, pp.71–72; AdelicAlgebraicGroups §1.1 integral models; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.F.7.)

**Checks.**

- At rank zero the zero submodule is the unique full lattice. At rank one it is not full and is excluded.
- If all matrices of `J`, including its inverses, have integral entries, the standard coordinate lattice is stable. The subgroup of all diagonal powers `diag(p^m,1)` is noncompact and has no stable full lattice.
- For positive rank, `pL` is another stable full lattice and is distinct from `L`. This includes `p=2`; equality would contradict Nakayama's theorem for the nonzero finite `ℤ_p`-module `L`.
- Over `𝔽_q((t))`, the integral group preserves `𝔽_q[[t]]^n`; at positive rank its `t`-multiple is distinct.
- Over `𝔽_q((t))` at rank zero, the zero model is the unique integral model.
- The cyclic subgroup generated by multiplication by `t` on `𝔽_q((t))` is noncompact and preserves no full lattice.
- Under conjugation the correct stabilizer is `gJg⁻¹` on `gL`; multiplying back by `g⁻¹` recovers `L`.
- In `Sym²(ℚ²)` the image of the divided-power lattice has basis `e₁²,2e₁e₂,e₂²` inside the symmetric-power lattice, of index two. The two lattices agree away from two but are nonhomothetic at two. In degree `m`, all denominator primes are at most `m`.
- Rank-one `O_E` lattices can be nonprincipal fractional ideals. The assertion that every one is generated by a single element is valid only with a principality hypothesis.
- Membership in the standard lattice is coordinatewise `p`-integrality.
- For `E=ℚ` and the trivial rank-one representation, the stable finite-adelic lattices are `cẐ`, `c∈𝔸_f^×`, determined up to `Ẑ^×`.
- For split `G` over `ℤ`, the Kostant `ℤ`-form of `V_λ` is `G(Ẑ)`-stable.
- For `GL₂/ℚ` and `V=Sym^{k−2}(ℚ²)`, `Sym^{k−2}(Ẑ²)` is `GL₂(Ẑ)`-stable and differs from the divided-power lattice only at primes `p≤k−2`; for `p>k−2` they agree at `p`, which is why integral Eichler–Shimura comparisons assume `p>k−2`.

### 5.7 Fields of rationality and descent

Define `galoisTwist(πf,τ)` by scalar extension through `τ∈Aut(ℂ)` on the smooth finite-part representation, and `rationalityField` as the fixed field of its isomorphism stabilizer. This section is the common owner of the scalar twist, stabilizer field and Clozel arithmetic finiteness theorem below, for arbitrary number fields. Define `IsFieldOfDefinition(E,πf)` by an actual smooth `E`-model and a scalar-extension equivalence. Expose `twist_id`, `twist_comp`, `rationalityField_le`, `model_baseChange` and finite-level Hecke-eigenvalue comparisons. A field of definition contains the rationality field, but descent requires a cocycle and its obstruction. No discontinuous automorphism of ℂ is applied to a topological infinity representation. (Harder–Raghuram, §2.3.4, pp.14–15.)

Prove `localExactDescent` for an irreducible admissible complex representation of `GL_n(F_v)` at a finite place: it has a model over its rationality field, unique up to complex homothety. The proof must construct rational descent of the local representation, using the multiplicity-one model in the generic case and the rational local classification for the remaining case; these are additional local rational-descent targets, not consequences of Schur's lemma alone. Combine these local models by restricted tensor product to prove `glFinitePart_exactDescent` for irreducible admissible finite-adelic GLₙ representations, with rationality field the compositum of the local rationality fields. No algebraicity, automorphy or number-field conclusion is assumed in these two representation-theoretic descent statements. (Clozel, Propositions 3.1–3.2, pp.102–104.)

Prove Clozel's arithmetic rationality theorem for regular algebraic cuspidal GLₙ representations in the cohomological normalization: the rationality field is a number field and the finite part has a model over that exact field. For a general reductive group, formulate the arithmetic theorem with an explicit smooth admissible Hecke-stable cuspidal cohomology summand over the coefficient number field, with compatible level maps and finite-dimensional fixed-level spaces, containing the occurrence under consideration; then its rationality field is a number field and a model exists over a finite extension. Construct the Hecke idempotent/isotypic descent and the obstruction-killing finite extension. The existence of that rational cuspidal summand is an additional hypothesis in the general-group statement, not a consequence of merely being cohomological; for regular C-algebraic cuspidal `GLₙ`, use the connected-compact cuspidal cohomology summand with its component action, by Clozel's theorem above. (Clozel, Théorème 3.13, p.112, and Proposition 3.16 with proof, pp.122–123; Harder–Raghuram, §2.3.4, pp.14–15.)

For every regular C-algebraic cuspidal `GLₙ` representation `π` over a number field, let `V_λ` be the algebraic coefficient selected by `χ_π∞=χ_(V_λ^∨)`. Prove central balance and `HasConnectedCohomologyWith(π∞,V_λ)`. Prove `galoisConjugate_cuspidal`: for each `τ∈Aut(ℂ)` there is a regular C-algebraic cuspidal representation `π^τ` whose finite part is `galoisTwist(πf,τ)` and for which `HasConnectedCohomologyWith((π^τ)∞,τV_λ)` holds, with its component action retained. Full-`K` cohomology is the component-invariant part and is nonzero exactly when that part is nonzero in some degree. Coefficient conjugation permutes labelled embeddings and extends algebraic scalars. Construct the conjugate on isomorphism classes and prove identity and composition compatibility. For independence of choices, use §5.5's purity and §2.5's Weil parameters with §5.1's infinitesimal character. The essentially tempered parameter is determined by these weights, except for a real sign choice fixed by the central character. Weak approximation determines the global central character from its finite part; §3.5's factorization identifies the full representation. (Clozel, Théorème 3.13, p.112, and Lemme 3.14, pp.114–120; Buzzard–Gee, §7.2, p.37.)

**Checks.**

- The trivial finite-part representation has rationality field ℚ and its evident ℚ-model. Its cuspidal GL₁ conjugate is itself for every `τ`.
- For a finite-order GL₁ Hecke character with a value `ζ_m`, the cuspidal conjugate has that value `τ(ζ_m)` and remains finite order; when `m≤2` every conjugate is the original character.
- The quadratic Hecke character of the odd primitive Dirichlet character `χ₋₄` has infinity component `sgn`, conductor `4` and rationality field ℚ, and every conjugate equals itself. It is regular C-algebraic and has connected-pair cohomology with the trivial coefficient; the component action is sign. Section 5.2's calculation excludes full-`O(1)` cohomology with every algebraic coefficient, including at `τ=id`.
- For a normalized GL₂ newform in the cohomological normalization, coefficient conjugation gives the cuspidal newform with coefficients `τ(a_n)` and the conjugated algebraic coefficient. Applying `τ⁻¹` recovers its automorphic isomorphism class; identity and successive conjugations give the identity and composite scalar twists of the finite part.
- A finite-order character whose values generate `ℚ(ζ_m)` has that rationality field; at order one or two it is ℚ.
- The two-dimensional complex irreducible of the quaternion group has rational character but no two-dimensional ℚ-model. It tests the difference between a stabilizer field and a field of definition.
- For a normalized GL₂ newform `f`, the finite part with infinity type `D_k(2−k)` has field `ℚ(a_n(f))`. The unitary normalization involves square-root prime factors in its normalized Hecke eigenvalues and does not inherit this equality automatically.
- A noncohomological Maass form is excluded from the arithmetic number-field theorem. Its finite GLₙ part still satisfies exact-field descent, without an assertion that this field is finite over ℚ.
- For an unramified GL₁ local character taking a uniformizer to a transcendental `t∈ℂ×`, the local rationality field and exact field of definition are `ℚ(t)`. This tests the distinction between exact-field descent and arithmetic finiteness.

### 5.8 Residual and torsion Hecke eigenclasses

For a commutative Hecke ring `T`, a coefficient ring `Λ`, a module `H` with the specified commuting coefficient/Hecke actions, define `TorsionEigenSystem` as data `(θ,c)` with a unital ring map `θ:T→Λ`, nonzero `c∈H`, and `tc=θ(t)c`. In arithmetic use the supplied integral Betti cohomology with a stable coefficient lattice and `Λ=O_E/λ^m`, `m≥1`. Use a neat level or the supplied stabilizer/groupoid complex; arbitrary orbifold cohomology does not acquire integral base change by declaration. Expose `system`, `class`, `eigen`, `map`, `map_system`, `maximalKernel` and `lattice_indep_awayFromIndex`. (Scholze, Introduction, Conjecture I.2 and discussion, pp.5–6, and §V.4 for the arithmetic setting.)

For finite-field `Λ`, the image of `θ` is a finite integral domain, hence a field; `ker θ` is maximal even without surjectivity. A `κ`-semilinear Hecke-equivariant map sends the system to `(κ∘θ,f(c))` only if `f(c)≠0`. Reduction from characteristic zero requires an actual integral eigenclass and a nonzero reduction, not only integral eigenvalues. Lattice independence away from the finite comparison index uses the explicit localization isomorphism of §5.6. Neither residual classes nor integral torsion are defined as classes already known to lift. For weight-two `GL₂/ℚ` eigenclasses, reduction from characteristic zero needs a neat level, a specified integral Hecke-equivariant model with base change and a characteristic-zero lifting theorem; that comparison belongs to the consumer and is not proved here.

**Checks.**

- The zero cohomology module has no eigenclass. The zero vector satisfies formal eigen-equations but fails the nonzero condition.
- The same nonfaithful class may support different systems: take `T=ℤ[X]`, `H=Λ=ℤ/8`, let `T` act by evaluation at `X=1`, and take `c=4`. Evaluation at `X=1` and at `X=−1` give distinct `TorsionEigenSystem` characters on this same class (`TorsionEigenSystem.same_class_distinct_characters`): the two polynomial values differ by an even integer, which kills `4` modulo eight.
- A field map with image a proper finite subfield still has maximal kernel. Surjectivity onto the chosen eigenvalue field is not required.
- In degree zero with trivial GL₂ coefficients, the raw `T_ℓ` eigenvalue is `1+ℓ`, the number of correspondence sheets; at `ℓ=2` it is three.
- For trivial integral coefficients, `H¹(Γ,ℤ)=Hom(Γ,ℤ)` is torsion-free. Nonliftable mod-`p` degree-one classes can instead come from `p`-torsion in `H²(Γ,ℤ)` through the universal-coefficient sequence. Calling that integral degree-one torsion would be incorrect.
- A specified integral weight-two cuspidal eigenclass gives a residual class only at primes where its reduction is nonzero. A semilinear map killing it supplies no eigenclass data.
- With trivial coefficients, `p`-torsion in `H²(Γ,ℤ)` is the `p`-torsion of `H₁(Γ,ℤ)=Γ^{ab}`; nonliftable mod-`p` classes in degree one come from it, as for Bianchi groups.

The residual scalar module `H=𝔽₂` with Hecke ring and eigenvalue ring both `𝔽₂`, character the identity and class `1`, gives a genuine nonzero `TorsionEigenSystem`. Its kernel is zero and maximal. This tests existence in characteristic two, whereas a zero coefficient module has no such data.

<a id="anemic-eigensystems"></a>

**Universal anemic presentation and eigensystems (`AnemicHeckePresentation`, `AnemicEigenSystem`).** Let `V` be an index type, `S⊂V` finite, and `𝒪` a commutative coefficient ring; in the arithmetic specialization `V` is the finite places of a number field. Define the universal presentation `𝕋^S= MvPolynomial ({v : V // v ∉ S} × Fin 2) 𝒪`, with generators `T_v=X(v,0)` and `S_v=X(v,1)`. For a commutative `𝒪`-algebra `R`, an `AnemicEigenSystem` is an algebra map `θ:𝕋^S→ₐ[𝒪]R`, with accessors `t_v=θ(T_v)` and `s_v=θ(S_v)`; it asserts neither automorphic occurrence nor a nonzero eigenclass. Prove that these two coordinates determine the map. Define `eigenIdeal(θ)=ker θ`, coefficient transport `map κ θ=κ∘θ`, and restriction on enlarging `S`. Define `AgreesAwayFromFinite` for systems with possibly different excluded sets by equality of both coordinates outside one finite set containing both excluded sets; prove reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, and compatibility with coefficient transport and restriction. Define `twist χ θ` for a unit-valued function on the good places by `(t_v,s_v)↦(χ_v t_v,χ_v² s_v)`, and prove the identity, composition and coefficient-transport laws. For a character twist require `χ_v` to be its unramified local value, enlarging `S` to contain its ramification.

For each actual good-prime Hecke module `H`, construct `anemicRealization:𝕋^S→ₐ[𝒪]T_H`, where `T_H` is the image generated by its arithmetic `T_v,S_v` operators in `End_𝒪(H)`. Prove their commutation from the supplied spherical Hecke theorem and disjoint-place actions; commutativity is a theorem about this image. The polynomial ring is the universal presentation; the acting anemic Hecke algebra is `T_H`, with its actual relations. This image need not contain inverses of central operators. An abstract `θ` descends to `T_H` exactly when `ker(anemicRealization)⊆eigenIdeal(θ)`. Prove the unique factorization under this hypothesis. Pulling a `TorsionEigenSystem T_H H R` back along the realization gives its universal ring character and the same nonzero eigenclass. Conversely, a universal system with a nonzero eigenclass and the relation-kernel inclusion gives an eigensystem on the image. Retain the `𝒪`-algebra compatibility of the character and coefficient action in this comparison. A finite eigenvalue field gives a maximal `eigenIdeal`, while a general coefficient algebra need not. (Mathlib, `Algebra/MvPolynomial/Eval.lean`, `MvPolynomial.aeval_X` and `MvPolynomial.aeval_unique`; Calegari–Geraghty, *Modularity lifting beyond the Taylor–Wiles method*, §§3.2.3–3.2.4 and §9.1, pp.85–86; Scholze, Introduction, pp.5–6, and §V.4.)

**Checks.**

- With `V∖S` empty the presentation is `𝒪`; its unique `𝒪`-algebra map to `R` has no prime coordinates. This gives no nonzero class in a zero Hecke module.
- Two systems with all `t_v=0` but `s_v=1` and `s_v=−1` at infinitely many good places over ℚ do not agree away from a finite set. Comparing traces alone loses this distinction.
- A proposed value `t_v=1` cannot descend through the relation `T_v=0` over a nonzero coefficient ring. A polynomial character alone does not satisfy automorphic relations.
- For the class `4` modulo eight with `ℤ[X]` acting by evaluation at `1`, evaluations at `1` and `−1` stay distinct as maps from a one-variable polynomial presentation, even though both satisfy the eigen-equations. The universal map and the eigenclass are separate data.
- Over `𝔽₇`, twisting coordinates `(t,s)=(2,3)` by `χ=2` gives `(4,5)`; a linear twist of the second coordinate would instead give six.

<a id="galois-anemic-eigensystem"></a>

**Galois eigensystems (`galoisAnemicEigenSystem`).** Let `ρ` be a continuous representation of the absolute Galois group of a number field on a finite free rank-two module over a commutative `𝒪`-algebra `R`, unramified outside `S`. Assume `q_v=N(v)` maps to a unit in `R` for every `v∉S`. Using arithmetic Frobenius from ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §§2.1–2.2, construct the universal system with `t_v=tr ρ(Frob_v)` and `s_v=q_v⁻¹ det ρ(Frob_v)`. Prove independence of the Frobenius lift and basis, coefficient change and unramified-character-twist compatibility, and the characteristic-polynomial formula `det(X−ρ(Frob_v))=X²−t_v X+q_v s_v`. Its kernel is `eigenIdeal` of this system. Descending it to an acting Hecke algebra retains precisely the relation-kernel hypothesis above; no attachment or automorphic existence theorem is inferred from the construction. (Calegari–Geraghty, §9.1, Conjecture B's normalization, p.86, specialized to rank two; Mathlib, `MvPolynomial.aeval` and `Matrix.charpoly_fin_two` in `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Charpoly/Coeff.lean`.)

**Checks.**

- Over `𝔽₇`, `q_v=3` and determinant six give `s_v=2`, so `q_v s_v=6`.
- If `q_v=0` in the coefficient field, the formula using `q_v⁻¹` is unavailable; in residual characteristic `p`, all places above `p` belong to `S`.
- Twisting `ρ` by an unramified character multiplies its trace by `χ_v` and its determinant by `χ_v²`, hence gives the two-coordinate twist above.

### 6.1 The Hermitian group datum and its centre

Define `HermitianGroupDatum` for a real reductive group with an elliptic Cartan modulo its full real split centre. Its data include a smooth real-group homomorphism `h:ℂ×→G(ℝ)`, its selected Hermitian symmetric component, the stabilizer `Kʰ=Z_G(h)(ℝ)`, and the eigenspace decomposition
`𝔤=𝔨ʰ⊕𝔭⁺⊕𝔭⁻`.
Require the Hermitian symmetric bracket relations, in particular `[𝔭⁺,𝔭⁺]=[𝔭⁻,𝔭⁻]=0`, and the conjugate-linear real-form identification between the two noncompact summands. These conditions are attached to the actual adjoint representation of `h`. They cannot be arbitrary unrelated subspaces with the same dimensions. Put `𝔭_h=𝔨ʰ⊕𝔭⁻`. Its group of coefficients is the selected Hodge stabilizer, not every component of a maximal compact of the full real group. (Harris, §3, pp.60–63; Boxer–Pilloni, §1.3.7, p.4.)

Let `A_h` be the identity component of the full real split centre. Require `Kʰ/A_h` compact and use its compact Cartan and actual character lattice. This quotient differs from the global `A∞`: for `Res_{F/ℚ}GSp₄`, with `F` totally real of degree `d`, `A_h≃(ℝ_{>0})^d` whereas `A∞` is its diagonal positive line. In every relative coherent tensor product, specify an `A_h`-character on the representation and its inverse on the coefficient before passing to the quotient. The balanced Lie pair is `(𝔭_h/𝔞_h,Kʰ/A_h)`. An unbalanced tensor does not descend. Global units and the other `d−1` real central directions are not silently removed from ordinary automorphic cohomology.

Expose `hodgeStabilizer`, `positiveSplitCentre`, `compactQuotient`, `pPlus`, `pMinus`, `parabolicLie`, `bracket_pp`, `bracket_mm`, `compactRootSystem` and `noncompactPositiveRoots`. The complex structure fixes the sign of noncompact roots; choose a positive system of compact roots separately, then a compatible full system. It does not canonically choose a Borel inside the compact group. Construct base change, products and restriction-of-scalars adapters using the supplied group operations. A geometric Shimura datum will later produce this datum; no geometric assertion is assumed here.

**Checks.**

- For SL₂, the compact weights on `𝔭⁺,𝔭⁻` are `2,−2`, and `dimℂ 𝔭⁻=1`. The two summands must not be interchanged while retaining the same holomorphic convention.

- For a compact group modulo its full real split centre, both noncompact summands are zero. Coherent cohomology then has degree zero only.
- In a product of two Hermitian factors, the antiholomorphic tangent dimension is the sum and `A_h` is the product of their split centres. It is not replaced by a diagonal copy without further quotient data.
- A coefficient with central weight `w` paired with a representation of the same nonzero weight has total weight `2w`; its balanced quotient is undefined. Pairing weights `w` and `−w` gives zero, including at weight one.
- On `SL₂`, quarter-turn conjugation on the complex noncompact tangent lines has eigenvalues `i²=−1` and `i^{−2}=−1`; to distinguish the weights use the infinitesimal values `2` and `−2`, which differ. The coefficient `−2` cancels the holomorphic weight `2`, while `+2` gives total `4`.
- For `SL₂`, there are no compact roots and the single noncompact positive root spans `𝔭⁺`.
- For `GSp₄` in compact coordinates the roots are `±(2,0;0)`, `±(0,2;0)`, `±(1,1;0)` and the compact `±(1,−1;0)`; the holomorphic noncompact positives are `(2,0;0)`, `(1,1;0)`, `(0,2;0)`. Either compact root may be declared positive, and both choices give the same holomorphic noncompact positives.

### 6.3 GSp₄ character lattices, roots and chambers

Use the split torus `diag(t₁,t₂,νt₁⁻¹,νt₂⁻¹)` and write its character as `(a,b;c_T)`, meaning `t₁^a t₂^b ν^{c_T}`. Its lattice is `ℤ³`, with no parity restriction. For the compact Cartan use `(a,b;c_H)`, with lattice `c_H≡a+b (mod 2)`. The central conversion is `c_H=a+b+2c_T`; this parity condition belongs to compact coordinates, not to split coordinates. Record both maps as equivalences of lattices and their inverse `(c_H−a−b)/2`. (Pitale–Schmidt, §2.1, pp.2–4; Calegari–Geraghty, *Minimal modularity lifting for nonregular symplectic representations*, §5.3, Theorem 5.5 and its proof, pp.21–22; Pilloni, §§15.2.1–15.2.2, pp.107–108.)

Choose the positive roots in split coordinates
`(1,−1;0), (0,2;−1), (1,1;−1), (2,0;−1)`.
The first is compact. Thus `ρ_T=(2,1;−3/2)` and `ρ_H=(2,1;0)`. The half-sum need not belong to the integral character lattice; Harish-Chandra parameters belong to its `ρ`-translate. For a dominant coherent weight `μ=(a,b;c_T)`, `a≥b`, the dual compact coefficient has weight
`σ=(−b,−a;a+b+2c_T)`.
In this convention its corresponding representation has Harish-Chandra parameter
`λ=(a−1,b−2;−a−b−2c_T)`;
its contragredient has parameter
`λ∨=(2−b,1−a;a+b+2c_T)`.
The parameter's central coordinate is its infinitesimal central character: the representation has weight `−a−b−2c_T`, cancelling the coefficient's weight `a+b+2c_T`. The coordinate maps extend as integer formulas to all triples; representation assertions require the dominance and chamber hypotheses stated here. Specify these as the coordinate comparison with the opposite-positive-root convention in Pilloni, not as a second incompatible definition of `ρ`. Reversing all positives changes `ρ_H` to `(−2,−1;0)` and `ρ_T` to `(−2,−1;3/2)`.

Define the four closed chambers inside compact dominance `x≥y` by
`C₀: x≥y≥0`, `C₁: x≥−y≥0`, `C₂: −y≥x≥0`, and `C₃: −y≥−x≥0`.
Their interiors are the four discrete-series chambers. The compact longest element swaps `x,y`; it is not the full longest element, which also changes signs. Contragredience sends `C_i` to `C_{3−i}`. A nondegenerate limit has compact regularity `x>y` and lies on a noncompact wall, with the compatible positive-system choice included. A point of the compact wall `x=y` is not made nondegenerate by naming a chamber. (Harris, Theorem 3.4, p.63; Pilloni, §§15.2.1–15.2.2, pp.107–108.)

Write `π(λ,C)` for the discrete series or nondegenerate limit with parameter `λ` in the closed chamber `C`; then `π(λ,C)^*≃π(−w_{0,M}λ,−w_{0,M}C)`. A split weight `μ` is regular when `(a−1,b−2)` lies in an open chamber and a limit weight when it lies in exactly two closed chambers. The identification of these coordinate predicates with the classified representations uses the real and Hermitian data of §§2.4 and 6.1. Expose `splitCharacter`, `compactCharacter`, `toCompact`, `toSplit`, `rho`, `coherentCoefficient`, `harishChandraParameter`, `dualParameter`, `chamber`, `compactRegular`, `nondegenerateLimit` and `dsRep_dual`. The three dominant-weight families meeting a noncompact wall are `(a,2)`, `a≥2`; `(a,3−a)`, `a≥2`; and `(1,b)`, `b≤1`. These are the integral solutions of `b−2=0`, `(a−1)+(b−2)=0`, and `a−1=0`, respectively, after compact regularity. The three wall equations and the compact equation `a−b+1=0` must be tested separately. For the notation `a=k+r`, `b=r`, `k≥0`, noncompact regularity is exactly `r≠2`, `k+r≠1`, `k+2r≠3`; the compact pairing is `k+1>0` automatically. Expose this equivalence as `regular_coherentWeight_iff`, keeping it distinct from a sufficiently large weight bound.

### 6.5 Coherent cohomology of discrete series and their limits

Fix a compact positive system and the holomorphic noncompact positives from §6.1. `H^q(𝔭_h,Kʰ;π(λ,Ψ)^*⊗V_τ)`
is one-dimensional exactly when `τ=λ−ρ` in the fixed compact-dominant convention and `q=q(λ,Ψ)`, and is zero otherwise. Balance the split centre before interpreting this notation. The representation in this formula is dualized. Applying the formula to an undualized automorphic representation requires §6.3's dual-parameter map. (Harris, Theorem 3.4, p.63; its original input is Blasius–Harris–Ramakrishnan.)

Prove the tempered converse: an irreducible tempered representation with nonzero coherent cohomology for an irreducible finite-dimensional coefficient is a discrete series or a nondegenerate limit of discrete series, on the selected component. An essentially tempered representation is first twisted by the central character required to balance the coefficient. **Checks.**

### 6.6 GSp₄ large weights and the three distinct classifications

For `λ=(x,y)`, define the root-separation predicate
`Separated(R,λ) :⇔ ∀t∈{x−y,x,x+y,y}, t=0 ∨ R<|t|`.
Keep strict inequality and allow a zero pairing. These are the four positive-coroot pairings for type `C₂`; central coordinates do not occur. For a classical dominant weight `κ=(k,l)`, set `λ_κ=(k−1,l−2)`, the semisimple projection `hcParameter` of §6.3's `harishChandraParameter`. At a specified split central coordinate `c_T`, the full parameter is `(k−1,l−2;−k−l−2c_T)` and the coefficient has the opposite central weight. Expose `corootValues`, `Separated`, `hcParameter`, `separated_of_weight_gaps`, `weight_gaps_of_separated` and Weyl/sign invariance. A sufficient elementary condition is `k−l>R+2` and either `l=2` or `l>R+2`, with `R>0`. Conversely, for `R>2`, `k≥l≥2` and separation, `R−1<k−l` and either `l=2` or `R<l`. The constants in these elementary implications need not be the same constant chosen by an analytic theorem. (BCGP21, §3.10, Theorem 3.10.1, p.70, and its proof, pp.72–74.)

First prove the global large-weight theorem for `Res_{F/ℚ}GSp₄`, `F` totally real, with the central-character normalization and weighted discrete Hilbert realization of BCGP21. There exists `R>2` such that, for every admissible weight `k_v≥l_v≥2` at every real place with the prescribed central parity, every actual discrete automorphic occurrence having nonzero coherent cohomology at that coefficient is essentially tempered at infinity whenever all `λ_{κ_v}` are `Separated(R,−)`. The constant is chosen before the weight, occurrence and cohomological degree. The central character in BCGP21 is `|·|²`; a norm twist to this normalization is specified, rather than asserted to be unitary. The large-weight result does not apply to an arbitrary abstract nonunitary local module. (BCGP21, §3.10, p.73, using Blasius–Harris–Ramakrishnan, Theorem 4.2.3.)

Second, for the singular family `λ=(a,0;c)`, `a<0`, with `λ∈X*(T)+ρ`, prove the essentially unitary local exclusion theorem: for fixed central normalization there is a bound `R` such that `−a≥R` and nonzero degree-zero, respectively degree-one, coherent cohomology with `V_{(−a+1,2;−c)}` force the holomorphic, respectively generic, nondegenerate limit associated to `λ`. This is the sign-corrected large-weight direction of Pilloni's Theorem 15.2.2.1(2), p.108: the parameter used immediately before it is negative and the subsequent classical weight tends to positive infinity. The printed condition `a≥R` cannot express that family. Keep the essentially unitary and fixed-central-normalization hypotheses; root separation alone is not a replacement for them.

Third prove the local lowest-weight theorem without a large-weight hypothesis: for an irreducible admissible positive-component representation and a dominant weight `k≥l≥2` with its specified central character, nonzero coherent degree zero at the dual compact coefficient of §6.3 forces the holomorphic lowest-weight representation, discrete series if `l>2`, nondegenerate limit if `l=2`. Its proof uses the lowering-annihilated minimal `U(2)`-type and the unique irreducible lowest-weight quotient, not an assertion that an arbitrary unitary representation is tempered. For the essentially tempered representations at this coefficient, the complete list is: a holomorphic discrete series in degree zero if `l>2`; a holomorphic limit in degree zero if `l=2`; and a generic limit in degree one if `l=2`. Each listed group is one-dimensional, and the other degrees vanish. This is a statement about the fixed dominant coefficient, not about every possible coefficient of a discrete-series packet. (Pitale–Schmidt, §2.3, equation (2), p.6; Harris, Theorem 3.4, p.63; Pilloni, Theorem 15.2.2.1(1), p.108.)

For a totally real product, tensor these local calculations and use Künneth. In the weight `(k_v,2)` case a choice of a generic limit at precisely `r` places contributes in degree `r`, with one-dimensional infinity cohomology. The representation-theoretic statement is in every degree. BCGP21's later geometric comparison in degrees at most one after a non-Eisenstein localization is a separate input, not an all-degree conclusion of the comparison used there. No effective value of the constants `R` is asserted, and small weights are not covered: a non-tempered essentially unitary module can have nonzero coherent cohomology at a small weight. In Pilloni's compact coordinates the condition `λ∈X*(T)+ρ` for `λ=(a,0;c)` means that `a−c` is odd. The local lowest-weight statement uses no localization, arithmetic occurrence or regularity.

**Checks.**

- At `(12,12)`, the pairings are `1,11,21,10`; the compact-root pairing violates separation for `R=5` despite large individual entries.

### 6.7 Higher-rank Siegel coherent packets

### 6.8 Scalar holomorphic discrete series

For `n≥1`, construct the scalar holomorphic discrete series for `Sp₂n(ℝ)` and `U(n,n)`. Let `χ` be `det` on `U(n)` in the symplectic case, and `det(g₁)det(g₂)⁻¹` on `U(n)×U(n)` in the unitary case. For integral `k>n`, respectively `k≥n`, there is a unique discrete series with minimal compact type `χ^k`. Its Harish-Chandra parameter is
`(k−1,k−2,…,k−n)`
in the symplectic case, and
`(k−1/2,k−3/2,…,k−n+1/2,n−1/2−k,…,1/2−k)`
in the unitary case. Prove the parameter from the minimal-type identity `Λ=λ+ρ_n−ρ_c` and regularity in the indicated chamber, then identify the representation by the classification. This assertion concerns the specified groups and compact types, not every similitude extension. (Scholze, Proposition V.1.1 and proof, pp.79–80.)

Expose `scalarHolomorphicSeries`, `minimalCompactType`, `scalarParameter`, `parameter_regular`, `minimalType_unique` and the product construction over all real places. The endoscopic and Galois consequences following that proposition are consumers, not additional conclusions of this local construction.

**Checks.**

- For `Sp₂=SL₂`, `k=2` gives parameter `1` and the usual holomorphic `D₂`. The boundary `k=1` gives parameter zero, a limit rather than the discrete series in this theorem.
- For `Sp₄`, `k=3` gives parameter `(2,1)`. At `k=2`, the zero last coordinate prevents regularity.

### 7.4 Algebraic modular forms with their actual coefficient action

### 7.5 Finite stabilizers, base change and automorphic realization

Let `t₁,…,t_h` represent the finite double-coset set supplied by AdelicAlgebraicGroups. Define the full stabilizer `Γ_i=G(F)∩t_iJt_i⁻¹`; on level coefficients its action is through `γ↦t_i⁻¹γt_i∈J`. Consequently `S(J,M)≃M^h`; if `M` is finite free, so is the form space, and arbitrary coefficient base change commutes with it. More generally, averaging proves base change for finite stabilizers whose orders are units in `A`. No such conclusion is asserted merely because the characteristic-zero invariants have the expected dimension. (BCGP25, Definition 5.7.2 and the discussion after it, pp.131–133.)

For an actual algebraic coefficient put `V_ℂ=V⊗_{E,ι}ℂ`. When the specified centre acts on it by a single character, construct the Hecke-equivariant comparison with `Hom_{G∞}(V_ℂ^∨,𝒜(G)^J)` using the matching archimedean central character. For arbitrary coefficients apply this comparison to each central-character summand. Use the supplied compact-mod-centre reduction, the matching central character and its specified norm twist to justify the discrete realization. If `m(π)` is the actual discrete occurrence multiplicity, the contribution of `π` is
`Hom_{G∞}(V_ℂ^∨,π∞)⊗(π_f)^J⊗ℂ^{m(π)}`.
Through finite levels its finite-part multiplicity is `m(π)·dim Hom_{G∞}(V_ℂ^∨,π∞)`, with contributions added when their finite parts are isomorphic. Only when `V_ℂ` is nonzero and irreducible does this reduce to infinity representation `π∞≃V_ℂ^∨` and multiplicity `m(π)`. The general comparison retains all irreducible infinity types and their coefficient multiplicities. Expose `stabilizer`, `stabilizer_finite`, `equivSum`, `IsSufficientlySmall`, `stabilizer_trivial`, `baseChange`, `automorphicEquiv` and `occurrenceMultiplicity`. (BCGP25, §5.7.2, pp.130–133, for the finite-level model; ALS.F.6 and compact Peter–Weyl for the discrete realization; the occurrence formula follows by applying the finite-dimensional Hom functor to that realization.)

**Checks.**

- A definite quaternion algebra over ℚ has no proper rational parabolics. Its constants are cuspidal; they are not assigned an Eisenstein summand by analogy with split GL₂.
- For the definite quaternion algebra ramified at `2,∞` and a maximal order, the ideal class number is one and trivial integral coefficients give `ℤ`. The count is the ideal class number, not the type number of maximal orders.
- For a nonsmall stabilizer `C₂` acting by sign on `ℤ`, integral invariants are zero, whereas invariants after reduction to `𝔽₂` are `𝔽₂`. Base change fails at two.
- For the trivial action of the same `C₂`, invariants commute with that reduction. Divisibility of the stabilizer order by the residue characteristic does not force failure for every module.
- Rank-zero coefficients remain zero under evaluation, restriction, trace and base change. A one-point class set with trivial stabilizer gives precisely `M`.
- For the trivial reductive group and `V_ℂ=ℂ²` with trivial action, the form space is `ℂ²`. The unique irreducible automorphic representation is one-dimensional with `m(π)=1`, while `dim Hom(V_ℂ^∨,π∞)=2`. It contributes two trivial finite-part constituents; there is no irreducible occurrence with two-dimensional infinity representation.
- GL₁ over ℚ or an imaginary quadratic field satisfies the two hypotheses. For a field with `r₁+r₂≥2`, its positive archimedean norm coordinates modulo the single global `A∞` contain a noncompact factor `ℝ^{r₁+r₂−1}`; compactness modulo the full centre alone does not suffice here.
- The norm-one torus of an imaginary quadratic field is compact at infinity and satisfies both hypotheses.

### 7.6 Central-character algebraic modular forms

For the broader hypothesis `G(F∞)/Z_G(F∞)` compact, take the central-`ψ` submodule of QuaternionicModularForms §2.1's common abstract carrier at level `J`, with a fixed continuous character `ψ:Z_f→A×`, trivial on `Z_G(F)`. Require the level action on `Z_f∩J` to be multiplication by `ψ⁻¹`. Denote this submodule by `CentralCharacterAlgebraicModularForm`, or
`S_ψ(J,M)={f:Gf→M : f(γgzu)=ψ(z)u⁻¹f(g)}`.
The relevant class set is `G(F)\Gf/(Z_fJ)`, finite as a quotient of the supplied ordinary finite class set. Extend the right coefficient action to `JZ_f` by `m|(uz)=ψ(z)σ(u⁻¹)m`; the overlap condition makes this independent of the factorization. The central submodule is thereby the same common abstract form carrier at level `JZ_f`, whose evaluation theorem has no compactness premise. This allows positive-unit-rank central tori excluded by §7.4. Expose `central`, `level`, `rational`, `effectiveStabilizer`, `equivSum` and `baseChange`; `res`, `trace` and `hecke` restrict the common maps to this submodule. Weighted Hecke sums require the same semigroup extension as before and its compatibility with `ψ`; they use `inverseActionHeckeEquiv`. (QuaternionicModularForms §§2.1 and 3.1; BCGP25, §5.7.2, pp.130–133, for the coefficient/level construction; the central arithmetic descent below is an additional theorem.)

Prove `centralArithmeticDescent`: at a class representative `t`, pairs `(γ,z,u)` with `γtzu=t` act on a value by `ψ(z)u⁻¹`. The action descends after dividing by rational-central pairs, because rational-centre triviality and overlap compatibility make their action the identity. Their image in the adjoint rational group lies in a compact set at infinity and in the compact finite-adelic image of `tJt⁻¹`. Adelic discreteness for the adjoint group therefore makes that image finite. This gives the finite effective stabilizer `Γ_t^eff` with its well-defined coefficient action and
`S_ψ(J,M)≃⊕_t M^{Γ_t^eff}`.
This proof imports rational-point discreteness for the adjoint group and constructs the central descent; it never declares the generally infinite full rational-central stabilizer finite. Base change follows when the effective stabilizer orders are invertible, or under a smallness hypothesis that makes these effective stabilizers trivial. State that latter effective condition explicitly rather than inferring it from torsion-freeness of an unrelated central lift.

**Checks.**

- For `Gₘ` over a real quadratic field, `Z_f=Gf`. With `ψ=1` and trivial coefficients the central class set has one element and `S_ψ=A`, despite the positive unit rank.
- For trivial centre the carrier and stabilizers agree with the level model, with no extra central quotient.
- Over a field, if a one-dimensional coefficient has a central level scalar unequal to `ψ(z)⁻¹`, every solution of the transformation equations is zero. These mismatched data are excluded from the compatible carrier.
- Over `ℤ/8`, the value `4` survives both scalar actions `1` and `−1`. Inequality of scalars alone does not force zero over a ring; injectivity of their difference is the needed cancellation hypothesis.
- A rational-central pair acts by `ψ(z)ψ(u)=ψ(γ)⁻¹=1`. Omitting the overlap inverse makes this cancellation fail.
- The sign-stabilizer reduction example of §7.5 also applies to effective stabilizers. Rational-central quotienting does not by itself remove every residual base-change obstruction.

### 7.7 Products, restriction of scalars and central transport

Transport automorphic functions and representations across the supplied topological adelic isomorphism for `Res_{E/F}G`, for every finite separable extension of number fields. Match rational points, Lie algebra, full maximal compact, height class and quotient measures. Prove compatibility with finite Hecke actions, constant terms, cuspidality, the specified central quotient, algebraic coefficients and all-degree ordinary/coherent cohomology where its Hermitian datum is given. In particular the ordinary invariants `q₀,ℓ₀` are preserved when the same global split-centre quotient is transported. Expose `resScalarsEquiv`, `resScalars_hecke`, `resScalars_constantTerm`, `resScalars_cusp` and `resScalars_cohomology`. (Borel–Jacquet, §4.1, p.194, for the identification through restriction of scalars, together with AdelicAlgebraicGroups §1.4.)

For an actual direct product, construct the algebraic external-tensor map `𝒜(G₁)⊗𝒜(G₂)→𝒜(G₁×G₂)`. Prove its injectivity as a map of function spaces and its compatibility with the product Hecke algebra. No topological density or completed-tensor isomorphism is inferred from this algebraic assertion. To prove that the resulting irreducible factors are themselves automorphic, construct `coefficientSlice`, `finiteTypeSliceDecomposition` and `automorphicProduct_factors` as follows. Fix a form `φ` on the product, product finite level `J₁×J₂`, a finite set of product compact types and a cofinite ideal `I⊂Z(𝔤₁)⊗Z(𝔤₂)` annihilating it. The kernels `I_i` of the maps `Z(𝔤_i)→(Z(𝔤₁)⊗Z(𝔤₂))/I` are cofinite, since their images lie in a finite-dimensional vector space. Every first-factor slice `g₁↦φ(g₁,g₂)` therefore belongs to a common finite-dimensional space of automorphic forms with fixed level, compact types and central ideal `I₁`, by the supplied Harish-Chandra finiteness theorem. Choose a basis `f₁,…,f_r` of this slice space and finitely many evaluation functionals separating its elements. Inverting their evaluation matrix expresses the coefficient functions `h_i(g₂)` as finite linear combinations of evaluations `φ(x_j,g₂)`. Rational invariance, the second finite level and compact types, annihilation by `I₂`, and moderate growth all pass to these coefficient functions; evaluation at finitely many fixed first coordinates preserves the required height bounds. Thus every `h_i` is an actual second-factor automorphic form and `φ=Σ_i f_i h_i`. This proves surjectivity of the algebraic tensor map, with no completion. Apply §3.4 to finite corners of subquotients of these algebraic tensor modules, tracking their factor subquotients, to obtain the irreducible classification with automorphy of both factors. Cuspidality is equivalent to cuspidality of both nonzero factors, using the parabolics `P₁×G₂` and `G₁×P₂`. Ordinary and coherent Künneth statements retain their own finiteness hypotheses. The generalized central-character direct sum of §3.2 is compatible with both transports. (Flath, Theorems 1–2, pp.179–181; Getz, §7.3, Theorem 7.11, p.37; Borel–Jacquet, §§1.7, 4.3, pp.191, 195.)

**Checks.**

- `Res_{F/ℚ}GL₂` for a real quadratic field gives Hilbert automorphic forms on `GL₂/F`, with two real factors and the same adelic finite levels.
- A separated nonzero product `f(g₁)h(g₂)` has slice rank one. A sum `f₁h₁+f₂h₂` with both pairs linearly independent has slice rank two; one evaluation at the identity need not detect it. The zero form has slice rank zero and needs no nonzero constituent.
- A product with the trivial group gives the original form space and degree range. A product of two cuspidal characters of tori remains cuspidal in the vacuous parabolic sense.
- A product with a noncuspidal split-GL₂ constituent is not cuspidal: its constant term along the product of the GL₂ Borel and the other full group is nonzero.
- `GL₂` is the central quotient `(Gₘ×SL₂)/μ₂`, not a direct product. Descent through that finite kernel requires compatible characters and is not an instance of the direct-product theorem.
- An inner form does not have the same automorphic function space by a change of notation. Transfer between it and the split group requires the separate transfer theorem of the downstream roadmap.
- The norm logarithm on GL₁ is generalized-central-character finite under transport. It is not replaced by a direct sum of honest central eigenvectors.

### Examples

The GL₂ dictionary fixes a single rotation, determinant, raising and Hecke convention all the way from a classical form to its finite factors and cohomological twist. The sign action on `ℤ` isolates the integral obstruction at nonsmall level. The real-quadratic torus demonstrates why the central-character definite-group carrier is separate from the discrete-rational-centre carrier. Direct products and restriction of scalars preserve those distinctions.
