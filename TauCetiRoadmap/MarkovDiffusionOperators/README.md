# Roadmap: Markov diffusion operators and the Bakry–Émery Γ-calculus

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the abstract theory of symmetric Markov diffusion operators of Bakry, Gentil and Ledoux [BGL, Chapter 3], together with its first consequences, the Poincaré, logarithmic Sobolev and Gaussian isoperimetric inequalities under a curvature-dimension condition [BGL, §§4.8, 4.9, 5.7, 8.5]. A *diffusion triple* `(E, μ, Γ)` is a σ-finite measure space with an algebra of test functions and a symmetric bilinear *carré du champ* `Γ` obeying the chain rule of first-order calculus. Its generator `L` is defined by integration by parts, `∫ g Lf dμ = −∫ Γ(f, g) dμ`. The Dirichlet form `ℰ(f) = ∫ Γ(f, f) dμ` closes to a self-adjoint operator and a symmetric sub-Markov semigroup `P_t = e^{tL}`. The iterated carré du champ `Γ₂(f) = ½ (L Γ(f) − 2 Γ(f, Lf))` defines the curvature-dimension condition `CD(ρ, n)`: `Γ₂(f) ≥ ρ Γ(f) + (Lf)²/n`.

The headline theorems, for a full triple with `CD(ρ, n)`, are:
* the strong gradient bound `√Γ(P_t f) ≤ e^{−ρt} P_t √Γ(f)` for every `ρ ∈ ℝ` [BGL, Theorem 3.3.18];
* mass conservation `P_t 1 = 1` on complete triples, and finiteness of `μ` when `ρ > 0` [BGL, Theorem 3.3.23];
* the extension of `Γ₂` and of the Hessian to the generator domain [BGL, Propositions 3.3.16–3.3.17];
* for a probability measure and `ρ > 0`, the Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with constant `1/ρ` when `n = ∞`, and with constant `(n−1)/(ρn)` when `1 < n < ∞` [BGL, Proposition 4.8.1, Theorem 4.8.4, Proposition 5.7.1, Theorem 5.7.4];
* the local Bobkov inequalities, equivalent to `CD(ρ, ∞)`, and for a probability measure and `ρ > 0` the Bobkov inequality `√ρ I(∫ f dμ) ≤ ∫ √(ρ I²(f) + Γ(f)) dμ`, with `I` the Gaussian isoperimetric profile; for Gibbs measures with `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0` it gives the Gaussian isoperimetric comparison `μ⁺(A) ≥ √ρ I(μ(A))` [BGL, Theorem 8.5.3, Corollaries 8.5.4–8.5.5];
* for every smooth `V` with positive-definite Hessian and `e^{−V}` integrable, the Brascamp–Lieb inequality `Var_{μ_V}(f) ≤ ∫ ⟨(∇²V)⁻¹ ∇f, ∇f⟩ dμ_V`, which improves the Lichnerowicz inequality, by Hörmander's L² method and by the Helffer–Sjöstrand representation [BGL, Theorem 4.9.1].

The theory is instantiated on the circle, Brownian motion on `ℝᵈ`, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator, the round spheres `S^{d−1}`, and the Gibbs measures `e^{−V} dx` on `ℝᵈ` for every smooth `V` with `e^{−V}` integrable. These instances carry `CD(0, 1)`, `CD(0, d)`, `CD(1, ∞)`, `CD(d−2, d−1)`, and `CD(ρ, ∞)` exactly when `∇²V ≥ ρ`, respectively.

The roadmap consumes Tau Ceti's strongly continuous semigroups and form-generated semigroups (the completed OneParameterSemigroups roadmap), and closed forms and the Friedrichs extension from OperatorTheory/UnboundedOperators. Elliptic regularity comes from the PDE roadmap; it is needed for the Gibbs instances. The roadmap feeds the Euclidean Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities of RandomMatrices §2.2, which it recovers for the Gaussian and for uniformly convex Gibbs measures. It also feeds OptimalTransport §11.9, whose Markov semigroup generated by `Δ − ∇V·∇` is the semigroup of the Gibbs triple. It recovers the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality of GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters by the semigroup proof of Bakry and Ledoux.

Layer 1 sets up diffusion triples and the Γ-calculus on test functions. Layer 2 builds the Dirichlet form, the self-adjoint generator and the Markov semigroup. Layer 3 adds ergodicity, essential self-adjointness (ESA) and conservativeness. Layer 4 introduces the extended algebra, full and compact triples, `Γ₂`, and `CD(ρ, n)`. Layer 5 extends the Γ-calculus to the generator domain. Layer 6 proves the gradient bounds and mass conservation. Layers 7 and 8 prove the Poincaré and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. Layer 9 builds the models. Layer 10 proves the Bobkov and Gaussian isoperimetric inequalities under `CD(ρ, ∞)`. Layer 11 proves the Brascamp–Lieb inequality for strictly convex potentials, and Prékopa's theorem from it.

## Conventions

- **Functions and measures.** `E` is a measurable space and `μ : Measure E` is σ-finite. When `μ` is finite it is a probability measure. Functions are real valued, `E → ℝ`. Equalities and inequalities between functions hold `μ`-almost everywhere (`=ᵐ[μ]`, `≤ᵐ[μ]`), except membership in an algebra, which is literal.
- **Operators on functions.** `Γ : (E → ℝ) → (E → ℝ) → (E → ℝ)` and `L : (E → ℝ) → (E → ℝ)` are total functions. Every hypothesis and conclusion about them is stated on the test algebra `A₀` or the extended algebra `A`; their values elsewhere carry no meaning and are never used. Write `Γ(f) = Γ(f, f)`.
- **Algebras.** The test algebra `A₀ : NonUnitalSubalgebra ℝ (E → ℝ)` consists of bounded measurable functions in every `Lᵖ(μ)`, `1 ≤ p < ∞`. It does not contain the constants when `μ` is infinite. The extended algebra `A : Subalgebra ℝ (E → ℝ)` contains `A₀` and the constants.
- **Signs.** `L` is nonpositive: `ℰ(f) = ∫ Γ(f) dμ = −∫ f Lf dμ ≥ 0`. The semigroup is `P_t = e^{tL}`, `t ≥ 0`.
- **Curvature-dimension.** `n : ℝ≥0∞` with `1 ≤ n`. The term `(Lf)²/n` is `(n⁻¹).toReal * (Lf)²`, so `n = ∞` removes it. Following BGL, `CD(ρ, n)` is defined on the test algebra `A₀`; on a full triple, prove that it extends to the specified extended algebra `A`. Never quantify over a separately supplied, unconstrained function class.
- **Inequalities.** The Poincaré inequality `P(C)` is `Var_μ(f) ≤ C ℰ(f)`. The logarithmic Sobolev inequality `LS(C)` is `Ent_μ(f²) ≤ 2C ℰ(f)`. These are the normalizations of Tau Ceti's `MeasureTheory.Measure.PoincareInequality` and `MeasureTheory.Measure.LogSobolevInequality`. Variance is Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.variance` and entropy is Tau Ceti's `MeasureTheory.entropy`.
- **Hilbert space.** The semigroup and generator act on `Lp ℝ 2 μ`. A function `f` in `A₀` and its class in `Lp ℝ 2 μ` are related by `MemLp.toLp`.

## Layer 1: diffusion triples and the Γ-calculus on test functions

### 1.1 Diffusion triples

A **diffusion triple** consists of `μ`, `A₀`, `Γ` and `L`, subject to [BGL, Definitions 3.1.1, 3.1.3, 3.1.8; hypotheses D1–D8]:
* `μ` is σ-finite, and a probability measure when finite;
* the σ-algebra is countably generated up to `μ`-null sets;
* elements of `A₀` are measurable, bounded, and in every `Lᵖ(μ)` with `1 ≤ p < ∞`, and `A₀` is dense in each of these `Lᵖ(μ)`;
* `A₀` is stable under `(f₁, …, f_k) ↦ ψ(f₁, …, f_k)` for every smooth `ψ : ℝᵏ → ℝ` with `ψ(0) = 0`;
* there is an exhaustion: an a.e. increasing sequence in `A₀` converging a.e. to `1`;
* `Γ` maps `A₀ × A₀` into `A₀` and is symmetric, bilinear and nonnegative;
* `Γ` satisfies the diffusion property `Γ(ψ(f₁, …, f_k), g) = Σᵢ ∂ᵢψ(f₁, …, f_k) Γ(fᵢ, g)`;
* `L` maps `A₀` into `A₀`, with `∫ g Lf dμ = −∫ Γ(f, g) dμ` and `∫ Lf dμ = 0` for `f, g ∈ A₀`.

`Suggested.lean` gives the structure. The linearity of `L` and the chain rule for `L` are theorems, not fields.

### 1.2 The Γ-calculus on `A₀`

Prove, for `f, g, h ∈ A₀` and smooth `ψ` with `ψ(0) = 0` [BGL, (3.1.1), (3.1.6), (3.1.8)]:
* `L` is linear on `A₀`;
* the fundamental identity `L(fg) = f Lg + g Lf + 2 Γ(f, g)`;
* the chain rule `L ψ(f₁, …, f_k) = Σᵢ ∂ᵢψ Lfᵢ + Σᵢⱼ ∂ᵢ∂ⱼψ Γ(fᵢ, fⱼ)`;
* the Leibniz rule `Γ(fg, h) = f Γ(g, h) + g Γ(f, h)`;
* the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality `Γ(f, g)² ≤ Γ(f) Γ(g)`;
* the `L²` continuity estimate `|∫ Γ(f, g) dμ| ≤ ‖Lf‖₂ ‖g‖₂` [BGL, D4];
* `∫ Γ(f, g) dμ = −∫ f Lg dμ`, a symmetric bilinear form on `A₀`.

The fundamental identity holds a.e. and needs the diffusion property and the density of `A₀`. Integration by parts alone, without them, does not determine `Γ` pointwise.

**Checks.**
- On the circle `ℝ/2πℤ` with `Γ(f, g) = f'g'` and `L = d²/dx²`: `L(cos²) = −2 cos 2x` and `2 cos·L cos + 2Γ(cos) = −2cos² + 2sin² = −2 cos 2x`.
- Two-point space `{0, 1}` with the Markov chain generator `Lf(x) = f(1−x) − f(x)` and uniform measure: the carré du champ defined by the fundamental identity is `Γ(f) = (f(1) − f(0))²/2`. Then `Γ(f², f) ≠ 2f Γ(f)` for `f = 1_{1}`, so this is not a diffusion triple.

*Needs:* Mathlib `NonUnitalSubalgebra`, `MemLp`, `Lp`, `ContDiff`, `fderiv`.

## Layer 2: the Dirichlet form, the generator and the Markov semigroup

### 2.1 The closed form

The form `ℰ(f, g) = ∫ Γ(f, g) dμ` on the image of `A₀` in `Lp ℝ 2 μ` is nonnegative, symmetric and closable, because `−L` is a nonnegative symmetric operator on that image. Construct its closure, the **Dirichlet form** `ℰ` with domain `𝒟(ℰ)`, as a closed form in the sense of UnboundedOperators UO-D06. Prove that `A₀` is a form core, and that `𝒟(ℰ)` with the norm `(‖f‖₂² + ℰ(f))^{1/2}` is a Hilbert space [BGL, Definition 3.1.9, §3.1.4].

### 2.2 The generator and the semigroup

The **generator** is the nonpositive self-adjoint operator `L : Lp ℝ 2 μ →ₗ.[ℝ] Lp ℝ 2 μ` that UO-D06 associates with `ℰ` (the Friedrichs extension of `L` on `A₀`). It extends `L` on `A₀`. Its domain is characterized by `u ∈ 𝒟(L)` with `Lu = w` if and only if `u ∈ 𝒟(ℰ)` and `ℰ(u, v) = −⟪w, v⟫` for every `v ∈ A₀` [BGL, §3.1.5].

The semigroup `P_t` is `IsCoercive.formSemigroup` of the coercive form `ℰ + ⟪·,·⟫` on `𝒟(ℰ)`, shifted by `StronglyContinuousSemigroup.expShift` with parameter `−1` (the form semigroup is `e^{−t} P_t`). Prove that:
* its generator is `L`;
* it is a symmetric contraction semigroup on `Lp ℝ 2 μ`;
* `P_t u ∈ 𝒟(L)` for `t > 0`, with `‖L P_t u‖₂ ≤ ‖u‖₂ / t`;
* `ℰ(P_t u)` is nonincreasing in `t`.

### 2.3 Positivity and the sub-Markov property

Prove the Beurling–Deny criteria for `ℰ`: normal contractions operate on `𝒟(ℰ)` and do not increase `ℰ`. Deduce that `P_t` is positivity preserving and sub-Markov: `0 ≤ u ≤ 1` implies `0 ≤ P_t u ≤ 1` [BGL, §§1.7, 3.1; FOT, Chapter 1; Davies, Chapter 1]. Deduce that `P_t` extends to a contraction of every `Lᵖ(μ)`, `1 ≤ p ≤ ∞`: on `L¹` and `L^∞` from sub-Markovianity and symmetry, and in between by the Riesz–Thorin theorem of PDE §2.2.

**Checks.**
- Circle, normalized Haar measure: `P_t cos(kx) = e^{−k²t} cos(kx)` and `ℰ(cos(kx)) = k²/2`.
- On `ℝ` with Lebesgue measure and `A₀ = C_c^∞(ℝ)`, `P_t` is convolution with the heat kernel `(4πt)^{−1/2} e^{−x²/4t}`.

*Needs:* §1.2; UnboundedOperators UO-D06; Tau Ceti `IsCoercive.formSemigroup`, `StronglyContinuousSemigroup.expShift`, `ContractionSemigroup`; PDE §2.2 (B9).

## Layer 3: ergodicity, essential self-adjointness and conservativeness

- **Essential self-adjointness (ESA).** `L` restricted to `A₀` is essentially self-adjoint, i.e. `A₀` is a core for the generator [BGL, Definition 3.1.10].
- **Ergodicity.** `Lu = 0` implies that `u` is constant, and zero when `μ` is infinite [BGL, Definition 3.1.11]. Prove that ergodicity is equivalent to: `ℰ(u) = 0` implies `u` constant.
- **Conservativeness.** For some, equivalently every, exhaustion `(u_k)`, `P_t u_k → 1` a.e. for every `t` [BGL, Definition 3.1.12]. When `μ` is finite this says `P_t 1 = 1`. Prove the independence of the exhaustion, and that conservativeness gives `∫ P_t f dμ = ∫ f dμ` for `f ∈ L¹(μ)`.
- **Finite measure.** If `μ` is finite and ESA holds, then `1 ∈ 𝒟(L)`, `L1 = 0`, `P_t 1 = 1`, and the triple is conservative.
- **Standard triples.** A standard triple is a diffusion triple that is ergodic and conservative [BGL, hypotheses S10–S11].

**Checks.**
- The open interval `(0, 1)` with Lebesgue measure, `A₀ = C_c^∞((0, 1))`, `Γ(f) = f'²`, is a diffusion triple that is not ESA: the Dirichlet and Neumann realizations of `d²/dx²` are two distinct self-adjoint extensions.
- The circle is ESA, ergodic and conservative.

*Needs:* Layer 2.

## Layer 4: extended algebras, full and compact triples, `Γ₂` and `CD(ρ, n)`

### 4.1 Extended algebra, full and compact triples

The **extended algebra** `A` [BGL, Definition 3.3.1; hypotheses F1–F9] is an algebra of measurable functions containing `A₀` and the constants. Require all of the following:
* **Ideal property (F1):** `f h ∈ A₀` whenever `f ∈ A` and `h ∈ A₀`.
* **Order detection (F2):** if `f ∈ A` and `∫ h f dμ ≥ 0` for every nonnegative `h ∈ A₀`, then `f ≥ 0` a.e.
* **Smooth composition (F3):** `A` is stable under smooth functions of several variables.
* **Extended operators (F4–F6):** `L` maps `A` to `A`; `Γ` maps `A × A` to `A` and is given there by `Γ(f, g) = ½(L(fg) − f Lg − g Lf)`; `Γ(f, f) ≥ 0`; and the diffusion and generator chain rules hold on `A`.
* **Integration by parts (F7):** for `f ∈ A` and `g ∈ A₀`, `∫ Γ(f, g) dμ = −∫ g Lf dμ = −∫ f Lg dμ`.
* **Semigroup stability (F8):** for `f ∈ A₀` and `t ≥ 0`, `P_t f` has a representative in `A`.
* **Connexity (F9):** `f ∈ A` and `Γ(f) = 0` imply that `f` is constant [BGL, Definition 3.3.7].

A **full triple** is a standard triple equipped with such an extended algebra and satisfying essential self-adjointness (ESA) for `L` on `A₀` [BGL, Definition 3.3.25 and hypotheses F10]. A **compact triple** is a full triple with `μ` a probability measure, `1 ∈ A₀` and `A = A₀` [BGL, Definition 3.3.26]; ESA is already part of the full-triple hypothesis. A diffusion triple is **complete** if there is a nonnegative exhaustion `(u_k)` in `A₀` with `Γ(u_k) ≤ 1/k` [BGL, Definition 3.3.9]. A diffusion triple is **weakly hypo-elliptic** if every `u ∈ L²(μ)` with `L*u = λu` for some `λ ∈ ℝ` is in `A` [BGL, Definition 3.3.8].

Prove that `L` and `Γ(u, u)` on `A` depend only on a.e. classes, and the chain rules `Γ(ψ∘g, h) = ψ'(g) Γ(g, h)` and `Γ(f²) = 4f² Γ(f)` on `A`.

### 4.2 `Γ₂` and the curvature-dimension condition

Define `Γ₂(f, g) = ½ (L Γ(f, g) − Γ(f, Lg) − Γ(g, Lf))` on `A` [BGL, Definition 3.3.12]. Define `CD(ρ, n)` on `A₀` as in the Conventions and prove its extension to `A` for full triples [BGL, Definition 3.3.14 and Remark 3.3.15]. Prove the following.
* `Γ₂` is symmetric and bilinear.
* The Hessian `H(f)(g, h) = ½ (Γ(g, Γ(f, h)) + Γ(h, Γ(f, g)) − Γ(f, Γ(g, h)))` satisfies the product rule (3.3.4).
* The chain rule for `Γ₂(ψ(f))`.
* `CD(ρ, n)` is monotone: it implies `CD(ρ', n')` for `ρ' ≤ ρ` and `n' ≥ n`.
* Reinforced curvature: under `CD(ρ, n)`, `Γ(Γ(f)) ≤ 4 (Γ₂(f) − ρ Γ(f)) Γ(f)` on `A` [BGL, (3.3.6)].
* The integrated identity `∫ Γ₂(f) dμ = ∫ (Lf)² dμ` for `f ∈ A₀` [BGL, (3.3.8)]. Hence `CD(ρ, n)` gives `ρ ℰ(f) + (1/n) ‖Lf‖₂² ≤ ‖Lf‖₂²` on `A₀`, and by ESA on `𝒟(L)`.

**Checks.**
- On `ℝᵈ` with Lebesgue measure: `Γ₂(f) = ‖∇²f‖²_HS ≥ (Δf)²/d`. So `CD(ρ, n)` holds if and only if `ρ ≤ 0` and `n ≥ d`.
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck on `ℝ`, `Lf = f'' − x f'`: `Γ₂(f) = f''² + f'²`. This gives `CD(1, ∞)`. For `n < ∞`, `CD(ρ, n)` holds for no `ρ`: at `f(x) = x`, it would need `1 ≥ ρ + x²/n` for every `x`.
- Circle: `Γ₂(f) = f''² = (Lf)²`, so `CD(0, 1)` holds.

*Needs:* Layer 3.

## Layer 5: the Γ-calculus on the generator domain

- **Weak hypo-ellipticity.** For a complete triple satisfying connexity and weak hypo-ellipticity: functions in `𝒟(ℰ) ∩ A` with `Γ = 0` are constant [BGL, Proposition 3.2.1], the triple is ergodic [BGL, Proposition 3.3.10], and it is ESA [BGL, Proposition 3.3.11].
- **`Γ` on the form domain.** Extend `Γ` to `𝒟(ℰ)` as an `L¹(μ)`-valued symmetric bilinear map, with the Leibniz rule, Cauchy–Schwarz, and agreement with `Γ` on `A ∩ 𝒟(ℰ)` [BGL, Propositions 3.3.5–3.3.6].
- **`Γ₂` and the Hessian on the generator domain** [BGL, Proposition 3.3.16]. On a full triple with `CD(ρ, n)`:
  * `Γ`, `Γ₂` and the Hessian `H` extend from `A₀` to `𝒟(L)`, as `L¹(μ)` limits along every graph-norm convergent sequence, independently of the sequence;
  * `∫ Γ₂(u) dμ = ‖Lu‖₂²` and `∫ Γ(u) dμ = −⟪Lu, u⟫`;
  * the product rules hold;
  * `CD(ρ, n)` and the Hessian inequality (3.3.7) extend to `𝒟(L)`.
- **Identification** [BGL, Proposition 3.3.17].
  * For `f ∈ A ∩ 𝒟(L)`, the extensions agree with the algebraic `Γ₂` and `H`, and `∫ Γ₂(f) dμ = ∫ (Lf)² dμ`.
  * Symmetric integration by parts `∫ Lk·g dμ = ∫ k·Lg dμ` holds for `k ∈ A ∩ 𝒟(L)` and `g ∈ A ∩ 𝒟(ℰ)` with `Lg ∈ L²`.
  * Converse: for `f ∈ A ∩ L²(μ)` with `Γ(f), Γ₂(f) ∈ L¹(μ)`, `f ∈ 𝒟(L)`. Prove this for complete triples with `CD(ρ, n)`, and for every full triple with `CD(ρ, n)` and `n < ∞`.

**Checks.**
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck: `f(x) = x` is in `A ∩ 𝒟(L)` with `Γ₂(f) = 1` and `∫ (Lf)² dγ = ∫ x² dγ = 1`.

*Needs:* Layer 4.

## Layer 6: gradient bounds and mass conservation

On a full triple with `CD(ρ, ∞)`, for every `ρ ∈ ℝ`, `f ∈ A₀` and `t ≥ 0`, prove:
* the strong gradient bound `√Γ(P_t f) ≤ e^{−ρt} P_t(√Γ(f))` [BGL, Theorem 3.3.18], through the interpolation `s ↦ ∫ P_s h · Φ_ε(Γ(P_{t−s} f)) dμ` of [BGL, Lemmas 3.3.20–3.3.21], with `Φ_ε(r) = √(r + ε²) − ε`;
* the weak gradient bound `Γ(P_t f) ≤ e^{−2ρt} P_t(Γ(f))` [BGL, Corollary 3.3.19].

If moreover the triple is complete, it is conservative [BGL, Theorem 3.3.23]. If in addition `ρ > 0`, then `μ` is finite.

**Checks.**
- On `ℝ`, with `f` smooth and compactly supported, `|∂ₓ P_t f| = |P_t f'| ≤ P_t |f'|`: equality in the strong bound with `ρ = 0` when `f' ≥ 0`.
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck: `∂ₓ P_t f = e^{−t} P_t f'`, so the strong bound holds with equality at `ρ = 1` for monotone `f`.

*Needs:* Layer 5.

## Layer 7: Poincaré inequalities and spectral gap under curvature

The **Poincaré inequality** `P(C)` of a diffusion triple with probability measure is `Var_μ(f) ≤ C ℰ(f)` for `f ∈ A₀` [BGL, Definition 4.2.1]. Prove the following.
* `P(C)` on `A₀` is equivalent to `P(C)` on `𝒟(ℰ)`.
* For `C > 0`, `P(C)` is equivalent to the spectral gap `ℰ(u) ≥ C⁻¹ ‖u − ∫u‖₂²`, and to the exponential decay `‖P_t u − ∫ u‖₂ ≤ e^{−t/C} ‖u − ∫ u‖₂` [BGL, §4.2].
* Under `CD(ρ, ∞)` with `ρ > 0`, a full triple with probability measure satisfies `P(1/ρ)` [BGL, Proposition 4.8.1].
* Under `CD(ρ, n)` with `ρ > 0` and finite `n > 1`, it satisfies `P((n−1)/(ρn))` [BGL, Theorem 4.8.4]. The case `n = ∞` is Proposition 4.8.1 above.
* For the Gibbs triples of Layer 9, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck included, the triple's `P(C)` is equivalent to Tau Ceti's `μ_V.PoincareInequality C`.

**Checks.**
- The circle is `CD(0, 1)` and has spectral gap `1`, attained by `cos x`. Curvature `0` gives no bound, and the inequality `P(1)` holds by Fourier series.
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck with `ρ = 1`: `P(1)` holds with equality for `f(x) = x`, agreeing with Tau Ceti's `poincareInequality_stdGaussian`.

*Needs:* Layers 4–5; Tau Ceti `MeasureTheory.Measure.PoincareInequality`; Mathlib `ProbabilityTheory.variance`.

## Layer 8: entropy and logarithmic Sobolev inequalities under curvature

The **logarithmic Sobolev inequality** `LS(C)` of a diffusion triple with probability measure is `Ent_μ(f²) ≤ 2C ℰ(f)` for `f ∈ A₀` [BGL, Definition 5.1.1], with `Ent_μ` Tau Ceti's `MeasureTheory.entropy`. Define the Fisher information `I_μ(f) = ∫ Γ(f)/f dμ` for positive `f`. Prove the following.
* `LS(C)` extends from `A₀` to `𝒟(ℰ)`.
* `LS(C)` implies `P(C)` [BGL, Proposition 5.1.3].
* Under `CD(ρ, ∞)` with `ρ > 0`, a full triple with probability measure satisfies `LS(1/ρ)` [BGL, Proposition 5.7.1]. The route is the de Bruijn identity `d/dt Ent_μ(P_t f) = −I_μ(P_t f)`, the Fisher-information decay `I_μ(P_t f) ≤ e^{−2ρt} I_μ(f)`, and convergence `Ent_μ(P_t f) → 0`.
* Under `CD(ρ, n)` with `ρ > 0` and `1 < n < ∞`, it satisfies `LS((n−1)/(ρn))` [BGL, Theorem 5.7.4], through the integrated inequalities (5.7.7)–(5.7.12) for `e^g`.
* For the Gibbs triples of Layer 9, the triple's `LS(C)` is equivalent to Tau Ceti's `μ_V.LogSobolevInequality C`.

**Checks.**
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, `ρ = 1`: `LS(1)` holds with equality for `f = e^{ax/2}`, agreeing with Tau Ceti's Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality.
- The round sphere `S²` (`d = 3`, `CD(1, 2)`) gets `LS(1/2)`.

*Needs:* Layers 6–7; Tau Ceti `MeasureTheory.entropy`, `MeasureTheory.Measure.LogSobolevInequality`.

## Layer 9: models

For each model, specify a concrete extended algebra `A`, prove the extended-algebra properties and ESA required in Layer 4, and compute its curvature-dimension bound. Merely asserting that a model is a full triple is not a target. Then instantiate Layers 6–8 on it.

- **Circle.** `AddCircle (2π)` with normalized Haar measure, `A₀ = A = C^∞(AddCircle (2π))`, `Γ(f) = f'²`, `L = d²/dx²`. A compact triple with `CD(0, 1)`, and `P_t` diagonal on the Fourier basis.
- **Brownian motion on `ℝᵈ`, `d ≥ 1`.** Lebesgue measure, `A₀ = C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)` and `A = C^∞(ℝᵈ)`, `Γ(f) = ‖∇f‖²`, `L = Δ`. A complete, conservative full triple with infinite measure and `CD(ρ, n) ⇔ ρ ≤ 0 ∧ n ≥ d`. `P_t` is convolution with the Gaussian kernel.
- **Ornstein–Uhlenbeck on `ℝᵈ`.** Standard Gaussian measure, `A₀ = C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)` and `A = C^∞(ℝᵈ)`, `L = Δ − x·∇`, `CD(1, ∞)`, with Hermite eigenfunctions (`Polynomial.hermite`) and the Mehler formula `P_t f(x) = ∫ f(e^{−t}x + √(1 − e^{−2t}) y) dγ(y)`.
- **Round spheres `S^{d−1} ⊂ ℝᵈ`, `d ≥ 2`.** The normalized surface measure (Mathlib `Measure.toSphere` of Lebesgue measure, normalized), `A₀ = A = C^∞(S^{d−1})` (equivalently, restrictions of smooth functions on `ℝᵈ`), and the Laplace–Beltrami operator. `Γ(f) = ‖∇f‖² − (x·∇f)²` on the sphere, for any smooth extension `f` to `ℝᵈ`. A compact triple with `CD(d − 2, d − 1)`. The spherical harmonics of degree `k` are eigenfunctions with eigenvalue `−k(k + d − 2)`. For `d ≥ 3` this gives the Lichnerowicz gap `d − 1` and `LS(1/(d − 1))`.
- **Gibbs measures on `ℝᵈ`.** For `V : ℝᵈ → ℝ` smooth with `∫ e^{−V} dx < ∞`, the probability measure `μ_V = e^{−V} dx / Z`, `A₀ = C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)`, the extended algebra `A = C^∞(ℝᵈ)`, and `L = Δ − ∇V·∇`.
  * It is complete, connected and weakly hypo-elliptic (by the interior regularity of PDE §5.1), hence ESA, ergodic and a full triple.
  * `Γ₂(f) = ‖∇²f‖²_HS + ⟪∇²V ∇f, ∇f⟫`, so `CD(ρ, ∞)` holds if and only if `∇²V ≥ ρ`.
  * For `ρ > 0`, `μ_V` satisfies `P(1/ρ)` and `LS(1/ρ)`, and hence Tau Ceti's `PoincareInequality (1/ρ)` and `LogSobolevInequality (1/ρ)`.

**Checks.**
- `V(x) = ‖x‖²/2` recovers Ornstein–Uhlenbeck.
- `V(x) = x⁴/4 + x²/2` on `ℝ`: `V'' = 3x² + 1 ≥ 1` gives `CD(1, ∞)`, and `P(1)` holds although `μ_V` is not Gaussian.
- `V(x) = (x² − 1)²` on `ℝ`: `V'' ≥ −4`, so `CD(−4, ∞)`. The gradient bound holds with growth `e^{4t}`, and the triple is still full.
- The two-point chain of §1.2 and the interval of Layer 3 are the non-examples.

*Needs:* Layers 6–8; Mathlib `AddCircle.haarAddCircle`, `ProbabilityTheory.stdGaussian`, `Polynomial.hermite`, `MeasureTheory.Measure.toSphere`; PDE §5.1 (E20).

## Layer 10: Bobkov and Gaussian isoperimetric inequalities under curvature

Define in this roadmap the Gaussian isoperimetric profile `I = φ ∘ Φ⁻¹ : [0, 1] → [0, 1/√(2π)]`, where `Φ` and `φ` are the standard normal distribution function and density. It is continuous and concave, symmetric about `1/2`, with `I(0) = I(1) = 0` and `I I'' = −1` on `(0, 1)` [BGL, (8.5.7)]. This layer defines the profile and develops its basic API; the Lean signatures take `I` as an explicit parameter. For `ρ ∈ ℝ`, `α ≥ 0` and `t ≥ 0`, set `c_α(t) = (1 − e^{−2ρt})/ρ + α e^{−2ρt}` when `ρ ≠ 0` and `c_α(t) = 2t + α` when `ρ = 0`. Prove the following.
* **Local Bobkov inequalities** [BGL, Theorem 8.5.3]. On a full triple, the following are equivalent: `CD(ρ, ∞)`; for some `α ≥ 0`, for every `f ∈ A₀` with values in `[0, 1]` and every `t ≥ 0`, `√(I²(P_t f) + α Γ(P_t f)) ≤ P_t(√(I²(f) + c_α(t) Γ(f)))`; the same for every `α ≥ 0`. The direct implication goes through the interpolation of [BGL, Lemma 8.5.6], as in Layer 6, and the converse through a Taylor expansion at `t = 0`.
* **Bobkov inequality** [BGL, Corollary 8.5.4]. A full triple with probability measure and `CD(ρ, ∞)`, `ρ > 0`, satisfies `√ρ I(∫ f dμ) ≤ ∫ √(ρ I²(f) + Γ(f)) dμ` for every `f ∈ 𝒟(ℰ)` with values in `[0, 1]`, with `Γ` on `𝒟(ℰ)` from Layer 5. It follows from the local inequality with `α = 1/ρ`, for which `c_α(t) = 1/ρ`, and `P_t f → ∫ f dμ` by ergodicity.
* **Bobkov implies log-Sobolev.** The Bobkov inequality applied to `ε g²`, `ε → 0`, with `I(v) ∼ v √(2 log(1/v))` as `v → 0`, gives `LS(1/ρ)` [BGL, §8.5.2]. This recovers Proposition 5.7.1 of Layer 8.
* **Gaussian isoperimetry for Gibbs measures** [BGL, (8.5.10)–(8.5.11), Corollary 8.5.5]. Let `V` be smooth on `ℝᵈ` with `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0`, and `μ_V` the Gibbs measure of Layer 9. Bounded Lipschitz functions are in `𝒟(ℰ)` with `Γ(f) = ‖∇f‖²` a.e. For every closed `A ⊆ ℝᵈ`, with `A_r = Metric.thickening r A` and the Minkowski content `μ_V⁺(A) = liminf_{r ↓ 0} (μ_V(A_r) − μ_V(A))/r`:
  * `√ρ I(μ_V(A)) ≤ μ_V⁺(A)`, from the Bobkov inequality applied to `(1 − d(·, A)/r)₊`, `r ↓ 0`;
  * For every `a ∈ ℝ` with `Φ(a) ≤ μ_V(A)` and every `r > 0`, `Φ(a + √ρ r) ≤ μ_V(A_r)`. If `0 < μ_V(A) < 1` and `μ_V(A_r) < 1`, this is equivalent to `Φ⁻¹(μ_V(A_r)) ≥ Φ⁻¹(μ_V(A)) + √ρ r`. The CDF formulation avoids assigning real values to `Φ⁻¹` at 0 and 1; handle the null and full-measure endpoint cases explicitly when deriving the inverse-CDF form.

**Checks.**
- `I(v) = φ(Φ⁻¹(v))` gives `I' = −Φ⁻¹` and `I'' = −1/I` on `(0, 1)`.
- For `α = 1/ρ`, `c_α(t) = (1 − e^{−2ρt})/ρ + e^{−2ρt}/ρ = 1/ρ` for every `t`.
- Standard Gaussian, `V(x) = ‖x‖²/2`, `ρ = 1`: the half-space `H = {x · u ≤ r}` has `μ(H) = Φ(r)` and `μ⁺(H) = φ(r) = I(Φ(r))`, so the isoperimetric inequality is an equality. The two isoperimetric statements agree with the Minkowski-content and enlargement forms of GaussianIsoperimetryAndClusters, Layer 8.
- `V(x) = x⁴/4 + x²/2` on `ℝ`, `ρ = 1`: for `A = (−∞, a]`, `μ_V⁺(A)` is the density `e^{−V(a)}/Z`, and the inequality says `Φ⁻¹(μ_V((−∞, a]))` grows at least as fast as `a`.

*Needs:* Layers 5, 6, 9; the Gaussian profile defined and analyzed in this layer; Mathlib `Metric.thickening`.

## Layer 11: improved Lichnerowicz inequalities: Brascamp–Lieb, Helffer–Sjöstrand and Hörmander's L² method

Throughout, `V : ℝᵈ → ℝ` is smooth, strictly convex, and has positive-definite Hessian at every point; assume `e^{−V}` is integrable. Let `μ_V` be the Gibbs probability measure of Layer 9, with generator `L = Δ − ∇V·∇`. No uniform lower bound `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0` is assumed, except where stated.
* **Brascamp–Lieb inequality** [BGL, Theorem 4.9.1; BrascampLieb]. For every `f ∈ C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)`, `Var_{μ_V}(f) ≤ ∫ ⟨(∇²V)⁻¹ ∇f, ∇f⟩ dμ_V`. Consequently `Var_{μ_V}(f) ≤ ∫ ‖∇f‖²/λ_min(x) dμ_V`, with `λ_min(x)` the least eigenvalue of `∇²V(x)` [BGL, (4.9.4)]. When `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0` this recovers, and improves pointwise, the Lichnerowicz inequality `P(1/ρ)` of Layer 7.
* **Hörmander's L² method** [Hörmander]. This is the proof of the Brascamp–Lieb inequality. First assume `∇²V ≥ ε > 0`. By Layer 7, `μ_V` satisfies `P(1/ε)`, so for `f ∈ C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)` there is `φ ∈ 𝒟(L)` with `−Lφ = f − ∫ f dμ_V`. By interior regularity (PDE §5.1), `φ` is smooth, and the integrated Bochner identity of Layers 5 and 9, `‖Lφ‖₂² = ∫ (‖∇²φ‖²_HS + ⟨∇²V ∇φ, ∇φ⟩) dμ_V`, gives `∫ ⟨∇²V ∇φ, ∇φ⟩ dμ_V ≤ Var_{μ_V}(f)`. Then `Var_{μ_V}(f) = ∫ ⟨∇f, ∇φ⟩ dμ_V`, and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the form `⟨(∇²V)⁻¹·, ·⟩` concludes. For any potential satisfying the standing assumptions but without a uniform positive lower bound, apply this to `V_ε = V + ε‖x‖²/2` and let `ε ↓ 0`, using `(∇²V + ε)⁻¹ ≤ (∇²V)⁻¹` and `μ_{V_ε} → μ_V`.
* **Helffer–Sjöstrand representation** [HelfferSjostrand; BGL, Lemma 4.9.2, (4.9.2)]. On `H = L²(μ_V; ℝᵈ)`, let `L⃗` act componentwise by `L`, and let `M` be multiplication by `∇²V`.
  * Prove the commutation `∇Lf = (L⃗ − M) ∇f` for smooth `f`.
  * The operator `M − L⃗` on `C_c^∞(ℝᵈ; ℝᵈ)` is symmetric and nonnegative. Let `𝐀` be its Friedrichs extension (UO-D06).
  * Prove the operator monotonicity of Lemma 4.9.2: if `ε ≤ A₁ ≤ A₂` as forms on a common core, with `ε > 0`, then `⟨A₂⁻¹u, u⟩ ≤ ⟨A₁⁻¹u, u⟩`.
  * When `∇²V ≥ ρ > 0`, `𝐀 ≥ ρ`, and `Cov_{μ_V}(f, g) = ⟨𝐀⁻¹ ∇f, ∇g⟩_H` for `f, g ∈ C_c^∞(ℝᵈ)`. With Lemma 4.9.2 applied to `A₁ = M` and `A₂ = 𝐀`, this gives a second proof of the Brascamp–Lieb inequality.
* **Prékopa's theorem from the Brascamp–Lieb inequality** [CorderoErausquin2005]. Let `φ : ℝᵐ × ℝᵈ → ℝ` be smooth and convex, with `∇²_x φ(t, x)` positive definite for every `(t, x)` and `e^{−φ(t, ·)}` integrable for every `t`. Then `t ↦ −log ∫ e^{−φ(t, x)} dx` is convex. For the second-derivative proof, additionally assume differentiation under the integral is justified to second order; for example, for each compact `K ⊆ ℝᵐ`, require integrability of `x ↦ sup_{t ∈ K} e^{−φ(t,x)}(1 + Σᵢ |∂_{tᵢ}φ(t,x)|² + Σᵢⱼ |∂²_{tᵢtⱼ}φ(t,x)|)`. Under these hypotheses its second derivative in a direction `s ∈ ℝᵐ` is `E[∂²_s φ] − Var(∂_s φ)` under the probability measure proportional to `e^{−φ(t, ·)}`. The Brascamp–Lieb inequality bounds `Var(∂_s φ)` by `E[⟨(∇²_x φ)⁻¹ ∇_x ∂_s φ, ∇_x ∂_s φ⟩]`, and the joint convexity of `φ`, through the Schur complement of `∇²_{(t,x)} φ`, makes the difference nonnegative. This is the real case of Berndtsson's complex Prékopa theorem.

**Checks.**
- Gaussian `N(0, Σ)` with `Σ` positive definite, `V(x) = ⟨Σ⁻¹x, x⟩/2`, `∇²V = Σ⁻¹`: the Brascamp–Lieb inequality reads `Var(f) ≤ ∫ ⟨Σ ∇f, ∇f⟩ dμ`, with equality for every linear `f = ⟨a, x⟩`. Lichnerowicz gives only `Var(f) ≤ λ_max(Σ) ∫ ‖∇f‖² dμ`, with equality only when `a` is a top eigenvector of `Σ`.
- Ornstein–Uhlenbeck, `Σ = I`: the constant vector field `u = e₁` satisfies `M u = u` and `L⃗ u = 0`, so `𝐀⁻¹ ∇x₁ = e₁` and `Cov(x₁, x₁) = 1`.
- `V(x) = x⁴/4 + x²/2` on `ℝ`: `Var(f) ≤ ∫ f'²/(3x² + 1) dμ_V`, strictly below the bound of `P(1)` when `f'` does not vanish a.e. on `ℝ ∖ {0}`.
- `V(x) = √(1 + x²)` on `ℝ`: `V'' = (1 + x²)^{−3/2} > 0` has infimum `0`, so `CD(ρ, ∞)` fails for every `ρ > 0`. The Brascamp–Lieb inequality still gives `Var(f) ≤ ∫ (1 + x²)^{3/2} f'² dμ_V`.
- Prékopa, `m = d = 1`, `φ(t, x) = x²/2 + b t x + a t²/2` with `a ≥ b²`, so that `φ` is jointly convex: `−log ∫ e^{−φ(t, x)} dx = (a − b²) t²/2 − log √(2π)`, convex, and affine exactly in the Schur-complement equality case `a = b²`.

*Needs:* Layers 5, 7, 9; UnboundedOperators UO-D06; PDE §5.1 (E20), for the smoothness of `φ`.
