### Named results

- **The Hasse bound** — an elliptic curve over `𝔽_q` satisfies [`(#E(𝔽_q) − (q + 1))² ≤ 4q`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/HasseBound.html#WeierstrassCurve.hasse_bound). It is proved through the positive-definite degree form on the Frobenius pencil, and with rationality of the zeta function it gives the Riemann hypothesis for `E`.
- **The Mordell–Weil theorem** — the points of an elliptic curve over a number field form a [finitely generated group](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/MordellWeil/FinitelyGenerated.html#WeierstrassCurve.Affine.fg_point_of_numberField).
- **Finiteness of the Selmer group** — over a number field, [`Sel_m(E/K)` is finite](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/GaloisCohomology/Selmer/Finite.html#WeierstrassCurve.finite_selmerGroup) (AEC X.4.2). The proof treats rational `E[m]` first, then passes to the division field, using Hermite's finiteness theorem for extensions of bounded degree.
- **Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich with finite coefficients** — under potential good reduction, a curve has good reduction exactly when [inertia fixes `E[m]`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/TateModule/TorsionCriterion.html#WeierstrassCurve.HasPotentialGoodReduction.hasGoodReduction_iff_forall_mem_inertiaSubgroup), for `m` prime to the residue characteristic. This is statement (iii) of the roadmap's three.
- **Tate's algorithm computes `c_p`** — the algorithm's [local index](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/TateAlgorithm/LocalIndex.html#WeierstrassCurve.tateLocalIndex) equals `[E(K) : E₀(K)]` at every reduction symbol. As a consequence the index is [finite](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Affine/Point/Index/Bound.html#WeierstrassCurve.Affine.finiteIndex_nonsingularReduction), at most 4 away from split multiplicative reduction, and `v(Δ_min)` at split multiplicative reduction (AEC VII.6.1).

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Selmer groups and Sha.** `Sel_m(E/K)` and `Ш(E/K)` are defined through general Selmer structures on continuous cohomology, and `0 → E(K)/mE(K) → Sel_m → Ш[m] → 0` is exact. Restriction to extensions preserves both.
- **The division field.** [`K(E[m])`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/GaloisCohomology/DivisionField.html#WeierstrassCurve.divisionField) is a finite Galois extension whose Galois group is the kernel of the action on `E[m]`. Both the Selmer finiteness argument and the inertia criteria for good reduction go through it.
- **The dual isogeny in general.** Every isogeny has a dual, built by factoring it as a separable map after a power of Frobenius. It makes the degree a quadratic form and drives the index and regulator formulas along isogenies.

## The frontier

- **The Tate curve's uniformisation.** The coordinate series [satisfy the Tate equation off `q^ℤ`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/TateCurve/Equation.html#TauCeti.equation_tateUniformizationX_tateUniformizationY_of_notMem_zpowers), descend to `Kˣ/q^ℤ` and commute with base change. Still unproved: the map is a homomorphism, it is bijective onto `E_q(L)`, and it is Galois-equivariant.
- **Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich (ii).** Potential good reduction should be equivalent to finite inertia image on `T_ℓ E`. This now blocks (i) too: an unramified `T_ℓ` gives unramified division fields, which the finite-level criterion turns into good reduction once potential good reduction is known.
- **The formal logarithm and exponential.** The formal group law, its torsion, and its identification with the kernel of reduction are in place. Neither the logarithm nor the exponential is defined.
- **The BSD quotient and Cassels' theorem (stretch).** Several pieces are in place. The regulator transforms [in Cassels' form](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/MordellWeil/Regulator/Isogeny.html#TauCeti.Isogeny.Hom.regulator_mul_index_dual_eq_index_mul_regulator), isogenous curves have the [same local polynomials](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EllipticCurve/Isogeny/Frobenius/Isogenous.html#TauCeti.Isogeny.localPolynomial_eq), and the `φ`-descent sequence of a separable isogeny is exact. Still missing: the real period, `φ`-Selmer groups, and local Tate duality.
- **The semistable algorithmic–Artin conductor comparison.** This is a Layer 8 milestone for the Frey application. It is not established here whether any of it exists.
