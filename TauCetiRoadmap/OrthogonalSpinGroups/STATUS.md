## Where this roadmap stands

Layers 1 and 2 are each down to a few items: Layer 1 lacks the general-field readings of Spin in dimensions four and five, and Layer 2 lacks the topology of SO(Q) inside O(Q). ### Named results

- **The image of Spin is the spinor kernel**: for a nondegenerate form over a field of characteristic not two, the image of `Spin(Q) → SO(Q)` is exactly the kernel of the spinor norm ([`range_spinToSpecialOrthogonal_eq_ker_spinorNorm`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/SpinorNorm/Basic.html#CliffordAlgebra.range_spinToSpecialOrthogonal_eq_ker_spinorNorm)).
- **Spin is the even unitary group up to dimension five, and not beyond**: for `1 ≤ dim V ≤ 5`, Spin(Q) = U(C₀, σ) ([`evenUnitaryGroup_le_lipschitzGroup`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/LowRank/Five.html#CliffordAlgebra.evenUnitaryGroup_le_lipschitzGroup)). Over an infinite field the inclusion is strict from dimension six on ([`range_spinGroup_toUnits_ne_evenUnitaryGroup_of_six_le_finrank`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/LowRank/Six.html#CliffordAlgebra.range_spinGroup_toUnits_ne_evenUnitaryGroup_of_six_le_finrank)).
- **Compactness criterion**: over a locally compact field such as ℝ or `ℚ_p`, O(Q) is compact exactly when Q is anisotropic ([`isCompact_orthogonalGroup_iff`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/Topology/Algebra/QuadraticForm/OrthogonalGroup/Compact.html#TauCeti.QuadraticMap.isCompact_orthogonalGroup_iff)). The same holds for Spin, because `Spin → SO` is a proper map ([`isProperMap_spinToSpecialOrthogonal`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/Topology/Algebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/Proper.html#CliffordAlgebra.isProperMap_spinToSpecialOrthogonal)). Over ℝ, anisotropic means definite.
- **Local spinor kernel indices**: over `ℚ_p` in dimension at least three the spinor norm is onto, and the image of Spin has index four for odd p ([`index_range_spinToSpecialOrthogonal_eq_four_of_three_le_finrank`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LocalField/QuadraticForm/SpinorNorm/HigherRank.html#TauCeti.QuadraticMap.index_range_spinToSpecialOrthogonal_eq_four_of_three_le_finrank)) and index eight at p = 2. An anisotropic binary form has spinor-norm image of index two ([`index_range_spinorNorm_of_finrank_eq_two_of_anisotropic`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LocalField/QuadraticForm/SpinorNorm/Basic.html#CliffordAlgebra.index_range_spinorNorm_of_finrank_eq_two_of_anisotropic)).
- **Ternary Spin is a quaternion norm-one group**: over any field, a nondegenerate three-dimensional Spin group is the norm-one group of a quaternion algebra ([`exists_spinGroupEquivQuaternionUnitary_of_finrank_eq_three`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/LowRank/Three.html#CliffordAlgebra.exists_spinGroupEquivQuaternionUnitary_of_finrank_eq_three)), and SO(Q) is that algebra's unit group modulo its centre.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- [`cliffordNorm`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Lipschitz/ReverseNorm.html#CliffordAlgebra.cliffordNorm), the reverse norm on the Lipschitz group. The spinor norm descends from it, and its continuity is what makes the spinor kernel open ([`isOpen_ker_orthogonalSpinorNorm`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/Topology/Algebra/CliffordAlgebra/Spin/SpinorNorm.html#CliffordAlgebra.isOpen_ker_orthogonalSpinorNorm)).

Every rational point lies in them at almost every prime, which is what the diagonal maps of 3E need.

### Roadmap coverage

**Layer 0** is done: 0A to 0F, plus the worked examples, which are SO of the hyperbolic plane as `Kˣ` through the diagonal torus and O(Q) = {±1} on a line. **Layer 1** is partial. 1A to 1E are done, with 1B's ungraded centre now `K ⊕ Kω` in odd dimension. 1F has Spin = U(C₀, σ) through dimension five, the dimension-three quaternion reading, the discriminant algebra as the centre of C₀, and strictness in dimension six. It lacks their identification with the unit square classes at almost every prime. 3D has finite adelic points only. 3E has its integral carriers but not the diagonal maps or the discreteness results. 3F and 3G are untouched.

## The frontier

- **SO inside O (2B)**: SO(Q) closed and open in O(Q), and locally compact, with a continuous determinant. These also give the compactness of the derived `U_p^{SO}` that 3C asks for.
- **Reference images at almost every prime (3C)**: `θ_p(U_p^{SO})` should equal the unit square classes for the integral family in dimension at least three, with the dimension-one rejection test. The unit-square-class index and counts are already in place ([`unitSquareClasses_index`](https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LocalField/UnitSquareClasses.html#TauCeti.unitSquareClasses_index)).
- **Diagonals and the adelic spinor norm (3E, 3F)**: the rational diagonal maps with their commuting square, discreteness in the full adeles with the finite-adelic counterexample, and the spinor norm on adelic SO with its evaluation rule. The full adeles first need the away and archimedean flavours of 3D.
- **Low-rank readings (1F)**: over a general field, C₀ in dimension four as `B₁ × B₂` or as a quaternion algebra over the discriminant field, and U(C₀, σ) in dimension five as the symplectic group `Sp(C₀, σ)`.
- **Double cosets (3G)**: the three comparison maps on `G(ℚ) \ G(𝔸_f) / U`. Nothing for them is stated yet.
