# The Ado–Iwasawa theorem and faithful finite-dimensional Lie representations

Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field has a faithful finite-dimensional representation. In
characteristic zero this is Ado's theorem; Iwasawa removed the characteristic restriction. The result says
that an abstract finite-dimensional Lie algebra `L` can be embedded in the commutator Lie algebra of
`Module.End K V` for some finite-dimensional `K`-vector space `V`.

This roadmap builds the theorem as a reusable theory of finite-dimensional Lie representations. The
headline embedding is not the first target. The useful core is the construction of finite-dimensional
quotients of the universal enveloping algebra that still separate the canonical copy of `L`, together with
the extension machinery that makes such quotients equivariant under derivations. The summit is the
strengthened form proved by Hochschild: the faithful representation may be chosen so that every element
acting nilpotently in the adjoint representation acts nilpotently in the constructed representation.

The two characteristic regimes use genuinely different engines:

- in characteristic zero, build a faithful nilrepresentation on the center, extend it through a flag in the
  solvable radical by derivation-stable cofinite ideals of enveloping algebras, use Levi decomposition for
  the final extension, and add the adjoint representation to remove the remaining kernel;
- in characteristic `p`, construct central `p`-polynomials in the augmentation ideal of `U(L)`, make `U(L)`
  finite over the commutative algebra they generate, and apply the generalized Krull intersection theorem
  to a power of the central ideal.

These tracks meet only after each has produced the same faithful finite-target interface. A proof of the
characteristic-zero theorem must not be silently presented as the arbitrary-field result, and no use of
Lie's theorem, Levi decomposition, division by factorials, or characteristic-zero trace identities may enter
the positive-characteristic track.

## Scope, conventions, and the target

Throughout, `K` is a field, `L` is a Lie `K`-algebra, and `[FiniteDimensional K L]`. A representation is a
Mathlib Lie homomorphism

```lean
ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V
```

with `Module.End K V` carrying the commutator bracket. It is **faithful** precisely when `ρ` is injective,
equivalently when `LieModule.ker K L V = ⊥` after transporting the action to a `LieModule`. We retain the
explicit map in existence statements: an existential typeclass alone would hide the representation that
later consumers need to compose.

The **nilradical** `nil L` means, throughout this roadmap, the largest nilpotent ideal of `L`: the
`sSup` of those `I : LieIdeal K L` that are nilpotent *as Lie algebras*. Mathlib has no such
declaration, so Layer 0 below builds it. ⚠ It is **not** Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal K L`
(`Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Nilpotent.lean`), which is `sSup {N | LieModule.IsNilpotent L N}`, the largest
ideal on which `L` acts nilpotently. The two differ: for `L = ⟨x, y⟩` with `⁅x, y⁆ = y` the only
nonzero proper ideal is `span y` and `⁅L, span y⁆ = span y`, so `maxNilpotentIdeal K L = ⊥` while
`nil L = span y`. Every use of "nilradical" below is `nil L`, and the containment
`maxNilpotentIdeal K L ≤ nil L` is one of Layer 0's lemmas.

A **nilrepresentation** is a finite-dimensional representation on which every element of `nil L` acts
nilpotently. This is Fulton–Harris's term and their condition (§E.2). The stronger Hochschild
condition is pointwise:

```lean
∀ x : L, IsNilpotent (LieAlgebra.ad K L x) → IsNilpotent (ρ x)
```

It implies the nilrepresentation condition and gives a faithful nilrepresentation whenever `L` is
nilpotent. It is stronger than bare Ado–Iwasawa and is the final theorem of this roadmap.

The characteristic-`p` track uses the **augmentation map** `ε : U(L) →ₐ[K] K`, induced by the zero Lie
homomorphism, its kernel `U⁺(L)`, and the center

`C = Z(U(L)) ∩ U⁺(L)`.

Here `U(L) = UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L`. Central ideals generated by subsets of `C` are two-sided;
quotients are taken as associative `K`-algebras. Distinguish this `C` from the center `Z(L)` of the Lie
algebra.

⚠ Ideals of `U(L)` are `Ideal (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L)` — which in Mathlib means a left ideal
— carrying `Ideal.IsTwoSided` where two-sidedness is needed, rather than the separate
`TwoSidedIdeal` type. That is the side of the choice with the powers, `FG` and quotient-ring API the
positive-characteristic track uses (`Ideal.Quotient.ring` takes `[I.IsTwoSided]`, and
`Ideal.FG.pow` assumes it). Every ideal named below that must be two-sided says so and carries the
instance; nothing relies on a coercion to make a one-sided ideal look two-sided.

The summit is:

```lean
theorem adoIwasawa [FiniteDimensional K L] :
    ∃ (V : Type u) (_ : AddCommGroup V) (_ : Module K V)
      (_ : FiniteDimensional K V) (ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V),
      Function.Injective ρ
```

and the strengthened theorem adds preservation of adjoint nilpotence. No algebraic-closure hypothesis and
no restriction on `ringChar K` appears in either statement.

## Why this is its own roadmap

[The Lie-groups roadmap](../LieGroups/README.md) needs the real case to prove Lie's third theorem by
embedding a real Lie algebra in `𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)`. [The highest-weight roadmap](../LieHighestWeight/README.md)
already owns PBW as a shared algebraic foundation, but its main theory is semisimple and characteristic
zero. Ado–Iwasawa needs neither highest weights nor classification; it needs cofinite quotients,
derivations, nilpotent and solvable ideals, and a separate positive-characteristic commutative-algebra
engine. Putting those targets in either neighboring roadmap would obscure their generality and make the
arbitrary-characteristic theorem appear to follow from characteristic-zero structure theory.

The original 1948 proof constructs finite-dimensional splitting algebras. This roadmap targets the theorem,
not that proof artifact. It follows the derivation-stable enveloping-algebra proof in characteristic zero and
Hochschild's central-ideal proof in positive characteristic because those routes expose broadly reusable
Mathlib APIs. A future splitting-algebra development can coexist with this one, but it is not a hidden
prerequisite here.

## What Mathlib and Tau Ceti already provide

- **Lie algebras, ideals, modules, and representations.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Basic.lean` provides
  `LieHom` and `LieModule`, `Ideal.lean` provides `LieIdeal`, `Abelian.lean` provides `LieModule.ker`,
  and `OfAssociative.lean` provides `LieModule.IsFaithful` and `LieAlgebra.ad`. `Module.End` is
  `Mathlib/Algebra/Module/End.lean`, with its Lie structure from `OfAssociative.lean`.
- **Nilpotence and solvability.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Nilpotent.lean` provides lower central series,
  `LieRing.IsNilpotent`, `LieModule.IsNilpotent`, `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal` (which, as the
  conventions above warn, is not the nilradical this roadmap needs), and the relation to nilpotent
  endomorphisms. `Solvable.lean` provides the derived series and `LieAlgebra.radical`.
- **Engel and Lie's theorems.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Engel.lean` and `LieTheorem.lean` provide the
  triangularization/nilpotence results used in the characteristic-zero track. Note the exact
  hypotheses: `LieModule.exists_nontrivial_weightSpace_of_isSolvable` carries
  `[LieModule.IsTriangularizable k L V]` as well as `[CharZero k]`, and triangularizability is not
  automatic over `ℝ` or `ℚ`. Layer 3 says how the track supplies it. Lie's theorem is not used in
  positive characteristic.
- **Base change.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/BaseChange.lean` and `Derivation/BaseChange.lean` extend
  scalars for Lie algebras and Lie derivations, and `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/CartanCriterion.lean` is an
  existing example of a characteristic-zero structural result proved this way. Layer 3 uses them.
- **Derivations and semidirect sums.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/Derivation/Basic.lean`,
  `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/AdjointAction/Derivation.lean`, and `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/SemiDirect.lean`
  provide `LieDerivation`, inner derivations, and `LieAlgebra.SemiDirectSum` (notation `K ⋊⁅ψ⁆ L`).
  Mathlib's existing `Derivation` API is for commutative algebras; it has neither
  the required noncommutative associative-derivation structure nor a lift of a Lie derivation to `U(L)`.
- **The universal enveloping algebra.** `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping.lean` provides
  `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L`, the canonical Lie map `ι`, and the universal property `lift`. It does
  not currently prove that `ι` is injective, that `U(L)` is a domain or Noetherian for finite-dimensional
  `L`, or that it has the PBW ordered-monomial basis needed here.
- **Commutative algebra, including the Krull intersection theorem this roadmap needs.** Mathlib
  already contains module-finiteness, Noetherian modules, integral dependence, and the Artin–Rees and
  Krull results in `Mathlib/RingTheory/Filtration.lean`. In particular
  `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff` **is** the generalized Krull intersection theorem Hochschild
  cites (he cites Zariski–Samuel p. 253 for it), in the generality needed here:

  ```lean
  theorem Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff [IsNoetherianRing R] [Module.Finite R M] (x : M) :
      x ∈ (⨅ i : ℕ, I ^ i • ⊤ : Submodule R M) ↔ ∃ r : I, (r : R) • x = x
  ```

  Layer 7 applies it and does not restate or reprove it.
- **Finite-target reductions.** The Etingof representation-theory formalization already states the
  arbitrary-field theorem and proves that it is equivalent to a finite-dimensional associative target of
  `U(L)` that remains injective on `L`; see
  [`Remark2_9_3.lean`](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/Etingof-RepresentationTheory-draft1/blob/master/EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter2/Remark2_9_3.lean).
  That project deliberately leaves the theorem itself outside its proof boundary. The reduction is useful
  design evidence, not a proof source to copy.

## The shared PBW dependency

The concrete PBW theorem is not in Mathlib. A 2025–2026
[Lean Zulip thread](https://leanprover.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/287929-mathlib4/topic/The.20plan.20for.20PBW.20theorem/near/583326631)
records two active approaches: an abstract categorical proof and a Diamond-Lemma/normal-form proof.
[Mathlib PR #36936](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/36936) is an open draft of the
abstract categorical PBW theorem. Layer 1 uses any relevant API present in Tau Ceti's pinned Mathlib and
supplies the remaining concrete filtration, injectivity, ordered-monomial, and finiteness corollaries in the
shared Tau Ceti PBW development.

## What is missing

The missing library is substantially larger than the final existential theorem:

- the nilradical `nil L` (the largest nilpotent ideal) and its basic theory, which Mathlib does not have;
- PBW consequences for arbitrary fields: injectivity of `ι`, ordered monomials, filtrations, functoriality
  for general Lie maps and quotients, domains and Noetherianity of `U(L)`, and finite spanning from
  central monic relations;
- the augmentation ideal and lower-central-series-weighted PBW truncations;
- a reusable type for derivations of a noncommutative associative algebra, and extension of
  `LieDerivation K L L` to such a derivation of `U(L)`, functorially and compatibly with commutators;
- derivation-stable cofinite ideals in enveloping algebras of solvable Lie algebras;
- extension of nilrepresentations across split Lie-ideal extensions;
- the characteristic-zero structure lemmas needed by the extension argument: derivation-invariance of
  the radical and nilradical, `D(rad L) ≤ nil L`, and a dependency-closed proof of Levi decomposition;
- central `p`-polynomials in `U(L)` and module-finiteness over the commutative algebra they generate,
  together with the identity `(UC)^n = J^n · U(L)` that lets Mathlib's Krull intersection theorem
  reach the two-sided filtration;
- the nilpotent-extension lemma and the semisimple-element step behind Hochschild's
  characteristic-zero strengthening;
- the strengthened Hochschild theorem in each characteristic and the characteristic-dispatch step that
  yields a theorem stated over an arbitrary field.

`Suggested.lean` pins only the load-bearing public interfaces. The definitive work below also includes the
supporting API and examples that make those interfaces usable.

## The build, in layers

### Layer 0: representations and finite enveloping targets

Build the interface before either proof track.

- **Faithfulness dictionary.** Prove that injectivity of `ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V`, triviality of its
  kernel, and `LieModule.IsFaithful K L V` are equivalent for the induced action. Supply constructors in
  both directions without installing surprising global instances.
- **Left-regular representation.** For a finite-dimensional associative `K`-algebra `A`, prove that
  `Algebra.lmul K A` is injective and that composing an injective Lie map `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ A` with left
  multiplication gives a faithful finite-dimensional representation.
- **Finite-target reduction.** If `q : U(L) →ₐ[K] A` and `q ∘ ι` is injective, obtain Ado's
  representation on `A`. Conversely, extend a faithful finite-dimensional representation along the
  universal property to `U(L) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`. State the equivalence.
- **Direct sums separate finitely many directions.** A finite direct sum of representations has kernel the
  intersection of their kernels. Deduce that if each nonzero direction is detected locally, finite
  dimensionality of `L` lets finitely many representations assemble into a faithful one. This is useful
  outside the selected proof as well.
- **The nilradical.** Define `nil L`, the largest nilpotent ideal of `L`, as the `sSup` of the
  nilpotent `LieIdeal K L`, and prove it is itself nilpotent for `L` Noetherian, by the same
  sum-of-two-nilpotent-ideals argument that makes the `sSup` well behaved. Prove the basic theory the
  later layers use: an ideal is nilpotent iff it is `≤ nil L`; every Lie-algebra automorphism preserves
  `nil L`; `Z(L) ≤ nil L ≤ radical K L`;
  `x ∈ nil L → IsNilpotent (LieAlgebra.ad K L x)`; `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal K L ≤ nil L`, with
  the two-dimensional nonabelian algebra as the witness that the containment is strict; and
  `nil L = ⊤ ↔ LieRing.IsNilpotent L`. This is general Lie theory, wanted independently of Ado, and
  belongs in `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/Nilradical.lean` rather than beside the Ado proof.

Acceptance: the reduction specializes to the adjoint representation when `Z(L) = 0`; for an abelian
`n`-dimensional algebra, construct an explicit faithful square-zero representation as a sanity check.
For `L = ⟨x, y⟩` with `⁅x, y⁆ = y`, compute `nil L = span y` and `maxNilpotentIdeal K L = ⊥`, so that
the distinction the conventions draw is pinned by a theorem rather than by prose.

### Layer 1: the PBW consequences Ado uses

The PBW project is owned by [the highest-weight roadmap](../LieHighestWeight/README.md). Its PBW unit
has the following exact supplier contract, stated with no characteristic or algebraic-closure hypothesis.
**That roadmap is accountable for these targets**; this layer records the contract because Ado–Iwasawa
is its first consumer and needs every item over `𝔽₂`. They live in the common Tau Ceti PBW development,
never in a second PBW theorem beside the Ado proof. The separate noncentral monic-relation argument used
to prove `U(S)/B^m` finite-dimensional in Layer 3 remains owned by this roadmap.

- **Canonical embedding.** Prove `Function.Injective (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι K)` over a field.
- **PBW filtration and ordered monomials.** Expose the degree filtration, its multiplicativity, the
  associated-graded equivalence with `SymmetricAlgebra K L`, and the ordered-monomial basis attached to a
  basis of `L`. The basis-free theorem and the concrete basis corollary are both required.
- **Functoriality.** For every Lie map, prove compatibility of the induced enveloping-algebra map with
  the filtration and associated-graded map. Derive the corresponding ordered-monomial-span statements
  for subalgebras, surjections and quotients, and direct sums. The later semidirect and central-polynomial
  arguments must not re-prove straightening identities by hand.
- **Domain and Noetherian corollaries.** For finite-dimensional `L`, prove that `U(L)` has no zero divisors
  and is left and right Noetherian. Route these through the associated graded symmetric algebra and
  filtered-to-graded transfer in reusable ring-theoretic form.
- **Finite spanning from central monic relations.** If each member of a finite ordered generating basis
  satisfies a monic relation over a central commutative subalgebra `R`, prove that the bounded PBW
  monomials span `U(L)` over `R`. Package this as a module-finiteness theorem, not merely a dimension
  count inside the eventual proof. This is the exact result consumed by Layer 6.

Acceptance: for an abelian `L`, recover the polynomial/symmetric algebra basis and the familiar domain and
Noetherian instances. The statements must work over finite fields and in characteristics `2` and `3`.

### Layer 2: augmentation, weighted PBW truncations, and the nilpotent engine

- **Augmentation.** Define `ε : U(L) →ₐ[K] K` from the zero Lie map, prove `ε (ι x) = 0`, and develop its
  kernel `U⁺(L)` as a two-sided ideal. Prove its universal property among algebra maps killing `L`.
- **Powers and brackets.** Prove that a length-`n` iterated bracket maps into `(U⁺(L))^n`. State the exact
  indexing convention and test it on the Heisenberg algebra, where the central bracket belongs to the
  square of the augmentation ideal. Do not identify `L ∩ (U⁺(L))^n` with the lower central series unless
  a separately stated dimension-subalgebra theorem supplies the precise hypotheses, particularly in
  positive characteristic.
- **Weighted truncation for nilpotent algebras.** When `L` is nilpotent, choose a basis adapted to its lower
  central series and give each basis vector its lower-central weight. Use PBW straightening to prove that
  the span of ordered monomials of weight at least `N` is a **two-sided** ideal — weights add on both
  sides and straightening only raises weight, so there is no reason to settle for the one-sided
  statement — and choose `N` strictly larger than every basis weight. The quotient by that ideal is a
  finite-dimensional `K`-algebra, so it is a target for Layer 0's finite-target reduction, while the
  canonical copy of `L` remains injective.
- **Nilpotent action.** Left multiplication on the weighted quotient is a faithful representation. Prove
  that every `x : L` acts nilpotently: multiplication by an element of the augmentation ideal strictly
  raises weight until it reaches the truncated ideal. This is the characteristic-free Birkhoff form.

Acceptance: construct and compute the representation for the abelian and three-dimensional Heisenberg
Lie algebras. Check explicitly that the quotient action detects the center; the adjoint representation does
not.

## A. The characteristic-zero track

### Layer 3: derivations of enveloping algebras and stable cofinite ideals

- **Noncommutative derivations.** Introduce in Tau Ceti a structure for a `K`-linear endomorphism of a
  possibly noncommutative `K`-algebra satisfying `D (ab) = D(a)b + aD(b)`. Supply composition,
  commutator, descent to stable quotients, and the resulting Lie-algebra structure. Keep this distinct
  from Mathlib's commutative-algebra `Derivation` until that API itself is generalized to bimodules.
- **Lift Lie derivations.** For `D : LieDerivation K S S`, construct a noncommutative associative
  derivation `Dᵁ` of `U(S)`; prove `Dᵁ (ι x) = ι (D x)`, uniqueness on the algebra generated by `ι(S)`,
  linearity in `D`, and `[D₁,D₂]ᵁ = [D₁ᵁ,D₂ᵁ]`. Prove naturality under intertwining Lie maps.
- **Scalar extension and descent.** The next milestone is the one place in this track where the
  splitting hypothesis on Mathlib's Lie theorem bites: `LieModule.IsTriangularizable` holds over an
  algebraically closed `K` but not over `ℝ` or `ℚ`. Resolve it once, structurally, and not by adding a
  closure hypothesis to the theorem: prove that `nil` and `radical` commute with base change to `K̄`
  for finite-dimensional `L` over a characteristic-zero field, and that a `LieDerivation K L L`
  extends to `L ⊗[K] K̄`, so that a containment of ideals may be checked after extension and descended.
  Also prove the linear range-membership descent used in Layer 5: if the scalar extension of
  `s : S` belongs to the range of `t ↦ ⁅s,t⁆`, then already `s = ⁅s,t⁆` for some `t : S` over `K`.
  Build on `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/BaseChange.lean` and `Derivation/BaseChange.lean`;
  `Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/CartanCriterion.lean` is Mathlib's own precedent for arranging a
  characteristic-zero structural result this way. Nothing downstream base-changes the representation
  itself, only these structural statements.
- **Nilradical under derivations.** Over characteristic zero, prove that every `D : LieDerivation K L L`
  satisfies `D (radical K L) ≤ nil L`, and in particular that a derivation of a solvable
  finite-dimensional Lie algebra maps it into its nilradical. Deduce `D (nil L) ≤ nil L`; this is the
  characteristic-zero derivation-invariance statement deliberately absent from Layer 0. For every ideal
  `I` of `L`, derive the identity `nil I = nil L ⊓ I`: derivation-invariance makes `nil I` an `L`-ideal,
  and the reverse containment is immediate. These are the substantive statements for which the previous
  milestone's descent is used.
- **Cofinite kernels.** Extend a finite-dimensional representation `σ` of a solvable `S` to an algebra map
  `U(S) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`; its kernel is a cofinite two-sided ideal. Relate nilpotence of `σ(x)` to
  nilpotence of `ι(x)` modulo that ideal.
- **The nilradical-generated ideal is nilpotent modulo `I`.** Given a cofinite two-sided ideal `I` for
  which every nilradical element is nilpotent modulo `I`, let `Q` be the two-sided ideal of `U(S)/I`
  generated by the image of `nil S`, and let `B` be its preimage in `U(S)`. Use Engel's theorem for the
  left-multiplication action of `nil S`, followed by PBW straightening that moves nilradical factors
  together, to choose `m` with `Q^m = 0`; equivalently, `B^m ≤ I`.
- **Lifted derivations have range in `B`.** From `D(S) ≤ nil S` and the fact that `ι(S)` generates
  `U(S)`, prove `Dᵁ(U(S)) ≤ B` for every lifted derivation. Do not assert that `B` itself is stable:
  `I` was not assumed stable. The Leibniz rule and the range containment give the exact statement needed,
  `Dᵁ(B^m) ≤ B^m`.
- **The power remains cofinite.** For an ordered basis `x₁, …, x_d` of `S`, choose monic one-variable
  polynomials `Pᵢ` with `Pᵢ(ι(xᵢ)) ∈ B`. Then `Pᵢ(ι(xᵢ))^m ∈ B^m`; use their leading terms and the
  ordered PBW basis to give a finite spanning set for `U(S)/B^m`. This is the noncentral monic-relation
  argument owned here, not Layer 1's central-subalgebra module-finiteness theorem.
- **Nilpotence survives the refinement.** If `x ∈ nil S` and `ι(x)^e ∈ I`, then
  `ι(x)^(em) ∈ I^m ≤ B^m`. Thus every nilradical element remains nilpotent modulo `J = B^m`.
  Package these milestones as the derivation-stable cofinite refinement `J ≤ I` consumed by Layer 4.

Acceptance: prove the refinement is unchanged by replacing `I` with the kernel of an isomorphic
representation, and verify it for a two-dimensional nonabelian solvable Lie algebra.

### Layer 4: extending nilrepresentations across split ideal extensions

Let `S` be a solvable ideal of `L` and `H` a complementary Lie subalgebra. The split hypothesis is
stated externally, through Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.SemiDirectSum`: with
`ψ : H →ₗ⁅K⁆ LieDerivation K S S` the map `ad` induces by restricting to `S`, the hypothesis is

```lean
Nonempty (L ≃ₗ⁅K⁆ S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H)
```

Prove once that internal data — `S` an ideal, `H` a subalgebra, `IsCompl S.toSubmodule
H.toSubmodule` — produces such a `ψ` and such an equivalence, and state the layer's theorems against
the external form so that later clients never re-derive it. This is also what makes the last
milestone's "functoriality under equivalence" say something.

- **Multiplication-plus-derivation action.** Starting from a nilrepresentation `σ` of `S`, choose the
  derivation-stable cofinite ideal `J` from Layer 3. On `U(S)/J`, define `y + z : S ⊕ H` to act by left
  multiplication by `ι(y)` plus the descended derivation induced by `ad(z)|S`.
- **Representation law.** Prove the commutator identities among two multiplication operators, a
  multiplication and a lifted derivation, and two lifted derivations. Conclude that the construction is a
  Lie homomorphism `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K (U(S)/J)`.
- **Kernel control.** Prove `ker ρ ∩ S ≤ ker σ`. This is the invariant that permits iteration; do not replace
  it by an unproved assertion that `σ` itself extends on the same carrier.
- **Nilpotence control.** If `nil L = nil S`, or if `L` is nilpotent, prove the extended representation
  remains a nilrepresentation. (These are the two hypotheses of Fulton–Harris Proposition E.5.)
  Separate the two arguments and state the hypotheses that make the latter's
  multiplication-plus-derivation sum nilpotent.
- **Functoriality under equivalence.** Transport the construction across equivalences of split extensions
  and across equivalent starting representations. Later clients should consume the extension theorem
  rather than its chosen vector-space complement.

Acceptance: extending the faithful square-zero representation of the center through a codimension-one
ideal preserves detection of that center.

### Layer 5: assemble Ado in characteristic zero

- **A faithful nilrepresentation of the center.** For a basis of `Z(L)`, construct commuting square-zero
  endomorphisms that detect every basis direction. Record a basis-free existence theorem and one explicit
  model.
- **The flag passes through the nilradical.** Put `N = nil L` and `R = radical K L`, and construct a
  codimension-one flag

  `Z(L) = S₀ ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ S_a = N ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ S_b = R`.

  Below `N`, use the normalizer property for proper subalgebras of a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie
  algebra to refine from `Z(L)` to `N`, with every term an ideal of the next; every larger term in these
  steps is nilpotent. Above `N`, first prove `[L,R] ≤ N` by applying Layer 3 to inner derivations. It
  follows that every vector subspace `T` with `N ≤ T ≤ R` is a Lie subalgebra and an `L`-ideal, so an
  ordinary vector-space flag supplies the codimension-one steps. Layer 3's ideal formula gives
  `nil T = nil L ⊓ T = N` for every such `T`. Thus every application of Layer 4 uses one of its two
  stated hypotheses: the larger algebra is nilpotent below `N`, and the two nilradicals agree above it.
  The same ideal formula gives `nil R = N`, justifying the final extension from `R` to `L`.
- **Levi decomposition: quotient induction.** Prove Levi decomposition without Ado or any linear
  structure theorem derived from Ado, following Fulton–Harris Appendix E. Induct on `finrank K L`.
  If `R` contains a nonzero proper `L`-ideal `A`, apply the induction hypothesis to `L/A`, pull its
  complement back to an algebra whose radical is `A`, and apply induction there. This reduces to the
  case where `R` has no nonzero proper `L`-ideal. Then `[R,R]` is proper because `R` is solvable, so
  minimality makes `R` abelian, and minimality also gives `[L,R] = 0` or `[L,R] = R`.
- **Levi decomposition: split the two minimal-radical cases.** If `[L,R] = 0`, the adjoint action factors
  through the semisimple quotient `L/R`; use Weyl complete reducibility to complement the submodule `R`
  in `L`. If `[L,R] = R`, form the nested `L`-submodules of `Module.End K L`
  used in the standard proof: `A` consists of `ad r` for `r ∈ R`, `B` consists of endomorphisms with
  range in `R` and restriction zero on `R`, and `C` consists of endomorphisms with range in `R` whose
  restriction to `R` is scalar. Prove `A ≤ B ≤ C`, `C/B ≃ K`, `L · C ≤ B`, and `R · C ≤ A`.
  Weyl complete reducibility for the semisimple quotient splits `C/A → C/B`; a lift `φ` of `1` has
  `φ|R = id` and `L · φ ≤ A`, and `{x : L | x · φ = 0}` is the required complement. The exact supplier
  here is the arbitrary-characteristic-zero form of Weyl complete reducibility in the highest-weight
  roadmap, including scalar-extension and descent from its algebraically closed development.
- **Levi decomposition: exported interface.** Package the complement as a `LieSubalgebra` and export the
  semidirect-product existence theorem in the `Nonempty (L ≃ₗ⁅K⁆ R ⋊⁅ψ⁆ S)` form consumed by Layer 4.
  Conjugacy of Levi complements is not a target: no named consumer here requires it.
- **Iterate kernel control.** Extend the center representation along the flag and then across the Levi
  complement. The resulting representation `ρ₀` satisfies `ker ρ₀ ∩ Z(L) = 0`.
- **Add the adjoint representation.** Since `ker(ad) = Z(L)`, the direct sum `ρ₀ ⊕ ad` is faithful.
  Prove `adoCharZero`, and prove that the construction is a nilrepresentation.

The characteristic-zero half of Hochschild's strengthening is a separate argument on top of that, not
a corollary of it, and it decomposes as follows. Throughout, `M` is a finite-dimensional `L`-module on
which `nil L` acts nilpotently, and `x : L` has `ad x` nilpotent; the goal is that `x` acts
nilpotently on `M`.

- **A nilpotent-extension lemma.** If a Lie subalgebra `H` acts nilpotently on a finite-dimensional
  module `M`, an element `y` acts nilpotently, and `⁅y,H⁆ ≤ H`, then the Lie algebra spanned by `y`
  and `H` acts nilpotently on `M`. Include the special case in which `H` is a nilpotent ideal whose
  elements act nilpotently. Hochschild uses this repeatedly on two different modules; state it once for
  a general module and derive all uses.
- **The semisimple component acts nilpotently.** Write `L = S ⊕ radical K L` by Levi and `x = s + r`
  accordingly. Prove that `ad x` nilpotent forces `ad_S s` nilpotent, and then that `ad s` is
  nilpotent on `L` and `s` is nilpotent on `M`. Hochschild does this by producing `t : S` with
  `⁅s, t⁆ = s` and applying Lie's theorem to the solvable subalgebra spanned by `s` and `t`; the
  `⁅s, t⁆ = s` step is the easy half of the `sl₂`-triple existence statement for a semisimple Lie
  algebra and should be named as its own target, reusing whatever Mathlib's
  `LieAlgebra.IsKilling.exists_isSl2Triple_of_weight_isNonZero` and `Sl2.lean` already give. This is
  the second place the splitting hypothesis on Lie's theorem appears. Prove the statement after scalar
  extension, then use Layer 3's range-membership descent to obtain `t` over the original field; nilpotence
  of the resulting endomorphisms also descends.
- **The radical criterion.** Prove
  `r ∈ R ∧ IsNilpotent (LieAlgebra.ad K L r) → r ∈ N`. Indeed `[L,R] ≤ N` makes `N + K r` an ideal;
  the extension lemma makes its adjoint action nilpotent, so Engel makes it a nilpotent ideal, whence it
  is contained in `N`.
- **Assemble the nested extension argument.** On the adjoint module `L`, the extension lemma first shows
  that `H = span(s,N)` acts nilpotently. Since `[x,s] = [r,s] ∈ [R,L] ≤ N` and `[x,N] ≤ N`, the element
  `x` normalizes `H`; apply the lemma again, using the assumed nilpotence of `ad x`, to the algebra
  spanned by `x` and `H`. Its element `r = x - s` is therefore adjoint-nilpotent, so the radical criterion
  gives `r ∈ N`. Finally, on `M`, the actions of `s` and `N` are nilpotent, hence `span(s,N)` acts
  nilpotently and contains `x = s + r`. Deduce
  `exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charZero`.

Acceptance: for centerless `L`, the construction reduces to `ad`; for nilpotent `L`, it agrees at the level
of guarantees with Layer 2 and remains a nilrepresentation. For `L = sl₂` over `ℚ`, where `nil L = ⊥`
so the nilrepresentation condition is vacuous, the strengthened theorem still forces the images of
the nilpotent root vectors to be nilpotent; this is the case that distinguishes it from `adoCharZero`.

## B. The positive-characteristic track

### Layer 6: central `p`-polynomials and module-finiteness

Assume `[CharP K p]` with `p` prime.

- **Frobenius commutator identity.** In an associative algebra of characteristic `p`, prove
  `ad(a^(p^e)) = ad(a)^(p^e)` with the exact interpretation of each `ad`. Build the supporting binomial
  and iterated-commutator API once.
- **Central `p`-polynomials.** For each `x : L`, finite-dimensionality of `L` makes the sequence
  `(ad x)^(p^e)` linearly dependent. Turn a monic linearized relation into
  `c_x = x^(p^e) + Σᵢ aᵢ x^(p^i) ∈ C`. The polynomial has zero constant term, so `c_x` lies in the
  augmentation ideal as well as the center. Record naturality under field extension, and under
  **surjective** Lie maps, where the image of a central `p`-polynomial is again one because the image
  of `ι(L)` still generates. ⚠ Naturality fails for arbitrary Lie maps: for `L = K x` abelian, `ad x = 0`
  so `c_x = x` works, but its image under `K x → ⟨x, y⟩` with `⁅x, y⁆ = y` is not central. The general
  statement that does survive is that the image commutes with the image of the source.
- **The adjoint-nilpotent specialization.** If `ad x` is nilpotent, choose `e` with
  `x^(p^e) ∈ C`. This is the step that later forces `x` itself to act nilpotently.
- **Finite central generating family.** For a basis `x₁, …, x_d` of `L`, choose central `p`-polynomials
  `c₁, …, c_d` and let `R = K[c₁, …, c_d]`. Prove `R` is a commutative Noetherian `K`-algebra and,
  using PBW reduction of exponents, `U(L)` is a finite `R`-module.
- **Full central ideal.** Let `C = Z(U(L)) ∩ U⁺(L)` and `UC` be the ideal it generates, which is
  two-sided because `C` is central. Left Noetherianity gives a finite generating set for `UC` as a
  left ideal, and it may be taken inside `C` (a left ideal generated by a set has a finite generating
  subset of that set); enlarge it by the basis `p`-polynomials and retain module-finiteness over the
  resulting `R`. This full ideal, rather than only the chosen basis polynomials, is what yields
  Hochschild's strengthened nilpotence conclusion.
- **The central ideal is extended from `R`.** With `S ⊆ C` that finite generating set and `J` the
  ideal of `R` it generates, prove `UC = J • (⊤ : Submodule R (U(L)))` and hence
  `(UC)^n = J^n • ⊤` for every `n`. Centrality of `S` is the whole content. This is the milestone
  that lets a theorem about a commutative ideal acting on a module say something about powers of a
  two-sided ideal of `U(L)`, and Layer 7 is stated so that nothing else has to.

Acceptance: for a one-dimensional abelian algebra, obtain the familiar central relation in `K[x]`; for the
two-dimensional algebra `[x,y]=y`, compute central `p`-polynomials and verify the commutator identities in
characteristics `2` and `3`.

### Layer 7: the generalized Krull intersection and finite quotients

- **Apply Mathlib's Krull intersection theorem.** The commutative input is
  `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Filtration.lean`), already in the
  generality wanted: `R` commutative Noetherian, `M` a finite `R`-module, and
  `x ∈ ⨅ i, J ^ i • ⊤ ↔ ∃ r : J, (r : R) • x = x`. Do not restate or reprove it. The work here is
  discharging its hypotheses for `M = U(L)` over the central subalgebra `R`: `R` Noetherian and
  `Module.Finite R (U(L))` are Layer 6.
- **Torsion-free/domain corollary.** From that equivalence, an element of `⋂ₙ J^n • ⊤` satisfies
  `(1 - r) • x = 0` for some `r ∈ J`. PBW makes `U(L)` a domain, so it suffices that `1 - r ≠ 0`,
  which holds because `J ⊆ U⁺(L)` and the augmentation sends `1 - r` to `1`. Conclude
  `⋂ₙ J^n • (⊤ : Submodule R (U(L))) = 0`, and hence, by Layer 6's `(UC)^n = J^n • ⊤`,
  `⋂ₙ (UC)^n = 0`.
- **Separate `L` at a finite stage.** The descending subspaces `L ∩ (UC)^n` have zero intersection. Since
  `L` is finite-dimensional, one is zero. Make the stabilization argument a reusable lemma about a
  finite-dimensional subspace meeting a filtered intersection.
- **Finite-dimensional quotient.** Prove `U(L)/(UC)^n` is finite-dimensional over `K`: it is finite over
  `R/J^n`, and the latter is spanned by finitely many monomials in the central generators of total degree
  `< n`. Expose an explicit finite spanning set.

Acceptance: every use of a commutative ideal occurs in `R`, and every ideal of the noncommutative `U(L)`
is explicitly two-sided and carries `Ideal.IsTwoSided`. No coercion may make the Krull theorem appear
to apply directly to a noncommutative ideal: the only passage between the two sides is Layer 6's
`(UC)^n = J^n • ⊤`, cited by name.

### Layer 8: assemble the positive-characteristic theorem

- Choose `n` with `(UC)^n ∩ L = 0` and let `A = U(L)/(UC)^n`.
- The quotient map is injective on `ι(L)`, so Layer 0's left-regular construction gives a faithful
  finite-dimensional representation.
- If `ad x` is nilpotent, Layer 6 gives `x^(p^e) ∈ C`; a sufficiently large power belongs to `(UC)^n`, so
  `x` acts nilpotently on `A`. Prove the positive-characteristic Hochschild theorem,
  `exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charP`, and derive `adoCharP` from it.
- Prove independence from the selected basis and central generating family at the theorem level. The
  quotient itself need not be canonical, but its guarantees must not mention those choices.

Acceptance: instantiate the theorem over finite fields, including `𝔽₂`; no `CharZero`, `IsAlgClosed`, or
characteristic-size hypothesis may be inferable in the declaration.

### Layer 9: the arbitrary-field summit and consumers

- **Characteristic dispatch.** Use `ringChar K`. In the zero case install the local `CharZero K`
  instance and apply `exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charZero` from Layer 5; in the nonzero
  case use primality of the characteristic of a field to install `CharP K p` and apply
  `exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charP` from Layer 8. Dispatch on the strengthened
  statements, not on `adoCharZero`/`adoCharP`, so that the summit does not have to be reproved.
  Package this instance plumbing separately from the mathematics.
- **Hochschild's strengthened theorem.** Over an arbitrary field, produce a faithful finite-dimensional
  `ρ` preserving adjoint nilpotence. Derive the bare `adoIwasawa` theorem and the faithful
  nilrepresentation theorem for nilpotent `L`.
- **Matrix form.** Given a basis of `V`, transport `ρ` to an injective Lie homomorphism
  `L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) K`. Conversely, show the matrix and endomorphism formulations are
  equivalent.
- **Lie's third theorem interface.** Export the real specialization in exactly the form consumed by
  [the Lie-groups roadmap](../LieGroups/README.md), without importing manifold theory into this roadmap.
- **Residual finite-dimensionality at degree one.** Record that the canonical copy of `L` in `U(L)` is
  separated by a finite-dimensional algebra quotient. Do not claim that all of `U(L)` is residually
  finite-dimensional unless that stronger theorem is proved independently.

Acceptance: the arbitrary-field declaration has only `[Field K]`, the Lie structures, and
`[FiniteDimensional K L]`; `#print axioms` in the implementation repository reports no project axiom or
placeholder for it.

## Ordering and parallel work

Layer 0 can begin immediately. Layer 1 uses the shared PBW work named in `LieHighestWeight`, consuming
whatever API is already available in Tau Ceti and its pinned Mathlib and implementing missing pieces in Tau
Ceti. Layer 2 consumes Layers 0–1 and provides a substantial first deliverable: the
arbitrary-characteristic nilpotent case by a weighted PBW quotient, independently of the central-ideal
construction used later in characteristic `p`.

After Layer 1, the two main tracks are independent. Layers 3–5 form the characteristic-zero track:
scalar extension and derivation lifts, stable ideals, split-extension machinery, then the radical/Levi
assembly and Hochschild's strengthening. Layers 6–8 form the positive-characteristic track: central
`p`-polynomials and the extended-ideal identity, then Krull, then the finite quotient. Layer 7 is short
now that the commutative theorem is Mathlib's, so Layer 6 is the substantial half. Layer 9 needs both
completed tracks only because its statement has no characteristic hypothesis; the real consumer in
`LieGroups` can use Layer 5 as soon as that track lands.

Within Layer 5, the Levi unit consumes only the arbitrary-characteristic-zero Weyl complete-reducibility
contract of `LieHighestWeight`; its direct induction is independent of Ado and may proceed alongside the
stable-ideal work. The flag and Hochschild assembly then consume the characteristic-zero nilradical API of
Layer 3, especially `[L, radical K L] ≤ nil L` and the nilradical formula for ideals.

The largest risks are not the final compositions. They are concrete PBW infrastructure, the
derivation-stable ideal lemma, Levi decomposition at the right generality, the scalar-extension and
descent step that keeps the characteristic-zero track honest over non-closed fields, and the clean
separation of commutative central-subalgebra ideals from two-sided ideals of `U(L)`. Claims should be
cut along those boundaries.
