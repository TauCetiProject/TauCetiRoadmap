# Roadmap: the Ado–Iwasawa theorem and faithful finite-dimensional Lie representations

**Summary.** Every finite-dimensional Lie algebra over a field has a faithful finite-dimensional
representation: Ado's theorem in characteristic zero, Iwasawa's in general. The roadmap builds it
as a reusable theory: finite-dimensional quotients of the universal enveloping algebra `U(L)` that
still separate `L`, the largest nilpotent ideal of `L` (its nilradical), derivations of `U(L)`
extending those of `L`, and the extension of representations across split extensions. Throughout,
`K` is a field and `L` a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over `K`; no statement assumes `K`
algebraically closed.

The headline theorems are these.

- **Ado–Iwasawa.** If `L` is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over an arbitrary field `K`, there
  are a finite-dimensional `K`-vector space `V` and an injective Lie algebra homomorphism from `L`
  to the endomorphisms of `V` with the commutator bracket; equivalently, `L` is isomorphic to a Lie
  subalgebra of the `n × n` matrices over `K` for some `n`.
- **Hochschild's strengthening.** The representation can be chosen so that every element `x` of
  `L` whose adjoint action `ad x` is nilpotent acts by a nilpotent endomorphism. In particular the
  nilradical acts nilpotently, and a nilpotent `L` has a faithful representation by nilpotent
  endomorphisms.
- **Birkhoff's theorem, in every characteristic.** If `L` is nilpotent, `U(L)` has a two-sided
  ideal of finite codimension, spanned by the ordered monomials of large weight for a basis adapted
  to the lower central series, whose quotient still contains `L` and on which every element of `L`
  acts nilpotently by left multiplication.
- **Levi's theorem**, proved without Ado's theorem: in characteristic zero, `L` is the semidirect
  sum of its solvable radical and a semisimple subalgebra.

The two characteristics use different engines, which meet only in a final dispatch on the
characteristic. In characteristic zero a faithful representation of the centre is extended along
a chain of ideals through the radical, by derivation-stable ideals of finite codimension in
enveloping algebras, then across a Levi complement, and the adjoint representation removes the
remaining kernel. In characteristic `p` every element of `L` has a central `p`-polynomial in
`U(L)`, `U(L)` is finite over its Noetherian centre, and the generalized Krull intersection theorem
separates `L` in a quotient by a power of the ideal generated by the central elements of
augmentation zero; Lie's theorem, Levi's theorem and division by factorials do not enter.

The Tau Ceti library carries the whole development, whose declarations the layers below name; the
targets left to this roadmap are supporting statements. The PBW theorem and its corollaries, in
the area of [LieHighestWeight](../LieHighestWeight/README.md), supply injectivity of `L → U(L)` over
any field and the Noetherian and domain properties of `U(L)`; Mathlib supplies the enveloping
algebra, Lie derivations, semidirect sums and the generalized Krull intersection theorem; and
[LieGroups](../LieGroups/README.md) uses the real matrix form to prove Lie's third theorem.

Layer 0 sets up faithfulness, the finite-target reduction and the nilradical, Layer 1 the PBW
consequences, Layer 2 the nilpotent case. Layers 3–5 are the characteristic-zero track, Layers 6–8
the positive-characteristic track, and Layer 9 joins them over an arbitrary field.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 needs nothing of this roadmap. Layer 1 consumes the PBW development over an arbitrary
field, including `𝔽₂`. Layer 2 consumes Layers 0–1 and is a first deliverable on its own: the
arbitrary-characteristic nilpotent case by a weighted PBW quotient, independent of the
central-ideal construction used later in characteristic `p`.

After Layer 1 the two tracks are independent. Layers 3–5 form the characteristic-zero track:
derivation lifts, scalar extension and descent, and derivation-stable ideals (Layer 3); extension
across split extensions (Layer 4); Levi decomposition, the flag through the radical and the
assembly with Hochschild's strengthening (Layer 5). Within Layer 5 the Levi unit needs only Weyl's
complete reducibility and is independent of the stable-ideal work; the flag and Hochschild's
argument need the characteristic-zero nilradical theory of §3.4. Layers 6–8 form the
positive-characteristic track: central `p`-polynomials and the extended-ideal identity (Layer 6),
the Krull intersection and the finite quotient (Layer 7), and the assembly (Layer 8). Layer 9
needs both tracks only because its statement has no characteristic hypothesis; the real case
used by LieGroups follows from Layer 5 alone.

Each `*Needs:*` line lists what its statement and its route use, item by item, so that a target
proved by another route is not blocked behind an unrelated section. A route names a proof and its
inputs; it does not make that proof mandatory.

## Conventions

1. **Scalars and algebras.** `K : Type u` is a field and `L : Type v` a Lie algebra over `K`
   (`[LieRing L] [LieAlgebra K L]`), finite-dimensional (`[FiniteDimensional K L]`) unless a
   statement says otherwise; existence statements produce carriers in `Type (max u v)`. Statements
   about the nilradical are made over a commutative ring `R` with `[IsNoetherian R L]` where they
   need it, which a finite-dimensional `L` over a field satisfies.
2. **Representations.** A representation is a Lie algebra homomorphism
   `ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V`, with `Module.End K V` carrying the commutator bracket
   (`LieRing.ofAssociativeRing`). It is **faithful** when `ρ` is injective, equivalently
   `ρ.ker = ⊥` (Mathlib `LieHom.ker_eq_bot`), equivalently when the action of `L` on `V` through
   `ρ` is `LieModule.IsFaithful` (§0.1). Existence statements keep the explicit map
   `∃ (V : Type (max u v)) (_ : AddCommGroup V) (_ : Module K V) (_ : FiniteDimensional K V) (ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V), Function.Injective ρ`:
   an existential type class would hide the representation that consumers compose with.
3. **The nilradical.** `nil L` is the largest nilpotent ideal of `L`: the supremum of the ideals
   that are nilpotent *as Lie algebras*, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical K L`. It is
   **not** Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal K L`, the supremum of the ideals on which `L`
   acts nilpotently. The two differ: on the two-dimensional algebra with basis `x, y` and
   `⁅x, y⁆ = y`, the only nonzero proper ideal is `K y`, and `⁅L, K y⁆ = K y`, so
   `maxNilpotentIdeal` is `⊥` while `nil L = K y` (§0.5). Every use of "nilradical" below is `nil L`.
   We call this algebra the affine-line algebra; it is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine K`,
   with `x` the dilation and `y` the translation.
4. **Nilrepresentations and Hochschild's condition.** A *nilrepresentation* is a
   finite-dimensional representation on which every element of `nil L` acts nilpotently (Fulton–Harris,
   Appendix E, §E.2, whose term this is). *Hochschild's condition* on `ρ` is pointwise:
   `∀ x : L, IsNilpotent (LieAlgebra.ad K L x) → IsNilpotent (ρ x)`. It implies the
   nilrepresentation condition, since elements of `nil L` are `ad`-nilpotent (§0.5), and for a
   nilpotent `L` it says that every `ρ x` is nilpotent.
5. **The enveloping algebra and its augmentation.** `U(L)` is Mathlib's
   `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L` with `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι K : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ U(L)` and the
   universal property `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`. The *augmentation* `ε : U(L) →ₐ[K] K` is
   the counit `Bialgebra.counitAlgHom K U(L)`, which is the lift of the zero Lie homomorphism; its
   kernel, the *augmentation ideal* `U⁺(L)`, is `(TauCeti.HopfIdeal.augmentation K U(L)).toIdeal`.
   The *central augmentation* `C = Z(U(L)) ∩ U⁺(L)` is `TauCeti.HopfIdeal.centralAugmentation`, and
   `UC`, the ideal it generates, is `TauCeti.HopfIdeal.centralAugmentationIdeal`. `Z(U(L))` is the
   centre `Subalgebra.center K U(L)` of the associative algebra, not the centre `Z(L)` of the Lie
   algebra.
6. **Ideals of `U(L)`.** An ideal of `U(L)` is an `Ideal (UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K L)` (in
   Mathlib a left ideal) carrying `Ideal.IsTwoSided` where two-sidedness is needed, not the
   separate type `TwoSidedIdeal`: that side carries the powers, finiteness and quotient-ring API
   used below (`Ideal.Quotient.ring` assumes `[I.IsTwoSided]`). Every ideal below that must be
   two-sided says so; nothing relies on a coercion to make a one-sided ideal look two-sided.
   Commutative ideals occur only in commutative subrings of the centre (Layers 6–7), and pass to
   `U(L)` only through the extended-ideal identity of §6.5.
7. **Lower central series.** `LieModule.lowerCentralSeries K L L n` is indexed from `0`, with term
   `0` equal to `L` and term `1` equal to `⁅L, L⁆`; a bracket of `n + 1` elements lies in term `n`.
8. **Characteristic discipline.** A theorem proved in characteristic zero is not presented as the
   arbitrary-field result, and no use of Lie's theorem, Levi decomposition, division by factorials
   or characteristic-zero trace identities enters the positive-characteristic track (Layers 6–8).
   In characteristic `p`, the statements carry `[Fact p.Prime] [CharP K p]`, or `[ExpChar R p]`
   with `p ≠ 1`, as the library does.
9. **Names.** A statement given with a Mathlib or Tau Ceti declaration name in code font is that
   declaration; the supplier contracts at the end list them. A statement introduced by "Prove" is a
   target of this roadmap, and the name proposed for it is written in prose.

## Layer 0: representations and finite enveloping targets

The interface both proof tracks feed into.

### 0.1 The faithfulness dictionary

For `ρ : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K V`, the action of `L` on `V` through `ρ` is Mathlib's
`LieRingModule.compLieHom` with `LieModule.compLieHom`, and its `LieModule.toEnd` is `ρ`. Prove
the faithfulness dictionary: for that action, `LieModule.IsFaithful K L V` holds iff `ρ` is
injective iff `ρ.ker = ⊥`, and `LieModule.ker K L V = ρ.ker`; conversely, for a Lie module `V`,
`LieModule.IsFaithful K L V` holds iff the representation `LieModule.toEnd K L V` is injective
(Mathlib `LieModule.isFaithful_iff`) iff `LieModule.ker K L V = ⊥`
(`LieModule.isFaithful_iff_ker_eq_bot`). Supply the passage in both directions as definitions and
theorems, without a global instance that turns every representation into a module structure.
*Needs:* Mathlib `LieRingModule.compLieHom`, `LieModule.compLieHom`, `LieModule.toEnd`,
`LieModule.IsFaithful`, `LieModule.isFaithful_iff`, `LieModule.isFaithful_iff_ker_eq_bot`,
`LieModule.ker`, `LieHom.ker_eq_bot`.

**Checks.**
- For `L = sl₂` the adjoint action is faithful, the centre being `0`; for an abelian `L ≠ 0` it is
  not, the adjoint representation being zero (Mathlib `LieAlgebra.isFaithful_self_iff`: the
  adjoint module is faithful iff `center = ⊥`). For the Heisenberg algebra with basis `x, y, z` and
  `⁅x, y⁆ = z` central, the adjoint representation is nonzero and kills `z`: a definition of
  faithfulness as "`ρ ≠ 0`" would accept it, injectivity does not.

### 0.2 The left-regular representation

For a finite-dimensional associative `K`-algebra `A` and a Lie homomorphism `q : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ A`,
left multiplication gives the representation `LieHom.leftRegularRep q : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K A`.
It is injective iff `q` is (`LieHom.leftRegularRep_injective_iff`, resting on injectivity of left
multiplication, Mathlib `Algebra.lmul_injective`); `x` is in its kernel iff `q x = 0`
(`LieHom.leftRegularRep_eq_zero_iff`); and `x` acts nilpotently iff `q x` is nilpotent in `A`
(`LieHom.isNilpotent_leftRegularRep_iff`). Hence an injective Lie homomorphism into a
finite-dimensional associative algebra gives a faithful finite-dimensional representation
(`TauCeti.ado_of_finiteAssociativeEmbedding`).
*Needs:* Mathlib `Algebra.lmul_injective`; Tau Ceti `LieHom.leftRegularRep`,
`LieHom.leftRegularRep_injective_iff`, `LieHom.leftRegularRep_eq_zero_iff`,
`LieHom.isNilpotent_leftRegularRep_iff`, `TauCeti.ado_of_finiteAssociativeEmbedding`.

### 0.3 The finite-target reduction

If `q : U(L) →ₐ[K] A` is an algebra map to a finite-dimensional `K`-algebra and `x ↦ q (ι x)`
is injective, then `L` has a faithful finite-dimensional representation, on `A`
(`TauCeti.ado_of_finiteEnvelopingTarget`). Conversely a faithful representation on `V` extends by
the universal property to `U(L) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`, injective on the canonical copy of `L`. The
two conditions are equivalent (`TauCeti.faithfulRepresentation_iff_finiteEnvelopingTarget`, for
`K` and `L` in one universe), for every Lie algebra `L`, finite-dimensional or not.
*Needs:* §0.2; Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ado_of_finiteEnvelopingTarget`, `TauCeti.faithfulRepresentation_iff_finiteEnvelopingTarget`.

**Checks.**
- If `Z(L) = 0`, the adjoint representation is faithful and the reduction is realised by
  `A = Module.End K L` and the lift of `ad`; for the affine-line algebra this is a
  two-dimensional faithful representation. For the Heisenberg algebra, whose centre is `K z`, `ad`
  kills `z`, and the reduction needs another target (§2.3).

### 0.4 Direct sums separate finitely many directions

The product of two representations, `LieHom.prodRepresentation`, has kernel the intersection of
the two kernels (`LieHom.ker_prodRepresentation`) and is faithful iff the kernels are disjoint
(`LieHom.prodRepresentation_injective_iff`); the same holds for a family
(`LieHom.piRepresentation`, `LieHom.ker_piRepresentation`, `LieHom.piRepresentation_injective_iff`).
Consequently, if every nonzero `x : L` is detected by some finite-dimensional representation, then
finite-dimensionality of `L` lets finitely many of them assemble into a faithful one
(`TauCeti.exists_faithfulRepresentation_of_pointSeparating`). The characteristic-zero assembly uses
this in the form "`ρ₀ ⊕ ad` is faithful when `ker ρ₀ ∩ Z(L) = 0`" (§5.4).
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `LieHom.prodRepresentation`, `LieHom.ker_prodRepresentation`,
`LieHom.prodRepresentation_injective_iff`, `LieHom.piRepresentation`, `LieHom.ker_piRepresentation`,
`LieHom.piRepresentation_injective_iff`, `TauCeti.exists_faithfulRepresentation_of_pointSeparating`.

### 0.5 The nilradical

The nilradical `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical K L` is defined as
`sSup {I : LieIdeal K L | LieRing.IsNilpotent I}` (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_def`). The sum of
two nilpotent ideals is nilpotent (`LieIdeal.isNilpotentSup`), which makes the supremum well
behaved, and for `L` Noetherian the nilradical is itself nilpotent (the instance
`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradicalIsNilpotent`). Its basic theory:

- every nilpotent ideal lies in it (`LieIdeal.le_nilradical`), and for `L` Noetherian an ideal is
  nilpotent iff it lies in it (`LieIdeal.isNilpotent_iff_le_nilradical`); it is below an ideal `J`
  iff every nilpotent ideal is (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_le_iff`);
- a Lie algebra isomorphism `e : L ≃ₗ⁅K⁆ L'` carries `nil L` onto `nil L'`
  (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_map_equiv`), so every automorphism preserves it;
- `Z(L) ≤ nil L ≤ radical K L` (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.center_le_nilradical`,
  `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_le_radical`);
- for `L` Noetherian every `x ∈ nil L` has `ad x` nilpotent
  (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.isNilpotent_ad_of_mem_nilradical`);
- `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal K L ≤ nil L` (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal_le_nilradical`),
  strictly for the affine-line algebra (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.maxNilpotentIdeal_lt_nilradical`);
- for `L` Noetherian, `nil L = ⊤` iff `L` is nilpotent (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_eq_top_iff`).

Derivation-invariance of the nilradical is a characteristic-zero statement and is §3.4.
*Needs:* Mathlib `LieRing.IsNilpotent`, `LieAlgebra.radical`, `LieAlgebra.center`,
`LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra over a field, `nil L = K y` and `maxNilpotentIdeal = ⊥`
  (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.nilradical_eq_translationIdeal`,
  `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.maxNilpotentIdeal_eq_bot`): `K y` is abelian, hence nilpotent as a
  Lie algebra, while `ad x` is the identity on it, so `L` does not act nilpotently on it. Defining
  the nilradical as `maxNilpotentIdeal` would give `⊥`, and the nilrepresentation condition would
  become vacuous for this algebra.
- For `sl₂` the nilradical is `0`; for an abelian or a Heisenberg algebra it is everything.

### Examples

- For an abelian `L` of dimension `n`, `L` acts on `K × L` by `x · (a, v) = (0, a x)`:
  the representation `TauCeti.abelianSquareZeroRepresentation`, faithful
  (`TauCeti.abelianSquareZeroRepresentation_injective`), with all products `ρ x ∘ ρ y` zero
  (`TauCeti.abelianSquareZeroRepresentation_mul_eq_zero`), on a space of dimension `n + 1`
  (`TauCeti.exists_faithful_squareZeroRepresentation`). The adjoint representation of `L` is zero.
- For the affine-line algebra, `x ↦ [[1, 0], [0, 0]]` and `y ↦ [[0, 1], [0, 0]]` is a faithful
  representation by affine maps of the line, and `y` acts nilpotently, as §0.5 requires of a
  nilrepresentation.

### Dependencies

Mathlib: Lie homomorphisms and modules, `LieModule.IsFaithful`, the universal enveloping algebra
and its universal property, `LieAlgebra.radical`, `LieAlgebra.maxNilpotentIdeal`. Tau Ceti: the
left-regular representation, the finite-target reduction, product representations and the
nilradical, with the declarations named above. Nothing of Layers 1–9 is used.

## Layer 1: the PBW consequences Ado uses

The PBW theorem and its corollaries are consumed here over an arbitrary field, with no
characteristic or algebraic-closure hypothesis; Ado–Iwasawa needs every item over `𝔽₂`. They are
one shared Tau Ceti development, never a second PBW theorem beside the Ado proof (Jacobson,
*Lie Algebras*, Chapters V–VI).

### 1.1 The canonical embedding

For `L` free as a module, in particular for every Lie algebra over a field, `ι : L → U(L)` is
injective (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι_injective`), and the images of a basis are linearly
independent (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.linearIndependent_ι_basis`).
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι_injective`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.linearIndependent_ι_basis`.

### 1.2 The PBW filtration and ordered monomials

The degree filtration `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwFiltration K L k` is multiplicative,
`F_i · F_j = F_(i+j)` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwFiltration_mul`), and exhaustive
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.iSup_pbwFiltration_eq_top`). Basis-free, the symmetric
algebra is isomorphic to the associated graded algebra
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwAssociatedGradedEquiv`, for `L` free). For a basis `b` of `L`
indexed by a linearly ordered type, the ordered monomials form a basis `Module.Basis.pbwBasis` of
`U(L)`, the vector at a multi-exponent `n` being the increasing product of the `ι (b i) ^ n i`
(`Module.Basis.pbwBasis_eq_prod_pow`); those of degree at most `k` span `F_k`
(`Module.Basis.span_image_pbwBasis_eq_pbwFiltration`), and `Module.Basis.pbwEquiv` is the resulting
linear isomorphism with the polynomial ring.
*Needs:* Mathlib `SymmetricAlgebra`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra with `x < y`, the ordered monomials are `x^a y^b`; the product
  `y x` is not one of them, and equals `x y − y`, of degree `2` but with a degree-one correction.
  So `Module.Basis.pbwEquiv` is a linear and not an algebra isomorphism; it is multiplicative
  exactly when `L` is abelian.

### 1.3 Functoriality

For every Lie homomorphism `f : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ M`, the induced map of enveloping algebras preserves the
filtration (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.mapFiltration`) and induces the map of associated
graded algebras compatible with the symmetric algebra
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.mapAssociatedGraded`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.mapAssociatedGraded_comp_pbwAssociatedGradedMap`), and it carries
the span of the ordered monomials in a family `e` onto the span of those in `f ∘ e`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_span_orderedPBWMonomials`). For surjections and quotients
the filtration maps onto the filtration
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_pbwFiltration_eq_of_surjective`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_mkQ_pbwFiltration`); for an injective `f` over a field the
induced map is injective and meets the filtration in the filtration of the source
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_injective`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_pbwFiltration_eq_inf_range_of_injective`); for a direct
sum, `U(L × M)` is `U(L) ⊗ U(M)` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.prodEquivTensor`). The
semidirect and central-polynomial arguments of Layers 3–7 use these and re-prove no straightening
identity by hand.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 1.4 Domain and Noetherian corollaries

For `L` finite-dimensional over a field, `U(L)` has no zero divisors and is left and right
Noetherian. More generally, `U(L)` is a domain when the coefficient ring is a domain and `L` is free
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.instIsDomain`), it is left Noetherian when the coefficient ring
is Noetherian and `L` is finite (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.instIsNoetherianRing`), and then
right Noetherian (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.instIsNoetherianRingMulOpposite`). Both pass
from the associated graded algebra, the symmetric algebra for `L` free and a quotient of it in
general, by the filtered-to-graded transfers
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.noZeroDivisors_of_noZeroDivisors_pbwAssociatedGraded` and
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_of_isNoetherianRing_pbwAssociatedGraded`.
*Needs:* §1.2; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 1.5 Finite spanning from central monic relations

Let `q : U(L) →ₐ[K] A` be surjective, `S` a commutative ring of scalars of `A`, and `e` a finite
family spanning `L`. If each `q (ι (e i))` is a root of a monic polynomial over `S`, the bounded
ordered products of the `q (ι (e i))` span `A` over `S`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.span_range_bounded_orderedPowerProducts_eq_top`), and `A` is a
finite `S`-module (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.moduleFinite_of_isIntegral_of_span_eq_top`).
A commutative subalgebra `R` of the centre of `U(L)` is made a ring of scalars by
`Subalgebra.centralSubalgebraAlgebra`; with `q` the identity this is the module-finiteness theorem
consumed by Layer 6. A relation of degree `0` would read `1 = 0` and is excluded by monicity.
*Needs:* §1.2; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### Examples

- For an abelian `L`, `U(L)` is the symmetric algebra, the ordered monomials are the monomials,
  `Module.Basis.pbwEquiv` is an algebra isomorphism with the polynomial ring, and the domain and
  Noetherian properties are those of a polynomial ring.
- Over `𝔽₂`, `𝔽₃` and every finite field the statements of this layer hold without change: none
  carries a characteristic hypothesis.

### Dependencies

Mathlib: the universal enveloping algebra, symmetric algebras, filtrations and Noetherian rings.
Tau Ceti: the PBW development named above. Nothing of Layer 0 is used.

## Layer 2: augmentation, weighted PBW truncations, and the nilpotent engine

### 2.1 The augmentation

The augmentation `ε` is the counit of `U(L)`, equal to the lift of the zero Lie homomorphism
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.counitAlgHom_eq_lift_zero`); it vanishes on `ι x`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι_mem_augmentation`); its kernel `U⁺(L)` is the ideal
generated by `ι L`, which is two-sided (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.augmentation_toIdeal_eq_span_range_ι`,
`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.augmentation_toIdeal`), and `U⁺(L)^n` is generated by the products of `n` elements
of `ι L` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.augmentation_toIdeal_pow_eq_span_range_ι_pow`). Its
universal property: an algebra map `f : U(L) →ₐ[K] A` kills `ι L` iff it is `u ↦ ε(u) · 1`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.eq_ofId_comp_counitAlgHom_iff`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.apply_eq_algebraMap_counit_of_ι_eq_zero`).
*Needs:* Mathlib `Bialgebra.counitAlgHom`, `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`; Tau Ceti the Hopf
structure on `U(L)` and the declarations named above.

### 2.2 Powers and brackets

With Convention 7, an element of the `n`-th term of the lower central series maps into
`U⁺(L)^(n+1)` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι_mem_augmentation_toIdeal_pow_of_mem_lowerCentralSeries`);
in particular `ι ⁅x, y⁆ ∈ U⁺(L)^2` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.ι_lie_mem_augmentation_toIdeal_sq`).
The intersection `L ∩ U⁺(L)^n` is not identified with a term of the lower central series: such an
identification is a dimension-subalgebra theorem with its own hypotheses, particularly in positive
characteristic, and no statement here uses one.
*Needs:* §2.1; Mathlib `LieModule.lowerCentralSeries`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the Heisenberg algebra with basis `x, y, z` and `⁅x, y⁆ = z` central, `ι z ∈ U⁺^2` and
  `ι z ∉ U⁺^3` (`TauCeti.ι_heisenbergZ_not_mem_augmentation_toIdeal_cube`), with `z` in term `1`
  of the lower central series. An indexing that sent term `n` into `U⁺^(n+2)` would put `ι z` in
  `U⁺^3`, which is false; one that sent it into `U⁺^n` would only give `ι z ∈ U⁺`.

### 2.3 Weighted truncation for nilpotent algebras

Let `b` be a basis of `L` indexed by a linear order and `w` a weight on the indices compatible with
the bracket: the coordinate of `⁅b i, b j⁆` along `b k` vanishes whenever `w k < w i + w j`. Give an
ordered monomial the sum of the weights of its factors. For every `N`, the span of the ordered
monomials of weight at least `N` is the ideal `Module.Basis.weightedPBWIdeal`, **two-sided**
(`Module.Basis.weightedPBWIdeal_isTwoSided`): weights add on both sides and straightening only
raises weight. If every weight is positive the quotient is a finite module
(`Module.Basis.moduleFinite_quotient_weightedPBWIdeal`), and if every weight is below `N` the
canonical copy of `L` injects into it (`Module.Basis.quotient_weightedPBWIdeal_ι_injective`). For `L`
nilpotent and finite-dimensional, a basis adapted to the lower central series, each vector weighted
by its depth in the series, satisfies these hypotheses with some `N`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_weightedPBWIdeal_of_isNilpotent`). The quotient is then a
finite-dimensional target for §0.3.
*Needs:* §§1.2–1.3; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the Heisenberg algebra with weights `(1, 1, 2)` for `(x, y, z)` and `N = 3`, the quotient has
  the basis of the seven ordered monomials `1, x, y, z, x², x y, y²` of weight `< 3`
  (`TauCeti.HeisenbergWeightedPBW.finrank_quotient`), and `ι z` is nonzero in it
  (`TauCeti.HeisenbergWeightedPBW.mk_heisenbergZ_ne_zero`). With the same weights and `N = 2` the
  quotient has basis `1, x, y` and `ι z` lies in the ideal, so the canonical copy of `L` does not
  inject: the condition that every weight be below `N` is needed. Weighting `z` by `1` violates the
  bracket condition, since `⁅x, y⁆ = z` and `1 < 1 + 1`.
- For an abelian `L` with all weights `1` and `N = 2`, the quotient is `K ⊕ L` with `L · L = 0`,
  the square-zero algebra of §0's example (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.augmentationSqEquiv`
  identifies `U(L) / U⁺^2` with `TrivSqZeroExt K L`).

### 2.4 The nilpotent action

Left multiplication on the weighted quotient is the representation
`Module.Basis.weightedPBWRepresentation`; it is faithful when every weight is below `N`
(`Module.Basis.weightedPBWRepresentation_injective`), and when every weight is positive each `x : L`
acts with `N`-th power zero (`Module.Basis.weightedPBWRepresentation_pow_eq_zero`): multiplication
by an element of `U⁺` raises weight by at least one. Hence a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra
over any field has a faithful finite-dimensional representation by nilpotent endomorphisms
(`TauCeti.exists_faithful_weightedPBWRepresentation`). This is the characteristic-free Birkhoff form.
*Needs:* §§0.2, 2.3; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### Examples

- The Heisenberg algebra acts faithfully on the seven-dimensional quotient of §2.3, and the action
  detects its centre (`TauCeti.heisenbergTruncatedRepresentation_injective` and
  `TauCeti.heisenbergTruncatedRepresentation_heisenbergZ_ne_zero`, on `U / U⁺^3`, which is the same
  quotient: a product of three elements of `L` has weight at least `3`, and conversely an ordered
  monomial `x^a y^b z^c` of weight `a + b + 2c ≥ 3` lies in `U⁺^(a+b+2c)`, `z` lying in `U⁺^2`); the
  adjoint representation kills `z`.
- For an abelian `L` the representation is the square-zero representation of §0's example.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–1. Mathlib: the counit of a bialgebra, the lower central series. Tau Ceti: the Hopf
algebra structure of `U(L)`, the augmentation ideal and the weighted PBW development named above.

## Layer 3: derivations of enveloping algebras and stable cofinite ideals

The first layer of the characteristic-zero track (Fulton–Harris, Appendix E, §E.2, pp. 499–503;
Asgarli, *Ado's Theorem*).

### 3.1 Noncommutative derivations

Mathlib's `Derivation` is a derivation of a commutative algebra into a module. For a possibly
noncommutative `K`-algebra `A`, the `K`-linear endomorphisms `D` with `D (a b) = D a · b + a · D b`
form the Lie subalgebra `TauCeti.derivationLieAlgebra K A` of `Module.End K A`, closed under the
commutator; for a commutative `A` it is `Derivation K A A`
(`TauCeti.derivationEquivDerivationLieAlgebra`). The derivations preserving a submodule form
`TauCeti.stableDerivations`; a derivation preserving a two-sided ideal `I` descends to `A ⧸ I`
(`TauCeti.derivationQuotientHom`), and a derivation whose range lies in `I` preserves every power of
`I` (`TauCeti.mem_stableDerivations_pow_of_range_le`).
*Needs:* Mathlib `Derivation`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 3.2 Lifting Lie derivations

For `D : LieDerivation K L L`, `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation K L D` is a
derivation `Dᵁ` of `U(L)` with `Dᵁ (ι x) = ι (D x)`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation_ι`) and the Leibniz rule
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation_mul`). It is unique: two derivations of
`U(L)` agreeing on `ι L` are equal (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.derivation_ext`). The lift is
a Lie algebra homomorphism `D ↦ Dᵁ`, so linear in `D` with `[D₁, D₂]ᵁ = [D₁ᵁ, D₂ᵁ]`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivationHom`), and natural: if `f : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ L'`
intertwines `D` and `E`, the induced map of enveloping algebras intertwines `Dᵁ` and `Eᵁ`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.map_envelopingDerivation`). The range of `Dᵁ` lies in a
two-sided ideal `I` iff `ι (D x) ∈ I` for all `x`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation_range_le_iff`), and then `Dᵁ` preserves
every power of `I` (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation_mem_stableDerivations_pow`).
*Needs:* §3.1; Mathlib `LieDerivation`, `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`; Tau Ceti the
declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the inner derivation `D = ad x`, `Dᵁ` is the commutator with `ι x`
  (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.envelopingDerivation_inner`). A lift defined by
  `Dᵁ (ι x₁ ⋯ ι xₙ) = ι (D x₁) ⋯ ι (D xₙ)` would be multiplicative rather than a derivation, and on
  `ι x · ι y` would give `ι (D x) ι (D y)` instead of `ι (D x) ι y + ι x ι (D y)`.

### 3.3 Scalar extension and descent

Mathlib's Lie theorem, `LieModule.exists_nontrivial_weightSpace_of_isSolvable`, assumes
`[LieModule.IsTriangularizable K L V]` as well as `[CharZero K]`, and triangularizability holds over
an algebraically closed field but not over `ℝ` or `ℚ`. The characteristic-zero track resolves this
structurally, by checking containments of ideals after extension of scalars and descending them,
and never by adding a closure hypothesis to a theorem; Mathlib's Cartan criterion
(`Mathlib/Algebra/Lie/CartanCriterion.lean`) is a precedent for arranging a characteristic-zero
structural result this way. Nothing downstream extends the scalars of a representation, only these
structural statements.

In characteristic zero, for `L` finite-dimensional and `A` a principal ideal domain over `K` (for
instance a field extension), the radical commutes with extension of scalars,
`radical A (A ⊗[K] L) = (radical K L).baseChange A` (`LieAlgebra.radical_baseChange`); for every
commutative `A`, the extended nilradical lies in the nilradical of the extension
(`LieAlgebra.baseChange_nilradical_le`); nilpotence of an ideal descends along a faithfully flat
extension (`LieIdeal.isNilpotent_baseChange_iff`); a Lie derivation extends to
`LieDerivation A (A ⊗[K] L) (A ⊗[K] L)` (`LieDerivation.baseChange`); and range membership
descends: if `1 ⊗ s` lies in the range of `ad (1 ⊗ s)` on `A ⊗[K] L`, with `A` faithfully flat, then
`s = ⁅s, t⁆` for some `t : L` (`LieAlgebra.exists_eq_lie_of_one_tmul_mem_range_ad`).

Prove that the nilradical commutes with extension of scalars to an algebraic closure: for `K` of
characteristic zero, `L` finite-dimensional and `K̄` an algebraic closure of `K`,
`nil (K̄ ⊗[K] L) = (nil L).baseChange K̄`, as Lie ideals of `K̄ ⊗[K] L`. One inclusion is
`LieAlgebra.baseChange_nilradical_le`. *Route.* The nilradical of `K̄ ⊗[K] L` is carried to itself
by every semilinear Lie automorphism `σ ⊗ id` with `σ` in the Galois group of `K̄` over `K`
(nilpotent ideals go to nilpotent ideals), so it is spanned by its Galois-fixed vectors, which form
an ideal `N'` of `L` with `N' ⊗ K̄` equal to it; `N'` is nilpotent by
`LieIdeal.isNilpotent_baseChange_iff`, hence `N' ≤ nil L`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `LieSubmodule.baseChange`, `AlgebraicClosure`, the Galois group of `K̄` over `K`
and its fixed field (characteristic zero is perfect); Tau Ceti `LieAlgebra.baseChange_nilradical_le`,
`LieIdeal.isNilpotent_baseChange_iff`, `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.nilradical_map_equiv`.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra over `ℚ`, both sides are `ℚ̄ y`: the extension of scalars is the
  affine-line algebra over `ℚ̄`, whose nilradical is `ℚ̄ y` by §0.5. The radical, on both sides, is
  the whole algebra, so the target is not the radical statement `LieAlgebra.radical_baseChange`
  again.

### 3.4 The nilradical under derivations

Over a field of characteristic zero, for `L` finite-dimensional: every derivation `D` maps the
radical into the nilradical (`LieDerivation.apply_mem_nilradical_of_mem_radical`), in particular a
derivation of a solvable `L` maps `L` into `nil L` (`LieDerivation.apply_mem_nilradical_of_isSolvable`);
the nilradical is stable under every derivation
(`LieDerivation.apply_mem_nilradical_of_mem_nilradical`), and so is the radical, its image lying
in `nil L ≤ radical K L`; `⁅L, radical K L⁆ ≤ nil L`, by applying
the first statement to inner derivations (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.lie_radical_le_nilradical`); and for
every ideal `I`, the nilradical of `I` as a Lie algebra consists of the elements of `I` lying in
`nil L`, `nil I = nil L ⊓ I` (`LieIdeal.restrict_nilradical`): derivation-invariance makes `nil I`
an ideal of `L`, and the reverse inclusion is immediate. No characteristic-free version of the
first statement is asserted. *Route* (Fulton–Harris, Appendix E, §E.2; Asgarli): these are the
statements for which the descent of §3.3 is used, a containment of ideals being checked after
extension of scalars to an algebraic closure, where Lie's theorem applies, and descended.
*Needs:* §§0.5, 3.3; Mathlib `LieDerivation`, `LieAlgebra.radical`; Tau Ceti the declarations named
above.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra every derivation `D` has `D x, D y ∈ K y`: writing
  `D x = a₁ x + b₁ y` and `D y = a₂ x + b₂ y`, applying `D` to `⁅x, y⁆ = y` gives `a₂ = 0` and
  `a₁ = 0`. This agrees with `D L ≤ nil L = K y` for the solvable algebra `L`.
- For `L = sl₂`, `nil L = 0` and `ad h ≠ 0`: a version of the first statement with `L` in place of
  `radical K L` would fail, and the solvability hypothesis of the second cannot be dropped.

### 3.5 Cofinite kernels

For a Lie homomorphism `f : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ A` into an algebra that is Noetherian as a `K`-module, for
instance a finite-dimensional representation `σ` of a solvable `S` extended to
`U(S) →ₐ[K] Module.End K V`, the kernel of the lift is a two-sided ideal of finite codimension
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.finite_quotient_ker_lift`), and `f x` is nilpotent iff some
power of `ι x` lies in that kernel
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isNilpotent_iff_exists_pow_ι_mem_ker_lift`).
*Needs:* Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 3.6 The derivation-stable cofinite refinement

Let `I` be a two-sided ideal of `U(S)` of finite codimension and `N` a Lie ideal of `S` whose
elements are nilpotent modulo `I`. Let `B = I ⊔ N·U(S)`, the two-sided ideal generated by `I` and
`ι N` (`LieIdeal.envelopingIdeal`), and `Q` its image in `U(S) ⧸ I`. *Route.* Engel's theorem for the
left multiplication action of `N` on `U(S) ⧸ I`, with PBW straightening moving the factors from `N`
together, gives `m` with `Q^m = 0`, that is `B^m ≤ I`. If `D S ≤ N`, then `Dᵁ (U(S)) ≤ B`, since
`ι S` generates `U(S)`; `B` itself need not be stable, `I` not being assumed stable, but the Leibniz
rule and the range containment give `Dᵁ (B^m) ≤ B^m` (§3.2). For an ordered basis `x₁, …, x_d` of
`S` there are monic polynomials `Pᵢ` with `Pᵢ (ι xᵢ) ∈ B`, as `U(S) ⧸ B` is finite-dimensional;
their leading terms and the ordered PBW basis show that `U(S) ⧸ B^m` is finite-dimensional
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.moduleFinite_quotient_pow`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.moduleFinite_quotient_pow_of_isIntegral`). This noncentral
monic-relation argument is distinct from the central module-finiteness theorem of §1.5. Finally,
if `x ∈ N` and `ι(x)^e ∈ I`, then `ι(x)^(e m) ∈ B^m`, so elements of `N` stay nilpotent modulo
`J = B^m`.

Packaged: for `N` and `I` as above, some power `J = (I ⊔ N·U(S))^n` lies in `I`, has
finite-dimensional quotient, is stable under `Dᵁ` for every derivation with `D S ≤ N`, and has
exactly the nilpotent elements modulo it that `I` has
(`LieIdeal.exists_cofinite_refinement_stableDerivations`). For `S` solvable and finite-dimensional
over a field of characteristic zero, with `N = nil S`, every derivation qualifies by §3.4, and the
refinement is stable under every lifted derivation
(`Ideal.exists_cofinite_refinement_stableDerivations_of_isSolvable`). This is the input of Layer 4.
Solvability enters through `D S ≤ nil S` (§3.4): for a semisimple `S` and `D = ad x ≠ 0`,
`nil S = 0` while `D S ≠ 0`, so the range containment `Dᵁ (U(S)) ≤ B` fails, and the statement is
made for solvable `S`, as Layer 4 uses it.
*Needs:* §§1.2–1.3, 3.2, 3.4, 3.5; Mathlib `LieModule.isNilpotent_iff_forall` (Engel); Tau Ceti
`LieIdeal.envelopingIdeal` and the declarations named above.

### Examples

- For the affine-line algebra `S` and its two-dimensional affine representation `σ` (§0), the
  nilradical `K y` acts by the square-zero matrix, and every derivation of `S` is inner
  (`ad x` and `ad y` span the two-dimensional space of derivations of §3.4's Check), so the kernel
  `I` of `σ` on `U(S)` is stable under every lifted derivation, the lift of `ad u` being the
  commutator with `ι u` (§3.2).
- Isomorphic representations of `S` have the same kernel on `U(S)`, so the input `I` of §3.6, and
  the refinement built from it, depend only on the isomorphism class of the representation.

Layers 0–1. Mathlib: Lie derivations, extension of scalars of Lie algebras and modules, Engel's
theorem, Lie's theorem with its triangularizability hypothesis, algebraic closures and Galois

base-change results for the radical, the nilradical and derivations, the characteristic-zero
nilradical theory, cofinite kernels and the cofinite refinement named above.

## Layer 4: extending nilrepresentations across split ideal extensions

Let `S` be a Lie algebra, `H` a Lie algebra and `ψ : H →ₗ⁅K⁆ LieDerivation K S S`; the split
extension is Mathlib's semidirect sum `S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H` (`LieAlgebra.SemiDirectSum`), and the hypothesis
"`L` is a split extension of `H` by the ideal `S`" is stated externally, as
`Nonempty (L ≃ₗ⁅K⁆ S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H)` (Fulton–Harris, Appendix E, §E.2, Proposition E.5).

### 4.1 Split extensions in external form

For an ideal `S` of `L` and a Lie subalgebra `H` with `IsCompl S.toSubmodule H.toSubmodule`, the
restriction of `ad` gives `LieIdeal.ad S : L →ₗ⁅K⁆ LieDerivation K S S`, and
`LieIdeal.semiDirectSumEquiv` identifies `S ⋊⁅(LieIdeal.ad S).comp H.incl⁆ H` with `L`
(`LieIdeal.nonempty_lieEquiv_semiDirectSum`). The layer's theorems are stated against the external
form, so no client re-derives it.
*Needs:* Mathlib `LieAlgebra.SemiDirectSum`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 4.2 The multiplication-plus-derivation representation

Given a two-sided ideal `J` of `U(S)` stable under every `(ψ h)ᵁ`, the element `(s, h)` of
`S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H` acts on `U(S) ⧸ J` by left multiplication by `ι s` plus the derivation descended from
`(ψ h)ᵁ` (`TauCeti.envelopingQuotientDerivation`). The commutator identities among two
multiplication operators, a multiplication and a lifted derivation, and two lifted derivations make
this a Lie homomorphism `TauCeti.envelopingQuotientRep : S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H →ₗ⁅K⁆ Module.End K (U(S) ⧸ J)`.
*Needs:* §§3.1–3.2, 4.1; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 4.3 Kernel control

If `J` lies in the kernel of the extension of `σ` to `U(S)`, then every element of `S` killed by
`TauCeti.envelopingQuotientRep` is killed by `σ` (`TauCeti.ker_envelopingQuotientRep_comp_inl_le`).
This is the invariant that permits iteration; it does not assert that `σ` itself extends on the
same carrier.
*Needs:* §4.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ker_envelopingQuotientRep_comp_inl_le`.

### 4.4 Nilpotence control

For `S` finite-dimensional and `σ` a finite-dimensional representation of `S`, the extension exists
with kernel control and with an element of `S` acting nilpotently iff it does under `σ`, whenever
every `ψ h` takes values in a Lie ideal `N` of `S` acting nilpotently under `σ`
(`TauCeti.exists_semiDirectSum_rep_of_forall_mem`, with `J` from §3.6). The two hypotheses of
Fulton–Harris, Proposition E.5, are separated:

- in characteristic zero, for `S` solvable and `σ` a nilrepresentation, if the nilradical of
  `S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H` lies in the image of `nil S` (for `S` an ideal of `L`, the condition `nil L = nil S`),
  the extension is a nilrepresentation with kernel control
  (`TauCeti.exists_semiDirectSum_rep_of_isSolvable`);
- in every characteristic, if `S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H` is nilpotent and `σ` acts by nilpotent endomorphisms, the
  extension acts by nilpotent endomorphisms with kernel control
  (`TauCeti.exists_semiDirectSum_rep_of_isNilpotent`). The hypotheses that make the
  multiplication-plus-derivation sum of an element `y` nilpotent are that `σ` acts by nilpotent
  endomorphisms and that every vector of `S` is killed by some power of the derivation
  `ψ y.right`, which holds when `S ⋊⁅ψ⁆ H` is nilpotent (the third clause of
  `TauCeti.exists_semiDirectSum_rep_of_forall_mem`).

*Needs:* §§3.4, 3.6, 4.2–4.3; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 4.5 Transport along equivalences

Prove the internal form of the extension theorems of §4.4 and their invariance under the choices
made: for `L` finite-dimensional, an ideal `S` and a Lie subalgebra `H` with
`IsCompl S.toSubmodule H.toSubmodule`, and a finite-dimensional representation `σ` of `S`,

- in characteristic zero, if `S` is solvable, `σ` a nilrepresentation and `nil L = nil S` (as
  subsets of `L`), there is a finite-dimensional nilrepresentation `ρ` of `L` with
  `{s ∈ S | ρ s = 0} ⊆ ker σ`;
- if `L` is nilpotent and `σ` acts by nilpotent endomorphisms, there is a finite-dimensional
  representation `ρ` of `L` by nilpotent endomorphisms with `{s ∈ S | ρ s = 0} ⊆ ker σ`;

and these conclusions are unchanged when the equivalence `L ≃ S ⋊ H` of §4.1 is replaced by another
equivalence of split extensions, and when `σ` is replaced by an equivalent representation (`σ`
conjugated by a linear isomorphism `V ≃ₗ[K] V'`). Clients consume these statements and not the
chosen complement. *Route.* Compose the representations of §4.4 with the equivalence of §4.1;
kernels and nilpotence are carried along equivalences and conjugation.
*Needs:* §§4.1, 4.4; Tau Ceti `LieIdeal.restrict_nilradical` for the comparison of `nil L = nil S`
with the hypothesis of `TauCeti.exists_semiDirectSum_rep_of_isSolvable`.

### Examples

- In the Heisenberg algebra with centre `Z = K z`, the abelian ideal `S = K y ⊕ K z` has
  codimension one, complemented by `K x`, with `ψ x` the derivation `y ↦ z`, `z ↦ 0`. Starting from
  the faithful square-zero representation of `Z`, extended first to `S` (where `ψ = 0`) and then to
  the whole algebra, kernel control keeps `z` acting nonzero at each step; the adjoint
  representation of `S`, being zero, would lose it.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–3. Mathlib: semidirect sums of Lie algebras. Tau Ceti: the semidirect-sum equivalence for
complementary ideals and subalgebras, the multiplication-plus-derivation representation and its
kernel and nilpotence control named above.

## Layer 5: assemble Ado in characteristic zero

Throughout, `K` has characteristic zero, `L` is finite-dimensional, `N = nil L` and
`R = radical K L` (Fulton–Harris, Appendix E, §E.2; Asgarli).

### 5.1 A faithful nilrepresentation of the centre

The centre `Z(L)` is abelian, and the square-zero representation of §0's example is a faithful
representation of it by commuting endomorphisms with all products zero, on `K × Z(L)`; it is a
basis-free model (`TauCeti.abelianSquareZeroRepresentation`), and for a basis `z₁, …, z_k` of
`Z(L)` it sends `zᵢ` to the square-zero endomorphism `(a, v) ↦ (0, a zᵢ)`, detecting every basis
direction (`TauCeti.exists_faithful_squareZeroRepresentation`). Every element acts nilpotently, so
it is a nilrepresentation of the nilpotent algebra `Z(L)`.
*Needs:* §0's example; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 5.2 The flag through the nilradical

Prove the flag lemma: there are Lie subalgebras `Z(L) = S₀ ≤ S₁ ≤ ⋯ ≤ S_b = R` and an index `a`
with `S_a = N` such that each `S_i` is an ideal of `S_(i+1)` of codimension one, every `S_i` with
`i ≤ a` is nilpotent, and every `S_i` with `i ≥ a` is an ideal of `L` with `nil S_i = N`; in
particular `nil R = N`. *Route.* Below `N`, a proper subalgebra `T` of the nilpotent algebra `N`
is properly contained in its normalizer, so adding one vector of the normalizer outside `T` gives a
subalgebra in which `T` is an ideal of codimension one; start from `Z(L)`, which lies in `N`
(`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.center_le_nilradical`). Above `N`, `⁅L, R⁆ ≤ N`
(`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.lie_radical_le_nilradical`), so every subspace `T` with `N ≤ T ≤ R` is a Lie
subalgebra and an ideal of `L`, and an ordinary flag of subspaces supplies the steps;
`nil T = N ⊓ T = N` by `LieIdeal.restrict_nilradical`. Each step is a split extension (a line
complementing an ideal of codimension one is a subalgebra), and every application of §4.5 uses one
of its two hypotheses: the larger algebra is nilpotent below `N`, and the nilradicals agree above
it.
*Needs:* §§0.5, 3.4; Mathlib `LieSubalgebra.normalizer`, `LieSubmodule.ucs_eq_self_of_normalizer_eq_self`;
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.center_le_nilradical`, `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.lie_radical_le_nilradical`,
`LieIdeal.restrict_nilradical`.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra, `Z(L) = 0`, `N = K y`, `R = L`: the flag is `0 < K y < L`, with
  `a = 1`; `K y` is an ideal of `L`, and `nil L = K y`. Starting the flag at `0` and using only
  nilpotence of the larger algebra at every step would fail at `K y < L`, `L` not being nilpotent.

### 5.3 Levi decomposition

Over a field of characteristic zero, the radical of a finite-dimensional `L` has a complementary
Lie subalgebra (`TauCeti.exists_leviComplement`), and `L` is the semidirect sum
`R ⋊⁅ψ⁆ S` of its radical and a semisimple subalgebra `S` acting by derivations, in the form
`Nonempty (L ≃ₗ⁅K⁆ R ⋊⁅ψ⁆ S)` with `LieAlgebra.IsSemisimple K S` (`TauCeti.exists_leviDecomposition`),
which is what Layer 4 consumes. The proof uses neither Ado's theorem nor any linear structure theorem
derived from it. *Route* (Fulton–Harris, Appendix E). Induct on `finrank K L`; more generally, a
solvable ideal `I` with `L ⧸ I` semisimple has a complementary subalgebra
(`LieIdeal.exists_lieSubalgebra_isCompl_of_isSolvable`). If `I` contains a nonzero proper `L`-ideal
`A`, apply induction to `L ⧸ A`, pull its complement back to a subalgebra whose radical is `A`, and
apply induction there. This reduces to an `I` with no nonzero proper `L`-ideal; then `⁅I, I⁆` is
proper because `I` is solvable, so `I` is abelian, and minimality gives `⁅L, I⁆ = 0` or
`⁅L, I⁆ = I`. If `⁅L, I⁆ = 0`, the adjoint action factors through the semisimple quotient and Weyl's
complete reducibility complements `I` (`LieIdeal.exists_isCompl_of_le_center`). If `⁅L, I⁆ = I`,
consider the `L`-submodules of `Module.End K L`: `A` of the `ad r` with `r ∈ I`, `B` of the
endomorphisms with range in `I` vanishing on `I`, and `C` of those with range in `I` acting on `I`
by a scalar (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.homVanishingOn`, `TauCeti.homScalarOn`). Then `A ≤ B ≤ C`,
`C ⧸ B ≃ K`, `L · C ≤ B` and `I · C ≤ A`; complete reducibility for the semisimple quotient splits
`C ⧸ A → C ⧸ B`, a lift `φ` of `1` has `φ` the identity on `I` and `L · φ ≤ A`, and
`{x : L | x · φ = 0}` is the complement (`LieIdeal.exists_lieSubalgebra_isCompl_of_isLieAbelian`).
The internal form `IsCompl` is converted to the external form by §4.1. Conjugacy of Levi complements
is not a target: no statement here uses it.
*Needs:* §4.1; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.IsSemisimple`, `LieAlgebra.IsKilling`; Tau Ceti
`LieSubmodule.exists_isCompl_of_le_ker` (Weyl's complete reducibility for an action through a
semisimple quotient) and the declarations named above.

### 5.4 Ado's theorem in characteristic zero

Extend the representation of §5.1 along the flag of §5.2 by §4.5 and then across the Levi
complement by §§4.5 and 5.3 (applicable since `nil R = N` by §5.2): the result `ρ₀` is a
nilrepresentation of `L` with `ker ρ₀ ∩ Z(L) = 0`. Since the kernel of `ad` is `Z(L)`, the sum
`ρ₀ ⊕ ad` is faithful (§0.4), and it is still a nilrepresentation, `ad` being nilpotent on `N`
(§0.5). This gives `TauCeti.adoCharZero` and its nilrepresentation form
`TauCeti.exists_faithful_nilrepresentation_charZero`.
*Needs:* §§0.4–0.5, 4.5, 5.1–5.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.adoCharZero`,
`TauCeti.exists_faithful_nilrepresentation_charZero`.

**Checks.**
- For `L` with `Z(L) = 0`, such as `sl₂` or the affine-line algebra, `ad` alone is faithful, and
  the sum `ρ₀ ⊕ ad` is faithful whatever `ρ₀` is.
- For `L` nilpotent, `N = L = R`, the flag runs from `Z(L)` to `L` through nilpotent algebras, there
  is no Levi step, and the result acts by nilpotent endomorphisms, the guarantee of §2.4.

Hochschild's strengthening in characteristic zero is a separate argument on top of this, not a
corollary of it (Hochschild 1966). In §§5.5–5.8, `M` is a finite-dimensional `L`-module on which
`N` acts nilpotently and `x : L` has `ad x` nilpotent; the goal is that `x` acts nilpotently on `M`.

### 5.5 The nilpotent-extension lemma

If a Lie subalgebra `H` acts nilpotently on a module `M`, an element `y` of the normalizer of `H`
acts nilpotently, and `M` is Noetherian, then every element of the Lie algebra spanned by `y` and
`H` acts nilpotently (`LieSubalgebra.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_mem_lieSpan_insert_of_forall`; as a
nilpotent module, `LieSubalgebra.lieModule_isNilpotent_lieSpan_insert`). The special case of a Lie
ideal `I` whose elements act nilpotently: every element of `K y + I` acts nilpotently
(`LieIdeal.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_mem_span_singleton_sup`), and for the adjoint module and `I = N`,
every element of `K y + N` is `ad`-nilpotent when `y` is
(`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.isNilpotent_ad_of_mem_span_singleton_sup_nilradical`). It is stated once for a
general module and used on the adjoint module and on `M`.
*Needs:* §0.5; Mathlib `LieSubalgebra.normalizer`, `LieModule.IsNilpotent`; Tau Ceti the
declarations named above.

### 5.6 The semisimple component acts nilpotently

Write `L = S ⊕ R` with `S` a Levi complement (§5.3) and `x = s + r` accordingly. Prove that `ad x`
nilpotent forces `ad_S s` nilpotent, where `ad_S` is the adjoint representation of `S`.
*Route.* The projection `L → L ⧸ R ≅ S` is a Lie homomorphism sending `x` to `s`, and `ad` of the
image of an `ad`-nilpotent element is nilpotent on the quotient. Then `S` is semisimple, so its
Killing form is nondegenerate, and an `ad`-nilpotent `s` of `S` satisfies `⁅s, t⁆ = s` for some
`t : S` (`TauCeti.exists_lie_eq_self_of_isNilpotent_ad`), over the original field, with no algebraic
closure and no `sl₂`-triple: for `z ∈ ker (ad s)`, `ad z` commutes with the nilpotent `ad s`, so
`ad s ∘ ad z` is nilpotent and `κ(s, z) = 0`; hence `s ∈ (ker (ad s))ᗮ`, which is `range (ad s)` by
invariance and nondegeneracy of `κ` and rank–nullity (`TauCeti.mem_range_ad_self_of_isNilpotent_ad`).
An element with a nonzero adjoint eigenvalue, `⁅t, s⁆ = −s`, acts nilpotently in every
finite-dimensional representation (`TauCeti.isNilpotent_apply_of_lie_eq_smul`), so `s` acts
nilpotently on `L` and on `M` (`TauCeti.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_isNilpotent_ad`, applied to `S`). This
keeps the Jacobson–Morozov theorem out of the dependencies; Hochschild applies Lie's theorem to the
solvable algebra spanned by `s` and `t`, and the eigenvalue argument replaces it.
*Needs:* §5.3; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.IsKilling`, `LieAlgebra.ad`, Lie algebra quotients; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.exists_lie_eq_self_of_isNilpotent_ad`, `TauCeti.mem_range_ad_self_of_isNilpotent_ad`,
`TauCeti.isNilpotent_apply_of_lie_eq_smul`, `TauCeti.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_isNilpotent_ad`.

**Checks.**
- For `sl₂` and `s = e`, `t = h / 2` gives `⁅e, h / 2⁆ = −e`, and `⁅e, −h / 2⁆ = e`; the argument
  produces `t = −h / 2` up to an element of `ker (ad e) = K e`. For `s = h`, `ad h` is not
  nilpotent and `h` is not in `range (ad h)`, which is spanned by `e` and `f`.

### 5.7 The radical criterion

If `r ∈ R` and `ad r` is nilpotent, then `r ∈ N`
(`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.mem_nilradical_of_mem_radical_of_isNilpotent_ad`). *Route.* `⁅L, R⁆ ≤ N` makes
`K r + N` an ideal; §5.5 makes its adjoint action nilpotent, so by Engel's theorem it is a nilpotent
ideal, hence contained in `N`.
*Needs:* §§3.4, 5.5; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.isNilpotent_iff_forall`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.mem_nilradical_of_mem_radical_of_isNilpotent_ad`.

### 5.8 Hochschild's strengthening in characteristic zero

On the adjoint module, §5.5 shows that `H = K s + N` acts nilpotently, `s` being `ad`-nilpotent
(§5.6). Since `⁅x, s⁆ = ⁅r, s⁆ ∈ ⁅R, L⁆ ≤ N` and `⁅x, N⁆ ≤ N`, `x` normalizes `H`; applying §5.5
again, with `ad x` nilpotent, to the algebra spanned by `x` and `H`, the element `r = x − s` is
`ad`-nilpotent, and §5.7 gives `r ∈ N`. On `M`, `s` and `N` act nilpotently, so `K s + N` acts
nilpotently and contains `x = s + r`. So in characteristic zero every `ad`-nilpotent element acts
nilpotently on every finite-dimensional module on which `N` acts nilpotently
(`LieSubalgebra.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_isNilpotent_ad_of_isCompl_radical`, and for an explicit
homomorphism `LieSubalgebra.isNilpotent_apply_of_isNilpotent_ad_of_isCompl_radical`), and the
representation of §5.4 satisfies Hochschild's condition: that is
`TauCeti.exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charZero`.
*Needs:* §§5.3–5.7; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### Examples

- For `L = sl₂` over `ℚ`, `nil L = 0`, so the nilrepresentation condition is vacuous, while
  Hochschild's condition requires `ρ e` and `ρ f` nilpotent. In characteristic zero every
  finite-dimensional representation of `sl₂` meets it (§5.6), and by §5.8 the nilrepresentation
  condition implies Hochschild's condition for every `L`; in characteristic `p` they differ (§8.2).
- For a nilpotent `L`, the representation of §5.4 agrees at the level of guarantees with that of
  §2.4: faithful, by nilpotent endomorphisms.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–4. Mathlib: Engel's theorem, the Killing form, semisimple Lie algebras, normalizers,
semidirect sums. Tau Ceti: the Levi decomposition and Weyl's complete reducibility for an action
through a semisimple quotient, the square-zero representation, the nilpotent-extension lemma, the
`⁅s, t⁆ = s` theorem and the characteristic-zero assembly named above.

## Layer 6: central `p`-polynomials and module-finiteness

Throughout, `K` has prime characteristic `p` (`[Fact p.Prime] [CharP K p]`) and `L` is
finite-dimensional (Hochschild 1966; Jacobson, *Lie Algebras*, Chapters V–VI and pp. 202–203).

### 6.1 The Frobenius commutator identity

In an associative algebra `A` of exponential characteristic `p`, `ad (a ^ p ^ n) = (ad a) ^ p ^ n`,
where on the left `ad` is the commutator with an element and on the right a power of the
endomorphism `ad a` of `A` (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.ad_pow_expChar_pow`); hence, for `p ≠ 1`, `ad a` is
nilpotent iff some `a ^ p ^ n` is central
(`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.isNilpotent_ad_iff_exists_pow_expChar_pow_mem_center`). The supporting binomial
and iterated-commutator API is `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.ad_pow_eq_sum`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `LieAlgebra.ad`, `ExpChar`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For `a = diag(1, 0)` in `2 × 2` matrices, `a² = a`, so `ad (a²) = ad a`, with eigenvalue `−1` on
  `E₂₁`, while `(ad a)²` has eigenvalue `+1` there: the identity fails over `ℚ` with `p = 2`, and
  holds over `𝔽₂`, where `−1 = 1`.

### 6.2 Central `p`-polynomials

For each `x : L`, finite-dimensionality makes the endomorphisms `(ad x) ^ p ^ i` of `L` linearly
dependent; a monic linearized relation turns into a central element
`c_x = x ^ p ^ e + Σ_{i < e} aᵢ x ^ p ^ i` of `U(L)`, with `ι x` in place of `x`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_pCentralPolynomial`). It has zero constant term, so it
lies in the augmentation ideal as well as the centre, that is in `C`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pPolynomial_ι_mem_augmentation_toIdeal`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pPolynomial_ι_mem_centralAugmentation`); `e` is not asserted
minimal. Under a **surjective** Lie homomorphism `f`, the same polynomial in `f x` is central, the
image of `ι L` still generating (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pPolynomial_map_mem_center_of_surjective`).
For an arbitrary Lie homomorphism, the image of `c_x` commutes with the image of `U(L)`
(Mathlib `Commute.map`) and need not be central.

Prove naturality under extension of the field: for a field extension `K'` of `K`, if
`ι x ^ p ^ e + Σ_{i < e} aᵢ • ι x ^ p ^ i` is central in `U(L)`, then the same expression in
`ι (1 ⊗ x)`, with the same coefficients, is central in `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra K' (K' ⊗[K] L)`.
*Route.* The algebra map `U(L) → U(K' ⊗[K] L)` induced by `x ↦ ι (1 ⊗ x)` has image generating the
target over `K'`, and carries a central element to one commuting with that image.
*Needs:* §6.1; Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`, `Subalgebra.center`, `Commute.map`; Tau
Ceti the declarations named above.

**Checks.**
- For the affine-line algebra in characteristic `p`, `ad x` is idempotent (`x ↦ 0`, `y ↦ y`) and
  `(ad y)² = 0`, so `x^p − x` and `y^p` are central
  (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.ι_dilation_pow_sub_ι_dilation_mem_center`,
  `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.ι_translation_pow_mem_center`), while `x^p` and `x` are not. In
  characteristic `2`, `⁅x², y⁆ = x y + y x = 2 x y − y = y`, so `⁅x² − x, y⁆ = 0` and
  `⁅y², x⁆ = −2 y² = 0`; the same computation holds in characteristic `3` with `x³ − x` and `y³`.
- Naturality fails for an arbitrary Lie homomorphism: for the abelian `K x`, `ad x = 0` and
  `c_x = x` (`e = 0`) is central in `K[x]`, but its image under the inclusion `x ↦ x` into the
  affine-line algebra is `ι x`, which is not central, since `ι u` is central only for `u = 0`
  (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.AffineLine.ι_mem_center_iff_eq_zero`). The inclusion is not surjective.

### 6.3 The adjoint-nilpotent specialization

If `ad x` is nilpotent, some `ι x ^ p ^ e` is central
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_pow_ι_mem_center_of_isNilpotent_ad`), hence lies in `C`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_pow_ι_mem_centralAugmentation_of_isNilpotent_ad`). This
is the step that later forces `x` to act nilpotently (§8.2).
*Needs:* §6.1; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 6.4 A finite central generating family

For the basis `x₁, …, x_d` of `L` given by `Module.finBasis`, there are central `p`-polynomials
`c₁, …, c_d` of augmentation zero such that `R₀ = K[c₁, …, c_d]` is a commutative Noetherian
`K`-algebra over which `U(L)` is a finite module
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_pCentralGenerators_moduleFinite`), by §1.5, each `ι xᵢ`
being a root of the monic polynomial `T ^ p ^ eᵢ + Σ aᵢⱼ T ^ p ^ j − cᵢ` over `R₀` (PBW reduction of
exponents). Consequently the centre `Z(U(L))` is Noetherian and `U(L)` is a finite module over it
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isNoetherianRing_center`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.moduleFinite_over_center`).
*Needs:* §§1.5, 6.2; Tau Ceti `Subalgebra.centralSubalgebraAlgebra` and the declarations named above.

### 6.5 The full central ideal and its extension from a commutative ring

`UC`, the ideal generated by `C`, is two-sided because `C` is central
(`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.instIsTwoSidedCentralAugmentationIdeal`). By left Noetherianity (§1.4) it has a
finite generating set contained in `C` (`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.exists_finset_span_eq_centralAugmentationIdeal`).
This full ideal, not only the ideal generated by the chosen basis polynomials, yields Hochschild's
strengthened conclusion, since every adjoint-nilpotent `x` has a power `ι x ^ p ^ e` in `C` (§6.3).

The extended-ideal identity lets a theorem about a commutative ideal acting on a module speak about
powers of the two-sided ideal `UC`. With `Z = Z(U(L))` and `J = C`, regarded as the ideal of `Z` of
elements of augmentation zero (`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.centralAugmentationCenterIdeal`), `UC` is the
extension `J · U(L)` (`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.map_centralAugmentationCenterIdeal`), and for every
commutative ring `R` with an algebra structure on `U(L)` (so acting through the centre) and every
ideal `J` of `R`, `J^n • U(L) = (J · U(L))^n` as `R`-submodules
(`Ideal.pow_smul_top_eq_restrictScalars_map_pow`, with `Ideal.smul_top_eq_restrictScalars_map` for
`n = 1`). Hence `(UC)^n = J^n • U(L)` for every `n`, with `R = Z`; for any commutative subalgebra `R`
of the centre containing a finite generating set `S ⊆ C` of `UC` and `J` the ideal of `R` generated
by `S`, the same identity holds, `J · U(L)` being the ideal generated by `S`. Centrality is the whole
content; Layer 7 is stated so that this is the only passage between the two sides.
*Needs:* §§1.4, 6.4; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### Examples

- For the one-dimensional abelian `L = K x`, `U(L) = K[x]`, `C = x K[x]`, `UC = (x)`, and
  `c_x = x` is the familiar central relation; `(UC)^n = (x^n)`.
- For the affine-line algebra and the basis `x, y`, the central `p`-polynomials `x^p − x` and
  `y^p` generate `K[x^p − x, y^p]`, a polynomial ring in two variables, and `U(L)` is free over it
  on the `p²` monomials `x^a y^b` with `a, b < p`: the leading terms of the products
  `(x^p − x)^i (y^p)^j x^a y^b` are the ordered monomials, each once.

### Dependencies

Layers 1–2. Mathlib: centres of algebras, Noetherian rings, finite modules, extended ideals. Tau
Ceti: the Frobenius identity, central `p`-polynomials, module-finiteness over the centre and the
central augmentation ideal named above.

## Layer 7: the generalized Krull intersection and finite quotients

Throughout, `K` has prime characteristic `p` and `L` is finite-dimensional (Hochschild 1966).

### 7.1 The Krull intersection theorem

The commutative input is Mathlib's generalized Krull intersection theorem
`Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff`: for `R` commutative Noetherian, `M` a finite `R`-module and
`J` an ideal of `R`, `x ∈ ⨅ i, J ^ i • ⊤` iff `r • x = x` for some `r ∈ J` (Hochschild cites
Zariski–Samuel, p. 253, for it). It is not restated. For `R` Noetherian and an `R`-algebra `A`
finite over `R` it reads: `x ∈ ⨅ n, (J · A)^n` iff `r x = x` for some `r ∈ J` (`Ideal.mem_iInf_map_algebraMap_pow_iff`),
and for a Hopf algebra finite over its Noetherian centre, with `UC` in place of `J · A`
(`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.mem_iInf_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow_iff`). The hypotheses are discharged for
`U(L)` over its centre by §6.4, and every commutative ideal in the argument lives in the centre.
*Needs:* §§6.4–6.5; Mathlib `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff`; Tau Ceti the declarations named
above.

**Checks.**
- The domain hypothesis of §7.2 is needed: in `R = A = K × K` with the idempotent `r = (1, 0)` and
  `J = (r)`, every power of `J` is `J`, and `⋂ Jⁿ = J ≠ 0`; the criterion is met by `x = r`, with
  `(1 − r) r = 0`.

### 7.2 The domain corollary

An element of `⋂ (UC)^n` satisfies `(1 − z) x = 0` for some central `z` of augmentation zero; since
`U(L)` has no zero divisors (§1.4) and `ε(1 − z) = 1`, `1 − z ≠ 0`, so `x = 0`:
`⋂ₙ (UC)^n = 0` (`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.iInf_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow_eq_bot`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.iInf_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow_eq_bot`; in the commutative
form, `Ideal.iInf_map_algebraMap_pow_eq_bot`).
*Needs:* §§1.4, 7.1; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 7.3 Separating `L` at a finite stage

The subspaces `L ∩ (UC)^n` decrease and have zero intersection; `L` being finite-dimensional, one
of them is zero (`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_forall_ι_eq_zero_of_mem_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow`).
The stabilization is a reusable lemma: an Artinian submodule `W` of any module meets some member of
an antitone family of submodules with zero infimum in `0`, and then every later member
(`Submodule.exists_forall_inf_eq_bot_of_antitone`; for the powers of an ideal of an algebra,
`Ideal.exists_forall_inf_restrictScalars_pow_eq_bot`); nothing is assumed of the ambient module.
*Needs:* §7.2; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 7.4 The finite-dimensional quotient

For every `n`, `U(L) ⧸ (UC)^n` is finite-dimensional over `K`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.finiteDimensional_quotient_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow`) and
nonzero for `n ≠ 0` (`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.nontrivial_quotient_centralAugmentationIdeal_pow`): it is
finite over `R₀ ⧸ (c₁, …, c_d)^n`, since each `cᵢ` lies in `UC` (`TauCeti.finite_quotient_pow_of_algebraMap_mem`).

Prove the explicit finite spanning set: with `c₁, …, c_d` and the exponents `e₁, …, e_d` of §6.4
for the basis `x₁, …, x_d`, the images in `U(L) ⧸ (UC)^n` of the products
`c₁^β₁ ⋯ c_d^β_d · ι x₁^m₁ ⋯ ι x_d^m_d` with `β₁ + ⋯ + β_d < n` and `0 ≤ mᵢ < p ^ eᵢ` span it over
`K`. *Route.* The bounded monomials span `U(L)` over `R₀` (§1.5), and `R₀ ⧸ (c₁, …, c_d)^n` is
spanned by the monomials in the `cᵢ` of total degree `< n`.
*Needs:* §§1.5, 6.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.span_range_bounded_orderedPowerProducts_eq_top`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_pCentralGenerators_moduleFinite`.

**Checks.**
- For the one-dimensional abelian `L = K x`, `c = x` with `e = 0`, and the spanning set is
  `1, x, …, x^(n−1)`, of size `n = dim K[x] ⧸ (x^n)`: it is a basis. For `n = 2` the quotient is the
  square-zero algebra and `L ∩ (UC)^2 = 0`.

### Examples

- For the affine-line algebra in characteristic `p`, `UC` contains `x^p − x` and `y^p`, and
  `U(L) ⧸ (UC)^n` is spanned by the `(x^p − x)^i (y^p)^j x^a y^b` with `i + j < n` and `a, b < p`.

### Dependencies

Layers 1, 6. Mathlib: the generalized Krull intersection theorem, Artinian modules. Tau Ceti: the
Krull intersection for extended and central augmentation ideals, the filtered-intersection lemma and
the finite quotient named above.

## Layer 8: assemble the positive-characteristic theorem

Throughout, `K` has prime characteristic `p` and `L` is finite-dimensional (Hochschild 1966).

### 8.1 The separating quotient and its representation

Choose `n` with `L ∩ (UC)^n = 0` (§7.3) and let `A = U(L) ⧸ (UC)^n`, finite-dimensional (§7.4). The
quotient map is injective on the canonical copy of `L`, and every `ad`-nilpotent `x` becomes
nilpotent in `A`: some `ι x ^ p ^ e` lies in `C ⊆ UC` (§6.3), so `ι x ^ (n p ^ e) ∈ (UC)^n`
(`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isNilpotent_quotient_ι_of_isNilpotent_ad`, for every `n`,
and for the left-regular representation
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isNilpotent_quotient_leftRegularRep_of_isNilpotent_ad`). Packaged:
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_algHom_eq_zero_iff_ι_eq_zero` (the algebra target) and
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.exists_representation_eq_zero_iff_ι_eq_zero` (its left-regular
representation, whose kernel is the kernel of `ι`), stated for `U(L)` without zero divisors, which
§1.4 supplies.
*Needs:* §§0.2, 1.4, 6.3, 7.3–7.4; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 8.2 Hochschild's theorem and Ado's theorem in characteristic `p`

With `ι` injective (§1.1), the representation of §8.1 is faithful and satisfies Hochschild's
condition: `TauCeti.exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charP`, from which Ado's theorem in
characteristic `p` is read off (`TauCeti.adoCharP`). The statements mention no basis and no central
generating family: the quotient depends on those choices, its guarantees do not.
*Needs:* §§1.1, 8.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence_charP`,
`TauCeti.adoCharP`.

**Checks.**
- In characteristic `p`, Hochschild's condition is strictly stronger than the nilrepresentation
  condition for a given representation. For `p ≥ 3`, `sl₂` over `𝔽_p` is simple, so `nil = 0` and
  every representation is a nilrepresentation; but on `𝔽_p^p` with basis `v₀, …, v_(p−1)`, the
  operators `e vᵢ = v_(i+1)` (indices mod `p`), `h vᵢ = 2 i vᵢ` and `f vᵢ = −i (i − 1) v_(i−1)`
  satisfy the relations of `sl₂`, and `e^p` is the identity, so the `ad`-nilpotent `e` does not act
  nilpotently. (Checked for `p = 3, 5, 7`.) The representation of §8.1 excludes this.
- The theorem applies over every finite field, `𝔽₂` included, and its statement carries no
  `CharZero`, `IsAlgClosed` or bound on `p`.

### Examples

- For the one-dimensional abelian `L` over `𝔽_p`, `n = 2` gives `A = K[x] ⧸ (x²)` and the
  two-dimensional square-zero representation.

### Dependencies

## Layer 9: the arbitrary-field summit and consumers

### 9.1 Characteristic dispatch and Hochschild's theorem

For a field, `ringChar K` is `0` or a prime (Mathlib `CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero`); in the first case
`CharZero K` holds (`CharP.ringChar_zero_iff_CharZero`) and §5.8 applies, in the second
`CharP K (ringChar K)` with a prime characteristic and §8.2 applies. The dispatch is on the
strengthened statements, so the summit is not proved twice, and it is instance plumbing kept apart
from the mathematics. The result is Hochschild's theorem over an arbitrary field: a faithful
finite-dimensional representation satisfying Hochschild's condition
(`TauCeti.exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence`; Hochschild 1966).
*Needs:* §§5.8, 8.2; Mathlib `ringChar`, `CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero`,
`CharP.ringChar_zero_iff_CharZero`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_faithful_preserving_ad_nilpotence`.

### 9.2 The Ado–Iwasawa theorem and the nilpotent case

Over an arbitrary field: a faithful finite-dimensional nilrepresentation
(`TauCeti.exists_faithful_nilrepresentation`), the Ado–Iwasawa theorem `TauCeti.adoIwasawa` (Iwasawa
1948), and for `L` nilpotent a faithful representation by nilpotent endomorphisms
(`TauCeti.exists_faithful_nilrepresentation_of_isNilpotent`). The hypotheses are `[Field K]`, the Lie
structures and `[FiniteDimensional K L]` only.
*Needs:* §§0.5, 9.1; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 9.3 The matrix form

A faithful representation on `V` with a basis of `V` gives an injective Lie homomorphism
`L →ₗ⁅K⁆ Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) K`, and conversely a matrix algebra acts on the coordinate space; the
two formulations are equivalent for every Lie algebra
(`TauCeti.faithfulRepresentation_iff_exists_injective_lieHom_matrix`). Hence every finite-dimensional
Lie algebra embeds in some `𝔤𝔩_n(K)` (`TauCeti.exists_injective_lieHom_matrix`) and is isomorphic to a
Lie subalgebra of `Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) K` (`TauCeti.exists_lieSubalgebra_matrix_nonempty_equiv`).
*Needs:* §9.2; Mathlib `LinearMap.toMatrix`, `Module.finBasis`; Tau Ceti the declarations named above.

### 9.4 The real specialization

At `K = ℝ`, `TauCeti.exists_injective_lieHom_matrix` is the embedding `L ↪ 𝔤𝔩_n(ℝ)` of a
finite-dimensional real Lie algebra in the form LieGroups Layer 4 consumes for Lie's third theorem.
Nothing of manifold theory enters this roadmap.
*Needs:* §9.3.

### 9.5 Residual finite-dimensionality in degree one

The canonical copy of `L` in `U(L)` is separated by a finite-dimensional algebra quotient of `U(L)`
(`TauCeti.exists_finiteEnvelopingTarget`, the Ado–Iwasawa theorem read through §0.3). This is a
statement about degree one only: no statement here asserts that every element of `U(L)` survives in
some finite-dimensional quotient.
*Needs:* §§0.3, 9.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_finiteEnvelopingTarget`.

### Examples

- The affine-line algebra embeds in `𝔤𝔩₂(K)` by §0's example, and the Heisenberg algebra in the
  strictly upper triangular `3 × 3` matrices, by `x ↦ E₁₂`, `y ↦ E₂₃`, `z ↦ E₁₃`, a faithful
  representation by nilpotent matrices.

## Provenance

This section is secondary; it maps targets to an existing formalization and prescribes nothing. The
Etingof representation-theory formalization states the arbitrary-field theorem and proves that it is
equivalent to a finite-dimensional associative target of `U(L)` that remains injective on `L`, in
four reductions matching §§0.2–0.3, with a typechecked statement of the theorem and deliberately no
proof of it; see
[`Remark2_9_3.lean`](https://github.com/FormalFrontier/Etingof-RepresentationTheory-draft1/blob/master/EtingofRepresentationTheory/Chapter2/Remark2_9_3.lean).
Those reductions inform the Layer 0 interface without prescribing names or proof structure; they are
design evidence, not a proof source to copy.

  *nilrepresentation* and of Layer 4's hypotheses (Proposition E.5).
