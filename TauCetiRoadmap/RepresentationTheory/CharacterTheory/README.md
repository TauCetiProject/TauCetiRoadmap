# Roadmap: character theory of finite groups, and computing character tables

**Summary.** The character of a finite-dimensional representation of a finite group `G` sends
`g` to the trace of the map by which `g` acts. Characters are constant on conjugacy classes, and
in characteristic zero a representation is determined up to equivalence by its character. Over an
algebraically closed field whose characteristic does not divide `|G|`, the irreducible characters
form a basis of the functions constant on classes. So they are as many as the conjugacy classes,
and their values form a square matrix, the character table.

This roadmap develops that theory and the exact computation of the table. `G` is a finite group
throughout. The general theory is stated over a field `k` that is algebraically closed with
`char k ∤ |G|`, the table itself over `ℂ`, and arithmetic in the cyclotomic field `ℚ(ζ_e)`, where
`e` is the exponent of `G`. Much of the classical theory is already in Tau Ceti's
character-table library; the roadmap adds theorems it does not yet contain.

The first main theorem is the Burnside–Brauer theorem. Let `U` be a faithful finite-dimensional
complex representation (only the identity acts trivially), and let `r` be the number of distinct
values of its character. Then every irreducible representation of `G` is a summand of some
tensor power `U^⊗n` with `0 ≤ n < r`, where `U^⊗0` is trivial. For the two-dimensional
irreducible `U` of `S₃` the values are `2, 0, −1`, so `r = 3`, and the sign representation first
appears in `U^⊗2 = 1 ⊕ sgn ⊕ U`. These multiplicities have an explicit generating series: for any
`U` and `X`, `∑ₙ dim Hom_G(X, U^⊗n) zⁿ = |G|⁻¹ ∑_g χ_X(g⁻¹) (1 − χ_U(g) z)⁻¹` as formal power
series. When `U` is faithful of positive dimension and `X` is irreducible, this series is not a
polynomial, so `X` occurs in arbitrarily high tensor powers of `U`.

The second describes the field `ℚ(χ)` generated by the values of an irreducible complex character
`χ`. Embed `K = ℚ(ζ_e)` in `ℂ` in any way. An automorphism `σ` of `K` with `σ(ζ_e) = ζ_e^j` acts
on characters by `(σ·χ)(g) = χ(g^j)`, and `ℚ(χ)` is the image in `ℂ` of the subfield of `K` fixed
by the stabilizer of `χ`. For the sign character of `S₃`, `e = 6` and every `σ` fixes it, so
`ℚ(χ) = ℚ` although `K = ℚ(√−3)`.

The third concerns computation. In the group algebra the class sums multiply with natural-number
structure constants. On an irreducible representation with character `χ`, the sum of a class `C`
acts by the scalar `|C| χ(g)/χ(1)` for `g ∈ C`, its central character value. By the
Dixon–Schneider theorem, the structure constants and the integrality of character values
determine the table up to row order. Tau Ceti already has an executable solver that works modulo
a prime and lifts residues to exact elements of `ℤ[ζ_e]`. Any table it returns is the character
table up to row order, but its completeness theorem assumes a coordinate bound on an exact
certificate. Let `A_e` be the largest absolute value of a coordinate of a power of `ζ_e` in the
power basis, and at least `1`. The roadmap proves that every central character value lies in
`ℤ[ζ_e]` with coordinates at most `B_G = |G|·A_e` in absolute value. Hence the solver succeeds at
every prime `p > 2B_G` meeting its existing conditions, such primes exist, and its prime search
terminates. For `S₄`, `B_G = 24`; the solver's own condition `2⌊√|G|⌋ < p` admits `p = 13`, too
small to recover the central value `8` from its residue.

Besides Mathlib and that library, the roadmap builds on SemisimpleAlgebras, for Schur's lemma and
isotypic components. From InductionRestriction it takes induced characters, used for Frobenius
groups and `GL₂(𝔽_q)`, and the Mackey criterion, used for `GL₂(𝔽_q)`. For `GL_n(𝔽_q)` it takes the
Harish-Chandra, Gelfand–Graev and Deligne–Lusztig theory of finite reductive groups from ReductiveGroups,
symmetric functions from SchurWeyl, Iwahori–Hecke algebras of type A from TypeAHeckeAlgebras, and the
classification of matrices up to similarity from ClassicalArithmeticCompletion. McKaySkewGroup uses the
Burnside–Brauer theorem for the connectedness of the McKay graph.

Layers 0–4 develop ordinary character theory and contain the first two theorems. Layer 5 records
the Dixon–Schneider characterization, and Layer 6 proves the third theorem. Layers 7–9 tie the
library's Frobenius–Schur indicator, Frobenius groups and `GL₂(𝔽_q)` to this roadmap's table and
pairing. Layer 9a extends Layer 9 to `GL_n(𝔽_q)`: Green's classification of its irreducible characters,
with the derivatives, Hecke algebras, Gauss sums and Shintani descent around it. Layers 10 and 11 work
out examples, up to the normal modes of a tetrahedron of springs.

Suggested home: `TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/CharacterTable/`, mirroring Mathlib's
`RepresentationTheory/`.

## How to read the build

Layers 0 and 1 are supplier layers: they fix the names and conventions of the class-function and
class-algebra objects this roadmap consumes and add the two executable adapters it needs. Layer 2
fixes the native irreducible enumeration and finite models, Layer 3 builds multiplicity theory,
regrouping, the characteristic-positive congruence, product groups, the Burnside–Brauer theorem and the generating series; Layer 4 the
arithmetic of values and the field of values. Layer 5 records the specification the solver is
proved against. Layer 6 is the summit of strand C: the exact-coordinate bound and the completeness
and termination of the pinned solver. Layers 7–9 are independent applications built on Layer 3 and
the pinned library; Layer 9a builds on Layers 0–3 and 9 and on the finite reductive groups of
ReductiveGroups; Layers 10 and 11 are worked families on Layers 0–3. `README.md` is normative;
`Suggested.lean` states the headline theorems (the two solver theorems, Burnside–Brauer and its
generating series, cross-universe regrouping, product classification, the field of values, the exact-ring comparisons and bounds, the
arbitrary-universe principal-series criterion, and the quartic transverse energy) against the
pinned APIs with the few definitions they need, and nothing else.

## Conventions

- **The group.** `G` is a finite group throughout (`[Group G] [Finite G]`, and `[Fintype G]`
  `[DecidableEq G]` for anything computed). Write `Nat.card G` for its order and `Monoid.exponent G`
  for its exponent `e`; `e ≠ 0` for a finite group (`Monoid.neZero_exponent_of_finite`).
- **The coefficient field, and the three arenas.** The **theory** (orthogonality, completeness, the
  count, the structure theory) is stated over a field `k` that is **algebraically closed with
  `char k ∤ |G|`**, where Mathlib's `char_orthonormal` and `k[G] ≅ ∏ Matₙᵢ(k)` live. The
  **character table as a concrete object** is over `ℂ`. Arithmetic statements live in the
  cyclotomic field `ℚ(ζ_e)`. Do not bundle "algebraically closed and `char ∤ |G|`" into a class;
  spell the hypotheses each result uses, and state each transfer between the three arenas
  explicitly.
- **Exact arithmetic computes; `ℂ` only specifies.** `Complex` is not a computable type, so nothing
  is evaluated in `ℂ`. Strand C computes in the exact cyclotomic-integer type
  `TauCeti.Cyclotomic e`, coefficient vectors on the power basis of `ℤ[ζ_e] = ℤ[X]/Φ_e`, with a
  definite embedding `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.complexEmbedding : Cyclotomic e →+* ℂ` sending the
  generator `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.zeta e` to the distinguished root `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.complexRoot e`.
  The complex table is the image of the exact table under this embedding. The Galois group that
  acts on character values is `Gal(ℚ(ζ_e)/ℚ)`, through the power maps `σ_j : ζ ↦ ζ^j`
  (`IsPrimitiveRoot.autToPow`); no automorphism of `ℂ` is ever chosen.
- **The group algebra is the spine; `FDRep` is the mirror.** Develop the theory on
  `k[G] = MonoidAlgebra k G`, its modules, `Representation.character` and
  `Representation.IsIrreducible`; transport along `Rep.equivalenceModuleMonoidAlgebra` where the
  categorical statement is cleaner, and keep the `FDRep`/`CategoryTheory.Simple` mirror in step.
  Use Mathlib's vocabulary (`ConjClasses G`, `MonoidAlgebra`, `IsIntegral`, `IsPrimitiveRoot`,
  `CyclotomicField`, `Monoid.exponent`), never a private synonym.
- **The pairing is bilinear; Hermitian is a `ℂ`-only refinement.** The pairing
  `⟨f₁, f₂⟩ = (Nat.card G)⁻¹ • ∑ g, f₁ g * f₂ g⁻¹` on class functions
  (`TauCeti.ClassFunction.characterPairing`) is bilinear and symmetric over a general field and
  not Hermitian there. Over `ℂ`, and only for virtual characters, `f₂ g⁻¹ = conj (f₂ g)`, so it
  agrees with the conjugate-linear inner product; that is a separate `ℂ`-lemma, and "Hermitian"
  is reserved for it. The orientation used for tensor powers is
  `⟨χ_{U^⊗n}, χ_X⟩ = finrank ℂ (X ⟶ U^⊗n)`.
- **Central characters versus character values.** The solver's primary object is the
  **central-character table** `Ω`, `Ω_{χ,j} = ω_χ(K_j)` (values on class sums), not the ordinary
  table `X`, `X_{i,j} = χᵢ(g_j)`; they are related by `ω_χ(K_j) = |C_j| · χ(g_j) / χ(1)`, both are
  named, and the ordinary table is a conversion from `Ω`.
- **Computable means compiler-executable.** "Compute" means a definition the compiler executes on
  `[DecidableEq G] [Fintype G]` data. Kernel reduction is not required and not available: the
  pinned `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.lift` is executable but does not reduce in the kernel, because
  `ZMod.inv` is `Nat.gcdA`, defined by well-founded recursion; facts about it are proved through
  its specification lemmas, never by `decide`. Mathlib's noncomputable character API is the
  specification the solver is proved against, not a step in it.
- **Left eigenrows.** Class-multiplication matrices follow Tau Ceti's convention
  `(M_i)_{j,k} = a_{i,k,j}`, acting on row vectors from the left (`Matrix.vecMul`, `ᵥ*`); a left
  eigenspace is the right kernel of `M_iᵀ − λI`, never of `M_i − λI`. The witness is in Layer 5.

## Layer 0: class functions and their pairing

### 0.1 Class functions

`TauCeti.ClassFunction k G` is the submodule of `G → k` of functions constant on conjugacy classes;
every `Representation.character ρ` and `FDRep.character V` is a member, by `char_conj`.
`TauCeti.ClassFunction.equivConjClasses : ClassFunction k G ≃ₗ[k] (ConjClasses G → k)` makes the
character table a square matrix indexed by classes, and
`TauCeti.ClassFunction.finrank_eq_card_conjClasses` gives `finrank k (ClassFunction k G) = Nat.card (ConjClasses G)` for finite `G`. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ClassFunction` and the two
declarations named.

### 0.2 The pairing

`TauCeti.ClassFunction.characterPairing f₁ f₂ = (Nat.card G)⁻¹ • ∑ g, f₁ g * f₂ g⁻¹` is bilinear,
symmetric and nondegenerate when `Nat.card G` is invertible in `k`
(`TauCeti.ClassFunction.characterPairing_nondegenerate`); its value is the raw sum of Mathlib's
`char_orthonormal` (`TauCeti.ClassFunction.characterPairing_apply`), so `char_orthonormal` reads
`⟨χᵢ, χⱼ⟩ = δᵢⱼ` on irreducibles. Over `ℂ`, virtual-character inversion gives
`f g⁻¹ = conj (f g)` by `TauCeti.conj_apply_of_mem_virtualCharacters`, so the pairing of two
virtual characters is `(Nat.card G)⁻¹ ∑ g, f₁ g * conj (f₂ g)`.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.conj_apply_of_mem_virtualCharacters` and the pairing declarations above.

**Checks.**
- Nondegeneracy needs the invertibility hypothesis: for `k = 𝔽₂` and `G = C₂`, the normalization
  `(Nat.card G)⁻¹` is `0`, the pairing vanishes identically, and "nondegenerate" would assert that
  every class function is zero.
- For `G = C₃` and the character `χ(g) = ω`, `⟨χ, χ⟩ = (1 + ω·ω² + ω²·ω)/3 = 1` with the `g⁻¹`
  convention, whereas the model "`(Nat.card G)⁻¹ ∑ f₁ g * f₂ g`" gives `(1 + ω² + ω)/3 = 0`.
- For `S₃` the pairing of the two-dimensional character `(2, 0, −1)` with the trivial character is
  `(2 + 3·0 + 2·(−1))/6 = 0`, and with itself `(4 + 0 + 2)/6 = 1`.

### Examples

The orthonormality of the rows of the `S₃` table `(1,1,1)`, `(1,−1,1)`, `(2,0,−1)` under the
class-size weighted pairing, and the failure of the unweighted sum `∑_C f₁(C) f₂(C⁻¹)` to be
orthonormal on the same rows.

### Dependencies

Mathlib `Representation.character`, `char_conj`, `FDRep.char_orthonormal`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ClassFunction` and its pairing API.

## Layer 1: the group algebra, its center, and structure constants

### 1.1 Class sums and the center

`TauCeti.classSum k C = ∑_{g ∈ C} g ∈ k[G]` for `C : ConjClasses G`; it lies in the center
(`TauCeti.classSum_mem_center`), the class sums are a basis of `Z(k[G])`, and
`finrank k (Z(k[G])) = Nat.card (ConjClasses G)` (`TauCeti.finrank_center_monoidAlgebra`); the
center is commutative. The structure constants `TauCeti.structureConstant Cᵢ Cⱼ Cₖ = #{(x, y) : x ∈ Cᵢ, y ∈ Cⱼ, x * y = gₖ}` for a fixed `gₖ ∈ Cₖ` are representative-independent natural
numbers with `classSum Cᵢ * classSum Cⱼ = ∑ₖ aᵢⱼₖ • classSum Cₖ` (`TauCeti.classSum_mul`,
`TauCeti.classSumCenter_mul`); they are the entire input to Dixon–Schneider. Each class sum is
integral over `ℤ` as an element of `Z(ℤ[G])` (`TauCeti.isIntegral_classSum`,
`TauCeti.isIntegral_classSumCenter`), the base of central-character integrality; this is the
statement in the integral class center, the `ℤ`-subalgebra of `Z(ℤ[G])` spanned by the class sums,
not in `ℤ[G]` itself, where it is trivial. *Needs:* the named Tau Ceti declarations.

### 1.2 Executable class sums

Two adapters are targets of this layer, on `[Fintype G] [DecidableEq G]` data `d : TauCeti.ClassData G`:

- the class sum of the numbered class `i : Fin d.numClasses` as the finite sum
  `∑_{g ∈ d.classFinset i} of k G g` in `k[G]`, equal to `TauCeti.classSum k (d.classOf i)`;
- its coefficient function `g ↦ if d.index g = i then 1 else 0`, computable, equal to the
  coefficient of the group-algebra element at `g` (by `TauCeti.classSum_coeff`).

The group-algebra additive instance is noncomputable; executable class constants and matrices use
the numbered finite data. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ClassData`, `TauCeti.classSum_coeff`.

**Checks.**
- For `G = S₃`, class of transpositions `C` with `|C| = 3`: `a_{C,C,1} = 3` (three ordered pairs
  `(t, t)`), `a_{C,C,C} = 0`, and `a_{C,C,T} = 3` for `T` the class of `3`-cycles; a definition counting
  unordered pairs would give `a_{C,C,1} = 3` but `a_{C,C,T} = 3/2`.
- For the trivial group the center is `k`, with one class sum `1` and `a_{1,1,1} = 1`.
- The coefficient function of the numbered class sum at `g ∉ C_i` is `0` and at `g ∈ C_i` is `1`,
  for every numbering `d` of the same group.

### Examples

`S₃` with its three class sums and the multiplication table of its class algebra;
`DihedralGroup 4` with five classes.

### Dependencies

Mathlib `MonoidAlgebra`, `ConjClasses`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.classSum`, `TauCeti.structureConstant`,
`TauCeti.ClassData`.

## Layer 2: Wedderburn, the irreducible index, and the count

### 2.1 Wedderburn and the sum of squares

For `k` algebraically closed with `Nat.card G` invertible, `TauCeti.exists_algEquiv_pi_matrix`
gives a noncomputable `k[G] ≃ₐ[k] ∏ᵢ Matₙᵢ(k)` over a finite index with every `nᵢ > 0`, and
`TauCeti.sum_sq_eq_card_of_algEquiv_pi_matrix` gives `∑ᵢ nᵢ² = |G|` (from `finrank k k[G] = |G|`).
This layer consumes Mathlib's Artin–Wedderburn directly; what SemisimpleAlgebras supplies is the
block/simple-module dictionary `blocks_equiv_simpleModules` of its Layer 2, which §2.2 may consume
instead of reproving. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsSemisimpleRing.exists_algEquiv_pi_matrix_of_isAlgClosed`,
Maschke; Tau Ceti as named.

### 2.2 The native irreducible enumeration and finite models

Use `TauCeti.finEquivIrreducibleCharacters k G`, indexed by `Fin (Nat.card (ConjClasses G))`,
with degree `TauCeti.characterDegree k i` and model `TauCeti.irreducibleRepresentation k i` on
`Fin (TauCeti.characterDegree k i) → k`. Write `Zᵢ` for that model. A simple module in any
carrier universe is assigned its class through the block classification and its module-class
bijection; the enumeration is chosen, while the isomorphism class is intrinsic. This is the
lookup used to regroup the simple summands in §3.2.
*Needs:* §2.1; `TauCeti.exists_nonempty_equiv_blockRepresentation`,
`TauCeti.nonempty_equiv_index_simpleSubmoduleClasses`,
`TauCeti.ClassFunction.exists_nonempty_equiv`, and `TauCeti.irreducibleRepresentation`.
**Checks.**
- For `G = S₃` and `k = ℂ` the index has three elements with degrees `1, 1, 2`; `∑ nᵢ² = 6`.
- For the trivial group the index is a singleton with `Z = (Fin 1 → k)` and the trivial action.
- The class lookup of `S = Zᵢ.asModule` itself is `i`, and the lookup of `ULift (Zᵢ.asModule)` in a
  higher universe is the same `i`; a lookup defined only for carriers in the universe of `k`
  cannot be applied to the second.

### 2.3 The center splits, and the count

`Z(k[G]) ≃ₐ[k] (index → k)` by central-character evaluation
(`TauCeti.centerMonoidAlgebraAlgEquivPi`): central characters separate points of the center, the
sharp form Layer 5 needs. Combined with Layer 1's center dimension,
`Nat.card (index) = Nat.card (ConjClasses G)`
(`TauCeti.card_eq_card_conjClasses_of_algEquiv_pi_matrix`). *Needs:* §2.1, Layer 1.

**Checks.**
- `S₃`: three classes, three irreducibles; `Q₈` and `D₄`: five each.
- The evaluation map on the center of the trivial group algebra `k` is the identity `k ≃ k`.

### Examples

`S₃`, `D₄`, `Q₈`, `A₄` (four irreducibles of degrees `1, 1, 1, 3`, four classes).

## Layer 3: the character table, multiplicities, and tensor generation

The additivity, multiplicity, decomposition and recognition targets of this layer concern
finite-dimensional representations at arbitrary carrier universes.

### 3.1 Completeness and additivity

The irreducible characters span `ClassFunction k G`
(`TauCeti.ClassFunction.le_span_irreducibleCharacters`); with `char_orthonormal` and the count they
are an orthonormal basis `Basis (index) k (ClassFunction k G)`. Use `Representation.char_prod` and `FDRep.char_biprod` for binary sums. Prove the finite
`Representation.directSum` character identity by induction on the finite index; use
`char_iso` to transport across equivalent presentations. *Needs:* Mathlib `char_iso`; Tau Ceti as
named.

### 3.2 Pairing to Hom, intrinsic multiplicities and cross-universe regrouping

For finite-dimensional `ρ : Representation k G V` and `σ : Representation k G W`, Mathlib's
`Representation.card_inv_mul_sum_char_mul_char_eq_finrank` is the identity
`⟨σ.character, ρ.character⟩ = finrank k (IntertwiningMap ρ σ)`, with only the finite-group and
invertible-order hypotheses; in `FDRep` it is `FDRep.scalar_product_char_eq_finrank_equivariant`.
Own the comparison `finrank k (FDRep.of Zᵢ ⟶ V) = finrank k (IntertwiningMap Zᵢ V.ρ)` through
`Representation.linHom.invariantsEquivFDRepHom` and `Representation.invariantsEquivIntertwiningMap`; do not reprove either pinned
identity.

Under finite `G`, algebraically closed `k` and invertible `Nat.card G`, define the **intrinsic
multiplicity** `intrinsicMultiplicity`, written `m_ρ(i) := finrank k (IntertwiningMap Zᵢ ρ)` for finite-dimensional `ρ`, with `Zᵢ`
the finite model of §2.2; the pairing identity gives `⟨ρ.character, Zᵢ.character⟩ = m_ρ(i)`.

Prove the cross-universe regrouping: Maschke makes `k[G]` semisimple and finite-dimensionality
makes `ρ.asModule` finitely generated, so `IsSemisimpleModule.exists_linearEquiv_fin_dfinsupp`
decomposes it into finitely many simple submodules `Sⱼ`, possibly in a universe other than that of
`k`; assign each its class by §2.2; identify intertwiners from `Zᵢ` with `k[G]`-linear maps
(`IntertwiningMap.equivLinearMapAsModule`); `LinearMap.bijective_or_eq_zero` makes the Hom space
zero when the classes differ, and when they agree a chosen equivalence identifies it with
`Module.End k[G] Zᵢ.asModule`, a finite-dimensional division `k`-algebra, which SemisimpleAlgebras
Layer 1 collapses to `k`. Package the two cases as the cross-universe Hom-dimension formula, and
prove `nonempty_regrouping`: the number of summands in class `i` is `m_ρ(i)`, by additivity of
equivariant Hom over the finite sum, so `ρ.asModule` is a finite direct sum of exactly `m_ρ(i)`
copies of `Zᵢ.asModule`, expressed as `Π₀ i, Fin (m_ρ(i)) → Zᵢ.asModule`.
This is the finite-model regrouping refinement of the canonical isotypic decomposition
(Fulton–Harris, §2.4, formula (2.31), p. 23; multiplicities: Corollary 2.16, p. 16). *Needs:* §2.2; Mathlib `IsSemisimpleModule.exists_linearEquiv_fin_dfinsupp`,
`LinearMap.bijective_or_eq_zero`, `IntertwiningMap.equivLinearMapAsModule`; SemisimpleAlgebras
Layer 1.

- Multiplicity is invariant under equivalences of representations and under transport of the carrier.
- It is additive on finite direct sums, is `1` on its own simple model and `0` on other models.
- The regrouping equivalence has an inverse and is `k[G]`-linear; its existence is intrinsic,
  although choosing bases of multiplicity spaces is needed to choose an equivalence.

**Checks.**
- For `ρ` the regular representation of `S₃`, `m_ρ(i) = degree i`, giving `1, 1, 2`.
- For `ρ = Zⱼ`, `m_ρ(i) = 1` when `i = j` and `0` otherwise; a model using `finrank k (IntertwiningMap ρ Zᵢ)` with the arguments reversed gives the same dimensions in any finite semisimple category, even over a nonsplit field,
  and cannot fix orientation: for the trivial group over `ℝ`, both `Hom(ℝ, ℝ²)` and
  `Hom(ℝ², ℝ)` have dimension `2`. The source and target in the definition fix orientation.
- For the trivial group and `ρ` on `Fin 3 → k`, `m_ρ = 3`; on the zero module it is `0`,
  and every summand of the regrouped module is zero.

### 3.3 The finite-sum comparison

For a finite family of finite-dimensional representations `ρᵢ`, construct the `k[G]`-linear
comparison from `(Representation.directSum ρ).asModule` to `Π₀ i, ρᵢ.asModule`, sending a vector
in summand `i` to the same vector in the `i`-th component. Transport the regrouping of §3.2
through `TauCeti.Representation.equivOfAsModuleLinearEquiv` to representations. For a simple
submodule use `Subrepresentation.subrepresentationSubmoduleOrderIso`; for finite-dimensional
summands use `FDRep.of` and `Rep.equivalenceModuleMonoidAlgebra` for the categorical mirror.
This comparison is induced by the direct-sum universal property (Fulton–Harris, §3.4,
pp. 36–37, the equivalence of group representations and group-algebra modules).
*Needs:* §3.2; Mathlib `Representation.directSum`, `Representation.asModuleEquiv`,
`Subrepresentation.subrepresentationSubmoduleOrderIso`; Tau Ceti's module-equivalence adapter.

- The forward map and inverse send each supported vector to itself; their composites are identities.
- The comparison intertwines the `k[G]`-action and is natural for a family of intertwining maps.
- It commutes with reindexing the family, including the empty and singleton families.

**Checks.**
- An empty family gives the zero module on both sides, with dimension `0`.
- One trivial summand on `k²` sends `(1,0)` to `(1,0)` in both directions; a zero map fails.
- For `C₂` over `ℂ`, trivial and sign summands send `(2,3)` to `(2,3)`, and the generator sends
  this to `(2,−3)` on both sides; swapping the summands without reindexing gives `(−2,3)`.

### 3.4 Multiplicity API and congruence of characters

The intrinsic multiplicities obey `m_{ρ.prod σ}(i) = m_ρ(i) + m_σ(i)`, `m_{Zⱼ}(i) = δᵢⱼ`, and
`finrank k V = ∑ᵢ m_ρ(i) * degree i`; with §3.1 and the regrouping,
`ρ.character = ∑ᵢ (m_ρ(i) : k) • χᵢ`. For `[CharP k p]` with `0 < p`, `p ∤ |G|` and `k`
algebraically closed, the character basis gives the sharp criterion
`ρ.character = σ.character ↔ ∀ i, Nat.ModEq p (m_ρ(i)) (m_σ(i))`. This is a deduction from the character-basis expansion: equality of coordinates means equality
of the natural-number casts in `k`, equivalent to congruence modulo `p`. The inputs are
`TauCeti.ClassFunction.basisOfIrreducibleCharacters` and Mathlib `CharP.cast_eq_iff_mod_eq`;
the characteristic-zero analogue is Fulton–Harris, Corollaries 2.14–2.16, p. 16, not a source
claim about characteristic `p`. *Needs:* §3.1–3.3 and these two pinned declarations.

**Checks.**
- For the trivial group over `k` of characteristic `p`, one trivial copy and `p + 1` trivial copies
  have the same character and different dimensions, so recognition fails in characteristic `p` and
  the congruence criterion is sharp.

### 3.6 External tensor products and irreducibles of product groups

transport of intertwiners and equivalences, and the associativity, factor-swap, unit, dual and
finite-direct-sum comparisons. Keep it distinct from `Representation.prod` (a direct sum) and from

characteristic-zero `k`. Prove `χ_{ρ ⊠ σ}(g, h) = χ_ρ(g) χ_σ(h)` and the factorization of the pairing of Layer 0; for

### 3.7 Faithful tensor generation over ℂ (Burnside–Brauer)

For every finite `G` and `U : FDRep ℂ G`, define `characterValues U : Finset ℂ`, the set
`S_U = {χ_U(g) | g ∈ G}`, and `characterValueCount U := |S_U|`, written `r_U`. API: membership
and image, the description as the image of `TauCeti.ClassFunction.toConjClasses (ClassFunction.ofFDRep U)` on `ConjClasses G`, `0 < r_U ≤ Nat.card (ConjClasses G)`, invariance
under isomorphism of representations and under isomorphism of groups with transport of the action.
No choice of class representatives or irreducible enumeration enters.

With `Lagrange.basis` define `P_U(T) = ∏_{z ∈ S_U, z ≠ χ_U(1)} (T − z)/(χ_U(1) − z)`: its degree is
`r_U − 1`, it is `1` at `χ_U(1)` and `0` at the other nodes, so
`P_U(χ_U(g)) = if χ_U(g) = χ_U(1) then 1 else 0`; distinct nodes make the denominators nonzero and
a zero node causes no exception; give the coefficient expansion in powers `0, …, r_U − 1` including
the constant term.

If `U.ρ` is injective, `χ_U(g) = finrank ℂ U.V ↔ g = 1`: this is the pinned finite-order trace
criterion `Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff` with `FDRep.ker_eq_bot_iff`, whose proof
diagonalizes the finite-order operator and uses its unit-modulus eigenvalues (an arbitrary matrix
of trace `dim U` need not be the identity). So `P_U ∘ χ_U` is the indicator of `1`, and for a simple
`X : FDRep ℂ G`, bilinearity gives `⟨P_U ∘ χ_U, χ_X⟩ = χ_X(1)/|G| = dim X/|G| ≠ 0`. Own the
coefficient/pairing interchange `∑_{n < r_U} coeff(P_U, n) ⟨χ_U^n, χ_X⟩`, consume
`Representation.tensorPower` with its zero-fold action and `char_tensorPower`, and the pairing-to-Hom
identity in the orientation `⟨χ_{U^⊗n}, χ_X⟩ = finrank ℂ (X ⟶ U^⊗n)`. Over `ℂ` the order is
invertible and a simple object has positive dimension; if all these Hom dimensions vanished the
nonzero pairing would vanish. Hence the theorem `exists_pos_finrank_hom_tensorPower`: for
faithful `U` and simple `X` there is `n < r_U` with `0 < finrank ℂ (X ⟶ tensorPowerFDRep U n)`,
where `tensorPowerFDRep U n = FDRep.of (U.ρ.tensorPower n)`; and its weaker form with
`n < Nat.card (ConjClasses G)` (Isaacs, Theorem 4.3; Steinberg, *On the Burnside–Brauer–Steinberg
theorem*, Introduction). Occurrence is in the actual tensor power, not in a virtual-character
expression; §3.2 identifies the number with the intrinsic multiplicity, giving the
nonzero-intertwiner, injective-intertwiner and direct-summand forms, and the transport
`exists_pos_finrank_intertwining_tensorPower` to representations at arbitrary carrier universes
through `FDRep.ofShrink`. No irreducibility, self-duality, two-dimensionality or unitary embedding
of `U` is assumed.

Give also the grouped Vandermonde route: enumerate the nodes `z_a`, set `b_a = ∑_{χ_U(g) = z_a} χ_X(g⁻¹)`, and use `∑_a b_a z_a^n = 0` for `0 ≤ n < r_U`; the Vandermonde determinant forces all
`b_a = 0`, contradicting the identity fibre `b_{χ_U(1)} = dim X`. This uses the same bilinear
convention, with `χ_X(g⁻¹) = conj(χ_X(g))` and no conjugation of `z_a`. *Needs:* §3.2, §3.5;
Tau Ceti `Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff`, `FDRep.ker_eq_bot_iff`, `Representation.tensorPower`,
`Representation.char_tensorPower`, `FDRep.ofShrink`; Mathlib `Lagrange.basis`,
`Matrix.det_vandermonde`, `FDRep.scalar_product_char_eq_finrank_equivariant`.

**Checks.**

### 3.8 Tensor-multiplicity generating series and positive powers

For `U, X : FDRep ℂ G`, put `m_n = finrank ℂ (X ⟶ U^⊗n)` and define
`tensorMultiplicitySeries U X := PowerSeries.mk (n ↦ m_n) : PowerSeries ℂ`, written `F_X`. The
theorem `tensorMultiplicitySeries_eq_characterFractions` is

`F_X(z) = |G|⁻¹ ∑_{g ∈ G} χ_X(g⁻¹) · (1 − χ_U(g) z)⁻¹`,

each factor inverted as a formal power series with constant coefficient `1`; no analytic
convergence enters. Group the sum by `S_U` with `b_a = ∑_{χ_U(g) = a} χ_X(g⁻¹)`, and in `ℂ[z]`
construct `Q(z) = ∏_{a ∈ S_U} (1 − a z)` and `N_X(z) = |G|⁻¹ ∑_{a ∈ S_U} b_a ∏_{b ≠ a} (1 − b z)`;
prove `Q(0) = 1` and `Q · F_X = N_X` after `Polynomial.toPowerSeries`, with coefficient,
constant-term, reindexing, representation-isomorphism and group-isomorphism comparisons. For
`R(T) = ∏_{a ∈ S_U} (T − a) = ∑_{j ≤ r_U} c_j T^j` prove the recurrence `∑_{j ≤ r_U} c_j m_{n+j} = 0`
for every `n ≥ 0`. A zero character value is a node of `R` and contributes the factor `1` to `Q`;
the zero-dimensional `U` is included.

When `U` is faithful and `d = dim U > 0`, §3.7 gives `b_d = dim X` and `χ_U(g) ≠ d` for `g ≠ 1`, so
for nonzero `X`, `N_X(1/d) = (dim X/|G|) ∏_{a ≠ d} (1 − a/d) ≠ 0` while `Q(1/d) = 0`; hence `F_X`
is not a polynomial (a polynomial identity `Q F_X = N_X` would fail at `1/d`). For simple `X` this
is `arbitrarily_large_tensorPowers`: for every `B` there is `n ≥ B` with `m_n > 0`. The sharp
positive-power bound `exists_positive_tensorPower_le_valueCount`, `1 ≤ n ≤ r_U`, follows by
replacing `P_U(T)` with `(T/d) P_U(T)`, of zero constant term and degree `r_U`; if all positive
coefficients vanished, the constant series would be `m_0 = dim Hom_G(X, 1)`, which is `0` for a
nontrivial simple `X` and `1` for the trivial one. The hypothesis `d > 0` is essential: the zero
representation of the trivial group is faithful and all its positive tensor powers are zero.
*Needs:* §3.7; Mathlib `PowerSeries`, the inverse of a power series with unit constant term,
`Polynomial.toPowerSeries`; Steinberg, Introduction and Remark 1.

## Layer 4: the arithmetic of character values

### 4.1 Diagonalizability, per arena

Over general `k` with `char ∤ |G|`, `ρ g` annihilates the squarefree `X^d − 1` (`d = orderOf g ∣ e`),
so it is semisimple (`Module.End.isSemisimple_of_squarefree_aeval_eq_zero`) with eigenvalues among
the `d`-th roots of unity in `k`. Over `ℂ` those eigenvalues are complex `e`-th roots of unity, so
`χ(g)` is a sum of `χ(1)` roots of unity of modulus one. Own the adapter that a semisimple
endomorphism over `ℂ` with a single eigenvalue is scalar, and the finite-sum equality case of the
triangle inequality for unit complex numbers. *Needs:* Mathlib as named.

### 4.2 Values are cyclotomic integers

Over `ℂ`, exhibit the eigenvalues of `ρ g` as `e`-th roots and lift their sum into
`K = CyclotomicField e ℚ`: for an embedding `ι : K →+* ℂ` carrying a chosen primitive root to a
primitive root of `ℂ`, every complex `e`-th root lies in the image (`IsPrimitiveRoot.map_of_injective`,
`IsPrimitiveRoot.eq_pow_of_pow_eq_one`), and there is a multiset `μ` of exactly `χ(1)` elements of
`K` with `ξ^e = 1`, `χ_K(g) = μ.sum` and `ι(χ_K(g)) = χ(g)`. Mathlib already proves
`Representation.isIntegral_character`; the targets are the lift, its membership in the ring of
integers of `K`, and the embedding-transfer adapter (`isIntegral_algHom_iff` for the injective
`ι.toIntAlgHom`). No cyclotomic-integrality theorem is stated over an arbitrary `k`. *Needs:* §4.1;
Mathlib `CyclotomicField`, `IsPrimitiveRoot.map_of_injective`, `isIntegral_algHom_iff`.

### 4.4 The Galois action and the field of values

For a characteristic-zero field `K` containing a primitive `n`-th root `ζ`, `n ≠ 0`,
`exponent G ∣ n`, and an algebraically closed extension `L` with `K`, `L`, `G` in one universe,
construct the additive equivalence from `TauCeti.virtualCharacters K G` to
`TauCeti.virtualCharacters L G` by scalar extension. Its surjectivity is
`TauCeti.virtualCharacters_eq_map_of_isPrimitiveRoot`; injectivity follows pointwise from the
field embedding. For `K = ℚ(ζ_e)` define the action of `Gal(K/ℚ)` on the latter lattice by
transporting the pointwise action on the former. These are targets of this subsection
(Serre, §§12.3–12.4; the action is the transport of the scalar-extension bijection).

- The equivalence sends `f` to `ι ∘ f`; its inverse is the unique descended function, with both
  inverse laws, and it preserves the integer coefficients of virtual characters.
- The action satisfies `1 · f = f` and `(στ) · f = σ · (τ · f)`; on a descended value it sends
  `ι(f(g))` to `ι(σ(f(g)))`. It is independent of the realization chosen for a character.
- For `j = (hζ.autToPow ℚ σ).val`, prove `(σ · χ)(g) = χ(g^j)`: over `K`, the operator of any
  realization of `g` has eigenvalues among the powers of `ζ`, since its minimal polynomial
  divides the separable polynomial `X^n − 1`. Trace is the sum of these eigenvalues with their
  multiplicities, and `σ` raises each to the `j`-th power. Extend additively to virtual characters.
- Prove preservation of irreducible characters, hence a permutation action on that set: a simple
  `L`-representation has an absolutely irreducible model over `K` by
  `FDRep.exists_simple_nonempty_iso_baseChange_of_isPrimitiveRoot`; twisting its matrices by `σ`
  preserves absolute irreducibility, and scalar extension gives the required irreducible row.

This value calculation uses only automorphisms of `K`; it does not apply the algebraically
closed coefficient-field theorem `TauCeti.Representation.map_ofCharacter_eq_powMap` to `K`.
The resulting action supplies the fixed-field theorem below and the class-power identity of §6.6.
*Needs:* §4.1–4.2; the two pinned descent declarations above; Mathlib
`IsPrimitiveRoot.autToPow`, finite-dimensional eigenvalue decomposition and field embeddings.

The target of this subsection is `fieldOfValues_eq_map_fixedField_stabilizer`: for `G : Type`
finite, `K` with `IsCyclotomicExtension {exponent G} ℚ K` and a field embedding `K → ℂ`, an
irreducible complex character `χ` (a member of `TauCeti.irreducibleCharacters ℂ G`), and the
subgroup `H ≤ Gal(K/ℚ)` of those `σ` with `χ(g ^ j) = χ(g)` for all `g` (`j` the cyclotomic
exponent of `σ` relative to the distinguished primitive root of `K`, so `H` is the stabilizer of
`χ` for the action above), the field `ℚ(χ) = ℚ(χ(g) : g ∈ G) ⊆ ℂ` is the image under the embedding
of the fixed field `K^H` (Isaacs, Chapter 9; Serre, Ch. 12, §§12.3–12.4). Route: by the descent
equivalence `χ = ι ∘ χ_K` for a `K`-valued `χ_K`; the value formula and injectivity of `ι` show

### 4.6 Background: Burnside's `pᵃqᵇ` theorem

recalled for orientation: the central value `|C| χ(g)/χ(1)` and `χ(g)` are integral, Bézout makes

§4.1 makes `ρ g` scalar; for a nonabelian simple group no `g ≠ 1` has a class of prime-power size
(column orthogonality against the identity class, `1 + p·β = 0` with `β` integral, `IsIntegral.ratCast_iff`);
and strong induction on the order with `IsPGroup.center_nontrivial`, `Subgroup.index_centralizer_eq_ncard`,
`Sylow.card_eq_multiplicity`, `IsPGroup.isNilpotent` and `Group.isSolvable_iff_subgroup_quotient`
(Isaacs, Ch. 3; Burnside). This application is not a prerequisite of the solver.

## Layer 5: the Dixon–Schneider characterization (the specification)

This layer records the theorem that the structure constants plus Layer 4's arithmetic determine the
table, and the checker it yields; it is stated over `ℂ`, and strand C computes it. All of it is
pinned; the roadmap fixes the conventions and the witnesses.

### 5.1 The coordinate identity and the class-multiplication matrices

From multiplicativity of `ω_χ` and `Kᵢ Kⱼ = ∑ₖ aᵢⱼₖ Kₖ`: `ω_χ(Kᵢ) ω_χ(Kⱼ) = ∑ₖ aᵢⱼₖ ω_χ(Kₖ)`, the
algebraic fact the eigenvector theorem rests on. `TauCeti.classMultMatrix i` is `(Mᵢ)ⱼₖ = aᵢₖⱼ`,
acting on row vectors from the left (`ᵥ*`); the `{Mᵢ}` commute (`TauCeti.classMultMatrix_commute`),
and the row `v_χ = (ω_χ(K₁), …, ω_χ(K_r))` of `TauCeti.centralCharacterTable ℂ G` satisfies
`v_χ ᵥ* Mᵢ = ω_χ(Kᵢ) v_χ` (`TauCeti.isClassEigenrow_centralCharacterTable`): the rows of `Ω` are
common left eigenvectors. Conversely a vector `v` with `v(1) = 1` is a common left eigenvector of
every `Mᵢ` iff it is the evaluation of an algebra homomorphism `Z(ℂ[G]) → ℂ` on the class sums
(`TauCeti.isClassEigenrow_iff_exists_algHom`): the homomorphisms of the split class algebra are
finite in number, orthonormality forbids repetitions, and the row count exhausts them. The
column/`*ᵥ` convention would need the transposed matrix `(Mᵢ)ⱼₖ = aᵢⱼₖ`; exactly one pairing of
matrix and side is pinned. *Needs:* Layer 1, §2.3, §4.3; Tau Ceti as named.

**Checks (the convention witness).**
- `S₃` with classes `1`, `T` (transpositions, size `3`), `R` (`3`-cycles, size `2`):
  `K_T² = 3K_1 + 3K_R`, `K_T K_R = 2K_T`, `K_R² = 2K_1 + K_R`. In the order `(1, T, R)` the pinned
  `(M_T)_{j,k} = a_{T,k,j}` is `M_T = [[0,3,0],[1,0,2],[0,3,0]]`. The central rows are `(1,3,2)`
  (trivial), `(1,−3,2)` (sign) and `(1,0,−1)` (degree `2`), and `v ᵥ* M_T` is `(3,9,6)`,
  `(−3,9,−6)`, `(0,0,0)`: eigenvalues `3, −3, 0 = ω_χ(K_T)`. The column action `M_T *ᵥ v` sends
  `(1,3,2)` to `(9,5,9)`, not a multiple: the convention is pinned by this example.
- The identity class gives `M_1 = 1`, eigenvalue `1` on every row, which both conventions satisfy;
  it is not a witness.
- The trivial group: one `1 × 1` matrix `[[1]]`.

### 5.2 Normalization, degree recovery, and the specification

Normalize each eigenrow by `ω_χ(K₁) = 1`. Then `d_χ² = |G| / ∑ⱼ |Cⱼ|⁻¹ ω_χ(Kⱼ) ω_χ(Kⱼ⁻¹)` recovers
only the square of the degree, and the checker additionally requires `d_χ ∈ ℕ`, `d_χ > 0`,
`d_χ ∣ |G|` and `∑_χ d_χ² = |G|`, which pin the degree and hence, through
`χ(gⱼ) = d_χ · ω_χ(Kⱼ)/|Cⱼ|`, the ordinary table from `Ω`. `TauCeti.IsCharacterTableSpec G X` over
`ℂ` says: rows are orthonormal class functions of the right count, each normalized row is a common
left eigenvector of the `{Mᵢ}`, and the degrees are positive integers dividing `|G|` with
`∑ dᵢ² = |G|`. `TauCeti.characterTable ℂ G` satisfies it
(`TauCeti.isCharacterTableSpec_characterTable`), and any `X'` satisfying it equals the table up to
a permutation of rows (`TauCeti.characterTable_unique_rows`): "the algorithm produced *a* table"
means "the algorithm produced *the* table". *Needs:* §5.1; Tau Ceti as named.

**Checks.**

  `d = 2`.
- The formula alone admits `d = −2`; the positivity clause excludes it, and the integrality clause
  excludes `d = √2` for the hypothetical row with `∑ = 3`.
- The identity-class normalization fails for a row with `ω(K₁) = 0`: such a row is not a central
  character (every central character has `ω(K₁) = 1`).

## Layer 6: exact reconstruction for the executable solver

The pinned solver `TauCeti.ClassData.dixonCyclotomicCharacterTable?` returns an `Option` of an
exact central table `omega`, ordinary table `table` and positive natural degrees
(`TauCeti.ClassData.CyclotomicCharacterTableData`), and its soundness theorem applies to every
successful result. Its completeness theorem is conditional on a bound on the coefficients of an
exact certificate. This layer supplies that certificate with an explicit bound, hence an explicit
prime threshold at which the solver is complete, and the termination of its prime search
`TauCeti.ClassData.characterTableDixon?`. It replaces none of the pinned objects and weakens no
certificate.

### 6.1 The exact ring and its comparison with the ring of integers

`TauCeti.Cyclotomic e` has computable ring operations, decidable equality, the generator
`TauCeti.Cyclotomic.zeta e`, coordinates `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.coeff x k` (the stored list is
descending; `coeff x k` reads the ascending power coordinate), `ofCoeffList`, `equivAdjoinRoot`,
and for `e > 0` the embedding `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.complexEmbedding` sending `zeta e` to the primitive
root `complexRoot e`, injective. `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.evalRingHom` evaluates at any root of the
mapped cyclotomic polynomial, and `reduceRingHom` at a primitive root supplies the solver's
reductions; no evaluation at a nonroot is asserted.

The target `exists_ringOfIntegers_equiv_integralPowerBasis` compares the exact ring with Mathlib's
cyclotomic field on the same generator: for `K = CyclotomicField e ℚ` with its distinguished
primitive root `ζ = IsCyclotomicExtension.zeta e ℚ K`, there are a ring isomorphism
`E : Cyclotomic e ≃+* 𝓞 K` and a field embedding `ι : K →+* ℂ` such that `ι ζ = complexRoot e`,
`E (zeta e) = ζ`, `ι ∘ algebraMap ∘ E = complexEmbedding` on every exact element, and the
coordinates of `E x` in the integral power basis `hζ.integralPowerBasis` (of dimension `φ(e)`,
`IsPrimitiveRoot.integralPowerBasis_dim`) are the native `coeff x k`, `k < φ(e)`; not an arbitrary
integral basis. Route: `IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.isIntegralClosure_adjoin_singleton` and
`cyclotomicRing_isIntegralClosure` identify `𝓞 K` with `ℤ[ζ] ≅ ℤ[X]/Φ_e`, `equivAdjoinRoot`
identifies the exact ring with the same quotient, and the embedding is the one of
`IsCyclotomicExtension` composed with the choice of root; `ι` exists because `K` is a number field.
*Needs:* Mathlib as named; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.equivAdjoinRoot`,
`TauCeti.Cyclotomic.complexEmbedding_zeta`, `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.isPrimitiveRoot_complexRoot`.

### 6.6 The exact certificate, the class-power identity and the degree identity

Construct the exact ordinary and central tables from the irreducible rows and §6.4, in the native
`ClassData` numbering. Injectivity of the embedding transfers central normalization,
multiplicativity, orthogonality, degree positivity and divisibility, the sum of degree squares and
`dᵢ · omegaᵢⱼ = |Cⱼ| · tableᵢⱼ` to `TauCeti.ClassData.IsCyclotomicCharacterTableSpec`, whose
conjugation comparison carries complex conjugation. The theorem
`exists_bounded_exact_character_table`: for `d : ClassData G` and `e = exponent G` there is an
exact `out` satisfying the specification, with `TauCeti.ClassData.complexTableOfCyclotomic e out.table = TauCeti.characterTable ℂ G` and every coordinate of every `omega` entry bounded by
`B_G`. The pinned solver enumerates row permutations at every primitive conjugate root and every
degree vector satisfying the degree constraints; its certificate-reduction theorem proves
injectivity of the reduced rows at every conjugate root, and
`TauCeti.ClassData.isSome_dixonCyclotomicCharacterTable_of_spec` constructs the compatible
permutations. The tuple is indexed by `Fin φ(e)` through `TauCeti.Cyclotomic.primitiveExponent`,
`conjugateRoot` and `conjugateResidues`; one reduction does not determine an entry, and Frobenius is
trivial when `p ≡ 1 (mod e)`. The inequality `2|coeff(w_C, k)| < p` is exactly the hypothesis of
`TauCeti.Cyclotomic.lift_eq_of_conjugateResidues_eq`.

Two further theorems about the central rows, tied to `TauCeti.centralCharacterTable ℂ G`.
For `G : Type v`, `K : Type` an exponent cyclotomic field with an embedding into `ℂ`,
`exists_centralRow_classPower` asserts existence of a `K`-valued row whose embedded values are
the native row, with the power identity for every root-compatible automorphism:

- **The class-power Galois identity** (`exists_centralRow_classPower`). For `gcd(b, e) = 1`, powering `g ↦ g^b` is a
  conjugation-equivariant bijection of `G` with inverse exponent modulo `e`, so it induces a
  permutation `P_b` of `ConjClasses G` that is representative-independent, fixes the identity class
  and preserves class sizes. For an irreducible `χ`, `Ω_χ(P_b(C)) = σ_b(Ω_χ(C))`, where `σ_b` is
  the automorphism `ζ ↦ ζ^b` of `ℚ(ζ_e)` applied to the descended values (§4.4): this follows from
  `Ω_χ(C) = |C| χ(C)/χ(1)`, since `|C|` and `χ(1)` are fixed by `σ_b` and `χ(g^b) = σ_b(χ(g))`.
  Transfer the identity to the exact elements of §6.4 through the injective generator-compatible
  embedding, and derive the corresponding identity of reductions at the conjugate roots
  (Serre, §12.4, for the root action; the central-value identity is derived here by substitution
  in `TauCeti.Representation.centralCharacter_classSumCenter_mul_character_one`).
- **The degree identity.** With `n = |G|`, `s_C = |C|` (`s_C ∣ n`, so `n/s_C` is exact natural
  division) and `C⁻¹` the class of inverses, `∑_C (n/s_C) Ω_χ(C) Ω_χ(C⁻¹) = (n/χ(1))²` in `ℚ(ζ_e)`,
  from §5.2; and the positive integer `m` with `m² · T = n²` is unique, since `T ≠ 0` in a field of
  characteristic zero and positive integer squares determine their roots. This is a deduction
  from row orthogonality (Fulton–Harris, Theorem 2.12, p. 16): substituting `Ω = s_C χ/d` gives
  `T = (n/d²) ∑_C s_C χ(C)χ(C⁻¹) = n²/d²`, using `d > 0` and `n > 0`.

*Needs:* §4.4–4.5, §5.2, §6.4, §6.5; Tau Ceti as named.

**Checks.**
- `S₃`, `e = 6`, `b = 5`: `P_5` fixes every class (`t⁵ = t`, `r⁵ = r⁻¹ ∼ r`), and `σ_5` is complex
  conjugation on `ℚ(ζ₆)`, which fixes the rational entries of the `S₃` table.
- `C₅`, `χ(g) = ζ₅`, `b = 2`: `P_2` sends the class of `g` to that of `g²`, and
  `Ω(g²) = ζ₅² = σ_2(ζ₅)`; the inverse convention `σ_{b⁻¹} = σ_3` would give `ζ₅³`. For `e = 3`
  the two conventions coincide (`2⁻¹ ≡ 2 (mod 3)`), so `C₃` is not a witness.

## Layer 7: the Frobenius–Schur indicator and real/quaternionic type

### 7.1 The indicator and the trichotomy

`TauCeti.Representation.frobeniusSchurIndicator ρ = |G|⁻¹ ∑_g ρ.character (g²)` on the module spine

### 9a.1 Conjugacy classes of `GL_n(𝔽_q)`

The conjugacy classes of `G` correspond to the functions `μ` from the monic irreducible polynomials `f ≠ X` over `F`
to partitions with `Σ_f deg(f)·|μ(f)| = n`: the semisimple part of an element of the class has characteristic
polynomial `∏_f f^{|μ(f)|}`, and its unipotent part, in the centraliser `∏_f GL_{|μ(f)|}(𝔽_{q^{deg f}})` of the
semisimple part, has Jordan type `μ(f)` in each factor. The unipotent classes correspond to the partitions of `n`. The
number `c_n(q)` of classes satisfies

`Σ_{n≥0} c_n(q) t^n = ∏_{i≥1} (1 − t^i)/(1 − q t^i)`,

and the number of semisimple classes is `q^n − q^{n−1}` for `n ≥ 1`. The number of `G`-classes of pairs `(T, θ)` of a
rational maximal torus `T` of `GL_n` and a character `θ` of `T^{Fr}` is also `c_n(q)`; for a standard Levi subgroup
`L = ∏_i GL_{n_i}` the number of `L(F)`-classes of such pairs with `T ⊂ L` is the number `∏_i c_{n_i}(q)` of conjugacy
classes of `L(F)`.

Route: a semisimple `s` makes `F^n` a module over `F[X]/(charpoly)`, which splits by the Chinese remainder
theorem, and its centraliser is the unit group of the endomorphism ring of that module,
`∏_f GL_{m_f}(𝔽_{q^{deg f}})` for characteristic polynomial `∏_f f^{m_f}`. Similarity of matrices is classified by
invariant factors (ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §3.59); regrouping the invariant factors into elementary divisors,
with the Jordan decomposition of an element into commuting semisimple and unipotent parts, gives the
parametrisation, and the semisimple classes are the characteristic polynomials, the monic polynomials of degree

generating function `(1 − t)/(1 − qt) = ∏_d (1 − t^d)^{−N*_q(d)}` (unique factorisation); the class count is
`∏_d (Σ_μ t^{d|μ|})^{N*_q(d)} = ∏_d ∏_{i≥1} (1 − t^{di})^{−N*_q(d)}`, and substituting `t ↦ t^i` in the first identity
gives the product. Pairs: by the twisted classes of the Weyl group (ReductiveGroups §7a.2), a pair is up to
conjugacy a cycle type `τ ⊢ n` with a character `θ = ⊠_i θ_i` of `∏_i 𝔽_{q^{τ_i}}^×` up to the normaliser
`∏_k (ℤ/k ≀ S_{a_k})` (`a_k` the number of parts equal to `k`, `ℤ/k` acting by Frobenius). A character of `𝔽_{q^r}^×`
whose Frobenius orbit has size `d ∣ r` factors through the norm to `𝔽_{q^d}^×` (by Hilbert 90 the kernel of the norm
is `{x^{q^d−1}}`), so the classes of pairs correspond to a label `κ` of degree `n` with a partition `ν_O ⊢ κ(O)` for
each orbit, the parts of `ν_O` being the `τ_i/|O|` with `θ_i` over `O`: every pair is conjugate to one
`(T_{κ,ν}, θ_{κ,ν})`, with `κ` and `ν` unique. The orbits of size `d` number `N*_q(d)`, so the count is
`∏_O Σ_k p(k) t^{|O|k} = ∏_d ∏_{i≥1} (1 − t^{di})^{−N*_q(d)}`, the product above. For `L = ∏_i GL_{n_i}` both counts are
products over the factors.

### 9a.2 The mirabolic subgroup and derivatives

Write `P_n = GL_{n−1} ⋉ V_n` with `V_n` the unipotent radical of matrices `[[1_{n−1}, v], [0, 1]]`, and define the exact
functors `Ψ⁻(τ) = τ^{V_n}` to representations of `GL_{n−1}(F)` and its left adjoint `Ψ⁺` (inflation). For `n ≥ 2` the
stabiliser in `GL_{n−1}` of the character `v ↦ ψ₀(v_{n−1})` of `V_n` is the mirabolic subgroup `P_{n−1}`; define
`Φ⁻(τ)`, the `(V_n, ψ₀(v_{n−1}))`-isotypic part, a representation of `P_{n−1}(F)`, and
`Φ⁺(ρ) = Ind_{P_{n−1}V_n}^{P_n}(ρ ⊗ ψ₀(v_{n−1}))`. For `n = 1`, `P_1 = GL_0 = 1` and `V_1 = 1`, so `Ψ^±` are the identity,
and `V_1` has no nontrivial character: `Φ^±` occur only on `P_m` with `m ≥ 2`.

(a) For `n ≥ 2`, `Ψ⁻Ψ⁺ ≅ 1`, `Φ⁻Φ⁺ ≅ 1`, `Φ⁻Ψ⁺ = 0`, `Ψ⁻Φ⁺ = 0`. For `n ≥ 1` every representation `τ` of `P_n(F)`
satisfies `τ ≅ ⊕_{i=1}^n (Φ⁺)^{i−1}Ψ⁺(τ^{(i)})` with the **derivatives** `τ^{(i)} = Ψ⁻(Φ⁻)^{i−1}τ`, representations of
`GL_{n−i}(F)` (for `n = 1` the single term `τ = Ψ⁺Ψ⁻τ`); `dim (Φ⁺)^{i−1}Ψ⁺(ρ) = dim ρ·∏_{j=n−i+1}^{n−1}(q^j − 1)`. The
representations `(Φ⁺)^{i−1}Ψ⁺(ρ)`, for `1 ≤ i ≤ n` and `ρ` irreducible of `GL_{n−i}(F)`, are irreducible and pairwise
non-isomorphic, and exhaust the irreducible representations of `P_n(F)`.

(b) For a representation `π` of `G` put `π^{(i)} = (π|_{P_n})^{(i)}`. Then `π^{(i)}` is the space of
`(U_i, ψ)`-coinvariants, on the second factor, of `*R_{GL_{n−i}×GL_i}^{GL_n}(π)`, as a representation of
`GL_{n−i}(F)`. For irreducible `π`: `π` is generic iff `π^{(n)} ≠ 0`, and then `π^{(n)}` is one-dimensional; `π` is
cuspidal iff `π|_{P_n} ≅ (Φ⁺)^{n−1}Ψ⁺(1) = Ind_U^{P_n(F)} ψ`. In particular every cuspidal representation is generic
and has degree `ϖ_{n−1}(q) = ∏_{i=1}^{n−1}(q^i − 1)`.

(c) Every unipotent element of `G` is conjugate into `P_n(F)`, and for a cuspidal `π` and `u` unipotent of Jordan type
`μ`, `π(u) = (−1)^{n−ℓ(μ)} ∏_{i=1}^{ℓ(μ)−1}(q^i − 1)`.

(d) For an irreducible cuspidal `σ` of `GL_d(F)`, `σ × σ` has two constituents, each of multiplicity one: a generic one
of degree `q^d·ϖ_{2d−1}(q)/(q^d − 1)` and a non-generic one of degree `ϖ_{2d−1}(q)/(q^d − 1)`, whose restriction to
`P_{2d}` is `(Φ⁺)^{d−1}Ψ⁺(σ)`.

Route: (a) the finite-field form of the theory of derivatives (Gelfand–Kazhdan; Bernstein–Zelevinsky; Zelevinsky,
Ch. III): the exact sequence `0 → Φ⁺Φ⁻ → 1 → Ψ⁺Ψ⁻ → 0` on representations of `P_n`, split because the groups are
finite, comes from the decomposition of `τ|_{V_n}` into the `V_n`-invariants and the orbit of nontrivial characters of
`V_n`, which `GL_{n−1}(F)` permutes transitively with stabiliser `P_{n−1}(F)` of index `q^{n−1} − 1` (so the stabiliser
in `P_n(F)` is `P_{n−1}V_n`), for `n ≥ 2`; iterate down to `P_1 = 1`, where `τ = Ψ⁺Ψ⁻τ`. (b) Unwinding `(Φ⁻)^{i−1}` and
`Ψ⁻`, `π^{(i)}` is the space of coinvariants of the unipotent radical of the parabolic subgroup of type `(n−i, i)`
(trivial character) times `U_i` (character `ψ`), the stated Whittaker coinvariant of the Harish-Chandra restriction;
genericity is `i = n` with Frobenius reciprocity and multiplicity one (ReductiveGroups §7i.2); cuspidality is the
vanishing of every `*R` with `i < n`. For the criterion by the mirabolic restriction, by (a) it says that `π^{(i)} = 0`
for `i < n` and that `π^{(n)}` is a line. If `π` is not cuspidal, let `(∏_{i≤r} GL_{n_i}, ⊠_i σ_i)`,
`r ≥ 2`, be its cuspidal support (ReductiveGroups §7h.2): by adjunction and transitivity (ReductiveGroups §7c.1),
`*R_{GL_{n−n_r}×GL_{n_r}}^{GL_n}(π)` has a constituent `τ ⊠ σ_r`, and `σ_r`, cuspidal of smaller rank, is generic by
induction on `n`, so `π^{(n_r)} ≠ 0` with `n_r < n`. The degree: `|P_n(F)|/|U| = ϖ_{n−1}(q)`. (c) A unipotent element
fixes a nonzero linear form, hence is conjugate into `P_n`, and by (b) `π|_{P_n} = ρ_n` with `ρ_k = (Φ⁺)^{k−1}Ψ⁺(1)` on
`P_k`. By induction on `k`, the value of `ρ_k` at a unipotent `u ∈ P_k` with `ℓ` Jordan blocks (in `GL_k`) is
`(−1)^{k−ℓ}∏_{i=1}^{ℓ−1}(q^i − 1)`: write `u = (a, v) ∈ GL_{k−1} ⋉ V_k`; the induced-character formula gives
`ρ_k(u) = Σ_w ψ₀(w(v))·ρ_{k−1}(a_w)` over the nonzero linear forms `w` on `F^{k−1}` with `w∘a = w`, `a_w` being `a`
read in the stabiliser of `w`; the forms fixed by `a` make up a space of dimension `ℓ(a)` whose annihilator is
`Im(a − 1)`, so the sum is `q^{ℓ(a)} − 1` if `v ∈ Im(a − 1)` (and then `u` has `ℓ(a) + 1` blocks) and `−1` otherwise (and
then `u` has `ℓ(a)` blocks). (d) The Harish-Chandra restriction of `σ × σ` to `GL_d × GL_d` is `2(σ ⊠ σ)` (Mackey formula,
ReductiveGroups Layer 7d); the normaliser of `GL_d × GL_d` exchanges the two blocks, so the stabiliser of `σ ⊠ σ` in
it has order two modulo `GL_d × GL_d`, and the endomorphism algebra of `σ × σ` is two-dimensional and commutative
(ReductiveGroups §7h.3): two constituents, each of multiplicity one. The other Harish-Chandra restrictions of `σ × σ`
to `GL_{2d−i} × GL_i`, `0 < i < 2d`, `i ≠ d`, vanish by the Mackey formula and cuspidality, so by (b) the derivatives of
each constituent vanish except in degrees `d` (equal to `σ`) and `2d`. Put
`D = ϖ_{2d−1}(q)/(q^d − 1)`. Then `dim(σ × σ) = ϖ_{2d}(q)/(q^d − 1)² = (q^d + 1)D`; the two derivatives in degree `d`
contribute `2D` by (a); the remainder `(q^d − 1)D = ϖ_{2d−1}(q)` shows that `(σ × σ)^{(2d)}` is a line. As derivatives are
additive, exactly one constituent is generic, and the degrees are `D + ϖ_{2d−1}(q) = q^dD` and `D`.

### 9a.4 Green's cuspidal characters

For a regular `θ ∈ Ξ_n`, `σ_θ = (−1)^{n−1} R_{T_{(n)}}^G(θ)` is an irreducible cuspidal character of `G` of degree
`ϖ_{n−1}(q)`; `σ_θ = σ_{θ′}` iff `θ′ = θ^{q^j}` for some `j`. Further: `σ_θ` has central character `θ|_{F^×}`;
`σ_θ ⊗ (α∘det) = σ_{θ·(α∘N)}` for `α ∈ Ξ_1`; `σ_θ^∨ = σ_{θ⁻¹}`; at a regular elliptic element (characteristic polynomial
irreducible) with eigenvalue `z ∈ 𝔽_{q^n}`, `σ_θ = (−1)^{n−1} Σ_{j=0}^{n−1} θ(z^{q^j})`; at a regular semisimple element that
is not elliptic, `σ_θ = 0`; at `su` with `s` semisimple and `u` unipotent commuting with `s`, `σ_θ(su) = 0` unless the
characteristic polynomial of `s` is `f^{n/d}` with `f` irreducible of degree `d ∣ n`, in which case, with `z` a root of `f`
and `ℓ_d(u)` the number of Jordan blocks of `u` in `C_G(s) ≅ GL_{n/d}(𝔽_{q^d})`,

`σ_θ(su) = (−1)^{n−1} (Σ_{j=0}^{d−1} θ(z^{q^j}))·∏_{i=1}^{ℓ_d(u)−1}(1 − q^{di})`.

Route: `θ` regular is in general position for `T_{(n)}`, whose Weyl group `N_G(T_{(n)})/T_{(n)}^{Fr} ≅ ℤ/n` acts by
Frobenius (ReductiveGroups §7a.2); so irreducibility up to the sign `ε_Gε_{T_{(n)}} = (−1)^{n−1}` and the degree are the
degree and irreducibility theorems (ReductiveGroups §9F.7), the orbit criterion is the inner product formula
(§9F.4), and cuspidality is the cuspidality theorem (§9F.9), `T_{(n)}` lying in no proper rational Levi subgroup
(`𝔽_{q^n}^×` acts irreducibly on `F^n`). Central character and dual from the definition of `R_T^G(θ)` (§9F.2); twist
from the character formula (§9F.3), as in the route of §9a.7. Values: the character formula (§9F.3), where for `s` with characteristic polynomial `f^{n/d}` the torus `T_{(n)}` lies in
`C(s) ≅ Res_{𝔽_{q^d}/F} GL_{n/d}`, and for other `s` no conjugate of `s` lies in `T_{(n)}`. It remains to show that the
Green function `Q^{C(s)}_{T_{(n)}}` at a unipotent element with `ℓ_d(u)` blocks is `∏_{i=1}^{ℓ_d(u)−1}(1 − q^{di})`. Over

identifies `C(s)^{Fr}` with `GL_{n/d}(𝔽_{q^d})` and the rational parabolic and Levi subgroups of `C(s)`, with their rational
points and unipotent radicals, with those of `GL_{n/d}` over `𝔽_{q^d}`; so it identifies the Harish-Chandra functors and
cuspidality on both sides (it does not identify Deligne–Lusztig varieties, and none is needed). `T_{(n)}` lies in no
proper rational Levi subgroup of `C(s)`, `N_{C(s)^{Fr}}(T_{(n)})/T_{(n)}^{Fr} ≅ ℤ/(n/d)` acts by `x ↦ x^{q^d}`, and for a
character `θ′` of `𝔽_{q^n}^×` with `n/d` distinct `q^d`-conjugates (one exists) the cuspidality and irreducibility
theorems in `C(s)` make `(−1)^{n/d−1}R^{C(s)}_{T_{(n)}}(θ′)` an irreducible cuspidal character of `GL_{n/d}(𝔽_{q^d})`. Its
values at unipotent elements are §9a.2(c) over `𝔽_{q^d}`, and on unipotent elements it is `(−1)^{n/d−1}Q^{C(s)}_{T_{(n)}}`,
which gives the product.

### 9a.5 Hecke algebras of Harish-Chandra induced cuspidal representations

Let `L = ∏_i GL_{d_i}^{m_i}` (block diagonal, the blocks of each `i` consecutive), with standard parabolic subgroup `P`,
and `σ = ⊠_i σ_i^{⊠ m_i}` with `σ_i` pairwise non-isomorphic irreducible cuspidal representations of `GL_{d_i}(F)`. Then
`W_G(L, σ) = {x ∈ N_G(L) : ˣσ ≅ σ}/L(F) ≅ ∏_i S_{m_i}`, realised by the block permutation matrices. For each `i`,
`σ_i × σ_i` has exactly two constituents, each of multiplicity one (§9a.2(d)); let `a_i ≥ 1` be the ratio of their
degrees, so that `a_i = q^{d_i}`. `End_G(R_L^G σ)` has a basis `T_w`, `w ∈ ∏_i S_{m_i}`, with `T_w` supported on the double
coset `P ẇ P`, `T_w T_{w′} = T_{ww′}` when lengths add, and `(T_s − a_i)(T_s + 1) = 0` for each simple reflection `s` of the
factor `S_{m_i}`. Thus `End_G(R_L^G σ) ≅ ⊗_i H_{m_i}(a_i)`, where `H_m(a)` is the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of `S_m` with
these relations, the algebra of TypeAHeckeAlgebras §1.1 at the parameter `√a` after the substitution `T_s ↦ √a·T_s`;
it is split semisimple, and the constituents of `R_L^G σ` correspond to its simple modules, with multiplicity the
dimension. For `L = T` and `σ = 1` this is Iwahori's theorem `End_G(Ind_B^G 1) ≅ H_n(q)`.

Route: `W_G(L, σ)`: the normaliser of `L` permutes the blocks, and a permutation preserves `σ` iff it preserves each
family of `σ_i`-blocks, the `σ_i` being pairwise non-isomorphic. The block permutation matrices permute the tensor
factors of `σ`, which extends `σ` to `L(F) ⋊ ∏_i S_{m_i}` and gives operators `B_w` supported on the double cosets
`P ẇ P`; the dimension of `End_G(R_L^G σ)` is `|W_G(L, σ)|` and its simple modules correspond to the constituents
(ReductiveGroups §7h.3), so the `B_w` form a basis, and when lengths add `P ẇ P ẇ′ P = P ẇẇ′ P`, which gives
`B_wB_{w′} = B_{ww′}`. The `T_w` of the statement are `∏_i c_i^{ℓ_i(w)}·B_w`, with one nonzero constant `c_i` for each
factor `S_{m_i}` (`ℓ_i` the length of the component in that factor), fixed by the quadratic relation; `c_i` is not 1 in
general (Brundan–Dipper–Kleshchev, (2.5b)). The quadratic relation is computed in the Levi subgroup `M ⊃ L` in
which the two `σ_i`-blocks exchanged by `s` form one block `GL_{2d_i}`, where `R_L^M σ` is `σ_i × σ_i` tensor the other
blocks: there the endomorphism algebra is two-dimensional, the two roots of the relation are fixed by the degrees of
the two constituents, and `R_M^G` (transitivity, ReductiveGroups §7c.1) carries it to `End_G(R_L^G σ)`; the ratio
`a_i = q^{d_i}` is §9a.2(d). Split semisimplicity: the criterion of TypeAHeckeAlgebras §1.2, `∏_r (1 + a + ⋯ + a^{r−1}) ≠ 0`,
holds for `a > 0`.

### 9a.6 The characteristic map of a label

Fix a label `κ` of degree `n`, the cuspidal characters `σ_O = σ_{θ_O}` of `GL_{|O|}(F)` (§9a.4), the Levi subgroup
`L_κ = ∏_O GL_{|O|}^{κ(O)}` and `σ_κ = ⊠_O σ_O^{⊠κ(O)}`. Let `Λ_κ = ⊗_O Λ_ℂ^{κ(O)}` (the parts of degree `κ(O)`), with the
tensor product of the Hall inner products, and define the linear map `ch_κ : Λ_κ → (class functions on G)` by

`ch_κ(⊗_O p_{ν_O}) = (−1)^{n + Σ_O κ(O)} R_{T_{κ,ν}}^G(θ_{κ,ν})`.

Then:
(a) `ch_κ` is an isometry onto the span `V_κ` of the `R_T^G(θ)` with `(T, θ)` of label `κ`, and `V_κ` is the span of the set
`E_κ` of irreducible constituents of these `R_T^G(θ)`; the `V_κ` are pairwise orthogonal and span all class functions;
(b) `ch_{κ′+κ″}(f′·f″) = ch_{κ′}(f′) × ch_{κ″}(f″)` (Harish-Chandra product from `GL_{n′} × GL_{n″}`); in particular
`ch_κ(⊗_O p_1^{κ(O)}) = R_{L_κ}^G(σ_κ)`;
(c) `χ_{gen(κ)} = ch_κ(⊗_O e_{κ(O)})` is an irreducible character, the unique constituent of `Γ_n` in `E_κ`;
(d) for every Green datum `λ` with label `κ`, `χ_λ = ch_κ(⊗_O s_{λ(O)})` is an irreducible character; the `χ_λ` are
pairwise distinct, `E_κ = {χ_λ : |λ| = κ}`, and `E_κ` is the Harish-Chandra series of `(L_κ, σ_κ)`, with

### 9a.7 Green's parametrisation and degree formula

The irreducible characters of `G` are the `χ_λ` of §9a.6, `λ` running over the Green data of weight `n`, without
repetition. The degree of `χ_λ` is

`χ_λ(1) = ϖ_n(q) ∏_O q_O^{n(λ(O))} / H̃_{λ(O)}(q_O)`.

Twisting and duality: `χ_λ ⊗ (α∘det) = χ_{α·λ}` and `χ_λ^∨ = χ_{λ∘inv}`, where `α·λ` and `λ∘inv` transport `λ` along the
maps of orbits `O ↦ α·O` (the orbit of `θ_O·(α∘N)`, `α ∈ Ξ_1`) and `O ↦ O⁻¹`. Every irreducible cuspidal character is some
`σ_θ` (§9a.4), so the cuspidal characters are in bijection with the Frobenius orbits of degree `n`. The degree formula
rests on the `q`-hook formula in `Λ_ℂ`: the principal specialisation
`s_μ(1, t, t², …) = t^{n(μ)} ∏_{boxes} (1 − t^{h(box)})^{−1}`, an identity of power series in `t` whose two sides are
expansions of rational functions; the degree formula uses the rational functions at `t = q_O`.

Route: §9a.6(a), (d): the `E_κ` partition the irreducible characters and `E_κ = {χ_λ : |λ| = κ}` without repetition. The
degree: `ch_κ(⊗p_{ν_O})(1) = (−1)^{Σ_O(κ(O) − ℓ(ν_O))} ϖ_n(q)/∏_{O,i}(q_O^{ν_{O,i}} − 1)` by the degree formula for
Deligne–Lusztig characters (ReductiveGroups §9F.7 (a)), which is `ϖ_n(q)` times the value at `⊗p_{ν_O}` of the
specialisation `⊗_O g_O ↦ ∏_O (−1)^{deg g_O} g_O(1, t, t², …)|_{t=q_O}` (`p_k(1, t, …) = (1 − t^k)⁻¹`); expand
`s_μ = Σ_ν χ^μ(ν)p_ν/z_ν` (SchurWeyl §7.6) and apply the `q`-hook formula. The `q`-hook formula: the bialternant formula
at `x_i = t^{i−1}`, `i ≤ N`, is a ratio of Vandermonde determinants in powers of `t`, giving
`s_μ(1, …, t^{N−1}) = t^{n(μ)} ∏_{boxes}(1 − t^{N+c(box)})/(1 − t^{h(box)})`, `c` the content; let `N → ∞` in `ℤ[[t]]`.
Twisting and duality: for every pair `(T, θ)`, `R_T^G(θ) ⊗ (α∘det) = R_T^G(θ·(α∘det)|_{T^{Fr}})` and
`R_T^G(θ)^∨ = R_T^G(θ⁻¹)` (the character formula, ReductiveGroups §9F.3: `det(su) = det s`, and the Green functions are
rational integers, §9F.2, with `Q(u⁻¹) = Q(u)`). For the pairs `(T_{κ,ν}, θ_{κ,ν})`, `α∘det` restricted to
`T_{κ,ν}^{Fr}` is `⊠_{O,i} α∘N`, so `θ_{κ,ν}·(α∘det) = θ_{α·κ,ν}` with the representatives `θ_O·(α∘N)` of the orbits `α·O`,
and `θ_{κ,ν}⁻¹ = θ_{κ∘inv,ν}`; the orbit sizes and the signs of §9a.6 are unchanged. Hence `ch_κ(f) ⊗ (α∘det) = ch_{α·κ}(f)`
and `ch_κ(f)^∨ = ch_{κ∘inv}(f)` for every `f ∈ Λ_κ`, first for `f = ⊗_O p_{ν_O}` and then by linearity; at
`f = ⊗_O s_{λ(O)}` this is the formula. Cuspidal characters: `χ_λ` lies in the Harish-Chandra series of `(L_κ, σ_κ)`
(§9a.6(d)), so it is cuspidal iff `L_κ = G`, iff `κ` is a single orbit of size `n` with `κ(O) = 1`, iff `χ_λ = σ_θ`.

### 9a.8 Uniformity, Green functions and Green's virtual characters

(a) Every class function on `G` is a linear combination of the `R_T^G(θ)`. Explicitly, for a Green datum `λ` of label `κ`,

`χ_λ = (−1)^{n + Σ_O κ(O)} Σ_ν (∏_O χ^{λ(O)}(ν_O)/z_{ν_O}) R_{T_{κ,ν}}^G(θ_{κ,ν})`,

the sum over the tuples `ν = (ν_O)`, `ν_O ⊢ κ(O)`. For a unipotent character (`λ` supported on the orbit of `1`, with value
`μ` there) this is `χ_μ = (n!)⁻¹ Σ_{w∈S_n} χ^μ(w) R_{T_{τ(w)}}^G(1)`, `τ(w)` the cycle type of `w`. Special values of the Green
functions `Q_T^G`: `Q_{T_τ}^G(u_{(n)}) = 1`; `Q_{T_τ}^G(1) = (−1)^{n−ℓ(τ)} ϖ_n(q)/∏_i (q^{τ_i} − 1)`;
`Q_{T_{(n)}}^G(u_μ) = ∏_{i=1}^{ℓ(μ)−1}(1 − q^i)`.

(b) (Green's virtual characters.) For a homomorphism `θ̃ : F̄^× → ℂ^×` let `b_{θ̃}^{(n)}(x) = Σ_α θ̃(α)`, the sum over the
eigenvalues `α ∈ F̄^×` of `x ∈ GL_n(F)` counted with multiplicity. Green's class functions `J_a(θ̃)` on `GL_a(F)`,
`a ≥ 1`, satisfy, and are determined by, the recursion

`b_{θ̃}^{(n)} = Σ_{a=1}^{n} J_a(θ̃) × 1_{GL_{n−a}(F)}`   for every `n ≥ 1`,

the term `a = n` being `J_n(θ̃)` itself (**Green's virtual characters**). Then `J_n(θ̃)` is the Deligne–Lusztig character of
the Coxeter torus (deduced in the route below from ReductiveGroups Layer 9F):

`J_n(θ̃) = R_{T_{(n)}}^G(θ̃|_{𝔽_{q^n}^×})`.

In particular `b_{θ̃}^{(n)}` is a virtual character, `J_n(θ̃) = (−1)^{n−1}σ_θ` when `θ = θ̃|_{𝔽_{q^n}^×}` is regular (§9a.4), and
`*R_L^G J_n(θ̃) = 0` for every proper standard Levi subgroup `L`.

Route: (a) §9a.6(a) for uniformity; the formula is the definition of `ch_κ` applied to
`⊗_O s_{λ(O)} = ⊗_O Σ_{ν_O} χ^{λ(O)}(ν_O)p_{ν_O}/z_{ν_O}` (SchurWeyl §7.6). Special values: Green functions at regular unipotent
elements (ReductiveGroups §9F.10, stated for the `q`-Frobenius of an `𝔽_q`-structure, which `Fr` is) and the degree
formula (§9F.7 (a)), with `ε_Gε_{T_τ} = (−1)^{n−ℓ(τ)}`; and §9a.2(c) with §9a.4, `Q_{T_{(n)}}^G` being `(−1)^{n−1}σ_θ` on
unipotent elements (§9F.2).
(b) By induction on `n`. The vanishing of the Harish-Chandra restrictions of `R_{T_{(n)}}^G(θ̃|)` is ReductiveGroups
§9F.9 (through the Mackey formula §9F.5 (b)): `T_{(n)}` lies in no proper rational Levi subgroup (§9a.4). Write
`β_a = R_{T_{(a)}}^{GL_a}(θ̃|_{𝔽_{q^a}^×})` and `ξ_n = b^{(n)} − Σ_{a<n} β_a × 1`. For `0 < c < n`, the Harish-Chandra restriction to

Layer 7c, of a function of the eigenvalues, which are those of the two diagonal blocks). By the Mackey formula
(ReductiveGroups Layer 7d) the restriction of `β_a × 1_{GL_{n−a}}` to `GL_c × GL_{n−c}` is a sum over the splittings

### 9a.9 Generic and semisimple characters

The **semisimple label** of `χ_λ` is `|λ| : O ↦ |λ(O)|`; in the labelled form it is the monic polynomial `∏_O f_O^{κ(O)}` of
degree `n` with nonzero constant term. By §9a.6(d) the irreducible characters of label `κ` form one Harish-Chandra
series, that of `(L_κ, σ_κ)`. `χ_λ` is generic iff every `λ(O)` is a column `(1^{κ(O)})`; it is called **semisimple** iff every
`λ(O)` is a row `(κ(O))`. Each label carries exactly one generic and one semisimple character; there are `q^n − q^{n−1}`
of each, with degrees

`q^{η(κ)} ϖ_n(q)/∏_O ϖ_{κ(O)}(q_O)` (generic), `η(κ) = Σ_O |O|·κ(O)(κ(O)−1)/2`, and `ϖ_n(q)/∏_O ϖ_{κ(O)}(q_O)` (semisimple).

The Gelfand–Graev representation is the sum of the generic characters, one for each label: `Γ_n = Σ_κ χ_{gen(κ)}`.
In particular `⟨Γ_n, Γ_n⟩ = q^n − q^{n−1}`, the number of generic irreducible representations of `G` and of its
semisimple classes.

Route: the series from §9a.6(a), (d). Genericity: by §9a.6(c), `χ_{gen(κ)} = χ_λ` for the columns (`e_m = s_{(1^m)}`), and
it is the only constituent of `Γ_n` of label `κ`; `Γ_n` is multiplicity free (ReductiveGroups §7i.2), so
`Γ_n = Σ_κ χ_{gen(κ)}`. Degrees from §9a.7: a column of length `k` has `n((1^k)) = k(k−1)/2` and `H̃_{(1^k)}(t) = ϖ_k(t)`; a
row has `n = 0` and the same hook product. The count: the labels of degree `n` are, in the labelled form, the monic
polynomials of degree `n` with nonzero constant term, `q^n − q^{n−1}` of them (§9a.1). `⟨Γ_n, Γ_n⟩`: multiplicity one
(ReductiveGroups §7i.2) and one generic character for each label.

### 9a.10 Counting

`Σ_n c_n(q)t^n = ∏_{i≥1}(1 − t^i)/(1 − qt^i)` (§9a.1); the number of cuspidal irreducible characters is `N*_q(n)`; the number of

lower bound counts the semisimple classes (§9a.1); the upper bound is Maslen–Rockmore's.

### 9a.11 Gauss sums of cuspidal characters

For an irreducible representation `σ` of `G` the operator `Σ_{x∈G} ψ₀(tr x)σ(x)` commutes with `σ` and is therefore a scalar
`w(σ)`, the **Gauss sum** of `σ`. For `θ ∈ Ξ_n` put `g_n(θ) = Σ_{z∈𝔽_{q^n}^×} θ(z)ψ₀(Tr_{𝔽_{q^n}/F} z)` (Mathlib's `gaussSum`
for `θ` and `ψ₀ ∘ Tr`). For regular `θ ∈ Ξ_n`,

`w(σ_θ) = (−1)^{n−1} q^{n(n−1)/2} g_n(θ)`.

Route: `x ↦ ψ₀(tr x)` is a class function, so the operator commutes with `σ(G)` and Schur's lemma applies. Taking
traces, `w(σ_θ)·ϖ_{n−1}(q) = Σ_x σ_θ(x)ψ₀(tr x)`. By §9a.4, `σ_θ(su) = 0` unless the characteristic polynomial of `s` is `f^m` with
`f` irreducible of degree `d`, `dm = n`, and then `σ_θ(su) = (−1)^{n−1}(Σ_{j<d}θ(z^{q^j}))·Q(u)` with `z` a root of `f` and `Q` the
Green function of the Coxeter torus `T_c` of `C(s) ≅ GL_m(𝔽_{q^d})` (§9a.8(a)), while `tr(su) = m·Tr_{𝔽_{q^d}/F}(z) = Tr_{𝔽_{q^n}/F}(z)`.
For every rational maximal torus `T`, `Σ_{u unipotent} Q_T(u) = |G|/|T^{Fr}|`: `Σ_θ R_T^G(θ)` vanishes off the unipotent
elements and equals `|T^{Fr}|·Q_T` on them (the character formula, ReductiveGroups §9F.3, with §9F.2), and
`Σ_θ ⟨R_T^G(θ), 1⟩ = 1` (ReductiveGroups §9F.5 (c)). Applied to `C(s)` and `T_c` (`|T_c^{Fr}| = q^n − 1`), the
elements with semisimple part conjugate to `s` contribute
`(|G|/|C(s)(F)|)·(−1)^{n−1}(Σ_{j<d}θ(z^{q^j}))ψ₀(Tr z)·|C(s)(F)|/(q^n − 1)`; summing over the classes, each polynomial `f` carrying
its `d` roots, gives `(−1)^{n−1}|G|·g_n(θ)/(q^n − 1)`, and `|G| = q^{n(n−1)/2}ϖ_n(q)`.

*Needs:* §9a.4, §9a.8(a); ReductiveGroups §§9F.2, 9F.3, 9F.5 (c) (Green functions, the character formula,
`⟨R_T^G(θ), 1⟩`); SemisimpleAlgebras Layer 1 (Schur's lemma); Mathlib `gaussSum`, `AddChar`, `MulChar`.

(Kondo, *On Gaussian sums attached to the general linear groups over finite fields*, 1963, the Theorem of the
Introduction, p. 245, proved in §2.2, in the case of a cuspidal character.)

**Checks.**

### 9a.12 Shintani descent for `GL_n`

Let `F ⊂ F′ = 𝔽_{q^d}` (`d ≥ 1`), `G = GL_n(F)`, `G′ = GL_n(F′)`, `Fr` the automorphism of `G′` raising every entry to the `q`-th
power (of order `d`), and `Nm(δ) = δ·Fr(δ)⋯Fr^{d−1}(δ)` for `δ ∈ G′`. Elements `δ, δ′` of `G′` are **`Fr`-conjugate** if
`δ′ = y⁻¹δFr(y)` for some `y ∈ G′`; an **`Fr`-class function** is constant on `Fr`-conjugacy classes.

(a) *Norm classes.* For `δ ∈ G′`, `Nm(δ)` is conjugate in `G′` to an element `γ ∈ G`, unique up to conjugacy in `G` (the **norm
class** of `δ`); `δ ↦` (norm class) is a bijection from the `Fr`-conjugacy classes of `G′` onto the conjugacy classes of `G`;
and `{y ∈ G′ : y⁻¹δFr(y) = δ}` is isomorphic to the centraliser of `γ` in `G`. Hence
`#{δ ∈ G′ : Nm(δ) ∼ γ}·|G| = #(class of γ)·|G′|`.

(b) *The Shintani lift.* For a class function `f` on `G`, `Sh_d(f)(δ) = f(γ)` (`γ` in the norm class of `δ`) is an `Fr`-class
function on `G′`; `|G′|⁻¹ Σ_δ Sh_d f(δ)·conj(Sh_d f′(δ)) = |G|⁻¹ Σ_γ f(γ)·conj(f′(γ))`; and
`Sh_d(ω∘det) = (ω∘N_{F′/F})∘det` for every character `ω` of `F^×`.

(c) *Harish-Chandra induction.* Let `M = ∏_i GL_{n_i}` be a standard Levi subgroup with standard parabolic subgroup
`P = MU`, and `Sh_d^M = ⊠_i Sh_d` the lift for `M`. For an `Fr`-class function `φ` on `M(F′)` put
`R_M^{Fr}(φ)(δ) = |P(F′)|⁻¹ Σ_{y ∈ G′, y⁻¹δFr(y) ∈ P(F′)} φ(pr_M(y⁻¹δFr(y)))`, with `pr_M : P(F′) → M(F′)` the projection along
`U(F′)`. Then `Sh_d(R_M^G f) = R_M^{Fr}(Sh_d^M f)` for every class function `f` on `M(F)`.

(d) *Shintani's lifting theorem.* For every irreducible character `χ` of `G` there are an irreducible representation `ρ` of
`G′` with `ρ∘Fr ≅ ρ`, an extension `ρ̃` of `ρ` to `G′ ⋊ ⟨Fr⟩` and a sign `ε ∈ {±1}` with `Sh_d(χ)(δ) = ε·χ_{ρ̃}(δ·Fr)` for
every `δ ∈ G′`; and `χ ↦ ρ` is a bijection from the irreducible characters of `G` onto the isomorphism classes of
`Fr`-stable irreducible representations of `G′`.

Route: (a) Lang's theorem for `GL_n` over `F̄` (ReductiveGroups Layer 7a), with `Fr` extended to the `q`-Frobenius of
`GL_n(F̄)`, gives `x` with `δ = x⁻¹Fr(x)`; then `Nm(δ) = x⁻¹Fr^d(x)`, and `γ = Fr^d(x)x⁻¹` is `Fr`-fixed (from `Fr(x) = xδ` and
`Fr^d(δ) = δ`), so `γ ∈ G` and `Nm(δ) = x⁻¹γx`. Matrices over `F′` conjugate over `F̄` are conjugate over `F′`, and elements of
`G` conjugate in `G′` are conjugate in `G` (rational canonical form, ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §3.56). `x` is determined
by `δ` up to `x ↦ gx` with `g ∈ G`, which conjugates `γ` in `G`; replacing
`δ` by `y⁻¹δFr(y)` replaces `x` by `xy` and keeps `γ`. If `x` and `x′` give the same `γ`, then `y = x⁻¹x′` satisfies
`Fr^d(y) = y`, so `y ∈ G′` and `x′⁻¹Fr(x′) = y⁻¹δFr(y)`: injectivity. Surjectivity: for `γ ∈ G` choose `x` with `Fr^d(x)x⁻¹ = γ`
(Lang for `Fr^d`) and put `δ = x⁻¹Fr(x)`. Finally `y ↦ xyx⁻¹` maps `{y ∈ G′ : y⁻¹δFr(y) = δ}` onto the centraliser of `γ` in
`G`, so the `Fr`-class of `δ` has `|G′|/|C_G(γ)|` elements, which is the count. (b) The sum over `G′` groups by norm classes
with the fibres of (a); `det Nm(δ) = N_{F′/F}(det δ)`. (c) Let `δ = x⁻¹Fr(x)` and `γ = Fr^d(x)x⁻¹` as in (a). For `y ∈ G′`,
`δFr(y)P = yP` says that the coset `xyP ∈ G(F̄)/P(F̄)` is `Fr`-fixed, hence lies in `(G/P)(F)` (Lang for the connected `P`),
and `γ·xyP = xyP`; conversely, for a coset `c ∈ (G/P)(F)` fixed by `γ`, `x⁻¹c` is fixed by `Fr^d`, hence of the form `yP` with
`y ∈ G′`, and `δFr(yP) = yP`. So `yP(F′) ↦ xyP` is a bijection from the cosets with `y⁻¹δFr(y) ∈ P(F′)` onto the points of
`(G/P)(F)` fixed by `γ`. If `xy = zp⁻¹` with `z ∈ G` and `p ∈ P(F̄)`, then `y⁻¹δFr(y) = pFr(p)⁻¹` and `z⁻¹γz = Fr^d(p)⁻¹p`, so by
the construction of (a) in `P` and in `M` the image of `z⁻¹γz` in `M(F)` lies in the norm class of the image of `y⁻¹δFr(y)` in
`M(F′)`. Comparing with `R_M^G(f)(γ) = Σ_{zP ∈ (G/P)(F), γzP = zP} f(pr_M(z⁻¹γz))` (ReductiveGroups Layer 7c, the adapter
`R = Ind ∘ Inf`) term by term gives the identity. (d) The proof is Shintani's, and is cited; in outline: for
`1 ≤ r ≤ n` let `Σ_r(θ̃)` be the class function whose value at `x` is the `r`-th elementary symmetric function of the
`θ̃(α)`, `α` the eigenvalues of `x` (`Σ_1 = b_{θ̃}^{(n)}` of §9a.8 (b)); it is a virtual character (Green 1955, Theorem 1).
The tensor-power construction of Shintani's Lemma 1-4 attaches to a virtual character `X` of `G′` a virtual character of
`G′ ⋊ ⟨Fr⟩` whose value at `δ·Fr` is `X(Nm(δ))`; applied to the `Σ_r(θ̃)` over `F′` it lifts the `Σ_r(θ̃)` of `G`. Every
irreducible character of `G` is an integral combination of the `Σ_r(θ̃)` and of Harish-Chandra products from proper
standard Levi subgroups (Green's theorem, in the form of Shintani's Lemma 2-10); eigenvalue sums alone give rational
combinations only. With (c) and induction on `n`, the lift of an irreducible character is the restriction to the coset
of a virtual character of `G′ ⋊ ⟨Fr⟩` (his Lemma 2-11), of norm one there by (b), hence a root of unity times the
restriction of one irreducible extension (his Lemmas 1-1 to 1-3), and for a suitable extension a sign (his Lemma 2-11
and Theorem 1). By (a) and (b) the lift is an isometry from the class functions on `G` onto the `Fr`-class functions on
`G′` (the two spaces have the same dimension), so the lifts of the irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis of
the latter; the restrictions to the coset of the extensions of non-isomorphic `Fr`-stable irreducible representations
are orthogonal and nonzero; so `χ ↦ ρ` is a bijection (his Theorem 1, with the count of the `Fr`-stable irreducible
representations in his Lemma 2-7).

*Needs:* §9a.1, §9a.8(b); ReductiveGroups Layers 7a, 7b, 7c; InductionRestriction Layer 2 (induced characters);
ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §3.56 (the rational canonical form).

(Shintani, *Two remarks on irreducible characters of finite general linear groups*, 1976: (a) Lemma 2-6; (b) its
Corollary; (c) Lemmas 2-8 and 2-9; (d) Lemmas 1-1 to 1-4, Definition 2-5 and Lemma 2-10 (Green's theorem), Lemma 2-11,
Lemma 2-7, Theorem 1.)

**Checks.**

`(r, s)`-signs `(+,+)`, `(−,+)`, `(+,−)`, `(−,−)` and `H`-eigenvalues `−2, 0, 0, 0`; construct their
orthogonal projectors. For stiffness `κ > 0`, mass `m > 0` and rest vector `v ≠ 0`, derive the
linearization of the pair force `Fᵢ = κ(‖r‖ − ‖v‖) r/‖r‖`, `r = v + uⱼ − uᵢ`, `Fⱼ = −Fᵢ`: the
acceleration on `i` is `(κ/m) P_v(uⱼ − uᵢ)` with `P_v(w) = ⟪v, w⟫ v/‖v‖²`; pin equal masses and
stiffnesses.

The transverse zero mode, made exact (`transverseLength`, `transverseEnergy`,
`transverseEnergy_quartic`): with rest length `1` and `u₁ = (0, ε)`, `u₂ = (0, −ε)`, the relative
vector is `(1, −2ε)` of length `ℓ(ε) = √(1 + 4ε²)`, the potential is
`E(ε) = (κ/2)(ℓ(ε) − 1)² = 8κε⁴/(ℓ(ε) + 1)²`, and the force on particle `1` is
`F₁(ε) = κ(1 − 1/ℓ(ε))(1, −2ε)`, `F₂ = −F₁`. Prove `E(0) = E′(0) = E″(0) = 0`, `E(ε) > 0` for
`ε ≠ 0`, the limit `E(ε)/ε⁴ → 2κ` as `ε → 0`, `ε ≠ 0` (the punctured neighbourhood is necessary:
real division gives `0` at `ε = 0`), and that the first force coordinate is positive for `ε ≠ 0`.
Hence the affine paths `u₁(t) = (0, at)`, `u₂(t) = (0, −at)`, `a ≠ 0`, which `H` annihilates, fail
the exact Newton equation at every `t ≠ 0` for every `m > 0`: their acceleration is zero and the
exact force is not. Compare with the rotated rest configurations `p₁(ε) = (½ − ½cos 2ε, ½ sin 2ε)`,
`p₂(ε) = (½ + ½cos 2ε, −½ sin 2ε)`, at distance `1`, zero potential, and the same tangent
`(0,1,0,−1)` at `ε = 0`; these are configuration curves, not solutions of the unconstrained spring
equation. This is the limitation of the linear approximation in the tetrahedron handout
(knutson-tetrahedron, pp. 1–3). *Needs:* Layer 3, SemisimpleAlgebras Layer 1; Mathlib `Real.sqrt`,
differentiation and `Filter.Tendsto` on `nhdsWithin`.

**Checks.**
- `H(1,0,−1,0) = (−2,0,2,0)`, `H(0,1,0,1) = 0`, `H(1,0,1,0) = 0`, `H(0,1,0,−1) = 0`.
- `(0,1,0,1)` ("both up") is sent by `r` to `(0,−1,0,−1)`: `r = −1`, `s = +1`; the handout's page 2
  prints the labels `r = 1, s = −1` for this vector, which is the interchange of the two signs.
- At `ε = 1/2`: `ℓ = √2` and `E(1/2)/(1/2)⁴ = 8κ/(√2 + 1)² ≈ 1.37κ`, below the limit `2κ`; at
  `ε = 0` the quotient is `0/0 = 0` in Lean, which is why the limit is punctured.
- The rotated configurations have `|p₂ − p₁| = |(cos 2ε, −sin 2ε)| = 1` and derivative
  `(sin 2ε, cos 2ε, −sin 2ε, −cos 2ε) → (0,1,0,−1)`.

### 11.2 Three particles on a line

`H = [[−1,1,0],[1,−2,1],[0,1,−1]]` and `r(x₁, x₂, x₃) = (−x₃, −x₂, −x₁)`. The representation has one
trivial and two sign constituents; `(1,0,−1)`, `(1,1,1)`, `(1,−2,1)` have eigenvalues `−1, 0, −3`.
Identify the trivial line and the sign isotypic plane, and prove that `H` is not scalar on that
plane: an equivariant operator preserves isotypic components and acts on multiplicity spaces, not
necessarily on chosen irreducible copies (knutson-tetrahedron, p. 3). *Needs:* as §11.1.

**Checks.**
- `χ = (3, −1)`: `⟨χ, 1⟩ = (3 − 1)/2 = 1`, `⟨χ, sgn⟩ = (3 + 1)/2 = 2`.
- `H(1,−2,1) = (−3, 6, −3)`; `H(1,1,1) = 0`; `H(1,0,−1) = (−1,0,1)`.
- The vector `(1,0,−1)` is `r`-fixed (`r(1,0,−1) = (1,0,−1)`): the trivial line is the
  "pull outer particles apart" mode, not the uniform translation.

### 11.3 The tetrahedron

Vertices `p₁ = (0,0,0)`, `p₂ = (1,1,0)`, `p₃ = (1,0,1)`, `p₄ = (0,1,1)`, `c = (½,½,½)`, `qᵢ = pᵢ − c`.
Construct the orthogonal action `R_σ` with `R_σ qᵢ = q_{σ(i)}` for `σ ∈ S₄` and the displacement
representation `(σ·u)ᵢ = R_σ u_{σ⁻¹(i)}` on `(ℝ³)⁴`; identify the ambient module with the sum-zero
submodule of the four-point permutation representation with its inner-product normalization. For
each edge `i < j` let `z_{ij} ∈ ℝ¹²` have block `pᵢ − pⱼ` at `i`, its negative at `j`, zero
elsewhere; define `H = −∑_{i<j} z_{ij} z_{ij}ᵀ`, symmetric, negative semidefinite and
`S₄`-equivariant. Each edge has squared length `2`, so physical unit-direction springs give the
acceleration operator `(κ/(2m)) H`, and the pair-spring matrix on the `(1,2)` edge has entries
`±1`.

In the class order `(1⁴), (2,1²), (3,1), (4), (2,2)` the displacement character is `(12, 2, 0, 0, 0)` with decomposition `1 ⊕ E ⊕ 2V ⊕ (sgn ⊗ V)`, `V` the standard three-dimensional module and `E`
the two-dimensional irreducible. Construct the orthogonal decomposition and intertwiners:
translations `uᵢ = t` and infinitesimal rotations `uᵢ = A qᵢ`, `Aᵀ = −A`, give the kernel, of
dimension `6`, of types `V` and `sgn ⊗ V`; diagonal trace-zero symmetric `A` gives the `E`
eigenspace, dimension `2`, eigenvalue `−2`; symmetric `A` with zero diagonal gives the other `V`,
dimension `3`, eigenvalue `−4`; the breathing line `uᵢ = a qᵢ` has eigenvalue `−8`. Every
displacement is uniquely a translation plus `uᵢ = A qᵢ`; rotations are `Λ² V` through the
Euclidean metric; the two copies of `V` are distinguished by translation versus vibration, not by
Schur on their sum. Prove the characteristic polynomial `X⁶ (X+2)² (X+4)³ (X+8)` and the kernel and
eigenspace dimensions; for `m, κ > 0` the six vibrational frequencies are `√(κ/m)` twice, `√(2κ/m)`
three times and `2√(κ/m)` once; construct the solutions of `u″ = (κ/(2m)) H u` on these modes and
the affine solutions on the zero modes (knutson-tetrahedron, pp. 3–5).

Restriction to the vertex stabilizer `S₃` and the square subgroup `D₄ = ⟨(1234), (13)⟩`: the
invariant spaces have dimensions `3` and `2`. For `S₃` construct a fixed vector on the breathing
line and one in each standard copy, and prove that the `S₄`-orbit of the seed `uⱼ = Aᵢ qⱼ`,
`Aᵢ = qᵢ qᵢᵀ − ¼ I` for the fixed vertex `i`, consists of exactly four vectors summing to zero and
spanning the vibrational standard module (the four `Aᵢ` are distinct, sum to zero and span the
off-diagonal symmetric matrices); the translation seeds `uⱼ = qᵢ` give the four-point standard
comparison, and the `S₃`-orbit of the seed is a singleton. For `D₄` construct the fixed line in `E`,
separate it from breathing by orthogonal projection, and prove that the `S₄`-orbit of the seed
`A = diag(−1, 2, −1)` in `uⱼ = A qⱼ` is the three diagonal matrices with one entry `2` and the
others `−1`, summing to zero as their sole linear relation and spanning the trace-zero diagonal
plane; its `D₄`-orbit is a singleton. Give the restriction characters and fixed-space projectors;
these are symmetric spanning sets, not a canonical basis in an irreducible. *Needs:* Layer 3,
§3.5, §10.2; Mathlib matrices.

**Checks.**
- The characteristic polynomial of `H` is `X¹² + 24X¹¹ + 228X¹⁰ + 1104X⁹ + 2880X⁸ + 3840X⁷ + 2048X⁶ = X⁶(X+2)²(X+4)³(X+8)`; `trace H = −24 = −2·2 − 4·3 − 8` and `−12·2 = −24` from the twelve
  diagonal entries `−2`.
- `u = (A qᵢ)ᵢ` with `A = diag(1, −1, 0)` satisfies `H u = −2u`; with `A = [[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,0]]`,
  `H u = −4u`; with `A = I`, `H u = −8u`; with `A = [[0,1,0],[−1,0,0],[0,0,0]]` or `u = (t)ᵢ`,
  `H u = 0`.
- Let `σ = (123)`, `τ = (12)`, with rightmost factor acting first, and let `u` be supported
  only at vertex `1`, where `u₁ = q₄ ≠ 0`. The correct action sends both `σ·(τ·u)` and
  `(στ)·u` to the vector supported at vertex `3`, of value `q₄`. For the wrong rule
  `(B_σ u)ᵢ = R_σ u_{σ(i)}`, `B_σ B_τ u` is supported at vertex `1`, while `B_(στ) u` is
  supported at vertex `3`: the group law fails. A transposition alone equals its inverse
  and cannot distinguish the conventions.
- The `D₄`-seed `diag(−1, 2, −1)` has orbit `{diag(2,−1,−1), diag(−1,2,−1), diag(−1,−1,2)}` under
  `S₄`, whose sum is `0`.

The virtual-character descent theorem requires a characteristic-zero field `K` containing a
primitive `n`-th root `ζ`, `n ≠ 0`, `exponent G ∣ n`, an algebraically closed extension `L`,
and `K`, `L`, `G` in one universe. The separate
`TauCeti.Representation.map_ofCharacter_eq_powMap` requires an algebraically closed coefficient
field, `(n : k) ≠ 0`, `g^n = 1` for every `g`, and an endomorphism raising every `n`-th root to
the same `j`-th power; it does not itself define an action over `K`. The pinned `simple_GL2PrincipalSeries_iff` specializes the field to `Type`; its
arbitrary-universe form is a target of Layer 9.
