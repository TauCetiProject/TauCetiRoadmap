## Where this roadmap stands

**At a glance.** The family is eleven roadmaps, none finished. The semisimple-algebra and
compact-group lanes now carry real theorems; the combinatorial lanes (root systems, symmetric group,
quivers) have deep foundations and no summits; the Lie-theoretic lanes have infrastructure but not
yet a theorem.

### Named results

- **Schur orthogonality for compact groups** — matrix coefficients of inequivalent finite-dimensional
  irreducibles are orthogonal in `L²(G)`
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/SchurOrthogonality.html#TauCeti.ContRepresentation.schur_orthogonality>),
  and both the normalized matrix coefficients and the irreducible characters are orthonormal systems
  there
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Compact/Orthonormal.html#TauCeti.ContRepresentation.orthonormal_characterLp>).
- **The Wedderburn structure of the group algebra** — over an algebraically closed field whose
  characteristic does not divide the order, `k[G]` splits as a product of matrix algebras indexed by
  the conjugacy classes
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/CharacterTable/Wedderburn.html#TauCeti.exists_algEquiv_pi_matrix>).
- **Skolem-Noether** — two algebra maps of a simple algebra into a finite-dimensional central simple
  algebra differ by conjugation by a unit
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/Algebra/CentralSimple/SkolemNoether.html#TauCeti.skolemNoether>).
- **The dominant chamber is a strict fundamental domain** — every weight has exactly one dominant
  weight in its Weyl orbit, and the stabilizer of a dominant weight is generated by the reflections
  in the walls through it
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/RootSystem/FundamentalDomain.html#TauCeti.existsUnique_mem_orbit_inter_dominantChamber>);
  the longest element `w₀` comes with it
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/RootSystem/LongestElement.html#TauCeti.longestElement>).
- **The double centralizer theorem**, with Jacobson-Chevalley density
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/Semisimple/DoubleCentralizer.html#TauCeti.toModuleEnd_moduleEnd_bijective>).

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **The Weyl module and the Schur functor** — the image of a Young symmetrizer on a tensor power of
  the standard representation of `GL n`, depending up to isomorphism only on the shape and nonzero
  exactly when that shape has at most `n` rows
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/ClassicalGroups/WeylModule.html#TauCeti.YoungTableau.weylModule>).
- **The Lie-group exponential map**, from integral curves of left-invariant vector fields, with
  left-invariant derivations identified with the tangent space at the identity
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/Geometry/Lie/Exponential/Basic.html#lieExp>).
- **The graded pieces of the Clifford filtration are exterior powers**
  (<https://taucetiproject.github.io/TauCeti/docs/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Graded.html#TauCeti.CliffordAlgebra.filtrationGradedEquiv>),
  the deformation statement the structure theorem and the spin module rest on.

### Roadmap coverage

Compact groups are done through Layer 4 and much of Layer 6 (both orthogonality relations,
characters, the class functions of `L^p` as a closed subspace); Peter-Weyl and class-function
completeness are untouched, and the `SU(2)` engine has its torus and torus conjugacy but not its
irreducibles. Semisimple algebras hold Layers 2 to 5 in the central simple case and reach Layer 6
only as far as `A ⊗ Aᵐᵒᵖ ≅ Matₙ(K)`; general Artin-Wedderburn with uniqueness and the Brauer group
are absent. Character theory is done through Layer 2 and has the arithmetic of character values from
Layer 6, with Layer 5 half-built, but Layer 3 — table, orthogonality, completeness — is entirely
missing. Root systems cover Layers 1 to 4, including the braid relations of the Coxeter matrix;
Layer 5 has only the `DynkinType` datatype. Induction and restriction hold Layers 0 to 2 plus the
projective groundwork, with the inertia group newly in place for Clifford theory and Mackey
untouched. Classical groups have Layers 0, 1 and the start of Layer 2; Schur-Weyl
has Layers 0 to 3 with standard tableaux and the hook-length formula only for a single row or
column; quivers have Layers 0, 1, 4, the radical of a path algebra, and existence but not uniqueness
in Krull-Schmidt. The highest-weight roadmap has an `sl₂` basis and the `gl n` root-space
decomposition and Borel subalgebra and nothing above; Lie groups have Layer 0; spin representations
have Layer 0 and the map `Pin(Q) → O(Q)`.

## The frontier

- **Peter-Weyl** (compact groups Layer 5). The orthonormal system is in place; what remains is
  density of the representative subalgebra in `C(G)`, for which no convolution-operator machinery
  exists yet. Class-function completeness follows it.
- **Character theory Layer 3** is still the largest gap and the one most work depends on. The
  Wedderburn blocks are counted but not identified with irreducible characters, which is what the
  Dixon-Schneider eigenrow machinery waits on.
- **Highest weights for the Weyl modules** (classical groups Layer 3). Irreducibility, the maximal
  torus and the weight decomposition are missing; the dominant weights of `GL n`, with the
  determinant-twist decomposition into a Young diagram and a power of the determinant, exist as
  vocabulary.
- **The Clifford structure theorem and the Pin double cover** (spin Layers 1 and 2). Surjectivity of
  `Pin(Q) → O(Q)` needs Cartan-Dieudonné, its kernel is uncomputed, and the roadmap routes the
  structure theorem through a Fock model of the spin module that does not exist yet.
- **Krull-Schmidt uniqueness, then Gabriel's theorem** (quiver Layers 2 and 5). Existence of a
  decomposition, Fitting's lemma and the vertex projectives and injectives as bricks are done; the
  exchange argument remains, and Gabriel additionally needs reflection functors on representations.
