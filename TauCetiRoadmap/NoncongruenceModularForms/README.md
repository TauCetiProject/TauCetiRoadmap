# Roadmap: noncongruence subgroups of the modular group and their modular forms

**Summary.** A subgroup of finite index in `PSL₂(ℤ)` is the same thing as a transitive action of `PSL₂(ℤ)`
on a finite set with a marked point, and since `PSL₂(ℤ)` is generated by an element of order
two and an element of order three subject to no further relation, such an action is a pair of
permutations `(σ_S, σ_R)` with `σ_S² = σ_R³ = 1` generating a transitive group. Everything the
LMFDB displays about a finite-index subgroup — its index, its genus, its cusp widths, its
elliptic points, its level, whether it is a congruence subgroup, the monodromy group and the
passport of the Belyi map `X(Γ̄) → X(1)` — is read off those two permutations, and the question
"is this subgroup a congruence subgroup?" is decided by a finite computation on them
(Wohlfahrt's theorem and Hsu's test). This roadmap builds that dictionary, the invariants, the
congruence test, the alternative presentation by Farey symbols, the modular curve of an
arbitrary finite-index subgroup as a Belyi cover of `X(1)`, and the theory of modular forms on
such subgroups as far as the LMFDB's noncongruence tables need it: Fourier expansions in
`q^{1/w}` at every cusp, the unbounded-denominators theorem of Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang stated as
the characterization of congruence forms, Scholl's Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer congruences, and the
failure of the Hecke operators off the congruence closure.

The primary carrier of the combinatorial half is a subgroup `Γ̄ : Subgroup PSL(2, ℤ)` with
`[Γ̄.FiniteIndex]`, where `PSL(2, ℤ)` is Mathlib's
`Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup (Fin 2) ℤ`, the quotient of `SL(2, ℤ)` by its center
`{±1}`. The LMFDB's `noncong` data (`psl2z_index`, `permS`, permR, `permT`) is projective
data, and so is every invariant of the quotient `Γ̄\ℍ`. The analytic half is stated for
`Γ : Subgroup SL(2, ℤ)` with `[Γ.FiniteIndex]`, because modular forms of odd weight see the
difference between `Γ` and `±Γ`; its forms live in Mathlib's `ModularForm (Γ.map (mapGL ℝ)) k`
and nothing here introduces a second form carrier. The two halves meet through the preimage
`±Γ` and the image `Γ̄`, with the index relation `[PSL₂(ℤ) : Γ̄] = [SL₂(ℤ) : ±Γ]`.

Suggested home: `TauCeti/NumberTheory/ModularGroup/` for Layers 0–5 (the triangle-group
presentation, the coset dictionary, the invariants, Wohlfahrt, Hsu, Farey symbols), with the
Belyi-map identification of Layer 6 in `TauCeti/NumberTheory/ModularGroup/Belyi.lean`, and
`TauCeti/NumberTheory/ModularForms/Noncongruence/` for Layers 7–9.

## Scope and ownership

### Owned here

- The identification `Δ(3, 2, ∞) ≅ PSL₂(ℤ)` of the modular group with a triangle group, with
  the generators `S`, `T`, R = ST, `U = ST⁻¹` pinned (Layer 0).
- The **coset dictionary** between finite-index subgroups `Γ̄ ≤ PSL₂(ℤ)` and transitive
  permutation triples `(σ_U, σ_S, σ_T)` with `σ_U³ = σ_S² = 1`, `σ_T σ_S σ_U = 1`, with
  conjugacy of subgroups corresponding to simultaneous conjugation of the permutations
  (Millington), and finiteness of its classes at fixed index (Layer 1).
- The **invariants read from permutations**: cusp widths, number of cusps, elliptic points of
  orders two and three, genus, the monodromy group `PSL₂(ℤ)/core(Γ̄)`, and the theorems
  identifying each with its analytic counterpart on `Γ̄\ℍ` (Layer 2).
- The permutation interpretation of the geometric level, its comparison with signed and
  strict widths, the congruence closure and finite-quotient obstructions (Layer 3).
  ModularFormsPartII W1.3, W1.5–W1.6 and W1.8 own geometric level, intersection/core
  laws and signed Wohlfahrt. The strict-level extension retains signs in SL₂(ℤ).
- **Hsu's congruence criterion** as a finite list of relations on the permutation pair, with the
  Hamilton–Loeffler extension to subgroups of `SL₂(ℤ)` (Layer 4).
- **Farey symbols**: the datum, its generators, Kulkarni's correspondence, and the invariant
  formulas, with the translation to and from the permutation triple (Layer 5).
- The modular curve `X(Γ̄)` of an arbitrary finite-index `Γ̄` as a **Belyi cover** of
  `X(1) ≅ ℙ¹`, with the theorem that its monodromy triple is the modular triple of `Γ̄`
  (Layer 6).
- **Modular forms on finite-index subgroups**: the cusp expansions in `q^{1/w_c}` at every cusp,
  the dimension formulas for `M_k(Γ)` and `S_k(Γ)` at every finite-index `Γ` (ModularForms
  Layer 10C's argument, which that layer runs for congruence subgroups), the congruence
  closure's role, the double-coset operators `Tₚ` on `M_k(Γ)` and Berger's theorem on them
  (Layer 7).
- **Bounded denominators** for congruence forms with coefficients in a number field, the
  statement of the **unbounded-denominators theorem** on this roadmap's carrier, and the
  **Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer congruences** (Layer 8).
- The **LMFDB invariant layer** for the `noncong` tables: every column of
  `noncong_modular_curve`, `noncong_modular_form_space` and `noncong_modular_form` that is a
  mathematical invariant, and the intrinsic label prefix (Layer 9).

## Conventions

- **Matrices and generators.** `S := !![0, −1; 1, 0]` and `T := !![1, 1; 0, 1]` are Mathlib's
  `ModularGroup.S` and `ModularGroup.T`. This roadmap names R := S * T = !![0, −1; 1, 1]
  (order six in `SL₂(ℤ)`, `R³ = −1`; it fixes `ρ = e^{2πi/3}`) and
  `U := S * T⁻¹ = !![0, −1; 1, −1]` (order three in `SL₂(ℤ)`, `U³ = 1`; it fixes `e^{πi/3}`).
  Bars denote images in `PSL₂(ℤ)`: `S̄² = 1`, R̄³ = Ū³ = 1, and Ū = S̄ R̄⁻¹ S̄⁻¹. Other
  sources use `TS`, `(TS)⁻¹` or `(ST)⁻¹` as the order-three generator; each is conjugate in
  `PSL₂(ℤ)` to R̄ or to R̄⁻¹, and Layer 1 records the translation.
- **The triangle group.** `PSL₂(ℤ)` is identified with Tau Ceti's
  `TriangleGroup 3 2 0 = ⟨x, y, z | x³, y², z⁰, z·y·x⟩` by `x ↦ Ū`, `y ↦ S̄`, `z ↦ T̄`
  (`Suggested.lean`: `modularHom`, `modularHom_bijective`). The parameter order `(3, 2, ∞)` rather
  than `(2, 3, ∞)` is forced by the Belyi convention below: the branch point `0` of
  `j/1728` carries the order-three stabilizer. `TriangleGroup 2 3 0` is reached through
  Tau Ceti's `rotate` whenever a source needs it.
- **Cosets and the action.** For `Γ̄ ≤ PSL₂(ℤ)` the coset space is Mathlib's
  `PSL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ̄` of **left** cosets `g Γ̄` with the left-multiplication action
  `h • (g Γ̄) = (h g) Γ̄`, and a **numbering** is an equivalence
  `e : PSL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ̄ ≃ Fin n`. The permutation of `h ∈ PSL₂(ℤ)` is
  `e.permutationRepresentation h : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` (Tau Ceti's
  `Equiv.permutationRepresentation`), a group homomorphism, so permutations compose as
  functions: `(στ)(i) = σ(τ(i))`. The **base point** is `e ⟦1⟧`, and `Γ̄` is its stabilizer.
  Sources that act on right cosets `Γ̄ g` on the right produce the same permutations read in
  the opposite composition order; the translation is the anti-automorphism `g ↦ g⁻¹`, which
  fixes every invariant of Layer 2.
- **The modular triple.** `modularTriple Γ̄ e : TauCeti.PermutationTriple n` is
  `(σ0, σ1, σinf) := (ρ Ū, ρ S̄, ρ T̄)` with `ρ := e.permutationRepresentation`. It satisfies
  Tau Ceti's relation `σinf * σ1 * σ0 = 1` because `T̄ S̄ Ū = T̄ S̄ S̄ T̄⁻¹ = 1`, and
  `σ0³ = σ1² = 1`. The names `permS := ρ S̄ = σ1`, `permT := ρ T̄ = σinf` and
  permR := ρ R̄ = σ1⁻¹ σ0⁻¹ σ1 = permS * permT are the LMFDB's, but its rows (the
  Berghaus–Monien–Radchenko database) are written in the **opposite** convention: right cosets
  `Γ̄ g`, the right action, permutations composed left to right. Read as elements of
  `Equiv.Perm`, a row satisfies permR = permT * permS, not `permS * permT` (their entry
  `15_1_2_1_0_2_a` has `σ_T = σ_R ∘ σ_S ≠ σ_S ∘ σ_R`). A row enters the library through
  `equivOppositeConvention` (1.1): under the numbering of left cosets induced from the row's
  numbering of right cosets by `g Γ̄ ↦ Γ̄ g⁻¹`, the modular triple is
  `(σ0, σ1, σinf) = (permS * permT, permS, permT⁻¹)`, and ρ R̄ = permR⁻¹.
- **Cusp widths are cycle lengths of `σinf`, fixed points included.** A fixed point of
  `σinf` is a cusp of width `1`. The multiset `cuspWidths` therefore has sum `n` and
  cardinality the number of cusps; Mathlib's `Equiv.Perm.cycleType` omits fixed points and is
  never used as the width multiset without the correction `n − cycleType.sum` of width-one
  cusps. The number of cusps is Tau Ceti's `orbitCount σinf`.
- **Elliptic points.** `ε₂ := #Fix(σ1)` and `ε₃ := #Fix(σ0)`, the numbers of fixed points
  of `ρ S̄` and `ρ Ū`. These count `Γ̄`-orbits of elliptic points of `ℍ` of periods `2` and
  `3`, and are the `nu2`, `nu3` of the literature and the `n_e2`, `n_e3` of the LMFDB.
- **Genus** is Tau Ceti's `PermutationTriple.genus` of the modular triple, defined from the
  Euler characteristic `χ = orbitCount σ0 + orbitCount σ1 + orbitCount σinf − n` by
  `χ = 2 − 2g`. That this is the genus of the compact Riemann surface `X(Γ̄)` is a theorem of
  Layer 2, not a definition.
- **Finite reduction.** For N ≥ 1 write Q_N = SL₂(ℤ/N)/⟨−I⟩, the quotient by the
  image of the two integral scalar matrices. It need not be Mathlib's quotient by
  the full center: at N = 8 the two orders are 192 and 96, respectively.
- **The general level** `N(Γ̄) := orderOf σinf`, equal to the least common multiple of the
  cusp widths (Layer 3). For `Γ ≤ SL₂(ℤ)` the **strict level** `N_s(Γ)` is the least common
  multiple of the strict widths, the least `h > 0` with `σ T^h σ⁻¹ ∈ Γ` at the cusp `σ∞`;
  `N_s(Γ) ∈ {N(Γ̄), 2 N(Γ̄)}`, and the two agree when `−1 ∈ Γ`.
- **Congruence** is Mathlib's `IsCongruenceSubgroup` on `Subgroup SL(2, ℤ)`. For
  `Γ̄ ≤ PSL₂(ℤ)` the statement "`Γ̄` is a congruence subgroup" means
  `IsCongruenceSubgroup (±Γ̄)` where `±Γ̄ := Γ̄.comap (QuotientGroup.mk' _)` is `lift Γ̄` in
  `Suggested.lean`. A subgroup `Γ ≤ SL₂(ℤ)` with `−1 ∉ Γ` may fail to be congruence while
  `±Γ` is congruence; the two notions are never conflated.
- **Hsu's generators.** Hsu's test is stated in terms of `L := T` and `R_H := !![1, 0; 1, 1]`,
  and `R_H = S⁻¹ T⁻¹ S`, so that `ρ L̄ = σinf` and ρ R̄_H = ρ(Ū S̄) = σ0 * σ1. The names
  `L`, `R_H` are reserved for this layer; R without subscript is `S T`.
- **Farey symbols** are written as increasing sequences of reduced fractions with the two
  ends `−∞ = (−1)/0` and `∞ = 1/0`, consecutive terms unimodular, and side labels `even`,
  `odd` or a free label occurring exactly twice, with the nondegeneracy condition of §5.1; the generator attached to a side is the
  explicit matrix of Layer 5, and the polygon lies **above** the broken boundary.
- **Modular forms** on `Γ ≤ SL₂(ℤ)` are Mathlib's `ModularForm (Γ.map (mapGL ℝ)) k` and
  `CuspForm (Γ.map (mapGL ℝ)) k`, with the slash action and all conventions of the modular
  forms roadmap. The **expansion at `∞`** of `f` is `qExpansion w f` with
  `w := (Γ.map (mapGL ℝ)).strictWidthInfty`, the strict width (so an irregular cusp in odd
  weight expands in `q^{1/2w}` with only odd exponents, exactly as in ModularForms Layer 1),
  and the expansion at a cusp `c = σ∞`, `σ ∈ SL₂(ℤ)`, is the expansion at `∞` of
  `f ∣[k] σ`, a form on `σ⁻¹ Γ σ`. "Fourier coefficients of `f`" without qualification means
  the coefficients `a_m(f)` of `qExpansion w f` at `∞`.
- **Bounded denominators** means: there is `D ∈ ℕ`, `D ≠ 0`, with `D · a_m(f) ∈ ℤ` for all
  `m`. It is stated inline as that hypothesis, never as a named predicate.
- **The Belyi map** of `Γ̄` is `J := j/1728 : X(Γ̄) → ℙ¹`, unramified outside `{0, 1, ∞}`, with
  `j` the normalized level-one invariant of ModularForms (`j(ρ) = 0`, `j(i) = 1728`), so that
  its monodromy triple in the Belyi maps roadmap's ordering `(σ0 over 0, σ1 over 1, σinf
  over ∞)` is the modular triple: order-three monodromy over `J = 0`, order-two over `J = 1`.
- **Permutations in prose** are written in cycle notation on `{1, …, n}`; the worked examples
  number the cosets so that the base coset is point `1` unless stated otherwise.

## Layer 0: the modular group as a triangle group

### 0.1 The pinned generators

Define R := S * T and `U := S * T⁻¹` in `SL(2, ℤ)`, and prove by computation (`decide`
works on `SL(2, ℤ)` equalities, as Mathlib's `S_inv` shows): `S ^ 2 = -1`, `S ^ 4 = 1`,
R ^ 3 = -1, R ^ 6 = 1, `U ^ 3 = 1`, S * R⁻¹ * S⁻¹ = -U, T = S⁻¹ * R = -S * R,
`T⁻¹ = S⁻¹ * U`, R * U⁻¹ = S * T ^ 2 * S⁻¹, so that each of S, T, R, U is expressed in the
others with its sign recorded. Prove `-1 ∈ Subgroup.center SL(2, ℤ)`
and that the center is exactly `{1, -1}` (`Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.mem_center_iff`;
`Nat.card (Subgroup.center SL(2, ℤ)) = 2`). Define the images S̄, T̄, R̄, Ū : PSL(2, ℤ) through
`toPSL := QuotientGroup.mk' (Subgroup.center SL(2, ℤ))` and prove `S̄ ^ 2 = 1`, R̄ ^ 3 = 1,
`Ū ^ 3 = 1`, `T̄ * S̄ * Ū = 1`, R̄ = S̄ * T̄, Ū = S̄ * R̄⁻¹ * S̄⁻¹, and that `S̄, T̄` generate
`PSL(2, ℤ)` (image of `SL2Z_generators`). Prove `orderOf S̄ = 2`, orderOf R̄ = 3,
`orderOf T̄ = 0`.

*Needs:* Mathlib only.

The projection and the three projective generators are `toPSL`, `Sbar`, `Tbar` and `Ubar`; the integral order-three generator is `U`.

**Checks.**

- S² = −I, U³ = I and TSU = −I; after projection these are all identity relations.
- R³ = −I while U³ = I, distinguishing the two integral lifts of order-three projective generators.
- T² = [[1,2],[0,1]] differs from I in PSL₂(ℤ); no finite-order relation is imposed on the cusp generator.

### 0.2 The homomorphism from the triangle group

`modularHom : TriangleGroup 3 2 0 →* PSL(2, ℤ)` is Tau Ceti's `TriangleGroup.lift Ū S̄ T̄`
with the four relations of 0.1 (`z ^ 0 = 1` is trivial). Prove `modularHom x = Ū`,
`modularHom y = S̄`, `modularHom z = T̄`, and surjectivity from the generation statement of
0.1 together with `TriangleGroup.range_eq_closure`.

*Needs:* 0.1; Tau Ceti `TriangleGroup.lift`, `range_eq_closure`.

**Checks.**

- The images of x, y, z have projective orders 3, 2, ∞ respectively.
- The reversed product TSU projects from −I to 1; the integral product is not I.
- The trivial homomorphism sends T to 1, whereas this map sends it to the nonidentity translation T̄.

### 0.3 Injectivity: `PSL₂(ℤ) = ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/3`

Prove `Function.Injective modularHom`, hence an isomorphism TriangleGroup 3 2 0 ≃* PSL(2, ℤ)
by `MulEquiv.ofBijective`. The route is the ping-pong lemma on the action of `PSL₂(ℤ)` on
`ℙ¹(ℚ) = OnePoint ℚ`, in Alperin's form: with `X₊ := {q > 0}` and `X₋ := {q < 0}`, `S̄`
maps `X₋` into `X₊`, while R̄ and R̄² both map `X₊` into `X₋` (for `q > 0`,
R̄ • q = −1/(q + 1) ∈ (−1, 0) and R̄² • q = −1 − 1/q < −1). The two disjoint nonempty sets
and the two cyclic subgroups `⟨S̄⟩ ≅ ℤ/2`, ⟨R̄⟩ ≅ ℤ/3 satisfy the hypotheses of Mathlib's
`Monoid.CoprodI.lift_injective_of_ping_pong` (its cardinality hypothesis is met by `|ℤ/3| = 3`),
which gives that
`Monoid.CoprodI (fun i : Fin 2 ↦ [Multiplicative (ZMod 2), Multiplicative (ZMod 3)] i) →* PSL(2, ℤ)`
is injective; compose with the isomorphism of the free product with the two-generator
presentation `⟨x, y | x³, y²⟩`, which is `TriangleGroup 3 2 0` by Tau Ceti's
`equivTwoGenerator` (the relator `(y·x)⁰` is trivial). The exact ping-pong sets must be
checked by computation on `OnePoint ℚ`: `S̄ • q = -1/q`, R̄ • q = -1/(q+1),
R̄² • q = -(q+1)/q. A reduced-word argument in the style of Serre, *Trees*, I.4.2, is an
acceptable alternative; the statement is the same.

Corollaries, each a named theorem: `PSL(2, ℤ) ≃* Monoid.CoprodI` of `ℤ/2` and `ℤ/3`; every
element of finite order in `PSL(2, ℤ)` is conjugate to `1`, `S̄`, R̄ or R̄² (torsion in a
free product); every torsion-free subgroup of finite index is free, of rank `1 + n/6` where
`n` is its index (not needed below but the standard companion statement, and the
`n ≡ 0 (mod 6)` constraint it carries); and the abelianization `PSL(2, ℤ)ᵃᵇ ≃+ ZMod 6`.

*Needs:* 0.2; Mathlib `Monoid.CoprodI.lift_injective_of_ping_pong`; Tau Ceti
`equivTwoGenerator`; FreeGroupsAndLimitGroups §4.11 (Kurosh). *Source:* Serre, *Trees*, Chapter I, §4.2; Alperin 1993.

The freeness headline is `exists_freeGroupBasis_of_torsionFree`.

### 0.4 Lifts and the projective index

For `Γ̄ : Subgroup PSL(2, ℤ)` define `lift Γ̄ := Γ̄.comap toPSL` (the group written `±Γ̄`) and
prove: `-1 ∈ lift Γ̄`; `(lift Γ̄).map toPSL = Γ̄`; `(lift Γ̄).index = Γ̄.index`
(`Subgroup.index_comap_of_surjective`); `lift` is an order embedding from subgroups of
`PSL(2, ℤ)` onto the subgroups of `SL(2, ℤ)` containing `-1`, with inverse `map toPSL`;
`lift` commutes with conjugation. For `Γ : Subgroup SL(2, ℤ)` define
psl2Index Γ := (Γ.map toPSL).index and prove psl2Index Γ = Γ.index if `-1 ∈ Γ`
(`Subgroup.index_map_eq`) and 2 * psl2Index Γ = Γ.index otherwise (`Γ ⊔ center` has index
`2` over `Γ`). `Γ.FiniteIndex ↔ (Γ.map toPSL).FiniteIndex`. The LMFDB's `psl2z_index` is
psl2Index.

*Needs:* 0.1; Mathlib index API.

**Checks.**

- The lift of the full projective group is SL₂(ℤ), with both indices 1.
- The lift of the trivial projective group is {±I}, not {I}; both I and −I project to 1.
- Γ(3) excludes −I: its integral index is 24 and its projective index is 12; adjoining −I gives integral index 12.

### Examples

The projective image of Γ(2) is torsion-free of index 6 and free rank 2. Its lift contains −I; Γ(3) has integral index 24 and projective index 12.

## Layer 1: the coset dictionary

### 1.1 The modular triple

For `Γ̄ : Subgroup PSL(2, ℤ)` with `[Γ̄.FiniteIndex]` and a numbering
`e : PSL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ̄ ≃ Fin n`, define `modularTriple Γ̄ e : PermutationTriple n` as in the
conventions, with `ρ := e.permutationRepresentation`. Prove `σ0 ^ 3 = 1`, `σ1 ^ 2 = 1`,
`σinf = ρ T̄`, the three LMFDB permutations `permS := ρ S̄ = σ1`, `permT := ρ T̄ = σinf`,
permR := ρ R̄ with the identities permR = permS * permT (since R̄ = S̄ T̄) and
permR = permS⁻¹ * σ0⁻¹ * permS (since R̄ = S̄⁻¹ Ū⁻¹ S̄), `n = Γ̄.index`
(`Subgroup.index_eq_card` and `Fintype.card_fin`), and `n ≠ 0`.

- *Comparison with `cosetTriple`.* Through the isomorphism of §0.3,
  `modularTriple Γ̄ e = TriangleGroup.cosetTriple (Γ̄.comap modularHom) e'` for the numbering
  `e'` of `TriangleGroup 3 2 0 ⧸ Γ̄.comap modularHom` obtained from `e` through the coset
  coset bijection along that isomorphism (`Γ̄` need not be normal); in particular `modularTriple` is connected
  (`isConnected_cosetTriple`), its base point `e ⟦1⟧` has stabilizer `Γ̄` under
  `toPerm` (`comap_stabilizer_toPerm_cosetTriple`), and the kernel of `ρ` is `Γ̄.normalCore`
  (`ker_toPerm_cosetTriple`, Mathlib `normalCore_eq_ker`).
- *Independence of the numbering.* `(modularTriple Γ̄ e).Equivalent (modularTriple Γ̄ e')`
  for any two numberings (`equivalent_cosetTriple`), so modularIsoClass Γ̄ : IsoClass n is
  well defined.
- *Monodromy group.* `(modularTriple Γ̄ e).monodromyGroup = ρ.range ≅ PSL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ̄.normalCore`
  (first isomorphism theorem), of order `[PSL₂(ℤ) : core(Γ̄)]`; it is the LMFDB's
  `monodromy_group`. `Γ̄` is normal iff the monodromy group has order `n` iff the triple is
  regular in the sense of the Belyi maps roadmap's 4.6.
- *The opposite conventions.* Under the bijection `g Γ̄ ↦ Γ̄ g⁻¹` from left to right cosets,
  the right action of `h` on right cosets corresponds to the left action of `h⁻¹`, so the
  permutations a source reading right cosets `Γ̄ g` with the right action attaches to
  `S̄, Ū, T̄` are componentwise the inverses of `σ1, σ0, σinf` (transported along that
  bijection), composed in the opposite order; this is Tau Ceti's `equivOppositeConvention`,
  and it is the translation an LMFDB row goes through: the row (permS, permR, permT) gives
  `(permS * permT, permS, permT⁻¹)` (Conventions).
  The triple of the mirror `J Γ̄ J⁻¹`, `J = diag(1, −1) ∈ GL₂(ℤ)`, is equivalent to
  `(σ1 σ0⁻¹ σ1⁻¹, σ1, σinf⁻¹)`, because `J⁻¹ S J = S⁻¹`, `J⁻¹ T J = T⁻¹` and
  J⁻¹ U J = -R. Both are proved as equalities of triples after the stated relabeling,
  since every source uses one of them.

*Needs:* Layer 0; Tau Ceti `cosetTriple` and its theorems; `Equiv.permutationRepresentation`.

The action homomorphism is `ρ`; its specializations are `modularTriple`, `permS` and `permT`.

**Checks.**

- On the full group the triple is (1,1,1), with one marked coset.
- On Γ₀(2), with the numbering in the worked examples, σ₁ = (1 2), σ₀ = (1 3 2), σ∞ = (1 3), and σ∞σ₁σ₀ = 1.
- The normal index-3 subgroup has σ₁ = 1 and σ∞ a 3-cycle; thus the monodromy group has order 3 = index, whereas Γ₀(2) has monodromy order 6 > 3.

### 1.2 Millington's theorem

- **Conjugacy.** For finite-index `Γ̄, Γ̄'` with numberings `e, e'` of the same degree `n`,
  `(modularTriple Γ̄ e).Equivalent (modularTriple Γ̄' e') ↔ ∃ g : ConjAct PSL(2, ℤ), g • Γ̄ = Γ̄'`.
  Forward: an equivalence `τ` conjugates `ρ` to `ρ'`; the stabilizer of `τ (e ⟦1⟧)` under
  `ρ'` is `Γ̄`, and point stabilizers of a transitive action are conjugate
  (`MulAction.stabilizer_smul_eq_stabilizer_map_conj`). Backward: conjugation by `g` carries
  cosets to cosets and gives a relabeling.
- **Surjectivity.** Every connected `t : PermutationTriple n` with `t.σ0 ^ 3 = 1` and
  `t.σ1 ^ 2 = 1` is equivalent to `modularTriple Γ̄ e` for `Γ̄` the stabilizer of a point under
  `toPerm t` transported along the isomorphism of §0.3 (`equivalent_cosetTriple_of_comap_stabilizer_eq`),
  and `Γ̄.index = n`.
- **The bijection.** Conjugacy classes of subgroups of index `n` in `PSL(2, ℤ)` biject with
  isomorphism classes of connected triples of degree `n` with `σ0 ^ 3 = σ1 ^ 2 = 1`; stated as
  an `Equiv` between the quotient of `{Γ̄ // Γ̄.index = n}` by conjugacy and the subtype of
  `IsoClass n` cut out by the (class-invariant) order conditions.
- **Subgroups, not classes.** The finer statement: subgroups of index `n` biject with
  connected triples of degree `n` with the order conditions **and a marked point**, modulo
  relabelings fixing the marked point, i.e. with transitive `PSL₂(ℤ)`-sets of size `n` with a
  marked point. Record the count: the number of subgroups of index `n` is
  `Σ_{classes} n / #Aut`, with `#Aut` the automorphism group of the triple, which is the
  normalizer quotient `N(Γ̄)/Γ̄`.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib stabilizer-conjugacy lemmas. *Source:* Millington 1969, Theorem 2, pp.351–357; Kurth–Long, arXiv:0710.1835v1, §4.

The headline signatures are `millington`, `exists_modularTriple_equivalent` and `card_subgroups_of_class`.

## Layer 2: the invariants read from the permutations

is the arithmetic subgroup of `GL(2, ℝ)` carrying the analytic statements.

### 2.1 Cusp widths and the number of cusps

Define `cuspWidths Γ̄ e : Multiset ℕ := σinf.fullCycleType`
and `cuspCount Γ̄ e := orbitCount σinf`. The generic sum and positivity laws are
`Equiv.Perm.sum_fullCycleType` and `Equiv.Perm.pos_of_mem_fullCycleType`; the card
is the orbit count. Prove the geometric identifications below; both specializations are
independent of `e` and invariant under conjugation of `Γ̄`, and `cuspWidths` is determined by
the isomorphism class of the modular triple.

- **The width at `∞`.** The length of the cycle of `σinf` through the base point is
  `Subgroup.integerCuspWidth Γ_ℝ`, which equals `Γ_ℝ.strictWidthInfty = Γ_ℝ.widthInfty` (the
  group contains `-1`): the coset `T̄^j Γ̄` equals `Γ̄` iff `T^j ∈ lift Γ̄` iff
  `integerCuspWidth ∣ j` (`T_pow_mem_iff`).
- **The width at a cusp.** For `σ ∈ SL(2, ℤ)` the length of the cycle of `σinf` through
  the coset `e ⟦σ̄⁻¹ Γ̄⟧` equals the integer width at `∞`
  of the conjugate `(σ⁻¹ (lift Γ̄) σ).map (mapGL ℝ)`, which is the width of the cusp `σ∞` of
  `Γ̄` in the sense of Fuchsian orbifolds' `CuspDatum.width` for the cusp datum of the image
  of `Γ̄` in `PSL(2, ℝ)` at `σ∞` whose scaling is the projective image of σ⁻¹. The cycle of a coset `g Γ̄` under `σinf` corresponds to the
  double coset `Γ̄ g⁻¹ ⟨T̄⟩`: the map from cycles of `σinf` to `Γ̄\PSL₂(ℤ)/⟨T̄⟩` is a bijection
  carrying cycle length to the number of right cosets in the double coset.
- **Cusps are cusp orbits.** `cuspCount Γ̄ e = Nat.card (CuspOrbits Γ_ℝ)`: Mathlib's
  `cosetToCuspOrbit` is the surjection `SL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ_ℝ.comap (mapGL ℝ) → CuspOrbits Γ_ℝ`,
  `⟦g⟧ ↦ [g⁻¹ • ∞]`, and two cosets have the same image iff they differ by left
  multiplication by `±T^j`, i.e. lie in the same cycle of `σinf`. The
  same count equals `Nat.card (CuspOrbit Γ_ℙ)` for the image `Γ_ℙ` of `Γ̄` in `PSL(2, ℝ)`,
  through Fuchsian orbifolds' `CuspOrbit` and the theorem that the cusp points of a
  finite-index subgroup of `PSL₂(ℤ)` are exactly `ℙ¹(ℚ)` (`isCusp_iff_isCusp_SL2Z`,
  `isCusp_SL2Z_iff`, and `isCuspPoint_iff_of_isFiniteRelIndex`).
- **Width ratios.** For `Γ̄' ≤ Γ̄` of finite index and a cusp `c`, using the same scaling
  for both cusp data, the width of `c` for `Γ̄'`
  is the width for `Γ̄` times the ramification index of `X(Γ̄') → X(Γ̄)` over `c`, the
  Fuchsian roadmap's `exists_width_eq_nat_mul`; in permutation terms, the cycles of `σinf'`
  over a cycle of `σinf` have lengths that are multiples of it, summing to its length times
  the fibre size.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `CuspOrbits`; Tau Ceti `integerCuspWidth`, Fuchsian cusp data.
*Source:* Diamond–Shurman §3.8; Shimura §1.6.

### 2.2 Elliptic points

Define `ellipticTwo Γ̄ e := #{i | σ1 i = i}` and `ellipticThree Γ̄ e := #{i | σ0 i = i}`. Prove

### 2.3 The genus

### 2.5 Congruence subgroups as permutation data

For `N ≠ 0`, `PSL₂(ℤ)` acts on `ℙ¹(ℤ/N)` (pairs `(x : y)` with `gcd(x, y, N) = 1` modulo
units) through reduction, and the stabilizer of `(1 : 0)` is `Γ̄₀(N)`; so
`modularTriple Γ̄₀(N)` is the triple of that action under a numbering of `ℙ¹(ℤ/N)`, with
`σinf = (x : y) ↦ (x + y : y)`, `σ1 = (x : y) ↦ (−y : x)`, `σ0 = (x : y) ↦ (−y : x − y)`.
Prove it, and derive from it: `[PSL₂(ℤ) : Γ̄₀(N)] = N ∏_{p ∣ N} (1 + 1/p)`
(Tau Ceti's `Gamma0Projective_index`), the cusp widths of `Γ̄₀(N)`: the cusps of
`Γ̄₀(N)` correspond to pairs `(d, u)` with `d ∣ N` and `u ∈ (ℤ/gcd(d, N/d))ˣ`, the cusp `u/d`
having width `N/gcd(d², N) = N/(d · gcd(d, N/d))`, so there are `Σ_{d ∣ N} φ(gcd(d, N/d))`
cusps (Diamond–Shurman §3.8 and Corollary 3.7.2; Tau Ceti's `Cusp/Width.lean` and
`Cusp/Count.lean`) — and the elliptic counts of 2.2. Likewise for `Γ̄(N)`:
cosets are `Q_N`, so `n = |Q_N|`, every cusp has width `N` and there are
`n/N` of them, `ε₂ = ε₃ = 0` for `N ≥ 2`, and `|Q_N| = (N³/2) ∏_{p ∣ N} (1 − p⁻²)` for
`N ≥ 3`, `= 6` for `N = 2`, `= 1` for `N = 1`; these are the theorems index Gamma,
card SL zmod and `index_Gamma_map_toPSL` the order obstruction of 3.5 uses. The comparison with the adelic-images roadmap's cusp data is
the statement that for `Γ̄ ⊇ Γ̄(N)` the modular triple is the triple of the action of
`Q_N` on the cosets of the image of `Γ̄`, so both roadmaps compute the same multiset.

*Needs:* 1.1, 2.1–2.3; Mathlib `Gamma`, `Gamma0`, `ZMod`. *Source:* Diamond–Shurman §3.8,
§1.2 (the index of `Γ(N)`); Shimura §1.6.

- At N = 8, SL₂ has order 384; quotienting by ±I gives 192, whereas quotienting by the four-element center gives 96.
- At N = 12, SL₂ has order 1152; the two quotient orders are 576 and 288.

## Layer 3: the general level and Wohlfahrt's theorem

### 3.1 The level

`generalLevel Γ̄ e := orderOf σinf`. Prove `generalLevel = (cuspWidths Γ̄ e).lcm`, that it is
positive, independent of `e` and conjugation-invariant, that `T^N ∈ lift Γ̄` for `N` the level,
that every conjugate `σ T^N σ⁻¹` (`σ ∈ SL₂(ℤ)`) lies in `lift Γ̄`, and that `N` is the least
positive integer with that property: `generalLevel Γ̄ e` is the least `N > 0` such that the
normal closure of `T^N` in `SL(2, ℤ)` is contained in `lift Γ̄`. Record `N ≤ n` is false in
general (`{4, 3}` at index `7` has level `12`) and `N ∣ lcm(1, …, n)` is true.

For `Γ : Subgroup SL(2, ℤ)` of finite index define the **strict level** `strictLevel Γ` as the
least `N > 0` with `σ T^N σ⁻¹ ∈ Γ` for all `σ`, prove it exists, equals `lcm` over the cusps
of the strict widths (the `strictWidthInfty` of the conjugates), equals `generalLevel` of the
image when `-1 ∈ Γ`, and in general divides `2 · generalLevel (Γ.map toPSL)` with quotient
`1` or `2` (`relIndex_strictPeriods`). The width of the image `Γ.map toPSL` at `σ∞` is the
least `m > 0` with `σ T^m σ⁻¹ ∈ ±Γ`, equivalently `±σ T^m σ⁻¹ ∈ Γ` (the *signed* width of
`Γ`), and the strict width of `Γ` at `σ∞` is that number or twice it; so `N(Γ.map toPSL)` is
the `lcm` of the signed widths of `Γ`, and adjoining `−1` does not change it.

**Intersections and normal cores.** The level is the order of `T̄` in `PSL₂(ℤ)/core(Γ̄)`
(`ker ρ = Γ̄.normalCore`, 1.1), and `core(Γ̄₁ ⊓ Γ̄₂) = core(Γ̄₁) ⊓ core(Γ̄₂)`. Prove, for
finite-index `Γ̄, Γ̄₁, Γ̄₂ ≤ PSL₂(ℤ)`: the width of `Γ̄₁ ⊓ Γ̄₂` at each cusp `σ∞` is the `lcm`
of the widths of `Γ̄₁` and of `Γ̄₂` at `σ∞`; `N(Γ̄₁ ⊓ Γ̄₂) = lcm(N(Γ̄₁), N(Γ̄₂))`
(generalLevel inf); and `N(Γ̄.normalCore) = N(Γ̄)` (generalLevel normalCore). The strict
level satisfies the same two identities for finite-index `Γ, Γ₁, Γ₂ ≤ SL(2, ℤ)` with no
hypothesis on `−1` (strictLevel inf, strictLevel normalCore): `{m ∈ ℤ | σ T^m σ⁻¹ ∈ Γ}`
is a subgroup of `ℤ`, and `N_s(Γ)` generates the intersection of these subgroups over all `σ`.

*Needs:* 2.1, 0.4, 1.1; Mathlib `strictPeriods`, `IsRegularAtInfty`, `Subgroup.normalCore`.

**Checks.**

- For the full group both levels are 1; for Γ(2) both are 2.
- The width multiset {4,3} has sum 7 and lcm 12, refuting a level-at-most-index bound.
- The subgroup {γ ∈ Γ(2) : aγ ≡ 1 mod 4} has signed level 2 and strict level 4, distinguishing the two definitions.

### 3.4 The congruence closure

### 3.5 The order obstruction

If `lift Γ̄` is congruence of level `N`, then `Γ̄ ⊇ Γ̄(N)` and `Γ̄.index ∣ [PSL₂(ℤ) : Γ̄(N)]`, so

```text
IsCongruenceSubgroup (lift Γ̄) → Γ̄.index ∣ ((Gamma N).map toPSL).index ,
```

with the right-hand side computed by 2.5. Contrapositive: whenever `n ∤ (N³/2) ∏ (1 − p⁻²)`,
`Γ̄` is noncongruence, with no further computation. This settles every noncongruence class of
index `7`: their levels are `6, 6, 10, 12` and `7 ∤ 72, 360, 576`. It does **not** settle the
index-`8`, level-`8` classes (`8 ∣ 192`); the two forms below, which read the monodromy group
rather than the index, settle the noncongruence ones, and since no obstruction ever proves a
subgroup congruent, Layer 4 is the criterion. State also the
group-theoretic form: when `Γ̄` is congruence of level `N`, the monodromy group of `Γ̄` is a
quotient of `Q_N`, hence its order divides `|Q_N|`; in every case it is
generated by `σ1` and `σ0`, of orders dividing `2` and `3`, with `orderOf (σ1 * σ0) = N`
(as `σ1 σ0 = σinf⁻¹`, 3.1).

**The composition-factor obstruction.** If `lift Γ̄` is congruence of level `N`, then every
nonabelian simple composition factor of the monodromy group `PSL₂(ℤ)/core(Γ̄)` is isomorphic
to `PSL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` for a prime `ℓ ≥ 5` dividing `N`, and the factors of one composition series
come from distinct primes. In Lean a composition factor is a section `H ⧸ K` of the monodromy
group with `H` subnormal (Mathlib's `Subgroup.IsSubnormal`), `K ≤ H` normal in `H` and
`IsSimpleGroup (H ⧸ K)` (`compositionFactor_of_isCongruenceSubgroup`). Proof: the monodromy
group is a quotient of `PSL₂(ℤ)/Γ̄(N) ≅ SL₂(ℤ/N)/{±1}` (3.3, and `ker ρ = core(Γ̄)` of 1.1),
so by Jordan–Hölder its composition factors are among those of
`SL₂(ℤ/N) ≅ ∏_{p^e ∥ N} SL₂(ℤ/p^e)`; the kernel of `SL₂(ℤ/p^e) → SL₂(𝔽_p)` is a `p`-group,
`SL₂(𝔽₂) ≅ S₃` and `SL₂(𝔽₃)` are solvable, and for `p ≥ 5` the composition factors of
`SL₂(𝔽_p)` are `ℤ/2` and `PSL₂(𝔽_p)`, simple by Mathlib's
`Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup.rank_two_simple`. Contrapositive: a nonabelian simple
composition factor of the monodromy group that is not some `PSL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` with `ℓ ∣ N` makes `Γ̄`
noncongruence. Two cases recur: `A_n` for `n ≥ 6` (simple by Mathlib's
`alternatingGroup.isSimpleGroup`) is no `PSL₂(𝔽_ℓ)`, since `ℓ ∣ n!/2` forces `ℓ ≤ n` and then
`ℓ(ℓ² − 1)/2 < n³ ≤ n!/2`, so a subgroup of index `n ≥ 6` with monodromy group `A_n` or `S_n`
is noncongruence; and `PSL₂(𝔽_ℓ)` with `ℓ ∤ N` is excluded even when its order divides
`|Q_N|` (`A₅ ≅ PSL₂(𝔽₅)` has order `60 ∣ 660 = |PSL₂(𝔽₁₁)|`, yet is no composition
factor of a quotient of `PSL₂(ℤ/11)`).

*Needs:* 3.3, 2.5, 1.1; Mathlib `Subgroup.IsSubnormal`, `IsSimpleGroup`,
`Matrix.ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup.rank_two_simple`, `alternatingGroup.isSimpleGroup`.

## Layer 4: Hsu's congruence test

Let `N := generalLevel Γ̄ e`, `L := σinf = ρ L̄` and R_H := σ0 * σ1 = ρ R̄_H
(`R_H = !![1, 0; 1, 1] = S⁻¹ T⁻¹ S`, so `L` and `R_H` generate the monodromy group). Write
`a^{1/k}` for `a^{h}` where `h` is the inverse of `k` modulo the relevant modulus
(`(k : ZMod m)⁻¹.val`); `L^N = R_H^N = 1`.

### 4.1 The principle

`IsCongruenceSubgroup (lift Γ̄)` iff `Γ̄.normalCore ⊇ Γ̄(N)` iff the monodromy group
`⟨L, R_H⟩ ≅ PSL₂(ℤ)/core(Γ̄)` is a quotient of `Q_N` compatibly with the generators,
iff `L, R_H` satisfy the defining relations of a presentation of `Q_N` on the
generators L̄, R̄_H (with `L^N = R_H^N = 1` automatic). The first equivalence is 3.3 and the
normality of `Gamma N`; the second is the universal property of a presented group. This
identifies the congruence question to **finitely many word equalities in `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`**,
hence to a finite relation criterion.

*Needs:* 3.3, 1.1.

### 4.2 The presentations of `Q_N` and the test

The milestone owns the three presentations (odd `N`, `N = 2^k`, mixed) in the generators
`L, R_H`, with proofs that the displayed relations hold in `Q_N` (those of the two
even cases already in `SL₂(ℤ/N)`) and generate all relations, and the resulting theorem `hsu : IsCongruenceSubgroup (lift Γ̄) ↔ hsuTest L R_H N`
where `hsuTest` is the conjunction of the following equalities. Modular inverses
are inverses in `ZMod`; the mixed exponents are CRT residues, not search procedures:

- **`N` odd:** `(R_H² · L^{−1/2})³ = 1`, with `1/2` the inverse of `2` modulo `N`.
- **`N = 2^k`, `k ≥ 1`:** with `S_H := L^{20} R_H^{1/5} L^{−4} R_H^{−1}` (`1/5` the inverse of
  `5` modulo `N`),
  - (A1) `(L R_H⁻¹ L)⁻¹ · S_H · (L R_H⁻¹ L) · S_H = 1`,
  - (A2) `S_H⁻¹ R_H S_H = R_H^{25}`,
  - (A3) `(S_H R_H⁵ L R_H⁻¹ L)³ = (L R_H⁻¹ L)²`.
- **`N = e · m` with `e = 2^k ≥ 2` and `m ≥ 3` odd:** let `c ≡ 1 (mod m)`, `c ≡ 0 (mod e)`,
  `d ≡ 0 (mod m)`, `d ≡ 1 (mod e)` (Chinese remainder), `a := L^c`, `b := R_H^c`, `l := L^d`,
  `r := R_H^d`, `s := l^{20} r^{1/5} l^{−4} r^{−1}` (`1/5` modulo `e`), and `1/2` modulo `m`;
  - (B1) `a⁻¹ r⁻¹ a r = 1`,
  - (B2) `(a b⁻¹ a)⁴ = 1`,
  - (B3) `(a b⁻¹ a)² · (a⁻¹ b)³ = 1`,
  - (B4) `(a b⁻¹ a)² = (b² a^{−1/2})³`,
  - (B5) `(l⁻¹ r l⁻¹) · s · (l r⁻¹ l) · s = 1`,
  - (B6) `s⁻¹ r s = r^{25}`,
  - (B7) `(l r⁻¹ l)² = (s r⁵ l r⁻¹ l)³`.

The words are read in the group `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` with Mathlib's multiplication, where
`ρ` is a homomorphism, so a word evaluates to `ρ` of the same word in the matrices. The test
is insensitive to the coset convention: in the right-coset convention, with permutations
composed left to right, a word evaluates to the inverse of its left-convention value
(transported along `g Γ̄ ↦ Γ̄ g⁻¹`), so each relation holds in one convention iff it holds
in the other. The 2-power and mixed relations use
`R_H^{25}` and `L^{20}`, not misprints: they come from Behr–Mennicke-style presentations of
`SL₂(ℤ/2^k)`. In the odd case the single relation is the whole test; the word
`(R_H² L^{−1/2})³` evaluates to `−1` in `SL₂(ℤ/N)` for the generating matrices, so it is a
relator of `Q_N` and not of `SL₂(ℤ/N)`, which is why 4.4 doubles the modulus. The sign of the
exponent `−1/2` matters: `(R_H² L^{+1/2})³` is not central in `SL₂(ℤ/N)`.

Acceptance: the test holds on every class of index `≤ 6`, on the two index-`7`
classes with cusp widths `{7}` and on `Γ̄₀(11)`, and `false` on the four other index-`7`
classes (by (B1)–(B7) at `N = 6, 10, 12`) and on the two noncongruence index-`8` classes (by
(A1) at `N = 8`); these are Checks on the explicit permutations of the worked
examples, with the witness of §4.3 as the independent check in the index-`8`
case.

*Needs:* 4.1; Mathlib `ZMod`, Chinese remainder. *Source:* Hsu 1996, Theorem 3.1 (the three
cases); the mixed-case relations as implemented in Kurth's KFarey package and Sage's
`arithgroup_perm`.

The relation predicate is `hsuTest`, and the projective criterion is `hsu`.

### 4.4 Subgroups of `SL₂(ℤ)` not containing `−1`

For `Γ ≤ SL(2, ℤ)` with `-1 ∉ Γ` the permutations of `SL(2, ℤ)` on `SL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ` have
`ρ S` of order `4` and `ρ(−1) ≠ 1`, and the test of 4.2 applies with its relations unchanged
at the **even modulus** `2 · strictLevel Γ`:

```text
IsCongruenceSubgroup Γ ↔ hsuTest (ρ T) (ρ R_H) (2 · strictLevel Γ) ,
```

with `strictLevel Γ ∈ {N, 2N}` by 3.1 (`hsu_strict`; the statement is also true, and
redundant, when `-1 ∈ Γ`). The modulus is doubled for one reason: Hsu's odd-`N` relation
`(R_H² L^{−1/2})³` evaluates to `−1` in `SL₂(ℤ/N)`, so it is a relator of `Q_N` and
not of `SL₂(ℤ/N)`, and on `SL(2, ℤ) ⧸ Γ` with `ρ(−1) ≠ 1` it fails for congruence subgroups
of odd strict level (`Γ₁(5)`, strict level `5`); the relations of the two even cases evaluate
to `1` in `SL₂(ℤ/N)`. The proof is 4.1 with `Gamma (2 N_s)` in place of `Gamma N`
(`Gamma N_s ≤ Γ` by the `SL₂` form of 3.3, hence `Gamma (2 N_s) ≤ Γ.normalCore`) and
Hamilton–Loeffler's theorem that at an even modulus the relations of 4.2 present
`SL₂(ℤ/N)` on L̄, R̄_H. This is the layer in which 3.3(iii)'s witness is verified: its
`SL(2, ℤ)`-permutations on `24` points, read at modulus `2 · 12 = 24`, fail the mixed-case
relations.

*Needs:* 4.2, 3.3, 3.1. *Source:* Kiming–Schütt–Verrill 2011; Hamilton–Loeffler 2014.

## Layer 5: Farey symbols

### 5.1 The datum

A **Farey symbol** is a list `x_0 < x_1 < ⋯ < x_m` of rationals `x_i = a_i/b_i` in lowest
terms with `b_i > 0`, extended by `x_{−1} := −∞ = (−1)/0` and `x_{m+1} := ∞ = 1/0`, such that
consecutive terms are unimodular, `a_{i+1} b_i − a_i b_{i+1} = 1` for `−1 ≤ i ≤ m` (so `x_0`
and `x_m` are integers), together with a label on each of the `m + 2` sides
`s_i := (x_{i−1}, x_i)`, `0 ≤ i ≤ m + 1`, from `inductive SideLabel | even | odd | free (j : ℕ)`,
each free label occurring on exactly two sides. Require nondegeneracy: either the list
has at least two entries, or an odd side occurs. Equivalently, 6r + 3e₂ + 4e₃ > 6.
This condition is part of `FareySymbol`, not a finite-index hypothesis on its generated group.
Write `e₂`, `e₃`, `r` for the numbers of even
sides, odd sides and free pairs (`e₂ + e₃ + 2r = m + 2`).

- Equality is equality of fractions and labels; proof fields do not change the datum.
- For fixed natural numbers A, B ≥ 1 and L, the symbols with |aᵢ| ≤ A,
  bᵢ ≤ B and every free label less than L form a finite set. A strictly increasing
  list draws without repetitions from the finite set of eligible rationals, so no
  separate length bound is needed.
- Reflection x ↦ −x reverses the list and labels and conjugates the subgroup by
  diag(1,−1); translation by j ∈ ℤ conjugates it by Tʲ. Translation is an operation,
  not an identification of distinct symbols.

**Checks.**

- The even/even singleton at 0 has score 6 and is excluded: its two matrices both
  equal [[0,1],[-1,0]], generating C₂, of infinite index.
- The singleton with two occurrences of one free label also has score 6; its free
  matrices are −I and I in the two orientations, both projectively trivial. It is excluded, while even/odd has score 7 and odd/odd score 8.
- With A = B = 1 the eligible fractions are exactly −1, 0, 1. A denominator bound
  alone admits [j] with even/odd labels for every integer j; an unbounded free-label
  alphabet also gives infinitely many literal labelings of the Γ(2) example.

*Needs:* Mathlib `ℚ`.

The counts are `FareySymbol.evenCount`, `FareySymbol.oddCount` and `FareySymbol.freePairs`; the label type is `SideLabel`.

### 5.2 The generators

To a side with endpoints `a/b < c/d` (unimodular, `bc − ad = 1`) attach, with
`M := !![a, c; b, d]` of determinant `−1`:

- even: `g_even := M · S · M⁻¹ = !![−(ab + cd), a² + c²; −(b² + d²), ab + cd]`, of trace `0`,
  swapping the endpoints and fixing the point `M · (−i)` of the geodesic `(a/b, c/d)` (since
  `det M = −1`, `M` maps `ℍ` to the lower half-plane, so the fixed point in `ℍ` is
  `M · (−i)`, not `M · i`; for `(−1/0, 0/1)` it is `i`);
- odd: `g_odd := M · !![1, −1; 1, 0] · M⁻¹ = !![−(ab + ad + cd), a² + ac + c²; −(b² + bd + d²), ab + bc + cd]`,
  of trace `1`, cycling `a/b ↦ (a + c)/(b + d) ↦ c/d ↦ a/b` and fixing `M · e^{−πi/3} ∈ ℍ`;
- a free pair of sides `(a/b, c/d)`, `(a'/b', c'/d')`:
  `g_free := M' · S · M⁻¹ = !![−(a'b + c'd), aa' + cc'; −(bb' + dd'), ab' + cd']`, mapping
  `a/b ↦ c'/d'` and `c/d ↦ a'/b'`.

Prove the determinants are `1`, the stated traces, the stated action on the endpoints, and
that in `PSL(2, ℤ)` the even generator has order `2`, the odd generator order `3`, and the
free generator is hyperbolic or parabolic (parabolic exactly when the two sides share an
endpoint, and then it generates the stabilizer of that cusp up to the width computed in
5.4). Define `FareySymbol.group F : Subgroup PSL(2, ℤ)` as the subgroup generated by the
images under `SL(2, ℤ) → PSL(2, ℤ)` of `g_even` for every even side, `g_odd` for every odd side
and `g_free` for every free pair, each side `(x_{i−1}, x_i)` read with its endpoints in lowest
terms with nonnegative denominators and with `x_{−1} = (−1)/0`, `x_{m+1} = 1/0`. Listing a free
pair in the other order replaces `g_free` by `−g_free⁻¹`, so the group does not depend on the
order. Define the special polygon of F as the region of `ℍ` above the broken boundary (the
full geodesics on even and free sides; on an odd side the two geodesic segments from the
endpoints to the fixed point of `g_odd`). `FareySymbol.endpoints` is the list of homogeneous endpoints with signed infinities;
`FareySymbol.group` is `Subgroup.closure` of the projected side matrices. The acceptance
file states `FareySymbol.group_finiteIndex`, `FareySymbol.exists_group_eq` and
`FareySymbol.group_index`. The special-polygon carrier and its presentation are
geometric construction targets here.

**Checks.**

- `x = [0]`, sides `(even, odd)`: the generators are `S⁻¹` and `−U`, and the group is all of
  `PSL(2, ℤ)`.
- `x = [0, 1]`, pairings `(free 1, even, free 1)`: the generators are `−T` (the pair
  `(−∞, 0) ∼ (1, ∞)`) and `!![−1, 1; −2, 1]`, and the preimage of the group in `SL(2, ℤ)` is
  Mathlib's `Gamma0 2`.
- `x = [0, 1, 2]`, pairings `(free 2, free 1, free 1, free 2)`: the generators are `−T²` and
  `−!![3, −2; 2, −1]`, and the preimage of the group is Mathlib's `Gamma 2` (which contains `−1`).
- `x = [0, 1, 2]`, pairings `(even, free 1, odd, free 1)`: the generators are `S⁻¹`,
  `−!![3, −1; 1, 0]` and `!![−4, 7; −3, 5]`, and the group is the index-`7` class with cusp
  widths `{4, 3}`, whose preimage is not a congruence subgroup (`IsCongruenceSubgroup` fails).

*Needs:* 5.1; Mathlib matrices. *Source:* Kulkarni 1991 §§2–3; Kurth–Long, arXiv:0710.1835v1, §3.3, Theorem 3, p.7.

The three matrix formulas are `FareySymbol.evenGenerator`, `FareySymbol.oddGenerator` and `FareySymbol.freeGenerator`.

### 5.3 Kulkarni's theorem

(i) For every Farey symbol `F`, the special polygon of F is a fundamental domain for
`FareySymbol.group F`, which has finite index, and the generators with the relations
`g_even² = 1`, `g_odd³ = 1` present it; this is an instance of the Poincaré polygon theorem
of FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 2.3. Before applying it, prove the special-polygon
admissibility theorem: successive unimodular intervals have disjoint interiors;
their even half-sides and the two odd segments bound a convex region, with angles
π at even midpoints, 2π/3 at odd vertices and zero at ideal vertices. When the list
has at least two entries its ideal hull has positive area; for a singleton an odd
side encloses positive area, while two non-odd sides collapse to the same geodesic.
The matrices in §5.2 reverse the oriented paired sides, the finite vertex cycles
have exactly orders 2 and 3, and each ideal vertex cycle is a nonidentity parabolic
with positive translation length equal to §5.4's width sum. Thus the cycles satisfy
the polygon theorem, and Gauss–Bonnet gives area (6r+3e₂+4e₃−6)π/3.
Nondegeneracy alone is not used as a replacement for these geometric proofs.
(ii) Every finite-index `Γ̄ ≤ PSL(2, ℤ)` is `FareySymbol.group F` for some `F`
; prove existence of a special polygon in the Farey tessellation whose paired
sides generate Γ̄, with area [PSL₂(ℤ):Γ̄]π/3 (Kurth–Long, §3.1, Theorem 2).
(iii) Two symbols define the same subgroup iff they are related
by the moves Kulkarni lists (relabeling of free pairs, change of basepoint along the Farey
graph); conjugate subgroups correspond to symbols related additionally by `PSL₂(ℤ)`-moves.
For (iii), passing to the modular triple (§5.5) is a possible proof route. The index formula of (i) is
`index + 6 = 6 r + 3 e₂ + 4 e₃` (5.4).

**Checks.**

- The index formula on the worked symbols: `x = [0]` gives `0 + 3 + 4 = 7`, index `1`;
  `Γ̄₀(2)` gives `6 + 3 = 9`, index `3`; `Γ̄(2)` gives `12`, index `6`; `Γ̄₀(11)` (`r = 3`)
  gives `18`, index `12`; the `{4, 3}` class gives `6 + 3 + 4 = 13`, index `7`.
- Presentation at the smallest case: the symbol `x = [0]` presents `PSL(2, ℤ)` as
  `⟨g_even, g_odd | g_even², g_odd³⟩`, the free product `ℤ/2 ∗ ℤ/3` of 0.3.
- (ii) on a congruence example: `Γ̄₀(11)` is the group of the symbol
  `x = [0, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 1]` with pairings `(free 1, free 2, free 3, free 2, free 3, free 1)`.

*Needs:* 5.2; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 2 (polygon theorem), Layer 3 (cusp data).
*Source:* Kulkarni 1991, Theorems 3.2–3.3 and §6; Kurth–Long, arXiv:0710.1835v1, §3.1, Theorems 1–2, p.5.

### 5.4 Invariants of a Farey symbol

Let t be the number of classes of the vertices
`{∞, x_0, …, x_m}`, the two ends `−∞` and `∞` counting as the single vertex `∞`, under the
equivalence relation generated by `a/b ∼ c/d` across each even and each odd side `(a/b, c/d)`
and by `a/b ∼ c'/d'`, `c/d ∼ a'/b'` across each free pair `(a/b, c/d)`, `(a'/b', c'/d')`. Then:

```text
index + 6   = 6 r + 3 e₂ + 4 e₃
genus       = (r + 1 − t) / 2
cusps       = t
ε₂ = e₂ ,   ε₃ = e₃
```

and the width of the cusp class `C` is

```text
width(C) = [∞ ∈ C] · (x_m − x_0) + Σ_{x_i ∈ C, 0 ≤ i ≤ m} (b_{i−1} + b_{i+1}) / b_i + #{odd sides with endpoints in C} ,
```

where `b_{−1} = b_{m+1} = 0`. Each formula is a theorem about `FareySymbol.group F`; the
proofs count Farey triangles in the polygon (each full triangle contributes one translate of
the standard fundamental domain at each of its three ideal vertices; the third of a triangle
cut off by an odd side contributes one translate to the class of its two endpoints) and
compute the Euler characteristic of the quotient cell complex (`V = t + e₂ + e₃`,
`E = r + e₂ + e₃`, `F = 1`). Every formula is verified against the worked examples below.
Neither `t` nor the formulas are in `Suggested.lean`.

**Checks.**

- `x = [0]`: one class `{∞, 0}` of width `0 + 0 + 1 = 1`, so `t = 1` and `genus = 0`.
- `Γ̄₀(2)`: classes `{∞}` of width `x_1 − x_0 = 1` and `{0, 1}` of width `2`, so `t = 2` and
  `genus = (1 + 1 − 2)/2 = 0`, matching `cuspWidths = {2, 1}` of 2.1.
- `Γ̄₀(11)`: classes `{∞}` and `{0, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 1}` of widths `1` and `3 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 3 = 11`,
  so `t = 2` and `genus = (3 + 1 − 2)/2 = 1`.
- The `{4, 3}` class: classes `{∞, 0}` of width `2 + 1 = 3` and `{1, 2}` of width
  `2 + 1 + 1 = 4`; the last `1` is the odd side's term, and a width formula without it gives a
  multiset not summing to the index `7`.
- Comparison with Layer 2: `t`, `e₂`, `e₃` and the widths agree with `cuspCount`, `ellipticTwo`,
  `ellipticThree` and `cuspWidths` of the modular triple of `FareySymbol.group F`.

*Needs:* 5.3, 2.1–2.3. *Source:* Kulkarni 1991 §6; Kurth–Long, arXiv:0710.1835v1, §3.4, p.8.

### 5.5 Farey symbols and finite actions

For an admissible Farey symbol F, prove that its polygon decomposes into exactly
[PSL₂(ℤ) : FareySymbol.group F] standard-domain tiles. The induced action on those tiles is the modular
triple of the generated subgroup. Conversely, every connected modular triple has
an admissible Farey symbol realizing its marked stabilizer. The invariant formulas
of §5.4 agree with the cycle formulas of Layer 2. These are existence and comparison
theorems, with no executable conversion or termination target (Kurth–Long,
arXiv:0710.1835v1, §5.1 and §5.3).

*Needs:* §§5.3–5.4; §1.2.

**Checks.**

- The even/odd singleton gives one tile, trivial permutations and index 1.
- The Γ(2) symbol has six tiles and cusp cycles {2,2,2}, whose sum is 6.
- The symbol with counts (r,e₂,e₃) = (1,1,1) has seven tiles and widths {4,3}; changing its odd side to even would change the tile count to 6.

### 7.2 Expansions at every cusp

For `σ ∈ SL(2, ℤ)` the slash `f ↦ f ∣[k] σ` is a linear isomorphism
`M_k(Γ) ≃ₗ[ℂ] M_k(σ⁻¹ Γ σ)` preserving cusp forms (Mathlib's `conjGL`/`ConjAct` action on
subgroups and the slash action's multiplicativity), and the **expansion of `f` at the cusp
`σ∞`** is `qExpansion w_σ (f ∣[k] σ)` with `w_σ := ((σ⁻¹ Γ σ).map (mapGL ℝ)).strictWidthInfty`,
the strict width of `Γ` at `σ∞`. Prove: independence of the choice of `σ` with the same
`σ∞` up to the substitution `q^{1/w} ↦ ζ q^{1/w}`, `ζ` a `w`-th root of unity (and a sign
`(−1)^k` when the representative differs by `−1`), exactly as in FuchsianOrbifolds Layer
3.4; `f = 0` iff its expansion at one cusp vanishes; `f` is a cusp form iff every cusp
expansion has zero constant term; the order of `f` at a cusp in the width parameter
(ModularForms Layer 1's dictionary, the Diamond–Shurman errata convention) is the order of
the power series at a regular cusp and half of it at an irregular one, where the strict
width is twice the width (in either parity of `k`: in even weight only even exponents
occur, in odd weight only odd ones); and the **valence bound**
Σ_cusps ord_c(f) ≤ k · psl2Index Γ / 12 for `f ≠ 0`, orders read in the width parameter,
the inequality shadow of the valence formula of ModularForms Layer 1, which is consumed, not
reproved. The LMFDB's `cusp_width` and `valuation` of a form are `w_σ` at `σ = 1` and the
order at `∞` (its groups contain `−1`, so every cusp is regular there).

*Needs:* 7.1, 2.1; Mathlib slash action; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 3; ModularForms Layer 1.

### 7.3 Dimensions

ModularForms Layer 10C proves the dimension formulas for congruence subgroups (its Layer 10A
specializes the Fuchsian curve to them). Its argument uses no congruence property: the
weight-`k` automorphy line bundle is built chart by chart on the compactified quotient from
the elliptic and cusp charts of FuchsianOrbifolds Layers 1, 3 and 4, the Riemann–Roch chain
and the genus comparison of 10B hold on every compact Riemann surface, the upper bounds are
Layer 1's valence formula, which quantifies over every finite-index `Γ`, and the reference
meromorphic section in even weight is a quotient of level-one forms. This milestone owns the
statement for every finite-index `Γ`, proved by that argument on `X(Γ̄)` (6.1), and
instantiates it on the invariants of Layer 2 — for even `k ≥ 4`, with `g, ε₂, ε₃, ε∞` those
of `Γ̄ = Γ.map toPSL` (`g` the genus of 2.3, equal to `RiemannSurface.genus X(Γ̄)` there),

```text
dim M_k(Γ) = (k − 1)(g − 1) + ⌊k/4⌋ ε₂ + ⌊k/3⌋ ε₃ + (k/2) ε∞
dim S_k(Γ) = (k − 1)(g − 1) + ⌊k/4⌋ ε₂ + ⌊k/3⌋ ε₃ + (k/2 − 1) ε∞ ,   dim S_2(Γ) = g ,
```

For odd k ≥ 3 and −I ∉ Γ, let r = `regularCuspCount Γ`, the number of cusp orbits
whose integral scaling gives `IsRegularAtInfty`, and s = ε∞ − r. Its API is:

- The regularity property is independent of the integral scaling and of the cusp representative.
- The count r is at most ε∞, and ε∞ − r counts precisely the irregular orbits.
- If −I ∈ Γ then r = ε∞; if −I ∉ Γ, r is even by the weight-one dimension identity.

Then

    dim M_k = (k−1)(g−1) + floor(k/3) ε₃ + (k/2)r + ((k−1)/2)s
    dim S_k = (k−1)(g−1) + floor(k/3) ε₃ + ((k−2)/2)r + ((k−1)/2)s.

Here only floor(k/3) is rounded; the cusp fractions are rational. The headline
`finrank_odd` doubles both equalities in ℤ. In weight one, `finrank_one` asserts
2(dim M₁ − dim S₁) = r and dim S₁ = 0 if r > 2g−2. In the other case,
Riemann–Roch and Clifford give 0 ≤ dim S₁ ≤ (2g+2−r)/4; retain the actual
automorphy line to distinguish weight-one spaces with the same signature.
If −I ∈ Γ, all odd-weight spaces vanish by the native parity theorem
(Diamond–Shurman Theorem 3.6.1; ModularForms Layer 10C). The LMFDB's
`noncong_modular_form_space.dim` is `Module.finrank ℂ S_k(Γ)` or `M_k(Γ)` according to the
space's kind, and the roadmap records both. A corollary wanted for Layer 8: `dim S_k(Γ) > 0`
for every even `k ≥ 4` with `(k − 1)(g − 1) + ⌊k/4⌋ ε₂ + ⌊k/3⌋ ε₃ + (k/2 − 1) ε∞ > 0`, hence
for all sufficiently large even `k`, since the coefficient of `k` in that expression is
`(g − 1) + ε₂/4 + ε₃/3 + ε∞/2 = n/12 > 0` by 2.3.

Layers 1, 3, 4.

### 7.4 Restriction, the congruence closure, and the Hecke operators

For `Γ ≤ Γ'` the inclusion `M_k(Γ') ↪ M_k(Γ)` (Mathlib's restriction along `≤`), and for
`Γ^c := congruenceClosure Γ` the **congruence part** `M_k(Γ^c) ⊆ M_k(Γ)` and its Petersson
orthogonal complement `S_k(Γ)^{nc}` inside `S_k(Γ)` (ModularForms Layer 3's Petersson product,
built there for every finite-index subgroup). **The operators.** For `α := !![1, 0; 0, p]`,
`p` prime, `Γ ∩ α⁻¹ Γ α` has finite index in `Γ` (`Γ` and `α⁻¹ Γ α` have finite index in
`SL(2, ℤ)` and `α⁻¹ SL(2, ℤ) α`, which meet in `Γ⁰(p)`, of finite index in both), so
`Γ α Γ = ⊔ᵢ Γ αᵢ` is a finite union of right cosets, and `Tₚ f := Σᵢ f ∣[k] αᵢ` (the
slash by `GL₂(ℚ)⁺`, normalized as ModularForms Layer 2 normalizes its `Tₚ`) is a
well-defined `ℂ`-linear endomorphism of `M_k(Γ)` preserving `S_k(Γ)`, built on Mathlib's
double-coset objects (`HeckeCoset`). Use Tau Ceti's
`HeckeRing.GL2.heckeSlashModularFormEnd` and `HeckeRing.GL2.heckeSlashCuspFormEnd`;
this milestone proves the required finite commensurability and the comparison with
ModularForms Layer 2 at Γ₁(N), p ∤ N.
**Berger's theorem**: for a prime `p` not dividing the strict level of `Γ`, `Tₚ` equals the
trace from `Γ` to `Γ^c` followed by the operator `Tₚ` of `Γ^c`, so its image lies in
`M_k(Γ^c)`; consequently it annihilates `S_k(Γ)^{nc}`, and for a noncongruence `Γ` with
`S_k(Γ^c) = 0` every good `Tₚ` is zero on `S_k(Γ)`. The proof is Berger's; its inputs are
Wohlfahrt's theorem (3.3), the description of the congruence closure (3.4) and the coset
decomposition of `Γ α Γ` above, and the noncongruence comparison is proved here. This
is the theorem that makes the Hecke-eigenform vocabulary of ModularForms **unavailable** on
the noncongruence part, and it is why Layer 8 speaks of congruences and not of eigenvalues.
The headline `berger` uses the native double-coset endomorphisms. Its trace is the
unnormalized sum over right cosets Γaᵢ in Γᶜ; it asserts existence of a form on Γᶜ
with that underlying function and equality of the two resulting operators on ℍ.
Finiteness of both double-coset decompositions and positivity of their representatives
are explicit hypotheses of the signature, discharged by the commensurability target.

### 7.5 Algebraic coefficients

## Layer 8: unbounded denominators and the Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer congruences

### 8.1 Bounded denominators for congruence forms

*Consumed: the rational case.* AlgebraicModularFormsAndSerreWeights §2.9's
`TauCetiRoadmap.AlgebraicModularFormsAndSerreWeights.KatzModularForms.boundedDenominators`: for a form of integer weight on a subgroup
containing `Gamma N`, `N ≥ 1`, whose width-`N` expansion has rational coefficients `a_n`,
`∃ D ≥ 1, ∀ n, D · a_n ∈ ℤ`, the primes dividing `N` included. On this roadmap's carrier: a
congruence `Γ` contains `Gamma N` for `N = N_s(Γ)` (3.3), so `T^N ∈ Γ`, the strict width `w`
at `∞` divides `N`, and the width-`N` coefficients are the `a_m(f)` placed at the exponents
`(N/w) m`, zero elsewhere; the hypothesis "every `a_m(f) ∈ ℚ`" and the conclusion read the same
on both expansions.

*Owned here: coefficients in a number field.* If `Γ` is a congruence subgroup and
`f ∈ M_k(Γ)` has Fourier coefficients in a number field `K`, then the coefficients have bounded
denominators: there is an integer `D ≠ 0` with `D · a_m(f) ∈ 𝒪_K` for all `m`
(`exists_bounded_denominators_numberField`). The rational case does not give this by
restriction of scalars, since the coefficientwise `ℚ`-components of `f` are modular only once
its Galois conjugates are, so the route proves it directly. Route (for `k ≥ 1`; `M_0` is the
constants and `M_k = 0` for `k < 0`): `Γ ⊇ Gamma N`, and `τ ↦ Nτ` carries `M_k(Gamma N)`
into `M_k(Γ₁(N²))` (the standard conjugation `!![N, 0; 0, 1]`). The space `M_k(Γ₁(N²))` is
spanned by forms with coefficients in number fields and bounded denominators: by the
nebentypus and cusp–Eisenstein decompositions of ModularForms Layer 0 it is spanned by that
layer's Eisenstein series, whose coefficients it gives by generalized Bernoulli numbers and
twisted divisor sums, together with `S_k(Γ₁(N²))`; by the newform decomposition (Layers 3–4)
the latter is spanned by the level raises `g(dτ)` of newforms `g` of level dividing `N²`,
whose coefficients are algebraic integers in `CoefficientField g` by Layer 8 (8W in weight
`1`). (Layer 8's integral object is the modular-symbol module, not a lattice of forms; it
enters only through the integrality of the eigenvalues.) A form with coefficients in `K`
lying in the `ℂ`-span of finitely many forms with coefficients in a number field `L` lies in
their `KL`-span (a linear system over `KL`, finitely many coefficients by the Sturm bound of
7.1), hence has bounded denominators. This is the elementary direction of the dichotomy and
it is stated as a separate milestone because 8.2 is its converse.

*Needs:* 7.1, 3.3; ModularForms Layers 0, 3, 4, 8; AlgebraicModularFormsAndSerreWeights
§2.9 (the rational case). *Source:* Shimura 1971, §3.5; Diamond–Shurman §4 and Theorem 5.8.3.

The number-field transport is `exists_bounded_denominators_numberField`.

### 8.2 The unbounded-denominators theorem

**Theorem (Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang).** Let `f ∈ M_k(Γ)`, `Γ ≤ SL(2, ℤ)` of finite index,
`k ∈ ℤ`, with Fourier coefficients `a_m(f)` at `∞` (in `q^{1/w}`, `w` the strict width) such
that `D · a_m(f) ∈ ℤ` for all `m` and some `D ≠ 0`. Then there is a congruence subgroup
`Γ' ≤ SL(2, ℤ)` and `g ∈ M_k(Γ')` with `g = f` as functions on `ℍ`. Equivalently
(with 8.1): for `f` with rational coefficients, `f` is modular for a congruence subgroup iff
its coefficients have bounded denominators; and for a noncongruence `Γ` every
`f ∈ M_k(Γ) ∖ M_k(Γ^c)` with rational coefficients has unbounded denominators. The
coefficient field `ℚ` is the source's main statement; the source records that it holds with
`ℚ` replaced by any number field (its Remark 6.3.1), and the milestone states that form too,
as `∃ D ∈ 𝒪_K ∖ {0}, ∀ m, D a_m ∈ 𝒪_K`. The source's meromorphy-at-cusps theorem is supplied by ModularFormsPartII W6.3–W6.5.
Prove the transport: embed the native holomorphic form into its weak-form carrier;
compare the actual Fourier germs after enlarging a strict period (coefficients are
inserted at multiple exponents, with zeros elsewhere); apply the rational or number-field
supplier theorem; and transport holomorphy back along equality on ℍ. At each cusp,
passing between commensurable groups changes a local coordinate by a positive power,
so a pole cannot disappear on pullback. This gives the native `ModularForm` conclusion,
not merely a weak form with possible poles.

*Needs:* §§7.2, 8.1; ModularFormsPartII W1.2, W6.3–W6.5. *Source:* Calegari–Dimitrov–Tang, *The unbounded denominators
conjecture*, Theorem 1.0.1 and Remark 6.3.1 (numbering of arXiv:2109.09040v4).

The signatures on native forms are `unbounded_denominators` and `unbounded_denominators_numberField`.

**Checks.**

- The zero form is the zero form on SL₂(ℤ); all cleared coefficients are 0.
- Δ/11 has a congruence extension and clearing denominator 11; demanding D coprime to the level would reject it.
- At a width-2 cusp, the width-6 expansion sends coefficients a₁,a₂ to exponents 3,6 and inserts zero at 1,2,4,5; boundedness is unchanged in both directions.

### 8.3 The Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer congruences

**Hypothesis (Scholl's).** `(X(Γ̄), J)` has a model over `ℚ` in which the cusp `∞` is a
`ℚ`-rational point: in the vocabulary of 6.3, `ℚ` is a field of definition of the Belyi pair
(BelyiMaps 11.5) by a model in which the point of `X(Γ̄)` at `∞` is rational. A condition on
the coefficients of one form does not replace it. Under it, a `ℚ`-rational local parameter
`t` at `∞` has `q^{1/w} = κ t + O(t²)` for a constant `κ` with `κ^w ∈ ℚˣ` (`w` the width,
since `j = q^{−1} + ⋯` lies in `ℚ(X(Γ̄))`), and the coefficients `a_m` below are those of
the expansion in `κ⁻¹ q^{1/w}` (this rescaling is the reason for the LMFDB's `u^m b_m`
presentation, 7.5).

For odd k, require in addition a ℚ-model of the signed weight-(k−2) coefficient system and
its cusp trivialization, obtained from a fine elliptic cover with descent data.
At an irregular marked cusp use the degree-two formal cusp cover and its rational
marked point; its parameter has the strict width. The coarse uniformizer has the
signed width and cannot serve as a strict-width parameter. The coefficients in the
odd-weight assertion are read in that signed datum's rational formal coordinate.
The coarse Belyi pair alone does not specify that sign or its possible quadratic
twist. Prove the even-weight descent from the pair, and the odd-weight comparison
for this additional datum; do not discard the central action on odd symmetric powers
(Li–Long–Yang, arXiv:math/0311287v3, §5, Proposition 5.1, pp.11–13).

**Theorem (Scholl).** Let `k ≥ 2`, let `Γ` have finite index with Scholl's hypothesis, and
`d := dim S_k(Γ)`. Then there is `M ≥ 1` such that `S_k(Γ)` has a basis with coefficients in
`ℤ[1/M]`, and a finite set of primes `T ⊇ {p ∣ M}` such that for every prime `p ∉ T` there
are integers `A_1(p), …, A_{2d}(p)`, with `X^{2d} + A_1 X^{2d−1} + ⋯ + A_{2d}` having all
complex roots of absolute value `p^{(k−1)/2}` (so `A_{2d}(p) = ±p^{d(k−1)}`), such that for
every `f ∈ S_k(Γ)` with coefficients in `ℤ[1/M]` and every `m ≥ 1`

```text
padicNorm p ( Σ_{i=0}^{2d} A_i(p) · a_{m p^{d−i}}(f) ) ≤ p^{−(k−1)(1 + padicValNat p m)} ,   A_0(p) = 1 ,
```

with `a_{m p^{d−i}} := 0` when the index is not an integer. For `d = 1` this is the
three-term congruence: an integer `A_p` with `|A_p| ≤ 2 p^{(k−1)/2}` and a sign
`ε_p ∈ {±1}` with

```text
padicNorm p ( a_{mp} − A_p · a_m + ε_p · p^{k−1} · a_{m/p} ) ≤ p^{−(k−1)(1 + padicValNat p m)} ,
```

where `a_{m/p} := 0` if `p ∤ m`. The sign is not pinned to `+1`: the source gives `A_{2d}(p)`
only through `|A_{2d}(p)| = p^{d(k−1)}`, the Atkin–Swinnerton-Dyer form carries a root of
unity `μ_p` there, and for a congruence `Γ` with `d = 1` the recurrence has `ε_p = χ(p)` for
the nebentypus `χ`. The integers `A_i(p)` are the coefficients of the characteristic
polynomial of geometric Frobenius on the cohomological realization constructed below;
in the coefficient theorem they are existentially quantified integers, and the statement is nonvacuous because the congruences must hold for
**all** `m` and all `f` simultaneously with a single `A_i(p)`. The congruence is a statement
about the coefficients `a_m` indexed by `m`; it is **not** the Hecke recurrence (7.4 shows
`Tₚ` need not act), and for `d = 1` with `Γ` congruence it reduces to the Hecke recurrence
with `A_p = a_p` when `a_1 = 1`. The coefficient theorem is an output of the following owned noncongruence comparison,
not a bibliographic input. Its arithmetic-model and parabolic-cohomology carriers are
not present at the pins, as recorded in the acceptance-file docstring.

**The noncongruence realization.** Over the field of definition, normalize the pullback
of a fine modular curve along J, pull back its universal elliptic curve and the
rank-two degree-one coefficient system, and form its (k−2)-nd symmetric power.
The parabolic space is the image of compactly supported H¹ in ordinary H¹;
construct the descent data for Γ, including the central sign in odd weight, and prove
independence of the fine cover and compatibility with pullback and trace. Its Betti
realization is S_k(Γ) ⊕ conjugate(S_k(Γ)), so has dimension 2d; in weight two it
is H¹ of X(Γ̄). The construction specializes ModularCurvesPartII §§14.1–14.4,
whose generic coefficient, boundary and comparison constructions are imported;
the noncongruence normalization and signed descent are owned here.

**The integral de Rham realization.** Construct the logarithmic Gauss–Manin connection
on that pullback, its symmetric powers and the parabolic de Rham image. Prove the
Eichler–Shimura comparison with the form space and the rational structure determined
by the chosen formal cusp coordinate. Construct a finite free lattice after a single
localization ℤ[1/M], retaining the denominator ideals of the descent projector and
of all cusp-coordinate maps before localization. Use CrystallineCohomology CR.2.3
and CR.DDR.6.11–CR.DDR.6.14 for smooth and logarithmic crystalline–de Rham comparison;
prove here that it identifies the actual parabolic images and their Frobenius actions,
not just ambient rational cohomology. The bound p > k−2 enters the integral
symmetric-power comparison; include those finitely many primes in T explicitly.

**The common Frobenius polynomial.** Prove independence of ℓ and equality of the
étale and crystalline characteristic polynomials for this descended realization,
using the geometric trace comparison on the fine cover and compatibility with its
projector. Prove that the polynomial is monic of degree 2d and lies in ℤ[X].
DeligneWeightsAndPurity §6.4 supplies purity of parabolic curve cohomology for
pure weight-(k−2) coefficients; identify our elliptic coefficient system with that
input, giving absolute values p^((k−1)/2). None of these claims is an assumption
on freely chosen integers Aᵢ.

**The Fourier comparison.** Prove that Cayley–Hamilton on the integral parabolic
realization implies the displayed coefficient congruence, with the denominator
bound (k−1)(1+v_p(m)). This is the noncongruence integral comparison of Scholl
§5, Theorem 5.4, with Li–Long §2, equation (2.4), p.5; it is a target here on

The exceptional set is determined by the models, descent and weight, not chosen

## Layer 9: the LMFDB invariant layer

### 9.1 The LMFDB columns and the intrinsic label prefix

Each item names the LMFDB column it realizes and its mathematical definition.

- **`noncong_modular_curve`.** `psl2z_index` — `Γ̄.index` (0.4, 1.1). `permS`, permR,
  `permT` — 1.1, read through the opposite convention (Conventions): a row satisfies
  permR = permT * permS in `Equiv.Perm` and gives the modular triple
  `(permS * permT, permS, permT⁻¹)`. `genus` — 2.3. `n_c` — `cuspCount`
  (2.1). `n_e2`, `n_e3` — 2.2. `cusp_widths` — `cuspWidths` (2.1). `is_congruence` —
  `IsCongruenceSubgroup (lift Γ̄)`, decided by 4.2 and certified by 3.5 or 4.3.
  `monodromy_group` — the monodromy group of 1.1 with its transitive-group label (6.2).
  `passport_reps`, `passport_embeddings`, `belyi_map`, `K`, `L`, `v_L` — the Belyi pair of
  6.3, consumed from BelyiMaps. `elliptic_curve`, `hyperelliptic_curve` — models, not owned
  here. `label`, `friends` — not targets.
- **`noncong_modular_form_space`.** `weight` — `k`; `dim` — 7.3; `mf_curve` — the curve
  above.
- **`noncong_modular_form`.** `weight`; `cusp_width` — `w` of 7.2 at `∞`; `valuation` — the
  order at `∞` (7.2); `coefficient_numerators`, `coefficient_denominators`, `K` — the
  factors `b_m ∈ K` of the presentation `a_m = u^m b_m` of the coefficients at `∞` (7.5).
  8.2 speaks of the `a_m`: they have bounded denominators exactly on the congruence part,
  and the stored `b_m` differ from them by the powers `u^m`. `u`, `u_str`, `v` — the
  rescaling constant and the embedding data of the LMFDB's presentation of `K`, a database
  convention, not a target.
- **The intrinsic label prefix.** A `noncong` label is the signature
  `(psl2z_index, genus, n_c, n_e2, n_e3)` followed by a passport index and a Galois-orbit
  letter (Berghaus–Monien–Radchenko's labels, e.g. `15_1_2_1_0_2_a`), so
  labelPrefix Γ̄ := (psl2z_index, genus, n_c, n_e2, n_e3), with `n_c = cuspCount` of 2.1:
  a conjugacy invariant and a mirror invariant. The cusp widths enter the label only through
  the passport index, which is enumerative and not a target. The prefix does
  **not** determine the conjugacy class (not even the finer invariant vector of 2.4 does),
  which is why the label needs the enumerative tail. Nor does it determine the level (the
  prefix `(7, 0, 2, 1, 1)` is shared by the four index-`7` noncongruence classes, of levels
  `6, 6, 10, 12`) or the congruence flag (the index-`8` quadruple with prefix
  `(8, 0, 1, 2, 2)`, two congruence and two not, which share even the invariant vector).

*Needs:* Layers 1–8.

**Checks.**

- The full group has intrinsic prefix (1,0,1,1,1).
- The four index-7 noncongruence classes share prefix (7,0,2,1,1), while their levels are 6,6,10,12.
- The four index-8 classes with prefix (8,0,1,2,2) have the same widths {8}, but two pass Hsu and two fail.

## Worked examples (acceptance criteria)

Permutations are in the pinned convention (left cosets, functions composed right to left;
`permS = σ1`, `permT = σinf`, permR = permS · permT, `σ0 = permS · permT⁻¹`), on `{1, …, n}`,
base coset `1` unless stated; an LMFDB row of the same subgroup is in the opposite convention
(Conventions). Every number below was recomputed from the permutations and
checked against an independent computation of the same group.

- **`PSL₂(ℤ)` itself** (`n = 1`): all permutations trivial; `ε₂ = ε₃ = 1`, `cuspWidths = {1}`,
  `genus = 0`, level `1`, congruence; `12·0 + 3 + 4 + 6 = 13 = 12 + 1` (2.3). Its Farey symbol
  has `x = [0]`, sides `(−∞, 0)` even and `(0, ∞)` odd, `r = 0`, with generators `S̄` (5.2's
  `g_even` for `a/b = −1/0`, `c/d = 0/1` is `S⁻¹`) and `Ū` (`g_odd` for `0/1`, `1/0` is
  `!![0, 1; −1, 1] = −U`): `index + 6 = 0 + 3 + 4 = 7`, `t = 1`, `g = (0 + 1 − 1)/2 = 0`,
  width `= 0 + (0 + 0)/1 + 1 = 1` (5.4).
- **The index-`2` subgroup** `Γ̄² = ker(PSL₂(ℤ) → ℤ/2)`: `permS = (1 2)`, permR = (),
  `permT = (1 2)`; `ε₂ = 0`, `ε₃ = 2`, widths `{2}`, `g = 0`, level `2`, congruence (the
  unique class of index `2`, 1.3).
- **`Γ̄₀(2)`** (`n = 3`, cosets `ℙ¹(ℤ/2) = {(0:1), (1:0), (1:1)}` numbered `1, 2, 3`; the base
  coset of `Γ̄₀(2)` is point `2`): `permS = (1 2)`, permR = (1 3 2), `permT = (1 3)`,
  `σ0 = (1 3 2)`; `ε₂ = 1`, `ε₃ = 0`, widths `{2, 1}`, `g = 0`, level `2`, congruence.
  Farey symbol `x = [0, 1]`, pairings `(free 1, even, free 1)`: `index + 6 = 6 + 3 = 9`,
  `t = 2`, `g = (1 + 1 − 2)/2 = 0`; the even generator of side `(0, 1)` is
  `!![−1, 1; −2, 1] = −!![1, −1; 2, −1]`, trace `0`, and the free generator of the ordered pair
  `(−∞, 0) ∼ (1, ∞)` is `−T`; the free pair identifies `∞ ∼ ∞` and `0 ∼ 1`, the even side
  identifies `0 ∼ 1`, so the vertex classes are `{∞}`, of width `x_1 − x_0 = 1`, and `{0, 1}`,
  of width `(0 + 1)/1 + (1 + 0)/1 = 2`.
- **The other index-`3` class**, `permS = ()`, permR = (1 2 3), `permT = (1 2 3)`: `ε₂ = 3`,
  `ε₃ = 0`, widths `{3}`, `g = 0`, level `3`, congruence (the preimage of the Klein
  four-group of `PSL₂(ℤ/3) ≅ A₄`); together with `Γ̄₀(2)` it exhausts index `3`.
- **`Γ̄₀(4)`** (`n = 6`): `permS = (1 2)(3 5)(4 6)`, permR = (1 5 2)(3 4 6),
  `permT = (1 3 6 5)`; `ε₂ = ε₃ = 0`, widths `{4, 1, 1}`, `g = 0`, level `4`, congruence;
  `dim M_2 = −1 + 0 + 0 + 3 = 2`, `dim S_2 = 0`.
- **`Γ̄(2)`** (`n = 6`, cosets `PSL₂(ℤ/2) ≅ S₃`): `permS = (1 3)(2 5)(4 6)`,
  permR = (1 4 5)(2 6 3), `permT = (1 6)(2 4)(3 5)`; widths `{2, 2, 2}`, `ε₂ = ε₃ = 0`,
  `g = 0`; Farey symbol `x = [0, 1, 2]`, pairings `(free 2, free 1, free 1, free 2)`:
  `index + 6 = 12`, `t = 3`, `g = (2 + 1 − 3)/2 = 0`, generators `−T²` and `−!![3, −2; 2, −1]`, with the same projective images as their negatives.
- **Index `6`, all eight classes** (1.3), by `(ε₂, ε₃, widths, g)`: `(4, 0, {6}, 0)`,
  `(2, 0, {5, 1}, 0)`, `(2, 0, {3, 3}, 0)`, `(2, 0, {4, 2}, 0)`, `(0, 3, {6}, 0)`,
  `(0, 0, {4, 1, 1}, 0) = Γ̄₀(4)`, `(0, 0, {6}, 1)` (the commutator subgroup, the unique
  genus-one class of index `≤ 7`), `(0, 0, {2, 2, 2}, 0) = Γ̄(2)`. All eight are congruence
  (4.2), consistent with 3.5: their levels `6, 5, 3, 4, 6, 4, 6, 2` all have `6 ∣ |Q_N|`.
- **`Γ̄₀(11)`** (`n = 12`, cosets `ℙ¹(ℤ/11)` numbered `(0:1) = 1`, `(1:0) = 2`, `(1:x) = x + 2`;
  base coset `2`): `permS = (1 2)(3 12)(4 7)(5 9)(6 10)(8 11)`,
  permR = (1 12 2)(3 11 7)(4 6 9)(5 8 10), `permT = (1 3 8 6 5 11 4 10 9 7 12)`;
  `ε₂ = ε₃ = 0` (`−1` and `−3` are non-squares mod `11`), widths `{11, 1}`, `g = 1`
  (`12 + 0 + 0 + 12 = 24 = 12 + 12`), level `11`, congruence; `dim S_2 = 1` and
  `dim S_4 = 2`, the space of the elliptic curve `11.a`. Farey symbol `x = [0, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 1]`,
  pairings `(free 1, free 2, free 3, free 2, free 3, free 1)`, `r = 3`: `index + 6 = 18`,
  classes `{∞}` and `{0, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 1}`, `g = (3 + 1 − 2)/2 = 1`, widths `1` and
  `3 + 1 + 3 + 1 + 3 = 11`; the free generator of the pair `(0, 1/3) ∼ (1/2, 2/3)` is
  `!![−7, 2; −11, 3]` and that of `(1/3, 1/2) ∼ (2/3, 1)` is `!![−8, 3; −11, 4]`.
- **Index `7`: the six classes** (1.3), the smallest index with a noncongruence subgroup.
  Two congruence classes, mirror images of each other, with `(ε₂, ε₃, widths, g) =
  (3, 1, {7}, 0)`, level `7`: `permS = (3 4)(5 6)`, `permR = (1 2 3)(4 5 7)`,
  `permT = (1 2 4 6 5 7 3)`, and `permS = (3 4)(6 7)`, permR = (1 2 3)(4 5 6),
  `permT = (1 2 4 5 7 6 3)` (preimages of the two classes of `S₄ ≤ PSL₂(F₇)`, Hsu's odd
  test at `N = 7` holds). Four **noncongruence** classes, all with `ε₂ = ε₃ = 1`,
  `g = 0`, two cusps:
  - widths `{6, 1}`, level `6`: `permS = (2 3)(4 5)(6 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(5 6 7),
    `permT = (1 3 2 5 7 4)`;
  - widths `{6, 1}`, level `6`: `permS = (2 3)(4 6)(5 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(3 5 7),
    `permT = (1 3 7 2 6 4)` — the mirror of the previous class and not conjugate to it
    (2.4);
  - widths `{4, 3}`, level `12`: `permS = (2 3)(4 5)(6 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(3 5 6),
    `permT = (1 3 4)(2 5 7 6)`, self-mirror; Farey symbol `x = [0, 1, 2]`, pairings
    `(even, free 1, odd, free 1)`: `index + 6 = 6 + 3 + 4 = 13`, classes `{∞, 0}` of width
    `2 + 1 = 3` and `{1, 2}` of width `2 + 1 + 1 = 4` (the odd side contributes `1`),
    generators `S`, `!![3, −1; 1, 0]`, `!![−4, 7; −3, 5]`;
  - widths `{5, 2}`, level `10`: `permS = (2 3)(4 6)(5 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(3 5 6),
    `permT = (1 3 7 5 4)(2 6)`, self-mirror.
  Each is noncongruence by the order obstruction (3.5): `7 ∤ 72 = |Q_6|`,
  `7 ∤ 360 = |Q_10|`, `7 ∤ 576 = |Q_12|`. Their monodromy groups are
  `AGL₁(𝔽₇) = C₇ ⋊ C₆` (order `42`, solvable) for the two level-`6` classes and `S₇` for the
  classes of levels `12` and `10`, whose composition factor `A₇` gives the composition-factor
  obstruction of 3.5 as a second proof; the two congruence classes have monodromy group
  `PSL₂(𝔽₇)` of order `168`, and `7 ∣ 7`. Hsu's mixed test (B1)–(B7) fails on each
  of the four. For each of the four, `dim M_2 = 1`, `dim S_4 = 1`, `dim S_6 = 2` (7.3, 8.4).
- **Index `8`: the seven classes.** `(0, 2, {7, 1}, 0) = Γ̄₀(7)` (`permS = (1 2)(3 8)(4 5)(6 7)`,
  permR = (1 8 2)(3 7 5), `permT = (1 3 6 7 4 5 8)` on `ℙ¹(ℤ/7)`), `(0, 2, {4, 4}, 0)` of
  level `4`, `(0, 2, {6, 2}, 0)` of level `6`, all congruence; and **four classes with the
  same invariant vector** `(2, 2, {8}, 0)`, level `8`:
  - congruence: `permS = (2 3)(4 6)(5 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(3 5 8),
    `permT = (1 3 7 5 8 2 6 4)`; and `permS = (2 3)(4 5)(7 8)`, permR = (1 2 4)(5 6 7),
    `permT = (1 3 2 5 6 8 7 4)`;
  - noncongruence: `permS = (3 4)(5 6)(7 8)`, permR = (1 2 3)(4 5 7),
    `permT = (1 2 4 6 5 8 7 3)`; and `permS = (2 3)(4 5)(6 7)`, permR = (1 2 4)(5 6 8),
    `permT = (1 3 2 5 7 6 8 4)`.
  Here `8 ∣ 192 = |Q_8|`, so the index form of 3.5 is silent; its monodromy forms are
  not: the two noncongruence classes have monodromy group `PGL₂(𝔽₇)`, of order `336 ∤ 192` and
  with composition factor `PSL₂(𝔽₇)`, `7 ∤ 8`, while the two congruence classes have monodromy
  group `GL₂(𝔽₃)` of order `48 ∣ 192`. Hsu's relation (A1) at `N = 8` fails for the
  two noncongruence classes and all of (A1)–(A3) hold for the two congruence ones; the
  witness `!![73, −32; 16, −7] ∈ Γ(8)` sends the base coset of the first noncongruence
  class to coset `6` (4.3); its congruence closure is all of `PSL₂(ℤ)` (3.4). For all four,
  `dim M_2 = 0`, `dim S_4 = 2`, `dim M_4 = 3`.
- **Hsu's own examples.** The subgroup of index `10` with `permT` of cycle type `(5, 3, 2)`
  and the subgroup of index `18` with cycle type `(8, 3, 3, 2, 2)` from Hsu's paper (Sage's
  `HsuExample10`, `HsuExample18`) are noncongruence of levels `30` and `24`, both of genus `0`.
- **Boundary checks.** (i) The pair (permS, permR) = ((1 2), (1 2 3)) on three points has
  `permS² = 1`, `permR³ = 1` and is transitive, so it **is** a class: index `3`,
  permT = permS · permR = (2 3), a relabeling of `Γ̄₀(2)`; whereas `((1 2), (1 2 3 4))` is
  rejected by `permR³ ≠ 1`, and `((1 2), (3 4 5))` on five points is rejected by
  intransitivity (its two orbits give the index-`2` subgroup and the index-`3` normal
  subgroup, not a subgroup of index `5`).
  (ii) The number `ε₂ = 3` at index `3` is consistent with `ε₂ ≡ n (mod 2)` and `ε₂ = 2` at
  index `3` is rejected by 2.2. (iii) A cusp-width multiset not summing to the index is
  rejected by 2.1. (iv) `cuspWidths` with lcm `12` at index `7` shows `N ≤ n` is false (3.1).
  (v) The lift witness of 3.3(iii): a subgroup `Γ ≤ SL₂(ℤ)` with `±Γ = Γ₀(6)` and `-1 ∉ Γ`
  that is not congruence, while `Γ₀(6)` is.
