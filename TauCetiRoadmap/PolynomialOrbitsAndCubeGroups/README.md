# Roadmap: polynomial orbits on nilmanifolds and Host–Kra cube groups

**Summary.** A nilmanifold is a quotient `G/Γ` of a connected, simply connected nilpotent real Lie
group `G` by a discrete cocompact subgroup `Γ`; the circle `ℝ/ℤ` and the Heisenberg nilmanifold
are the first examples. A polynomial sequence in `G` is a map `g : ℤ → G` whose iterated discrete
derivatives eventually vanish, measured against a filtration `G = G_0 ⊇ G_1 ⊇ ⋯ ⊇ G_{s+1} = {1}`
with `[G_i, G_j] ⊆ G_{i+j}`. This roadmap proves the qualitative theory of the orbits
`n ↦ g(n)Γ`. Its headline is **Leibman's theorem**: `g(n)Γ` is equidistributed for the Haar
measure of `G/Γ` if and only if no nontrivial horizontal character `η : G → ℝ/ℤ` (a continuous
homomorphism vanishing on `Γ`) makes `η ∘ g` constant; equivalently, if and only if the
projection of the orbit to the horizontal torus `G/[G, G]Γ` is equidistributed. The induction
that proves it gives at the same time an ineffective single-scale form: on a fixed filtered
nilmanifold, for a compact family of test functions and `δ > 0`, there are finitely many
horizontal characters and a constant `A` such that, whenever an average over `0 ≤ n < N` misses the
Haar integral by `δ`, one of these characters `η` has `η(g(n)) = ∑_j α_j binom(n, j)` with
`‖α_j‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≤ A / N^j` for every `j ≥ 1`. Every orbit closure is a finite union of
sub-nilmanifolds on which the orbit, restricted to a residue class, is equidistributed.

The second object is the **Host–Kra cube group** `HK^ι(G_•)` of a filtered group: the subgroup of
`G^{{0,1}^ι}` generated by the face elements `g^{[F]}`, `g ∈ G_{codim F}`, one for each upper face
`F` of the cube. As an abstract group it is built in NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences; here, for a
filtered nilmanifold, it becomes again a filtered nilmanifold, carries a unique
invariant probability, and is the space in which the cube configurations
`(g(b_0 + ω · b))_{ω ∈ {0,1}^ι}` of a polynomial sequence live. The roadmap proves that the
configurations of a sequence of polynomials that is irrational at scale `N` become equidistributed
on the cube nilmanifold, the qualitative counting lemma for cubes. The last layer proves the
topological polynomial recurrence theorem for nilpotent group actions by Bergelson's PET induction:
for a minimal action of a nilpotent group on a compact space, finitely many polynomial sequences
vanishing at `0` return every nonempty open set to itself simultaneously.

The roadmap builds on **NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences**, which supplies filtrations of groups,
polynomial sequences and their Taylor expansions, abstract cube groups, rational filtered
nilmanifolds with Mal'cev bases, and horizontal and vertical characters; on
**ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory** §2, whose Weyl theorem on tori is the abelian case and whose
finite van der Corput inequality drives the induction; and on **AmenableGroups** Layer 9, whose
fixed point theorem for solvable groups produces the invariant measure on a nilmanifold.
**NilpotentLieHeightsAndFiltrations** supplies the filtrations of Lie algebras behind rationality,
and **NilsequenceEquidistribution** owns the quantitative theory with explicit bounds, which this
roadmap does not state.

Layer 0 adds to the abstract cube groups their filtration, face coordinates, symmetries, Leibman
squares and polynomial configurations. Layer 1 is Fourier
analysis for continuous actions of a compact torus. Layer 2 proves existence and uniqueness of the
invariant probability on a nilmanifold and its behaviour under fibrations, covers and
sub-nilmanifolds. Layer 3 treats the horizontal projection, level characters, irrationality
and the phases of polynomial sequences. Layer 4 makes cube groups and squares into nilmanifolds.
Layer 5 proves the van der Corput lemma, the ineffective single-scale theorem and Leibman's
criterion. Layer 6 proves the limit laws: cube equidistribution for irrational sequences, sequential
factorisation and Leibman's orbit-closure theorem. Layer 7 is PET induction and polynomial
recurrence.

## Conventions

1. **Groups and commutators.** Groups are multiplicative. The commutator is Mathlib's
   `⁅a, b⁆ = a * b * a⁻¹ * b⁻¹` (`commutatorElement_def`); `⁅H, K⁆` is Mathlib's commutator of
   subgroups.
2. **Filtrations.** Filtrations are the `ℕ`-filtrations of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences
   (its Convention 2 and 0.1): subgroups `G_i`, antitone, with `⁅G_i, G_j⁆ ≤ G_{i+j}`; no condition
   is imposed on `G_0`, and a filtration with `G_0 = G_1 = G` is *full*, a hypothesis stated where
   used. It has degree at most `s` when `G_{s+1} = ⊥`. The shifted filtration `G^{+k}` has levels
   `G_{i+k}`.
3. **Polynomial maps.** `poly(ℤ^m, G_•)` is the group of polynomial maps of
   NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences (0.2–0.3, the Lazard–Leibman group) for the degree filtration on
   `ℤ^m`: `∂_{h_1} ⋯ ∂_{h_j} g (n) ∈ G_j` for all `j ≥ 0`, `n`, `h_1, …, h_j`, with
   `∂_h g (n) = g (n + h) * (g n)⁻¹`; in particular `g` takes values in `G_0`. For `m = 1` maps are
   `ℤ → G`. Taylor coefficients `g(n) = ∏_j a_j^{binom(n, j)}`, `a_j ∈ G_j`, are those of
   NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.4.
4. **Nilmanifolds.** `G` is a connected, simply connected nilpotent real Lie group and `Γ ≤ G` a
   discrete cocompact subgroup. In NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences `G` is the BCH group of a real
   nilpotent Lie algebra (its 1.1, which identifies it with the simply connected Lie group), and
   every such pair `(G, Γ)` with a filtration by rational ideals is a rational filtered nilmanifold
   (its 2.6); statements are proved in that model. `Suggested.lean` states them for a nilmanifold
   given by a Mal'cev presentation `P : MalcevPresentation G d s` (its 2.4), with `Γ = P.lattice`
   and the full rational filtration `P.filtration`. `G ⧸ Γ` carries the quotient topology and its
   Borel σ-algebra, and `G` acts on it on the left by homeomorphisms. A closed connected subgroup
   `H` is *rational* when `Γ ∩ H` is cocompact in `H` (NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2), and a
   filtration is *rational* when its levels are closed, connected and rational. Rational elements
   (`σ^r ∈ Γ` for some `r ≥ 1`) are those of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 4.3.
5. **Equidistribution.** A sequence `x : ℕ → X` in a compact Hausdorff space is equidistributed
   for a Borel probability `μ` when its empirical measures converge to `μ`:
   `Tendsto (TauCeti.Probability.empiricalMeasure x) atTop (𝓝 μ)`. This is
   `AsympEquidistributed` of ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.1. A map `x : ℤ → X` is
   equidistributed when `n ↦ x n` (`n : ℕ`) is. For maps on `ℤ^m`, the average of `f` over the box
   `B(N) = ∏_i [0, N_i)` is `|B(N)|⁻¹ ∑_{n ∈ B(N)} f(x n)`, and `x` is box-equidistributed when
   these averages converge to `∫ f dμ` for every `f ∈ C(X, ℂ)` along every sequence of boxes whose
   least side length tends to infinity.
6. **Characters.** Horizontal characters `η : G → ℝ/ℤ` (continuous homomorphisms vanishing on
   `Γ`) and vertical characters are those of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 4.4; in Lean a character
   is `G →* Multiplicative UnitAddCircle`, nontrivial when `≠ 1`. For `θ ∈ ℝ` or `θ ∈ ℝ/ℤ`,
   `‖θ‖_{ℝ/ℤ}` is Mathlib's norm on `UnitAddCircle`, the distance to the nearest integer.
7. **Tori.** `T^d = UnitAddTorus d = d → UnitAddCircle` for a finite type `d`, with Haar
   probability the product of `AddCircle.haarAddCircle`; `e(k · t)` is Mathlib's
   `UnitAddTorus.mFourier k t`.
8. **Cubes.** For a finite type `ι`, the discrete cube `{0,1}^ι` is `Finset ι`, a vertex `ω` being
   the set of coordinates equal to `1`; arrays are maps `Finset ι → G`, and `|ω| = ω.card`. The
   upper face of `D : Finset ι` is `{ω | D ⊆ ω}`, of dimension `|ι| − |D|`.
9. **Names.** Declarations of Mathlib and Tau Ceti are cited by full name; this roadmap's own
   prototypes live in `TauCetiRoadmap.PolynomialOrbitsAndCubeGroups` and are cited by short name.
   `Suggested.lean` also carries short prototypes of the supplier objects of
   NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences it needs, in the shapes of that roadmap's `Suggested.lean`
   (`GroupFiltration`, `AddGroupFiltration`, `IsPolynomialMap`, `weightedFiltration`,
   `horizKernel`, `MalcevPresentation`, and `face`, `hkCube`, `IsHorizontalCharacter` for objects
   of its README); these are that roadmap's targets, written out only so that the signatures here
   elaborate. A sequence `g : ℤ → G` is polynomial (`IsPolySeq F g`) when
   `IsPolynomialMap (weightedFiltration fun _ : Unit => 1) F (fun n => g (n ()))`.

## Layer 0: cube groups and Leibman squares

Everything in this layer holds for an arbitrary group `G` with an `ℕ`-filtration `G_•` and a finite
type `ι` with decidable equality. No topology is used. The Host–Kra cube group of a filtered group
as an abstract group, its cube characterisation of polynomial maps, and the unique face
factorisation of its elements for a full filtration are NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.3; this layer
builds on them.

### 0.1 Shifted cube groups and their filtration

- `face D : G →* (Finset ι → G)` sends `g` to the array equal to `g` at the vertices `ω ⊇ D` and to
  `1` elsewhere (NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences' face element). `HK^ι(G_•)` is the subgroup generated
  by `face D (G_{|D|})`, `D : Finset ι`, that is `HK_{1,…,1}(G)` of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.3
  in the indexing of Convention 8; `hkCube G_• ι k` is `HK^ι(G^{+k}_•)` for the shifted filtration
  `G^{+k}_i = G_{i+k}`, so `HK^ι(G_•) = hkCube G_• ι 0`.
- `⁅face D g, face D' h⁆ = face (D ∪ D') ⁅g, h⁆`.
- `k ↦ hkCube G_• ι k` is antitone, `hkCube G_• ι k` is normal in `HK^ι(G_•)`, and
  `⁅hkCube G_• ι j, hkCube G_• ι k⁆ ≤ hkCube G_• ι (j + k)` (`commutator_hkCube_le`). So the levels
  `hkCube G_• ι k`, viewed in `HK^ι(G_•)`, form a filtration `HK^ι(G_•)_•` of `HK^ι(G_•)`, of degree
  at most `s` when `G_•` has degree at most `s`, and full when `G_•` is.
- Functoriality: a homomorphism `φ : G →* G'` with `φ(G_i) ≤ G'_i` maps `hkCube G_• ι k` into
  `hkCube G'_• ι k` coordinatewise; for a normal subgroup `N` with the quotient filtration, the
  coordinatewise quotient map sends `hkCube G_• ι k` onto `hkCube (G/N)_• ι k`.

*Needs:* NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.1 (shifted and quotient filtrations), 0.3; Mathlib
`Subgroup.map`, `iSup`, `Subgroup.commutator_le`.

### 0.2 Face coordinates of shifted and integral arrays

Fix a linear order on `Finset ι` refining `|D| ≤ |D'|`. For every filtration (full or not) and
every `k`, each element of `hkCube G_• ι k` is uniquely a product `∏_D face D (g_D)` in this order
with `g_D ∈ G_{|D|+k}`, the coefficient `g_D` being computed recursively from the entries at the
vertices `ω ⊆ D` (the entry at `D` divided by the product of the earlier factors evaluated at `D`);
for `k = 0` and a full filtration this is the factorisation of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.3
(Green–Tao, Linear equations in primes, Appendix E, (E.1) and Lemma E.10; Green–Tao, arithmetic
regularity, §3, (3.2)). For a subgroup `Γ`, an array of `HK^ι(G_•)` has all entries in `Γ` if and
only if all its face coordinates do. *Needs:* 0.1; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.3.

**Checks.**
- An element of `HK^ι(G_•)` equal to `1` at every vertex other than `Finset.univ` has its entry at
  `Finset.univ` in `G_{|ι|}`.
- For `G` abelian with the full filtration `G_0 = G_1 = G`, `G_2 = ⊥` (degree `1`) and `ι = Fin 2`,
  `HK^ι(G_•)` is the group of parallelograms `(x, x + a, x + b, x + a + b)` at the vertices
  `∅, {0}, {1}, {0,1}`. For the filtration with `G_0 = G` and `G_1 = ⊥` it is the diagonal.

### 0.3 Symmetries and restrictions

- The hyperoctahedral group acts on arrays: a permutation `σ` of `ι` by `ω ↦ σ '' ω`, and the
  reflection in `a : ι` by `ω ↦ symmDiff ω {a}`. `HK^ι(G_•)` is invariant under both (the reflection
  of `face D g` with `a ∈ D` is `face (D ∖ {a}) g * face D g⁻¹`, and `G_{|D|} ≤ G_{|D|−1}`).
- Restriction to a lower face: for `a : ι`, the maps `(Finset ι → G) → (Finset {i // i ≠ a} → G)`
  reading off the face `a ∉ ω` and the face `a ∈ ω` send `HK^ι(G_•)` onto `HK^{ι∖a}(G_•)` and onto
  `HK^{ι∖a}(G_•)`, respectively. Inserting a coordinate: the doubling map
  `(Finset ι' → G) → (Finset ι → G)`, `x ↦ (ω ↦ x(ω ∩ ι'))`, for `ι' ⊆ ι`, maps `HK^{ι'}(G_•)` into
  `HK^ι(G_•)`.
- The diagonal `face ∅ (G)` lies in `HK^ι(G_•)`.

*Needs:* 0.1, 0.2.

### 0.4 Leibman squares

For `ι = Unit`, arrays are pairs `(x, y)` (entries at `∅` and `Finset.univ`), and
`hkCube G_• Unit k = {(x, y) | x, y ∈ G_k, x⁻¹ y ∈ G_{k+1}} = G_k ×_{G_{k+1}} G_k`
(`mem_hkCube_unit_iff`). Assume `G_•` full of degree at most `s ≥ 1`.

- The **Leibman square** is `G^□ = G ×_{G_2} G = hkCube G_• Unit 1`, with the filtration
  `G^□_0 = G^□`, `G^□_i = G_i ×_{G_{i+1}} G_i` for `i ≥ 1`. Its top level `G^□_s` is the diagonal
  `Δ(G_s)`, which is central in `G^□`, and the quotient filtration on `G^□/Δ(G_s)` has degree at
  most `s − 1`.
- **The square of a polynomial sequence.** If `g ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` and `g(0) = 1`, then for every
  `h ∈ ℤ` the sequence `n ↦ (g(1)^{−h} g(n + h), g(n))` lies in `G^□` and in `poly(ℤ, G^□_•)`.
  (Modulo `G_2` the sequence `g` is `n ↦ g(1)^n`; derivatives of order `j` of the two coordinates
  differ by an element of `G_{j+1}`, and conjugation by `g(1)^{−h}` moves `G_j` by `G_{j+1}`.)

*Needs:* 0.1; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.2–0.3 (polynomial maps: closure under products, shifts,
conjugation and coordinatewise maps).

**Checks.**
- For `G = ℝ` with `G_1 = ℝ`, `G_2 = ⊥` (degree `1`), `G^□` is the diagonal of `ℝ²` and
  `n ↦ (g(1)^{−h} g(n + h), g(n))` is `n ↦ (αn, αn)` for `g(n) = αn`.
- For degree `2` on `G = ℝ` (`G_2 = ℝ`, `G_3 = ⊥`), `G^□ = ℝ²`, and `g(n) = αn²` (additively) gives
  the pair `(α(n + h)² − hα, αn²)`. Modulo `Δ(G_2)`, identified with `ℝ` by `(x, y) ↦ x − y`, it is
  `n ↦ 2αhn + α(h² − h)`, of degree `1 = s − 1`.

### 0.5 Cube configurations are polynomial

For `g ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` the map `(b_0, b) ↦ (ω ↦ g(b_0 + ∑_{i ∈ ω} b_i))`, from `ℤ × (ι → ℤ)` to
arrays, takes values in `HK^ι(G_•)` and lies in `poly(ℤ^{1+ι}, HK^ι(G_•)_•)`; more generally the
same holds for `g ∈ poly(ℤ^m, G_•)` and configurations `ω ↦ g(n_0 + ∑_{i ∈ ω} h_i)` with
`n_0, h_i ∈ ℤ^m`. For a Taylor expansion `g(n) = ∏_{j ≤ s} a_j^{binom(n, j)}` (`a_j ∈ G_j`, product
in increasing `j`) the face coordinates of the configuration are products of the `a_j` raised to
Boolean binomial polynomials in `(b_0, b)`, those of the face `D` involving only `a_j` with
`j ≥ |D|`. This is the statement that the Leibman group of the cube forms
`Ψ_ω(b_0, b) = b_0 + ∑_{i ∈ ω} b_i` is `HK^ι(G_•)` (Green–Tao, arithmetic regularity, §3).
*Needs:* 0.1–0.2; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.3–0.4 (Taylor expansion, polynomial maps on `ℤ^m`,
closure under affine changes of variable).

## Layer 1: Fourier analysis of compact torus actions

`X` is a compact Hausdorff space with a continuous action `T^d × X → X`, written `t +ᵥ x`
(`[AddAction (UnitAddTorus d) X] [ContinuousVAdd (UnitAddTorus d) X]`).

### 1.1 Weight components

For `F ∈ C(X, ℂ)` and `k : d → ℤ`, the weight component is
`F_k(x) = ∫_{T^d} F(t +ᵥ x) · conj(e(k · t)) dt`. Then:

- `F_k ∈ C(X, ℂ)`, `‖F_k‖ ≤ ‖F‖`, and `F ↦ F_k` is continuous and `ℂ`-linear;
- `F_k` has weight `k`: `F_k(t +ᵥ x) = e(k · t) F_k(x)`; a function of weight `k` is its own
  `k`-component and has `l`-component `0` for `l ≠ k`;
- products add weights and conjugation negates them;
- for a `T^d`-invariant Borel probability `ν`, `∫ F_k dν = 0` for `k ≠ 0` and `∫ F_0 dν = ∫ F dν`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `UnitAddTorus.mFourier`, `AddCircle.haarAddCircle`, `Measure.pi`,
`continuous_parametric_integral_of_continuous`.

### 1.2 Density of weight vectors

- The Fejér means `σ_R F = ∑_{|k_i| < R} ∏_i (1 − |k_i|/R) F_k` converge to `F` uniformly as
  `R → ∞`, uniformly for `F` in a compact subset of `C(X, ℂ)`.
- Hence the functions of some weight span a dense star subalgebra of `C(X, ℂ)`.
- If `X` is a metric space on which every `t` acts by an isometry, `F_k` is `L`-Lipschitz when `F`
  is, and the Lipschitz functions of some weight span a dense subalgebra.
- The functions of weight `0` are exactly the continuous functions constant on orbits, that is the
  pull-backs of `C(X ⧸ T^d, ℂ)` along the orbit map (Mathlib `MulAction.orbitRel.Quotient`, in its
  additive form).

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `StarSubalgebra`. The Fejér kernel on `T^d`,
`∏_i ∑_{|k_i| < R} (1 − |k_i|/R) e(k_i t_i)`, its nonnegativity, unit mass and concentration at `0`
are built here.

**Checks.**
- On `X = T^d` acting on itself, `(e(m · ·))_k = [m = k] e(m · ·)`, and `F_k = F̂(k) e(k · ·)` with
  `F̂(k)` Mathlib's `UnitAddTorus.mFourierCoeff F k`.
- On the trivial action every `F` has weight `0`.

## Layer 2: invariant probabilities on nilmanifolds

Standing hypotheses: Convention 4.

### 2.1 Existence and uniqueness

**Theorem.** `G ⧸ Γ` carries exactly one `G`-invariant Borel probability measure
(`existsUnique_smulInvariant_probabilityMeasure`), written `μ_{G/Γ}`; it is regular and charges
every nonempty open set.

Route. Existence: `G` is nilpotent, hence solvable, and acts on the compact Hausdorff space
`G ⧸ Γ` by homeomorphisms, so AmenableGroups `exists_regular_smulInvariantMeasure_of_isSolvable`
(its §9.6) gives a regular invariant probability; minimality of the transitive action gives
positivity on open sets (Mathlib
`MeasureTheory.measure_isOpen_pos_of_smulInvariant_of_compact_ne_zero`). Uniqueness: induction on
the dimension of `G`. Let `Z` be the last nontrivial term of the lower central series, a central
rational subgroup (NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2); `Z/(Z ∩ Γ)` is a torus `T^d` acting freely on
`G ⧸ Γ` (2.2). An invariant probability `ν` is `T^d`-invariant, so by 1.1 it is determined by its
values on weight-`0` functions, which are pull-backs from `(G/Z) ⧸ (ΓZ/Z)`; the push-forward of `ν`
there is invariant, hence unique by induction. The base case `G = ℝ^n`, `Γ` a lattice, is uniqueness
of Haar probability on the compact group `ℝ^n ⧸ Γ` (Mathlib
`MeasureTheory.Measure.isAddLeftInvariant_eq_smul`). *Needs:* AmenableGroups §9.6;
NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2, 2.5 (rationality of the lower central series, quotient
nilmanifolds); 1.1–1.2; Mathlib `MeasureTheory.SMulInvariantMeasure`, `ProbabilityMeasure`.

**Checks.**
- For `G = ℝ`, `Γ = ℤ`, `μ_{G/Γ}` is `AddCircle.haarAddCircle` transported along
  `ℝ ⧸ ℤ ≃ UnitAddCircle`.
- For a fundamental domain `𝓕` of `Γ` and a Haar measure `μ_G` with `μ_G 𝓕 = 1`, `μ_{G/Γ}` is the
  quotient measure of Mathlib's `MeasureTheory.QuotientMeasureEqMeasurePreimage` (connected
  nilpotent Lie groups are unimodular).

### 2.2 Fibrations and central tori

Let `N` be a closed connected rational normal subgroup.

- `ΓN/N` is discrete cocompact in `G/N`, and `p : G ⧸ Γ → (G/N) ⧸ (ΓN/N)` is a continuous open
  surjection with fibres the translates of `N/(N ∩ Γ)`; `p_* μ_{G/Γ} = μ_{(G/N)/(ΓN/N)}`.
- Fibre averages: for `F ∈ C(G ⧸ Γ, ℂ)`, `x ↦ ∫_{N/(N∩Γ)} F(u • x) du` is continuous, descends along
  `p`, and integrates to `∫ F dμ_{G/Γ}` (disintegration of `μ_{G/Γ}` over `p`).
- If `N` is central, `N ⧸ (Γ.subgroupOf N)` is a torus `T^{dim N}` (a connected abelian Lie group
  with a cocompact lattice, `ℝ^d ⧸ ℤ^d` in Mal'cev coordinates), acting continuously and freely on
  `G ⧸ Γ`; Layer 1 applies, and the weight components of `F` are its components of fixed vertical
  frequency when `N = G_s` (vertical characters of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 4.4).

*Needs:* 2.1, Layer 1; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2, 2.5, 4.4 (quotient nilmanifolds and Mal'cev
bases of rational subgroups, vertical characters).

### 2.3 Sub-nilmanifolds, translates and finite covers

- For a closed connected rational subgroup `H` and a rational element `σ`, `H ∩ σΓσ⁻¹` is cocompact
  in `H`, the orbit `H • (σΓ)` is closed in `G ⧸ Γ`, and
  `H ⧸ (H ∩ σΓσ⁻¹) → G ⧸ Γ`, `h ↦ hσΓ`, is a homeomorphism onto it. Its *Haar measure*
  `μ_{H•σΓ}` is the push-forward of the invariant probability of `H ⧸ (H ∩ σΓσ⁻¹)`; for `c ∈ G`, the
  translate `c • μ_{H•σΓ}` is the Haar measure of `cHc⁻¹ • (cσΓ)`.
- For `Λ ≤ Γ` of finite index, `G ⧸ Λ → G ⧸ Γ` is a finite covering map and pushes `μ_{G/Λ}` to
  `μ_{G/Γ}`. For a rational `σ`, `Γ ∩ σΓσ⁻¹` has finite index in `Γ` and in `σΓσ⁻¹`.
- A finite average `d⁻¹ ∑_{r<d} μ_r` of such Haar measures is a Borel probability on `G ⧸ Γ`; the
  limit laws of Layer 6 have this form.

*Needs:* 2.1–2.2; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2, 4.3 (rational subgroups, rational elements,
commensurability).

## Layer 3: horizontal phases and irrationality

Standing hypotheses: Convention 4, and an `ℕ`-filtration `G_•`. Horizontal characters, the
horizontal torus `G/[G, G]Γ`, vertical characters, the horizontal spaces
`Horiz_j(G) = G_j/K_j` with `K_j = G_{j+1} · ∏_{0<i<j} ⁅G_i, G_{j−i}⁆`, their tori
`Horiz_j(G)/Horiz_j(Γ)` and the horizontal Taylor coefficients `Taylor_j(g) ∈ Horiz_j(G)` are those
of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.5 and 4.4.

### 3.1 The horizontal projection

- `π_ab : G ⧸ Γ → G ⧸ (⁅G, G⁆ ⊔ Γ)` is continuous, surjective and equivariant, and pushes `μ_{G/Γ}`
  to Haar probability on the horizontal torus (2.2 with `N = ⁅G, G⁆`, closed, connected and
  rational). Horizontal characters are exactly the composites of `π_ab` with the continuous
  characters of the horizontal torus.
- In a Mal'cev basis adapted to the lower central series, the first `m_ab = dim G − dim ⁅G, G⁆`
  coordinates `ψ_1, …, ψ_{m_ab}` give an isomorphism of the horizontal torus with `T^{m_ab}`, and
  horizontal characters are `η = k · (ψ_1, …, ψ_{m_ab})` modulo `1`, `k ∈ ℤ^{m_ab}`.

*Needs:* 2.2; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.3, 4.4; Mathlib `UnitAddTorus`.

**Checks.**
- On the Heisenberg nilmanifold with `x, y, z` the superdiagonal and corner entries, horizontal
  characters are `(x, y, z) ↦ ax + by` modulo `1` with `a, b ∈ ℤ`; `(x, y, z) ↦ x/2` modulo `1` is a
  continuous homomorphism `G → ℝ/ℤ` that does not vanish on `Γ`.

### 3.2 Level characters and irrationality

For `j ≥ 1`, a *level-`j` character* is a continuous homomorphism `ξ : G_j → ℝ/ℤ` vanishing on
`K_j` and on `Γ ∩ G_j`, that is the pull-back of a continuous character of the torus
`Horiz_j(G)/Horiz_j(Γ)` (`IsLevelCharacter`; Green–Tao, arithmetic regularity, Definitions A.5–A.6,
where these characters define irrationality). For a full filtration the level-`1` characters are
the horizontal characters vanishing on `G_2`. A sequence `g_N ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` is **irrational at
scales `L_N`** when `L_N^j ‖ξ(Taylor_j(g_N))‖_{ℝ/ℤ} → ∞` for every `1 ≤ j ≤ s` and every nontrivial
level-`j` character `ξ` (`IsIrrationalAt`, stated with the Taylor coefficients `a_{j,N} ∈ G_j` of
NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.4, which represent `Taylor_j(g_N)`). For a rational element `σ` and a
closed connected rational subgroup `H`, the same notions are taken relative to the lattice
`H ∩ σΓσ⁻¹` of `H` (the Mal'cev characters of sub-nilmanifolds); for commensurable lattices the
two irrationality conditions agree, since `‖θ‖ ≤ q ‖θ/q‖`.
*Needs:* 3.1, 2.3; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.4, 0.5, 4.4.

### 3.3 Phases of polynomial sequences

- For a horizontal character `η` and `g ∈ poly(ℤ^m, G_•)` of degree at most `s` with Taylor
  coefficients `a_J ∈ G_{|J|}`, `η(g(n)) = ∑_{|J| ≤ s} η(a_J) binom(n, J)` in `ℝ/ℤ`; so `η ∘ g` is
  constant if and only if `η(a_J) = 0` for all `J ≠ 0`.
- If all `η(a_J)`, `J ≠ 0`, are rational (have finite order in `ℝ/ℤ`), some power `η^q` (`q ≥ 1`),
  nontrivial when `η` is, makes `η^q ∘ g` constant.
- The horizontal projection `π_ab ∘ g` is, in the coordinates of 3.1, the torus polynomial
  `torusPolynomial` of ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.6 whose coefficients are the reductions
  modulo `1` of the monomial coefficients of the real polynomials `n ↦ ψ_i(g n)`
  (NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 4.1); so `polynomial_equidistribution_iff` of its §2.5 decides its
  equidistribution.

*Needs:* 3.1; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.4, 4.1; ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §§2.5–2.6.

## Layer 4: cube nilmanifolds

Standing hypotheses: Convention 4, a full rational filtration `G_•` of degree at most `s`, and a
finite type `ι`. `Γ^ι` is `Subgroup.pi Set.univ (fun _ => Γ)` in `Finset ι → G`.

### 4.1 Cube groups are nilmanifolds

- `hkCube G_• ι k` is a closed connected subgroup of the Lie group `Finset ι → G`, the face
  coordinates of 0.2 give a homeomorphism `HK^ι(G_•) ≃ₜ ∏_D G_{|D|}`, and `HK^ι(G_•)` is a
  connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group.
- `Γ^ι ∩ HK^ι(G_•)` is discrete and cocompact in `HK^ι(G_•)`; in face coordinates it is
  `∏_D (Γ ∩ G_{|D|})` (the coefficients of an integral array are integral, by the recursion of 0.2
  and the rationality of the `G_j`). The filtration `HK^ι(G_•)_•` is rational for this lattice.
- A Mal'cev basis of `HK^ι(G_•)` adapted to `HK^ι(G_•)_•` is obtained by concatenating, over the
  faces `D` in the order of 0.2, a Mal'cev basis of `G` adapted to `G_•` restricted to `G_{|D|}`
  (in the sense of NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences).

The same holds for the Leibman square `G^□` of 0.4 with lattice `Γ^□ = (Γ × Γ) ∩ G^□`, and for
`G^□/Δ(G_s)` with the image lattice.

*Needs:* Layer 0; 2.3; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 2.2–2.3 (Mal'cev bases, rational subgroups).

### 4.2 Haar measure on cube nilmanifolds

**Theorem.** `HK^ι(G_•) ⧸ (Γ^ι ∩ HK^ι(G_•))` carries a unique `HK^ι(G_•)`-invariant Borel
probability (`existsUnique_hkCube_invariant`), and its push-forward `μ^{[ι]}` along the closed
embedding into `(G ⧸ Γ)^{Finset ι}` is the *cube measure*. Then:

- each vertex marginal of `μ^{[ι]}` is `μ_{G/Γ}`, and its marginal on a lower face is `μ^{[ι∖a]}`;
- `μ^{[ι]}` is invariant under the hyperoctahedral symmetries of 0.3 and under `face ∅ (G)`;
- for `ι = Unit`, `μ^{[Unit]}` is `μ_{G/Γ} ⊗ μ_{G/Γ}` (as `HK^{Unit}(G_•) = G × G` for a full
  filtration), and the square nilmanifold `G^□ ⧸ Γ^□` has the Haar measure of 2.1;
- for `F ∈ C(G ⧸ Γ, ℂ)` with vertical frequency `ξ ≠ 0` and `s ≥ 2`, `(x, y) ↦ F(x) conj(F(y))` is
  invariant under `Δ(G_s)` and has integral `0` on `G^□ ⧸ Γ^□` (integrate over the fibres of
  `{1} × G_s`).

*Needs:* 4.1, 2.1–2.3.

- In degree `2` on `G = ℝ` (`G_1 = G_2 = ℝ`, `G_3 = ⊥`), `HK^{Fin 2}(G_•) = ℝ^4` and `μ^{[Fin 2]}`

## Layer 5: van der Corput and Leibman's theorem

### 5.1 The van der Corput lemma

For a bounded `u : ℤ^m → ℂ` and boxes `B(N)` with least side tending to infinity: if for every
`h ∈ ℤ^m` the correlations `|B(N)|⁻¹ ∑_{n ∈ B(N)} u(n + h) conj(u(n))` converge to `γ_h` and the box
averages of `h ↦ γ_h` over `∏_i [0, H)` tend to `0` as `H → ∞`, then the box averages of `u` tend to
`0`. The finite form, for `m = 1`, is the finite lag inequality of
ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5; this layer proves its `ℤ^m` form by averaging `H^m`
translates of the box and applying Cauchy–Schwarz, and the qualitative lemma above for sequences of
compact-space values `n ↦ F(x(n))`. *Needs:* ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5
(`finite_lag_inequality`).

### 5.2 The ineffective single-scale theorem

**Theorem.** Assume Convention 4 and a full rational filtration `G_•` of degree at most `s`. Let
`K ⊆ C(G ⧸ Γ, ℂ)` be compact and `δ > 0`. There are a finite set `U` of nontrivial horizontal
characters and `A > 0` such that for every `N ≥ 1` and every `g ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)`: if some `F ∈ K` has

`|N⁻¹ ∑_{0 ≤ n < N} F(g(n)Γ) − ∫ F dμ_{G/Γ}| ≥ δ`,

then some `η ∈ U` and real numbers `α_0, …, α_s` with `η(g(n)) = ∑_{j ≤ s} α_j binom(n, j)` in
`ℝ/ℤ` for all `n ∈ ℤ` satisfy `‖α_j‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≤ A / N^j` for every `1 ≤ j ≤ s`
(`exists_finset_horizontalCharacter_of_discrepancy`). The `α_j` modulo `1` are the values
`η(a_j)` at the Taylor coefficients (3.3), so the conclusion does not depend on the choice of the
`α_j`.

This is the qualitative shadow of Green–Tao's quantitative Leibman theorem (polynomial orbits,
Theorem 2.9): `U` and `A` depend on `G/Γ`, `G_•`, `K` and `δ` with no explicit bound. The
quantitative statement is NilsequenceEquidistribution's.

Route, by induction on `s`; the statement for degree `s − 1` is used on quotient and square
nilmanifolds.
1. *Reductions.* Left translation by a compact set of representatives of `G ⧸ Γ` and the compactness
   of `K` reduce to `g(0) = 1` (the image of `K` under the translations is compact). If `s = 1`, `G`
   is abelian and the statement is the single-scale Weyl inequality for linear phases
   (`weyl_sum_linear_inverse` of ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5a) on `T^{m_ab}`.
2. *Vertical Fourier.* By 1.2 and the compactness of `K`, every `F ∈ K` is within `δ/4` of a sum of
   boundedly many vertical-frequency components (2.2), drawn from a compact family. The component of
   frequency `0` descends to `(G/G_s) ⧸ (ΓG_s/G_s)`, of degree `s − 1` with the same horizontal
   characters.
3. *Van der Corput.* For a component `F` of frequency `ξ ≠ 0`, `∫ F = 0`; a large average gives, by
   the finite lag inequality, a positive proportion of `h ∈ [1, H)` (with `H` depending only on `δ`)
   for which the average of `F ⊗ conj F` along the square sequence of 0.4 is large. By 4.2 this is a
   discrepancy on the square nilmanifold `G^□/Δ(G_s)`, of degree `s − 1`, against a compact family.
4. *Induction and pigeonhole.* The degree-`(s − 1)` statement gives, for each such `h`, a character
   from a finite set `U^□`; one `η^□ ∈ U^□` serves a positive proportion of `h`. Horizontal
   characters of `G^□` decompose into a horizontal character of `G` on the diagonal and a character
   of `G_2` on `{1} × G_2` vanishing on `⁅G, G_2⁆`; the smoothness of `η^□` along the square
   sequences for many `h`, together with the Vinogradov lemma (`vinogradov_lemma`,
   ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5a) applied to the `h`-dependence, yields a nontrivial
   horizontal character of `G`, from a finite set fixed in advance, with the required smoothness of
   `η ∘ g`.

This is the induction of Green–Tao's polynomial-orbits paper run with ineffective constants.
*Needs:* 5.1, Layers 1–4; ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5a.

### 5.3 Leibman's theorem

**Theorem (Leibman).** Assume Convention 4 and `g ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` for a filtration `G_•` of finite
degree (not necessarily rational). The following are equivalent
(`leibman_equidistributed_tfae`):

1. `n ↦ g(n)Γ` is equidistributed for `μ_{G/Γ}`;
2. `n ↦ π_ab(g(n)Γ)` is equidistributed on the horizontal torus;
3. no nontrivial horizontal character `η` makes `η ∘ g` constant;
4. `n ↦ g(n)Γ` is totally equidistributed (along every progression `qn + r`, `q ≥ 1`).

Route. 1 ⇒ 2 by 2.2. 2 ⇔ 3 ⇔ 4 for the horizontal projection is `polynomial_equidistribution_iff`
(ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5) through 3.3. 3 ⇒ 1: the logarithm of `g` is a polynomial
map to the Lie algebra, so `g` is polynomial for the rescaled lower central filtration
`G_i = G_(⌈i/D⌉)` for some `D` (NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences 0.1, 1.3), which is full and rational;
if 1 fails for a continuous `F`, 5.2 with `K = {F}` gives, for infinitely many `N`, a character from
the finite set `U` with `‖η(a_j)‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≤ A/N^j`, so one `η ∈ U` satisfies this for infinitely many
`N`, whence `η(a_j) = 0` for every `j ≥ 1` and `η ∘ g` is constant (3.3), so 3 fails. 4 follows from
3 applied to `n ↦ g(qn + r)`, again polynomial: if `η ∘ g (qn + r)` is constant, the `η(a_j)`,
`j ≥ 1`, have finite order in `ℝ/ℤ`, and 3.3 gives a nontrivial power of `η` violating 3.
(Leibman, *Pointwise convergence of ergodic averages for polynomial sequences of translations on a
nilmanifold*.) *Needs:* 5.2, 3.3, 2.2; ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2.5.

**Checks.**
- On `G = ℝ`, `Γ = ℤ`, `g(n) = αn²` is equidistributed exactly for irrational `α`: horizontal
  characters are `x ↦ kx` modulo `1`, and `kαn²` is constant modulo `1` for some `k ≠ 0` iff
  `α ∈ ℚ`.
- On the Heisenberg nilmanifold, `g(n) = (nα, nβ, 0)` (coordinates as in 3.1, with `1, α, β`
  linearly independent over `ℚ`) is equidistributed, although its central coordinate is not
  controlled; `g(n) = (nα, 0, 0)` is not, since `η(x, y, z) = y` vanishes along it.

### 5.4 Many parameters

For `g ∈ poly(ℤ^m, G_•)`, 5.2 holds for boxes: for each compact `K` and `δ > 0` there are `U` and
`A` such that a discrepancy `≥ δ` of the average over a box `∏_i [0, N_i)` with all `N_i ≥ N` forces
some `η ∈ U` with `‖α_J‖_{ℝ/ℤ} ≤ A / N^{|J|}` for all `J ≠ 0` in the binomial expansion of 3.3; and
5.3 holds with box-equidistribution in 1, 2 and with constancy modulo `1` of the polynomial `η ∘ g`
on `ℤ^m` in 3 (Leibman, the `ℤ^d` version). Route: the proof of 5.2 with the box form of 5.1.
*Needs:* 5.1–5.3.

## Layer 6: limit laws of polynomial orbits

Standing hypotheses: Convention 4, a full rational filtration `G_•` of degree at most `s`.

### 6.1 Cube equidistribution for irrational sequences

**Theorem.** Let `a_{j,N} ∈ G_j` (`0 ≤ j ≤ s`, `N ∈ ℕ`), `g_N(n) = ∏_{j ≤ s} a_{j,N}^{binom(n, j)}`
(product in increasing `j`), and suppose `(g_N)` is irrational at scales `N` (3.2). Then for every
finite type `ι` the law of `(g_N(b_0 + ∑_{i ∈ ω} b_i)Γ)_{ω : Finset ι}`, for `(b_0, b)` uniform on
`[0, N)^{1+ι}`, converges weakly to `μ^{[ι]}` (`tendsto_cubeAverage_of_isIrrationalAt`, stated
with `[0, N]`, which changes nothing). For `ι` empty:
`n ↦ g_N(n)Γ`, `0 ≤ n < N`, becomes equidistributed for `μ_{G/Γ}`.

The same holds for boxes whose side lengths lie between `cN` and `CN` and whose base point lies in
`[−CN, CN]^{1+ι}`, uniformly over such boxes for fixed `0 < c ≤ C`, and with `b_0` restricted to a
fixed residue class modulo `d` and the `b_i` to multiples of `d`.

Route. By 0.5 the configuration map is a polynomial map `ℤ^{1+ι} → HK^ι(G_•)` whose Taylor
coefficients are products of the `a_{j,N}` along Boolean binomials. A horizontal character `η` of
`HK^ι(G_•)` kills commutators, so its phase along the configuration is additive in the face
coordinates; if `η` is nontrivial, some binomial coefficient `α_J` (`|J| = j ≥ 1`) of the phase is
`ξ(a_{j,N})` for a nontrivial level-`j` character `ξ` of `G_•`, up to a bounded integer multiple
(take the largest `j` on which the relevant restriction of `η` is nontrivial). So irrationality of
`(g_N)` excludes, for large `N`, every character in the finite set `U` that 5.4 provides on the cube
nilmanifold (4.1). This is the qualitative counting lemma for the cube forms
(Green–Tao, arithmetic regularity, Theorem 1.11; the cube forms satisfy its flag condition by its
Corollary 3.4(ii)). *Needs:* 0.5, Layer 4, 5.4, 3.2.

### 6.2 Sequential factorisation

**Theorem.** Let `g_N ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` and `L_N → ∞`. After passing to a subsequence there are a
rational filtration `H_•` with `H_j ≤ G_j` and `H_1 = H_0`, an integer `d ≥ 1`, rational elements
`σ_0, …, σ_{d−1}`, and factorisations `g_N(n)Γ = ε_N(n) g'_N(n) γ_N(n) Γ` such that:
- `g'_N ∈ poly(ℤ, H_•)` is irrational at scales `L_N` for `(H_•, H ∩ Γ)`;
- `γ_N(n)Γ = σ_{n mod d}Γ` for every `n`;
- `β ↦ ε_N(L_N β)` converges locally uniformly on `ℝ` to a continuous `ε : ℝ → G`.

Route: if irrationality fails for some fixed bound along a
subsequence, the character of 5.2 splits off a smooth and a rational factor and leaves a polynomial
in a rational subfiltration of strictly smaller total dimension; finitely many steps end in an
irrational sequence. Rational subgroups of bounded complexity are finitely many, and bounded
rational factors have finitely many residue patterns, which fixes `H_•`, `d` and the `σ_r` along a
subsequence. Green–Tao's factorisation lemma (arithmetic regularity, Lemma 2.9) is the quantitative
form, which this theorem does not need. *Needs:* 5.2, 3.2, 2.3; NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences
2.2, 4.3 (rational subgroups, rational elements).

### 6.3 Limit laws

For the factorisation of 6.2 (with `H = H_0`):
- the empirical laws of `g_N(n)Γ`, `0 ≤ n < ⌊L_N⌋`, converge weakly to
  `d⁻¹ ∑_{r<d} ∫_0^1 ε(β) • μ_{H•σ_rΓ} dβ`;
- for every finite `ι`, `β ∈ (0, 1)` and residue `r`, the law of the cube configuration of `g_N` at
  base `b_0 ≈ βL_N`, `b_0 ≡ r (mod d)`, with increments `b_i ∈ d·[0, αL_N)`, converges as `N → ∞`
  and then `α → 0` to the image of the Haar measure of `HK^ι(H_•) ⧸ (HK^ι(H_•) ∩ (σ_rΓσ_r⁻¹)^ι)`
  translated by the diagonal of `ε(β)` and right-translated by `σ_r`.

Route: 6.1 for `H_•` with the lattice `H ∩ σ_rΓσ_r⁻¹`, commensurable with `H ∩ Γ` (2.3), after
freezing the smooth factor on boxes of side `αL_N`. *Needs:* 6.1–6.2, 2.3.

### 6.4 Leibman's orbit-closure theorem

**Theorem (Leibman).** For `g ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•)` there are `d ≥ 1`, a closed connected rational
subgroup `H` and rational elements `σ_0, …, σ_{d−1}` and `c ∈ G` such that, for each `r < d`,
`n ↦ g(dn + r)Γ` is equidistributed for the Haar measure of the sub-nilmanifold
`c H c⁻¹ • (cσ_rΓ)`; the orbit closure of `g` is the union of these `d` sub-nilmanifolds.
Route: 6.2–6.3 for the constant sequence `g_N = g`, `L_N = N`, along every subsequence; for a fixed
polynomial a bounded smooth factor is constant, because `N^j ‖θ‖_{ℝ/ℤ}` bounded for all `N` forces
`θ ∈ ℤ`, and the limit does not depend on the subsequence, by 5.3 applied to `n ↦ g(dn + r)` on the
sub-nilmanifold. (Leibman, *Pointwise convergence of ergodic
averages for polynomial sequences of translations on a nilmanifold*.) *Needs:* 6.2–6.3, 5.3.

**Checks.**
- On `G = ℝ`, `Γ = ℤ`: `g(n) = n/2 + n²√2` is equidistributed (`d = 1`, `H = ℝ`); for `g(n) = n/3`,
  `H` is trivial, `d = 3`, and the closure is three points; for `g(n) = n/2 + n√2`, `d = 1` and
  `H = ℝ` again, while `n ↦ g(2n)` and `n ↦ g(2n + 1)` are each equidistributed as well.

## Layer 7: PET induction and polynomial recurrence

`G` is a group with a full filtration `G_•` of degree at most `s` (so `G` is nilpotent),
acting on a topological space `X` by homeomorphisms, `[MulAction G X] [ContinuousConstSMul G X]`;
`G` carries no topology. Put `L = s + 1` and `S = {p ∈ poly(ℤ, G_•) | p(0) = 1}`.

### 7.1 Minimal subsystems

A nonempty compact `X` contains a nonempty closed `G`-invariant subset on which the action is
minimal (Zorn's lemma on closed invariant nonempty subsets, ordered by reverse inclusion); for a
minimal action on a compact space and nonempty open `U`, finitely many translates
`g_1⁻¹ • U, …, g_m⁻¹ • U` cover `X` (Mathlib `IsCompact.exists_finite_cover_smul`). *Needs:* Mathlib
`MulAction.IsMinimal`, `zorn_superset_nonempty`.

### 7.2 PET weights

- *PET filtration.* `S_d = {p ∈ poly(ℤ, G_{•+(L−d)}) | p(0) = 1}` for `d ≤ L`, `S_d = S` for
  `d ≥ L`. Each `S_d` is a normal subgroup of `S`, `S_0 = {1}`, and `d ↦ S_d` is monotone. The
  *PET degree* of `p ∈ S` is the least `d` with `p ∈ S_d`. For `G = ℤ` (additively) with `G_i = ℤ`
  for `i ≤ s`, `S_d` is the set of integer-valued polynomials of degree less than `d` vanishing at
  `0`.
- *Two lowering properties.* For `p ∈ S` of PET degree `d ≥ 1`, `n ∈ ℤ` and `g ∈ G`, the maps
  `k ↦ p(n)⁻¹ p(n + k) p(k)⁻¹` and `k ↦ g⁻¹ p(k) g p(k)⁻¹` lie in `S_{d−1}`.
- *Classes and weight.* `p, p' ∈ S` of the same PET degree `d` are *equivalent* when
  `p' p⁻¹ ∈ S_{d−1}`. The *PET weight* of a finite `P ⊆ S` is the multiset of the PET degrees of the
  equivalence classes meeting `P ∖ {1}`.
- *Reduction.* For `q ∈ P ∖ {1}` of least PET degree and a finite `I ⊆ ℤ`, the reduced family is
  `{k ↦ p(n)⁻¹ p(n + k) q(k)⁻¹ | p ∈ P, n ∈ I} ⊆ S`. Its PET weight is smaller than that of `P` in
  the Dershowitz–Manna order (Mathlib `Multiset.IsDershowitzMannaLT`): classes of degree above that
  of `q`, and of that degree but not containing `q`, persist, and the class of `q` is replaced by
  classes of smaller degree. Conjugating every member by one `g ∈ G` does not change the weight.

(Bergelson, *Weakly mixing PET*; Bergelson–Leibman.) *Needs:* NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences
0.2–0.3 (polynomial maps, closure under shifts, products and conjugation); Mathlib
`Multiset.wellFounded_isDershowitzMannaLT`.

### 7.3 Topological polynomial recurrence

**Theorem.** Let `X` be compact, the action minimal (`[MulAction.IsMinimal G X]`), and `P ⊆ S`
finite. For every nonempty open `U ⊆ X` and every `N` there are `n > N` and `x ∈ U` with
`p(n) • x ∈ U` for every `p ∈ P` (`exists_smul_mem_of_isMinimal`).

Route: well-founded induction on the PET weight of `P` (7.2). For weight `0` every member is `1`.
Otherwise cover `X` by `g_1⁻¹ • U, …, g_m⁻¹ • U` (7.1), apply the induction hypothesis to reduced
families conjugated by the `g_i` repeatedly to build `m + 1` points and return times, and conclude
by the pigeonhole principle, as in Bergelson–Leibman's proof of the topological polynomial van der
Waerden theorem, there for commuting transformations. *Needs:* 7.1–7.2.

### 7.4 Colourings

- **Polynomial van der Waerden in nilpotent groups.** For a finite colouring `χ : G → C`, a finite
  `P ⊆ S` and `N`, there are `n > N` and `a ∈ G` with `χ(p(n) a) = χ(a)` for all `p ∈ P`
  (`exists_monochromatic_polynomial`). Route: 7.3 for `G` acting on the orbit closure of `χ` in the
  compact space `C^G` by `(a • ψ)(b) = ψ(b a)`, restricted to a minimal subsystem (7.1), with `U` a
  cylinder `{ψ | ψ(1) = c}` meeting it.
- **Polynomial words.** If `G` is nilpotent with lower central series `γ_i`, `a_1, …, a_r ∈ G` and
  `p_1, …, p_r ∈ ℤ[X]` vanish at `0` with degrees at most `D ≥ 1`, then
  `k ↦ a_1^{p_1(k)} ⋯ a_r^{p_r(k)}` lies in `S` for the filtration `G_i = γ_{⌈i/D⌉}` (`G_0 = G`). So
  the previous item applies to every finite family of polynomial words in a nilpotent group.
- **Finite alternatives.** For finite `P ⊆ S` and a finite set `C` there is a finite set
  `O ⊆ {n | 0 < n} × G` such that every `χ : G → C` has some `(n, a) ∈ O` with `χ(p(n) a) = χ(a)`
  for all `p ∈ P`: the sets `{χ | ∀ p ∈ P, χ(p(n) a) = χ(a)}` are clopen in the compact space `C^G`
  and cover it.

*Needs:* 7.1, 7.3; Mathlib `Pi.compactSpace`, `isClopen_discrete`, `lowerCentralSeries`.

**Checks.**
- For `G = ℤ` (additively, `G_1 = G_2 = ℤ`, `G_3 = ⊥`) and `P = {n ↦ n, n ↦ 2n, …, n ↦ kn}`, 7.4
  is van der Waerden's theorem; for `P = {n ↦ n²}` it is the Furstenberg–Sárközy theorem for
  colourings.
- `p(0) = 1` cannot be dropped: for `G = ℤ`, `p(n) = 1 + 2n` (additively) and the colouring by
  parity, `χ(p(n) + a) ≠ χ(a)` for every `n` and `a`.

## Downstream consumers

NilsequenceEquidistribution and GowersInverseTheorem use the horizontal and level characters
(Layer 3), the Haar measures of nilmanifolds and sub-nilmanifolds (Layer 2) and the cube groups with
their face coordinates (Layers 0 and 4). Counting and regularity arguments over the integers that
need limits of cube configurations along polynomial orbits use Layer 6.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns:

- Host–Kra cube groups `HK^ι(G_•)` of an arbitrary filtered group, their filtrations, face
  coordinates, symmetries and restrictions, the Leibman square and the square of a polynomial
  sequence, and the statement that cube configurations of polynomial maps are polynomial in the
  cube group;
- weight components, Fejér density and orbit-constant functions for continuous actions of compact
  tori on compact spaces;
- existence and uniqueness of the invariant probability on a nilmanifold, its compatibility with
  fibrations, central tori, finite covers, sub-nilmanifolds and translates;
- horizontal characters, the horizontal torus, level characters, irrationality at scales, and phases
  of polynomial sequences;
- cube nilmanifolds and square nilmanifolds with their lattices, Mal'cev bases and Haar measures;
- the van der Corput lemma on `ℤ^m` in qualitative form, the ineffective single-scale theorem,
  Leibman's equidistribution criterion in one and many parameters;
- cube equidistribution for irrational sequences, sequential factorisation, limit laws and Leibman's
  orbit-closure theorem;
- minimal subsystems, PET filtrations and weights, topological polynomial recurrence for nilpotent
  group actions and its colouring corollaries.

It does not own:

- **NilmanifoldsAndNilsequences:** filtrations of groups, polynomial maps and the Lazard–Leibman
  theorem, Taylor expansions, Host–Kra cube groups as abstract groups with the cube
  characterisation and the face factorisation for full filtrations, horizontal spaces and
  horizontal Taylor coefficients; BCH groups, rational filtered nilmanifolds, rational subgroups,
  Mal'cev bases, quotients and restrictions, rational elements, basepoint normalisation; metrics
  and Lipschitz functions; horizontal and vertical tori, characters and frequencies; nilsequences,
  nilcharacters and the quantitative vertical Fourier decomposition (its 5.3). Layer 1 here is the
  qualitative Fourier theory of an arbitrary continuous torus action on a compact space; Layer 0
  adds to the abstract cube groups only their shifted levels as a filtration, face coordinates
  for shifted and integral arrays, symmetries, restrictions, Leibman squares and configurations.
- **The Leibman square** `G ×_{G_2} G` with its filtration is owned here (0.4, 4.1), as the tool of
  the induction for Leibman's theorem; NilsequenceEquidistribution consumes it for its quantitative
  induction rather than redefining it.
- **NilsequenceEquidistribution and GowersInverseTheorem:** every equidistribution or factorisation
  statement with explicit dependence of its constants on `δ` and on the complexity of the
  nilmanifold (Green–Tao's quantitative Leibman theorem and factorisation theorem, and their
  degree–rank and multidegree forms), and the inverse theorem.
- **NilpotentLieHeightsAndFiltrations:** filtrations of Lie algebras, heights and BCH arithmetic.
- **ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §2:** equidistribution as weak convergence, Weyl's criterion,
  Weyl's theorem for polynomial sequences in tori and its single-scale forms, the finite lag
  inequality and van der Corput's difference theorem on tori, and the Vinogradov lemma.
- **AmenableGroups Layer 9:** the Markov–Kakutani theorem, its solvable form and invariant measures
  for solvable actions on compact spaces.
- **GowersUniformityNorms:** Gowers and box norms and cubes in abelian groups; nothing here is
  stated for Gowers norms.
- **ClassicalErgodicTheory:** measure-preserving systems, unique ergodicity, and Host–Kra seminorms
  and cube measures of general systems; the cube measures here are Haar measures of nilmanifolds.
- **ArithmeticRamseyTheory and Mathlib:** van der Waerden's and the Hales–Jewett theorem in their
  finite forms; Layer 7 proves the polynomial theorem for nilpotent groups, which neither states.

## Provenance

A Lean development of Hindman's finite sums-and-products theorem formalizes, in a coordinate model
(a homeomorphism `G ≃ ℝ^n` with polynomial group law and `Γ` the integer points), most of Layers
0–7: cube groups and their face coordinates, Leibman squares and their filtrations, uniqueness of
invariant measures on nilmanifolds and cube nilmanifolds, horizontal and level characters, the
ineffective single-scale theorem in the binomial form of 5.2 and a monomial form for compact
families and for `1`-Lipschitz tests, Haar limits of cube configurations for irrational Taylor
coefficients, sequential factorisation in the form of 6.2, minimal subsystems, PET weights with the
Dershowitz–Manna order, recurrence for clopen sets of minimal systems, and the colouring
corollaries. Much of it is derived from openai/math (Apache-2.0, commit `adc7f124`). The roadmap
replaces the coordinate model by Lie groups with lattices, states recurrence for open sets, and adds
multiparameter equidistribution and the orbit-closure theorem; the source is not prescriptive.
