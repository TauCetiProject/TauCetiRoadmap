# Spectral manifolds and reconstruction

Develop geometric spectral triples as usable analytic and algebraic objects, and reconstruct compact smooth manifolds from commutative spectral data. The main theorem recovers the smooth algebra, oriented manifold, Hermitian bundle, first-order operator, and, under the scalar-symbol condition, its Riemannian metric. Irreducible Clifford modules and compatible real structures give separate spin-c and spin refinements. Products, perturbations, finite models, and closed-manifold examples make the interfaces usable beyond the reconstruction theorem.

## Scope and conventions

The analytic core is [NoncommutativeGeometry N4–N5](../NoncommutativeGeometry/README.md#n4-weak-ideals-generalized-limits-and-dixmier-traces): use its spectral triples, compact-resolvent calculus, bounded commutators, closed derivation δ(T)=[|D|,T], regularity and logarithmic ideal/Dixmier trace. This roadmap adds geometric axioms, their module/measure interfaces and reconstruction. It does not introduce another spectral-triple or unbounded-operator carrier.

Use a separable complex Hilbert space H, conjugate-linear first inner-product slot, a faithful unital star representation π of a unital complex star algebra 𝒜, and a densely defined self-adjoint native `LinearPMap` D. Identify 𝒜 with its represented image only after proving injectivity. Its C*-closure is A. Geometric dimensions p are positive integers unless the finite dimension-zero construction is explicitly invoked. H is nonzero in positive-dimensional geometric packages. Reconstructed manifolds are compact Hausdorff second-countable smooth real manifolds without boundary, dimension p; connectedness is imposed only on the results that say so. Bundle ranks may be positive locally constant functions on a disconnected base.

Compact resolvent is compactness of `(D−i)⁻¹`; native `TauCeti.LinearPMap.resolvent D i` inverts `i−D`, so differs by a minus sign. Use N5's eigenbasis-independent calculus and prove equal domains before comparing partial operators. Let `Λ=(1+D²)^(1/2)`. Extend `|D|⁻p` by zero on ker D; the finite kernel is retained in domain, index, and eigenvalue statements. A modification of D on its kernel changes singular-trace expressions by finite rank, not its original kernel or index. Norms, summability and local estimates use zero-based singular values μ_j.

Our negative geometric Clifford convention is `c(α)c(β)+c(β)c(α)=−2g*(α,β)Id`, with c(real α)*=−c(real α). For an oriented orthonormal coframe use `Γ=i^(−p(p+1)/2)c(e₁)…c(e_p)`. Then Γ*=Γ and Γ²=1; this convention agrees with SpinGeometry's even-dimensional chirality and fixes the odd Clifford factor by Γ=+1. In odd dimension p=2ℓ+1, SpinGeometry G1 uses `ω_Spin=i^(ℓ+1)c(e₁)…c(e_p)`; our convention is `Γ=(−1)^(ℓ+1)ω_Spin`. Thus Γ=+1 selects its ω_Spin=(−1)^(ℓ+1) factor. Prove and use this dictionary in odd restriction, product and charge-conjugation comparisons. Products are ordered first factor then second, with Pauli matrices σ₁=[[0,1],[1,0]], σ₂=[[0,−i],[i,0]], σ₃=diag(1,−1). Fourier phases follow the consumed native 2π convention.

The commutative reconstruction theorem below has definite strengthened hypotheses. Ordinary regularity alone is not substituted for regularity of every smooth-domain endomorphism, nor is an arbitrary Hochschild orientation cycle silently antisymmetrized. General noncommutative axiom packages have their stated algebraic, analytic and index consequences; no classification of all noncommutative manifolds is asserted. Lorentzian, boundary, noncompact reconstruction, the Standard Model and a general quantum Lip-norm/state-space compactness theory are outside this roadmap's geometric classification. The last is distinct from the classical distance theorem consumed here.

## SM1. Geometric structures on the spectral core

Develop structures extending N5's core rather than copying it. Define morphisms of represented triples by a unital star algebra isomorphism and a unitary intertwining the representation and D, including equality of transported domains. For graded/real triples require the grading/J intertwiners. Prove identity, composition, inverse, extensionality and transport of every following axiom. Develop the groupoid of these isomorphisms; an arbitrary algebra homomorphism is not declared to intertwine Dirac operators.

Build the smooth core `H∞=⋂_(m≥0)Dom D^m` with seminorms `ξ↦‖Λ^mξ‖`. Prove dense inclusion in H and in each power graph domain, completeness, nuclearity from positive-dimensional weak eigenvalue bounds, and independence of |D| versus Λ powers. N5's iterated commutator estimates give the action of 𝒜 on H∞; prove continuity of action, restricted D and bounded regular endomorphisms. Establish all partial-operator power/domain and restriction comparisons used here. On the common smooth core define `δ₁(T)=[D²,T]Λ⁻¹`. Prove that boundedness of all iterates of δ₁ is equivalent to boundedness of all iterates of δ, including domain invariance and estimates `‖δ^n(T)‖≤Σ_(k≤2n)C_(n,k)‖δ₁^k(T)‖`. Use the resolvent integral for `D²Λ⁻¹`, and the bounded commuting difference from |D|, to handle the finite kernel; this is the D²-symbol/filtration test used below.

An even triple has a self-adjoint unitary γ commuting with π(𝒜), preserving Dom D and satisfying Dγ=−γD there. Allow a zero grading summand, as needed by finite models. An odd triple has no grading in its data. This **spectral parity** (presence of γ) is one of three separate data, together with the metric dimension p of SM2 and, for a real structure, its KO-dimension n mod8. A real structure of KO-dimension n is defined on an even triple when n is even and on an odd triple when n is odd (Ćaćić 2012, Definition 2.5); a grading which a triple with odd n happens to carry is not part of its real-structure data. The metric dimension is tied to neither: the strongly oriented package of SM2 has spectral parity p mod2, so its real refinements have n≡p mod2, while the weakly oriented package keeps p independent of the spectral parity and of n. The represented orientation operator χ=π_D(c) of SM2 is a further object; it is the grading only in the strongly oriented even case. Test the separation on three examples: the full Hodge–de Rham triple of a closed oriented manifold of odd dimension p, with exterior-parity grading and complex conjugation, is an even real triple of KO-dimension 0 and metric dimension p (Ćaćić 2012, Example 2.9), weakly but not strongly oriented; the Spin Dirac triple of a closed Spin p-manifold has n=p and spectral parity p mod2; replacing γ by −γ keeps every real structure of even KO-dimension, with the same ε″, and reverses the strong orientation sign.

A real structure is an antiunitary `J:H≃ₗᵢ⋆[ℂ]H`, with J(Dom D)=Dom D and

`J²=ε Id,  JD=ε′ DJ,  Jγ=ε″γJ` (the last only for even n, where the triple carries γ).

Fix the conventional signs as follows; for even n also construct the alternative Jγ, with signs `(εε″,−ε′,ε″)`.

| KO n mod8 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ε | +1 | +1 | −1 | −1 | −1 | −1 | +1 | +1 |
| ε′ | +1 | −1 | +1 | +1 | +1 | −1 | +1 | +1 |
| ε″ | +1 | — | −1 | — | +1 | — | −1 | — |

The rows are `epsilon`, `epsilonPrime` and, indexed by k with n=2k, `epsilonDoublePrime`. `RealStructure D n` is the antiunitary with its first two equations at the signs of column n, so ε and ε′ cannot be chosen apart from n; `EvenRealStructure D k` adds a grading of D (a `Grading`) and the third equation in KO-dimension `evenKO k`=2k, so ε″ and γ occur exactly in even KO-dimension. Neither records the metric dimension.

Define `b°=Jπ(b*)J⁻¹`. Prove it is a complex-linear unital star representation of 𝒜ᵒᵖ. The order-zero condition is `[π(a),b°]=0`; real first-order is `[[D,π(a)],b°]=0`. Derive the right bounded-commutator/domain identities from JD=ε′DJ; these are needed for a bimodule KK class. Give the opposite, conjugate, unitary transport and Jγ comparisons with their actual signs. The fixed algebra `{a: Jπ(a*)J⁻¹=π(a)}` is central under order zero; prove star/algebra closure. In a commutative geometric real refinement require this fixed algebra to equal 𝒜, not merely lie in its centre.

## SM2. Finite, oriented and real geometric axiom packages

State independent conditions, their forgetful maps, and an assembled geometric package. The conventional seven-axiom presentation groups the following data as dimension, regularity, finiteness/absolute continuity, reality, first order, orientability and Poincaré duality. SM9 specifies the last precisely. The commutative reconstruction result SM6 uses the first five analytic/geometric conditions listed below and does not assume reality or Poincaré duality.

1. **Dimension p.** Require `sup_j (j+1)^(1/p) μ_j(Λ⁻¹)<∞`. Prove equivalence with a counting bound `N_D(R)≤C(1+R)^p`, independence under finite-rank kernel changes and membership of `|D|⁻p` in N4's logarithmic ideal. For p=1 this is the pointwise weak ideal, a stronger condition than membership of the logarithmic ideal alone. Derive S_q summability for q>p and heat trace finiteness. The nonzero lower counting bound is proved in SM6, not built into this upper-bound axiom.
2. **Regularity.** Consume N5 regularity of π(a) and [D,π(a)] and its closed graph seminorm topology. Define **endomorphism regularity** to mean that every algebraic 𝒜-linear endomorphism of H∞ extends boundedly to H and lies in every δ-domain. The general **strongly regular package** includes both ordinary N5 regularity and endomorphism regularity. In the finite Hermitian setting SM3 proves the bounded extension first, so it is not an unspecified extra boundedness choice. Give matrix, adjoint, composition, restriction and transport APIs. In the commutative order-one branch, multiplication by a and [D,a] are 𝒜-linear endomorphisms of H∞, so endomorphism regularity implies ordinary regularity there. For a noncommutative represented left action these operators need not be 𝒜-linear; retain ordinary regularity as its separate input. An arbitrary regular bounded perturbation of D is not asserted to preserve all the geometric axioms.
3. **Finiteness and absolute continuity.** Use the represented **left** 𝒜-action. Give an actual integer N, self-adjoint idempotent e∈M_N(𝒜), and a left-module isomorphism `H∞≃𝒜^N e` (row vectors, matrix on the right). Its conjugate-first left Hermitian form is `h(ξ,η)=Ση_i ξ_i*`, so `h(aξ,bη)=b h(ξ,η)a*`. Prove positivity in A, nondegeneracy and the left finite-frame identities. On entering the commutative branch set `f=eᵀ`. The complex-linear 𝒜-module map `v↦vᵀ` identifies these rows with the column model `f𝒜^N`, because `(ve)ᵀ=fvᵀ`; its inverse is transpose. Prove `f²=f=f*` and transport the Hermitian form to the usual `Σξ_i*η_i`. Consume native `Matrix.mulVecLin_transpose` in its commutative scope for the row/column linear-map comparison. Express the projection condition by native `IsStarProjection`, using `isStarProjection_iff'` and `Matrix.star_eq_conjTranspose` to compare it with the displayed equations; `ProjectivePresentation` records the column model `f𝒜^N` with this native predicate. Hermitian e gives `eᵀ=bar(e)` and does not give `eᵀ=e`. Conjugate transpose is conjugate-linear and is not this represented-module isomorphism. Keep the original left-row convention over noncommutative 𝒜; the ordinary transpose comparison here requires commutativity. For real triples compare the right action through J with HilbertModules' opposite-algebra/right-module convention; never silently use the right-module inner-product formula with the represented left action. Fix an N4 admissible Dixmier trace Tr_ω obtained from the logarithmic Cesàro generalized-limit construction specified in SM4, and impose

   `⟨ξ,π(a)η⟩=Tr_ω(π(a h(ξ,η))|D|⁻p)`.

   This equation, for all a,ξ,η, is the absolute-continuity condition with that left-Hermitian convention. Derive traciality of τ_D on the regular smooth algebra by showing `[|D|⁻p,a]` is trace class using N5's iterated commutator/ideal estimates, before invoking trace cyclicity in noncommutative examples. Do not replace it by a Hilbert-valued inner product without its A-valued form. Define `τ_D(a)=Tr_ω(π(a)|D|⁻p)`; prove positivity, continuity on A, and, in the commutative case, full support using faithful π and the finite frame. The reconstruction proof may fix one admissible ω; independence of all generalized limits is a separate measurable-operator conclusion, proved for the geometric operators in SM7.
4. **Order one.** In the commutative package require `[[D,a],b]=0`. In the real noncommutative package use the right action b°. Give the derivation/module and adjoint identities on H∞ and on Dom D before passing to bounded extensions.
5. **Orientation.** Use N1's Hochschild complex. For commutative 𝒜 require a finite p-chain c, antisymmetric in its last p entries, with bc=0; in the strongly oriented package the triple is even with `π_D(c)=γ` when p is even and odd with `π_D(c)=1` when p is odd, where `π_D(a₀⊗…⊗a_p)=π(a₀)[D,π(a₁)]…[D,π(a_p)]`. For the real noncommutative package use coefficients 𝒜⊗𝒜ᵒᵖ, with first coefficient represented by `π(a₀)b₀°`; use `(a·(x⊗y°)·b)=(axb)⊗y°` as the coefficient bimodule. Its boundary is `b(m⊗a₁⊗…⊗a_p)=ma₁⊗a₂⊗…⊗a_p +Σ_(j=1)^(p−1)(−1)^j m⊗…⊗a_j a_(j+1)⊗… +(−1)^p a_p m⊗a₁⊗…⊗a_(p−1)`. Prove comparison with N1's coefficient-complex construction and its representation; the opposite factor is a commuting coefficient, not a replacement for these two module actions. Build functoriality, sums of cycles, normalization, permutation signs and the smooth differential-form comparison. For a commutative algebra antisymmetrization has range in cycles and induces the exterior-form summand; do not assert every Hochschild homology class has an antisymmetric representative before smoothness has been proved.

Also define the **weakly oriented commutative package**: π_D(c)=χ is a self-adjoint unitary commuting with 𝒜, and `[D,a]χ=(−1)^(p+1)χ[D,a]`; χ need not be a grading for D. It retains strong regularity, the other four conditions and, when metric recovery is required in this variant, the explicit scalar-square condition `[D,a]²∈π(𝒜)` for self-adjoint a. Its spectral parity is separate data: a grading γ, when present, commutes with π(𝒜) and anticommutes with D, and is required neither to equal χ nor to commute with it; since each [D,a] anticommutes with γ, `γχ=(−1)^pχγ`. A real structure on a weakly oriented package has KO-dimension of the parity of its spectral grading (SM1), independently of p. Strong orientation is the subclass χD=−Dχ for even p and χ=1 for odd p, regarded as the even triple with grading χ, respectively the odd triple; any other grading γ of the weak package is forgotten in that comparison. Prove the conversion in SM7; it accommodates Hodge–de Rham data without confusing exterior parity and volume chirality.

## SM3. Smooth projective modules, functions and spectral measure

On native locally convex spaces with `WithSeminorms` and a countable separating family of seminorms, construct the compatible translation-invariant metric from `Σ 2^(−n)min(1,p_n(x−y))`, and prove equivalence of completeness with Cauchy convergence in every seminorm. Develop finite products, closed subspaces, complete graph domains and closed-subspace quotients with their seminorm/topology comparisons. Prove the Fréchet open mapping theorem for a continuous surjective linear map between complete metrizable locally convex real/complex spaces by Baire category and successive small lifts; deduce continuity of the inverse of a continuous linear bijection and the Fréchet closed graph theorem. Mathlib's Banach closed graph theorem and its sigma-compact-group open mapping theorem are not substituted without their stronger hypotheses. These precise Fréchet results supply the comparisons below.

Build the smooth Serre–Swan theorem on a compact smooth base with no preassigned spectral metric: finite projective C∞(X)-modules correspond to smooth finite-rank bundles, including variable locally constant ranks, Hermitian forms and maps. Use PDO P1.0's native smooth section module, SBC's bundles and K5a's already specified complete smooth-section topology; smooth orthogonal projection matrices arise from finite smooth frames and a Gram normalization. Conversely construct the image bundle of a smooth self-adjoint idempotent using open invertible minors/smooth local frames. Prove evaluation, restriction, tensor, conjugate, dual, Hom and endomorphism comparisons, full faithfulness, inverse natural isomorphisms and the continuous-completion comparison with TKT T1/HilbertModules. A C(X)-completion alone is not the smooth theorem. This item requires only smooth bundle/section foundations, not a Dirac operator or SM6.

For the commutative package use the fixed `f=eᵀ` column convention from SM2. Before reconstruction let X=Spec A, λ the finite positive Riesz measure τ_D, and `E=fC(X)^N`, initially a continuous image bundle. Construct `H≃f L²(X,λ)^N` using the absolute-continuity equation and density. The transpose preserves the pointwise Hermitian form and its integrated norm, so this unitary extends the original represented left-module comparison. Identify A″ with the scalar L∞ multiplication algebra in this representation; λ has support X, each fibre rank is positive, and the measure is finite, so V2's hypotheses hold. For rows the right multiplier `R_B(v)=vB`, with `B=eBe`, satisfies `R_B∘R_C=R_(CB)`, giving `End_𝒜(𝒜^N e)≃(eM_N(𝒜)e)ᵒᵖ`. Transpose identifies this opposite corner with the ordinary column corner `fM_N(𝒜)f`: `(CB)ᵀ=BᵀCᵀ` and `(B*)ᵀ=(Bᵀ)*`. Prove the transported formula `End_𝒜(H∞)≃fM_N(𝒜)f`, its bounded extension, adjoint and norm estimate on the actual represented module. Its centre is `f𝒜`: if `r=Tr(f)=Tr(e)` and p_j are the polynomial rank projections, `p₀=0` and a scalar representative of a column-corner T is `Σ_(j>0) p_j Tr(T)/j`. Prove these assertions with the actual projection matrices and faithful action, rather than assuming the rank constant. Transport evaluation, frames, restriction, Hom, tensor, conjugate and dual comparisons through this same module equivalence; after reconstruction the resulting smooth bundle is `fC∞(X)^N`. Use this bundle and its endomorphisms in all later Clifford, connection, spin-c and Spin comparisons. Transport D with its full domain and smooth core, and γ and J when present, through the L² unitary; prove the intertwining equations, commutators, adjoints, δ-domains and graph norms. No self-conjugacy, reality or constant-rank hypothesis is added.

Prove, for T∈A″, equivalence of T∈𝒜, T and [D,T] δ-smooth, T δ-smooth, and T(H∞)⊂H∞. Use the centre formula and binomial domain estimates for powers of |D|. Deduce spectral invariance, automatic continuity of characters and Spec𝒜=SpecA. Prove completeness of 𝒜 for δ-seminorms using closedness of δ, and equivalence with the Sobolev seminorms `a↦(Σ_μ‖Λ^s aη_μ‖²)^(1/2)` for a finite generating frame. The corresponding endomorphism/module pairings are jointly continuous. Derive separability and nuclearity of 𝒜 and H∞, and metrizability of the compact spectrum X, from the compact-resolvent eigenvalue bound; do not assume X a manifold for these steps.

For the nuclear statements, construct the nuclear Fréchet-space interface used here on native locally convex spaces: use BanachSpaceTheory B4's existing nuclear-operator quotient-norm/absolutely-summable rank-one series between the Banach seminorm completions. Define a nuclear Fréchet space by nuclearity of the connecting map to each seminorm completion from a stronger one; prove independence of the defining family, the nuclear-space consequences for N1's existing completed projective tensor, and the finite-sum, closed-subspace and Hilbert-scale criteria needed here. Consume N1's locally convex tensor carrier, generic universal property and Fréchet completeness rather than constructing a competing completion. Do not rebuild B4's nuclear Banach-operator theory. In the spectral Hilbert scale choose sufficiently separated powers of Λ so their inclusion is trace class by the dimension bound. This proves nuclearity of H∞ and its finite closed subspaces and identifies the nuclear topology used in SM10. These are owned functional-analytic targets, not an assumption inferred from the word Fréchet.

Develop smooth joint functional calculus for commuting self-adjoint elements of 𝒜. After extending a smooth function near their joint spectrum with compact support, integrate its rapidly decaying Fourier transform against the commuting exponentials. Prove `δ^k(e^(isa))` grows polynomially, convergence in every Fréchet seminorm, the evaluation and chain rules. Construct partitions of unity in 𝒜 subordinate to a finite cover of X by such coordinate-domain opens, using cutoffs and this calculus. These precede SM6's identification with C∞(X).

## SM4. Lorentz estimates and spectral multiplicity

For every real p≥1 construct the symmetric norm ideal

`L^(p,1)={T compact: Σ_(j≥0)(j+1)^(1/p−1) μ_j(T)<∞}`.

Use OperatorIdeals' singular values and Ky Fan gauges. Prove completeness, finite-rank density, adjoint/unitary invariance, two-sided bounded-multiplication estimates, monotonicity and the trace-class p=1 identification. Prove `‖T‖_(p,1)≤C_p rank(T)^(1/p)‖T‖` for finite rank and `‖T‖_(p,1)≤C_p‖T‖₁^(1/p)‖T‖^(1−1/p)` for trace class, including p=1 separately. Establish the needed singular-value sum estimates directly, without assuming an unavailable interpolation theorem.

For a finite tuple of bounded self-adjoint operators t=(t₁,…,t_p), define the quasicentral modulus `k_(p,1)(t)` as the cofinal liminf of `max_j‖[B,t_j]‖_(p,1)` over finite-rank positive contractions B approaching Id in the order filter. Develop unitary invariance, restrictions/reducing sums, finite-dimensional zero cases and estimates from finite-rank cutoffs. Prove that strongly convergent positive finite-rank contractions can be used for an upper bound by replacing R with `P+(1−P)R(1−P)` for every fixed finite-rank projection P; strong convergence alone does not establish cofinality. Prove the Voiculescu multiplicity formula

`k_(p,1)(t)^p = γ_p ∫_(ℝ^p) m_ac(y)dy`, with `0<γ_p<∞`,

for integer p≥1 and a commuting self-adjoint p-tuple on separable H, with the same ideal norm throughout. Build the proof via the joint Lebesgue/singular spectral decomposition, quasicentral finite-rank constructions and the matching lower bound on absolutely continuous multiplicity; include the one-operator trace-class endpoint, constants under norm normalization and infinite multiplicity/infinite integral. This formula is a genuine theorem target here, not an assumption supplied by the mere existence of a direct integral.
