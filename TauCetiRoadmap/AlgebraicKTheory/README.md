# Algebraic K-theory in low degrees: matrices, projectives and patching

Develop the algebraic K-groups of arbitrary associative unital rings through degrees zero, one and two, with their usable matrix and projective-module APIs, relative groups, stable-range theorems, and Milnor patching. The central applications are computing low-degree invariants, transporting projectives across pullbacks, and constructing explicit connecting maps. All ring homomorphisms preserve one. Rings can be noncommutative and can be the zero ring. A division-ring or nonzero-space calculation states its nontriviality hypothesis.

Algebraic K₁ is the abelianization of the stable general linear group. It is not the group of connected components of a topological matrix group. For instance K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ) is ℂˣ, whereas K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(ℂ)=0. The comparison in A8 constructs the quotient map and its kernel; it does not identify these functors.

The definitive specification is this README. Suggested.lean illustrates some native-carrier signatures, rather than enumerating the targets.

## Conventions, dependencies and ownership

Use finitely generated projective **right** R-modules, expressed in Lean as left modules over Rᵐᵒᵖ. Matrices act on columns by left multiplication; this commutes with right scalar multiplication. Extension of scalars is P⊗ᵣS, with the native bimodule/opposite-ring encoding and the relation pr⊗s=p⊗f(r)s. Do not require R to be commutative to form this tensor product. Use the commuting-action vocabulary in Mathlib.Algebra.Module.Bimodule. Construct the balanced tensor over a noncommutative ring from the additive tensor over ℤ by quotienting the subgroup generated by pr⊗s−p⊗f(r)s; prove its balanced universal property, pure-tensor induction, functoriality, associator, unit isomorphisms, right S-action and finite-free base-change formulas here. This extends the native commutative tensor API and specializes back to it by a proved natural equivalence. Give the comparison with the left-module convention by passing to Rᵐᵒᵖ, including the transpose and inverse conventions on matrix automorphisms.


The balanced construction first acts on **all** modules. For a unital map f:R→S
of arbitrary associative rings, construct the additive extension functor on left
modules E_f(M)=S⊗_R M with relations (s f(r))⊗m=s⊗(r m), left S-action
a·(s⊗m)=(as)⊗m and map E_f(g)(s⊗m)=s⊗g(m). Reuse native
`ModuleCat.restrictScalars f` for restriction, whose R-action is r·n=f(r)·n.
Construct the natural adjunction
`Hom_S(E_f(M),N) ≃ Hom_R(M,Res_f(N))`
by h↦(m↦h(1⊗m)) and g↦(s⊗m↦s·g(m)). Prove balanced well-definedness,
linearity, both inverse laws and naturality in M,N. Give its unit m↦1⊗m,
counit s⊗n↦s·n, and both triangle identities on pure tensors before packaging
the native categorical adjunction. Use a common module universe containing the

restriction along an arbitrary ring map preserves finite projectivity, nor

[GrothendieckEulerForms Layers1–2](../GrothendieckEulerForms/README.md) owns the essentially-small categorical split K₀ construction, its additive universal property and group-completion comparison. Consume the actual TauCeti.CategoryTheory.GrothendieckGroup.Split API, including SplitK0.of, of_biprod, exists_eq_sub, liftEquiv, map and mapEquiv. ## A0. Finite projectives and matrix K₀

Use Proj(R)=(TauCeti.finiteProjectiveModules Rᵐᵒᵖ).FullSubcategory from TauCeti.Algebra.Category.ModuleCat.CartanMap.Basic. Adopt its existing native Module.Finite/Module.Projective objects, repleteness, zero, biproducts, essential smallness and induced exact structure; the theorem finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split identifies that structure as split. Extend its API with the finite-presentation/idempotent small models, explicit universe transport and direct-summand closure needed by the matrix comparison. A projective module here is not required to have a chosen basis or constant rank. Prove that every object is a direct summand of Rⁿ for some finite n and provide a complementary finite projective with actual splitting maps.

Use K₀(R) as the supplied split K₀ of this existing Proj(R), with its canonical comparison to the existing induced exact K₀, using finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split. Establish extension-of-scalars additive functors for all unital ring maps, including their associator/unit isomorphisms, and the functorial K₀ maps. Prove that base change preserves finite projectivity by transporting a finite free splitting, without a flatness hypothesis on S. Give identity/composition/naturality, equivalence invariance, finite products and matrix Morita equivalences, fixing the corner-module convention for Mₙ(R), n≥1.

Use idempotents e²=e in Mₙ(R), with no adjoint or topology. Relate their images as right modules to all finite projectives. Develop rectangular algebraic equivalence: e and f are equivalent if there are rectangular x,y with xy=e, yx=f, ex=x=xf and fy=y=ye; compare this with isomorphism of their images. Prove invariance under conjugation and direct sums, transport under ring maps and finite-index reindexing. Compare stable equivalence with stable similarity after adding zero blocks. The idempotent monoid V(R), modulo isomorphism of image modules and with direct sum, has the same group completion as K₀(R); construct the comparison and its class formula, rather than defining another K₀.

Prove every class is [P]−[Q] and also [e]−[Rᵐ] for an idempotent e, by taking a complement of Q. Equality [P]=[Q] means P⊕T≅Q⊕T for some finite projective T; replacing T by T⊕T′ gives a free stabilizer. Use this to produce explicit common identity/zero blocks and stable conjugators in matrix calculations. Do not assume cancellation in V(R). Describe the image of V(R) as the algebraic positive cone, without asserting it defines a proper order for arbitrary rings. Compute K₀ of a division ring as ℤ with free-rank generator, of ℤ as ℤ, of finite products by components and of matrix rings by the specified Morita map. Prove the zero-ring value and all size-zero matrix conventions.

## A1. Stable matrices, elementary operations and Whitehead's lemma

For each finite n let GLₙ(R) be native matrix units, and define eᵢⱼ(a)=1+aEᵢⱼ for i≠j. Prove inversion by replacing a by −a; same-index addition; distant commutation; and [eᵢⱼ(a),eⱼₖ(b)]=eᵢₖ(ab) for distinct indices, with commutator xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. State the index conditions on every identity; do not replace ab by ba. Prove ring-map, block embedding and reindexing formulas. Define Eₙ(R) as the subgroup generated by these units; provide finite-product membership witnesses, induction and lift/map lemmas. Supply upper and lower rectangular block-unipotent decompositions into elementary matrices.

Construct GL∞(R) as the directed colimit of GLₙ(R) under g↦diag(g,1), using native DirectLimit and proving the full directed-system laws. Provide canonical embeddings, injectivity, finite-stage representatives, equality at a common finite stage, multiplication/inverse at a common stage, universal mapping property and induced ring maps. Prove the comparison with finitary automorphisms of the countable free module that fix every basis vector outside a finite set and preserve its finite coordinate complement. Define E∞ as the union/generated subgroup of the embedded Eₙ, with the precise finite-stage membership criterion. Arbitrary finite index types compare by finite reindexing.

Give an explicit elementary factorization of diag(T,T⁻¹) for every T∈GLₙ(R), including n=0. Start with the three block-unipotent product [[0,T],[−T⁻¹,0]] and multiply by the constant block rotation [[0,−I],[I,0]] to obtain diag(T,T⁻¹); factor that constant rotation as well. Deduce conjugation invariance of E∞ and that stable commutators lie in E∞. Conversely every elementary generator is a stable commutator by adjoining a distinct third index and taking b=1. Prove E∞=[GL∞,GL∞], normality, perfectness of E∞, and the abelianity of GL∞/E∞. In finite rank do not assume Eₙ is normal or equal to a commutator subgroup without additional hypotheses.

Prove elementary products lift invertibly, with the same matrix size, through every surjective unital ring map, by lifting their finitely many coefficients. In particular diag(T,T⁻¹) lifts through any surjection to R. This never says that an arbitrary invertible matrix lifts. The class of all universally liftable matrices is not used as the defining relation: −1 lifts through every unital surjection and its class in K₁(ℤ) is nonzero. Supply this example explicitly.

## A2. Algebraic K₁ and projective automorphisms

Define K₁(R)=Abelianization(GL∞(R)) and identify it canonically with GL∞/E∞ using A1. Use additive notation for this abelian group and [uv]=[u]+[v]. Give the class map on every GLₙ, common-stage induction, the elementary and conjugation relations, block-sum formula [diag(u,v)]=[u]+[v], inverse formula, generation and universal property for elementary-invariant stable homomorphisms to abelian groups. Prove the colimit comparison with the finite-rank **abelianizations** of GLₙ; the individual Eₙ quotients need not be groups. Supply ring functoriality, zero-ring and finite-product formulas, and continuity under filtered colimits of unital rings by descending finite matrices and their finite inverse/elementary witnesses.

Give the intrinsic Bass presentation by projective automorphisms [P,u], with isomorphism invariance, composition [P,uv]=[P,u]+[P,v] and direct sums. Construct its comparison with the matrix definition by choosing P⊕Q≅Rⁿ and adjoining the identity on Q. Prove independence of complement, trivialization and stabilization. Prove that this is an isomorphism and derive the relation for an automorphism of a split short exact sequence, including its upper-triangular off-diagonal block. Develop naturality and additivity of the automorphism class, identity, inverse and conjugation. This is not a claim that every stabilized automorphism is trivial.

Construct the Morita equivalence K₁(Mₙ(R))≅K₁(R), n≥1, by flattening block matrices and compare it with the finite-projective equivalence in A0, including corner embeddings and repeated matrix sizes. Prove compatibility with products, filtered colimits and A6's boundaries. For commutative R, construct the split determinant map K₁(R)→Rˣ with scalar section, define SK₁ as its kernel and identify the resulting decomposition. Compute K₁(F)=Fˣ for fields by Gaussian elimination, K₁(D)=Dˣ/[Dˣ,Dˣ] for division rings using the Dieudonné determinant, K₁(ℤ)={±1}, and finite products/matrix algebras via these maps. ## A3. Relative groups and unitizations

For a two-sided ideal I⊲R, define GL∞(R,I)=ker(GL∞(R)→GL∞(R/I)) and E∞(R,I) as the normal closure in E∞(R) of elementary matrices with entries in I. Prove the relative commutator formulas, stability, normality in GL∞(R,I), and that K₁(R,I)=GL∞(R,I)/E∞(R,I) is abelian. Give finite matrix representatives and their stabilization, ring/ideal-map functoriality, products and independence of presentations. The map K₁(R,I)→K₁(R) has image ker(K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)); it is not defined as that kernel. Construct the connecting map K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I) in A5 and prove the exact sequence K₂(R)→K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I)→K₁(R)→K₁(R/I). No terminal zero is appended when units do not lift stably.

Define K₀(R,I) by triples (P,Q,α), with P,Q finite projective right R-modules and α:P⊗ᵣR/I≅Q⊗ᵣR/I, modulo isomorphism, direct sum and composition of identifications. Give generation, equivalences, elementary triples and the universal additive characterization. Construct K₁(R/I)→K₀(R,I) using a free pair and its gluing automorphism; prove K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)→K₀(R,I)→K₀(R)→K₀(R/I) exact at its three interior terms and all naturality diagrams. Compare with the relative group from finite-projective exact-category data supplied by GrothendieckEulerForms, constructing any needed relative presentation adapter here.

For a nonunital associative ring J use the integral Dorroh unitization J⁺=ℤ⊕J with (m,x)(n,y)=(mn,my+nx+xy), and define reduced Kᵢ(J) as ker(Kᵢ(J⁺)→Kᵢ(ℤ)), i=0,1,2. Build the augmentation splitting, maps for nonunital homomorphisms and the comparison with a unital ring regarded as nonunital via J⁺≅ℤ×J. For complex algebras also construct ℂ⊕A and the comparison appropriate to ℂ-linear maps; distinguish the base-ring-relative construction from arbitrary nonunital-ring excision. ## A4. Stable range and finite matrix sizes

Define right-unimodular rows (a₁,…,aₙ), with witnesses Σaᵢbᵢ=1, and Bass stable range at most s≥1: every right-unimodular (s+1)-row can be shortened to (aᵢ+aₛ₊₁cᵢ)ᵢ≤s that is right-unimodular. Prove the equivalent all-longer-row formulation, monotonicity, left/opposite-ring comparison, and invariance under ring isomorphism. Develop the elementary row-reduction, orbit and stabilization lemmas from this definition. Prove for stable range at most s that GLₙ(R)→K₁(R) is surjective for n≥s, and GLₙ(R)/Eₙ(R)→K₁(R) is an isomorphism for n≥s+1, proving normality there. For n≥max(3,s+1), deduce [GLₙ,GLₙ]=Eₙ and hence the corresponding isomorphism from the finite-rank abelianization. State the bounds as sufficient ranges, not as the smallest n in every ring; identify the finite-rank kernel outside the stable range by its actual quotient map. Explain the finite-field small-rank distinction: GL₂(𝔽₂)≅S₃ has nontrivial abelianization, while K₁(𝔽₂)=0.

Prove stable range one for semilocal rings, using reduction to their semisimple Artinian quotients and lifting modulo the Jacobson radical; the commutative specialization agrees with Mathlib's semilocal API. Prove the Bass bound s≤d+1 for commutative Noetherian rings of finite Krull dimension d, with the dimension/drop argument explicitly developed. Compute the Bass stable rank of C(X,ℂ), for nonempty compact Hausdorff X of finite covering dimension d, as floor(d/2)+1. Construct the Lebesgue covering-dimension API here. For compact Hausdorff X and d∈ℕ, dimension at most d means that every finite open cover of X admits a finite open refinement covering X in which each point belongs to at most d+1 members. A refinement member is contained in a member of the original cover. Use native finite families, open sets and refinement maps; prove equivalence of the finite-family formulations, monotonicity of the bound and invariance under homeomorphism. Define the dimension as the least such d, with infinity if there is no finite bound and the empty space assigned dimension −1. Supply the compact-Hausdorff closed-subspace, finite closed-cover, partition-of-unity and sphere-extension characterizations needed by the proof. In particular characterize dimension at most d by extension to X of every continuous map from a closed subset into Sᵈ. Develop the resulting approximation/extension lemmas for the stable-range upper bound and the nonextendable-map obstruction for its lower bound. This is Lebesgue covering dimension; no equality with the separate small inductive dimension is assumed. Derive the above explicit finite-matrix surjectivity and injectivity ranges for C(X). Give the real-valued counterpart d+1 with its real dimension obstruction and treat the empty space as the zero ring. The comparison of Bass and topological stable ranks used for these commutative function rings is a theorem here, with its density/shortening proof; no assertion for all Banach algebras is imported without its hypotheses. TopologicalKTheory may consume these ranges for its bundle stabilization, without supplying the algebraic Whitehead theorem backwards.

## A5. Steinberg groups and K₂

Define K₂(R) as that kernel, obtain its abelian group structure, naturality, products, filtered-colimit and matrix Morita comparisons, and the exact sequence 1→K₂(R)→St∞(R)→GL∞(R)→K₁(R)→0, interpreting the middle image as E∞. ## A6. Milnor patching and the boundary

Let π₁:A₁→Q and π₂:A₂→Q be arbitrary unital maps of arbitrary associative rings, and assume **at least one map is surjective**. First choose π₁ surjective; prove the symmetric formulation. Define D=A₁×_Q A₂ as the native subring {(a₁,a₂) | π₁(a₁)=π₂(a₂)} of the finite product, with projections pⱼ. Prove the ring universal property, base-change maps between squares and canonical isomorphisms, matrix/unit compatibility, kernels and quotient identifications, and the corresponding category-theoretic pullback comparison. Do not impose two surjective legs.

Define the category of patching triples (P₁,P₂,α), where Pⱼ is finite projective right Aⱼ and α:P₁⊗Q≅P₂⊗Q is Q-linear; morphisms are compatible pairs. Its inverse functor is the actual module

M(P₁,P₂,α)={(x,y)∈P₁×P₂ | α(x⊗1)=y⊗1}.

Construct its right D-action, prove finite projectivity and the two canonical base-change isomorphisms, and show that restriction/base change and patching are inverse additive equivalences. Prove full faithfulness, essential surjectivity, coherence of the comparison maps, and compatibility with direct sums, isomorphisms, idempotent images and maps of squares. The theorem is for finite projectives; it does not claim descent for all modules from these hypotheses alone. Give a finite complement and a matrix idempotent for a patched object, with an explicit finite construction; do not treat finite projectivity as an unexplained gluing axiom.

Fix α=u on Qⁿ and set M(u)={(a,b)∈A₁ⁿ×A₂ⁿ | uπ₁(a)=π₂(b)}. Normalize the boundary by

δ([u])=[M(u)]−[Dⁿ].

Relate this module formula to the following finite matrix formula. Choose an invertible lift X∈GL₂ₙ(A₁) of diag(u,u⁻¹), available by A1. Let e=diag(Iₙ,0ₙ) and E=(XeX⁻¹,e)∈M₂ₙ(D), using π₁(XeX⁻¹)=e. Then prove δ([u])=[E]−[(e,e)] in K₀(D), where the latter pair is the standard rank-n idempotent over D. The image of E is isomorphic to M(u) with the displayed orientation. This formula uses idempotents, and XeX⁻¹ need not be selfadjoint even when the inputs are C*-algebras.

Prove independence of the lift X, matrix size, representative u and all complement choices; allow a lift of diag(u,v) for any compatible invertible v after equal-size stabilization and show agreement. Supply the stabilizing block permutation/conjugation formulas, common-stage lemmas and free-complement steps used in those proofs. Prove δ([uv])=δ([u])+δ([v]), elementary vanishing, invariance under multiplication on either side by matrices lifted from either Aⱼ, and all naturality diagrams. Under swapping the two legs and the canonical identification of D, δ changes sign. Give each assertion on matrix representatives before passing to K₁.

With fᵢ=(Kᵢ(p₁),Kᵢ(p₂)) and gᵢ(x,y)=Kᵢ(π₁)x−Kᵢ(π₂)y, prove the sequence

K₁(D) →ᶠ¹ K₁(A₁)⊕K₁(A₂) →ᵍ¹ K₁(Q) →δ K₀(D) →ᶠ⁰ K₀(A₁)⊕K₀(A₂) →ᵍ⁰ K₀(Q)

is exact **at its four interior terms**: ker g₁=im f₁, ker δ=im g₁, ker f₀=im δ, and ker g₀=im f₀. Prove every adjacent composite zero and both containments at each named term, with compatible sign choices. In the K₀ proof explicitly stabilize arbitrary projective complements to free ones, convert rectangular equivalence to stable conjugacy, lift diag(U,U⁻¹) and align the quotient idempotents. In the kernel-of-f₀ proof construct the block-centralizer normal form and recover the actual boundary class. In the K₁ proof align stable representatives using elementary lifts; do not infer exactness solely by group-completing the projective patching equivalence. No injectivity of f₁ or surjectivity of g₀ is asserted without another hypothesis.

## A8. Comparison with topological K-theory

For a unital complex Banach algebra A, define the stable component-group functor using GLₙ(A)/GLₙ(A)₀ and compare it with the standard topological K₁ supplied by OperatorKTheory in the C* case. Every elementary matrix is connected to one through t↦eᵢⱼ(ta). Therefore construct the natural surjection K₁ᵃˡᵍ(A)→K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(A) and identify its kernel as the image of the stable identity component modulo E∞. Give representative, stabilization, multiplication and functoriality formulas, and compare projective/idempotent K₀ with the topological projection model in the C* case using the supplied functional-calculus homotopies. Show K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ)=ℂˣ and that the comparison kills every class, while K₀ compares by rank. Do not assert algebraic K₁ agrees with topological K₁ even for a dense holomorphically closed subalgebra; its topological homotopy quotient is an extra operation.

For a surjective continuous unital homomorphism of Banach algebras, prove exponentials and finite products of exponentials lift through matrix maps. Prove the identity component is the subgroup generated by a symmetric sufficiently small norm ball via logarithms, with strict bound ‖a−1‖<1, and hence equals the path component. All norm-contour/logarithm construction is consumed from BanachAlgebras' holomorphic functional calculus with its actual completeness and spectrum hypotheses. Spectral invariance of a noncomplete normed algebra alone supplies neither contour integrals in its own topology nor a continuous functional-calculus path; use the complete locally convex/matrix-inverse-closed hypotheses explicitly specified in OperatorKTheory K3 when invoking that comparison. Prove the comparison of the algebraic Milnor boundary with the analytic pullback boundary for C*-squares after the above algebraic-to-topological quotient, with the convention δ=[M(u)]−[Dⁿ]. OperatorKTheory owns the six-term cyclic extension sequence and Bott continuation; they do not follow for arbitrary rings from A6.
