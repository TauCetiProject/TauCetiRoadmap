# Algebraic K-theory in low degrees: matrices, projectives and patching

Develop the algebraic K-groups of arbitrary associative unital rings through degrees zero, one and two, with their usable matrix and projective-module APIs, relative groups, stable-range theorems, and Milnor patching. The central applications are computing low-degree invariants, transporting projectives across pullbacks, and constructing explicit connecting maps. All ring homomorphisms preserve one. Rings can be noncommutative and can be the zero ring. A division-ring or nonzero-space calculation states its nontriviality hypothesis.

Algebraic K₁ is the abelianization of the stable general linear group. It is not the group of connected components of a topological matrix group. For instance K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ) is ℂˣ, whereas K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(ℂ)=0. The comparison in A8 constructs the quotient map and its kernel; it does not identify these functors.

The definitive specification is this README. Suggested.lean is its acceptance test: it states the headline theorems that are statable against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti, with the few definitions they need, and its module docstring names the headlines that are not statable there and the carrier each lacks.

## Conventions, dependencies and ownership

Use finitely generated projective **right** R-modules, expressed in Lean as left modules over Rᵐᵒᵖ. Matrices act on columns by left multiplication; this commutes with right scalar multiplication. Extension of scalars is P⊗ᵣS, with the native bimodule/opposite-ring encoding and the relation pr⊗s=p⊗f(r)s. Do not require R to be commutative to form this tensor product. Use the commuting-action vocabulary in Mathlib.Algebra.Module.Bimodule. Adopt `TauCeti.BalancedTensorProduct ℤ R P S`, with R acting on S through f, from [Tau Ceti's balanced tensor API](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/9b58aa766a76235fda88e5b492731a0933188081/TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/TensorProduct/Balanced/Basic.lean). Its adjacent `Actions`, `Unit`, `Associator` and `Coherence` modules supply the outer actions, unit and associativity isomorphisms and their coherence. Use this quotient carrier, its balanced universal property (`lift`/`liftEquiv`), pure-tensor induction, extensionality and functorial `map`; do not construct a second quotient. The right S-action is the outer action of Sᵐᵒᵖ on the second factor, using `rightModule`; identify its pure-tensor formula and prove the required commuting-action instances. For left-module extension S⊗ᵣM use `BalancedTensorProduct ℤ R S M`, where S has right R-action s·r=s f(r), and its outer left S-action is `leftModule` on the first factor. The ℤ-actions commute with these ring actions. Give the native module-category functors, ring-map adapters, finite-free base-change formulas and natural comparison with the commutative tensor API here. Give the comparison with the left-module convention by passing to Rᵐᵒᵖ, including the transpose and inverse conventions on matrix automorphisms. No flatness assumption is used. Use this same carrier and API in checkouts where the cited modules still need to be built, rather than introducing a second tensor carrier. Prove that every object is a direct summand of Rⁿ for some finite n and provide a complementary finite projective with actual splitting maps.

Use K₀(R), written `KZero`, as the supplied split K₀ of this existing Proj(R), with its canonical comparison to the existing induced exact K₀, using finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split. Establish extension-of-scalars additive functors for all unital ring maps, including their associator/unit isomorphisms, and the functorial K₀ maps. Prove that base change preserves finite projectivity by transporting a finite free splitting, without a flatness hypothesis on S. Give identity/composition/naturality, equivalence invariance, finite products and matrix Morita equivalences, fixing the corner-module convention for Mₙ(R), n≥1.

Use idempotents e²=e in Mₙ(R), with no adjoint or topology. Relate their images as right modules to all finite projectives. Develop rectangular algebraic equivalence: e and f are equivalent if there are rectangular x,y with xy=e, yx=f, ex=x=xf and fy=y=ye; compare this with isomorphism of their images. Prove invariance under conjugation and direct sums, transport under ring maps and finite-index reindexing. Compare stable equivalence with stable similarity after adding zero blocks. The idempotent monoid V(R), modulo isomorphism of image modules and with direct sum, has the same group completion as K₀(R); construct the comparison and its class formula, rather than defining another K₀.

Prove every class is [P]−[Q] and also [e]−[Rᵐ] for an idempotent e, by taking a complement of Q. Equality [P]=[Q] means P⊕T≅Q⊕T for some finite projective T; replacing T by T⊕T′ gives a free stabilizer. Use this to produce explicit common identity/zero blocks and stable conjugators in matrix calculations. Do not assume cancellation in V(R). Describe the image of V(R) as the algebraic positive cone, without asserting it defines a proper order for arbitrary rings. Prove `kZero_divisionRing`: K₀ of a division ring D is ℤ, with the class of the free right module of rank one sent to 1 (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter II, §2, the division-ring example after Definition 2.1). K₀(ℤ) as ℤ, of finite products by components and of matrix rings by the specified Morita map. Prove the zero-ring value and all size-zero matrix conventions.

## A1. Stable matrices, elementary operations and Whitehead's lemma

For each finite n let GLₙ(R) be native matrix units, and define eᵢⱼ(a)=1+aEᵢⱼ for i≠j. Prove inversion by replacing a by −a; same-index addition; distant commutation; and [eᵢⱼ(a),eⱼₖ(b)]=eᵢₖ(ab) for distinct indices, with commutator xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. State the index conditions on every identity; do not replace ab by ba. Prove ring-map, block embedding and reindexing formulas. Define Eₙ(R), written `elementary`, as the subgroup generated by these units; provide finite-product membership witnesses, induction and lift/map lemmas. Supply upper and lower rectangular block-unipotent decompositions into elementary matrices.

Construct GL∞(R) as the directed colimit of GLₙ(R) under g↦diag(g,1), using native DirectLimit and proving the full directed-system laws. Provide canonical embeddings, injectivity, finite-stage representatives, equality at a common finite stage, multiplication/inverse at a common stage, universal mapping property and induced ring maps. Prove the comparison with finitary automorphisms of the countable free module that fix every basis vector outside a finite set and preserve its finite coordinate complement. Define E∞ as the union/generated subgroup of the embedded Eₙ, with the precise finite-stage membership criterion. Arbitrary finite index types compare by finite reindexing.

Give an explicit elementary factorization of diag(T,T⁻¹) for every T∈GLₙ(R), including n=0. Start with the three block-unipotent product [[0,T],[−T⁻¹,0]] and multiply by the constant block rotation [[0,−I],[I,0]] to obtain diag(T,T⁻¹); factor that constant rotation as well. Deduce conjugation invariance of E∞ and that stable commutators lie in E∞. Conversely every elementary generator is a stable commutator by adjoining a distinct third index and taking b=1. Prove E∞=[GL∞,GL∞], normality, perfectness of E∞, and the abelianity of GL∞/E∞ (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, Lemma 1.3.3). At finite size this is the pair `whitehead_commutator_elementary`, that diag(ghg⁻¹h⁻¹,Iₙ) is a product of elementary matrices of size 2n for all g,h∈GLₙ(R), and `elementary_le_commutator`, that Eₙ(R)≤[GLₙ(R),GLₙ(R)] when n≥3. In finite rank do not assume Eₙ is normal or equal to a commutator subgroup without additional hypotheses.

Prove elementary products lift invertibly, with the same matrix size, through every surjective unital ring map, by lifting their finitely many coefficients. In particular diag(T,T⁻¹) lifts through any surjection to R. This never says that an arbitrary invertible matrix lifts. The class of all universally liftable matrices is not used as the defining relation: −1 lifts through every unital surjection and its class in K₁(ℤ) is nonzero. Supply this example explicitly.

## A2. Algebraic K₁ and projective automorphisms

Define K₁(R)=Abelianization(GL∞(R)) and identify it canonically with GL∞/E∞ using A1. Use additive notation for this abelian group and [uv]=[u]+[v]. Give the class map on every GLₙ, common-stage induction, the elementary and conjugation relations, block-sum formula [diag(u,v)]=[u]+[v], inverse formula, generation and universal property for elementary-invariant stable homomorphisms to abelian groups. Prove the colimit comparison with the finite-rank **abelianizations** of GLₙ; the individual Eₙ quotients need not be groups. Supply ring functoriality, zero-ring and finite-product formulas, and continuity under filtered colimits of unital rings by descending finite matrices and their finite inverse/elementary witnesses.

Give the intrinsic Bass presentation by projective automorphisms [P,u], with isomorphism invariance, composition [P,uv]=[P,u]+[P,v] and direct sums. Construct its comparison with the matrix definition by choosing P⊕Q≅Rⁿ and adjoining the identity on Q. Prove independence of complement, trivialization and stabilization. Prove that this is an isomorphism and derive the relation for an automorphism of a split short exact sequence, including its upper-triangular off-diagonal block. Develop naturality and additivity of the automorphism class, identity, inverse and conjugation. This is not a claim that every stabilized automorphism is trivial.

Construct the Morita equivalence K₁(Mₙ(R))≅K₁(R), n≥1, by flattening block matrices and compare it with the finite-projective equivalence in A0, including corner embeddings and repeated matrix sizes. Prove compatibility with products, filtered colimits and A6's boundaries. For commutative R, construct the split determinant map K₁(R)→Rˣ with scalar section, define SK₁ as its kernel and identify the resulting decomposition. Compute K₁(F)=Fˣ for fields by Gaussian elimination, K₁(D)=Dˣ/[Dˣ,Dˣ] for division rings using the Dieudonné determinant, K₁(ℤ)={±1}, and finite products/matrix algebras via these maps. Build the Dieudonné determinant, its elementary reduction independence and multiplicativity here; it is not an unexplained supplier. State it as `dieudonne_determinant`: for a division ring D and n≥2 there is a surjective homomorphism GLₙ(D)→Dˣ/[Dˣ,Dˣ] with kernel exactly Eₙ(D), sending the diagonal matrix with one entry d and the others 1 to the class of d (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, 1.2.4 and Exercise 1.2). For n=1 the kernel is [Dˣ,Dˣ], not E₁(D)=1, so the bound n≥2 is needed for a noncommutative D. Normalize [−1]∈K₁(ℤ) as the nonzero order-two class.

## A3. Relative groups and unitizations

For a two-sided ideal I⊲R, define GL∞(R,I)=ker(GL∞(R)→GL∞(R/I)) and E∞(R,I) as the normal closure in E∞(R) of elementary matrices with entries in I. Prove the relative commutator formulas, stability, normality in GL∞(R,I), and that K₁(R,I)=GL∞(R,I)/E∞(R,I) is abelian. Give finite matrix representatives and their stabilization, ring/ideal-map functoriality, products and independence of presentations. The map K₁(R,I)→K₁(R) has image ker(K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)); it is not defined as that kernel. Construct the connecting map K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I) in A5 and prove the exact sequence K₂(R)→K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I)→K₁(R)→K₁(R/I). No terminal zero is appended when units do not lift stably.

For the additive extension functor T:Proj(R)→Proj(R/I), define the relative group K₀(R,I) by triples (P,α,Q), where P and Q are objects of Proj(R) and α:P⊗ᵣR/I≅Q⊗ᵣR/I is an isomorphism of right R/I-modules. Impose isomorphism invariance under isomorphisms f:P≅P′, g:Q≅Q′ with α′∘T(f)=T(g)∘α, the direct-sum relation (P,α,Q)+(P′,α′,Q′)=(P⊕P′,α⊕α′,Q⊕Q′) and the composition relation (P,α,Q)+(Q,β,S)=(P,β∘α,S); these give (P,id,P)=0 and (Q,α⁻¹,P)=−(P,α,Q). Construct the difference map K₀(R,I)→K₀(R), [(P,α,Q)]↦[P]−[Q], into the `TauCeti.SplitK0` group of A0, its maps for morphisms of ring/ideal pairs, the generation, elementary-triple and universal additive formulas, independence of small-model choices and compatibility with quotient maps and opposite conventions (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter II, §2).

Construct the ring boundary K₁(R/I)→K₀(R,I), sending the class of u∈GLₙ(R/I) to [(Rⁿ,u,Rⁿ)], and prove K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)→K₀(R,I)→K₀(R)→K₀(R/I) exact at its three interior terms with all naturality diagrams, using only the triple presentation above and A2's Bass projective-automorphism presentation of K₁. Every finite projective R/I-module is a summand of (R/I)ⁿ=T(Rⁿ), so the boundary on a projective automorphism is represented by adjoining the identity on its actual finite projective complement in a finite free module. Prove independence of that complement and its trivialization and agreement with the free-pair boundary. Neither the presentation nor these exactness proofs uses Q-theory, a higher K-spectrum or a homotopy-fibre comparison; those comparisons consume the groups and maps of A3.

For a nonunital associative ring J use the integral Dorroh unitization J⁺=ℤ⊕J with (m,x)(n,y)=(mn,my+nx+xy), and define reduced Kᵢ(J) as ker(Kᵢ(J⁺)→Kᵢ(ℤ)), i=0,1,2. Build the augmentation splitting, maps for nonunital homomorphisms and the comparison with a unital ring regarded as nonunital via J⁺≅ℤ×J. For complex algebras also construct ℂ⊕A and the comparison appropriate to ℂ-linear maps; distinguish the base-ring-relative construction from arbitrary nonunital-ring excision. No target asserts that integral reduced K-theory of an ideal equals every ambient relative group without an excision theorem. Export precisely these relative-unitization interfaces to NCG.

## A4. Stable range and finite matrix sizes

Define right-unimodular rows (a₁,…,aₙ), with witnesses Σaᵢbᵢ=1, and Bass stable range at most s, written `HasStableRangeLE R s`: every right-unimodular (s+1)-row can be shortened to (aᵢ+aₛ₊₁cᵢ)ᵢ≤s that is right-unimodular. Prove the equivalent all-longer-row formulation, monotonicity, left/opposite-ring comparison, and invariance under ring isomorphism. Develop the elementary row-reduction, orbit and stabilization lemmas from this definition. Prove for stable range at most s that GLₙ(R)→K₁(R) is surjective for n≥s, and GLₙ(R)/Eₙ(R)→K₁(R) is an isomorphism for n≥s+1, proving normality there (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, Remark 1.2.5 and Exercise 1.3). At finite size these are `surjective_stability`, that for n≥s every invertible matrix of size n+k is diag(h,Iₖ) times an elementary matrix, and `injective_stability`, that for n≥s+1 an invertible g of size n with diag(g,Iₖ) elementary is itself elementary. Stable range at most 0 holds only for the zero ring, since the row (1) is unimodular. For n≥max(3,s+1), deduce [GLₙ,GLₙ]=Eₙ and hence the corresponding isomorphism from the finite-rank abelianization. State the bounds as sufficient ranges, not as the smallest n in every ring; identify the finite-rank kernel outside the stable range by its actual quotient map. Explain the finite-field small-rank distinction: GL₂(𝔽₂)≅S₃ has nontrivial abelianization, while K₁(𝔽₂)=0.

Prove stable range one for semilocal rings, using reduction to their semisimple Artinian quotients and lifting modulo the Jacobson radical (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter I, Exercises 1.5(c) and 1.12(v)); the commutative specialization is `hasStableRangeLE_one_of_finite_maximalSpectrum`, for commutative rings with finitely many maximal ideals. Prove the Bass bound s≤d+1 for commutative Noetherian rings of Krull dimension at most d, `hasStableRangeLE_of_ringKrullDim_le`, with the dimension/drop argument explicitly developed (Bass, K-theory and stable algebra, Theorem V.3.5). **Checks.** ℤ does not have stable range 1: the row (2,5) is unimodular but 2+5c is never ±1; it has stable range at most 2, matching Krull dimension 1. Compute the Bass stable rank of C(X,ℂ), for nonempty compact Hausdorff X of finite covering dimension d, as floor(d/2)+1. Define the covering-dimension predicate `HasCoveringDimensionLE X d` for a topological space X and d∈ℕ: every open cover of X, taken as a set of open sets, has an open refinement, again a set of open sets each contained in a member of the cover, in which every point lies in at most d+1 distinct members. Counting distinct members rather than indices follows the design of the closed, unmerged Mathlib proposals [#43476](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43476) and [#43475](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43475), which are design sources and not installed declarations. Build on it the strict and finite-dimensional variants, the dimension value in WithBot ℕ∞ (the empty space has value ⊥, dimension −1, and ⊤ means no finite bound), monotonicity in d, homeomorphism invariance, closed-subspace monotonicity and the finite closed-cover theorem for compact Hausdorff spaces. **Checks.** A finite discrete space satisfies the predicate with d=0 via the cover by singletons; [0,1] fails it with d=0 because an open refinement of order one is a partition into open sets and [0,1] is connected, and satisfies it with d=1; the empty space satisfies it for every d.

For compact Hausdorff X and d∈ℕ, prove the finite/indexed-cover characterization of `HasCoveringDimensionLE`: every finite indexed open cover admits a finite indexed open refining cover (V_j), a parent map p with V_j⊂U_(p j), and `encard {j : x∈V_j}≤d+1` for every x. Preserve these parents and containment witnesses in every conversion. From the arbitrary-cover predicate take a finite subcover of a refinement, deduplicate/reindex it by its distinct nonempty open sets, and choose each original parent; indexed multiplicity then equals range multiplicity. Conversely take a finite subcover of any given open cover, apply the finite characterization, and compose parent assignments to refine the original cover; a range-order bound follows from the indexed bound. Compare finite-set and finite-family encodings and their refinement maps. Do not transfer a range bound to the original indexing without deduplication: two identical copies of the one-point space have range multiplicity one and indexed multiplicity two. Monotonicity, homeomorphism invariance, compact-Hausdorff closed-subspace monotonicity and the finite closed-cover theorem are stated for this single predicate and its value. Supply the compact-Hausdorff partition-of-unity and sphere-extension characterizations needed by the proof. In particular characterize `HasCoveringDimensionLE X d` by extension to X of every continuous map from a closed subset into Sᵈ. Develop the resulting approximation/extension lemmas for the stable-range upper bound and the nonextendable-map obstruction for its lower bound. This is Lebesgue covering dimension; no equality with the separate small inductive dimension is assumed. Derive the above explicit finite-matrix surjectivity and injectivity ranges for C(X). Give the real-valued counterpart d+1 with its real dimension obstruction and treat the empty space as the zero ring. In terms of the stable-range predicate both calculations are the equivalences `hasStableRangeLE_continuousMap_complex_iff`, that for compact Hausdorff X of finite covering dimension and s≥1, C(X,ℂ) has stable range at most s iff `HasCoveringDimensionLE X (2s−1)`, and `hasStableRangeLE_continuousMap_real_iff`, the same for C(X,ℝ) with `HasCoveringDimensionLE X (s−1)` (Vaserstein 1971). The bound s≥1 is needed: a one-point X has dimension 0 but C(X,ℂ)=ℂ does not have stable range at most 0. **Checks.** For X a point both rings are fields of stable range 1; for X=[0,1] the complex ring has stable range 1 and the real ring has stable range 2; for X the 2-sphere the complex ring has stable range 2. The comparison of Bass and topological stable ranks used for these commutative function rings is a theorem here, with its density/shortening proof; no assertion for all Banach algebras is imported without its hypotheses. TopologicalKTheory may consume these ranges for its bundle stabilization, without supplying the algebraic Whitehead theorem backwards.

## A5. Steinberg groups and K₂

For arbitrary R define Stₙ(R) on generators xᵢⱼ(a), i≠j, with xᵢⱼ(a)xᵢⱼ(b)=xᵢⱼ(a+b), distant-commutator relations for i≠l and j≠k, and [xᵢⱼ(a),xⱼₖ(b)]=xᵢₖ(ab) for distinct i,j,k. Use the native free-group/quotient-by-normal-relations API. Provide maps to Eₙ, coefficient maps, index inclusions/reindexing, colimit St∞ and its stable generator induction/universal property. Prove St∞→E∞ is surjective with central kernel and is the universal central extension of the perfect group E∞. Define K₂(R) as that kernel, obtain its abelian group structure, naturality, products, filtered-colimit and matrix Morita comparisons, and the exact sequence 1→K₂(R)→St∞(R)→GL∞(R)→K₁(R)→0, interpreting the middle image as E∞. Prove centrality in the stable group; do not assert this for every Stₙ without stable-rank hypotheses.

Give the relative Steinberg diagram for R→R/I and construct the connecting homomorphism in A3 by lifting words and comparing their elementary images. Prove independence of words and lifts and the exactness assertions there. For fields develop Steinberg symbols, bilinearity, {a,1−a}=0 and Matsumoto's presentation; distinguish this field computation from the tensor-symbol Milnor K-groups in arbitrary degrees. Compute K₂ of finite fields as zero using that presentation and its finite-field symbol calculation. The comparison of these degree-0, 1 and 2 groups with the ordinary ring K-spectrum belongs to GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §6.1.7 and §6.10, which consume A0–A2, this Steinberg construction and A6; no construction here depends on it.

A general Milnor square in A6 supplies the stated low-degree sequence, not an automatic homotopy-cartesian square of all algebraic K-spectra. Any higher-excision theorem must state and prove its additional homological hypotheses. No K₂(Q)→K₁(D) arrow or cyclic Bott continuation is asserted here merely from one surjective leg. The K₂ construction answers the low-degree object question independently of such a continuation.

## A6. Milnor patching and the boundary

Let π₁:A₁→Q and π₂:A₂→Q be arbitrary unital maps of arbitrary associative rings, and assume **at least one map is surjective**. First choose π₁ surjective; prove the symmetric formulation. Define D=A₁×_Q A₂ as the native subring {(a₁,a₂) | π₁(a₁)=π₂(a₂)} of the finite product, with projections pⱼ. Prove the ring universal property, base-change maps between squares and canonical isomorphisms, matrix/unit compatibility, kernels and quotient identifications, and the corresponding category-theoretic pullback comparison. Do not impose two surjective legs.

Define the category of patching triples (P₁,P₂,α), where Pⱼ is finite projective right Aⱼ and α:P₁⊗Q≅P₂⊗Q is Q-linear; morphisms are compatible pairs. Its inverse functor is the actual module

M(P₁,P₂,α)={(x,y)∈P₁×P₂ | α(x⊗1)=y⊗1}.

Construct its right D-action, prove finite projectivity and the two canonical base-change isomorphisms, and show that restriction/base change and patching are inverse additive equivalences. Prove full faithfulness, essential surjectivity, coherence of the comparison maps, and compatibility with direct sums, isomorphisms, idempotent images and maps of squares. The theorem is for finite projectives; it does not claim descent for all modules from these hypotheses alone. Give a finite complement and a matrix idempotent for a patched object, with an explicit finite construction; do not treat finite projectivity as an unexplained gluing axiom.

Fix α=u on Qⁿ and set M(u)={(a,b)∈A₁ⁿ×A₂ⁿ | uπ₁(a)=π₂(b)}. Normalize the boundary by

δ([u])=[M(u)]−[Dⁿ].

Relate this module formula to the following finite matrix formula. Choose an invertible lift X∈GL₂ₙ(A₁) of diag(u,u⁻¹), available by A1. Let e=diag(Iₙ,0ₙ) and E=(XeX⁻¹,e)∈M₂ₙ(D), using π₁(XeX⁻¹)=e. Then prove δ([u])=[E]−[(e,e)] in K₀(D), where the latter pair is the standard rank-n idempotent over D. The image of E is isomorphic to M(u) with the displayed orientation. This formula uses idempotents, and XeX⁻¹ need not be selfadjoint even when the inputs are C*-algebras.

Prove independence of the lift X, matrix size, representative u and all complement choices; more generally, for v∈GLₙ(Q) and m≥0 such that diag(u⊕Iₘ,v⊕Iₘ) lies in E₂₍ₙ₊ₘ₎(Q) with a recorded finite elementary-word witness, lift that word coefficientwise by A1 to X′∈E₂₍ₙ₊ₘ₎(A₁), form E′=(X′e′X′⁻¹,e′) with e′=diag(Iₙ₊ₘ,0), and show that [E′]−[(e′,e′)]=δ([u]), independently of v, of the stabilization and of the elementary witness. Invertibility and equal size alone do not suffice: diag(u,v) lifts by A1 only when it is elementary, and v=u⁻¹ is the case A1 factors explicitly. Equivalently start from [v]=−[u] in K₁(Q) and use A1's common-stage criterion to produce the stabilization and the witness. Supply the stabilizing block permutation/conjugation formulas, common-stage lemmas and free-complement steps used in those proofs. Prove δ([uv])=δ([u])+δ([v]), elementary vanishing, invariance under multiplication on either side by matrices lifted from either Aⱼ, and all naturality diagrams. Under swapping the two legs and the canonical identification of D, δ changes sign. Give each assertion on matrix representatives before passing to K₁.

With fᵢ=(Kᵢ(p₁),Kᵢ(p₂)) and gᵢ(x,y)=Kᵢ(π₁)x−Kᵢ(π₂)y, prove the sequence

K₁(D) →ᶠ¹ K₁(A₁)⊕K₁(A₂) →ᵍ¹ K₁(Q) →δ K₀(D) →ᶠ⁰ K₀(A₁)⊕K₀(A₂) →ᵍ⁰ K₀(Q)

is exact **at its four interior terms**: ker g₁=im f₁, ker δ=im g₁, ker f₀=im δ, and ker g₀=im f₀. Prove every adjacent composite zero and both containments at each named term, with compatible sign choices. In the K₀ proof explicitly stabilize arbitrary projective complements to free ones, convert rectangular equivalence to stable conjugacy, lift diag(U,U⁻¹) and align the quotient idempotents. In the kernel-of-f₀ proof construct the block-centralizer normal form and recover the actual boundary class. In the K₁ proof align stable representatives using elementary lifts; do not infer exactness solely by group-completing the projective patching equivalence. No injectivity of f₁ or surjectivity of g₀ is asserted without another hypothesis.

## A7. Gluing examples and calculations

For compact Hausdorff X,Y,S, continuous i:S→X and j:S→Y, let M be their pushout in compact Hausdorff spaces. Construct the isomorphism of C(M,ℂ) with the fibre product of C(X,ℂ) and C(Y,ℂ) over C(S,ℂ), with both norm/topological and underlying-ring comparisons. If i is a closed embedding, prove the restriction C(X)→C(S) is surjective by complex Tietze extension; no embedding or surjectivity is required of j. Consume [HilbertModules H5](../HilbertModules/README.md)'s compact-X comparison between finite-rank Hermitian bundles and finite projective section modules, with [TopologicalKTheory T1](../TopologicalKTheory/README.md)'s bundle category and clutching conventions. Supply the forgetting adapter to A0's algebraic finite-projective right C(X)-modules: H2's projection model equips every such module with a compatible Hermitian norm, and every algebraic module map is bounded and adjointable by its finite-matrix realization. Prove independence of metric choices up to module isomorphism, using all continuous fiberwise linear bundle maps as morphisms. Through this equivalence identify A6's patched projectives with the actual glued vector bundles, including gluing maps and section modules. Define the corresponding idempotent explicitly and show its class agrees with A6. For two closed disks glued along their boundary, use the fixed clutching convention and compare δ of a clutching matrix with the reduced bundle class on the sphere; OperatorKTheory/TopologicalKTheory supply the topological boundary/Bott comparison, rather than replacing the algebraic source group with topological K₁. Include the line bundle on S² from a degree-one circle clutching function and its sign under reversing the gluing.

Compute the arithmetic Milnor square A₁=ℤ, A₂=ℤ, Q=𝔽₅, π₁ reduction and π₂ the same reduction, and D={(a,b) | a≡b mod5}. Compute the units and the normalized sequence using K₁(ℤ)={±1}, K₁(𝔽₅)=𝔽₅ˣ and A0's K₀ calculations. Prove ker(K₀(D)→ℤ²)≅𝔽₅ˣ/{±1}≅ℤ/2 and identify its nonzero class by M(2); compute image K₀(D) in ℤ² as the diagonal and hence K₀(D)≅ℤ⊕ℤ/2 with rank-one generator. Obtain the compatible group identifications by explicit split maps, not only cardinalities. This example uses two surjective legs, while the gluing example above exercises the strictly one-surjective-leg case. For a concrete ring example with only one leg surjective, use A₁=𝔽₅[t]→Q=𝔽₅[t]/(t²) and A₂=𝔽₅→Q via constants; identify D≅𝔽₅+t²𝔽₅[t], its projections and patching modules, and verify the theorem without strengthening hypotheses. Construct Q, the constants map and the pullback isomorphism using native polynomial/ideal quotients.

## A8. Comparison with topological K-theory

For a unital complex Banach algebra A, define the stable component-group functor using GLₙ(A)/GLₙ(A)₀, functorial for continuous unital algebra homomorphisms, and identify it with OperatorKTheory K2's unitary K₁ in the C* case, naturally for unital *-homomorphisms. Continuity is needed: the functor is defined through norm-continuous paths, which a discontinuous ring homomorphism need not preserve. Every elementary matrix is connected to one through t↦eᵢⱼ(ta). Therefore construct the natural surjection K₁ᵃˡᵍ(A)→K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(A) and identify its kernel as the image of the stable identity component modulo E∞. Give representative, stabilization, multiplication and functoriality formulas for continuous unital homomorphisms. In the C* case consume OperatorKTheory K1's canonical natural isomorphism, for unital *-homomorphisms, between the projection-defined K₀ and `TauCeti.SplitK0` of its category of finitely generated projective right modules; identify that category with A0's Proj(A) by the equivalence of full subcategories of right A-modules, so that A0's K₀(A) is that same group, and construct no second category or projection comparison. Show K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ)=ℂˣ and that the comparison kills every class, while K₀ compares by rank. Do not assert algebraic K₁ agrees with topological K₁ even for a dense holomorphically closed subalgebra; its topological homotopy quotient is an extra operation.

For a surjective continuous unital homomorphism of complex Banach algebras, prove exponentials and finite products of exponentials lift through matrix maps. Prove the identity component is the subgroup generated by any sufficiently small norm ball about 1 contained in {g : ‖g−1‖<1}, using the convergent logarithm and its exponential path, and hence equals the path component. A norm ball about 1 need not be inverse invariant. If a symmetric generating neighborhood is used, take an open neighborhood U contained in that ball and replace it by U∩U⁻¹; prove openness, symmetry and containment rather than calling the ball symmetric. All norm-contour/logarithm construction is consumed from BanachAlgebras' holomorphic functional calculus with its actual completeness and spectrum hypotheses. Spectral invariance of a noncomplete normed algebra alone supplies neither contour integrals in its own topology nor a continuous functional-calculus path; use the complete locally convex/matrix-inverse-closed hypotheses explicitly specified in OperatorKTheory K3 when invoking that comparison. For a pullback square of unital C*-algebras along unital *-homomorphisms with π₁ surjective, prove that the algebraic Milnor boundary agrees, after the above algebraic-to-topological quotient, with the composite of OperatorKTheory K3's index boundary K₁(Q)→K₀(J) for the extension 0→J→A₁→Q→0, J=ker π₁, and the map K₀(J)→K₀(D) induced by J≅ker p₂⊆D; both are computed from a lift of diag(u,u⁻¹) as [XeX⁻¹]−[e], with the convention δ=[M(u)]−[Dⁿ]. OperatorKTheory owns the six-term cyclic extension sequence and Bott continuation; they do not follow for arbitrary rings from A6.
