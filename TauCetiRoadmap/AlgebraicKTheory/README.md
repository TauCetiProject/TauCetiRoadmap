# Algebraic K-theory in low degrees: matrices, projectives and patching

Develop the algebraic K-groups of arbitrary associative unital rings through degrees zero, one and two, with their usable matrix and projective-module APIs, relative groups, stable-range theorems, and Milnor patching. The central applications are computing low-degree invariants, transporting projectives across pullbacks, and constructing explicit connecting maps. All ring homomorphisms preserve one. Rings can be noncommutative and can be the zero ring. A division-ring or nonzero-space calculation states its nontriviality hypothesis.

Algebraic K₁ is the abelianization of the stable general linear group. It is not the group of connected components of a topological matrix group. For instance K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ) is ℂˣ, whereas K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(ℂ)=0. The comparison in A8 constructs the quotient map and its kernel; it does not identify these functors.

The definitive specification is this README. Suggested.lean is its acceptance test, not an exhaustive list of its targets: it states the headline theorems that are statable against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti, with the few definitions they need, and its module docstring names the headlines that are not statable there and the carrier each lacks.

## Conventions, dependencies and ownership

Use finitely generated projective **right** R-modules, expressed in Lean as left modules over Rᵐᵒᵖ. Matrices act on columns by left multiplication; this commutes with right scalar multiplication. Extension of scalars is P⊗ᵣS, with the native bimodule/opposite-ring encoding and the relation pr⊗s=p⊗f(r)s. Do not require R to be commutative to form this tensor product. Use the commuting-action vocabulary in Mathlib.Algebra.Module.Bimodule. Adopt `TauCeti.BalancedTensorProduct ℤ R P S`, with R acting on S through f, from Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Balanced.Basic`. At the pinned Tau Ceti this carrier is a ground-ring module only: it comes with its balanced universal property (`lift`/`liftEquiv`), pure-tensor induction, extensionality, functorial `map`, and the unit isomorphisms `lid` and `rid` of `TauCeti.LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Balanced.Unit`, but with no outer action of S, no associator and no coherence. Use this quotient carrier and its API; do not construct a second quotient. The outer actions, the associativity isomorphisms and their coherence are targets here. Construct the right S-action as the outer action of Sᵐᵒᵖ on the second factor, (p⊗s)·s′=p⊗ss′; give its pure-tensor formula and prove the required commuting-action instances. For left-module extension S⊗ᵣM use `BalancedTensorProduct ℤ R S M`, where S has right R-action s·r=s f(r), with the outer left S-action a·(s⊗m)=(as)⊗m on the first factor, constructed here. The ℤ-actions commute with these ring actions. Give the native module-category functors, ring-map adapters, finite-free base-change formulas and natural comparison with the commutative tensor API here. Give the comparison with the left-module convention by passing to Rᵐᵒᵖ, including the transpose and inverse conventions on matrix automorphisms. No flatness assumption is used. The module-category and finite-projective adapters are targets here.

The balanced construction first acts on **all** modules. For a unital map f:R→S of arbitrary associative rings, construct the additive extension functor on left modules E_f(M)=S⊗_R M with relations (s f(r))⊗m=s⊗(r m), left S-action a·(s⊗m)=(as)⊗m and map E_f(g)(s⊗m)=s⊗g(m). Give its unit m↦1⊗m, counit s⊗n↦s·n, and both triangle identities on pure tensors before packaging the native categorical adjunction. Use a common module universe containing the scalar and module carriers; provide transport along universe lifts. No flatness, injectivity, finiteness or commutativity hypothesis is needed. ## A0. Finite projectives and matrix K₀

Use Proj(R)=(TauCeti.finiteProjectiveModules Rᵐᵒᵖ).FullSubcategory from TauCeti.Algebra.Category.ModuleCat.CartanMap.Basic; Suggested.lean names it `FiniteProjectiveRight`. Adopt its existing native Module.Finite/Module.Projective objects, repleteness, zero, biproducts, essential smallness and induced exact structure; the theorem finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split identifies that structure as split. Extend its API with the finite-presentation/idempotent small models, explicit universe transport and direct-summand closure needed by the matrix comparison. A projective module here is not required to have a chosen basis or constant rank. That every object is a direct summand of a finite free module, with actual splitting maps, is Mathlib's `Module.Finite.exists_comp_eq_id_of_projective` (applied over Rᵐᵒᵖ); package from it a complementary finite projective object of Proj(R).

Use K₀(R), written `KZero`, as the supplied split K₀ of this existing Proj(R), with its canonical comparison to the existing induced exact K₀, using finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split. Establish extension-of-scalars additive functors for all unital ring maps, including their associator/unit isomorphisms, and the functorial K₀ maps. Prove that base change preserves finite projectivity by transporting a finite free splitting, without a flatness hypothesis on S. Give identity/composition/naturality and finite products. Equivalence invariance is Tau Ceti's `CategoryTheory.Equivalence.finiteProjectiveModulesK0Equiv` (`TauCeti.Algebra.Category.ModuleCat.CartanMap.Morita`, for an equivalence of module categories) and `RingEquiv.finiteProjectiveModulesK0Equiv` (`…CartanMap.RingEquiv`), stated for the exact K₀ and transported to `KZero` by `finiteProjectiveModulesExactStructure_eq_split`; the matrix Morita equivalence is Mathlib's `ModuleCat.matrixEquivalence` (`RingTheory.Morita.Matrix`) composed with these. For the right-module convention use the inverse of Mathlib's `RingEquiv.mopMatrix` (`Mathlib.Data.Matrix.Basic`, for any `[Mul α] [AddCommMonoid α]`), the ring isomorphism Mₙ(R)ᵐᵒᵖ≅Mₙ(Rᵐᵒᵖ), op(g)↦(gᵀ with entries in Rᵐᵒᵖ), which needs no commutativity. The targets kept here are the corner-module convention for Mₙ(R), n≥1, which sends a right R-module P to the right Mₙ(R)-module P¹ˣⁿ of rows, so that [R]↦[e₁₁Mₙ(R)] while the free module Mₙ(R) corresponds to n[R], and the matrix description of the resulting isomorphism K₀(R)≅K₀(Mₙ(R)).

Use idempotents e²=e in Mₙ(R), with no adjoint or topology. Relate their images as right modules to all finite projectives. Develop rectangular algebraic equivalence: e and f are equivalent if there are rectangular x,y with xy=e, yx=f, ex=x=xf and fy=y=ye; compare this with isomorphism of their images. Prove invariance under conjugation and direct sums, transport under ring maps and finite-index reindexing. Compare stable equivalence with stable similarity after adding zero blocks. The idempotent monoid V(R), modulo isomorphism of image modules and with direct sum, has the same group completion as K₀(R); construct the comparison and its class formula, rather than defining another K₀.

Prove every class is [P]−[Q] and also [e]−[Rᵐ] for an idempotent e, by taking a complement of Q. Equality [P]=[Q] means P⊕T≅Q⊕T for some finite projective T; replacing T by T⊕T′ gives a free stabilizer. Use this to produce explicit common identity/zero blocks and stable conjugators in matrix calculations. Do not assume cancellation in V(R). Describe the image of V(R) as the algebraic positive cone, without asserting it defines a proper order for arbitrary rings. Prove `kZero_divisionRing`: K₀ of a division ring D is ℤ, with the class of the free right module of rank one sent to 1 (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter II, §2, the division-ring example in the opening paragraphs of §2, before Lemma 2.1). Compute K₀(ℤ) as ℤ, K₀ of finite products by components and K₀ of matrix rings by the corner-module Morita map above. Prove the zero-ring value and all size-zero matrix conventions.

Give a concrete failure of cancellation over the real ring `R=C(S²,ℝ)`. For x∈S²⊂ℝ³ put `p(x)=I₃−xxᵀ`, prove p is an idempotent matrix over R, and set P=pR³. Identify P directly with the continuous fields `{v:S²→ℝ³:∀x,⟨x,v(x)⟩=0}` by its pointwise projection; no tangent-bundle adapter is needed, because [DifferentialGeometry 11.1](../DifferentialGeometry/README.md) (`exists_eq_zero_of_inner_eq_zero`) is stated for exactly such fields. Construct the splitting `P⊕R≅R³`, `(v,f)↦(x↦v(x)+f(x)x)`, with inverse `w↦(p w,x↦⟨x,w(x)⟩)`. Prove P is not free: evaluations force any free rank to be two, and a free basis would yield a nowhere-zero continuous field v with ⟨x,v(x)⟩=0, contradicting DifferentialGeometry 11.1, the hairy ball theorem `exists_eq_zero_of_inner_eq_zero`. This is the continuous-function version of the polynomial example in Weibel, The K-book, Chapter I, Example 1.2.2. Thus `P⊕R≅R²⊕R` but `P≄R²`, while `[P]=2[R]` in K₀(R). Supply this direct section/idempotent comparison rather than assuming a complex Serre–Swan theorem applies to real coefficients.

### A0.SphereComplexification. Real cancellation and complex frames

Resolve the scalar distinction in the real tangent-section example above. For `R_ℂ=C(S²,ℂ)` let `p_ℂ(x)=I₃−xxᵀ` be the entrywise complexification of the real projection above, and `P_ℂ=p_ℂ R_ℂ³`. Construct `P⊗_R R_ℂ≃P_ℂ` for pointwise scalar inclusion using A0's finite-idempotent base-change maps, with the formula `v⊗f↦(x↦f(x)v(x))`. Identify P_ℂ with the continuous complex vectors z(x) satisfying x·z(x)=0. This is a complex rank-two module; build its free basis explicitly, without assuming cancellation or a complex bundle-classification theorem.

Use coordinates x=(x₀,x₁,x₂) with x·x=1, set a=(1,i,0), `w(x)=a−(x₀+i x₁)x`, `d(x)=sqrt(1+x₂²)`, `u(x)=w(x)/d(x)` and `v(x)=x×conj(u(x))`, where × is the complex-bilinear extension of the native real cross product. Prove x·w=0 and `conj(w)·w=1+x₂²>0`, then that the columns u,v,x form a continuous unitary matrix. In particular u,v are an orthonormal basis of x's complex orthogonal complement. Give the inverse module maps `R_ℂ²→P_ℂ`, `(f,g)↦fu+gv`, and `z↦(conj(u)·z,conj(v)·z)`, including continuity, the reconstruction identity and compatibility with the pointwise Hermitian pairing. Thus this real cancellation counterexample becomes free after complex scalar extension. It cannot be used as a complex cancellation counterexample.

As a terminal comparison, distinguish this complexification from the complex line obtained on the real tangent plane by `J_x(y)=x×y`. Use DifferentialGeometry's outward sphere orientation, CharacteristicClasses C7's smooth identification of the sphere's tangent bundle with the bundle of fibres x^⊥, and C7's sphere Euler number and oriented-plane `e=c₁` comparison to give this line Chern number +2 and hence nontriviality. That characteristic-class comparison is downstream of A0's explicit frame and is not a prerequisite for the algebraic K₀/K₁ constructions. [SphereComplexificationTargets.lean](SphereComplexificationTargets.lean) pins the native cross-product and matrix formulas.

### A0.Mobius. Antiperiodic sections and scalar extension

Let `T=AddCircle (1:ℝ)`, `R_ℝ=C(T,ℝ)` and `R_ℂ=C(T,ℂ)`, and write [t] for its quotient coordinate. Define M_𝕜 to be the continuous functions v:ℝ→𝕜 with `v(t+1)=−v(t)`, for 𝕜=ℝ,ℂ. Give it the right R_𝕜-action `(v·f)(t)=v(t)f([t])`, pointwise inner product `conj(v(t))w(t)` descending to T, and norm `sup_(0≤t≤1)|v(t)|`. Prove boundedness, completeness and independence of the chosen unit interval, and the continuity/quotient-descent laws used here. This is the section module of the Möbius bundle, the nontrivial real line bundle on the circle, while every complex vector bundle on the circle is trivial (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter I, (4.9.1)); the treatment here is explicit and requires no general Serre–Swan theorem.

Put `u(t)=(cos(πt),sin(πt))` and `p([t])=u(t)u(t)ᵀ`. Prove p is a well-defined continuous symmetric idempotent of rank1 in M₂(R_ℝ). Construct the mutually inverse R_ℝ-linear maps `M_ℝ→pR_ℝ²`, `v↦([t]↦v(t)u(t))`, and `w↦(t↦⟨u(t),w([t])⟩)`. They preserve the pointwise inner product. Thus M_ℝ is a finite projective, with the native image/direct-summand API from A0. Prove it has no free basis: evaluations force any free rank to be1; a generator would be nowhere zero, while every continuous real antiperiodic function has a zero in [0,1] by the intermediate value theorem. Construct `M_ℝ⊕M_ℝ≃R_ℝ²` explicitly using the frame `u(t),u_perp(t)`, where `u_perp(t)=(−sin(πt),cos(πt))`, applied to the two antiperiodic coefficients.

In the algebraic K₀ already defined here, prove `η=[M_ℝ]−[R_ℝ]` has exact order2. The doubled splitting proves 2η=0. For nonvanishing, A0's free stabilization criterion would give `M_ℝ⊕R_ℝ^N≃R_ℝ^(N+1)` if η=0. Pull a putative basis back to ℝ in its frame with monodromy diag(−1,1,…,1). Its nonvanishing determinant would be a continuous real antiperiodic function, contradicting the same zero theorem. Build this evaluation/determinant argument explicitly; nonfreeness alone does not establish a nonzero K₀ class.

For complex scalars put `χ(t)=exp(πit)`. Prove χ is antiperiodic and has modulus1, and construct the isometric R_ℂ-linear isomorphism `R_ℂ≃M_ℂ`, `f↦(t↦χ(t)f([t]))`, with inverse `v↦([t]↦χ(t)⁻¹v(t))`. Identify scalar extension `M_ℝ⊗_(R_ℝ)R_ℂ≃M_ℂ` for the pointwise real-to-complex ring map, using A0's native balanced tensor carrier and the formula `v⊗f↦(t↦v(t)f([t]))`. Prove the resulting K₀ map sends η to0. This identifies the actual complexified module with a free line; it does not infer real freeness from complex freeness. [MobiusSectionTargets.lean](MobiusSectionTargets.lean) records the projection, real-zero and complex-factorization signatures on native continuous maps.

## A1. Stable matrices, elementary operations and Whitehead's lemma

For each finite n let GLₙ(R) be native matrix units, and define eᵢⱼ(a)=1+aEᵢⱼ for i≠j. Prove inversion by replacing a by −a; same-index addition; distant commutation; and [eᵢⱼ(a),eⱼₖ(b)]=eᵢₖ(ab) for distinct indices, with commutator xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. State the index conditions on every identity; do not replace ab by ba. Prove ring-map, block embedding and reindexing formulas. Define Eₙ(R), written `elementary`, as the subgroup generated by these units; provide finite-product membership witnesses, induction and lift/map lemmas. Supply upper and lower rectangular block-unipotent decompositions into elementary matrices.

Construct GL∞(R) as the directed colimit of GLₙ(R) under g↦diag(g,1), using native DirectLimit and proving the full directed-system laws. Provide canonical embeddings, injectivity, finite-stage representatives, equality at a common finite stage, multiplication/inverse at a common stage, universal mapping property and induced ring maps. Prove the comparison with finitary automorphisms of the countable free module that fix every basis vector outside a finite set and preserve its finite coordinate complement. Define E∞ as the union/generated subgroup of the embedded Eₙ, with the precise finite-stage membership criterion. Arbitrary finite index types compare by finite reindexing.

Give an explicit elementary factorization of diag(T,T⁻¹) for every T∈GLₙ(R), including n=0. Start with the three block-unipotent product [[0,T],[−T⁻¹,0]] and multiply by the constant block rotation [[0,−I],[I,0]] to obtain diag(T,T⁻¹); factor that constant rotation as well. Deduce conjugation invariance of E∞ and that stable commutators lie in E∞. Conversely every elementary generator is a stable commutator by adjoining a distinct third index and taking b=1. Prove E∞=[GL∞,GL∞], normality, perfectness of E∞, and the abelianity of GL∞/E∞ (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, Whitehead's Lemma 1.3.3 with the factorization (1.3.4), and Lemma 1.3.2). At finite size this is the pair `whitehead_commutator_elementary`, that diag(ghg⁻¹h⁻¹,Iₙ) is a product of elementary matrices of size 2n for all g,h∈GLₙ(R) (Lemma 1.3.3, (1.3.4)), and `elementary_le_commutator`, that Eₙ(R)≤[GLₙ(R),GLₙ(R)] when n≥3, because Eₙ(R) is perfect for n≥3 (Lemma 1.3.2). In finite rank do not assume Eₙ is normal or equal to a commutator subgroup without additional hypotheses.

Prove elementary products lift invertibly, with the same matrix size, through every surjective unital ring map, by lifting their finitely many coefficients. In particular diag(T,T⁻¹) lifts through any surjection to R. This never says that an arbitrary invertible matrix lifts. The class of all universally liftable matrices is not used as the defining relation: −1 lifts through every unital surjection and its class in K₁(ℤ) is nonzero. Supply this example explicitly.

## A2. Algebraic K₁ and projective automorphisms

Define K₁(R)=Abelianization(GL∞(R)) and identify it canonically with GL∞/E∞ using A1. Use additive notation for this abelian group and [uv]=[u]+[v]. Give the class map on every GLₙ, common-stage induction, the elementary and conjugation relations, block-sum formula [diag(u,v)]=[u]+[v], inverse formula, generation and universal property for elementary-invariant stable homomorphisms to abelian groups. Prove the colimit comparison with the finite-rank **abelianizations** of GLₙ; the individual Eₙ quotients need not be groups. Supply ring functoriality, zero-ring and finite-product formulas, and continuity under filtered colimits of unital rings by descending finite matrices and their finite inverse/elementary witnesses.

Give the intrinsic Bass presentation by automorphisms for an arbitrary small additive category C: the abelian group K₁ᴮ(C) generated by pairs [X,α], α∈Aut(X), with isomorphism invariance [Y,fαf⁻¹]=[X,α] for f:X≅Y, composition [X,αβ]=[X,α]+[X,β] and direct sums [X⊕Y,α⊕β]=[X,α]+[Y,β], functorial in additive functors. Prove that it already satisfies the split-exact relation: an upper-triangular automorphism of X⊕Y with diagonal blocks α,β has class [X,α]+[Y,β], because the unipotent factor becomes a commutator of elementary blocks after adding a second copy of Y. The projective automorphisms [P,u] are the case C=Proj(R). Construct its comparison with the matrix definition by choosing P⊕Q≅Rⁿ and adjoining the identity on Q. Prove independence of complement, trivialization and stabilization. Prove that this is an isomorphism and derive the relation for an automorphism of a split short exact sequence, including its upper-triangular off-diagonal block. Develop naturality and additivity of the automorphism class, identity, inverse and conjugation. This is not a claim that every stabilized automorphism is trivial.

Construct the Morita equivalence K₁(Mₙ(R))≅K₁(R), n≥1, by flattening block matrices and compare it with the finite-projective equivalence in A0, including corner embeddings and repeated matrix sizes. Prove compatibility with products and filtered colimits; compatibility with A6's boundary is proved in A6. For commutative R, construct the split determinant map K₁(R)→Rˣ with scalar section, define SK₁ as its kernel and identify the resulting decomposition. Compute K₁(F)=Fˣ for fields by Gaussian elimination, K₁(D)=Dˣ/[Dˣ,Dˣ] for division rings using the Dieudonné determinant, K₁(ℤ)={±1}, and finite products/matrix algebras via these maps. Build the Dieudonné determinant, its elementary reduction independence and multiplicativity here; it is not an unexplained supplier. State it as `dieudonne_determinant`: for a division ring D and n≥2 there is a surjective homomorphism GLₙ(D)→Dˣ/[Dˣ,Dˣ] with kernel exactly Eₙ(D), sending the diagonal matrix with one entry d and the others 1 to the class of d (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, 1.2.4 and Exercise 1.2). For n=1 the kernel is [Dˣ,Dˣ], not E₁(D)=1, so the bound n≥2 is needed for a noncommutative D. Normalize [−1]∈K₁(ℤ) as the nonzero order-two class.

## A3. Relative groups and unitizations

For a two-sided ideal I⊲R, define GL∞(R,I)=ker(GL∞(R)→GL∞(R/I)) and E∞(R,I) as the normal closure in E∞(R) of elementary matrices with entries in I. Prove the relative commutator formulas, stability, normality in GL∞(R,I), and that K₁(R,I)=GL∞(R,I)/E∞(R,I) is abelian. Give finite matrix representatives and their stabilization, ring/ideal-map functoriality, products and independence of presentations. The map K₁(R,I)→K₁(R) has image ker(K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)); it is not defined as that kernel. Prove that K₁(R,I)→K₁(R)→K₁(R/I) is exact at K₁(R); A5 extends this sequence to the left by K₂. No terminal zero is appended when units do not lift stably.

For a small additive functor T:C→D between small additive categories, define the relative group K₀(T) by triples (P,α,Q), where P and Q are objects of C and α:T(P)≅T(Q) is an isomorphism in D. Impose isomorphism invariance under isomorphisms f:P≅P′, g:Q≅Q′ with α′∘T(f)=T(g)∘α, the direct-sum relation (P,α,Q)+(P′,α′,Q′)=(P⊕P′,α⊕α′,Q⊕Q′) and the composition relation (P,α,Q)+(Q,β,S)=(P,β∘α,S); these give (P,id,P)=0, (Q,α⁻¹,P)=−(P,α,Q) and (P,T(γ),Q)=0 for every isomorphism γ:P≅Q. Construct the difference map K₀(T)→K₀(C), [(P,α,Q)]↦[P]−[Q], into the `TauCeti.SplitK0` group of C, the maps for additive commuting squares with a specified comparison isomorphism, generation by triples and the universal property. Weibel's presentation imposes additivity along every short exact sequence of triples instead of along direct sums only. Prove that the two presentations agree when T is full: after splitting P≅P′⊕P″ and Q≅Q′⊕Q″, the isomorphism α of a short exact sequence of triples is upper triangular with diagonal blocks α′,α″ and corner γ:T(P″)→T(Q′); write α as the unipotent automorphism with corner γα″⁻¹ composed with α′⊕α″, lift the unipotent factor to an automorphism v of Q by fullness, and use (Q,T(v),Q)=0. Prove that base change T_I=−⊗ᵣR/I:Proj(R)→Proj(R/I) is full: a right R/I-linear map P/PI→Q/QI lifts to P→Q because P is projective and Q→Q/QI is onto. Define K₀(R,I)=K₀(T_I), with its maps for morphisms of ring/ideal pairs, independence of small-model choices and compatibility with opposite conventions (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter II, Definition 2.10, Exercise 2.17(a)–(b) and Exercise 2.4(e)).

**Checks.**

- For T=id every triple (P,α,Q) is isomorphic to (Q,id,Q) through f=α and g=id, so K₀(id)=0; a presentation without the invariance relation would not vanish here.
- For D=0 the isomorphism α is unique and the difference map is an isomorphism K₀(T)≅K₀(C) with inverse [P]↦[(P,0,0)]; hence K₀(R,R)=K₀(R), while I=0 gives K₀(R,0)=0.
- For R=ℤ and I=5ℤ the five-term sequence below gives K₀(ℤ,5ℤ)≅𝔽₅ˣ/{±1}≅ℤ/2, generated by [(ℤ,2,ℤ)], with zero difference map since both modules have rank one; the class of 2 is nonzero because 2∉{±1} in 𝔽₅ˣ.
- Base change ℤ→ℚ is summand-dense but not full. There (ℤ²,diag(2,1/2),ℤ²)=(ℤ,2,ℤ)+(ℤ,1/2,ℤ)=(ℤ,1,ℤ)=0 by the direct-sum and composition relations, yet after adding any identity triple it is not isomorphic to an identity triple, since that would make diag(2,1/2)⊕Iₘ a product T(g)⁻¹T(f) of integral invertible matrices; so the equality criterion below needs fullness, while δ and exactness at K₀(T) and K₀(C) do not.

Call T summand-dense if every object of D is a direct summand of some T(P). For summand-dense T construct the boundary δ:K₁ᴮ(D)→K₀(T) on A2's automorphism presentation: for α∈Aut(X) choose X⊕Y≅T(P) and send [X,α] to the class of (P,α⊕id_Y,P) transported along that isomorphism. Prove independence of the complement and of the transport, preservation of composition and direct sums, and naturality in commuting squares of such functors. Fullness is not used: by the composition relation the class of (P,σβσ⁻¹,P) equals that of (P,β,P) for every automorphism σ of T(P), and two choices X⊕Y≅T(P) and X⊕Y′≅T(P′) are compared on T(P⊕P′)≅X⊕Y⊕X⊕Y′ by the automorphism exchanging the two copies of X. Prove that K₁ᴮ(D)→K₀(T)→K₀(C)→K₀(D) is exact at K₀(T) and K₀(C) (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter II, (2.10.2) and Exercise 2.17(c)–(e), stated there for cofinal functors). For full T prove the equality criterion: two triples have the same class in K₀(T) iff they become isomorphic after adding an identity triple (S,id,S) to each; the stable isomorphism classes satisfy the composition relation and have inverses because the automorphism `[[0,α⁻¹],[α,0]]` of T(P)⊕T(Q) is T of a product of three unipotent automorphisms, lifted by fullness, and diag(−1,1). Deduce that for T full and summand-dense the sequence K₁ᴮ(C)→K₁ᴮ(D)→K₀(T)→K₀(C)→K₀(D) is exact at its three interior terms: if δ[X,α]=0 the criterion writes α⊕id as T of an automorphism of C after stabilization. Base change T_I is full, as shown above, and summand-dense, since every finite projective R/I-module is a summand of (R/I)ⁿ=T_I(Rⁿ). Through A2's comparison of K₁ᴮ(Proj(R)) with matrix K₁ its boundary sends the class of u∈GLₙ(R/I) to [(Rⁿ,u,Rⁿ)], and the sequence becomes K₁(R)→K₁(R/I)→K₀(R,I)→K₀(R)→K₀(R/I), with all naturality diagrams for morphisms of ring/ideal pairs. Neither the presentation nor these exactness proofs uses Q-theory, a higher K-spectrum or a homotopy-fibre comparison; those comparisons consume the groups and maps of A3.

For a nonunital associative ring J use the integral Dorroh unitization J⁺=ℤ⊕J with (m,x)(n,y)=(mn,my+nx+xy), and define reduced Kᵢ(J) as ker(Kᵢ(J⁺)→Kᵢ(ℤ)), i=0,1; A5 gives the case i=2. Build the augmentation splitting, maps for nonunital homomorphisms and the comparison with a unital ring regarded as nonunital via J⁺≅ℤ×J. For complex algebras also construct ℂ⊕A and the comparison appropriate to ℂ-linear maps; distinguish the base-ring-relative construction from arbitrary nonunital-ring excision. No target asserts that integral reduced K-theory of an ideal equals every ambient relative group without an excision theorem. Export precisely these relative-unitization interfaces to NCG.

## A4. Stable range and finite matrix sizes

Define right-unimodular rows (a₁,…,aₙ), with witnesses Σaᵢbᵢ=1, and Bass stable range at most s, written `HasStableRangeLE R s`: every right-unimodular (s+1)-row can be shortened to (aᵢ+aₛ₊₁cᵢ)ᵢ≤s that is right-unimodular. Prove the equivalent all-longer-row formulation, monotonicity, left/opposite-ring comparison, and invariance under ring isomorphism. Develop the elementary row-reduction, orbit and stabilization lemmas from this definition. Prove for stable range at most s that GLₙ(R)→K₁(R) is surjective for n≥s, and GLₙ(R)/Eₙ(R)→K₁(R) is an isomorphism for n≥s+1, proving normality there (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, Remark 1.2.5 and Exercise 1.3). At finite size these are `surjective_stability`, that for n≥s every invertible matrix of size n+k is diag(h,Iₖ) times an elementary matrix, and `injective_stability`, that for n≥s+1 an invertible g of size n with diag(g,Iₖ) elementary is itself elementary. Stable range at most 0 holds only for the zero ring, since the row (1) is unimodular. For n≥max(3,s+1), deduce [GLₙ,GLₙ]=Eₙ and hence the corresponding isomorphism from the finite-rank abelianization. State the bounds as sufficient ranges, not as the smallest n in every ring; identify the finite-rank kernel outside the stable range by its actual quotient map. Explain the finite-field small-rank distinction: GL₂(𝔽₂)≅S₃ has nontrivial abelianization, while K₁(𝔽₂)=0.

Prove stable range one for semilocal rings, using reduction to their semisimple Artinian quotients and lifting modulo the Jacobson radical (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter I, Exercises 1.5(c) and 1.12(v)); the commutative specialization is `hasStableRangeLE_one_of_finite_maximalSpectrum`, for commutative rings with finitely many maximal ideals. Prove the Bass bound s≤d+1 for commutative Noetherian rings of Krull dimension at most d, `hasStableRangeLE_of_ringKrullDim_le`, with the dimension/drop argument explicitly developed (Bass, Algebraic K-Theory, Benjamin 1968, Theorem V.3.5, as recalled in Weibel, The K-book, Chapter I, Exercise 1.5(b)). **Checks.** ℤ does not have stable range 1: the row (2,5) is unimodular but 2+5c is never ±1; it has stable range at most 2, matching Krull dimension 1. Compute the Bass stable rank of C(X,ℂ), for nonempty compact Hausdorff X of finite covering dimension d, as floor(d/2)+1. Define the covering-dimension predicate `HasCoveringDimensionLE X d` for a topological space X and d∈ℕ: every open cover of X, taken as a set of open sets, has an open refinement, again a set of open sets each contained in a member of the cover, in which every point lies in at most d+1 distinct members. Counting distinct members rather than indices follows the design of the closed, unmerged Mathlib proposals [#43476](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43476) and [#43475](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/43475), which are design sources and not installed declarations. Build on it the strict and finite-dimensional variants, the dimension value in WithBot ℕ∞ (the empty space has value ⊥, dimension −1, and ⊤ means no finite bound), monotonicity in d, homeomorphism invariance, closed-subspace monotonicity and the finite closed-cover theorem for compact Hausdorff spaces. **Checks.** A finite discrete space satisfies the predicate with d=0 via the cover by singletons; [0,1] fails it with d=0 because an open refinement of order one is a partition into open sets and [0,1] is connected, and satisfies it with d=1; the empty space satisfies it for every d.

For compact Hausdorff X and d∈ℕ, prove the finite/indexed-cover characterization of `HasCoveringDimensionLE`: every finite indexed open cover admits a finite indexed open refining cover (V_j), a parent map p with V_j⊂U_(p j), and `encard {j : x∈V_j}≤d+1` for every x. Preserve these parents and containment witnesses in every conversion. From the arbitrary-cover predicate take a finite subcover of a refinement, deduplicate/reindex it by its distinct nonempty open sets, and choose each original parent; indexed multiplicity then equals range multiplicity. Conversely take a finite subcover of any given open cover, apply the finite characterization, and compose parent assignments to refine the original cover; a range-order bound follows from the indexed bound. Compare finite-set and finite-family encodings and their refinement maps. Do not transfer a range bound to the original indexing without deduplication: two identical copies of the one-point space have range multiplicity one and indexed multiplicity two. Monotonicity, homeomorphism invariance, compact-Hausdorff closed-subspace monotonicity and the finite closed-cover theorem are stated for this single predicate and its value. Supply the compact-Hausdorff partition-of-unity and sphere-extension characterizations needed by the proof. In particular characterize `HasCoveringDimensionLE X d` by extension to X of every continuous map from a closed subset into Sᵈ. Develop the resulting approximation/extension lemmas for the stable-range upper bound and the nonextendable-map obstruction for its lower bound. This is Lebesgue covering dimension; no equality with the separate small inductive dimension is assumed. Derive the above explicit finite-matrix surjectivity and injectivity ranges for C(X). Give the real-valued counterpart d+1 with its real dimension obstruction and treat the empty space as the zero ring. In terms of the stable-range predicate both calculations are the equivalences `hasStableRangeLE_continuousMap_complex_iff`, that for compact Hausdorff X of finite covering dimension and s≥1, C(X,ℂ) has stable range at most s iff `HasCoveringDimensionLE X (2s−1)`, and `hasStableRangeLE_continuousMap_real_iff`, the same for C(X,ℝ) with `HasCoveringDimensionLE X (s−1)` (Vaserstein 1971). The bound s≥1 is needed: a one-point X has dimension 0 but C(X,ℂ)=ℂ does not have stable range at most 0. **Checks.** For X a point both rings are fields of stable range 1; for X=[0,1] the complex ring has stable range 1 and the real ring has stable range 2; for X the 2-sphere the complex ring has stable range 2. The comparison of Bass and topological stable ranks used for these commutative function rings is a theorem here, with its density/shortening proof; no assertion for all Banach algebras is imported without its hypotheses. TopologicalKTheory may consume these ranges for its bundle stabilization, without supplying the algebraic Whitehead theorem backwards.

## A5. Steinberg groups and K₂

For arbitrary R define Stₙ(R) on generators xᵢⱼ(a), i≠j, with xᵢⱼ(a)xᵢⱼ(b)=xᵢⱼ(a+b), distant-commutator relations for i≠l and j≠k, and [xᵢⱼ(a),xⱼₖ(b)]=xᵢₖ(ab) for distinct i,j,k. Use the native free-group/quotient-by-normal-relations API. Provide maps to Eₙ, coefficient maps, index inclusions/reindexing, colimit St∞ and its stable generator induction/universal property. Prove St∞→E∞ is the universal central extension of the perfect group E∞: it is surjective, its kernel is central, and for every surjective homomorphism q′:E′→E∞ with central kernel there is exactly one homomorphism St∞→E′ whose composite with q′ is St∞→E∞ (Weibel, The K-book, Chapter III, Definition 5.3.1 and Theorem 5.5). Define K₂(R) as that kernel, obtain its abelian group structure, naturality, products, filtered-colimit and matrix Morita comparisons, and the exact sequence 1→K₂(R)→St∞(R)→GL∞(R)→K₁(R)→0, interpreting the middle image as E∞. Prove centrality in the stable group; do not assert this for every Stₙ without stable-rank hypotheses.

Give the relative Steinberg diagram for R→R/I and construct the connecting homomorphism K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I) to A3's relative group by lifting words and comparing their elementary images. Prove independence of words and lifts, and extend A3's sequence to K₂(R)→K₂(R/I)→K₁(R,I)→K₁(R)→K₁(R/I), exact at K₂(R/I) and K₁(R,I). For a nonunital ring J define reduced K₂(J) as ker(K₂(J⁺)→K₂(ℤ)) on A3's Dorroh unitization, with its augmentation splitting and maps for nonunital homomorphisms. For fields develop Steinberg symbols, bilinearity, {a,1−a}=0 and Matsumoto's presentation; distinguish this field computation from the tensor-symbol Milnor K-groups in arbitrary degrees. Compute K₂ of finite fields as zero using that presentation and its finite-field symbol calculation. The comparison of this Steinberg K₂ with π₂ of the ordinary ring K-spectrum belongs to GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §6.1.7 and §6.10, and the comparisons in degrees 0 and 1 to GeneralAlgebraicKTheory §3.14 (see the ownership paragraph); no construction here depends on them.

A general Milnor square in A6 supplies the stated low-degree sequence, not an automatic homotopy-cartesian square of all algebraic K-spectra. Any higher-excision theorem must state and prove its additional homological hypotheses. No K₂(Q)→K₁(D) arrow or cyclic Bott continuation is asserted here merely from one surjective leg. The K₂ construction answers the low-degree object question independently of such a continuation.

## A6. Milnor patching and the boundary

Let π₁:A₁→Q and π₂:A₂→Q be arbitrary unital maps of arbitrary associative rings, and assume **at least one map is surjective**. First choose π₁ surjective; prove the symmetric formulation. Use D=A₁×_Q A₂ as Mathlib's `RingHom.pullback π₁ π₂` (`RingTheory.LocalRing.Pullback`), the subring {(a₁,a₂) | π₁(a₁)=π₂(a₂)} of A₁×A₂, with projections pⱼ given by `RingHom.pullbackFst` and `RingHom.pullbackSnd`. Prove the ring universal property, base-change maps between squares and canonical isomorphisms, matrix/unit compatibility, kernels and quotient identifications, and the corresponding category-theoretic pullback comparison. Do not impose two surjective legs.

Define the category of patching triples (P₁,P₂,α), where Pⱼ is finite projective right Aⱼ and α:P₁⊗Q≅P₂⊗Q is Q-linear; morphisms are compatible pairs. Its inverse functor is the actual module

M(P₁,P₂,α)={(x,y)∈P₁×P₂ | α(x⊗1)=y⊗1}.

Construct its right D-action, prove finite projectivity and the two canonical base-change isomorphisms, and show that restriction/base change and patching are inverse additive equivalences. Prove full faithfulness, essential surjectivity, coherence of the comparison maps, and compatibility with direct sums, isomorphisms, idempotent images and maps of squares. The theorem is for finite projectives; it does not claim descent for all modules from these hypotheses alone. Give a finite complement and a matrix idempotent for a patched object, with an explicit finite construction; do not treat finite projectivity as an unexplained gluing axiom.

Fix α=u on Qⁿ and set M(u)={(a,b)∈A₁ⁿ×A₂ⁿ | uπ₁(a)=π₂(b)}. Normalize the boundary by

δ([u])=[M(u)]−[Dⁿ].

Relate this module formula to the following finite matrix formula. Choose an invertible lift X∈GL₂ₙ(A₁) of diag(u,u⁻¹), available by A1. Let e=diag(Iₙ,0ₙ) and E=(XeX⁻¹,e)∈M₂ₙ(D), using π₁(XeX⁻¹)=e. Then prove δ([u])=[E]−[(e,e)] in K₀(D), where the latter pair is the standard rank-n idempotent over D. The image of E is isomorphic to M(u) with the displayed orientation. This formula uses idempotents, and XeX⁻¹ need not be selfadjoint even when the inputs are C*-algebras.

Prove independence of the lift X, matrix size, representative u and all complement choices; more generally, for v∈GLₙ(Q) and m≥0 such that diag(u⊕Iₘ,v⊕Iₘ) lies in E₂₍ₙ₊ₘ₎(Q) with a recorded finite elementary-word witness, lift that word coefficientwise by A1 to X′∈E₂₍ₙ₊ₘ₎(A₁), form E′=(X′e′X′⁻¹,e′) with e′=diag(Iₙ₊ₘ,0), and show that [E′]−[(e′,e′)]=δ([u]), independently of v, of the stabilization and of the elementary witness. Invertibility and equal size alone do not suffice: diag(u,v) lifts by A1 only when it is elementary, and v=u⁻¹ is the case A1 factors explicitly. Equivalently start from [v]=−[u] in K₁(Q) and use A1's common-stage criterion to produce the stabilization and the witness. Supply the stabilizing block permutation/conjugation formulas, common-stage lemmas and free-complement steps used in those proofs. Prove δ([uv])=δ([u])+δ([v]), elementary vanishing, invariance under multiplication on either side by matrices lifted from either Aⱼ, and all naturality diagrams. Prove that δ commutes with A2's matrix Morita isomorphisms and finite-product decompositions applied to all four rings of the square, and with filtered colimits of squares. Under swapping the two legs and the canonical identification of D, δ changes sign. Give each assertion on matrix representatives before passing to K₁.

With fᵢ=(Kᵢ(p₁),Kᵢ(p₂)) and gᵢ(x,y)=Kᵢ(π₁)x−Kᵢ(π₂)y, prove the sequence

K₁(D) →ᶠ¹ K₁(A₁)⊕K₁(A₂) →ᵍ¹ K₁(Q) →δ K₀(D) →ᶠ⁰ K₀(A₁)⊕K₀(A₂) →ᵍ⁰ K₀(Q)

is exact **at its four interior terms**: ker g₁=im f₁, ker δ=im g₁, ker f₀=im δ, and ker g₀=im f₀. Prove every adjacent composite zero and both containments at each named term, with compatible sign choices. In the K₀ proof explicitly stabilize arbitrary projective complements to free ones, convert rectangular equivalence to stable conjugacy, lift diag(U,U⁻¹) and align the quotient idempotents. In the kernel-of-f₀ proof construct the block-centralizer normal form and recover the actual boundary class. In the K₁ proof align stable representatives using elementary lifts; do not infer exactness solely by group-completing the projective patching equivalence. No injectivity of f₁ or surjectivity of g₀ is asserted without another hypothesis.

## A7. Gluing examples and calculations

For compact Hausdorff X,Y,S, continuous i:S→X and j:S→Y, let M be their pushout in compact Hausdorff spaces. Construct the isomorphism of C(M,ℂ) with the fibre product of C(X,ℂ) and C(Y,ℂ) over C(S,ℂ), with both norm/topological and underlying-ring comparisons. If i is a closed embedding, prove the restriction C(X)→C(S) is surjective by complex Tietze extension; no embedding or surjectivity is required of j. Consume [HilbertModules H5](../HilbertModules/README.md)'s compact-X comparison between finite-rank Hermitian bundles and finite projective section modules, with [TopologicalKTheory T1](../TopologicalKTheory/README.md)'s bundle category and clutching conventions. Supply the forgetting adapter to A0's algebraic finite-projective right C(X)-modules: H2's projection model equips every such module with a compatible Hermitian norm, and every algebraic module map is bounded and adjointable by its finite-matrix realization. Prove independence of metric choices up to module isomorphism, using all continuous fiberwise linear bundle maps as morphisms. Through this equivalence identify A6's patched projectives with the actual glued vector bundles, including gluing maps and section modules. Define the corresponding idempotent explicitly and show its class agrees with A6. For two closed disks glued along their boundary, use the fixed clutching convention and compare δ of a clutching matrix with the reduced bundle class on the sphere; the reduced class is read in TopologicalKTheory T4's `K(S²)≅ℤ[β]/(β²)`, and the source group of δ stays the algebraic K₁ of C(S¹,ℂ) rather than being replaced by topological K₁. Include the line bundle on S² from a degree-one circle clutching function and its sign under reversing the gluing.

Compute the arithmetic Milnor square A₁=ℤ, A₂=ℤ, Q=𝔽₅, π₁ reduction and π₂ the same reduction, and D={(a,b) | a≡b mod5}. Compute the units and the normalized sequence using K₁(ℤ)={±1}, K₁(𝔽₅)=𝔽₅ˣ and A0's K₀ calculations. Prove ker(K₀(D)→ℤ²)≅𝔽₅ˣ/{±1}≅ℤ/2 and identify its nonzero class by M(2); compute image K₀(D) in ℤ² as the diagonal and hence K₀(D)≅ℤ⊕ℤ/2 with rank-one generator. Construct Q, the constants map and the pullback isomorphism using native polynomial/ideal quotients.

## A8. Comparison with topological K-theory

For a unital complex Banach algebra A, define the stable component group K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(A) as the colimit of the groups GLₙ(A)/GLₙ(A)₀ under g↦diag(g,1), where GLₙ(A)₀ is the identity component, a normal subgroup; it is functorial for continuous unital algebra homomorphisms. Continuity is needed: the functor is defined through norm-continuous paths, which a discontinuous ring homomorphism need not preserve. Every elementary matrix is connected to 1 through t↦eᵢⱼ(ta). Therefore construct the natural surjection K₁ᵃˡᵍ(A)→K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(A), which makes K₁ᵗᵒᵖ(A) abelian, and identify its kernel as the image of the stable identity component modulo E∞. Give representative, stabilization, multiplication and functoriality formulas for continuous unital homomorphisms. Show K₁ᵃˡᵍ(ℂ)=ℂˣ and that the comparison kills every class, because every GLₙ(ℂ) is connected. Do not assert algebraic K₁ agrees with topological K₁ even for a dense holomorphically closed subalgebra; its topological homotopy quotient is an extra operation.

For a surjective continuous unital homomorphism of complex Banach algebras, prove exponentials and finite products of exponentials lift through matrix maps. Prove the identity component is the subgroup generated by any sufficiently small norm ball about 1 contained in {g : ‖g−1‖<1}, using the convergent logarithm and its exponential path, and hence equals the path component. A norm ball about 1 need not be inverse invariant. If a symmetric generating neighborhood is used, take an open neighborhood U contained in that ball and replace it by U∩U⁻¹; prove openness, symmetry and containment rather than calling the ball symmetric. All norm-contour/logarithm construction is consumed from BanachAlgebras' holomorphic functional calculus with its actual completeness and spectrum hypotheses. Spectral invariance of a noncomplete normed algebra alone supplies neither contour integrals in its own topology nor a continuous functional-calculus path, so the statements here are for complete normed algebras. No six-term cyclic sequence or Bott continuation is asserted for arbitrary rings from A6.
