## FreeGroupsAndLimitGroups: 2026-10-21 to 2026-10-23 (`b9d4d78` to `bfcff79`)

Layer 1 opens with the conjugacy criterion for free groups: two elements are conjugate exactly when the cyclic reductions of their reduced words are rotations of each other ([`FreeGroup.isConj_iff_isRotated_reduceCyclically`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Conjugacy.html#FreeGroup.isConj_iff_isRotated_reduceCyclically)). On top of a new notion of proper power came the root and centralizer theory. Every nontrivial element is uniquely `r ^ k` with `r` not a proper power ([`IsFreeGroup.exists_maximalRoot`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/MaximalRoot.html#IsFreeGroup.exists_maximalRoot)). The centralizer of `r ^ k` is the cyclic group generated by `r` ([`IsFreeGroup.centralizer_singleton_eq_zpowers`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/MaximalRoot.html#IsFreeGroup.centralizer_singleton_eq_zpowers)), and two elements commute exactly when they are powers of a common element ([`IsFreeGroup.commute_iff_exists_common_zpow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Commuting.html#IsFreeGroup.commute_iff_exists_common_zpow)).

CSA groups are now defined ([`Group.IsCSA`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/CSA.html#Group.IsCSA)), together with commutation transitivity and maximal abelian centralizers. A negative check shows that `F₂ × ℤ` is not CSA. The layer's closing theorem is still missing: that maximal cyclic subgroups of a free group are malnormal, so free groups are CSA. Primitive elements have not been started either.

Layer 5 also began. It now has `G`-groups, the free `G`-group `G[X] = G ∗ F(X)` with its universal property ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.homEquiv`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Basic.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.homEquiv)), and algebraic sets, ideals, radicals and coordinate groups. One of the roadmap's boundary checks is proved: over `F(a, b)` the radical of `x²` is the normal closure of `x`, not of `x²` ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.rad_sq`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/AlgebraicSet.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.rad_sq)). Separation and discrimination are defined, but their separating counterexample is not yet there (TauCeti#19804).

## FreeGroupsAndLimitGroups: 2026-10-23 to 2026-10-25 (`bfcff79` to `e8ea0d1`)

The main theorem of Layer 1 has landed: free groups are CSA ([`IsFreeGroup.isCSA`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/CSA.html#IsFreeGroup.isCSA)), meaning every maximal abelian subgroup is malnormal. It follows from the fact that the cyclic subgroup generated by an element that is not a proper power is malnormal ([`IsFreeGroup.isTISubgroup_zpowers`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/CSA.html#IsFreeGroup.isTISubgroup_zpowers)). Primitive elements are now defined as members of some free basis ([`Group.IsPrimitive`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Primitive.html#Group.IsPrimitive)). They are never proper powers, and commutators are never primitive, so the two notions really do differ. Free reduction, word equality, multiplication and substitution are now shown to be primitive recursive on word codes ([`FreeGroup.primrec_reduce`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Computability.html#FreeGroup.primrec_reduce)). Conjugacy and proper-power membership are not yet covered.

Layer 5 advanced through six sections, §§5.4–5.9. The Baumslag–Myasnikov–Remeslennikov result that CSA groups with nonabelian coefficients are domains ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.IsGDomain.of_isCSA`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Domain.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.IsGDomain.of_isCSA)) gives that nonabelian free groups are domains. Over such domains the Zariski topology has the algebraic sets as exactly its closed sets ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isClosed_iff_V`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Zariski.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isClosed_iff_V)). Sanov's theorem shows that `[[1,2],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[2,1]]` freely generate a free subgroup of `SL(2, ℤ)` ([`TauCeti.injective_freeGroup_lift_transvection_two`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Linear.html#TauCeti.injective_freeGroup_lift_transvection_two)), so every free group of countable rank embeds there. Linear groups over Noetherian rings are equationally Noetherian ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_generalLinearGroup`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Linear.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_generalLinearGroup)). Both halves of "free groups are equationally Noetherian" are now present, but the corollary itself has not been stated.

Layers 2 and 3 have also begun. There are Serre graphs with orientations and reduced paths ([`TauCeti.SerreGraph`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Serre/Basic.html#TauCeti.SerreGraph)), the first-order theory of groups ([`FirstOrder.Language.Theory.group`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/ModelTheory/Algebra/Group/Basic.html#FirstOrder.Language.Theory.group)), and systems of equations and inequations ([`TauCeti.EqIneqSystem`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/EqIneqSystem.html#TauCeti.EqIneqSystem)).

## FreeGroupsAndLimitGroups: 2026-10-25 to 2026-11-06 (`e8ea0d1` to `a67a650`)

Layer 8 opened with its central equivalence: the theorem of Champetier and Guirardel that limit groups are exactly the finitely generated fully residually free groups ([`TauCeti.LimitGroup.isLimitGroup_iff_isFullyResiduallyFree`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/LimitGroup/Characterization.html#TauCeti.LimitGroup.isLimitGroup_iff_isFullyResiduallyFree)), proved over a compact space of marked groups (TauCeti#23017). A second characterization says a group is a limit group exactly when it is finitely generated and satisfies every universal sentence true in `F₂` ([`TauCeti.LimitGroup.isLimitGroup_iff_forall_isUniversal`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/LimitGroup/UniversalTheory.html#TauCeti.LimitGroup.isLimitGroup_iff_forall_isUniversal)). Around these came the structure of fully residually free groups (CSA, domains, the big powers property) and extensions of centralizers with their retractions. Still missing is the theorem that those retractions discriminate an extension (§8.6), and with it the iterated extensions.

Layer 5 is now complete apart from one counterexample. Free groups of countable rank are equationally Noetherian ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_of_freeGroupBasis`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Linear.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_of_freeGroupBasis)), which is Guba's theorem in finite rank. Over any equationally Noetherian group, closed sets split irredundantly into irreducible components, and an algebraic set is irreducible exactly when its coordinate group is discriminated ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isIrreducible_V_iff_isDiscriminatedBy`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Irreducible/Basic.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isIrreducible_V_iff_isDiscriminatedBy)). The `F × F` example of §5.3 is still to come. Layer 7 began with Malcev's equation ([`Group.IsVaught.malcevWord_eq_one_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/MalcevEquation.html#Group.IsVaught.malcevWord_eq_one_iff)). Over torsion-free CSA groups satisfying Vaught's condition, it collapses any finite system to a single equation. That free groups satisfy Vaught's condition is not established here.

Layer 2 reached the Stallings construction. It now has trees, folds, the Stallings graph of a list of words ([`TauCeti.StallingsGraph.stallingsGraph`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Stallings/Construction.html#TauCeti.StallingsGraph.stallingsGraph)) and product graphs that compute intersections, but not yet freeness of graph groups, Howson or Schreier. Layer 3 reduced quantifier-free, existential and universal formulas to word systems. Layers 4, 6 and 9 began with graphs of groups, polygonal surface models and Lyndon length functions.
