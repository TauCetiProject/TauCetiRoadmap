### Named results

- **The conjugacy criterion in free groups**: two elements are conjugate exactly when the cyclic reductions of their reduced words are cyclic rotations of each other ([`FreeGroup.isConj_iff_isRotated_reduceCyclically`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Conjugacy.html#FreeGroup.isConj_iff_isRotated_reduceCyclically)).
- **Free groups are CSA**: every maximal abelian subgroup of a free group is malnormal ([`IsFreeGroup.isCSA`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/CSA.html#IsFreeGroup.isCSA)). The proof uses the cyclic centralizers and maximal roots of the earlier sections. The maximal abelian subgroups are exactly the cyclic groups generated by elements that are not proper powers ([`IsFreeGroup.maximal_isMulCommutative_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/CSA.html#IsFreeGroup.maximal_isMulCommutative_iff)).
- **Nonabelian free groups are domains**: no two nontrivial elements `x`, `y` have every conjugate of `y` commuting with `x` ([`IsFreeGroup.isGDomain_id`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Domain.html#IsFreeGroup.isGDomain_id)). This is a special case of the Baumslag–Myasnikov–Remeslennikov result that CSA groups with nonabelian coefficients are domains ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.IsGDomain.of_isCSA`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Domain.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.IsGDomain.of_isCSA)).
- **Sanov's theorem**: `[[1,2],[0,1]]` and `[[1,0],[2,1]]` freely generate a free subgroup of `SL(2, ℤ)` ([`TauCeti.injective_freeGroup_lift_transvection_two`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Linear.html#TauCeti.injective_freeGroup_lift_transvection_two)). It follows that every free group of countable rank embeds in `SL(2, ℤ)` ([`TauCeti.exists_injective_freeGroup_SL`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Linear.html#TauCeti.exists_injective_freeGroup_SL)).
- **Linear groups are equationally Noetherian**: for a commutative Noetherian ring `R`, every system of equations over `GL(n, R)` has a finite subsystem with the same solutions ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_generalLinearGroup`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Linear.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.isEquationallyNoetherian_generalLinearGroup)).

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Coordinate groups** ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.CoordGroup`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/AlgebraicSet.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.CoordGroup)) are the quotients `G[X] ⧸ Rad(S)`. Their homomorphisms to `H` correspond to the solutions of `S`, which makes them the object that Layers 7, 8 and 11 study.
- **The Zariski topology** ([`TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.zariskiTopology`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAlgGeom/Zariski.html#TauCeti.GroupAlgGeom.zariskiTopology)) on tuples is generated by the complements of algebraic sets. Over a domain its closed sets are exactly the algebraic sets, because finite unions of algebraic sets are again algebraic. That is the hypothesis the irreducible-component theory of §5.10 needs.
- **Primitive elements** ([`Group.IsPrimitive`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/FreeGroup/Primitive.html#Group.IsPrimitive)) are the members of some free basis, defined using Mathlib's free bases. Primitive elements are never proper powers, and commutators are never primitive, so "primitive" and "not a proper power" stay distinct for Layer 11's generic families.
