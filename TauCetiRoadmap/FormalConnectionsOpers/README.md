# Formal algebraic connections, loop gauge groupoids and opers

Construct algebraic connections and their sheaf theory, formal loop gauge
prestacks, regular and punctured opers, Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction and its
Poisson cotangent algebroid. The resulting objects give a precise geometric
parameter space for critical-level representation theory. Connections are
relative de Rham connections; an oper remembers a transverse Borel reduction.
Isomorphisms of the underlying connections form a groupoid over the oper space.
They are not a global action on oper normal forms.

Work over a characteristic-zero field k for algebraic and formal calculus, over
a characteristic-zero field with split pinning for the adjoint semisimple
DS statements, and over ℂ for the general reductive oper-reduction
theorem in F6. The moduli and DS construction use every commutative k-algebra R,
including nonreduced algebras, rather than only field-valued points. Write
O_R=R[[t]], F_R=R((t)), D_R=Spf O_R and D_R×=Spec F_R. The relative derivative
kills R. Formal Laurent coefficients use the t-adic linear topology, with R discrete.
Function-algebra modules use F1/F5's pro-discrete and ! tensor topologies;
analytic comparisons use the ordinary complex topology.
Here R is a test algebra, not the topological algebra Fun Op. F5–F8 use
adjoint split semisimple G; F4 and F6 explicitly retain general reductive
intrinsic opers and F4 describes their central-isogeny comparison.
Continuous differentials are required: algebraic Kähler differentials of
R((t))/R alone are not the formal cotangent carrier.

## Foundations and boundaries

**Native Lie-series and coefficient conventions.** F4.3's lower complex-point and root-generation stage
consumes the Lie-group, tangent, center/derived and root-generation inputs from LieGroups Layers 0–6,
together with only the generic pre-compact-form Layer 7 carrier, global-exponential and realification
interfaces needed by the F2.4/F4.3 lower dictionary. This stage does not consume Layer 7's algebraic
compact-form specialization, Layers 8–9, or compact C5 restriction. The later compact F4.3 phase consumes
the Layer 7 algebraic compact-form specialization, the Layer 6 root/Weyl comparison and C5.1 before the
compact restriction application. Neither phase treats private `sorry` definitions in a Suggested file as
implementations. Coordinate substitution is contravariant:
`σ_φ(f)=f∘φ` and `σ_φ ∘ σ_ψ = σ_(ψ∘φ)` on the common domain where both substitutions are defined;
prove coefficientwise continuity and all domain/unit hypotheses. The cotangent Poisson bracket is
fixed by `[d f,d g]_P = d{f,g}` and the Leibniz rule in each coefficient; the completed cotangent target
must prove the differential, Jacobi, and continuity laws rather than assume a bracket field.

Finite-dimensional smooth groupoids and Poisson manifolds belong to
LieGroupoidsPoisson. This roadmap owns algebraic Lie–Rinehart and ind-formal
versions, using native Lie–Rinehart vocabulary. Smooth flat bundles and
monodromy belong to SmoothBundlesConnections B3–B5. ComplexKahlerGeometry K4
supplies holomorphic section/connection carriers, curve flatness and the flat
holomorphic/smooth/local-system comparison, consuming proposal279 M7’s bundle
mixin and cocycle gluing. F2.4 owns a common analytification construction for
separated finite-type smooth complex schemes with Ω¹ locally free of constant
rank d, together with F2.4’s comparison to Krull pure dimension. Its curve
specialization analytifies finite locally free sheaves and connections and
compares them with those carriers; this does not equate
formal de Rham local systems with finite étale or finite-monodromy local systems.
General root classification, highest-weight representation theory, PBW and
universal enveloping algebras stay with their existing owners.

Every construction below includes morphisms, extensionality, identity and
composition, base change with its hypotheses, naturality, local restriction,
and explicit comparisons between its coordinate and intrinsic descriptions.
The preceding proposed roadmap contracts are dependencies to implement in Tau
Ceti; their open status is never a reason to postpone construction.

## F1. Relative formal calculus and Tate modules

1. Extend formal derivative, product/chain rule, residue and substitution to
   arbitrary commutative R. Construct relative `Derivation R F_R F_R`, its
   coefficient formula d(t^j)=j t^(j−1), and dt-trivialized continuous
   differentials. Prove their universal property for continuous derivations
   into complete separated linearly topologized F_R-modules with compatible
   scalar multiplication. For the oper function/cotangent constructions pin the ! tensor completion:
   V⊗^!W=lim_(U,T)(V/U)⊗(W/T), over open linear submodules U,T, with the
   inverse-limit topology; prove presentation independence and its universal
   property for bilinear maps factoring continuously through these quotient
   systems. Build its relative module/sheaf form by finite-projective charts
   and descent. Distinguish it from ordered and jointly-continuous tensor
   completions; no algebraic tensor product is silently completed.
2. Define coordinate automorphisms by φ∈tR[[t]] with unit coefficient of t.
   Construct inverse substitution recursively, the group functor Aut(O),
   composition order, and its action on F_R and differential forms. Use
   FormalLaurentPuiseux L1's σ_φ∘σ_ψ=σ_(ψ∘φ), with (ψ∘φ)(t)=ψ(φ(t)).
   Therefore coordinate pullback is a right action, (q^φ)^ψ=q^(φ∘ψ). Prove
   d(f∘φ)=(f'∘φ)φ' dt and invariance of Res(f dt). These are continuous
   R-algebra automorphisms preserving the chosen formal disc; no statement
   about arbitrary abstract automorphisms of ℂ((t)) is intended.
3. Construct the relative Tate module V((t)) for finite projective R-modules V,
   lattices V[[t]], exact sequences, continuous duals and residue duality
   V((t))^∨_cont ≃ V^∨((t))dt, with lattice annihilators and coefficient
   formulas. Use completed local modules when V varies over a scheme. Define
   discrete, linearly compact and Tate modules by lattice/presentation data;
   prove independence of the lattice, base change on finite-projective charts,
   and the tensor/dual/descent rules used in F7–F8. The ℂ-point linear topology
   is not a substitute for a sheaf of such modules on all R-points.

## F2. Algebraic sheaf connections and differential operators

1. For a k-algebra A and an A-module M, define a connection as a k-linear map
   M→Ω¹_A/k⊗_A M satisfying the Leibniz identity. In the formal setting replace
   Ω¹ by F1's continuous differentials and tensor by the appropriate completed
   tensor. Construct the equivalent vector-field description: an A-linear map
   `Derivation k A A → Module.End k M` whose operators obey
   ∇_ξ(am)=a∇_ξm+ξ(a)m. Prove the equivalence when Ω¹ is finite projective;
   absent that hypothesis retain the differential-form definition. The target
   is k-linear endomorphisms; O-linear endomorphisms cannot differentiate.
2. Construct order-filtered algebraic differential operators on modules via
   iterated commutators with multiplication, the symbol map, first-jet/Atiyah
   exact sequence and its splitting criterion for a connection. Define the
   sheaf of these operators on schemes by restriction and localization; prove
   gluing, and for smooth schemes identify gr D_X with Sym T_X using PBW for
   Lie–Rinehart differential operators. Construct the Lie–Rinehart enveloping
   algebra by its relations [ξ,a]=ξ(a), [ξ,η]=their bracket and A-linearity.
   Its universal property, filtered PBW under projectivity, and equivalence
   between flat connections and modules with compatible O_X-action are targets
   here, not dependencies on an unnamed D-module roadmap.
3. Develop pullback along algebra maps and scheme morphisms, direct sums,
   tensors, duals, Hom, horizontal morphisms and curvature; prove Bianchi and
   flatness ⇔ ∇_[ξ,η]=[∇_ξ,∇_η]. Extend these to vector-bundle sheaves using
   `Scheme.PresheafOfModules` and its sheaf/finite-local-freeness conditions.
   Construct principal G-connections on fpqc torsors via local gauge forms
   and gluing, and prove independence of trivialization and the associated
   vector-bundle dictionary for a faithful algebraic representation.
4. Construct a common analytification for every separated finite-type smooth
   scheme X over ℂ whose Ω¹_X/ℂ is locally free of constant rank d. Call this
   constant differential dimension d. Construct the comparison with Krull pure
   dimension d. At every closed complex point x construct the cotangent-fibre
   isomorphism `Ω¹_X/ℂ⊗κ(x)≃m_x/m_x²`; smoothness makes the local ring regular,
   so its dimension is this cotangent dimension. Prove the finite-type field
   dimension theorem identifying the local dimension at a closed point with the
   dimension of its irreducible component; regularity makes that component unique
   locally. The differential rank is locally constant on a smooth scheme. Use
   the Jacobson property of finite-type complex schemes, so every nonempty open
   contains a closed point, to pass the closed-point rank calculation to every
   point. Prove the equivalence of constant differential dimension d with every
   irreducible component having Krull dimension d. Include the empty scheme
   separately, with its empty manifold for each selected d. Define X(ℂ) as the
   ℂ-morphisms Spec ℂ→X. For X=Spec A construct the Spec-adjunction equivalence
   with `A →ₐ[ℂ] ℂ`, natural under algebra maps. On a finite affine presentation
   identify these points with the common zero set of the defining
   polynomials in ℂ^N and use its subspace topology. Prove independence from
   generators/presentation by the polynomial coordinate maps and their inverse
   equations. For each localization D(f), evaluation identifies its points with
   {x:f(x)≠0}; prove the localization map is an open embedding. Use the principal
   open basis to prove this for every Zariski open immersion. For a finite affine
   cover construct the actual `TopCat.GlueData` of these point spaces and their
   overlaps, identify its glued point set with X(ℂ), and transport its topology.
   Prove that each affine-open point map is an `IsOpenEmbedding`, these maps cover,
   every affine open has the topology above, and common refinements identify the
   constructions by the identity on X(ℂ). These statements precede any manifold
   instance. Prove the affine tensor-product point comparison and its compatibility
   on open charts, giving (X×Y)^an≃X^an×Y^an as topological spaces. Prove closed
   immersions analytify to closed embeddings; the separated diagonal and this
   product comparison give Hausdorffness. Each affine piece is a second-countable
   subspace of a finite-dimensional complex space. For the finite family of its
   open images U_i, transport second countability to each subtype U_i and apply
   `TopologicalSpace.secondCountableTopology_of_countable_cover`, with openness,

   separability of X(ℂ). The local implicit-function charts need not be finite.

   Prove both finite-type complex-point Nullstellensatz applications. Every
   nonempty Zariski open of a finite-type complex scheme has a complex point:
   reduce to a nonempty principal affine open and a finite polynomial presentation,
   then use `MvPolynomial.eq_vanishingIdeal_singleton_of_isMaximal` and
   `MvPolynomial.isMaximal_iff_eq_vanishingIdeal_singleton`. For a reduced finitely
   generated complex algebra A, prove that an element vanishing under every
   `A →ₐ[ℂ] ℂ` is zero, using
   `MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical` on its presentation ideal.
   Keep the reduced hypothesis in this second statement; all R-valued formal

   comparison: identify the free differential rank n in each standard-étale

   composition and naturality in these charts. For products first use the native

   projections, pairing and associativity comparisons. Construct the natural complex-linear
   tangent isomorphism
   `τ_x:T_xX^an≃Der_ℂ(O_X,x,ℂ)≃(Ω¹_X/ℂ⊗κ(x))∨`: a vector acts on a regular
   germ by the derivative of its analytification. Prove germ independence,
   Leibniz, invertibility in the standard-étale coordinates, and the chain rule
   `τ_f(x)∘d(f^an)_x=(precompose f#)∘τ_x`, with the residue-field module actions

   Specialize to curves, d=1, using the complex continuous linear equivalence

   Own the rank-one fan scheme application before comparing its analytification:
   classify finite fans in a rank-one lattice as the empty fan, the zero cone,

## F3. Formal loop groups, connections and quotient prestacks

1. For the native commutative coordinate Hopf algebra H of an affine group G,
   define LG(R)=G(F_R), L⁺G(R)=G(O_R), and K_N(R)=ker(G(O_R)→G(R[t]/t^N))
   for N≥1. Use `TauCeti.HopfAlgebra.points`, its convolution group law and
   native counit-derivation Lie algebra. Construct their functors and maps,
   congruence inclusions, normality in L⁺G, separation ∩_N K_N={1}, proalgebraic
   presentation of L⁺G by jets, and the inverse-limit description of K_N.
   In characteristic zero construct
   exp_N:(t^N𝔤_R[[t]],BCH)≃*K_N(R), its inverse log_N, naturality in R,
   derivative equal to the identity at zero, and compatibility in N. For M>N
   construct the finite-jet equivalence (t^N𝔤_R[[t]]/t^M𝔤_R[[t]],BCH)
   ≃*K_N(R)/K_M(R) and all quotient-map commuting squares;
   no exponentiation of an arbitrary locally nilpotent module is implied.
2. Construct LG as an ind-affine functor by bounding poles in a finite
   presentation of H and of the inverse point. Prove the presentation's
   independence of embedding and cofinal bounds, functoriality and fpqc sheaf
   condition, including nonreduced R. Bound inverses as well as matrix entries:
   GL_n loops with bounded entries alone do not specify an invertible loop.
   Construct Lie(LG)=𝔤((t)), jets and differential of group morphisms by
   dual-number points, compatible with the native convolution tangent bracket.
3. Construct Conn_G(R)=d+A(t)dt with A∈𝔤⊗_k F_R; finite dimension identifies
   this algebraic tensor with the relative Tate coefficient module. Define
   dlog(g)=(dg)g⁻¹ intrinsically through dual-number tangent translation and
   prove gauge(g,A)=Ad(g)A−dlog(g), the cocycle identity for dlog, and
   gauge(h,gauge(g,A))=gauge(hg,A). With g=1+εx, infinitesimal variation is
   [x,A]−x'. Construct the tautological relative connection.
4. Construct the action groupoid of trivialized connections, with actual
   `Groupoid` instances, base-change functors and naturality; source A, target
   gauge(g,A), unit 1, inverse g⁻¹, composite h after g is hg. Its fpqc
   stackification on coefficient algebras is Loc_G(D×). Identify its objects
   intrinsically as relative connections on torsors trivializable after a
   coefficient-ring fpqc cover. Construct the groupoid of all G-torsors with
   relative connection over F_R using F2, and the comparison functor; prove
   full faithfulness and the stated essential-image criterion. Prove that
   torsors extending to O_R are in this image for smooth G by formal lifting
   after trivializing their t=0 fiber. F6 proves the all-torsor comparison
   on ℂ-points. Ordinary fpqc local triviality over Spec F_R is not by itself
   the coefficient-cover criterion over Spec R. Prove stabilizers equal
   horizontal automorphisms,
   and its deformation complex [𝔤((t))→𝔤((t))dt] with differential ∇_ad.
   Quotient sets retain only isomorphism classes; downstream category descent
   consumes the groupoid/prestack, including stabilizers.

## F4. Intrinsic opers and the DS/Kostant slice

1. For a pinned split semisimple group define an oper as a principal
   connection plus Borel reduction whose second fundamental form lies in
   the open B-orbit of 𝔤^(−1)/𝔟, where
   𝔤^(−1)=𝔟⊕Σ_i 𝔤_(−α_i): its negative-simple-root components are units and
   its other negative-root components vanish. Make this a sheaf condition,
   construct arrows preserving the reduction, restriction and base change.
   For an arbitrary connected reductive group use the same condition on its
   derived-root directions and retain the central connection directions.
   Prove the B-gauge presentation with invertible coefficients ψ_i f_i.
2. For adjoint semisimple G, use the simple-root character isomorphism of T
   to normalize all ψ_i to 1, obtaining (d+f+𝔟((t))dt)/N((t)), with
   f=Σ_i f_i. The normalized N-quotient does not automatically describe a
   nonadjoint group's opers; central isogenies, lift torsors and stabilizers
   must be retained by the intrinsic presentation.
3. Construct the split algebraic Chevalley–Kostant adapter in three stages:
   lower rank/point/tangent/center/derived and root-generation dictionary;
   LieGroups L7's algebraic compact-form specialization using that dictionary;
   then compact C5.1 restriction and invariant/rational descent. The lower stage
   uses no compact-form or C5 theorem. Generic L7 complex carriers/exponential
   are independent prerequisites of that lower stage.

   First establish the algebraic rank required by F2.4. For a connected reductive
   complex affine group G=Spec H, put I=ker ε and let 𝔤 be the native counit-valued
   derivations `Derivation ℂ H (TauCeti.Bialgebra.CounitAlgebra ℂ H ℂ)`.
   Construct the algebraic left Maurer–Cartan trivialization
