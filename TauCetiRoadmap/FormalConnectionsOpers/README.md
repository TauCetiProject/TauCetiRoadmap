# Formal algebraic connections, loop gauge groupoids and opers

Construct algebraic connections and their sheaf theory, formal loop gauge
prestacks, regular and punctured opers, Drinfeld–Sokolov reduction and its
Poisson cotangent algebroid. The resulting objects give a precise geometric
parameter space for critical-level representation theory. Connections are
relative de Rham connections; an oper remembers a transverse Borel reduction.
Isomorphisms of the underlying connections form a groupoid over the oper space.
They are not a global action on oper normal forms.

The ordinary form-connection carrier and elementary all-order differential-operator/principal-parts API in F2.1–F2.3 are over arbitrary commutative base rings, as specified there. Work over a characteristic-zero field k for the remaining algebraic and formal calculus, over
a characteristic-zero field with split pinning for the adjoint semisimple
DS statements, and over ℂ for the general reductive oper-reduction
theorem in F6. The moduli and DS construction use every commutative k-algebra R,
including nonreduced algebras, rather than only field-valued points. Write
O_R=R[[t]], F_R=R((t)), D_R=Spf O_R and D_R×=Spec F_R. The relative derivative
kills R. Formal Laurent coefficients use the t-adic linear topology, with R discrete.
Function-algebra modules use F1/F5's pro-discrete and ! tensor topologies;
analytic comparisons use the ordinary complex topology.
Here R is a test algebra, not the topological algebra Fun Op. F5–F8 use
adjoint split semisimple G; F4 and F6 explicitly retain general reductive
intrinsic opers and F4 describes their central-isogeny comparison.
Continuous differentials are required: algebraic Kähler differentials of
R((t))/R alone are not the formal cotangent carrier.

## Foundations and boundaries

**Native Lie-series and coefficient conventions.** F4.3's lower complex-point and root-generation stage
consumes the Lie-group, tangent, center/derived and root-generation inputs from LieGroups Layers 0–6,
together with only the generic pre-compact-form Layer 7 carrier, global-exponential and realification
interfaces needed by the F2.4/F4.3 lower dictionary. This stage does not consume Layer 7's algebraic
compact-form specialization, Layers 8–9, or compact C5 restriction. The later compact F4.3 phase consumes
the Layer 7 algebraic compact-form specialization, the Layer 6 root/Weyl comparison and C5.1 before the
compact restriction application. Neither phase treats private `sorry` definitions in a Suggested file as
implementations. Coordinate substitution is contravariant:
`σ_φ(f)=f∘φ` and `σ_φ ∘ σ_ψ = σ_(ψ∘φ)` on the common domain where both substitutions are defined;
prove coefficientwise continuity and all domain/unit hypotheses. The cotangent Poisson bracket is
fixed by `[d f,d g]_P = d{f,g}` and the Leibniz rule in each coefficient; the completed cotangent target
must prove the differential, Jacobi, and continuity laws rather than assume a bracket field.

Finite-dimensional smooth groupoids and Poisson manifolds belong to
LieGroupoidsPoisson. This roadmap owns algebraic Lie–Rinehart and ind-formal
versions, using native Lie–Rinehart vocabulary. Smooth flat bundles and
monodromy belong to SmoothBundlesConnections B3–B5. ComplexKahlerGeometry K4
supplies holomorphic section/connection carriers, curve flatness and the flat

mixin and cocycle gluing. ComplexComparisonPartII C−1.1 owns the scheme
analytification functor and its analytic ring sheaf; C0.4 and C5.1–C5.2 own its
coherent-module, differential-operator and connection adapters. F2.4 owns the
fixed-model manifold realization for separated finite-type smooth complex
schemes with Ω¹ locally free of constant rank d, together with the comparison
to Krull pure dimension and the identity-on-complex-points comparison with that
same analytic space. Its curve specialization compares the supplied finite
locally free sheaves and connections with those carriers; this does not equate
formal de Rham local systems with finite étale or finite-monodromy local systems.
General root classification, highest-weight representation theory, PBW and
universal enveloping algebras stay with their existing owners.

The stage order is F2.2’s algebraic operators and all-order principal parts →
ComplexComparisonPartII C5.1’s analytic operator adapter → F2.4’s connection
application, with C−1.1/C0.3–C0.4’s analytic-space and coherent-module prefix
supplying the same carriers. The prefix uses K1–K4 and the shared algebraic
connection operations, not F2.4’s manifold, toric, group or gauge applications.
F2.4’s point/tangent comparison and F4.3’s lower root-generation dictionary
precede LieGroups L7’s algebraic compact-form specialization; compact C5.1 of
CharacteristicClasses is used only in F4.3’s later invariant comparison.
Proper coherent GAGA and global algebraization are not prerequisites of this lane.

Every construction below includes morphisms, extensionality, identity and
composition, base change with its hypotheses, naturality, local restriction,
and explicit comparisons between its coordinate and intrinsic descriptions.
The preceding proposed roadmap contracts are dependencies to implement in Tau
Ceti; their open status is never a reason to postpone construction.

## F1. Relative formal calculus and Tate modules

1. Extend formal derivative, product/chain rule, residue and substitution to
   arbitrary commutative R. Construct relative `Derivation R F_R F_R`, its
   coefficient formula d(t^j)=j t^(j−1), and dt-trivialized continuous
   differentials. Prove their universal property for continuous derivations
   into complete separated linearly topologized F_R-modules with compatible
   scalar multiplication. For the oper function/cotangent constructions pin the ! tensor completion:
   V⊗^!W=lim_(U,T)(V/U)⊗(W/T), over open linear submodules U,T, with the
   inverse-limit topology; prove presentation independence and its universal
   property for bilinear maps factoring continuously through these quotient
   systems. Build its relative module/sheaf form by finite-projective charts
   and descent. Distinguish it from ordered and jointly-continuous tensor
   completions; no algebraic tensor product is silently completed.
2. Define coordinate automorphisms by φ∈tR[[t]] with unit coefficient of t.
   Construct inverse substitution recursively, the group functor Aut(O),
   composition order, and its action on F_R and differential forms. Use
   FormalLaurentPuiseux L1's σ_φ∘σ_ψ=σ_(ψ∘φ), with (ψ∘φ)(t)=ψ(φ(t)).
   Therefore coordinate pullback is a right action, (q^φ)^ψ=q^(φ∘ψ). Prove
   d(f∘φ)=(f'∘φ)φ' dt and invariance of Res(f dt). These are continuous
   R-algebra automorphisms preserving the chosen formal disc; no statement
   about arbitrary abstract automorphisms of ℂ((t)) is intended.
3. Construct the relative Tate module V((t)) for finite projective R-modules V,
   lattices V[[t]], exact sequences, continuous duals and residue duality
   V((t))^∨_cont ≃ V^∨((t))dt, with lattice annihilators and coefficient
   formulas. Use completed local modules when V varies over a scheme. Define
   discrete projective modules and their projective-dual topological
   pieces: for a discrete projective R-module Q, Q*=Hom_R(Q,R) has the topology
   of pointwise evaluation with R discrete. An elementary Tate R-module is
   P⊕Q* for discrete projective P,Q; a Tate R-module is a topological direct
   summand of one, with continuous R-linear morphisms. A lattice L is an open
   submodule such that L/U is finitely generated for every open submodule
   U⊂L. Prove lattices exist and form a neighbourhood basis. Construct completed
   coefficient base change S⊗̂_R M from S⊗_R P and (S⊗_R Q)* on elementary
   presentations and then on direct summands; prove independence of presentation,
   continuity, functoriality, identity/composition and associativity comparisons.
   Prove effective categorical descent of Tate modules and continuous morphisms
   for every faithfully flat coefficient map R→S, with this completed base
   change and its double/triple tensor-product coherences (Drinfeld, §3,
   Theorem 3.3). This includes the projective-presentation and lattice patching
   needed for effectivity, not only descent of an underlying abstract module.
   Compare with §1.1’s ordinary module descent on discrete finite-projective
   pieces; the completed descent is a construction target here.

   A continuous endomorphism T is topologically nilpotent when, for every pair
   of lattices L,L′, one has T^n L′⊂L for all sufficiently large n. Prove the
   unique continuous R[[t]]-action with t acting by T; when T is also an
   topological automorphism, extend it to R((t)). Prove that Tate R-modules with such a
   topologically nilpotent automorphism, and continuous morphisms commuting
   with it, are equivalent to finite projective R((t))-modules with their
   standard topology (Drinfeld, Lemma 3.9 and Theorem 3.10). Construct this
   standard topology by finite-projective summands of R((t))^n and prove its
   independence of the chosen presentation, its characterization as the initial
   topology for all R((t))-linear maps to R((t)), and both directions of the equivalence.
   Compare completed coefficient base change with S((t))⊗_(R((t)))M on every
   such finite-projective object, including its standard topology and morphisms.
   Prove that topological nilpotence is preserved and reflected by faithfully
   flat completed coefficient base change, as required for descent of the
   Laurent-module structure. No identity S⊗_R R((t))=S((t)) is assumed.
   Prove the lattice independence,
   base change, tensor/continuous-dual and descent rules used in F3 and F7–F8.
   Retain arbitrary, including nonreduced, coefficient algebras R; the ℂ-point
   linear topology is not a substitute for this sheaf on all R-points.

## F2. Algebraic sheaf connections and differential operators

1. For a k-algebra A and an A-module M, use CR.1.3’s ordinary
   `Crystalline.Connection`: an additive map M→M⊗_AΩ¹_A/k satisfying
   the Leibniz identity. Its base-linearity follows because d kills k;
   tensor symmetry gives this roadmap’s k-linear form M→Ω¹_A/k⊗_A M.
   Identify extension and curvature under that symmetry. Integrability
   means zero curvature for every such ordinary form connection.
   In the formal setting construct the extension with F1’s continuous
   differentials and the appropriate completed tensor; an ordinary algebraic
   Kähler-differential carrier does not itself supply this universal property.
   Construct evaluation on vector fields as an A-linear map
   `Derivation k A A → Module.End k M` obeying
   ∇_ξ(am)=a∇_ξm+ξ(a)m. Prove equivalence with the ordinary form carrier when
   Ω¹_A/k is finite projective. Without that hypothesis retain the ordinary
   form carrier and curvature-zero definition; the vector-field presentation
   alone need not recover the form or detect its curvature. Its operators
   are k-linear endomorphisms; O-linear endomorphisms cannot differentiate.
2. Construct order-filtered algebraic differential operators on modules via
   iterated commutators with multiplication, the symbol map, first-jet/Atiyah
   exact sequence and its splitting criterion for a connection. Define the
   sheaf of these operators on schemes by restriction and localization; prove
   gluing. For arbitrary commutative k, commutative k-algebras A and arbitrary
   A-modules M,N, prove the order-zero A-linearity criterion, additivity in the
   operator and multiplier, monotonicity, sums at a common order and the
   composition order bound. With I=ker(A⊗_k A→A), construct all-order principal
   parts P^m_A/k(M)=((A⊗_k A)/I^(m+1))⊗_(A,right)M with the left A-action and
   jet s↦1⊗s. Prove the natural bijection between A-linear maps P^m(M)→N
   and k-linear operators M→N of order at most m. Construct order-transition
   quotients and composition maps P^(m+n)(M)→P^m(P^n(M)), carrying jets to
   iterated jets, with unit, associativity, order-transition, localization and
   sheafified restriction laws. Individual finite-order operators extend
   uniquely to localized coefficient modules; identifying localized Hom with
   all Hom after localization additionally requires finite presentation of
   P^m(M). The order predicate and universal factorization require no
   characteristic, smoothness or projectivity hypothesis. For smooth schemes
   in characteristic zero identify gr D_X with Sym T_X through Lie–Rinehart
   PBW and the comparison with ordinary differential operators. Construct the Lie–Rinehart enveloping
   algebra by its relations [ξ,a]=ξ(a), [ξ,η]=their bracket and A-linearity.
   Its universal property and filtered PBW under projectivity are targets
   here. A vector-field connection satisfying the bracket identity is
   equivalently a module over this Lie–Rinehart enveloping algebra with the
   compatible O_X-action. Compare this with ordinary curvature-zero form
   connections under the explicit hypothesis that Ω¹_X/k is finite locally
   projective; on affine charts use F2.1’s finite-projective equivalence.
   Neither this form comparison nor the smooth characteristic-zero comparison
   with ordinary differential operators is asserted without its hypotheses.
   These are targets here, not dependencies on an unnamed D-module roadmap.
3. Consume CR.1.3’s ordinary extension, curvature, horizontal morphisms,
   tensor and base-change operations, and its Hom/dual connection when the
   first coefficient module is finite projective. Compare them under F2.1’s
   base-linearity and tensor-symmetry adapters, including direct sums and the
   unit connection, and prove Bianchi with those conventions. Ordinary
   integrability always means zero curvature. For every ordinary form
   connection zero curvature implies ∇_[ξ,η]=[∇_ξ,∇_η]; prove the converse,
   and hence the flatness criterion, when Ω¹_A/k is finite projective.
   The vector-field bracket identity is the Lie–Rinehart notion of flatness
   independently of this qualified comparison. Extend these operations and
   their qualified comparisons to vector-bundle sheaves by localization and
   gluing using `Scheme.PresheafOfModules` and its sheaf/finite-local-freeness
   conditions. Construct the continuous/formal versions and their restriction,
   completed-tensor, continuous Hom/dual and relative base-change comparisons
   using F1, rather than inferring them from ordinary algebraic differentials.
   Construct principal G-connections on fpqc torsors via local gauge forms
   and gluing, and prove independence of trivialization and the associated
   vector-bundle dictionary for a faithful algebraic representation.

   so its dimension is this cotangent dimension. Prove the finite-type field
