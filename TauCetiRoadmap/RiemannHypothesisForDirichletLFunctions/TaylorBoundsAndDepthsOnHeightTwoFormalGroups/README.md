# Roadmap: Taylor bounds and depths on height-two formal groups

**Summary.** This roadmap is local theory at a prime `p`: bounds for the Taylor coefficients of power series built on formal groups of height two, and bounds for the depth of a weight at the points of a unitary PEL moduli problem. The first strand works with the Gauss norms of multivariate power series over a complete non-archimedean field of characteristic `0` at chosen radii; with *letters*, logarithmic coordinates indexed by `ℤ/n′` on a product of Lubin–Tate formal groups of height two; with Hermitian biextension cocycles `β` written in letters, their weights and, for the Lubin–Tate law of height two, their classification; and with a linear programme, the *flow programme*, that prices the monomials of `β` which a derivative can absorb. The second strand defines the *depth* of a weight at a framed point of a unitary PEL moduli problem, the least valuation of a jet, against the harmonic polynomials of that weight, of an integral series times the exponential of a multiple of the polarisation series of an integral chart, and bounds it through flags of characters of the formal group and, along Frobenius orbits, through an abstract *row datum* whose block depth is bounded by the flow programme.

Write `γ_LT(p) = p/(p² − 1)` and `ρ_LT(p) = 1/(p² − 1)`. A function bounded on the Lubin–Tate formal group of height two has its `n`-th derivative at the origin divisible by `p^{γ_LT(p)n − O(log n)}`. The headline theorems are these.

(1) *Taylor bounds at weighted radii.* Let `p` be odd, `𝒦` a complete non-archimedean field of characteristic `0` with `|p| = p^{−1}`, `β` a series in the letters that satisfies the cocycle conditions below (coefficients in a discretely valued subfield, invariance under a torus of roots of unity, positive degree in the even and in the odd letters, and an integral exponential of its coboundary in logarithmic coordinates), `U` a series integral in the formal parameters, `g` the compositional inverse of the letter logarithms, `M` an integral matrix compatible with the letters, and radii `σ_i ≥ 0` and an order `κ` such that every bi-power monomial `y_i^{p^α}y_j^{p^{α′}}` of degree at most `κ` whose coefficient in `β_M` has valuation below `1/(p − 1) − ρ_LT(p^α + p^{α′})` satisfies `p^ασ_i + p^{α′}σ_j ≥ 1`. Then for every integer `a ≥ 1` bounding the entries of `κ`, `v_p(κ!·coeff_κ[U(g(My))·exp(±β_M(y))]) ≥ Σ_iκ_i(γ_LT − σ_i) − #ι·(log_p a + 1)`. For a single self-dual formal group the radii `1/(p + 1)` are admissible and the rate is `ρ_LT`, the true order for a CM elliptic curve at an inert prime.

(2) *The flow programme.* For `n′` even, a prime `p ≥ 3` and `D ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^{ℤ/n′}`, let `𝔉_p(D)` be `γ_LT·Σ_sD_s` minus the largest total `Σ_{s,i}x_{s,i}` of a flow `x ≥ 0`, indexed by the letters `s` and the odd `i < n′`, with `Σ_ip^ix_{s,i} + Σ_ix_{s−i,i} ≤ D_s` for every `s`. Then `𝔉_p(D)` is the maximum of `Σ_sD_s(γ_LT − σ_s)` over the vectors `σ ∈ [0, 1]^{ℤ/n′}` with `p^iσ_s + σ_{s+i} ≥ 1` for all `s` and odd `i < n′`; it lies between `ρ_LT·Σ_sD_s` and `γ_LT·Σ_sD_s`; and it equals `ρ_LT·Σ_sD_s` when `max D ≤ p·min D`.

(3) *The depth at the points of a unitary moduli problem.* Let `M` be a PEL moduli problem of unitary type whose polarisation `λ` is principal, for a CM field `E`, of signature `(p, q)` with `q < p` at the embeddings of a CM type `Φ`, and `y` an algebraic integral framed point of `M` with an integral chart above a prime `ℓ ≥ 5` unramified in `E` and prime to the level. Suppose that every orbit of the group generated by `Frob_ℓ` and complex conjugation on the embeddings of `E` is the union of a `Frob_ℓ`-orbit alternating between `Φ` and its conjugate and of the distinct conjugate orbit. Then for every dominant weight, every integer `n ≤ p` with `p + q − n ≥ 1` and `n ≥ p + q − n + 2`, and numbers `w(τ) > 0`, `T`, `θ₀`, `S_E`, `C₁` subject to the explicit conditions of the optimisation step and to `T·R_w^{tot} ≤ C₁·[E⁺ : ℚ]·(n + 1)·S_E`, the depth `ν` of the weight at `y`, with multiplier the level, on enough copies satisfies `T·ν ≤ Σ_{orbits}𝔉_ℓ(n·w) + C·err + T·R_w^{tot}/(ℓ − 1)`, with an explicit error term `err` and the remaining degree `R_w^{tot}` of the weight.

The roadmap rests on [HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms](../HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms/README.md), whose inverses of twisted systems of logarithms with their sharp radius, Dwork's lemma and quasi-logarithms in several variables and letter group carry the first strand, and whose colength bound for the Hodge–Tate image at an unramified place gives the characters of the second; on LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts for the Lubin–Tate logarithms of height two; on [PELModuli](../../PELModuli/README.md), [ShimuraCompactifications](../../ShimuraCompactifications/README.md) and [AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli](../../AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli/README.md) for unitary PEL moduli problems, semi-abelian schemes and the Mumford biextension; and on [IntegralWeylModulesOfGeneralLinearGroups](../IntegralWeylModulesOfGeneralLinearGroups/README.md), whose standard bideterminant basis with saturation turns jet bounds into bounds against products of minors. [LocalGainOfThetaChains](../LocalGainOfThetaChains/README.md) applies the roadmap to the steps of a chain of theta lifts.

There is one layer, numbered `4` as in the family's core. It sets up Gauss norms at radii and an integrality gap at degrees divisible by `p + 1`; weighs and classifies the biextension cocycles and writes the cocycle of a CM elliptic curve in letters; proves the Taylor bounds, on letters and on folded letters; studies the flow programme; bounds jets against products of minors, by weighted radii and by counting pairs, and jets of a Gaussian. It then defines framed points, integral charts and the depth of a weight, bounds the colength of the differentials of characters at an unramified prime and the depth by flags of characters, defines row data and bounds their block depth by the flow programme, attaches a row datum to an integral chart, and ends with the depth bounds at the points of a unitary moduli problem, orbit type by orbit type.

## Conventions

- `p` is a prime. From 4.19 on the prime is written `ℓ`; in 4.19f the pair `(p, q)` is a signature.
- `v_p` is the valuation with `v_p(p) = 1` on every complete extension of `ℚ_p`, and `|·|` the absolute value with `|p| = p^{−1}`; `lg_p(y) := log_p max(y, 1) + 1` (a real number: no integer part is taken). `C`, `C_A` denote absolute constants.
- Frobenius elements are arithmetic: `Frob_p` acts as `x ↦ x^p` on residue fields.
- Notation for Lubin–Tate laws, their logarithms `ℓ_𝔰` and the relation `≃` not restated here is that of LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts (its Conventions and §§4.1–4.3) and of HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms (Layer 3).

## Layer 4: Taylor bounds on height-two formal groups and depths of weights

The layer is local theory on formal groups: formal groups of height two, and polarised formal groups with an unramified action at the points of a unitary PEL moduli problem. It uses no target of the earlier core roadmaps of the family (FrobeniusRestrictedRieszSums, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups).

*Formal groups of height two.* A function that is bounded on the Lubin–Tate formal group of height two (logarithm
`Σ_e T^{p^{2e}}/p^e`) has its `n`-th derivative at the origin divisible by `p^{γ_LT(p)n − O(log n)}`, with
`γ_LT(p) = p/(p² − 1) = 1/p + 1/p³ + ⋯`; a deformation of height two over a ramified base need not have this rate. In the logarithmic coordinates of
two dual isotropic formal groups, a series of the form (bounded series)`·exp(−β)`, `β` a Hermitian biextension cocycle, gains
at the rate `γ_LT(p)` except for exactly one power of `p` for each bi-power monomial of `β` that a derivative can absorb.
Two local engines bound that price: weights at radii chosen by a linear programme, the *flow programme* (4.3, 4.5–4.7), and
the classification of `β` with a count of pairs of boxes (4.4, 4.4a, 4.8); 4.8a bounds the jets of a Gaussian. On the row
side, the *depth* of a weight at a framed point of a unitary PEL moduli problem (4.8b: a point with a semi-abelian model, framed
embedding by embedding; its integral charts, 4.8b′) is at most what flags of characters
of the formal group allow (4.18), given the unramified colength bound for the differentials of the characters (4.8c); along
Frobenius orbits it is at most the flow programme of 4.6. That bound is proved for an abstract *row datum* (a module of
typical curves with a pairing, polarisation series and two rank conditions: 4.19–4.19d); the chart of a point carries a row
datum (4.19e), and 4.19f gives the bounds at the points of a principally polarised unitary moduli problem of any signature, orbit
type by orbit type.

### 4.1 The Gauss norm at radii

*Origin:* adapted from Bosch, Güntzer and Remmert (1984): the Gauss norm of restricted power series, its
submultiplicativity, the change of radii and contractive substitution (their section 5.1; RestrictedPowerSeries §§1.1,
1.2, 2.2), here as weights at radii for series whose weighted coefficients are only bounded, also up to total degree `K`
and up to `κ` ((a), (b)); with the exponential and the logarithm at weight `1/(p−1)` ((c), (d)), Legendre's bound for
`v_p(κ!)` ((e)) and the constants `γ_LT`, `ρ_LT` ((f)). The two truncated forms of the substitution in (a) and the form
of (d) up to `κ` are this roadmap's own formulations; the forms of (b) in Mathlib's Gauss norm restate its weight forms,
and the sharper form of (e) is Legendre's formula.

*Method of the formal proof:* [FormalProof] states these facts for its own notion of weight, a bound for every
coefficient, or for the coefficients `n ≤ κ`, over an ultrametric normed field, not for Mathlib's Gauss norm, and proves
them coefficient by coefficient, with the ultrametric inequality and Legendre's formula: the substitution of (a) without
truncation; in (b) the persistence at larger radii and the additivity of weights under products, both without
truncation and up to `κ`; (c) up to `κ`; (d) without truncation; (e) in the form stated first, and with `log_p a` for
an integer `a ≥ 1` bounding the `κ_i`; and of (f) the two inequalities `n/(p−1) − v_p(n) ≥ 1/(p−1)` and
`(p^{2e} − 1)·ρ_LT ≥ e`, with `γ_LT − 1/(p+1) = ρ_LT`.

Let `𝒦` be a field of characteristic `0`, complete for a non-archimedean absolute value with
residue characteristic `p`, normalised by `|p| = p^{−1}`, and `ι` a finite set. For radii `r ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^ι` and `w ∈ ℝ`, a series
`f ∈ 𝒦[[x_i : i ∈ ι]]` *has weight `≥ w` at radii `r`* if `|coeff_n f|·∏_i p^{−n_ir_i} ≤ p^{−w}` for every `n ∈ ℕ^ι`, that is
`v_p(coeff_n f) ≥ w − Σ_i n_ir_i`; "up to `κ ∈ ℕ^ι`" asks this only for the `n ≤ κ`. Write
`‖f‖_r := MvPowerSeries.gaussNorm |·| (p^{−r_i})_i f` (Mathlib's Gauss norm, the real supremum of the
`|coeff_n f|·∏_i p^{−n_ir_i}`): `f` has weight `≥ w` at radii `r` exactly when this family is bounded above
(`MvPowerSeries.HasGaussNorm`) and `‖f‖_r ≤ p^{−w}`. The bound `‖f‖_r ≤ p^{−w}` alone does not give a weight, because a real
supremum of an unbounded family is `0` (Check). Put
`γ_LT(p) := p/(p² − 1) = Σ_{i ≥ 0} p^{−2i−1}` and `ρ_LT(p) := 1/(p² − 1)`. Prove:
(a) *Substitution:* if `a_i` (`i ∈ ι`) have zero constant term and weight `≥ r_i` at radii `r′`, and `f` has weight `≥ w` at radii
`r`, then `f(a)` has weight `≥ w` at radii `r′`; the same holds with the weights of `f` and `f(a)` read up to total degree `K` (the
coefficients `n` with `|n| ≤ K`), and with the weights of `f` and `f(a)` read up to `κ` when each `a_i` lies in
`Y_i𝒦[[Y_i]]` (one new variable `Y_i` for each `i`; then a monomial `≤ κ` of `f(a)` comes only from monomials `≤ κ` of `f`). For
substitutions mixing the variables the truncation up to `κ` does not pass (Check).
(b) A weight `≥ w` at radii `r` persists at all radii `r′ ≥ r`, and if `f` has weight `≥ w` and `g` weight `≥ w′` at radii `r`, then
`fg` has weight `≥ w + w′` there; both also up to `κ`. In Gauss-norm form, for `f`, `g` that have a Gauss norm at radii `r`: `f`
has one at every `r′ ≥ r`, with `‖f‖_{r′} ≤ ‖f‖_r`, and `‖fg‖_r ≤ ‖f‖_r‖g‖_r` (Mathlib `MvPowerSeries.gaussNorm_mul_le`).
(c) If `B(0) = 0` and `B` has weight `≥ 1/(p−1)` at radii `r`, then `exp(B)` has weight `≥ 0` there; also up to `κ` (the
coefficients of `exp(B)` up to `κ` depend only on those of `B` up to `κ`).
(d) If `D(0) = 0` and `exp(D) − 1` has weight `≥ 1/(p−1)` at radii `r`, then so has `D`; also up to `κ`.
(e) *The form of the formal proof.* `v_p(κ!) ≥ |κ|/(p−1) − #ι·lg_p|κ|`, `κ! := ∏_i κ_i!`. This roadmap's own sharper form, classical
(Legendre): the same with `⌊log_p max(|κ|, 1)⌋ + 1` in place of `lg_p|κ|`.
(f) `γ_LT + ρ_LT = 1/(p−1)`; `n/(p−1) − v_p(n) ≥ 1/(p−1)` for `n ≥ 1`; `(p^{2e} − 1)·ρ_LT ≥ e` for `e ≥ 0`.

*Route.* (a), (b): the coefficient formulas and the ultrametric inequality, coefficient by coefficient (which is what
makes the truncated forms hold); the Gauss-norm form of (b) from the weight form and the bound of each term by the supremum
(`MvPowerSeries.le_gaussNorm`). (c): `v_p(B^k/k!) ≥ k/(p−1) − v_p(k!) − Σ n_i r_i` and `v_p(k!) ≤ (k−1)/(p−1)`
(IntegralIwasawaTheory §4.1a, the factorial bound). (d): `log(1 + y) = Σ_k (−1)^{k+1}y^k/k` and (f). (e): the sharper form is
PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology §RD.6.35 (`v_p(n!) ≥ n/(p−1) − ⌈log_p(n + 1)⌉` for `n ≥ 1`, Legendre's formula
with the number of digits), where `⌈log_p(n + 1)⌉ = ⌊log_p n⌋ + 1`, summed over the coordinates with `κ_i ≥ 1` (`κ_i ≤ |κ|`; a
coordinate `κ_i = 0` contributes `0`); `⌊x⌋ ≤ x` gives the form with `lg_p`. (f): geometric series.

*Checks.* (Agreement) A weight is not a bound on Mathlib's Gauss norm alone. At `r = 0` the terms of `exp(T)`
have `|1/n!| = p^{v_p(n!)}`, unbounded, so `‖exp T‖_0 = 0` although `exp(T)` has no weight at all at radius `0`. Reading
"weight `≥ w`" as `‖f‖_r ≤ p^{−w}` would make (d) false: `D = T/p` has `‖exp(D) − 1‖_0 = 0` (its terms have absolute value
`p^{n + v_p(n!)}`) but `‖D‖_0 = p`; with weights, `exp(D) − 1` has no weight at radius `0` and (d) does not apply.
`exp(T)` has weight `≥ 0` at radius `1/(p−1)` but at no smaller radius: at `n = p^k`, `v_p(1/n!) = −(p^k − 1)/(p−1)`. The Lubin–Tate logarithm `Σ_e T^{p^{2e}}/p^e` has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at radius
`ρ_LT` by (f), with equality exactly at the monomials `T` and `T^{p²}`. (Truncation) For `p` odd, `κ = (1, 1)`, radii `0` and
`f = p^{−1}X_1²`, every coefficient of `f` up to `κ` is `0`, but the substitution `X_1 = Y_1 + Y_2`, `X_2 = 0` gives the coefficient
`2/p` at `Y_1Y_2`: the truncated form of (a) needs the hypothesis on the `a_i`. (Monotonicity) `‖T‖_0 = 1 > ‖T‖_1 = p^{−1}`.
(Restricted series) On restricted series (`|coeff_n f|·∏_ip^{−n_ir_i} → 0`) the untruncated (a) is a case of RestrictedPowerSeries
§2.2 (contractive substitution, constant terms allowed), and the Gauss-norm form of (b) is RestrictedPowerSeries §§1.2, 1.1 (change
of radii, submultiplicativity); (a) and (b) extend them to bounded families of coefficients and to the truncations up to `K` and `κ`.

*Sources:* (Legendre). *Needs:* Mathlib `MvPowerSeries.gaussNorm`, `MvPowerSeries.HasGaussNorm`, `MvPowerSeries.le_gaussNorm`,
`MvPowerSeries.gaussNorm_mul_le` (coefficients and substitution); IntegralIwasawaTheory §4.1a;
PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology §RD.6.35.

*In [FormalProof]* (namespace Formalism.DworkPowerSeriesWeights; the weights are WtGe, and WtGeLe up to `κ`): (a) without truncation is SubstWt, proved as substitution_weight_bound; (b) is RadMono, proved as radical_weight_monotone, with wtGe_mul and MulWtLe, proved as multiplicative_weight_le; (c) up to `κ` is ExpWtLe, proved as exponential_weight_le; (d) without truncation is LogStep, proved as logarithm_weight_induction; (e) is FactVal, proved as factorial_valuation_bound, with factValA for the form with `log_p a`; the two inequalities of (f) are Formalism.IncomingBounds.ValGain, proved as Formalism.IncomingBounds.padicValuationGain.

### 4.2 A valuation gap at degrees divisible by `p + 1`

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the corollary, an elementary integrality gap for the coefficients of monomials whose
total torus weight is `≡ 0 (mod p^{n′} − 1)`, and whose total degree is therefore divisible by `p + 1`, used in 4.3 to raise
the weight of a cocycle; the first statement, for every `n` with `p + 1` dividing `|n|`, is this roadmap's own
formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* for the corollary: the torus weights `w_s` are read modulo `p + 1`, where each is `1`, so
the total degree is `(p + 1)m`; the hypothesis then says that the integer `(p − 1)·v_p(c) + m` is positive, hence at
least `1`, which is the conclusion.

Let `𝒦` be as in 4.1, `n ∈ ℕ^ι` with `p + 1` dividing `|n|`, and `c ∈ 𝒦` with
`v_p(c) ∈ ℤ ∪ {∞}`. If `v_p(c) > −ρ_LT|n|`, then `v_p(c) ≥ 1/(p−1) − ρ_LT|n|`. *Corollary.* Let `n′ ≥ 2` be even and
give a letter `s ∈ ℤ/n′` the torus weight `w_s := (−1)^s p^s`; a monomial in variables carrying letters whose total
torus weight is `≡ 0 (mod p^{n′} − 1)` has total degree divisible by `p + 1`, so the first statement applies to its
coefficient when that coefficient has integer valuation. The first statement is this roadmap's own, more general form (route
below) and not a step of [FormalProof], which states a case of the corollary.

*Route.* Write `|n| = (p+1)m`, so `ρ_LT|n| = m/(p−1)`; the hypothesis says that the integer `(p−1)v_p(c) + m` is
positive, hence `≥ 1`. For the corollary, `(p+1) | (p^{n′} − 1)` because `n′` is even, and `w_s ≡ 1 (mod p+1)`.

*Check.* `T^{p+1}/p` has `v_p = −1 ≤ −ρ_LT(p+1) = −1/(p−1)`: the hypothesis fails and so does the conclusion
(`v_p ≥ 0`). This is the exceptional monomial of 4.3; in one letter it is the term `z^{p+1}/p` of the logarithm of
the theta function of a CM elliptic curve at an inert prime (4.5, Check).

*Needs:* none.

*In [FormalProof]:* Quant (`Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GenLogInverse.lean`), proved as weightQuantization (`Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/OneLetterRadiusIdentity.lean`), is the corollary for coefficients in a subfield with integer valuations, under the hypothesis `v_p(c) ≥ ρ_LT − ρ_LT|n|`, stronger than `v_p(c) > −ρ_LT|n|`.

### 4.3 The weight of a biextension cocycle in letters

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a lower bound `1/(p−1) − ρ_LT|n|` for the valuations of the coefficients of a
biextension cocycle written in the letters, with a loss of at most `1` at the bi-power monomials only.

*Method of the formal proof:* the compositional inverse `g` of the letter logarithms has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at radii `ρ_LT`, by
induction on the degree in `λ(g) = id`, so `exp(Δβ) − 1` has weight `≥ ρ_LT`; torus invariance and integer valuations
raise this to `1/(p−1)`, a logarithm step passes it to `Δβ`, and the coefficients of `β` are recovered through binomial
coefficients (Lucas' congruence, and `p² ∤ C(p^α, p^{α−1})`), with a loss of `1` exactly at the bi-power monomials.

Let `p` be an odd prime, `𝒦` as in 4.1, `𝒦₀ ⊂ 𝒦` a subfield on which `v_p` takes
integer values and which contains a primitive `(p^{n′} − 1)`-th root of unity `ζ`, `n′ ≥ 2` even. The *letter
logarithms* are `λ_s(T) := Σ_{e ≥ 0} T_{s−2e}^{p^{2e}}/p^e`, `s ∈ ℤ/n′` (series in formal variables `T_s`). Let
`β ∈ 𝒦₀[[x_s : s ∈ ℤ/n′]]` satisfy:
(B1) its coefficients lie in `𝒦₀`; (B2) `β(ζ^{w_s}x_s) = β(x)`, `w_s = (−1)^s p^s`; (B3) every monomial of `β` has
positive degree in the even letters and in the odd letters, and bidegree `≠ (1, 1)`; (B4) for
`Δ_evβ := β(x_ev + y_ev, x_odd) − β(x_ev, x_odd) − β(y_ev, x_odd)` and the analogous `Δ_oddβ`, the series
`exp(Δβ)(λ(T))` has integral coefficients and constant term `1`. Then `v_p(coeff_n β) ≥ 1/(p−1) − 1 − ρ_LT|n|` for
every `n`, and `v_p(coeff_n β) ≥ 1/(p−1) − ρ_LT|n|` unless `n` is a *bi-power monomial*:
`n = p^α e_s + p^{α′} e_{s′}` with `s` even, `s′` odd, `(α, α′) ≠ (0, 0)` and `s + α ≡ s′ + α′ (mod n′)`.

*Route.* (1) Torus invariance and coefficients in `𝒦₀` pass to `exp(Δβ)` (closure under sums, products,
substitution and `exp`). (2) `E := exp(Δβ)∘λ` is integral with `E(0) = 1`, so `E − 1` has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at radii
`ρ_LT`; the compositional inverse `g` of the letter logarithms has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at radii `ρ_LT`
(HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.1, the sharp radius for twisted systems, with `q = p²` and the
self-map `s ↦ s − 2` of `ℤ/n′`), so `exp(Δβ) − 1 = (E − 1)∘g` has weight `≥ ρ_LT` by 4.1(a). (3) Every monomial of
`exp(Δβ)` is torus invariant, so 4.2 raises the bound to `1/(p−1)`, and 4.1(d) passes it to `Δβ`. (4) The coefficient
of `Δ_evβ` at the monomial obtained from `n` by moving `j` of the `n_s` copies of an even letter `s` to the second set
of variables is `C(n_s, j)·coeff_n β`; unless the even part of `n` is one letter raised to a power of `p`, some
splitting has a binomial coefficient prime to `p` (Lucas), and `v_p C(p^α, p^{α−1}) = 1` (Kummer) gives the loss of
exactly `1`; likewise for odd letters. (B3) excludes `α = α′ = 0`, and torus invariance gives `s + α ≡ s′ + α′`.

*Check.* `n′ = 2`, `p = 3`: the torus-invariant monomials `x₀^a x₁^b` are those with `a ≡ 3b (mod 8)`. In degree `4`

`1/(p−1) − 1 − ρ_LT(p^i + 1) = −E(p, i)`, `E(p, i) := 1 + (p^i − p)/(p² − 1)`. For `i = 1` it is attained by the coefficient `𝔠/p` of 4.4
with `𝔠` a unit (valuation `−1 = −E(p, 1)`); for `i = 3` the value is `−(1 + p)`, which that coefficient does not realise; for every odd
`i` it is the valuation of the main coefficient of the polarisation series of a row datum (4.19a).

### 4.4 Classification of the biextension cocycles of the height-two Lubin–Tate law

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the normal form `G_𝔠 = 𝔠·A_𝔰(x, y) − 𝔰σ(𝔠)·A_𝔰(y, x)` of the biextension cocycles of
the Lubin–Tate laws `F_𝔰` of height two, with `𝔠` unique and `σ²𝔠 = 𝔠`, up to series with integral exponential (i), with
the class of `exp(−Φ)` after rescaling (ii) and its form in letters (iii); nearest published result: Mumford (1969), the
classification of biextensions of formal groups. For the sign `𝔰 = −1` these statements are this roadmap's own
formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* for the sign `𝔰 = +1`, a power-series computation: a cocycle is averaged over the roots of
unity of order `p² − 1`, a quasi-logarithm lemma and the operator `H ↦ p⁻¹(H^σ(x^p, y^p) − p·H)` bring it to the normal
form, a comparison of coefficients forces `σ²𝔠 = 𝔠`, and Dwork's lemma in several variables gives the equivalence; the
class of `exp(−Φ)` is read off from the valuations of the rescaled coefficients, and the cocycle is produced explicitly.
This departs from Mumford's classification (1969), which goes through Cartier and Dieudonné duality.

Let `p` be an odd prime, `𝒦₀` complete, discretely valued with `v_p(𝒦₀^×) = ℤ`, with a
ring endomorphism `σ` such that `v_p(σa − a^p) ≥ 1` on its valuation ring, containing a primitive `(p² − 1)`-th root of
unity; let `𝔰 ∈ {1, −1}` and let `F_𝔰` be the formal group law with logarithm `ℓ_𝔰(T) = Σ_{j ≥ 0} T^{p^{2j}}/(𝔰p)^j`
(the Lubin–Tate module of `𝔰p`, LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3). For series `f, g` without constant
term write `f ≃ g` if `exp(f − g)` has integral coefficients. Put
`A_𝔰(x, y) := Σ_{0 ≤ i ≤ j} x^{p^{2i}}y^{p^{2j+1}}/((𝔰p)^{i+j}p)` and `G_𝔠 := 𝔠·A_𝔰(x, y) − 𝔰σ(𝔠)·A_𝔰(y, x)`. All series of
(i) and (ii) are in the formal parameters `x, y` of `F_𝔰` (resp. `X_e, Y_e`), and "integral" means integral in the
coordinates named.
(i) *Classification.* If `β ∈ xy𝒦₀[[x, y]]` satisfies `β(F_𝔰(x₀, x₁), y) ≃ β(x₀, y) + β(x₁, y)`, the same in `y`, and
`β(ζx, y) ≃ β(x, ζ^p y)` for `ζ^{p²−1} = 1`, then there is a unique `𝔠` with `v_p(𝔠) ≥ 0`, `σ²𝔠 = 𝔠` and `β ≃ G_𝔠`;
conversely every such `G_𝔠` satisfies these hypotheses.
(ii) *The Dwork-type class.* Let `π_p^{p²−1} = p`, `U₀` integral in the formal parameters, and `Φ ≃ Σ_e β(X_e, Y_e)`
(finitely many pairs of formal parameters). In the rescaled formal parameters `X_e = π_p𝓍_e`, `Y_e = π_p𝓎_e`,
`exp(−Φ)·U₀ = Σ_{i ≥ 0} p^{−i}𝔥^i g_i` with all `g_i` integral in `(𝓍, 𝓎)` and
`𝔥 ≡ Σ_e (𝔠𝓍_e𝓎_e^p − 𝔰σ(𝔠)𝓍_e^p𝓎_e) (mod p)`.
(iii) *Letters.* Suppose `𝒦₀ ⊃ W(𝔽_{p^{n′}})`, `n′` even, `σ` the Frobenius, and let `ω_r, ω*_r` (`r < n′/2`) be bases of
`W(𝔽_{p^{n′}})` over `W(𝔽_{p²})` dual for the trace. Define *logarithmic letters* `z_{s,·}`, `s ∈ ℤ/n′`, by
`ℓ_𝔰(X_{r,·}) = Σ_{s even}σ^s(ω*_r)z_{s,·}` and `ℓ_𝔰(Y_{r,·}) = Σ_{s odd}σ^s(ω_r)z_{s,·}` (the dot an auxiliary index
set), their rescalings `𝒵_{s,·} := π_p^{−1}z_{s,·}`, and the *rescaled letter parameters*
`𝒮_{s,·} := Σ_{l ≥ 0} (−𝔰)^l 𝒵_{s−2l,·}^{p^{2l}}`. A series is integral in the `𝒵` exactly when it is integral in the
`𝒮` (the change of coordinates and its inverse are integral, HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.4 (e)), and in these
coordinates the class of (ii) has `𝔥 ≡ Σ_{s ∈ ℤ/n′} υ_s Σ_· 𝒮_{s,·}^p·𝒮_{s+1,·} (mod p)` with `υ_s = −𝔰σ(𝔠)` for `s` even
and `υ_s = 𝔠` for `s` odd. For several places with disjoint variables the classes multiply: the product of series of the
classes of `𝔥_v` is a sum `Σ ∏_v p^{−i_v}·∏_v 𝔥_v^{i_v}·g_{(i_v)}` over the families `(i_v)` of natural numbers, with all `g`
integral.
`𝔰 = 1` (logarithm `Σ_jT^{p^{2j}}/p^j`) is the case of [FormalProof]; for `𝔰 = −1` the statements (i)–(iii), read with the same
formulas, are this roadmap's own and are reduced to `𝔰 = 1` in the route.

*Route.* (i) Average `β(ζx, ζ^{−p}y)` over `ζ ∈ μ_{p²−1}` to get `G ≃ β` supported on monomials `x^ay^b` with
`a ≡ pb (mod p² − 1)`. Differentiating the additivity in the first variable gives `∂_xG ≡ ℓ_𝔰′(x)·f(y)` modulo
integral series with `δ_F f` integral (expand `exp` of the second difference at `x₁ = 0`); the quasi-logarithm lemma
(HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.4 (b): if `f(0) = 0` and `δ_F f` is integral then
`f = 𝔞·g₀ + 𝔟·ℓ_𝔰 + (integral)` with `v_p(𝔞) ≥ 0`, `𝔟` arbitrary, `g₀ := ℓ_𝔰(T^p)/p`) and the support condition (exponents
`≡ p`, while `ℓ_𝔰` lives on exponents `≡ 1`) give `∂_xG − 𝔠ℓ_𝔰′(x)·g₀(y)` integral. With `𝔇H := p^{−1}(H^σ(x^p, y^p) − pH)`
one shows that `𝔇G − 𝔰σ(𝔠)g₀(x)ℓ_𝔰(y) − σ(𝔠′)ℓ_𝔰(x)g₀(y)` is integral (`𝔠′` the constant of the transposed cocycle; the
tool is HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.4 (d): if `∂_xΦ` is integral then `Φ(φ + pY) − Φ(φ) ∈ p·(integral)`, applied to
`F(x₀, x₁)^p = F(x₀^p, x₁^p) + pY`, together with the two-variable form HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.4 (c)); comparing the coefficients of
`x·y^{p^{2i+1}}` relates `𝔠′` to `σ(𝔠)` and gives `σ²𝔠 = 𝔠`. The identity
`A_𝔰(x^p, y^p) = 𝔰p(g₀(x)ℓ_𝔰(y) − A_𝔰(y, x))` gives `𝔇G_𝔠 = 𝔰σ(𝔠)g₀(x)ℓ_𝔰(y) − 𝔠ℓ_𝔰(x)g₀(y)`, so `𝔇(G − G_𝔠)` is
integral and Dwork's lemma in several variables (HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.3: `exp Ψ` integral iff `𝔇Ψ` integral) gives `G ≃ G_𝔠`;
uniqueness by the same lemma and the coefficients of the monomials `x·y^{p^{2j+1}}`, `j ≥ 0` (Check). For `𝔰 = −1` either run these steps with `ℓ_{−1}` (the
identity for `A_𝔰` and the relations between the constants hold for both signs), or reduce to `𝔰 = 1` over
`W(𝔽_{p⁴})` by `Y ↦ 𝔲Y` with `𝔲^{p²−1} = −1`, using `ℓ_{−1}(𝔲Y) = 𝔲ℓ_{+1}(Y)`. (ii) After rescaling, the coefficient of
`x^{p^{2i}}y^{p^{2j+1}}` has valuation `(p^{2i} + p^{2j+1})/(p² − 1) − (i + j + 1)`, which is `1/(p−1) − 1` for
`i = j = 0` and `≥ 1/(p−1)` otherwise; a series all of whose coefficients have valuation `≥ 1/(p−1)` has integral
exponential (4.1(c)); and `exp(−(π_p^{p+1}/p)𝔥) = Σ_i (−1)^i(π_p^{(p+1)i}/i!)p^{−i}𝔥^i` with
`v_p(π_p^{(p+1)i}/i!) ≥ 0`. (iii) `ℓ_𝔰(π_p t)/π_p ≡ t + 𝔰t^{p²} (mod p)` with inverse `≡ Σ_l (−𝔰)^l t^{p^{2l}}`
(HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.4 (e)); `σ^s(ω)^p ≡ σ^{s+1}(ω)` and trace duality `Σ_r σ^s(ω*_r)σ^{s′}(ω_r) = δ_{ss′}` for `s ≡ s′ (mod 2)` collapse
the double sums. The class is stable under multiplication by integral series, integral substitution and
`𝔥 ↦ 𝔥 + p(·)`.

*Checks.* The coefficient of `xy^p` in `G_𝔠` is `𝔠/p`; for `𝔠` a unit it has valuation `−1 = 1/(p−1) − 1 − ρ_LT(p+1)`,
so after passing to logarithmic coordinates (`n′ = 2`) the bound of 4.3 is attained at the bi-power
monomial `e₀ + pe₁`. `G_𝔠 ≃ 0` only for `𝔠 = 0`: the coefficient of `x·y^{p^{2j+1}}` in `exp(G_𝔠)` is that of `G_𝔠` (the
higher powers have `x`-degree `≥ 2`), of valuation `v_p(𝔠) − j − 1`, and all of them are integral only for `𝔠 = 0`; the single
coefficient of `xy^p` gives only `p | 𝔠`.

### 4.4a The cocycle of the CM curve in letters

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a cocycle `β₀` of the law of `ℓ_{+1}` with (F1), (F2), such as that of a CM elliptic
curve, rewritten in the logarithmic letters through trace-dual bases and averaged over the torus, so that it satisfies
(B1)–(B4) of 4.3 and the Taylor bounds of 4.5 apply to it.

*Method of the formal proof:* a combination of letter logarithms with integral coefficients twisted by Frobenius satisfies
a functional equation of Hazewinkel type, hence is the Lubin–Tate logarithm of an integral series, so the cocycle in
logarithmic letters is biadditive and Hermitian up to integral exponentials by (F1) and (F2); the torus acts by rescaling
the trace-dual families, which the Hermitian rule absorbs after expanding in the dual family, and the mean divides a sum of
such relations by the integer `p^{n′} − 1`, prime to `p`.

Let `p ≥ 5`, `𝒦₀ ⊃ W(𝔽_{p^{n′}})` as in
4.4(iii), `F` the law of `ℓ := ℓ_{+1}` (4.4, the sign `𝔰 = +1`, the sign of the letter logarithms of 4.3), and `β₀ ∈ xy𝒦₀[[x, y]]` with
(F1) `β₀(F(x₀, x₁), y) ≃ β₀(x₀, y) + β₀(x₁, y)` and the
same in `y`, and (F2) `β₀([α]x, y) ≃ β₀(x, [σα]y)` for every integral `α` with `σ²α = α` (`[α] = ℓ^{−1}(α·ℓ)`). With
trace-dual bases `ω, ω*` as in 4.4(iii) put
`β̃ := Σ_r β₀(ℓ^{−1}(Σ_{s even}σ^s(ω*_r)z_s), ℓ^{−1}(Σ_{s odd}σ^s(ω_r)z_s))` in the logarithmic letters `z_s`, and let
`β` be its mean over the torus `z_s ↦ ζ^{w_s}z_s` (`ζ^{p^{n′}−1} = 1`, `w_s` of 4.2). Then: (a) `β` satisfies (B1)–(B4) of
4.3; (b) `β∘λ ≃ β̃∘λ`, and in the letter coordinates of the letter group (HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.5)
`β ≃ Σ_rβ₀(X_r, Y_r)`; (c) if, in hyperbolic formal parameters `(X_e, Y_e)`, `Θ = exp(−Φ)·U₀` with `U₀` integral and
`Φ ≃ Σ_e β₀(X_e, Y_e)`, then in letters `Θ = U(g(z))·exp(−Σ_{a,b}β(z_{·,a,b}))` with `U` integral, for every compositional
inverse `g` of the letter logarithms.

*Route.* `β₀∘ℓ^{−1}` is biadditive up to `≃` on series of functional-equation type and `σ`-Hermitian for the scalars
(HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §§3.2, 3.4, with (F1)–(F2)); the torus acts on `β̃` through
`(ω, ω*) ↦ (u^{−1}ω, uω*)`, which `β̃` absorbs up to `≃`, so `β ≃ β̃`; (B1)–(B4) follow from (F1)–(F2) and the
biadditivity. (c): substitute the letter group (HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.5) in `U₀` (integral substitution) and in `Φ` (by (b)). The
proofs of the biadditivity, of the torus action and of the substitution are not spelled out here.

*Check.* The sign matters in (c): for the law of `ℓ_{−1}`, `n′ = 2`, trivial trace bases, `β₀ = Φ = 0` and `U₀ = X`, the factor `U`
restricted to the even letter would be `ℓ_{−1}^{−1}(ℓ_{+1}(T)) = T + (2/p)T^{p²} + ⋯`, not integral; the sign `−1` is brought to
`+1` before passing to letters (by the homothety `Y ↦ 𝔲Y`, `𝔲^{p²−1} = −1`, of 4.4, route). The sign `−1` is that of an
elliptic curve over `ℤ_p` with good reduction and `a_p = 0`, for instance a CM elliptic curve over `ℚ` with good reduction at a
prime `p` inert in its CM field: over `W(𝔽_{p²})` its formal group is the Lubin–Tate module of `−p`
(LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.4; NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §§3.4–3.5). A curve whose complex multiplication
is defined only over the CM field can have the sign `+1`: the twist `u·y² = x³ − x` by `u = 2 + i` (of norm `5`, so a non-square
in `𝔽_49`) has good reduction at `7` and `2·50 − 64 = 36 = (7 − 1)²` points over `𝔽_49`, so over `W(𝔽_49)` its formal group is the

### 4.5 Taylor bounds at weighted radii

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a bound for the Taylor coefficients of an integral series times the exponential of a
biextension cocycle in the letters, at radii chosen variable by variable under the bi-power condition; for a single
self-dual formal group it gives the rate `ρ_LT`, the true order for a CM elliptic curve at an inert prime (Check). The
coefficient formula of the API paragraph for `lt(i) ≠ lt(j)`, with its remark on the Frobenius-twisted pairing modulo
`p`, is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* [FormalProof] has the statement in full, with the loss `#ι·(log_p a + 1)` for an integer
`a ≥ 1` bounding the `κ_i`, and the one-letter case. The proof is by weights at the radii `ρ_LT + σ_i` (4.1): the
integral factor composed with the inverse of the letter logarithms has weight `≥ 0`; `β_M` has weight `≥ 1/(p−1) − 1` at
radii `ρ_LT`, and `≥ 1/(p−1)` at every monomial that is not a bi-power monomial `p^αe_i + p^{α′}e_j`, so that under the
bi-power condition `exp(±β_M)` has weight `≥ 0` up to `κ`; and the factorial of `κ` supplies `1/(p−1)` for each
derivative. The one-letter case uses `(p^α + p^{α′})/(p+1) ≥ 1` for `α + α′` odd and `γ_LT − 1/(p+1) = ρ_LT`.

Setting of 4.3 (`p` odd), with variables `z_{s,a,b}` (`s ∈ ℤ/n′` a letter,
`a < N0`, `b < N1`), letter logarithms `λ_{s,a,b}(T) = Σ_e T_{s−2e,a,b}^{p^{2e}}/p^e` and inverse `g`. Let `ι` be finite
with a letter `lt(i)` for each `i`, `M` an integral matrix with `M_{(s,a,b),i} ≠ 0` only if `s = lt(i)`,
`β_M(y) := Σ_{a,b} β((Σ_i M_{(s,a,b),i}y_i)_{s ∈ ℤ/n′})`, `U` integral in the formal parameters, radii `σ_i ≥ 0` and
`κ ∈ ℕ^ι`. Assume the *bi-power condition*: whenever `p^α e_i + p^{α′}e_j ≤ κ` and the coefficient of
`y_i^{p^α}y_j^{p^{α′}}` in `β_M` has valuation `< 1/(p−1) − ρ_LT(p^α + p^{α′})`, then `p^ασ_i + p^{α′}σ_j ≥ 1`. Then for every
integer `a ≥ 1` with `κ_i ≤ a` for every `i`,
`v_p(κ!·coeff_κ[U(g(My))·exp(±β_M(y))]) ≥ Σ_i κ_i(γ_LT − σ_i) − #ι·(log_p a + 1)`.
*API.* For `lt(i) ≠ lt(j)` the coefficient of `y_i^{p^α}y_j^{p^{α′}}` in `β_M` is
`coeff_{p^αe_{lt i} + p^{α′}e_{lt j}}(β)·Σ_{a,b}M_{(lt i,a,b),i}^{p^α}M_{(lt j,a,b),j}^{p^{α′}}`, and modulo `p` the sum is
a Frobenius-twisted pairing of the two columns; when the sum lies in `p𝒪` (valuation `≥ 1`) the term is not bad. Positive
valuation does not suffice: the bad coefficient can have valuation `1/(p−1) − 1 − ρ_LT(p^α + p^{α′})`, one below the threshold
(4.3, Check), and a sum of valuation `1/e < 1` (a column multiplied by a uniformiser of a ramified extension of index `e`) leaves the
term below it. All other monomials of `β_M` have weight `≥ 1/(p−1)` at radii `ρ_LT`.
*One letter.* If there is one letter per variable and `β` is evaluated on `(L(y), u·L(y))` with `u` a unit and the
factor `±1/2` (the case of a single self-dual formal group), all `σ_i = 1/(p+1)` are admissible and
`v_p(κ!·coeff_κ[⋯]) ≥ ρ_LT|κ| − #ι·lg_p|κ|`.

*Route.* By 4.3 and the API, for `n ≤ κ`, `v_p(coeff_n β_M) ≥ 1/(p−1) − Σ n_i(ρ_LT + σ_i)` (at a bad pure monomial the
missing `1` is paid by `p^ασ_i + p^{α′}σ_j ≥ 1`). `U(g(My))` has weight `≥ 0` at radii `ρ_LT + σ` (4.1(a) without truncation,
`U` being integral, with HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.1); `exp(±β_M)` has weight `≥ 0` there up to `κ` by 4.1(c); multiply
(4.1(b) up to `κ`) and add `v_p(κ!) = Σ_iv_p(κ_i!)` from 4.1(e), coordinate by coordinate (`lg_pκ_i ≤ log_pa + 1`).
The exponent is `1/(p−1) − ρ_LT = γ_LT`: the factorial of the order of differentiation against the
radius of the formal exponential. One letter: `γ_LT − 1/(p+1) = ρ_LT` and `(p^α + p^{α′})/(p+1) ≥ 1` when `α + α′` is odd, with
`a := max(|κ|, 1)`.

*Check.* The one-letter exponent `ρ_LT` is the true order for one CM elliptic curve: for `y² = 4x³ − 4x` at `p = 7`
(inert in `ℚ(i)`), `g₂ = 4 = 60G₄` gives `G₄ = 1/15` and `G₈ = (3/7)G₄²` has `v_7 = −1`, so the coefficient `−G₈/8`
of `z⁸` in `log σ(z)` (`σ` the sigma function of the lattice, ModularCurvesPartII §1.4a) is the exceptional term of 4.2; the
derivatives of `σ` at `0` are divisible only by `p^{ρ_LT·m − O(log m)}`, not `p^{γ_LT·m − O(log m)}`.

### 4.5a Folded letters

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the Taylor bound at weighted radii for letters folded along a permutation of the
places.

*Method of the formal proof:* weights at the radii `ρ_LT + σ_z`, one radius per place: by 4.3 each cocycle has weight
`≥ 1/(p−1)` off the bi-power monomials and one less on them, where the two letters have opposite parity, so the exponents
differ by an odd number and the radius condition along the orbit pays the missing `1`; the exponential, the integral factor
composed with the inverse of the folded logarithms, the product and the factorial bound then follow from 4.1.

Setting of 4.3 with `p` odd and `n′ = 2i` letters, `i ≥ 1`. Let `Pl` be a finite
set (places) with a permutation `h` whose order on each orbit divides `i`, `R ⊂ Pl` a set of representatives of the orbits,
`pos(r, s) := h^s(r)` (so the place `pos(r, s)` carries the letters `s` and `s + i`), `ι` a finite index set, `β_r` (`r ∈ R`) series
satisfying (B1)–(B4) of 4.3, signs `sgn_r = ±1`, a unit `𝔲`, and `Θ` a series in variables `y_{z,b}` (`z ∈ Pl`, `b ∈ ι`) with
`Θ(𝔲·λ) = U·exp(Σ_r(sgn_r/2)Σ_bβ_r((y_{pos(r,s),b})_{s∈ℤ/2i}))(λ)`, `U` integral and `λ` the letter logarithms
`λ_{z,b}(T) = Σ_{e≥0}T_{h^{−2e}z,b}^{p^{2e}}/p^e`. Let `σ : Pl → ℝ_{≥0}` satisfy `p^eσ_z + σ_{h^ez} ≥ 1` for every `z` and every odd
`e ≥ 1`. Then for every multi-index `κ` with entries `≤ A`, `A ≥ 1`:
`v_p(κ!·coeff_κΘ) ≥ Σ_{z,b}κ_{z,b}(γ_LT − σ_z) − #(Pl × ι)·(log_pA + 1)`.

*Route.* As 4.5 with the radii `ρ_LT + σ_z`, one radius per place: the inverse of the letter logarithms has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at
radii `ρ_LT` (HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.1). *The weight of the folded cocycle.* Each `β_r` satisfies
(B1)–(B4) for `2i` letters, so by 4.3 it has weight `≥ 1/(p−1) − 1` at radii `ρ_LT`, and `≥ 1/(p−1)` off the bi-power monomials. At a
bi-power monomial `p^αe_s + p^{α′}e_{s′}` (`s` even, `s′` odd, `s + α ≡ s′ + α′ (mod 2i)`) the number `e := |α − α′|` is odd (parities),
and the place of the letter with the smaller exponent is `h^e` of the place of the other (`pos(r, s′) = h^{s′−s}pos(r, s)`); so the
radius condition gives `p^ασ + p^{α′}σ′ ≥ p^{min(α,α′)} ≥ 1`, which pays the missing `1`. Substituting the variable of the place
`pos(r, s)` for the letter `s` (each variable has weight `≥ 0` at radius `ρ_LT + σ_z`), the unit factor `±1/2` (`p` odd) and the sum
over `r` and `b` keep the weight `≥ 1/(p−1)` at the radii `ρ_LT + σ`; then 4.1 (c), (a), (b), (e). Since the radius does not depend on
the row, the bound passes to jets against products of minors without a frame argument: such a jet is an integral combination
of the numbers `κ!·coeff_κΘ` with `|κ_z| = |ν(z)|` at each place.

*Needs:* 4.1, 4.3, 4.5; HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §3.1.

*In [FormalProof]:* UpperTheory.norm_mfact_mul_coeff_le_of_B0S (`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/OrbitLabellings/NormMfactMulCoeffLeOfB0S.lean`), the permutation `h` written as an action of period `i` on the places.

### 4.6 The flow programme

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a linear programme that prices the bi-power monomials of a cocycle which a derivative
can absorb and gives the exponent of the Taylor bounds; its properties follow from duality of linear programmes and
complementary slackness. The convexity and the upper bound of (b), the last inequality of (d2), (e) and the support
clause of (g) are this roadmap's own formulations.

*Method of the formal proof:* duality is proved by a hyperplane separation from the closed convex set of the capacities
and values dominated by a flow, with compactness for the existence of an optimal flow and of an optimal dual vector; the
other properties follow from duality and complementary slackness. [FormalProof] states (a), with weak duality and the
existence of an optimal flow, also on a cycle of any length; the lower bound and the homogeneity of (b); (c); (d1); the
first sentence of (d2); (d3); (d4), with `8` in place of `12`; (f) for `p ≥ 5`; and the truncation of (g).

For `n′` even, `p ≥ 3` and `D ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^{ℤ/n′}` put
`𝔉_p(D) := γ_LT·Σ_s D_s − max{Σ_{s,i} x_{s,i} : x ≥ 0, i odd, 1 ≤ i < n′, Σ_i p^i x_{s,i} + Σ_i x_{s−i,i} ≤ D_s for all s}`
(a flow `x_{s,i}` counts bi-power monomials `y_s^{p^i}y_{s+i}`). A *dual radius vector* is a `σ ∈ [0, 1]^{ℤ/n′}` with
`p^iσ_s + σ_{s+i} ≥ 1` for all `s` and odd `i < n′`; it is *optimal for `D`* if `𝔉_p(D) = Σ_s D_s(γ_LT − σ_s)`. Prove:
(a) duality: `𝔉_p(D) = max{Σ_s D_s(γ_LT − σ_s) : σ a dual radius vector}`, attained at a `σ` satisfying the constraint for
every odd `i` (also `i ≥ n′`; this uses that `n′` is even, and fails on a cycle of odd length, (f));
(b) `𝔉_p` is positively homogeneous and convex, and `ρ_LT·ΣD ≤ 𝔉_p(D) ≤ γ_LT·ΣD`;
(c) `𝔉_p(D) = ρ_LT·ΣD` when `max D ≤ p·min D`;
(d) *perturbation:* let `σ` be optimal for `D`. (d1) For every `E ≥ 0`,
`𝔉_p(D) − Σ_sE_s·(σ_s − γ_LT)₊ ≤ 𝔉_p(D + E) ≤ 𝔉_p(D) + γ_LT·Σ_sE_s`. (d2) If `σ_s ≥ 2/p`, then in every maximal flow nothing
leaves the letter `s` and `D_s` equals the flow entering `s`; in particular `D_s ≤ Σ_{i odd}D_{s−i}/p^i`.
(d3) `Σ_{s : σ_s ≥ 2/p}D_s ≤ (Σ_sD_s)/p`. (d4) For `w ∈ ℝ_{>0}^{ℤ/n′}`, `k ≥ 3` and `0 ≤ E_s ≤ 4w_s`:
`𝔉_p((k−1)w + E) ≥ 𝔉_p((k−1)w) − 12·Σ_sw_s/p`;
(e) for `A ≥ 0`, an odd `i_* ≥ 1` and capacities `cap_j ≥ 0` (`j` odd `< i_*`) there is a radius `σ^{top} ≥ p^{−i_*}` with
`Aσ^{top} + Σ_{j odd < i_*} cap_j·(1 − p^jσ^{top})₊ ≤ Σ_{j odd < i_*} min(cap_j, A/p^j) + A/p^{i_*}`;
(f) *cycles of any length:* for an integer `n_o ≥ 2` (odd allowed) define `𝔉_p(D)`, `D ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^{ℤ/n_o}`, by the same programme
with flows `x_{s,i}`, `i` odd, `1 ≤ i < n_o`. Duality with attainment, (b), (c) and (d1)–(d3) hold for this programme (not the last clause of (a)). Let `p ≥ 5`, `κ > 0`, `w ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^{ℤ/n_o}` and `κw_s ≤ D_s ≤ κw_s + 4w_s`. Then there is
`σ ∈ ℝ_{≥0}^{ℤ/n_o}` with `p^eσ_s + σ_{s+e} ≥ 1` for all `s` and ALL odd `e ≥ 1`, and
`𝔉_p(κw) − κ·Σ_sw_s/p³ − 8·Σ_sw_s/p ≤ Σ_sD_s(γ_LT − σ_s)`. The route below uses only duality with attainment on the
cycle (an optimal dual in `[0, 1]^{ℤ/n_o}`), (d3), `p^{−3} < 2/p` and `γ_LT ≥ 1/p`, and gives (f) for every prime `p ≥ 3` (the
range `p ≥ 5` is the form of [FormalProof], Conventions);
(g) *truncation and support:* for every odd `i₀ ≥ 1` there is a flow with `x_{s,i} = 0` for `i ≥ i₀` whose value is at least the
maximum minus `p^{−i₀}·ΣD`; and a maximal flow can be chosen with at most `n′` nonzero `x_{s,i}` (both on a cycle of any length `n_o`,
with `n_o` in place of `n′`). The support clause is this roadmap's own, not a step of [FormalProof], and is not used in this
roadmap.

`Suggested.lean` states (a), the two bounds of (b) and (c), for a prime `p ≥ 3`, as `flowProgramme_duality`,
`flowProgramme_bounds` and `flowProgramme_eq_of_max_le_mul_min`, with the programme `flowProgramme` and the dual radius
vectors `IsDualRadiusVector`.

*Route.* (a) LP duality for a finite programme (Farkas' lemma); a bi-power constraint with `α, α′ ≥ 1` reduces by
dividing by `p`, and `i ≥ n′` reduces to `i − n′`, which keeps the parity. The last clause of (a) and this reduction are for
even cycles only: on a cycle of odd length an odd `i ≥ n_o` reduces to an even shift. (b) The uniform radii `1/(p+1)` are
feasible; summing the constraints bounds the flow by `ΣD/(p+1)`. (c) The flow using only `i = 1`,
`x_s = D_s/p − D_{s−1}/p² + D_{s−2}/p³ − ⋯`, is non-negative and has total `ΣD/(p+1)`. (d1) The set of dual radius
vectors does not depend on `D`, `σ` is one for `D + E`, and an optimal one for `D + E` is one for `D`. (d2)
Complementary slackness for a maximal flow and an optimal dual: a constraint `(s, i)` with `p^iσ_s + σ_{s+i} > 1`
carries no flow (`σ_s ≥ 2/p` makes every `(s, i)` slack), and `σ_s > 0` makes the constraint at `s` tight. (d3) The
letters of (d3) are filled by incoming flow, and the total flow is at most `ΣD/p` because `p·Σ_ix_{t,i} ≤ D_t`.
(d4) Apply (d1) to `D = (k−1)w` with an optimal `σ`: a letter with `σ_s < 2/p` loses at most `E_s·2/p ≤ 8w_s/p`, the
others at most `E_s ≤ 4w_s`, and they carry `Σw_s ≤ Σw/p` by (d3) applied to `(k−1)w`. (e) The left side is convex and
piecewise linear in `σ^{top}`; compare at the breakpoints `σ^{top} = p^{−j}`. (f) Duality with attainment, (b), (c), (d1)–(d3) hold on a
cycle of any length `n_o ≥ 2` with the same proofs (not the last clause of (a)). Take an optimal dual `σ⁰ ∈ [0, 1]^{ℤ/n_o}` for `κw`
(constraints for the odd `e < n_o`) and put `σ := max(σ⁰, p^{−3})`: an odd `e < n_o` inherits its constraint, and an odd `e ≥ n_o` is
`≥ 3`, so `p^eσ_s ≥ 1`. Pointwise, `D_s(γ_LT − σ_s) = κw_s(γ_LT − σ⁰_s) − κw_s(σ_s − σ⁰_s) + (D_s − κw_s)(γ_LT − σ_s)`; the middle term is
`≥ −κw_s/p³`; the last is `≥ −4w_s/p` at a letter with `σ⁰_s < 2/p` (then `σ_s < 2/p` and `γ_LT ≥ 1/p`) and `≥ −4w_s` at the others,
which carry `Σκw_s ≤ Σκw/p` by (d3) applied to `κw` (divide by `κ`). Summing gives `𝔉_p(κw) − κΣw/p³ − 8Σw/p`. (g) Deleting
the levels `i ≥ i₀` keeps a flow and loses at most `Σ_sΣ_{i≥i₀}x_{s,i} ≤ p^{−i₀}ΣD`, because
`p^{i₀}Σ_{i≥i₀}x_{s,i} ≤ Σ_ip^ix_{s,i} ≤ D_s`; a maximal flow may be taken at a vertex of the polytope in `ℝ^N`, where `N` linearly
independent constraints are tight, at most `n′` of them capacities, so at least `N − n′` coordinates vanish.

*Checks.* `n′ = 2`, `p = 3`: `D = (1, 1)` gives maximal flow `1/2` at `x = (1/4, 1/4)` and `𝔉_3 = 2·3/8 − 1/2 = 1/4 = ρ_LT·2`;
`D = (1, 0)` gives flow `0` and `𝔉_3 = γ_LT = 3/8`: a single direction gains at the full rate. The
perturbation bound needs the share of the letters of large radius: for `D = (1, δ)`, `E = (0, 4δ)`, `0 < 5δ ≤ 1/p`, the
loss `𝔉_p(D) − 𝔉_p(D + E) = 4δ(1 − γ_LT)` is not `O(ΣE/p)`, while (d1) gives it exactly (`σ_1 = 1`). Cycles of odd length
`n_o`: the programme of 4.6 on the `2n_o` positions of `ℤ/2n_o` with `n_o`-periodic capacities equals `2·𝔉^{full}(ℤ/n_o)`, where `𝔉^{full}` is
the programme on `ℤ/n_o` with flows of every odd length `< 2n_o` (an optimal dual can be taken `n_o`-periodic, the dual polytope and the
objective being invariant under the shift by `n_o`), and `𝔉^{full} ≤ 𝔉_p(ℤ/n_o) ≤ 𝔉^{full} + ΣD/p^{n_o}` for the programme of (f) (flows of
odd length `< n_o`); for `n_o = 3`, `p = 5`, `D = (1, 0, 0)`: `𝔉_5(ℤ/3) = γ_LT(5) = 5/24`, and the self-loop of length `3` gives
`𝔉^{full} = 5/24 − 1/126`; for `n_o = 1`, `𝔉^{full} = ρ_LT·D` (a single position).

*Sources:* (LP duality, complementary slackness). *Needs:* Mathlib `ProperCone.hyperplane_separation_point` and
`ProperCone.relative_hyperplane_separation` (Farkas' lemma for proper cones); Mathlib has no duality theorem for linear
programmes, and LP duality and complementary slackness for the finite programme here are proved from Farkas' lemma (with the
closedness of a finitely generated cone in `ℝ^N`, as `ProperCone.map` is the closure of the image).

### 4.7 Jet bounds against products of minors

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a bound for the coefficients of the jets of the series of 4.5 against products of
minors of a frame, with the exponent of the flow programme of 4.6. The span form of (i) and the corollary (ii) are this
roadmap's own formulations.

*Method of the formal proof:* one monomial is bounded by the Taylor bound of 4.5 with radii given by an optimal dual vector
of the flow programme, a top radius, and special radii on designated rows; an integral change of the non-top rows reduces a
frame whose Frobenius-twisted pairings have unit determinants to that case; and the bound for every coefficient of the jet
polynomial follows by evaluating at a generic frame, in the fraction field of the polynomial ring in the frame entries with
the Gauss norm. The route written here goes on from that coefficient bound, by Cauchy–Binet and the saturation of the span
of the products of minors, to the span form of (i); the coefficient bound is the form that the consumers of (i) in
[FormalProof] use.

Setting of 4.5 over `𝒦 = ℂ_p`, the completion of an algebraic closure of `ℚ_p` (`p ≥ 3`), and
`Θ(z) := U(g(z))·exp(±Σ_{a,b}β(z_{·,a,b}))`. For each letter `s` let `ν^{(s)}` be a Young diagram with at most `N0`
rows, `ν_0(s) ≥ ν_1(s)` its first two rows and `size(s) := |ν^{(s)}|`; let `𝔗 ⊂ ℤ/n′` be any set of top letters, `S ≥ 1`
an integer at least every size, and `2log_p S + 2 ≤ N0 − 1`. Put `c(s) := ν_1(s)` for `s ∈ 𝔗` and `ν_0(s)` otherwise;
`D_s := size(s) − [s ∈ 𝔗]·ν_0(s)`; `d⁺(s)` := the least odd `e ≥ 1` with `s + e ∈ 𝔗` (`∞` if none);
`R^{top}(s) := Σ_{e odd < d⁺(s)}(ν_0(s)/p^e − c(s+e))₊ + Σ_{e odd > d⁺(s)} ν_0(s)/p^e`;
`𝔚(s) := sup{c(s − i) : i odd ≥ 1, p^i ≤ size(s − i)}` (`0` if empty). For a frame `x = (x_{s,k,a})`
(`k, a < N0`) and column choices `I`, let `P_I(x; ∂) := ∏_s ∏_{columns c of ν^{(s)}} det[(x·∂)_{(s,k), I_c(k′)}]_{k,k′ < ht(c)}`,
`(x·∂)_{(s,k),b} := Σ_a x_{s,k,a}∂/∂z_{s,a,b}`, and `J_I(x) := [P_I(x; ∂)Θ](0)`, a polynomial in `x`.
(i) `J_I` lies in `p^B` times the lattice spanned over the valuation ring by the products of minors of `x`, where
`B := Σ_{s∈𝔗}(R^{top}(s) − γ_LT·𝔚(s)) + 𝔉_p(D) − n′N0N1(log_p S + 1)`; equivalently, every coefficient of the polynomial `J_I` has
valuation at least `B` (the span of the products of minors is saturated).
(ii) *Corollary.* Assume `ν_1(s) ≤ ν_0(s)`, `ν_0(s) + ν_1(s) ≤ size(s)`, `k ≥ 3`, and `w ∈ ℝ_{>0}^{ℤ/n′}` with
`D_s = (k−1)w_s + E_s` off `𝔗` and `(k−3)w_s + E_s` on `𝔗`, `0 ≤ E_s ≤ 4w_s`. Then
`B ≥ Σ_{s∈𝔗}R^{base}(s) + 𝔉_p((k−1)w) − 15·Σ_s w_s/p − γ_LTΣ_{s∈𝔗}𝔚(s) − C_A·n′N0N1(log_p S + 1)`, where
`R^{base}(s) := Σ_{e odd, e ≠ d⁺(s)}((ν_0 − ν_1)(s)/p^e − c(s+e))₊`.

*Route.* Radii: an optimal dual `σ` of 4.6 on the rows of a letter, the radius `σ^{top}_s` of 4.6(e) (`A = ν_0(s)`,
`i_* = d⁺(s)`) on the top row of a top letter, and `max(σ, 1 − p^jσ^{top}_{s₀})` or `max(σ, p^{−|j|})` on at most one row per
top and odd distance `j` ("designated" rows); these satisfy every bi-power condition, the top–top ones because
`σ^{top}_s ≥ p^{−d⁺(s)}`, and `γ_LTν_0(s)` minus the right side of 4.6(e) is `R^{top}(s)`. 4.5 then bounds one monomial for a
frame whose Frobenius-twisted pairings of top rows against non-designated rows lie in `p𝒪` (valuation `≥ 1`; a positive valuation
below `1` does not suffice, 4.5, API). Every monomial of a
product of minors has, after an integral change of the non-top rows, degree `ν_0(s)` in the top row and `≤ c(s)` in
the others, so the bound passes to `J_I`. A unimodular change of rows reduces any frame whose matrix of twisted
pairings has unit determinant to that case; a frame with residually algebraically independent entries (top rows
raised to `p`-power exponents) has unit determinant by Moore determinants (the number of designated rows per letter is
`≤ 2log_p S + 1`, whence the hypothesis on `N0`), and evaluation at such a frame computes the Gauss norm of a
polynomial. Cauchy–Binet puts `J_I` in the span of products of minors, and that span is saturated: an element of its
`𝒦`-span that is integral has integral coordinates in the standard bideterminant basis, whose leading monomials form a
unitriangular system (IntegralWeylModulesOfGeneralLinearGroups §0.1, the standard bideterminant basis over a domain). (ii): with `W = (k−1)w`
and `E′ = 2w` at the tops, `D + E′ = W + E`; `𝔉_p(D) ≥ 𝔉_p(W + E) − γ_LTΣE′ ≥ 𝔉_p(W + E) − (9/4)Σw/p` (4.6(d1)), and
`𝔉_p(W + E) ≥ 𝔉_p(W) − 12Σw/p` (4.6(d4)); and `R^{top}(s) ≥ R^{base}(s)` term by term (`ν_1(s) ≥ 0`, `c(s + e) ≥ 0`). The assembly of the bound (i) from these radii, with the term `γ_LT·𝔚(s)`
for the designated rows, and the reduction of an arbitrary frame to the case of unit determinant are not given here.

*Needs:* 4.5, 4.6; IntegralWeylModulesOfGeneralLinearGroups §0.1 (standard bideterminant basis and saturation).

*In [FormalProof]:* (i), in the form of the bound for every coefficient of the polynomial `J_I`, is ThmAGauss (`Formalism/GaussJetBounds/StatementP.lean`).

### 4.8 Jet bounds by counting pairs

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a bound for the jets of the series of the class of 4.4 (iii), such as the exponential
of a classified cocycle times an integral series, by counting the pairs of boxes that a product of minors can absorb, with
its consequences (J1)–(J3); for the class at the sign `𝔰 = −1` it is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* for `p` odd and the sign `𝔰 = 1`: the top rows are split off by an integral substitution
under which the class `Σ p^{−i}𝔥^i g_i` is stable and `𝔥` becomes, modulo `p` and terms of low degree `≥ p`, a sum of
top–top monomials and of top–low monomials times linear forms; the powers of `𝔥` are expanded by the multinomial theorem,
the linear forms are moved onto the operator as Hasse derivatives, the ultrametric inequality selects one term, whose
multiplicities are the pairs, and Legendre's formula gives the exponent. The consequences follow because a Hasse
derivative of a product of minors vanishes when a rank-one direction is hit too often, with an elementary charging of the
top–top pairs.

Let `F = Σ_{i ≥ 0} p^{−i}𝔥^i g_i` be any series in the class of 4.4(iii) for one place
(so in particular `exp(−Φ)·U` for every integral `U`, the class being stable under multiplication by integral series),
with letters `s ∈ ℤ/n′` and auxiliary index `· = (a, b)`, `a < N`, `b < N′`, and read jets in the logarithmic letters
`z_{s,·}` (unrescaled). Let `𝔗 ⊂ ℤ/n′` be top letters, `ξ_s` (`s ∈ 𝔗`) integral vectors, `n_{s,b} ∈ ℕ` multiplicities,
`α_s := Σ_b n_{s,b}`, and `Q` a product of minors with integral rows in the remaining directions. Call a *top–top pair*
`(s₁, i; s₂, i′; b)` with `s₁, s₂ ∈ 𝔗`, `s₁ + 1 + 2i ≡ s₂ + 2i′ (mod n′)`, using `p^{1+2i}` top boxes of `s₁` and
`p^{2i′}` of `s₂` in column `b`; and a *top–low pair* `(s, e′, b)`, using `p^{2e′+1}` top boxes of `s` in column `b`
and one derivative of `Q` in the direction `ξ_s^{(p^{2e′+1})} ⊗ e_b` at the letter `s + 2e′ + 1`
(`ξ^{(q)}` the vector of `q`-th powers of the coordinates). Then
`Jet := [∏_{s,b}(Σ_a ξ_{s,a}∂_{(s,a,b)})^{n_{s,b}}·Q(∂) F](0)` is `0`, or there are multiplicities `m^{tt}_φ`,
`m^{tl}_k` of pairs fitting into the boxes `n_{·,·}` and absorbed by `Q` (the Hasse derivative of `Q` in the pair
directions is nonzero) with
`v_p(Jet) ≥ γ_LT·Σ_{s∈𝔗}α_s − Σ_φ m^{tt}_φ − Σ_k m^{tl}_k − lg`, `lg := C·Σ_s(NN′·lg_p(2 + deg_s) + lg_p(2 + α_s)²)`,

*Consequences.* For the products of minors of Young diagrams `ν(s)` (as in 4.7) with top rows `ξ_s`, put

which caps the pairs: (J1) charge each top–top pair `τ_{s₁}^{p^α}τ_{s₂}^{p^{α′}}` to the top with the larger exponent,

### 4.8a Jets of a Gaussian against products of minors

*Origin:* adapted from Wick's (Isserlis') formula for the derivatives of a Gaussian: a `p`-adic bound for the jets of a
Gaussian against products of minors, by the number `c′_t` of pairs that the two diagrams can match.

*Method of the formal proof:* the exponential is expanded, which writes the jet as a sum of integral jets of `σ` times
`∏_t(−S·u_t)^{n_t}` times integers that are coefficients of `∏_tΛ_t^{n_t}/n_t!` applied to the symbol of the operator,
`Λ_t = Σ_{a,b}∂²/∂X_{(0,t),a,b}∂X_{(1,t),a,b}`; and `Λ_t^n` annihilates the product of the minors for `n > c′_t`, by
expanding the minors of one side into products of linear forms with one factor from the first row and counting how many
contractions the other side admits.

Let `p` be a prime, `H` a finite set, `S ∈ ℂ_p` with
`|S| ≤ p^{1/2}`, `u_t ∈ ℂ_p` with `|u_t| ≤ 1` (`t ∈ H`), `σ` a power series over `ℂ_p` in the variables `X_{(i,t),a,b}`
(`i ∈ {0, 1}`, `t ∈ H`, `a < N`, `b < N′`) with `n!·coeff_n σ` integral for every `n`, and for each `(i, t)` a Young diagram
`ν(i,t)` with, for each of its columns, of height `h ≥ 1`, a choice of `h` columns `b`. For frames `x_{(i,t)}` (matrices of
variables) let `P_{i,t}(x; ∂)` be the product, over the columns of `ν(i,t)`, of the minors of `x_{(i,t)}·∂` on the first `h`
rows and on the chosen columns. Then every coefficient of the polynomial
`x ↦ [∏_{i,t}P_{i,t}(x; ∂)·(exp(−S·Σ_tu_tΣ_{a,b}X_{(0,t),a,b}X_{(1,t),a,b})·σ)](0)` has absolute value at most `p^{½Σ_tc′_t}`,
`c′_t := |ν(0,t)| − ν(0,t)_0 + min(ν(0,t)_0, ν(1,t)_0)`; in particular the jet at integral frames is bounded by this
number. Consequently, for every subset `𝔗 ⊂ H` it is bounded by `p^{½Σ_tc_t}` with `c_t := |ν(0,t)|` for `t ∉ 𝔗` and
`c_t := |ν(0,t)| − ν(0,t)_0 + ν(1,t)_0` for `t ∈ 𝔗`, and a fortiori with `c_t := |ν(0,t)| − ν(0,t)_0 + ν(0,t)_1 + ν(1,t)_0` for
`t ∈ 𝔗`, the count used downstream (Downstream consumers).

*Route.* Expanding the exponential, the jet against an operator `P` is a sum over `n ∈ ℕ^H` of `∏_t(−S·u_t)^{n_t}` times an
integer times an integral jet of `σ`, the integer being a coefficient of `∏_tΛ_t^{n_t}P/n_t!`,
`Λ_t := Σ_{a,b}∂²/∂X_{(0,t),a,b}∂X_{(1,t),a,b}` acting on the symbol of `P`; and `Λ_t^n` annihilates the product of the minors
for `n > c′_t`; a term with every `n_t ≤ c′_t` has absolute value at most `∏_t|S|^{n_t} ≤ p^{½Σ_tc′_t}`. The vanishing is not
given here.

*Check.* `H = {t}`, `N = N′ = 1`, `|S| = p^{1/2}`, `u = 1`, `σ = 1`, `P_{0,t} = ∂_{X_0}^n`, `P_{1,t} = ∂_{X_1}^n` (`n` minors
of height `1` on each side, `ν(0,t) = ν(1,t) = (n)`): the jet is `n!·(−S)^n`, of absolute value `|n!|·p^{n/2}`, against
the bound `p^{n/2}` (`c′_t = n`, and `c_t = n` whether or not `t ∈ 𝔗`): the exponent `½` per matched pair is attained up to `|n!|`.

*Sources:* (Wick's theorem). *Needs:* Mathlib (determinants, power series).

### 4.8b Framed points, integral charts and the depth of a weight

*Origin:* definitions and conventions.

*Definitions.* Let `E` be a CM field with complex conjugation `a ↦ ā`, `E⁺` its maximal totally real
subfield, `f := [E⁺ : ℚ]`, and `M` a PEL moduli problem of unitary type for `E` (PELModuli M1.7: objects `(A, λ, i, α)`, an
abelian scheme with an action `i` of `𝒪_E`, a polarisation `λ` with `i(ā)^∨λ = λi(a)`, and a level structure). Let `R` be a
complete discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic `(0, p)` with fraction field `K_R`, containing `τ(𝒪_E)` for every
embedding `τ` of `E` into an algebraic closure of `K_R` (so the embeddings are maps `τ : 𝒪_E → R`); `τ̄ := τ∘c` is the conjugate
embedding.
- *Integral points.* An *integral point* `y` over `R` is an object `(A, λ, i, α)` of `M` over `K_R` together with a *semi-abelian
  model*: a semi-abelian scheme `𝒜` over `R` (ShimuraCompactifications 6.1: smooth, separated, commutative, with geometric fibres
  that are extensions of abelian varieties by tori) with an isomorphism `𝒜_{K_R} ≅ A`, an action of `𝒪_E` on `𝒜` extending `i`
  (also written `i`), and a rigidified symmetric line bundle `L` on `𝒜` with a cubical structure such that `φ_{L_{K_R}} = λ` and
  `(i(a) × 1)^*Λ(L) ≅ (1 × i(ā))^*Λ(L)` for `a ∈ 𝒪_E`, where `Λ(L) := m^*L ⊗ pr_1^*L^{−1} ⊗ pr_2^*L^{−1}` is the biextension of
  `𝒜 × 𝒜` of `L` (on the generic fibre the pull-back by `1 × λ` of the Poincaré biextension, AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli
  A2.3). An object of `M` over `R` (an abelian scheme) with a rigidified symmetric `L`, `φ_L = λ`, is an integral point with `𝒜 = A`.
  `y` is *algebraic* if it is the base change of an integral point over the localisation at a prime of the ring of integers of a
  number field.
- *Frames.* For an embedding `τ` let `Lie_τ := {u ∈ Lie(𝒜/R) : i(a)u = τ(a)u for all a ∈ 𝒪_E}` be the `τ`-eigenspace of the
  tangent module of the model, and `n_τ` the dimension of the `τ`-part of `Lie(A)` (the signature, PELModuli M1.3). `Lie_τ` is
  saturated in `Lie(𝒜/R)` (a kernel of `R`-linear maps into a torsion-free module) and `Lie_τ ⊗ K_R` is the `τ`-part of `Lie(A)`,
  so `Lie_τ` is free of rank `n_τ` (AInfCohomology §AI.1.3). A *frame* of `y` is a family of vectors `u_{τ,s}` (`τ` an embedding,
  `s < n_τ`) such that, for every `τ`, the `u_{τ,s}` (`s < n_τ`) form an `R`-basis of `Lie_τ`; a *framed point* is an integral point
  with a frame. Every integral point has frames, and two frames differ by an element of `∏_τGL_{n_τ}(R)`. At a prime `p` unramified
  in `E`, `Lie(𝒜/R)` is the direct sum of the `Lie_τ` (the idempotents of `𝒪_E ⊗ R` are integral; PELModuli §M0.27), so the frame
  vectors of all the embeddings together form an `R`-basis of `Lie(𝒜/R)`; at a prime ramified in `E` they need not (Check).
- *Integral charts.* An *integral chart* `𝔠` of a framed point `y` consists of coordinates `x = (x_1, …, x_g)` on the formal
  completion `𝒜̂` of `𝒜` along the zero section in which the group law `x +_F x′` and the action `[a](x)` (`a ∈ 𝒪_E`) are power
  series with coefficients in `R`; the frame, read as tangent vectors of `𝒜̂`; an element `w₀ ∈ 𝒪_E` with `w₀ + w̄₀ = 1` (it exists
  exactly when the relative trace `𝒪_E → 𝒪_{E⁺}` is onto, whose image is an ideal, for instance when `E` contains `ℚ(√−d)` with
  `d ≡ 3 (mod 4)`; not for `E = ℚ(i)`, where the trace of `ℤ[i]` is `2ℤ`, so that no point has an integral chart there); and the
  *polarisation series* `h(x, x′)`, a power series over `R[1/p]` in two copies of the coordinates with `h(x, 0) = h(0, x′) = 0`
  (*canonical gauge*), trivialising the biextension `Λ(L)` restricted to `𝒜̂ × 𝒜̂` (for an abelian scheme, the pull-back by
  `1 × λ` of the Poincaré biextension of `Â × Â^∨`): the series
  `exp(h(x, z) + h(x′, z) − h(x +_F x′, z))`, `exp(h(x, z) + h(x, z′) − h(x, z +_F z′))`, `exp(h(x′, x) − h(x, x′))` and
  `exp(h([a]x, x′) − h(x, [ā]x′))` (`a ∈ 𝒪_E`) have coefficients in `R`. Put `h_L(x) := h(x, [w₀]x)`.
- *Harmonic polynomials.* For `n ≥ 1` (the number of *copies*) let `x_{τ,s,b}` (`s < n_τ`, `b < n`) be variables, the
  coordinates of `n` copies of the tangent space along the frame; the *row group* `∏_τGL_{n_τ}` acts on the index `s`. A
  polynomial `P` in these variables is *harmonic* if `Σ_{b<n}∂²P/∂x_{τ,s,b}∂x_{τ̄,s′,b} = 0` for all `τ`, `s`, `s′`; for a weight
  `κ = (κ_{τ,0} ≥ κ_{τ,1} ≥ ⋯ ≥ κ_{τ,n_τ−1} ≥ 0)_τ` it is *of weight `κ`* if it lies in the `κ`-isotypic part of the polynomial
  ring for the row group. For instance `∏_τ∏_{i<n_τ}δ_{τ,i}^{κ_{τ,i} − κ_{τ,i+1}}` (`κ_{τ,n_τ} := 0`), `δ_{τ,i}` the minor of the matrix
  `(x_{τ,s,b})_{s,b}` on the rows `0, …, i` and on columns `B_τ(i)`, is of weight `κ`, and it is harmonic when, for every `τ`, the
  columns used at `τ` and at `τ̄` are disjoint. `ℋ_κ(n)` is the `R`-module of harmonic polynomials of weight `κ` with coefficients
  in `R`.
- *The depth.* `D_{u,b}` is the invariant derivation of `𝒜̂` along the tangent vector `u` acting on the copy `b` of the
  coordinates, and `P(D)` the operator obtained by substituting `D_{u_{τ,s},b}` for `x_{τ,s,b}`. For an integer `m_L ≥ 1` (the
  *multiplier*), the *depth* of `κ` at `y` in the chart `𝔠` on `n` copies with multiplier `m_L` is
  `dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n, m_L) := inf_{P, f} v_R(P(D)(f·exp(m_L·Σ_{b<n}h_L(x_b)))(0))`,
  the infimum over `P ∈ ℋ_κ(n)` and the power series `f` with coefficients in `R` in the `n` copies `x_b` of the coordinates
  (`v_R(0) = ∞`); `ν_y(κ; n, m_L) := inf_𝔠 dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n, m_L)` over the integral charts of `y`; and, for a place `𝔩` of a number
  field over which `M` is defined, `ν_𝔩(κ; n, m_L) := max(0, sup_y ν_y(κ; n, m_L))`, the supremum over the algebraic integral
  framed points above `𝔩` that have an integral chart (with `v_R` normalised by `v_R(ℓ) = 1`, `ℓ` the residue characteristic of
  `𝔩`); the cut-off at `0` is part of the definition. When `m_L` is not written
  it is the level of the level structure of the moduli problem.

*Checks.* (Frames) Two frames of `y` differ by `g ∈ ∏_τGL_{n_τ}(R)`; `g` acts on `ℋ_κ(n)`
bijectively (the harmonic and isotypic conditions are invariant under the row group, and `g`, `g^{−1}` are integral) and the
invariant derivations transform by `g`, so `dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n)` does not depend on the frame. (Frames at a ramified prime) Let
`E_w = ℚ_p(π)` be ramified quadratic, `𝒪_{E,w} = ℤ_p[π]`, `π² ∈ pℤ_p^×`, and `τ(π) = ϖ`, `τ′(π) = −ϖ` the two embeddings at `w`
(`ϖ ∈ R`, `ϖ² = π²`), and let `Lie(𝒜/R)` be, at `w`, free of rank one over `𝒪_{E,w} ⊗ R = R[X]/(X² − ϖ²)`. Then `Lie_τ = R·(X + ϖ)` and `Lie_{τ′} = R·(X − ϖ)`:
each is saturated of rank one, but the two vectors are congruent modulo `ϖ` and span a submodule of index `|2ϖ|^{−1}`, so frames
exist while no family of eigenvectors of the two embeddings is part of a basis. (Copies) `dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n)` does not
increase with `n`: a polynomial on `n` copies is one on `n + 1` copies. (Harmonicity) For `n_τ = n_{τ̄} = 1`, `κ = (1)` at `τ` and
`(1)` at `τ̄`: `x_{τ,0,0}x_{τ̄,0,0}` is of weight `κ` and not harmonic (its Laplacian is `1`), while `x_{τ,0,0}x_{τ̄,0,1}` is harmonic.

*Needs:* PELModuli §§M0.27, M1.3, M1.7; ShimuraCompactifications 6.1; AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli
A2.3; AInfCohomology §AI.1.3; Mathlib `PowerSeries`, `MvPolynomial`, `Matrix.det`.

### 4.8b′ Integral charts

*Origin:* adapted from Mumford (1969), biextensions of formal groups, and Hazewinkel (1978), chapter II, the logarithm of a
commutative formal group law over a `ℚ`-algebra: here for the formal completion of the semi-abelian model of a framed
point with its action of `𝒪_E`, integral coordinates with a polarisation series in canonical gauge (1), with Lazard's lemma
on symmetric 2-cocycles in dimension `g` ((Z) of the route), and the independence of the depth from the integral chart
(2); (3) is elementary linear algebra over a discrete valuation ring.

*Method of the formal proof:* (1) integral coordinates and normalised trivialising sections of the biextension over the
formal completion of the smooth model give unit series with coefficients in `R` for the two partial laws, the symmetry and
the action of `𝒪_E`, with their cocycle identities because the global functions on the powers of the model are constant;
over `K_R` these units are gauged away one after another, by the logarithm of the group law, by integrating symmetric
2-cocycles in logarithmic coordinates, and by the fact that every derivation of `𝒪_E` into a bimodule over `ℚ` is inner.
(2) Two charts differ by an integral change of coordinates, an integral unit and a biadditive series, which harmonic
polynomials do not see. (3) A saturated linearly independent family over a discrete valuation ring extends to a basis.
The route written here differs in two steps of (1): it proves (Z) through a formal group law of dimension `g + 1`, and it
removes the defect of the action embedding by embedding, by the vanishing of a derivation of `E` and by comparing `B_{aa₀}`
and `B_{a₀a}`.

Let `y` be a framed point over `R` (4.8b; the involution of `𝒪_E` is complex conjugation).
(1) `𝒜̂` has coordinates in which the group law and the action of `𝒪_E` have coefficients in `R`, and in any such coordinates `y`
has a polarisation series in canonical gauge (4.8b). Hence, if the relative trace `𝒪_E → 𝒪_{E⁺}` is onto (that is, `𝒪_E`
contains a `w₀` with `w₀ + w̄₀ = 1`), `y` has an integral chart.
(2) For every weight `κ`, every `n ≥ 1` and every multiplier `m_L`, `dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n, m_L)` is the same for all integral charts
`𝔠` of `y`; so `ν_y(κ; n, m_L) = dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n, m_L)` for any one of them, and, when the trace `𝒪_E → 𝒪_{E⁺}` is onto, the
supremum defining `ν_𝔩(κ; n, m_L)` runs over all algebraic framed points above `𝔩` (by (1)).
(3) In every integral chart of `y` and for every embedding `τ`, the frame vectors `u_{τ,s}` (`s < n_τ`) extend to a basis of the
module of coordinate tangent vectors and span a saturated submodule.

*Route.* (3) The coordinate tangent vectors `∂/∂x_i` at `0` form a basis of `Lie(𝒜/R)`; the frame at `τ` is a basis of `Lie_τ`
(4.8b); the eigenvector condition is stable under division by a nonzero scalar, so `Lie_τ` is saturated; a linearly independent
family with saturated span in a free module over a discrete valuation ring extends to a basis (the saturated span is a direct
summand, PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology §RD.0.30 (b)). (2) Two integral charts of `y`
are coordinates on the formal completion of the same model, related by an invertible change of coordinates with coefficients
in `R` whose linear part carries the frame to itself (the frame is part of `y`) and which carries the group law and the action of
one chart to those of the other; the invariant derivations along the frame and the power series with coefficients in `R` are
intrinsic, so the depth does not change once the two polarisation series are compared. Not given here: the comparison of the
polarisation series of two charts (that their difference, after the change of coordinates, has an exponential with
coefficients in `R`) and with it the invariance of the depth. (1) Formal coordinates of the smooth model along the unit section
give the group law and the action with coefficients in `R`. *The polarisation series.* Let `s` be a section of `Λ(L)` over
`𝒜̂ × 𝒜̂` with coefficients in `R` that is the rigidification on `𝒜̂ × 0` and on `0 × 𝒜̂`. In `s` the two partial group laws of
the biextension and its symmetry `Λ(L)_{(x,x′)} ≅ Λ(L)_{(x′,x)}` are multiplications by units `e₁(x, x′; z)`, `e₂(x; z, z′)`,
`e₃(x, x′)` with coefficients in `R`, equal to `1` when an argument is `0`. Two classical facts over a `ℚ`-algebra `Q` are
used, both proved here. (L) *Logarithm:* a commutative formal group law `F` of dimension `g` over `Q` has a unique `g`-tuple `lg`
of series without constant term, with identity linear part and `lg(x +_F x′) = lg(x) + lg(x′)`, and the series `φ` over `Q` with
`φ(x +_F x′) = φ(x) + φ(x′)` are the linear forms in `lg`. Route: the invariant derivations of a commutative law commute, so its
invariant differential forms are closed; over `Q` a closed formal 1-form is the differential of a series, and `lg` integrates
them; a series additive for `x + x′` has constant gradient, so it is linear. (Z) *Symmetric 2-cocycles* (Lazard's lemma over a
`ℚ`-algebra, in dimension `g`): a series `c(u, u′)` over `Q` with `c(u, 0) = c(0, u′) = 0`, `c(u, u′) = c(u′, u)` and
`c(u, u′) + c(u + u′, u″) = c(u, u′ + u″) + c(u′, u″)` is `ψ(u) + ψ(u′) − ψ(u + u′)` for a series `ψ` over `Q`. Route:
`(u, t)·(u′, t′) := (u + u′, t + t′ + c(u, u′))` is a commutative formal group law of dimension `g + 1` over `Q`; the last
component of its logarithm (L) has derivative `1` in `t` (differentiate its additivity in `t′` and put `u′ = 0`, `t′ = 0`), so
it is `t + ψ(u)`, and its additivity gives `c`. Over `K_R` let `lg` be the logarithm (L) of the formal group law of `𝒜̂`; by
(Z) in the coordinates `u = lg(x)`, its normalised symmetric 2-cocycles with coefficients in `K_R[[z]]` are coboundaries:
`log e₁` is the coboundary in
`(x, x′)` of a series `h₁(x, z)`; the compatibility of the two partial laws makes `log e₂` minus the coboundary of `h₁` in
`(z, z′)` additive in `x`, hence the coboundary in `(z, z′)` of a series linear in `lg(x)`; adding that series to `h₁` gives `h`
whose first two series of 4.8b are `e₁` and `e₂`, and `h(x, 0) = h(0, x′) = 0` because `e₁`, `e₂` are `1` when an argument is
`0`. A bilinear form in `lg(x)`, `lg(x′)` may be added to `h` without changing these. The symmetry is an involution of
biextensions, so `exp(h(x′, x) − h(x, x′))·e₃(x, x′) = exp B(x, x′)` with `B` such a form, alternating; `h + B/2` has third
series `e₃^{−1}`. For `a ∈ 𝒪_E` an isomorphism `(i(a) × 1)^*Λ(L) ≅ (1 × i(ā))^*Λ(L)` normalised by the rigidification is unique
(the global units of `𝒜 × 𝒜` are those of `R`), so it is the isomorphism of biextensions given by `Λ(L)_{K_R} = (1 × λ)^*P` and
`i(a)^∨λ = λi(ā)` (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli A2.3); these are additive and multiplicative in `a`, and the symmetry
exchanges those of `a` and `ā`. In `s` it is a unit `e_a(x, x′)` with coefficients in `R`, and
`exp(h([a]x, x′) − h(x, [ā]x′)) = e_a·exp B_a` with `B_a` bilinear in `lg`, additive in `a`, and
`B_{ab}(x, x′) = B_a([b]x, x′) + B_b(x, [ā]x′)`. On the `τ`- and `τ′`-parts of `Lie(A)` this makes `a ↦ B_a` satisfy
`B_{ab} = τ(b)B_a + τ̄′(a)B_b`: for `τ′ = τ̄` it is a derivation, zero because `E/ℚ` is separable; otherwise, comparing `B_{aa₀}`
and `B_{a₀a}` for an `a₀` with `τ(a₀) ≠ τ̄′(a₀)`, it is `(τ(a) − τ̄′(a))·Q`. The exchange of `a` and `ā` by the symmetry makes the
form `Q` assembled from these parts symmetric, and `h − Q` has fourth series `e_a` and the same first three. The hypothesis on
the trace gives `w₀`.

*Check.* At a prime unramified in `E`, (3) holds for the whole frame at once (4.8b); at a ramified prime it holds embedding by
embedding only (4.8b, Check).

### 4.8c The unramified colength bound

*Origin:* particular to this proof: at a prime `p ≠ 2` unramified in `E`, the differentials of finitely many characters of
the formal group of a principally polarised framed point span the dual of each eigenspace `Lie_{τ_s}` up to a colength
bounded by `|p|^{S(τ_s)}`, with `S(τ_s)` read off the ranks along the Frobenius orbit; it is the instance at such a point
of the bound of HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d (a), with which the nearest published result is named. The
statement for a general CM field `E` and a general PEL moduli problem of unitary type is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* the general colength bound for a semi-abelian scheme with a uniformisation and an action
unramified at `p` (HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d (a)) is applied to the model of the point, after its
uniformisation, its eigen-bases of the Lie algebra and the place cut out by the Frobenius orbit are supplied.
[FormalProof] states the result for the moduli problems of the fields `E` of 4.8b, at an algebraic integral framed point
with an integral chart, for `p ≠ 2` with `p ∤ disc(E)` and an injective Frobenius-ordered family of embeddings.

Let `y` be an algebraic integral framed point of `M` over `R` (4.8b) whose polarisation `λ` is principal, above a
prime `p ≠ 2` unramified in `E`, `O` the `p`-adically complete valuation ring of an algebraically closed valued field `C`
with a local homomorphism `R → O` (so the embeddings are maps `𝒪_E → O`), and `|·|` the absolute value of `C`. A *character* of `𝒜̂` over `O` is a homomorphism of formal groups `𝒜̂_O → Ĝ_m` (in coordinates in which the
group law is a power series `x +_F x′`: a power series `χ` over `O` with `χ(0) = 1` and `χ(x +_F x′) = χ(x)χ(x′)`); its differential
`dlog χ` at the origin is a linear form on `Lie(𝒜/R) ⊗ O`. Let `w` be a place of `E` above `p`, `τ_1, …, τ_{n_o}` the embeddings of
`E` at `w` in Frobenius order (`τ_{s+1} = Frob∘τ_s`, indices modulo `n_o`), and `r(τ) := n_τ`. Then for every `s` there are finitely
many characters `χ_1, …, χ_M` of `𝒜̂` over `O` such that the restrictions of the `dlog χ_b` to `Lie_{τ_s} ⊗ O` span a submodule `L_s`
of `Hom(Lie_{τ_s} ⊗ O, O)` with `Hom(Lie_{τ_s} ⊗ O, O)/L_s ≅ ⊕_iO/(c_i)` and `∏_i|c_i| ≥ |p|^{S(τ_s)}`,
`S(τ_s) := Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}·r(τ̄_{s+i+1})`. (In particular no `c_i` is `0`: `L_s` has full rank.)

*Route.* The instance of HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d (a) for the model `𝒜` of `y`, the order `𝒪_E` with complex
conjugation and `r(τ) = n_τ`: the embeddings `𝒪_E → R` of 4.8b, composed with `R → O`, are the embeddings `𝒪_E → O`, compatibly
with conjugation; a Frobenius-ordered cycle of pairwise distinct embeddings is the set of embeddings at one place above `p` (`p`
is unramified); the Rosati involution of the polarisation `λ = φ_{L_{K_R}}` is the conjugation (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli
§A3.9 with `i(ā)^∨λ = λi(a)`; 4.8b: `(i(a) × 1)^*Λ(L) ≅ (1 × i(ā))^*Λ(L)`); and the frame vectors at `τ` form a basis of
`Lie_τ ⊗ O` (4.8b). HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d (a) asks for a uniformisation of the semi-abelian scheme
(degeneration data over `R` exist by NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §§3.13, 3.14 and ShimuraCompactifications §6.6,
whose setting (6-a) specialises to a complete discrete valuation ring; their compatibility with the action of `𝒪_E` is not given
here); for an algebraic point the model has one after a finite extension of `R` inside `O`,
which changes neither the eigenspaces nor the colengths (computed in `O`); this passage is not given here. §0.5d (a) also asks
that the polarisation of the abelian part `B` of the uniformisation be principal; that a principal `λ` gives a principal
polarisation of `B` is not given here. When `𝒜` is an abelian scheme no uniformisation is needed (the abelian case of
HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d (a)), `B = 𝒜` is principally polarised by `λ`, and "algebraic" is not used.

*Checks.* For `E_w = ℚ_{p²}` and a one-dimensional formal group of height two (ranks `1` and `0` at the two embeddings of `w`, `0`
and `1` at their conjugates), `S = 1/p + 1/p³ + ⋯ = γ_LT(p)`, the valuation of the period of the Lubin–Tate character. For an
ordinary point (`r(τ̄) = 0` at the embeddings that follow `τ_s`), `S(τ_s) = 0`: the characters of the multiplicative part span.
`S(τ_s) ≤ max_τr(τ̄)/(p − 1)`, the bound that the Hodge–Tate cokernel gives (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5: the cokernel
is killed by `p^{1/(p−1)}`).

*Needs:* 4.8b; HodgeTateLatticesAndLubinTateLogarithms §0.5d; AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §A3.9;
NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §§3.13, 3.14; ShimuraCompactifications §6.6.

### 4.18 The depth of a weight and flags of characters

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the depth of a weight at a framed point is bounded by the valuations of the leading
principal minors of the pairings of the frame with the differentials of a flag of characters of the formal group (a); the
selection of the flag (b) and the minors on saturated families (c) are elementary linear algebra over a valuation ring. The
products clause of (b) is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* a product of characters, one in each copy, is an eigenfunction of the invariant derivations,
so the jet of the highest weight product of leading minors against it is that product evaluated at the matrix of the
pairings `⟨u, dlog χ⟩`; the polarisation factor is removed modulo a small ideal by pulling back under endomorphisms `a` with
`ā·a = N` small and `a ≡ 1` on the frame; flags are selected by induction on the number of rows, taking each time a column
whose new coordinate has the largest absolute value.

*The depth of a weight at a point is at most what characters of the formal group allow.*
(a) Let `y` be an integral framed point over `R` with an integral chart `𝔠` (4.8b), and `O` the `p`-adically complete valuation
ring of an algebraically closed valued field `C` with a local homomorphism `R → O`, with the embeddings `τ : 𝒪_E → O`. Assume:
(U1) for every `τ` some `τ(a₀) − τ(ā₀)`, `a₀ ∈ 𝒪_E`, is a unit of `O`; (U2) for every nonzero `x ∈ O` there are a natural number `N`
with `|N| < |x|` and, for each `τ`, an `a ∈ 𝒪_E` with `ā·a = N` and `|τ(a) − 1| < |x|`; (U3) `κ` is a weight as in 4.8b and `n` is at
least the number of frame vectors. If for every `τ` there are characters `χ_1, …, χ_{n_τ}` of `𝒜̂` over `O` (4.8c) such that every
leading principal minor of the matrix `(⟨u_{τ,s}, dlog χ_b⟩)_{s,b}` has absolute value `≥ |p|^{len(τ)}`, with `len(τ) ≥ 0`, then,
for every multiplier `m_L ≥ 1`, `dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n, m_L) ≤ v_R(p)·Σ_τlen(τ)·κ_{τ,0}`.
(b) *Flags from colengths.* Let `O` be a valuation ring and `M` an `s × N` matrix over `O` (`N` finite) whose columns span a submodule of
`O^s` containing `c·O^s` for some `c ≠ 0`. Then there are `s` distinct columns, in some order, such that for every `i ≤ s` the
first `i` of them, truncated to the first `i` rows, span the same module as all columns truncated to the first `i` rows.
Consequently their leading principal minors `δ_1, …, δ_s` satisfy `δ_i | δ_{i+1}`, and `|δ_s|` is the colength of the column span
(the product of the absolute values of its elementary divisors); in particular `|δ_i| ≥ |δ_s|` for all `i`. Applied to
characters: if the differentials of finitely many characters of a formal group over `O`, paired with vectors `u_1, …, u_s` in
this order, span a module of colength `x ≠ 0`, then `s` of the characters form a flag, every leading principal minor of
`(⟨u_a, dlog χ_b⟩)` having absolute value `≥ x`. With (a): under (U1)–(U3), if for every `τ` the differentials of the
characters restricted to the frame at `τ` span a module of colength `x_τ` with `x_τ ≥ |p|^{len(τ)}` (absolute values), then
`dep_y(κ; 𝔠, n) ≤ v_R(p)·Σ_τlen(τ)·κ_{τ,0}`. *Products.* If the colengths are known only through their product over a set `T` of
embeddings, `∏_{τ∈T}x_τ ≥ |p|^{Λ}`, the statement applies with the actual exponents `len(τ)` (`x_τ = |p|^{len(τ)}`, `Σ_{τ∈T}len(τ) ≤ Λ`),
and their part of the bound is `Σ_{τ∈T}len(τ)·κ_{τ,0} ≤ Λ·max_{τ∈T}κ_{τ,0}`; the mean exponent `Λ/#T` may not be put in for every `τ`
(Check).
(c) *Determinants on saturated families.* Let `α` be an `r × N` matrix over a valuation ring `O` whose cokernel has colength
`x ≠ 0`, and `S` an `n × r` matrix over `O` with a right inverse over `O`. Then some `n × n` minor of `S·α` has absolute value
`≥ x`. Applied to characters at a prime `p ≠ 2` unramified in `E`, at an algebraic integral framed point whose polarisation `λ` is
principal, with `τ_1, …, τ_{n_o}` and
`S(τ_s)` as in 4.8c: every family of `n` vectors `w_1, …, w_n` of the `τ_s`-part of the frame which is part of an integral basis of
it has `n` characters `χ_ρ` with `v(det(⟨w_q, dlog χ_ρ⟩)) ≤ S(τ_s)`. *One minor:* for one leading principal minor `Δ` of the frame at
`τ_s` and `k ≥ 0`, the *factor depth* `dep^{fac}_y(Δ^k) := inf_f v(Δ^k(D)(f)(0))`, the infimum over the power series `f` with
coefficients in `O` in the copies of the coordinates (products `∏_bf_b(x_b)` suffice for the upper bound; no polarisation factor),
is at most `k·S(τ_s)` (with `v(p) = 1`). If the ranks
`r(τ̄_{s+i})` are constant equal to `B`, then `S(τ_s) = B/(p − 1)`; if they alternate between `A` (`i` odd) and `B` (`i` even), then
`S(τ_s) = (pA + B)/(p² − 1) = B/(p − 1) + (A − B)·γ_LT(p)`.

*Route.* (a) Base change of the chart to `O` gives a formal biextension in canonical gauge for the polarisation series; for a
product `f` of characters in separate copies of the variables, corrected by the biextension, and invariant derivations,
`P(D)f = P(pairings)·f` (characters produce no factorials); for `P` the highest weight vector — a product of powers of leading
principal minors whose exponents (the gaps of the weight) sum to at most `κ_{τ,0}`, harmonic because the minors at `τ` and at
`τ̄` are taken in disjoint copies — the value at the pairing matrix is a product of leading principal minors, of valuation
`≤ Σ_τlen(τ)·κ_{τ,0}` (here `len(τ) ≥ 0` is used: `|p|^{len} ≤ 1`); (U1), (U2) let one replace the characters by germs built from
translates under endomorphisms `a` with `āa = N` small and `a ≡ 1` on the frame vectors, so that modulo the ideal `{|·| < |x|}`
the polarisation factor disappears; a jet over `O` is an `O`-combination of jets of `R`-integral data (the jet is `O`-bilinear in
`P` and in the finitely many coefficients of `f` it involves, and the `O`-lattice of harmonic polynomials is the base change of
the `R`-lattice, which is saturated), so it bounds the infimum over `R`. Not given here: the base change of the chart to a formal
biextension in canonical gauge, and the existence of the integral germ from (U1), (U2). (b) Induction on `i`: write the truncation
to `i + 1` rows of each column as a combination of the `i` selected columns plus a multiple `μ_b` of the last basis vector, and
select a column with `|μ_b|` largest; in a valuation ring the other `μ_b` are multiples of it, and it is a new column because `i`
columns cannot span a module containing `c·O^{i+1}`. Equal spans give the divisibility of the minors, and the last minor
generates the determinant ideal of the span, the zeroth Fitting ideal of its cokernel, which for a cokernel `⊕_iO/(c_i)` is
`(∏_ic_i)` (AInfCohomology §AI.1.3). Then (a) with the flag. (c) The cokernel of `Sα` is a quotient of the cokernel of
`α`, so the ideal generated by the `n × n` minors of `Sα` contains an element of absolute value at least the colength of `α`; a
finitely generated ideal of a valuation ring is generated by one of its generators; the colength is that of 4.8c. *One minor:*
4.8c gives the characters, (b) a flag with every leading minor of absolute value `≥ |p|^{S(τ_s)}`, and for a product of characters,
one in each copy, `Δ^k(D)(∏_bχ_b(x_b))(0)` is the `k`-th power of a leading minor of the pairing matrix (characters produce no
factorials); the two values of `S` are geometric series.

*Checks.* For `E_w = ℚ_{p²}` and a one-dimensional formal group of height two, `S = γ_LT(p)` (4.8c, Check): the depth bound of (a)
with `len = S` charges the whole first entry of the weight at the rate `γ_LT`, without the flow term of the programme of 4.6;
that is why the bounds along Frobenius orbits are 4.19–4.19f, not (a). (U1) fails at a place of `E⁺` above `p` ramified in `E`,
where `a ≡ ā` modulo the prime for every `a ∈ 𝒪_E`; (U2) fails at a place of `E⁺` inert in `E` (`āa = N` with `|N| < |x| ≤ 1` forces
`a` into the unique prime, so `|τ(a) − 1| = 1`). In (b) with `s = 1` the selected column is one with the largest entry; in (c)
with `n = r` and `S = 1` the minor is the determinant of the span. In the product form of (b), with `T = {τ, τ′}`, `Λ = 2B` and
exponents `(2B, 0)`: assigning `B` to each embedding would bound the part by `B(κ_{τ,0} + κ_{τ′,0})`, which is false when

of a single capacity is `γ_LT·D`, 4.6), so the rate `S(τ)` of the one-minor clause is the right one there. The factor depth has
no polarisation factor; the same bound for the depth of 4.8b, for powers of minors on isotropic combinations of the copies, is

### 4.19 Row data, blocks and the block depth

*Origin:* definitions and conventions.

*Definitions.* Let `ℓ ≠ 2` be a prime, `C` an
algebraically closed field of characteristic `0` with a real valuation `v`, `v(ℓ) = 1`, `O` its valuation ring, `𝔪` its maximal
ideal, and `n_o, n ≥ 1`, `j, j′ ≥ 0` integers. A *row datum* of period `n_o` over `(C, v)` consists of: for each *side*
`g ∈ {0, 1}` finite sets `I_g` (*tangent directions*) and `I^∨_g` (*dual directions*) with *types* in `ℤ/n_o`, each position `(g, a)`
being *big* or not, `(1 − g, a)` big exactly when `(g, a)` is not, with `#I_{g,a} = n + j` at the big positions and `j′` at the
others and `#I^∨_{g,a} = #I_{1−g,a}`; and
(RD1) for each side a module of *typical curves*, sequences `(x_s)_{s≥0}` of logarithm coefficients `x_s ∈ C^{I_g ⊔ I^∨_g}` (`x_0` the
*tangent*), stable under `t ↦ ct` (`c ∈ O`), the Verschiebung `V`, the Frobenius `F` and infinite sums, whose first nonzero
coefficient is integral, containing the constant curves along `I^∨_g` with integral coefficients and, for every type `a` and every
integral tangent vector of type `a`, a *pure* curve with that tangent whose `s`-th coefficient has type `a + s`. A *system of curves*
`(c)` with unimodular tangent matrix gives coordinates `t_c` with `X = Σ_{c,s}α_c(s)·t_c^{ℓ^s}` on the logarithm coordinates
(`α_c(s)`, `β_c(s)` the `I`- and `I^∨`-parts of the `s`-th coefficient of `c`); the *integral germs* are the integral power series in
the `t_c`, read as series in `X`, and `ζ_κ := −Σ_{c, s≥1}β_c(s)_κ·t_c^{ℓ^s}` (`κ ∈ I^∨_g`) are the *quasi-logarithms*;
(RD2) an integral pairing `pair : I^∨_g × I_{1−g} → O` respecting types, with an integral right inverse on the `I^∨` side;
(RD3) power series `h_{g,a,a′} ∈ C[[X, X′]]` in the variables of type `a` of side `g` and of type `a′` of the other side (the
*polarisation series*), with `h_{1,a′,a} = h_{0,a,a′}` and no monomial in the variables of one side only (*isotropy*);
(RD4) for `a′ − a` odd and `k` a direction of type `a′` of the other side, `∂h_{g,a,a′}/∂X′_k − Σ_κpair(κ, k)·ζ_κ` is an integral germ in
these variables;
(RD5) for a big `(g, a)` and *good* curves of type `a` (pure curves whose coefficient at `t^ℓ` has no tangent component): if `ℓ` times
the `I^∨`-part of the coefficient at `t^ℓ` of such a curve is `≡ 0 (mod 𝔪)`, its tangent is `≡ 0 (mod 𝔪)`; and if `N` good curves have
tangents independent modulo `𝔪` and `ℓ` times the tangent parts of their coefficients at `t^{ℓ²}` are `≡ 0 (mod 𝔪)`, then `N ≤ j′`;
(RD6) the ring of integral germs does not depend on the system of curves.
(RD4) and (RD5) are required of alternating data only; for a datum that is not alternating they are empty.
*Patterns.* The datum is *alternating* if `n_o` is even and `(g, a)` is big exactly when `g ≡ a (mod 2)`; *alternating with
identification* if moreover `n_o = 2n_c` with `n_c` odd and the position `(0, s)` is identified with `(1, s + n_c)` (they carry the
same variables); *constant* if every position of side `0` is big. Put `j_t := j + j′`, `γ := γ_LT(ℓ)`,
`E(ℓ, i) := 1 + (ℓ^i − ℓ)/(ℓ² − 1)` (an integer for `i` odd). A family of vectors is *saturated* if it is part of an integral basis.
*Blocks.* Blocks live in copies `x_ρ` of the variables, with the invariant derivations `D`: `Δ_{g,a}` is the `n × n` minor on `n`
frame vectors of type `a` of side `g` in `n` copies, and the *value* of a product `P` of minors against integral germs `f_ρ` is
`v(P(D)(∏_ρf_ρ(x_ρ)e^{h(x_ρ)})(0))`, `h` the sum of the polarisation series. A *bi-block* is `Δ_{g,a}^{ℓ^iy}Δ_{1−g,a+i}^{y}` (`(g, a)` big,
`i` odd `< n_o`), a *character block* `Δ_{g,a}^u`, a *monomial block* a product of powers of leading minors of one side. The
*character rate* is `ϱ_ch := nγ + j_t/(ℓ − 1)` for an alternating datum and `ϱ_ch := j_t/(ℓ − 1)` for a constant one, and
(RD7) is the property: for every big `(g, a)` and `u ≥ 0` there are characters `χ_1, …, χ_n` (integral germs of the form
`exp(linear form in X)`) with `v(Δ_{g,a}(D)^u(∏_ρχ_ρ(x_ρ))(0)) ≤ u·ϱ_ch`.
*Plans and the block depth.* For a dominant weight let `W(a)` be its gap at the index `n` at the big position of `a`, and `R_w` its
*remaining degree* on the orbit: the sum, over the two sides, the positions of the orbit and the indices `k′ ≥ 1`, of `k′` times the
gap of the weight at the index `k′`, the index `n` at the big position of each `a` excepted (so the degree of the weight on the orbit
is `n·Σ_aW(a) + R_w`). For an alternating datum a *plan* is a family of integers `y(a, i) ≥ 0` (`i` odd `< n_o`), `u(a) ≥ 0`
with `W(a) = Σ_iℓ^iy(a, i) + Σ_iy(a − i, i) + u(a)` for every `a`; for a constant datum a plan has `y = 0`. Its blocks are the
bi-blocks for the nonzero `y(a, i)`, the character blocks `Δ_{g,a}^{u(a)}` and monomial blocks for the remaining degree, in disjoint
copies, and its *value bound* is
`Σ_{(a,i) : y(a,i) ≠ 0}[n·y(a, i)((ℓ^i + 1)γ − 1) + n(log_ℓn + 1) + (i + 1)j_t(ℓ^i + 1)y(a, i)/(ℓ − 1)] + Σ_au(a)·ϱ_ch + R_w/(ℓ − 1)`
(the fixed cost `n(log_ℓn + 1)` is charged only for the bi-blocks that occur). For big `(g, a)`, odd `i < n_o` and `y ≥ 0`, the
bi-block of `(a, i, y)` *attains its cost* if there are integral germs `f_1, …, f_n` with
`v(Δ_{g,a}(D)^{ℓ^iy}Δ_{1−g,a+i}(D)^{y}(∏_ρf_ρ(x_ρ)e^{h_{g,a,a+i}(x_ρ)})(0)) ≤ n·y·((ℓ^i + 1)γ − 1) + n(log_ℓn + 1) + (i + 1)j_t(ℓ^i + 1)y/(ℓ − 1)`;
`𝒢(a, i)` denotes the set of these `y`, to which `0` is added. A plan is *admissible at level `N`* if `y(a, i) = 0` for `i ≥ N` and
every nonzero `y(a, i)` lies in `𝒢(a, i)`. The *block depth at level `N`*, `ν_bl(N)`, of the weight is the least value bound over the
plans admissible at level `N` (the plan `y = 0` is admissible at every level, and there are finitely many plans); it does not increase
with `N`, and it is this roadmap's own packaging, not an object of [FormalProof], which works with the plans themselves. For several
orbits, one row datum each, the block depth at level `N` is the sum over the orbits. A family of
sets `G(a, i)` of nonnegative integers *has windows of length `S`* if `0 ∈ G(a, i)` and every interval `[Y₀, Y₀ + S)` meets `G(a, i)`.

*Checks.* `n_o = 2`, `w` constant: the relaxed plan `y = W/(ℓ + 1)` at `i = 1` in both directions (a flow, not a plan unless
`ℓ + 1` divides `W`; for `W = 1` it is fractional) realises the flow of 4.6 (c); the trivial
plan `y = 0` has value bound `≈ nγΣW`, the bound of 4.18 with `len = S`. For the Lubin–Tate group of height two (`n = 1`,
`j = j′ = 0`, `n_o = 2`) and the cocycle `G_𝔠` of 4.4, `∂_yA(x, y)` is integral and `∂_yA(y, x) ≡ ℓ_𝔰′(y)·g₀(x)` modulo integral series, so
(RD4) holds with `ζ = g₀`.

### 4.19a Main term of the polarisation series

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a normal form for the polarisation series of an alternating row datum (4.19), whose
main coefficient has valuation `−E(ℓ, i)`, showing that the bound of 4.3 at the bi-power monomial `ℓ^ie_s + e_{s+i}` is
attained on a subspace of corank at most `(i + 1)j′ + j`.

*Method of the formal proof:* curves adapted to chains are taken from `U`; a rank and nullity statement modulo `𝔪` for
maps semilinear for `x ↦ x^{ℓ²}`, proved by extracting roots of the coefficients of relations in the algebraically closed
field, bounds along the chain and then against `U′` the number of main vectors lost; a unimodular change of `U′` makes the
main block `−1`; and the remainder is bounded by integrating in `X′` its derivative, which is an integral germ plus the
pairing times the remainder of the quasi-logarithms.

For an alternating row datum
(4.19), `ℓ ≥ 5`, `j_t < n`, a big `(g, a)`, an odd `i < n_o`, and saturated `n`-families `U` at `(g, a)`, `U′` at `(1 − g, a + i)`:
there are `n₀ ≥ n − (i + 1)j′ − j`, a saturated family `e_1, …, e_{n₀}` in the integral span of `U`, and a family `v′_1, …, v′_n` with the
same integral span as `U′`, such that
`h_{g,a,a+i}(Σ_cX_ce_c, Σ_bX′_bv′_b) = −ℓ^{−E(ℓ,i)}·Σ_{c≤n₀}X_c^{ℓ^i}X′_c + ℓ^{−E(ℓ,i)}Σ_{b>n₀,c}φ_{b,c}X_c^{ℓ^i}X′_b + R`, `φ_{b,c}` integral, every
monomial of `R` containing some `X′_b`, and the coefficients `c_μ` of `R` satisfying
`v(c_μ) ≥ 1/(ℓ − 1) − deg_X(μ)(ρ_LT + 3/(8ℓ^i)) − deg_{X′}(μ)(ρ_LT + 1/2 − 1/(8ℓ^i))`. The valuation `−E(ℓ, i) = 1/(ℓ − 1) − 1 − ρ_LT(ℓ^i + 1)` is the
bound of 4.3 at the bi-power monomial `ℓ^ie_s + e_{s+i}`: this target says that the bound is attained, on a subspace of corank
`≤ (i + 1)j′ + j`.

*Route.* Five steps. (1) *Chains:* from `U`, a system of curves with at least `n − ((i + 3)/2)j′` base curves whose tangents are
integral combinations of `U`, whose coefficient at `t^ℓ` has no tangent component, and which continue in chains of length `(i + 1)/2`
(the coefficient at `t^{ℓ²}` of a curve of level `s` is `ℓ^{−1}(g + ℓλ)`, `g` an integral matrix into the tangents of level `s + 1`, of types
`a, a + 2, …`, all big); each level costs the tangent rank `j′` of the small type in between. (2) *Main vectors:* for a base curve `c`,
`𝔳(c) ∈ O^{I^∨}` is the normalised coefficient at `X_c^{ℓ^i}` of the quasi-logarithms of type `a + i`; by induction along the chain,
with a rank–nullity statement modulo `𝔪` for a map semilinear for `x ↦ x^{ℓ²}` (`C` algebraically closed), the kernel of one step
having rank `≤ j′` (the second clause of (RD5)) and the first clause of (RD5) at the last step, all but `((i − 1)/2)j′` of the base
curves have main vectors independent modulo `𝔪`. (3) *Pairing with `U′`:* the matrix `(Σ_{κ,k}𝔳(c)_κ·pair(κ, k)·U′_{b,k})` loses
at most `j` more (`U′` is saturated of rank `n` in the tangent space of type `a + i` of the other side, of rank `n + j`, and the
pairing with `I^∨_g` is perfect there, (RD2)): `n₀` columns stay independent modulo `𝔪`, and a unimodular change `v′ = Y·U′` makes the
main block `−1`; the total defect is `((i + 3)/2 + (i − 1)/2)j′ + j = (i + 1)j′ + j`. (4) *Main term of the quasi-logarithm:* for `κ` of
type `a + i`, `ζ_κ(Σ_cX_ce_c) − ℓ^{−E}Σ_c𝔳(c)_κ·X_c^{ℓ^i}` has coefficients of valuation `≥ −deg·(ρ_LT + 3/(8ℓ^i))` (inversion of the
logarithm along the chains by a contraction in a weighted norm; on the height-two model `t = X − X^{ℓ²}/ℓ + ⋯` has valuation
`−(ℓ^{2s} − 1)/(ℓ² − 1)` at `X^{ℓ^{2s}}`, and `ζ = t^ℓ/ℓ + ⋯` has valuation `−E(ℓ, 2s + 1)` at `X^{ℓ^{2s+1}}`). (5) *Remainder:* by (RD4),
`∂/∂X′_b` of `h −` (main term) is an integral germ plus `Σ_κ(pair·v′_b)_κ` times the remainder of (4); the coordinates `t(X)` have weight
`≥ 0` at the radius `ρ_LT + 3/(8ℓ^i)`, those of the other side at `1/(ℓ − 1)`, so the derivative has weight `≥ 0` at the radii
`(ρ_LT + 3/(8ℓ^i), 1/(ℓ − 1))`; every monomial contains an `X′`, and integration in `X′` gives weight `≥ 1/(ℓ − 1)` at the radii
`(ρ_LT + 3/(8ℓ^i), ρ_LT + 1/2 − 1/(8ℓ^i))`. The integration needs `N·(1/2 − γ − 1/(8ℓ^i)) ≥ v_ℓ(N)` for every `N ≥ 1`: true for `ℓ ≥ 5`, false for
`ℓ = 3`, `N = 3` (`3·(1/2 − 3/8 − 1/24) = 1/4 < 1`): this is where `ℓ ≥ 5` is used. Steps (3)–(5) and the assembly are proved; the
existence of the chains (1), the rank lemma for one step and the passage at the last step in (2), the pairing lemma in (3) and the
main term (4) are not proved here.

*Checks.* For the Lubin–Tate group of height two and the cocycle `G_𝔠` of 4.4 (4.19, Check), the coefficient of `X^ℓY` in
logarithm coordinates is `−𝔰σ(𝔠)/ℓ`, of valuation `−1 = −E(ℓ, 1)` when `𝔠` is a unit; for the Serre tensor of that group with
`W(𝔽_{ℓ⁴})` (`n_o = 4`, tangent ranks `1, 0, 1, 0`) the quasi-logarithm of type `3` restricted to the line of type `0` is
`t^{ℓ³}/ℓ² − (t^{ℓ²}/ℓ)^ℓ/ℓ = −ℓ^{−(ℓ+1)}X^{ℓ³}(1 + ⋯)`, and `E(ℓ, 3) = 1 + ℓ`. The statement is special to alternating ranks: for the
Lubin–Tate group of height `n_o` (ranks `1, 0, …, 0`) the quasi-logarithm of type `j` begins with `X^{ℓ^j}/ℓ`, exponent `1` at every level.

*Sources:* (quasi-logarithms and the universal vector extension, Katz,
Mazur–Messing; biextensions of formal groups, Mumford). *Needs:* 4.1, 4.3, 4.19.

### 4.19b The capacity jet

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the capacity jet `𝒥(y)` written, by Cayley's identity, as an explicit factor times
`Σ_s (y choose s)·a_s` with bounds for the `a_s`, and a finite-difference argument giving in every interval of length
`S_int` a `y` at which `𝒥(y)` has small valuation.

*Method of the formal proof:* a shift identity moves the exponential of the bilinear term into the operator;
`det(X^{(L)} − ℓ^E·Y)` is expanded binomially around `det(X)^L` with valuation bounds for the terms; Cayley's identity for
`det(∂)` on powers of the determinant gives `𝒥(y)` as a constant times `Σ_s (y choose s)·a_s`; and iterated forward
differences show that this sum cannot have positive valuation at `S_int` consecutive integers, since it would then have
positive valuation at `0`, where it is `1`.

Let `C` be any field of characteristic `0` with a real valuation,
`v(ℓ) = 1`, `ℓ ≥ 5`, `i` odd, `L := ℓ^i`, `E := E(ℓ, i)` (4.19), `n₀ ≥ 1`, `X, Y` two `n₀ × n₀` matrices of variables, and `h₁ ∈ C[[X, Y]]`
with every monomial containing a `Y`-variable and
`v(coeff_μh₁) ≥ 1/(ℓ − 1) − deg_X(μ)(ρ_LT + 3/(8L)) − deg_Y(μ)(ρ_LT + 1/2 − 1/(8L))`. Put
`𝒥(y) := [det(∂_X)^{Ly}·det(∂_Y)^{y}·exp(−ℓ^{−E}Σ_{b,c}X_{bc}^LY_{bc} + h₁)](0)`. Then there is a sequence `(a_s)_{s≥0}`, independent of `y`,
with `a_0 = 1`, `𝒥(y) = (−ℓ^{−E})^{n₀y}·Cay(n₀, Ly)·Σ_{s≤y}(y choose s)·a_s` for every `y ≥ 0`, `Cay(n₀, N) := ∏_{t=1}^{N}∏_{c<n₀}(t + c)`, and
`v(a_s) ≥ s/8 − n₀²(3log_ℓn₀ + 2log_ℓs + i + 3)` for `s ≥ 1`; and every interval `[Y₀, Y₀ + S_int)` of integers,
`S_int = S_int(ℓ, i, n₀) := ⌈64n₀²(i + 3 + 3log_ℓ(n₀ + 1))⌉`, contains a `y` with `v(𝒥(y)) ≤ n₀·y·((L + 1)γ − 1) + n₀(log_ℓn₀ + 1)`.

*Route.* (1) *Shift:* `[F(∂_X)G(∂_Y)](e^{Σ_jζ_j(X)Y_j}g)(0) = [F(∂_X)·G(ζ(X) + ∂_Y)g]|_{Y=0}(0)`, here with
`ζ_{bc} = −ℓ^{−E}X_{bc}^L`, `G = det^y`, `g = exp(h₁)`. (2) `det(−ℓ^{−E}X^{(L)} + Y) = (−ℓ^{−E})^{n₀}(det(X)^L + ψ)`, `X^{(L)} := (X_{bc}^L)`,
`ψ := det(X^{(L)} − ℓ^EY) − det(X)^L ∈ ℤ[X, Y]`; so `𝒥(y) = (−ℓ^{−E})^{n₀y}Σ_{s≤y}(y choose s)⟨det^{Ly}, det^{L(y−s)}Q_s⟩`, `⟨F, R⟩ := F(∂)R(0)` the
apolar pairing and `Q_s` the part of degree `n₀Ls` in `X` of `ψ^s` applied to `g` (a monomial `X^{m₁}Y^{m₂}` acting as
`X^{m₁}·(∂_Y^{m₂}g)(X, 0)`), `Q_0 = 1`. (3) A monomial of `ψ^s` of `Y`-degree `K` has `K ≤ n₀s`, `X`-degree `n₀Ls − LK`, and coefficient
divisible by `ℓ^{EK + (s−K)₊}`: a factor of `Y`-degree `k ≥ 1` brings `ℓ^{Ek}`, and a factor of `Y`-degree `0` is
`det(X^{(L)}) − det(X)^L ≡ 0 (mod ℓ)` (Frobenius; `L` a power of `ℓ`). (4) `exp(h₁)` has weight `≥ 0` at the radii above (4.1 (c)); with
`(ρ_LT + 3/(8L))·LK + (ρ_LT + 1/2 − 1/(8L))·K = EK + K/(ℓ − 1) − K/8 − K/(8L)` and Legendre's formula for the factorial of the `Y`-exponent,
every coefficient of `Q_s` has valuation `≥ s/8 − n₀²(log_ℓ(n₀s) + 1)`. (5) *Cayley:* `det(∂)^a det^{a+b} = (Cay(n₀, a + b)/Cay(n₀, b))·det^b`
and the symmetry of the pairing give `⟨det^{Ly}, det^{L(y−s)}Q⟩·Cay(n₀, Ls) = Cay(n₀, Ly)·⟨det^{Ls}, Q⟩`, so `a_s = ⟨det^{Ls}, Q_s⟩/Cay(n₀, Ls)`, and
Legendre (`v(Cay(n₀, N)) ≤ n₀N/(ℓ − 1) + n₀(log_ℓn₀ + 1)`; the factorial of an exponent of degree `n₀N` in `n₀²` variables has valuation
`≥ n₀N/(ℓ − 1) − n₀²(log_ℓ(n₀N) + 1)`) gives the bound for `v(a_s)`. (6) *Window:* if `a_0 = 1` and `v(a_s) > 0` for `s ≥ S`, then
`f(y) := Σ_s(y choose s)a_s` cannot have positive valuation at `S` consecutive integers: its part of degree `< S` would have all its
finite differences of positive valuation at the first of them, hence positive valuation at every integer, hence at `0`, where it
is `a_0`. The bound of (5) is positive for `s ≥ S_int`, and `v((−ℓ^{−E})^{n₀y}Cay(n₀, Ly)) ≤ n₀y(L/(ℓ − 1) − E) + n₀(log_ℓn₀ + 1)` with
`L/(ℓ − 1) − E = (L + 1)γ − 1`. `ℓ ≥ 5` enters only through the constants.

*Checks.* The exponent identity `ℓ^i/(ℓ − 1) − E(ℓ, i) = (ℓ^i + 1)γ − 1`: for `i = 1`, `ℓ/(ℓ − 1) − 1 = 1/(ℓ − 1) = (ℓ + 1)ℓ/(ℓ² − 1) − 1`. The
statement is not a single-`y` statement: for `n₀ = 2`, `h₁ = 0`, `Σ_s(y choose s)a_s = (Ly + 1)^{−1}Σ_k(y choose k)/(Ly choose Lk) ≡ y + 1 (mod ℓ)`,
of positive valuation exactly when `y ≡ −1 (mod ℓ)`: the window is necessary.

*Sources:* (its ingredients classical: Cayley's identity, Legendre, finite differences). *Needs:* 4.1, 4.19.

### 4.19c Blocks and the value of a plan

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the three kinds of blocks of a row datum attain their costs (the bi-blocks for `y` in
windows of length `S_int`), and the value of the product of the blocks of a plan is at most its value bound, the values
adding over disjoint copies of the variables.

*Method of the formal proof:* (i) by the normal form of 4.19a, with the germ `t^{Ly}t′^{y}` in the copies beyond `n₀`, the
jet of a bi-block is `((Ly)!·y!)^{n−n₀}` times the capacity jet of 4.19b, whose window gives a good `y` in every interval;
(ii) the character bound (RD7) on the standard system of curves; (iii) monomials in the curve coordinates, where the jet is a
product of factorials bounded by Legendre's formula; (iv) the blocks of a plan are listed, each attains its bound, the
copies and the weight are counted, and the multiplicativity of jets over disjoint copies is used in the form in which the
point supplies it.

Let a row datum (4.19) with `ℓ ≥ 5`
and `j_t < n` be given; (ii), (iii) and (iv) for plans without bi-blocks (in particular for a constant datum) hold for every `ℓ ≥ 3`.
(i) *Bi-blocks* (alternating datum). The sets `𝒢(a, i)` of 4.19 have windows of length `S_int(ℓ, i, n)`.
(ii) *Character blocks.* Under (RD7), `Δ_{g,a}^u` has value `≤ u·ϱ_ch`.
(iii) *Monomial blocks.* For a product `P` of powers of leading minors of one side, of degree `δ`, there are integral germs with
`v(P(D)(∏f)(0)) ≤ δ/(ℓ − 1)`, and with value `0` when every row exponent is `< ℓ`.
(iv) *Plans.* If the weight is read on at least `n_o·n·N + 2(n + j_t)` copies of the variables, the value of the product of the
blocks of a plan admissible at level `N` (4.19), against the product of the germs of (i)–(iii) in disjoint copies, is at most its value
bound (under (RD7)); in particular the least such value is at most `ν_bl(N)`. For several orbits, one row datum each in disjoint copies, the
values add.

*Route.* (i) Complete `e_1, …, e_{n₀}` of 4.19a to a basis of the span of `U` and use `v′` (the minors change by units). In the copies
`ρ > n₀` put the germ `t_ρ^{Ly}t′_ρ^{y}`: the operator has degree `Ly` (resp. `y`) in the variables of every copy and every column, so
there only the leading monomial `X_{ρρ}^{Ly}X′_{ρρ}^{y}` contributes, the columns `> n₀` are used up, and on the first `n₀` copies the
operator is `det_{n₀}^{Ly}det_{n₀}^{y}`, against the main term and the remainder of 4.19a, which satisfies the hypothesis of 4.19b: the
value is `((Ly)!·y!)^{n−n₀}·𝒥(y)`, of valuation `≤ (n − n₀)(L + 1)y/(ℓ − 1) + v(𝒥(y))`, and `n − n₀ ≤ (i + 1)j_t`; for `n₀ = 0` only the
factorials remain; `ℓ ≥ 5` enters only here, through 4.19a and 4.19b. (ii) In copies that carry the variables of one side only the polarisation factor is `1` by isotropy (RD3), and
(RD7) bounds the jet. (iii) Monomials in coordinates dual to a saturated frame, each row of the weight in its own copy: only the
linear parts count, and the jet is a product of factorials of the row exponents (Legendre; `0` when they are `< ℓ`). (iv) Jets
multiply over disjoint copies, the operator being a product over the copies and the function `∏_ρf_ρ(x_ρ)e^{h(x_ρ)}` a product over
the copies; in a copy that carries a character or a monomial block only one side occurs, and by isotropy the exponential is `1`
there; in a copy of a bi-block only the component `h_{g,a,a+i}` occurs. The count of copies is what the blocks of a plan with levels
`< N` occupy.

*Needs:* 4.19, 4.19a, 4.19b.

### 4.19d The block depth is at most the flow programme

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a maximal flow of the programme is rounded into an admissible plan, so that `T` times
the block depth at level `i₀` of an alternating row datum is at most the flow programme `𝔉_ℓ(n·w)` plus an explicit error
and the term of the remaining degree.

*Method of the formal proof:* a maximal flow for the capacities `n·(1 − θ₀)·w/T` is truncated to the levels below `i₀` and
rounded down, entry by entry, to an admissible value in the windows, the slack going to character blocks; the value bound
of this plan is compared with the flow programme by its homogeneity and elementary estimates for `i₀` and the window
length.

Let an
alternating row datum (4.19) with `ℓ ≥ 5`, `j_t ≥ 1`, `n ≥ j_t + 2` be given, and let `w(a) > 0`, `T ≥ 1`, `0 ≤ θ₀ ≤ 1`, `S_E ≥ 0`,
`C₁ ≥ 0` with `(1 − θ₀)w(a) ≤ T·W(a) ≤ w(a) + C₁S_E` and `nθ₀ ≤ 5j_t`; let `i₀` be the least odd `i ≥ 3` with
`(n + 1)^{3/2} < ℓ^i`, and let the weight be read on at least `n_o·n·i₀ + 2(n + j_t)` copies per orbit. Then, under (RD7),
`T·ν_bl(i₀) ≤ 𝔉_ℓ(n·w) + C·err + T·R_w/(ℓ − 1)`, `err := log(n + 1)·j_t·(Σ_aw(a) + n_o·S_E)/ℓ + n_o·(n + 1)·S_E/ℓ + (n + 1)·ℓ^{−i₀}·Σ_aw(a) + T·n_o·n³log²(n + 1)`,
`𝔉_ℓ` the flow programme of 4.6 on `ℤ/n_o` and `C` depending only on `C₁`. When `j_t·log(n + 1) ≤ n + 1` the term `log(n + 1)·j_t·n_o·S_E/ℓ`
is at most `n_o(n + 1)S_E/ℓ`. For several orbits the bounds add; the last term then becomes `T·R_w/(ℓ − 1)` for the remaining degree
`R_w` summed over the orbits, which may be bounded in total (no bound orbit by orbit is needed).

*Route.* By the identity `Σ_au(a) = ΣW − Σ_{a,i}(ℓ^i + 1)y(a, i)`, the value bound of a plan is `n(γΣW − Σy)` plus errors, so a plan is
an integral flow (`x = n·y`, slack `n·u`). *Rounding:* let `W_θ := (1 − θ₀)w/T ≤ W`, `Y^{sup}(a, i) := 𝒢(a, i)` (4.19), which contains
`0` and meets every interval of length `S_int` (4.19c (i)); the rounding uses of these sets only that they have windows. Take a
maximal flow `x` for `W_θ` and delete the levels `i ≥ i₀`, losing at most `ℓ^{−i₀}ΣW_θ` (4.6 (g)); let `y(a, i)` be the largest
element of `Y^{sup}(a, i)` that is `≤ x(a, i)` (so `0 ≤ x − y < S_int`, `y(a, i) = 0` for `i ≥ i₀`, and the capacities hold for `W_θ`, a
fortiori for `W`); put
`u(a) := W(a) − Σ_iℓ^iy(a, i) − Σ_iy(a − i, i) ≥ 0`: a plan admissible at level `i₀`. Its value bound is at most
`𝔉_ℓ(nW_θ) + ℓ^{−i₀}nΣW_θ + nγΣ(W − W_θ) + j_tΣW/(ℓ − 1) + 2i₀·j_t·ΣW_θ/(ℓ − 1) + n_o·i₀·n(S_int + log_ℓn + 1)`
(the character blocks cost `j_t/(ℓ − 1)` per unit of `Σu ≤ ΣW`; the bi-blocks, which carry the factor `i + 1 ≤ i₀`, are bounded
through the capacities `W_θ`; the fixed cost of a bi-block is charged once for each of the at most `n_o·i₀` pairs `(a, i)` with
`i < i₀`). Multiply by `T`: `T𝔉_ℓ(nW_θ) = (1 − θ₀)𝔉_ℓ(nw) ≤ 𝔉_ℓ(nw)` (homogeneity), the excess `T(W − W_θ) ≤ θ₀w + C₁S_E` is paid
at the rate `nγ` with `nθ₀ ≤ 5j_t`; `T·2i₀j_tΣW_θ ≤ 2i₀j_tΣw` with `i₀ ≤ C·log(n + 1)` gives the first error term;
`T·j_tΣW ≤ j_t(Σw + C₁n_oS_E)` is absorbed in it and, as `j_t ≤ n`, in `n_o(n + 1)S_E/ℓ`; the remaining degree contributes
`T·R_w/(ℓ − 1)` (monomial blocks, 4.19c (iii)); and `i₀ ≤ C·log(n + 1)` together with `S_int ≤ C·n²log(n + 1)` for the levels used
gives the last term `T·n_o·n³log²(n + 1)` of `err`. The parity of `n_o` is not used.

*Checks.* `n_o = 2`, `w` constant: `𝔉_ℓ(n·w) = ρ_LT·n·Σw` (4.6 (c)).

### 4.19e The row datum of an integral chart

*Origin:* particular to this proof: an integral gauged formal group law carries the typical curves, the pairing and the
polarisation series of a row datum ((a), (b)), and the chart of a principally polarised point of a unitary moduli problem
is such a law along each Frobenius orbit disjoint from its conjugate, with (RD7) and with the depth bounded by the block
depth ((c)).

*Method of the formal proof:* the axioms of a row datum are verified one by one for an integral gauged formal group law:
the typical curves by Cartier's operations (Frobenius as a sum over `ℓ`-th roots of unity, pure curves by projectors and
averaging), with the quasi-logarithm additive modulo integral series; (RD4) by reading a system of curves as an integral
point of the law; (RD5) by a rank count against the non-degeneracy of the cocycle modulo `𝔪`, which at a point is proved by
contradiction from the perfect pairing on the `ℓ`-torsion of the abelian part through the torus extension; (RD7) from the
unramified colength bound; and the depth is compared with block values because jets multiply over disjoint copies and
products of blocks are harmonic of the given weight.

*Definitions.* Let `ℓ ≠ 2` and `(C, v)`, `O`, `𝔪` be as in
4.19, `C` complete. An *integral gauged formal group law* of period `n_o` over `(C, v)` consists of: a formal group law `F` in
variables `x` with coefficients in `O`, with logarithm `lg` and exponential `ex` (inverse series over `C`, tangent to the identity);
a series `h(x, x′)` over `C` with `h(x, 0) = h(0, x′) = 0` such that `exp(h(x, z) + h(y, z) − h(x +_F y, z))`,
`exp(h(x, y) + h(x, z) − h(x, y +_F z))` and `exp(h(y, x) − h(x, y))` have coefficients in `O`; a natural number `N` that is a unit
of `O` (the *level*); two
families of tangent vectors `u(g, c)` (`g ∈ {0, 1}`, `c ∈ I_g`), together part of an `O`-basis, with types in `ℤ/n_o`; endomorphisms
`M_0`, `M_1` of `F` with coefficients in `O`, linear in logarithm coordinates, `M_g` the projector onto the span of the vectors of
side `g`, adjoint to each other for `h` (`exp(h(M_gx, y) − h(x, M_{1−g}y))` integral); and for each side `g` an endomorphism with
coefficients in `O` acting on the vectors of type `a` of the side `g` by `ζ(a)`, with an adjoint for `h` that has coefficients in
`O` and acts on the vectors of type `a` of the other side by the same `ζ(a)`; here `ζ` is injective with values roots of unity of
order prime to `ℓ` and `ζ(a + 1) = ζ(a)^ℓ`. Its *polarisation series* are the components
`h_{g,a,a′}(X, X′)` of `N·h(ex(Σ_cX_cu(g, c)), ex(Σ_{c′}X′_{c′}u(1 − g, c′)))` in the variables of types `a` and `a′`; its
*quasi-logarithms* are `ζ_v(x) := −Σ_iv_i·(∂h/∂x′_i)(x, 0)`; its *typical curves* of side `g` are the sequences `(x_s)`,
`x_s ∈ C^{I_g ⊔ I_{1−g}}`, for which there are an integral curve `γ` of `F` and integral series `λ_q` without constant term
with `lg(γ(t)) = Σ_cu(g, c)Σ_sx_s(c)t^{ℓ^s}` and, for every direction `q` of the other side,
`Σ_{s≥0}x_s(q)t^{ℓ^s} = λ_q(t) − ζ_{u(1−g,q)}(γ(t)) + Σ_cζ¹_q(u(g, c))·Σ_{s≥0}x_s(c)t^{ℓ^s}`, the last sum over the `c ∈ I_g` of the
type of `q`, where `ζ¹_q` is the linear part of the quasi-logarithm `ζ_{u(1−g,q)}` (so the part of the quasi-logarithm that is
linear along the directions of the type of `q` is removed from every coefficient; the linear parts between different types are
integral, by the endomorphisms `ζ`, so the tangent component `x_0(q)` is an arbitrary integral number, `0` allowed).
*Statement.* (a) *Universal part.* For every integral gauged formal group law of period `n_o`:
(a1) the typical curves of each side form a module with the properties of (RD1), with `I^∨_g := I_{1−g}`;
(a2) every `h_{g,a,a′}` has no monomial in the variables of one side only (isotropy, (RD3));
(a3) for every system of curves with unimodular tangent matrix the integral germs are the power series with coefficients in `O` in
the coordinates `x` of `F`, read in logarithm coordinates; so their ring does not depend on the system (RD6);
(a4) if the datum is alternating: for `a′ − a` odd and a direction `k` of type `a′` of the other side, `∂h_{g,a,a′}/∂X′_k + N·ζ_k`
is an integral germ; and (RD2) holds with the pairing `−N·δ` (perfect, `N` a unit); so (RD4) holds;
(a5) if the ranks are `n + j` at the big positions and `j′` at the others and the datum is alternating, the first clause of (RD5)
implies the second.
(b) *Non-degeneracy.* For an alternating datum, the first clause of (RD5) holds at every big `(g, a)` if the cocycle is
non-degenerate modulo `𝔪`: for every integral curve with tangent nonzero modulo `𝔪` that is additive modulo `𝔪` in degrees `< ℓ`
in each variable, the part of `h(x, y) + h(x, z) − h(x, y +_F z)` that is linear in `x`, along its tangent, is not congruent
modulo `𝔪` to the coboundary `b(y +_F z) − b(y) − b(z)` of a series `b` with coefficients in `O`.
(c) *At the points.* Let `y` be an algebraic integral framed point of a PEL moduli problem of unitary type (4.8b) whose
polarisation `λ` is principal, with an integral chart, above a prime `ℓ ≠ 2` unramified in `E` and prime to the level, and
`C`, `O` as in 4.8c with `v(ℓ) = 1`. Let an orbit of
`Frob_ℓ` on the embeddings of `E` into `C`, `τ_{s+1} = Frob∘τ_s`, disjoint from the orbit of the conjugate embeddings, be given on
which the ranks `n_{τ_s}` alternate between `n + j` and `j′` (sides: the embeddings `τ_s` and their conjugates) or are constant equal
to `n + j` with conjugate rank `j′`, with `j′ < n`. Then the
chart over `C`, with the eigen-frame of the orbit, is an integral gauged formal group law, non-degenerate modulo `𝔪`;
by (a), (b) it carries a row datum (alternating or constant), with `I^∨_g = I_{1−g}`; (RD7) holds; and for every dominant weight and every plan of 4.19 the product of its
blocks is an integral harmonic polynomial of that weight (4.8b) whose jet against a product of germs in disjoint copies is the
product of the jets of the blocks, each with the component of `h` named by the block. Hence the depth of the weight at `y` with
multiplier the level (4.8b, on the copies the plan uses) is at most the value of the product of the blocks: `ν ≤ ν_bl(N)` for every
`N` such that the weight is read on at least the copies of 4.19c (iv) for that `N` (with 4.19c (iv)).

*Route.* (a1) Zero, negation (where `ℓ ≠ 2` is used), Teichmüller multiples and Verschiebung of typical curves are formal; sums use
the integrality of the group law, Frobenius and infinite sums the cocycle relations; the integrality of the first nonzero coefficient
and the existence of pure curves with prescribed integral tangent use the endomorphisms `ζ` (to separate types by averaging over
roots of unity) and the projectors. The assembly is proved; the five constructions beneath it (sums, Frobenius, infinite sums, first
coefficient, pure curves) are not proved here. (a2) From `h(x, 0) = h(0, x′) = 0`. (a3) Both rings are the integral series in the
coordinates of `F`; the identification of the integral germs of a system of curves with these series is not proved here. (a4) (i) A
system of curves is a point of `F` with integral coordinates in the curve parameters, and the quasi-logarithm of a direction `q` of the
other side at that point is an integral series plus `ζ_q` of the system plus a linear term; (ii) `∂h/∂X′_k` in exponential coordinates
is an integral series minus the quasi-logarithm of `u(1 − g, k)` at the point; subtracting and multiplying by `N`, the difference of
`∂h_{g,a,a′}/∂X′_k` and `−N·ζ_k` for `a′ − a` odd is integral. Statements (i), (ii) and, beneath them, the additivity of the
quasi-logarithm modulo integral series with two transport statements, are not proved here. (a5) By alternation `(1 − g, a + 1)` is
big and `(1 − g, a + 2)` is not. On the other side, at type `a + 1`, there are `M ≥ n + j − j′` good curves with saturated tangents; by
the first clause, applied to combinations with `ℓ`-th roots of the coefficients (`C` algebraically closed), the `I^∨`-parts at `t^ℓ` of
the `N` given curves are independent modulo `𝔪`; they pair into `𝔪` with the tangents of the `M` curves (here the hypothesis on
`t^{ℓ²}` is used); in the space of type `a + 1` of the other side, of dimension `n + j`, `N + M ≤ n + j`, so `N ≤ j′`. The existence of the
`M` good curves and the pairing into `𝔪` are not proved here. (b) The passage from non-degeneracy to the first clause of (RD5) is
not proved here. (c) The chart contains the formal group law, the action and the polarisation series in canonical gauge, all
integral; base change to `C` is coefficientwise (the existence of logarithm and exponential over `C` is not proved here;
BordismFormalGroups §1.3 gives it for a one-dimensional law). The
projectors and the endomorphisms `ζ` are limits of elements of `𝒪_E` (idempotents and Teichmüller representatives of `𝒪_E ⊗ ℤ_ℓ`,
`ℓ` unramified), with adjoints their conjugates: a coefficientwise limit of endomorphisms coming from `𝒪_E` is an integral
endomorphism whose adjoint is the limit of the conjugates. That the chart with the eigen-frame is an integral gauged formal group
law with these projectors and endomorphisms (three statements), and the labelling of the frame by the orbit at an orbit that alternates between `Φ` and `cΦ`, are not proved
here. Non-degeneracy: a degenerate direction would give, at a point of the special
fibre over an algebraically closed field, an infinitesimal subgroup on which the pairing of the polarisation is trivial, while
the Weil pairing on the `ℓ`-torsion of the generic fibre attached to the polarisation is perfect (the polarisation is principal,
AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §A3.7; `ℓ` prime to the level serves only to make the multiplier a unit) and the duality of
its finite parts extends over `R` through the torus extension (NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §3.6); the rest is not
proved here beyond this outline. (RD7): 4.8c with 4.18 (b), (c) for the `n` frame vectors of a big type:
for alternating ranks `S = (n + j)γ + j′ρ_LT ≤ nγ + j_t/(ℓ − 1)`, for constant ranks `S = j′/(ℓ − 1) ≤ j_t/(ℓ − 1)` (geometric series).
Blocks: jets multiply over disjoint copies, and in a copy only one side, or one component, of `h` occurs; that the product of the
blocks of a plan is an integral harmonic polynomial of the weight, and the comparison of the depth of 4.8b (polarisation factor
`m_L·h_L` of the chart, `m_L` the level) with the values of the row datum (components of `h`), are not proved here.

*Checks.* (c) is not stated at an orbit stable under conjugation (`τ_{s+n_c} = τ̄_s`): there the side `1` vectors at `s + n_c` are
the side `0` vectors at `s`, so the two families are not together part of a basis and the chart is not an integral gauged formal

direction twice, and so does its fourth power (signature `(4, 0)`, with `n = 3`, `j = 1`, `j′ = 0`, `j_t = 1`);

(a2) does not make the polarisation factor `1` in a copy carrying a single minor there. The correction in the definition of the
typical curves makes them independent of a bilinear term of `h`, in logarithm coordinates, between equal types, which the three
conditions on `h` do not see; without it the Verschiebung of a typical curve would have a first coefficient that need not be
integral. In the realisation `I^∨_g = I_{1−g}`, so `#I^∨_{g,a} + #I_{g,a} = n + j + j′` at every
position.

### 4.19f The depth at the points of a unitary moduli problem

*Origin:* particular to this proof: upper bounds for the depth of a weight at the points of a principally polarised unitary
moduli problem, by the type of each orbit, with the flow programme as main term on alternating orbits ((a), (g3)), and the
equality (o) for powers of leading minors on isotropic combinations of the copies. The remark after (o) is this roadmap's
own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* the bounds are proved for abstract packages of row data that carry the comparison of the depth
with block values, and the packages are built at the points separately. On alternating orbits a maximal flow is rounded
inside the windows of the bi-blocks to a plan whose blocks bound the depth; where Frobenius is the conjugation the row datum
has period `2` and is realised on pairs of copies, and the gap is split as `(ℓ+1)y + u` with `y` in a window of the
level-one bi-block; at an orbit of odd conjugate type the plan lives on the `n_c` embeddings of `Φ` inside a datum of period
`2n_c` with identified sides; constant orbits use character and monomial blocks only, and depth zero cuts the exponents
into pieces below `ℓ`.

Let `M` be a PEL moduli
problem of unitary type (4.8b) whose polarisation `λ` is principal, for a CM field `E` with CM type `Φ`, of signature `(p, q)`
at every `τ ∈ Φ` and `(q, p)` at every `τ̄`,

algebraic integral framed point of `M` with an integral chart above a prime `ℓ ≥ 3` unramified in `E` and prime to the level
(`ℓ ≥ 5` in (a), (g1), (g3)). The
*orbits* at `ℓ` are those of the group generated by `Frob_ℓ` and the conjugation on the embeddings of `E` (they correspond to the
places of `E⁺` above `ℓ`); each is `O ∪ Ō` for an orbit `O` of `Frob_ℓ` (`τ_{s+1} = Frob∘τ_s`, `Ō` the conjugate embeddings), and it is of type (A) if `O`

*Route.* (o) The equality rests on four statements: integral germs on the copies and on the combinations have the same values to

### Examples

- `exp(T)` has weight `≥ 0` at radius `1/(p−1)` and at no smaller radius, while Mathlib's Gauss norm of it at radius `0` is `0`, the real supremum of an unbounded family; and the Lubin–Tate logarithm `Σ_e T^{p^{2e}}/p^e` has weight `≥ ρ_LT` at radius `ρ_LT`, with equality exactly at the monomials `T` and `T^{p²}` (4.1, Checks).
- `T^{p+1}/p` is the exceptional monomial of 4.2 and 4.3: `v_p = −1 ≤ −ρ_LT(p+1)`, and the conclusion `v_p ≥ 0` fails (4.2, Check).
- For `n′ = 2`, `p = 3` the torus-invariant monomials `x₀^a x₁^b` are those with `a ≡ 3b (mod 8)`; in degree `4` both are bi-power, in degree `8` none is (4.3, Check). The coefficient `𝔠/p` of `xy^p` in `G_𝔠` attains the bound of 4.3 at the bi-power monomial `e₀ + pe₁` (4.4, Checks).
- For `y² = 4x³ − 4x` at `p = 7` the coefficient `−G₈/8` of `z⁸` in `log σ(z)` has `v_7 = −1`, so the derivatives of `σ` at `0` are divisible only by `p^{ρ_LT·m − O(log m)}`: the one-letter rate of 4.5 is the true order (4.5, Check).
- For `n′ = 2`, `p = 3`: `D = (1, 1)` gives `𝔉_3 = 1/4 = ρ_LT·2`, and `D = (1, 0)` gives `𝔉_3 = γ_LT = 3/8` (4.6, Checks).
- With one pair of variables and `n` minors of height `1` on each side, the jet of the Gaussian is `n!·(−S)^n`, against the bound `p^{n/2}` of 4.8a (4.8a, Check).
- At a ramified quadratic place, `Lie_τ = R·(X + ϖ)` and `Lie_{τ′} = R·(X − ϖ)` are saturated of rank one but together span a submodule of index `|2ϖ|^{−1}` (4.8b, Checks).
- For `E_w = ℚ_{p²}` and a one-dimensional formal group of height two, `S = γ_LT(p)` in 4.8c (4.8c, Checks).
- For `n₀ = 2`, `h₁ = 0` the capacity jet is, up to its leading factor, `≡ y + 1 (mod ℓ)`: the window of 4.19b is necessary (4.19b, Checks).
- In `ℚ(ζ_7)` the single orbit at `17` is of type (O) with `n_c = 3`, and a CM type of three consecutive embeddings does not alternate along it (4.19f, Checks).
