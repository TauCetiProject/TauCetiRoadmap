# Roadmap: integral Weyl modules of general linear groups

**Summary.** Let `λ = (λ_0 ≥ λ_1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ λ_{N−1})` be a dominant weight of `GL_N` and `V_λ` the irreducible representation of highest weight `λ` over a field of characteristic `0`. In the polynomial model, `V_λ` is the space of polynomials in an `N × N` matrix of variables `x`, homogeneous of degree `λ_s − λ_{N−1}` in the column `s` and invariant under adding a multiple of an earlier column to a later one, with `GL_N` acting on the rows and twisted by `det^{λ_{N−1}}`. It carries two integral structures: the *dual Weyl lattice* `∇_ℤ(λ)` of the polynomials of the model with integer coefficients, spanned by the products of minors on initial columns, and the *Weyl lattice* `Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊂ ∇_ℤ(λ)`, spanned by the images of the product `v_λ` of the leading principal minors under the divided powers of the row operations. They are dual to each other for the contravariant (Fischer) form normalised by `⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩ = 1`, they coincide after inverting every prime, and over a field of characteristic `p` they specialise to the Weyl module and the induced module. This roadmap measures how far apart the two lattices are, prime by prime and weight space by weight space, builds the integral branching maps from `GL_N` to `GL_{N−1}`, and computes the slopes of the lattices for the normalised Fischer form. The statements are over rings of characteristic `0` — the integers, their localisations, and discrete valuation rings of mixed characteristic — except the standard basis of the polynomial model, which holds over every integral domain, and two statements in characteristic `p`: the Weyl and induced modules over a field of characteristic `p` below the upper wall of the lowest alcove, and linkage for the reductions modulo a uniformiser.

The headline theorems are these. (1) *The sandwich exponent.* If `O` is a discrete valuation ring of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `p`, then `p^e·∇_O(λ) ⊂ Δ_O(λ)` with `e = (N(N−1)/2)·⌊log_p(λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1)⌋`; in particular the two lattices agree locally at every prime `p > λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1`, and `lcm(1, …, λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1)^{N(N−1)/2}·∇_ℤ(λ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ)`. A refinement proved in this roadmap, and not a statement of [FormalProof], replaces `Δ` by `(dim V_λ)·Δ` on the right of both inclusions; for `GL_2` the refined local inclusion is exact at every prime. Both rest on a denominator bound for the projector onto the invariants of any lattice stable under the Kostant form `U_ℤ`, that is, under the divided powers of the root operators and the binomials `C(E_{aa}, k)`. (2) *Integral branching.* If `σ` is a dominant weight of `GL_{N−1}` interlacing `λ`, the `GL_{N−1}`-equivariant map `V_σ → V_λ` sending `v_σ` to `c_Br·ξ`, with `ξ` an explicit product of minors and `c_Br` an explicit nonzero integer of absolute value at most `(λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 2)^{(N−1)D₁}` (`D₁` a count of interlacing defects), maps `∇_ℤ(σ)` into `∇_ℤ(λ)`; its adjoint maps `Δ_ℤ(λ)` into `Δ_ℤ(σ)`; their composite is `c_Br²·⟨ξ, ξ⟩` with `⟨ξ, ξ⟩ ≤ dim V_λ`; and the map is written down on bideterminants through a product of Capelli determinants. (3) *Block cuts and the graded Pieri bound.* The least exponent `ex(λ, ℓ)` with `ℓ^{ex(λ,ℓ)}·∇_ℤ(λ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)` satisfies `ex(λ, ℓ) ≤ ex(λ′, ℓ) + ex(λ″, ℓ) + a(N − a)·⌊log_ℓ w⌋` when `λ` is cut into its first `a` and its last `N − a` entries, `w` bounding the width of the range of its shifted weights; and for a block of `r` equal entries `w` placed before a dominant `λ₂`, the weight space of `∇_ℤ(λ)` reached by moving `i` boxes out of the block needs at most the exponent of the Levi factor plus `v_ℓ(∏_c(w − c)^{m(c)})`, the product over the contents `c` of the skew shapes that can occur; for `h < ℓ²` this gives an explicit four-part exponent. A simple subquotient of `∇(λ)/ℓ` has a highest weight linked to `λ` in multiset form. (4) *Slopes.* With `λ⁰ := λ − λ_{N−1}·(1, …, 1)`, `c_λ := ⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩_F/∏_sλ⁰_s!` equals the hook product of `λ⁰` divided by `∏_sλ⁰_s!` and lies in `[1, (λ⁰_0 + N − 1)^{N(N−1)/2}]`, and for twice the normalised slope, `−(|λ⁰|·log N + log c_λ) ≤ μ(Δ_ℤ(λ)) = −μ(∇_ℤ(λ)) ≤ 0` and `μ_max(Δ_ℤ(λ)) ≤ μ_max(∇_ℤ(λ)) ≤ |λ⁰|·log N + log c_λ`, also after extension of scalars to the integers of any number field.

The roadmap builds on [ReductiveGroups](../../ReductiveGroups/README.md), which supplies the Kostant integral form of the enveloping algebra, the integral Weyl and induced modules with their contravariant duality and base change, and the identification of the polynomial model with the induced module; on [RepresentationTheory/ClassicalGroups](../../RepresentationTheory/ClassicalGroups/README.md), which supplies the characteristic-zero highest-weight theory of `GL_N`, the Weyl dimension formula, the multiplicity-free branching `GL_N ↓ GL_{N−1}`, the Capelli generators and the Schur characters behind Pieri's rule; on [GroebnerGeometry](../../GroebnerGeometry/README.md), whose straightening of flag minors gives the standard bideterminant basis; and on [SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles](../SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles/README.md) and [HermitianFormsAndLattices](../HermitianFormsAndLattices/README.md), roadmaps of the family [RiemannHypothesisForDirichletLFunctions](../README.md), which supply the slopes and Hermitian lattices of Layer 3. In that family the theta-chain roadmaps consume the lattices, branching maps, exponents and slopes built here (Downstream consumers, below).

Layer 0 sets up the polynomial model over a domain with its standard bideterminant basis, proves the denominator bound for invariant projectors, deduces the sandwich exponent and its local and global forms, and records the agreement of the two lattices below the upper wall of the lowest alcove. Layer 1 proves integral branching from `GL_N` to `GL_{N−1}`. Layer 2 studies the exact exponent `ex(λ, ℓ)`: its behaviour under base change and tensor products, the block-cut inequality, linkage, and the graded Pieri bound weight space by weight space. Layer 3 computes the Fischer norm of a product of leading minors of a matrix of variables as a hook product, in particular that of `v_λ`, the slopes of the Weyl and dual Weyl lattices and of the monomial lattice, a bound for the maximal slope of the dual of an orthogonal sum of Weyl lattices of self-dual Hermitian lattices, and the maximal slope of a twisted Schur functor through a frame.

## Conventions

*Weights.* `N ≥ 1`; rows, columns and the entries of weights are indexed from `0`. A weight of `GL_N` is `λ ∈ ℤ^N`, dominant if `λ_0 ≥ ⋯ ≥ λ_{N−1}`; `|λ| := Σ_aλ_a`; `λ⁰ := λ − λ_{N−1}·(1, …, 1)`, a partition with at most `N − 1` nonzero parts (for dominant `λ`, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.DominantWeight.detShiftShape`); `u_λ(a) := λ_a + N − 1 − a` are the *shifted weights* (strictly decreasing for dominant `λ`); and the *width* is `w(λ) := u_λ(0) − u_λ(N − 1) = λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1`, the value `⟨λ + ρ, α_0^∨⟩` at the highest coroot. For a prime `p` and an integer `w ≥ 0`, `⌊log_p w⌋` is the largest `k ≥ 0` with `p^k ≤ w`, and `0` when `w < p` (including `w = 0`). `v_p` is the `p`-adic valuation; `C(n, k)` is the binomial coefficient.

*The polynomial model.* For a commutative ring `R` let `x = (x_{a,s})_{0 ≤ a, s < N}` be a matrix of variables (`a` the row, `s` the column) and, for a dominant `λ`, let `∇_R(λ) ⊂ R[x]` be the module of polynomials homogeneous of degree `λ⁰_s` in the column `s` that are invariant under every substitution `x ↦ x·(1 + T·e_{st})`, `s < t`, as an identity in `R[x][T]` (`e_{st}` the matrix unit). `GL_N(R)` acts by `(g·f)(x) = det(g)^{λ_{N−1}}·f(gᵗx)` ([FormalProof], `Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/CoefMod.lean` (`Formalism.L2.DIV.actP`)); the root subgroup `1 + T·e_{ab}` acts by the *row operation* `x_b ↦ x_b + T·x_a` (row `b` replaced by row `b` plus `T` times row `a`), and its coefficient of `T^k` is the *divided row operator* `E_{ab}^{(k)}`, an endomorphism of `∇_R(λ)` with integer coefficients. The operators `E_{ab} := Σ_sx_{a,s}·∂/∂x_{b,s} + λ_{N−1}·δ_{ab}` give the action of `𝔤𝔩_N`. The *weight* of a monomial is its vector of row degrees plus `λ_{N−1}·(1, …, 1)`; `∇_R(λ)_μ` is the weight space of weight `μ`. The *highest-weight vector* is `v_λ := ∏_{s<N}Δ_{s+1}^{λ_s − λ_{s+1}}` (`Δ_k` the leading `k × k` minor, `λ_N := λ_{N−1}`), of weight `λ`. The *Weyl lattice* `Δ_R(λ)` is the `R`-span of the images of `v_λ` under all finite words in the operators `E_{ab}^{(k)}` (`a ≠ b`, `k ≥ 1`). For a field `K` of characteristic `0`, `∇_K(λ) = Δ_K(λ)` is the irreducible representation `V_λ` (ClassicalGroups Layer 3; ReductiveGroups §10B, §10D); `∇_ℤ(λ)` and `Δ_ℤ(λ)` are lattices in `V_λ = ∇_ℚ(λ)`. Over every field `K` of characteristic `0`, AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactorsPartII §4.4 states the Cauchy decomposition of the polynomials in `x` under `GL_N × GL_N`, with the highest line of the summand of type `λ⁰` spanned by `v_λ`, normalised by the coefficient `1` of its diagonal monomial; `∇_K(λ)` is, up to the twist by `det^{λ_{N−1}}`, the part of that summand invariant under the column operations. `Suggested.lean` writes `λ⁰` as `normalizedWeight`, `Δ_k` as `leadingMinor`, the highest-weight vector of given column degrees as `highestWeightVector`, the column operation `x ↦ x·(1 + T·e_{st})` and the row operation as `columnOperation` and `rowOperation`, `E_{ab}^{(k)}` as `dividedRowOperator`, `∇_R(λ)` as `dualWeylLattice`, `Δ_R(λ)` as `weylLattice`, and `∇_R(λ)_μ` as `dualWeylLatticeWeightSpace`.

*The Kostant form.* `U_ℤ` is the subring of the enveloping algebra of `𝔤𝔩_N ⊗ ℚ` generated by the divided powers `E_{ab}^{(k)} = E_{ab}^k/k!` (`a ≠ b`) and the binomials `C(E_{aa}, k)`: Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantForm` for the families `(E_{ab})_{a≠b}` and `(E_{aa})_a` (ReductiveGroups §9A). On the polynomial model `E_{ab}^k/k!` acts as the divided row operator and `C(E_{aa}, k)` acts on a weight vector of weight `μ` by the integer `C(μ_a, k)`. A lattice is *`U_ℤ`-stable* if it is stable under these generators.

*The contravariant form.* The *Fischer form* on `ℂ[x]` is `⟨f, g⟩_F := Σ_α conj(f_α)·g_α·α!` (`f = Σ_αf_αx^α`, `α! := ∏_{a,s}α_{a,s}!`), conjugate-linear in the first argument ([FormalProof], `Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/CoefMod.lean` (`Formalism.L2.DIV.fockS`)). The *normalised form* on `∇_ℂ(λ)` is `⟨f, g⟩_λ := ⟨f, g⟩_F/⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩_F` ([FormalProof], `Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataCoeff.lean` (`Formalism.BasicNotions.normFock`)), so `⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩_λ = 1`. It is positive definite, rational on `∇_ℚ(λ)`, invariant under the unitary group `U(N)`, contravariant (`E_{ab}` and `E_{ba}` are adjoint), and distinct weight spaces are orthogonal. In the polynomial model `Δ_ℤ(λ)` and `∇_ℤ(λ)` are dual to each other for `⟨·,·⟩_λ` (ReductiveGroups §10B: the contravariant dual of the Weyl module is the induced module, through the top-normalised integral contravariant form). For a dominant weight `σ` of `GL_{N−1}` the same conventions apply to an `(N−1) × (N−1)` matrix of variables, and `GL_{N−1}` is embedded in `GL_N` as `g ↦ diag(g, 1)`.

*Tableaux and contents.* Young diagrams and semistandard tableaux (rows weakly increasing, columns strictly increasing) are Mathlib's `YoungDiagram` and `SemistandardYoungTableau`. The box in row `a` and column `c` (both from `0`) has *content* `c − a`; `H(μ)` is the product of the hook lengths of a partition `μ` (Tau Ceti's `YoungDiagram.hookLength`). The *bideterminant* of a filling `T` of the diagram of `λ⁰`, with entries in `{0, …, N − 1}` strictly increasing down each column, is `B_T := ∏_kμ(T_k)`, the product over the columns `k` of the diagram of the minor `μ(T_k)` of `x` on the rows listed in that column and the first `|T_k|` columns of `x`.

*Slopes.* Hermitian lattices over the integers of a number field and their slopes `μ̂`, `μ̂_max` are those of SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles. This roadmap writes `μ := 2μ̂` and `μ_max := 2μ̂_max` ([FormalProof], `Formalism/HarderNarasimhan/StatementPl.lean` (`Formalism.HN.muMaxDef`)), so that a `ℤ`-lattice of rank `r` with Gram matrix `G` has `μ = −(1/r)·log det G`, and the standard lattice `O_K^n` with the standard metrics has `μ = 0`. Hermitian spaces and lattices over a CM field are those of HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.0.

**Statements of the formal proof.** Where a target names [FormalProof], the form that proof establishes is recorded at the target: its
hypotheses, ranges and constants are those of the proof, including restrictions that the route written here does not
visibly use. A wider or sharper form stated beside it is this roadmap's own, proved by the route written here, and is not in

## Layer 0: The polynomial model and the sandwich exponent

This layer gives the polynomial model its integral basis, proves a denominator bound for the projector onto the invariants of a `U_ℤ`-stable lattice in any finite-dimensional representation, and deduces the sandwich exponent between `∇` and `Δ`, with its local and global forms, and the agreement of the two lattices below the upper wall of the lowest alcove.

### 0.1 Standard bideterminant basis and saturation

Let `R` be an integral domain with fraction field `F`, `N, n ≥ 1`, `x` an `N × n` matrix of variables, and `ν = (ν_0 ≥ ⋯ ≥ ν_{n−1} ≥ 0)` with at most `N` nonzero entries; define `∇_R(ν) ⊂ R[x]` as in the Conventions (degree `ν_s` in the column `s`, invariant under adding a multiple of an earlier column to a later one), and bideterminants `B_T` of fillings `T` of the diagram of `ν` (row `s` of the diagram has `ν_s` boxes and corresponds to the column `s` of `x`). Prove:

- every `B_T` lies in `∇_R(ν)` and is a weight vector, of weight the content vector of `T` (the number of entries equal to each `a`);
- for a lexicographic monomial order in which the main diagonal of every minor with increasing rows on initial columns is the leading term (GroebnerGeometry G5 fixes one), the leading monomial of `B_T` is `∏_{(s,k)}x_{T(s,k), s}`, the product over the boxes, with coefficient `1`; distinct semistandard `T` have distinct leading monomials;
- the `B_T` with `T` semistandard (entries `< N`) form an `R`-basis of `∇_R(ν)`;
- the `R`-span of all bideterminants of shape `ν` is `∇_R(ν)`; in particular it is saturated in `R[x]`: an element of the `F`-span of the bideterminants whose coefficients lie in `R` has coordinates in `R` in the standard basis; `∇_ℤ(ν) = ∇_ℚ(ν) ∩ ℤ[x]` and `∇_R(ν) = ∇_ℤ(ν) ⊗ R`;
- the rank of `∇_R(ν)` is the number of semistandard tableaux of shape `ν` with entries `< N`, which is `dim V_ν`.

*Route.* A minor on initial columns is invariant under adding an earlier column to a later one (the operation either stays inside its columns or misses them), so every `B_T` lies in `∇_R(ν)`; its weight is read off any monomial. The leading term of a flag minor is its diagonal (GroebnerGeometry G5), and the leading monomial of a product is the product of the leading monomials, which gives the second item; a weakly increasing row is determined by its multiset of entries, so distinct semistandard fillings have distinct leading monomials, and the standard bideterminants are linearly independent over `F`. For spanning: the leading exponent of every nonzero element of `∇_F(ν)` is the leading exponent of a standard bideterminant (this is where the invariance under the column operations enters: for `F` of characteristic `0` the step follows from ReductiveGroups §10D, by which the polynomials invariant under right multiplication by upper unipotent matrices form the flag-minor algebra, after base change to an algebraic closure of `F`, with the standard-monomial basis of GroebnerGeometry G5; for `F` of positive characteristic the proof of this step is not given here); subtract the corresponding standard bideterminant, whose leading coefficient is `1`, and induct on the leading monomial. Over `R` every subtraction keeps the coefficients in `R`, which gives spanning over `R` and the saturation at once. Base change follows from the basis, and the count is the Weyl dimension (ClassicalGroups §5.1 with Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.eval_one_diagramSchurPoly_eq_card_boundedSSYT`). The standard monomial theory is classical (Doubilet–Rota–Stein; De Concini–Eisenbud–Procesi). [FormalProof] states the basis over an integral domain as SSBasis (`Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility/StatementD.lean`) and the saturation as DualWeylSat and DualWeylIntRat (`Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility/StatementA.lean`).

*Needs:* GroebnerGeometry G5 (flag minors, their diagonal leading terms, the standard-monomial basis); ReductiveGroups §10D (the invariants of the right upper-unipotent action in characteristic `0`); ClassicalGroups §5.1 (Weyl dimension); Mathlib `SemistandardYoungTableau`, `YoungDiagram`, `MvPolynomial`, `MonomialOrder`, `Matrix.det`.

**Checks.**

- `ν = (2, 1)`, `N = 3`: there are `8` semistandard tableaux, the Weyl dimension of `(2, 1, 0)`. In the weight `(1, 1, 1)` the two standard bideterminants are `x_{10}·[0,2]` and `x_{20}·[0,1]` (`[a,b]` the minor on the rows `a, b` and the columns `0, 1`), and the third bideterminant of that weight is `x_{00}·[1,2] = x_{10}·[0,2] − x_{20}·[0,1]` (the Laplace expansion of a `3 × 3` determinant with a repeated column): a straightening with coefficients `±1`.
- Saturation, computed: for all `28` weights `(λ_0, λ_1, 0)` of `GL_3` with `λ_0 ≤ 6`, the matrix of all bideterminants against the monomials has, weight by weight, every Smith invariant factor equal to `1`, and the ranks are the Weyl dimensions.
- Column strictness alone does not give a basis: the shape `(2, 1)` with entries `< 3` has `9` column-strict fillings against rank `8`, the one relation being the Laplace relation above; a filling with a repeated entry in a column gives `0`.

### 0.3a The invariant projection of a lattice

Let `O` be a discrete valuation ring of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `p`, `K` its fraction field, and `U_ℤ` the Kostant form of `𝔤𝔩_N`. Let `W` be a finite-dimensional representation of `𝔤𝔩_N` over `K` that is the direct sum of its weight spaces for the `E_{aa}`, with integral weights; `M ⊂ W` a `U_ℤ`-stable `O`-submodule (not necessarily finitely generated, not necessarily spanning); `L ≥ 0` an integer with `|ν_a| ≤ L` for every weight `ν` of `W` with `Σ_aν_a = 0` and every `a`; and `Inv` the projector of `W` onto its invariants `W^{𝔤𝔩_N}` along the sum of the other isotypic components (`W` is completely reducible). Then

```text
p^{(N(N−1)/2)·⌊log_p(L + N − 1)⌋} · Inv(M) ⊂ M.
```

*Route.* *Weight zero.* If the eigenvalues of `E_{aa}` lie in `[−B, B]`, then `C(E_{aa} + B, B)·C(B − E_{aa}, B)` is `1` on the eigenvalue `0` and `0` on the others, and by Vandermonde's identity it is an integer combination of the `C(E_{aa}, k)`; the product over `a` is the projector `Θ_0` onto the weight `0`, so `Θ_0(M) ⊂ M`, and `Inv = Inv∘Θ_0`. *Step projectors.* For `0 ≤ c < N` put `Ω_c^0 := 1`, `Ω_c^{q+1} := Σ_{i<c}E_{ci}Ω_c^qE_{ic}` and `P_c := Σ_{q=0}^{L}(−1)^q·(c!/((q + c)!·q!))·Ω_c^q`. If `u` has weight `0` and `E_{ab}u = 0` for all `a ≠ b < c`, then `P_cu` has weight `0`, `E_{ab}P_cu = 0` for all `a ≠ b ≤ c`, and `P_cu − u ∈ Σ_{i<c}E_{ci}W`. Indeed `Ω_c^q` commutes with `E_{ab}` (`a, b < c`) and with `E_{cc}`; call `y` *of level `s`* if `E_{cc}y = −s·y` and `Σ_{i<c}E_{ji}E_{ic}y = (s + c)·E_{jc}y` for `j < c`; then `u` has level `0`, `E_{lc}y` has level `s + 1`, and by induction on `q`, `E_{jc}Ω_c^qy = Ω_c^qE_{jc}y + q(2s + c + q)·Ω_c^{q−1}E_{jc}y`. The coefficients `a_q` of `P_c` satisfy `a_{q+1}(q + 1)(c + q + 1) = −a_q`, so the sum telescopes to `E_{jc}P_cu = a_L·Ω_c^LE_{jc}u`, which is `0` because the innermost `L + 1` operators would reach a weight of degree `0` with an entry `−(L + 1)`. A vector of weight `0` killed by `E_{jc}` is a highest-weight vector of weight `0` for the `𝔰𝔩_2` of the pair `(j, c)` in a finite-dimensional module, hence killed by `E_{cj}` as well. *Assembly.* `Inv` is the projector onto an isotypic component of a completely reducible module, so it commutes with the action and kills `E_{ci}W`; hence `y_0 := Θ_0x`, `y_{c+1} := P_cy_c` ends at `y_N = Inv(x)`. *Denominators.* For a set `s` of indices different from `c` and `b ≥ 0` put `Ω^{[b]}_{c,s}(q) := (b!/((q + b)!·q!))·(Σ_{i∈s}E_{ci}(·)E_{ic})^q(1)`. The maps `X ↦ E_{ci}XE_{ic}` for distinct `i` commute, so `Ω^{[b]}_{c,s∪{i}}(q) = Σ_{k=0}^{q}C(q + b, k)^{−1}·E_{ci}^{(k)}·Ω^{[b]}_{c,s}(q − k)·E_{ic}^{(k)}`; with `v_p(C(n, k)) ≤ ⌊log_p n⌋` and induction on `|s|`, `p^{|s|·⌊log_p(L + N − 1)⌋}·Ω^{[b]}_{c,s}(q)(M) ⊂ M` whenever `q + b ≤ L + N − 1`. `P_c` is the sum of the cases `s = {i < c}`, `b = c`, `q ≤ L`, so `p^{c·⌊log_p(L + N − 1)⌋}·P_c(M) ⊂ M`, and `Σ_{c<N}c = N(N−1)/2`. The step projectors are truncated extremal projectors (Asherova–Smirnov–Tolstoy). [FormalProof] states the bound, with the product of the step projectors, over `ℚ_p` for a `ℤ_p`-closed subgroup stable under the divided powers, as InvInt and InvInv (`Formalism/ThetaLogCoordinates/TraceIsometryWeights/StatementA.lean`), and over a complete discrete valuation ring with `p ≥ 5` whose residue field contains a field of `p^{n′}` elements for some even `n′ ≥ 2` (AdmissibleDVR, `Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ObjPol.lean`), for a finitely generated `M` spanning `W`, as InvariantDenominatorLinftyBound (`Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/RootWeights/InvariantDenominatorLinftyBound.lean`).

*Needs:* the Kostant form (Conventions; ReductiveGroups §9A); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.complementedLattice_lieSubmodule_of_isSemisimple_center` (complete reducibility of a finite-dimensional `𝔤𝔩_N`-module on which the centre acts semisimply); Mathlib `Nat.factorization_choose_le_log`.

**Checks.**

- `N = 2`: `P_1 = Σ_q(−1)^q·f^{(q)}e^{(q)}/(q + 1)` with `e = E_{01}`, `f = E_{10}`. On a vector of weight `0` in the representation of dimension `2j + 1` it acts by `Σ_q(−1)^q·C(j + q, q)·C(j, q)/(q + 1)`, which is `1` for `j = 0` and `0` for `1 ≤ j ≤ 7` (computed): it is the projector onto the invariants of weight `0`.
- `W = End(K²)` with `M` the matrices over `O`: the weights of degree `0` are `(0, 0)`, `(1, −1)`, `(−1, 1)`, so `L = 1`; `Inv(f) = (tr f/2)·id`, and the exponent `⌊log_p 2⌋` is `1` for `p = 2`, attained by `f = e_{00}`, and `0` for odd `p`.

### 0.3 The sandwich exponent

Let `O`, `K`, `p` and `U_ℤ` be as in 0.3a, `λ` a dominant weight of `GL_N`, `V_λ` the irreducible representation of `𝔤𝔩_N` over `K` of highest weight `λ`, and `v` a highest-weight vector. Put

```text
Δ_O(λ) := O·U_ℤ·v,      ∇_O(λ) := { y ∈ V_λ : for every u ∈ U_ℤ the component of weight λ of u·y lies in O·v }.
```

### 0.2 Weyl and dual Weyl lattices below the upper wall of the lowest alcove

Let `p` be a prime and `λ` a dominant weight of `GL_N` with `λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1 < p`, equivalently `⟨λ + ρ, α^∨⟩ < p` for every positive root `α`. ### 1.1 Integral branching

```text
D₀ := Σ_{i=0}^{N−2} (σ_i − λ_{i+1}),     R_i := [λ_{i+1}, min(λ_i, λ_{i+1} + D₀)] ∩ ℤ,
```

## Layer 2: The exponent of the Weyl lattice and the graded Pieri bound

Throughout this layer `ℓ` is a prime and the shifted weights `u_λ(a) = λ_a + N − 1 − a` are those of the Conventions.

### 2.1 The exact exponent: base change, block cuts, linkage

*Definition.* `ex(λ, ℓ)` is the least integer `e ≥ 0` with `ℓ^e·∇_ℤ(λ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)`.

(a) `ex(λ, ℓ)` is finite. For every integral domain `O` containing `ℤ_(ℓ)` in which `ℓ` is not a unit (in particular every discrete valuation ring of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `ℓ`), `ex(λ, ℓ)` is the least `e` with `ℓ^e·∇_O(λ) ⊂ Δ_O(λ)`. For dominant weights `λ_1, …, λ_r` of `GL_N`, `ℓ^{Σ_iex(λ_i, ℓ)}` maps `⊗_i∇_O(λ_i)` into `⊗_iΔ_O(λ_i)`.

(b) *(Block cut.)* Let `0 ≤ a ≤ N`, `λ′ := (λ_0, …, λ_{a−1})` and `λ″ := (λ_a, …, λ_{N−1})`, dominant weights of `GL_a` and `GL_{N−a}`, and let `w ≥ 0` be such that all shifted weights of `λ` lie in an interval of length `w` (for instance `w = λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1`). Then

```text
ex(λ, ℓ) ≤ ex(λ′, ℓ) + ex(λ″, ℓ) + a(N − a)·⌊log_ℓ w⌋.
```

Globally: if `c₁, c₂` are integers with `c₁·∇_ℤ(λ′) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ′)` and `c₂·∇_ℤ(λ″) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ″)`, then `c₁c₂·lcm(1, …, w)^{a(N−a)}·∇_ℤ(λ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ)`.

`Suggested.lean` states the global form as `lcm_pow_smul_dualWeylLattice_le_weylLattice_of_blocks`, and the inequality, over every discrete valuation ring `O` of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `ℓ`, with exponents `e₁, e₂` such that `ℓ^{e₁}·∇_O(λ′) ⊂ Δ_O(λ′)` and `ℓ^{e₂}·∇_O(λ″) ⊂ Δ_O(λ″)` in place of `ex(λ′, ℓ)` and `ex(λ″, ℓ)` (an equivalent form, by the base change of (a), ExZEqO in [FormalProof]), as `pow_smul_dualWeylLattice_le_weylLattice_of_blocks`.

(c) Consequently `ex(λ, ℓ) ≤ C(N, 2)·⌊log_ℓ(λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1)⌋`, and `ex(λ, ℓ) = 0`, that is `Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ) = ∇_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)`, when `λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1 < ℓ`.

(c′) *(Total exponent.)* With `w := λ_0 − λ_{N−1} + N − 1`, the integer `D(λ) := ∏_pp^{ex(λ, p)}` divides `lcm(1, …, w)^{C(N,2)}`, `D(λ)·∇_ℤ(λ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ)`, and `log D(λ) ≤ C(N, 2)·ψ(w) ≤ C(N, 2)·w·log w`, `ψ` Chebyshev's function.

(d) *(Linkage in multiset form.)* Let `O` be a discrete valuation ring of characteristic `0` with uniformiser `ϖ` and residue characteristic `ℓ`, `Φ` a finite set, `λ = (λ_τ)_{τ∈Φ}` and `μ = (μ_τ)_{τ∈Φ}` families of dominant weights of `GL_N`, `∇_O(λ) := ⊗_{τ∈Φ}∇_O(λ_τ)`, and `𝒜_λ` the algebra of endomorphisms of `∇_O(λ)` generated by `∏_τGL_N(O)` and by the projectors onto the weight spaces of each factor. If a nonzero `𝒜_λ`-subquotient of `∇_O(λ)/ϖ` embeds into `∇_O(μ)/ϖ` compatibly with the group and the weight projectors, then for every `τ`

```text
∏_a (X + λ_τ(a) − a) = ∏_a (X + μ_τ(a) − a)   in 𝔽_ℓ[X],
```

that is, the multisets `{λ_τ(a) − a}` and `{μ_τ(a) − a}` agree modulo `ℓ`. Every weight `μ` of `∇(λ_τ)` has `|μ| = |λ_τ|` and `λ_τ(N − 1) ≤ μ(a) ≤ λ_τ(0)`.

(e) *(Constituents.)* Let `G` be a simple `𝒜_λ`-subquotient of `∇_O(λ)/ϖ`. Call a family `μ = (μ_τ)` of weights *carried by `G`* if one nonzero vector of `G` has weight `μ_τ` at every `τ` simultaneously. Let `μ` be a family of dominant weights carried by `G` for which `Σ_τΣ_a(N − a)·μ_τ(a)` is maximal among all families of weights carried by `G` (dominant or not). Then `G` embeds into `∇_O(μ)/ϖ` compatibly with the group and the weight projectors.

*Route.* (a) Finiteness: `∇_ℚ(λ) = Δ_ℚ(λ)` and both lattices are finitely generated (0.1). Base change: `∇_O(λ) = ∇_ℤ(λ) ⊗ O` (0.1) and `Δ_O(λ) = Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ O` (0.3 (c)); `O` is flat over `ℤ_(ℓ)`, and `(∇/Δ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ) ≅ ⊕_iℤ/ℓ^{a_i}` becomes `⊕_iO/ℓ^{a_i}O`, in which `ℓ^{a_i − 1} ≠ 0` because `ℓ` is not a unit of the domain `O`; so the least exponent is `max_ia_i` over both rings. Tensor products: replace one factor at a time. (b) Let `c₁, c₂` kill `∇/Δ` for the two blocks. Restriction of polynomials to block-diagonal matrices identifies the part of `∇_ℚ(λ)` of block depth `0` with `∇(λ′) ⊗ ∇(λ″)`, integrally and as a direct summand stable under `GL_a × GL_{N−a}` (PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructure §0.5 for the Levi subgroup `GL_a × GL_{N−a}`, over `ℤ_p` for every prime `p`), and gives an endomorphism `T` of `∇_ℚ(λ)` commuting with `𝔤𝔩_a × 𝔤𝔩_{N−a}`, zero in positive block depth, with `c₁c₂·T(∇_ℤ) ⊂ Δ_ℤ` and `T(v_λ) = v_λ`. This invariance is a step proved here, in the following form. *Let `W` be a representation of `𝔤𝔩_N` over `ℚ` in which every vector on which `E_{cc} − E_{dd}` acts by a scalar larger than `2K` is `0`, and `T ∈ W` a vector killed by `E_{cd}` for all `c, d` on the same side of `a` (both `< a` or both `≥ a`, `c = d` included). The exponent does not involve `v_ℓ(z)`. (c) Induction on `N` with `a = 1`; in rank one `∇ = Δ`. (c′) from (c) at every prime, with `∏_pp^{⌊log_p w⌋} = lcm(1, …, w)` and `ψ(w) = log lcm(1, …, w) ≤ π(w)·log w`. ### 2.2 The graded Pieri bound

*Definitions.* For integers `r, q ≥ 0` and `w ≥ 0`, a dominant `λ₂ = (λ₂(0) ≥ ⋯ ≥ λ₂(q − 1) ≥ 0)` with `λ₂(0) ≤ w`, and `i ≥ 0`, let `Sh(r, w, i, λ₂)` be the set of dominant `τ ⊇ λ₂` of length `q` with `|τ/λ₂| = i`, every column of the skew shape `τ/λ₂` of length at most `r`, and no box of content `w`. Let `m(c)` be the largest number of boxes of content `c` in `τ/λ₂` over `τ ∈ Sh(r, w, i, λ₂)`, and

```text
D(r, w, i, λ₂) := ∏_c (w − c)^{m(c)},
```

a positive integer dividing `((w + q − λ₂(q − 1) − 1)!)^r`, so that `(ℓ − 1)·v_ℓ(D) ≤ r·(w + q − λ₂(q − 1) − 1)`. `Suggested.lean` writes `Sh`, `m` and `D` as `pieriShapes`, `pieriContentMultiplicity` and `pieriDenominator`.

(a) *(Block first.)* Let `λ = (w^r, λ₂)`, a dominant weight of `GL_{r+q}`, `ξ : {0, …, r + q − 1} → ℤ` non-increasing, `i′ ∈ ℤ`, and `e_L ≥ 0` such that `ℓ^{e_L}·∇_ℤ(λ₂)_{μ₂} ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ₂) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)` for every weight `μ₂` of `GL_q` with `Σ_bξ(r + b)(λ₂(b) − μ₂(b)) ≤ i′`. Then for every `i ≥ 0` and every weight `μ` of `GL_{r+q}` with `Σ_{a<r}μ(a) = r·w − i` and `Σ_aξ(a)(λ(a) − μ(a)) ≤ i′`,

```text
ℓ^{e_L + v_ℓ(D(r, w, i, λ₂))} · ∇_ℤ(λ)_μ ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ).
```

More precisely, `ℓ^{e_L}·D(r, w, i, λ₂)·∇_ℤ(λ)_μ` lies in the `ℤ_(ℓ)`-span of the products of `i` undivided lowering operators `E_{ba}` (`a < r ≤ b`) applied to the Weyl lattice of the Levi factor `GL_q` generated from `v_λ`. `Suggested.lean` states the first assertion over every discrete valuation ring `O` of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `ℓ` (equivalent to the form over `ℤ_(ℓ)` by this roadmap's argument of 2.1 (a), applied weight space by weight space) as `pow_smul_dualWeylLatticeWeightSpace_le_weylLattice_of_block_first`.

(b) *(Block last.)* Let `λ = (λ₂, w^r)` with `w ≤ λ₂(b) ≤ M` for all `b`, and `λ₂^*(b) := M − λ₂(q − 1 − b)`. Under the hypothesis of (a) for the weights `μ₂` with `Σ_bξ(b)(λ₂(b) − μ₂(b)) ≤ i′`: for every weight `μ` with `Σ_{a≥q}μ(a) = r·w + i` and `Σ_aξ(a)(λ(a) − μ(a)) ≤ i′`, `ℓ^{e_L + v_ℓ(D(r, M − w, i, λ₂^*))}·∇_ℤ(λ)_μ ⊂ Δ_ℤ(λ) ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)`; the denominator does not depend on `M`. For a dominant `λ` of `GL_N` the *monomial lattice* `Mon_ℤ(λ)` is the `ℤ`-span of the monomials `x^α` of column degrees `λ⁰` (the column sums of `α` are `λ⁰_s`), with the normalised form `⟨·,·⟩_λ` extended to all polynomials of these degrees; the monomials are orthogonal, with `⟨x^α, x^α⟩_λ = α!/⟨v_λ, v_λ⟩_F`. ### 3.1 The Fischer norm of a product of leading minors

On the polynomials in the entries of an `n × l` matrix of variables (`l ≤ n`) with the Fischer form of the Conventions, `⟨f, g⟩ = Σ_μ conj(f_μ)·g_μ·μ!` (`f = Σ_μ f_μx^μ`, `μ! = ∏ μ_{ab}!`), let `Δ_k` be the leading `k × k` minor and `a_0 ≥ ⋯ ≥ a_{l−1} ≥ 0` integers, `a_l := 0`. Then

`‖∏_{k=1}^{l} Δ_k^{a_{k−1} − a_k}‖² = ∏_{i=0}^{l−1}(a_i + l − 1 − i)! / ∏_{0≤i<j<l}(a_i − a_j + j − i) = H(a)`,

the product of the hook lengths of the Young diagram `(a_0, …, a_{l−1})`. The same holds for the minors on any nested families of `k` rows and of `k` columns (`1 ≤ k ≤ l`), and the Fischer form is multiplicative on products of polynomials in variables of disjoint sets of rows.

*The Capelli identity.* Prove: for a commutative ring `R`, a `K × K` matrix of variables `x` and the polarisation operators `E_{ab} := Σ_s x_{a,s}·∂/∂x_{b,s}` on `R[x]`, multiplication by `det(x)` composed with `det(∂) := Σ_π sgn(π)·∏_k ∂/∂x_{π(k),k}` is the column-ordered Capelli determinant

```text
det(x)·det(∂) = Σ_{π∈S_K} sgn(π) · ∏_{k=0}^{K−1} ( E_{π(k),k} + (K − 1 − k)·δ_{π(k),k} ),   the factors in increasing order of k,
```

that is, the determinant `A_K(u)` of ClassicalGroups §6.6 (rows and columns there from `1`) at `u = K − 1`, evaluated on the polarisation operators. *Route.* Both sides are computed in the ring of operators generated by the multiplications `x_{a,s}` and the derivations `∂/∂x_{b,t}`, using only that the `x_{a,s}` commute, the derivations commute, and `∂/∂x_{b,t}∘x_{a,s} = x_{a,s}∘∂/∂x_{b,t} + δ_{ab}δ_{st}`: expanding the column-ordered product, the commutators of each derivative with the variables of the later factors produce the diagonal shifts `K − 1 − k`, and the antisymmetrisation over `π` turns the fully reordered terms into `det(x)·det(∂)` (Capelli; Howe–Umeda). [FormalProof] states the identity over `ℚ` as DetDetId (`Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/HookLengthBound/DetShiftNormOf.lean`), proved as capelli_determinant_identity (`Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/HookLengthBound/CapelliOps.lean`) from the operator identity CapAbsMain (`Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/HookLengthBound/CapelliDefs.lean`), which holds in every ring with those relations.

*Route.* Induction on the diagram, adding its columns from the shortest to the tallest: when a column of height `K` is added to a diagram with `a_K = 0` (the new column at least as tall as every column present), the vector `v_a = ∏_kΔ_k^{a_{k−1}−a_k}` is multiplied by `Δ_K`, and `Δ_Kv_a` involves only the first `K` rows and columns. The adjoint of multiplication by a variable for the Fischer form is the partial derivative, so the adjoint of `Δ_K` is the corresponding determinant `Δ_K(∂)` of derivatives, and `‖Δ_Kv_a‖² = ⟨Δ_K(∂)Δ_Kv_a, v_a⟩`. The Capelli identity (above) for the `K × K` block, applied to the highest-weight vector `Δ_Kv_a` of that block, gives `det(x)·det(∂)(Δ_Kv_a) = ∏_{i<K}(a_i + K − i)·Δ_Kv_a`; cancelling `det(x) = Δ_K` gives `Δ_K(∂)Δ_Kv_a = ∏_{i<K}(a_i + K − i)·v_a`, the ratio of the two hook products. Permutations of the variables preserve the Fischer form, which gives the nested families; monomials in disjoint variables have product factorials. The identity is known (the Capelli identity; the hook-length product of Frame, Robinson and Thrall); [FormalProof] states it for an `n × l` matrix as HookNorm (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TorsionBlocks/BottomMinor.lean`).

### 3.3 The dual of an orthogonal sum of Weyl lattices of self-dual lattices

Let `E` be a CM field of degree `2f` with complex conjugation `c_E`, `Φ` a CM type (`f` embeddings), and `𝕃` a number field containing the images of the embeddings in `Φ` (hence totally complex) and carrying an involution `c` with `c∘τ = τ∘c_E` for every `τ ∈ Φ` that is the complex conjugation at one complex place of `𝕃` (for instance `𝕃` a CM field, or a Galois extension of `ℚ`); let `X` be a totally positive definite Hermitian space of dimension `ν` over `E`, and `Λ⁺ = (Λ⁺_τ)_{τ∈Φ}` dominant weights of `GL_ν`. For a self-dual `O_E`-lattice `Λ ⊂ X` (`Λ^∨ = Λ`, HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.0), the *Weyl lattice of highest weight `Λ⁺` of `Λ`* is the `O_𝕃`-lattice `W_{Λ⁺}(Λ)` in `⊗_{τ∈Φ}V_{Λ⁺_τ}(X ⊗_{E,τ} 𝕃)` whose completion at every prime is `⊗_τΔ(Λ⁺_τ)` in the coordinates of a local basis of `Λ` (independent of the basis: the Weyl lattice is a representation of the group scheme `GL_ν` over `ℤ`, so `Δ_ℤ(Λ⁺_τ) ⊗ O` is stable under `GL_ν(O)` for every local ring `O`, ReductiveGroups §10B), with the invariant metric at every complex embedding `σ` of `𝕃`: the form `⟨·,·⟩_{Λ⁺_τ}` transported by an orthonormal basis of `X ⊗_{E,σ∘τ} ℂ` (independent of the basis by unitary invariance). Let `(Λ_i)_{i∈I}` be a nonempty finite family of self-dual lattices in `X`, and `M` the dual of the orthogonal sum `⊕_{i∈I}W_{Λ⁺}(Λ_i)`, the dual Hermitian lattice with the dual metrics. Let `R ≥ 1` with `Λ⁺_τ(0) − Λ⁺_τ(ν − 1) ≤ R` for every `τ`. Then

```text
μ_max(M) ≤ 4·f·ν·R·log(νR),
```

independently of the number of summands; more precisely `μ_max(M) ≤ Σ_{τ∈Φ} log H((Λ⁺_τ)⁰)`, the logarithm of the product of the Fischer norms of the highest-weight vectors. For an arbitrary number field `𝕃` containing the images the same bound follows by extension of scalars to such a field (SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §3.5).

*Route.* Put `s := ∏_{τ∈Φ}⟨v_{Λ⁺_τ}, v_{Λ⁺_τ}⟩_F`, a positive integer, the product of the hook products of the `(Λ⁺_τ)⁰` (3.2 (a)); `s` times the form of `W_{Λ⁺}(Λ)` is the tensor product of the Fischer forms themselves, transported. *(i) Duals.* The orthogonal sum `⊕_iW_{Λ⁺}(Λ_i)` is a lattice, and its Hermitian dual for the orthogonal sum of the forms is the orthogonal sum of the Hermitian duals of the summands (HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.0). The Hermitian dual of `W_{Λ⁺}(Λ)` is the *dual Weyl lattice* of the Hermitian dual `Λ^∨`: the `O_𝕃`-lattice whose localisation at every prime is `⊗_τ∇(Λ⁺_τ)` in the coordinates of a local basis of `Λ^∨`; locally this is the duality of `Δ` and `∇` for `⟨·,·⟩_λ` (Conventions) transported by the basis. As `Λ_i^∨ = Λ_i`, the Hermitian dual `N` of the sum is the orthogonal sum of the dual Weyl lattices of the `Λ_i`. *(ii) Integrality.* On the dual Weyl lattice of a self-dual `Λ`, `s` times the form takes values in `O_𝕃`: at a prime, in a local basis of `Λ` and a local basis at the conjugate prime, the Gram matrix of the Hermitian form is integral with unit determinant (self-duality), so the determinant twist is a unit, and the Fischer form of two polynomials with integral coefficients is integral (`⟨x^α, x^β⟩_F = α!·δ_{αβ}`), also after an integral linear substitution of the variables. The Gram matrix `H` of the sum is block diagonal, so `s·H` is integral on `N`; equivalently, `s` times the dual form is integral on the dual `M` of the sum, which `H` identifies with `N`. *(iii) Slopes.* A nonzero Hermitian lattice on which `s` times the form is integral has `μ_max ≤ log s`: for a sublattice of rank `r`, the determinant of `s` times the Gram matrix of a local basis is integral at every prime, so by the product formula its covolume is bounded below and its slope is at most `log s`. *(iv) The constant.* The entries of `(Λ⁺_τ)⁰` are at most `R₀ := ⌊R⌋`, so `⟨v_{Λ⁺_τ}, v_{Λ⁺_τ}⟩_F ≤ (ν!)^{2R₀}·(R₀!)^ν`, and `log((ν!)^{2R₀}·(R₀!)^ν) ≤ 4νR·log(νR)` for `R ≥ 1`; summing over the `f` embeddings, `log s ≤ 4·f·ν·R·log(νR)`. The number of summands does not enter, because the integrality in (ii) is checked summand by summand. The bound is [FormalProof]'s SlopeE (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Statements/CuspidalUnitaryTwists/SlopeE.lean`, first clause), and, for lattices already extended to `𝕃`, GlobalTheory.T.maxSlope_dual_sum_weyl_lattices_le (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/MaxSlopeDualSumWeylLatticesLeStatement.lean`), with the constant `4·f·ν·R·log(νR)` defined as bound in `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/IsSelfDualE.lean`.

*Needs:* 3.2; the duality of `Δ` and `∇` (Conventions); HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.0 (Hermitian duals, self-dual lattices); SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §§2.1, 3.5.

**Checks.**

- `ν = 1`, `R = 1`: each `W_{Λ⁺}(Λ_i)` is a tensor product over `τ` of powers of the self-dual line `Λ_i ⊗_{E,τ} O_𝕃`, of slope `0`, so `μ_max(M) = 0`, and the right side is `4·f·1·1·log 1 = 0`: the bound is attained.
- `ν = 2`, `Λ⁺_τ = (1, 0)` for one `τ` and `0` at the others, `Λ = O_E²` with the standard form: `W` is the standard lattice, `M` its dual, `μ_max(M) = 0 ≤ 4·f·2·log 2`.

### 3.4 The frame map of a twisted Schur functor

Let `E`, `f`, `Φ` be as in 3.3, `𝕃` a number field containing the images of the embeddings in `Φ`, `X` a totally positive definite Hermitian space of dimension `k` over `E` with coordinates orthonormal for the form, `Λ ⊂ X` an `O_E`-lattice of rank `k`, and for every `τ ∈ Φ` a dominant weight `λ(τ) = λ⁰(τ) + s_τ·(1, …, 1)` of `GL_k` (`λ⁰(τ)` a partition with at most `k` parts, `s_τ ∈ ℤ`). Let `𝒩` be the Hermitian `O_𝕃`-lattice of the elements of `⊗_τ∇_𝕃(λ(τ))`, written as functions `φ = ∏_τdet(z_τ)^{s_τ}·P((z_τ)_τ)` with the twist made explicit (`z_τ` a `k × k` matrix of variables, `P` of column degrees `λ⁰(τ)` in `z_τ`), that are integral in local bases of `Λ` — at every prime, `∏_ττ(det B)^{s_τ}·P((τ(B)z_τ)_τ)` has integral coefficients for a local basis `B` of `Λ` written in the coordinates of `X` — with the tensor product of the forms `⟨·,·⟩_{λ⁰(τ)}` at every complex embedding `σ` of `𝕃`. Let `y_1, …, y_k ∈ Λ` be linearly independent over `E`. Then

```text
μ_max(𝒩) ≤ Σ_{τ∈Φ} ( |λ⁰(τ)|·(log k + mean_σ log Σ_a⟨y_a, y_a⟩_{σ∘τ}) + log c_{λ⁰(τ)} ) − k·Σ_{τ∈Φ} s_τ·μ(Λ ⊗_{E,τ} O_𝕃),
```

the mean over the complex embeddings `σ` of `𝕃`; the twist term is `−2·log N(I)/[𝕃:ℚ]` for the fractional ideal `I` of the route, exactly.

*Route.* Let `Y ∈ GL_k(E)` be the matrix of the frame `(y_a)` in the coordinates of `X` and `φ_y(φ)` the family of coefficients of `P((τ(Y)z_τ)_τ)`; `φ_y` is injective. *Integrality.* Let `𝔡` be the fractional ideal of `E` generated at every prime by `det B` for a local basis `B` of `Λ`, and `I := ∏_ττ(𝔡)^{−s_τ}·O_𝕃`. Locally `Y = B·C` with `C` integral (the `y_a` lie in `Λ`); `∏_ττ(det B)^{s_τ}·P((τ(B)z_τ)_τ)` is integral for `φ ∈ 𝒩`, and substituting the integral matrices `τ(C)` keeps it integral; so `φ_y` maps `𝒩` into `I` times the monomial lattice `⊗_τMon(λ⁰(τ))`. *Norms.* The substitution `z ↦ τ(Y)z` has operator norm at most `‖τ(Y)‖_op^{|λ⁰(τ)|} ≤ (Σ_a⟨y_a, y_a⟩_{σ∘τ})^{|λ⁰(τ)|/2}` on the polynomials of degree `|λ⁰(τ)|` for the Fischer form (polar decomposition; a diagonal matrix acts on monomials by monomials in its entries), so `‖φ_yφ‖²_σ ≤ ∏_τ(Σ_a⟨y_a, y_a⟩_{σ∘τ})^{|λ⁰(τ)|}·‖φ‖²_σ`. *The twist.* `−2·log N(I)/[𝕃:ℚ] = Σ_τs_τ·log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔡)/f = −k·Σ_τs_τ·μ(Λ ⊗_{E,τ} O_𝕃)` (the Gram formula of SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §1.2 in the orthonormal coordinates of `X`); the determinant of the frame does not enter, because the map "value with its determinant factor" differs from `φ_y` by an element of `𝕃^×`, which changes no height (product formula). *Assembly.* The monomial lattice has `μ_max ≤ Σ_τ(|λ⁰(τ)|·log k + log c_{λ⁰(τ)})` (3.2 (d); monomials of a tensor product are orthogonal and slopes of lines add), multiplying by `I` adds `−2·log N(I)/[𝕃:ℚ]` (SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §2.1), and the slope inequality for the injective map `φ_y` (SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §2.4 (b)) gives the bound. The bound is derived in this route, from 3.2 (d) and the slope calculus of SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles. This statement is this roadmap's own: [FormalProof] has a maximal-slope test of this shape over totally complex number fields carrying an involution, and the frame bound only for the lattices it uses, with a further factor for the twist and the level. 

*Needs:* 3.2 (d); HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.0; SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §§1.2, 1.3, 1.7, 2.1, 2.4 (b).

**Checks.**

- `k = 1`, `X = E` with the form `z̄z`, `Λ = 𝔞` a fractional ideal of `E`, `s_τ = s` for every `τ` (so `λ⁰ = 0`, `c_0 = 1`): `𝒩` is the fractional ideal `I = ∏_ττ(𝔞)^{−s}·O_𝕃` with the standard metrics, of slope `μ(𝒩) = −2·log N(I)/[𝕃:ℚ] = s·log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞)`, and `μ(𝔞 ⊗_τ O_𝕃) = −log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞)/f`, so the right side is `−f·s·(−log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞)/f) = s·log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞)`: the bound is an equality, the frame contributes nothing, and the opposite sign of the twist term would give `−s·log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞)`, which is false whenever `s·log N_{E/ℚ}(𝔞) > 0`.
