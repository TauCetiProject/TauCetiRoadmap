# Roadmap: reducibility ideals and ultrafilter patching

**Summary.** This roadmap adds to the commutative algebra of [DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra](../../DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra/README.md) the statements that patching arguments with residually reducible data use. Rings are commutative with identity, and a local ring is nonzero. It builds Koszul Euler characteristics of sections of standard multigraded algebras over a local base, and the order `length(M/aM) − length(M[a])` of an element on a module finite over a discrete valuation ring; `ℤ`-graded local algebras with a torus-fixed integral point, with their cotangent lattices, two-signed points and a graded formal implicit function theorem; the generic determinantal incidence and determinantal sections; finite `ℤ/2`-graded algebras `𝔸 = B ⊕ N` with the reducibility ideal `ired(N)` of `B`, generated by the products of two odd elements; propagation of a property along the components of a spectrum; limit groups, and patching with a fixed ultrafilter of modules over a ring in which one element is inverted; and weighted presentations of coefficient rings.

The headline theorems are these. (1) *The graded cotangent bound* (4.9). Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring whose fraction field `E` has characteristic `0`, with normalized valuation `v` extended to an algebraic closure `Ē`. Let `A` be a `ℤ`-graded `O`-algebra whose part `A_0` of weight `0` is a complete Noetherian local `O`-algebra with the residue field of `O`, generated over `A_0` by finitely many homogeneous elements of weights in `{±1, ±2}`, with an `O`-point `x` that kills every nonzero weight. Let `I` be generated by homogeneous `F_0, …, F_r` of weight `−1` (`r ≥ 1`) and homogeneous elements of weights `−2`, `1`, `2`, and of weight `0` vanishing at `x`, and let `a ∈ A_0`. Assume (M1)–(M5): `x` is a graded smooth point at which the equations of weights `−1`, `−2` and `0` span the cotangent lattices of these weights over `E`, with one equation more than coordinates in weight `−1`, as many in weights `−2` and `0`, and one fewer in weights `1` and `2`; the image of `A_0` in `A/I` is finitely generated over `O`; over the restriction to `A_0` of an `Ē`-point `x₁ ≠ x` of `A/I` that kills every nonzero weight there are not both a point that kills the negative weights but not the positive ones and a point that kills the positive weights but not the negative ones; at every two-signed `Ē`-point of `A/I` any two point derivations are proportional; and `a` vanishes at `x` and at no two-signed point. Then the two-signed `Ē`-points of `A/I` form finitely many `𝔾_m`-orbits, and the length of the weight `−1` cotangent lattice at `x` modulo the `O`-span of the `F_i` is at most `2·Σ_ξ v(ξ(a))`, the sum over representatives `ξ` of these orbits. (2) *Determinantal presentations* (6.11). Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformizer `π`, `B` a complete Noetherian local `O`-algebra with the residue field of `O`, and `𝔸 = B ⊕ N` a commutative `ℤ/2`-graded `B`-algebra, finite over `B`, with even part `B`, odd part `N` and `N·N ⊆ 𝔪_B`; put `𝔧 = (π) + 𝔪_B² + ired(N)`. If `N` is generated by `m_1, …, m_g` and there are `X_1, …, X_{D₀} ∈ 𝔪_B` with `𝔪_B ⊆ (X) + 𝔧` and `O`-linear forms `λ_{ij}(X)` whose `g²` coefficient vectors are linearly independent modulo `π`, with `Σ_j λ_{ij}(X)m_j ∈ 𝔧·N` for every `i`, then every prime `Z` of `B` not containing `ired(N)` with `dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀` lies in every prime `Q ⊇ Ann_B N`. (3) *Patching up to an element* (6.13). Let `𝒰` be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on `ℕ`, `Λ` a complete Noetherian local ring with finite residue field, `S_∞ = Λ[[σ_1, …, σ_s]]`, `R` a Noetherian local ring with a ring map `S_∞ → R`, `f ∈ R`, `𝔦` an ideal of `R`, `𝒳` an `R`-module finite over `Λ` and killed by the `σ_i`, and `M_N` (`N ≥ 1`) `R`-modules such that: for all `j, k ≥ 1` and `𝒰`-almost all `N`, `M_N/(𝔪_Λ^j, (σ)^k)M_N` is free of finite rank over `S_∞/(𝔪_Λ^j, (σ)^k)`; for every `j` and `𝒰`-almost all `N` there are `R`-linear maps `𝒳/𝔪_Λ^j𝒳 ⇄ M_N/((σ), 𝔪_Λ^j)M_N` whose two composites are multiplication by `f`; and for some `n`, some generating set of `𝔦`, every generator `t`, every `j` and `𝒰`-almost all `N`, some `a ∈ R` with `(ft)^n − a ∈ 𝔪_R^j` kills `M_N`. Then the `(σ)`-adic completion `𝔹` of `R[1/f]` is Noetherian, and there is a finite `𝔹`-module `𝕄` on which `σ_1, …, σ_s` is quasi-regular, with `𝕄/(σ)𝕄 ≅ 𝔹 ⊗_R 𝒳` and `𝔦𝔹 ⊆ √(Ann_𝔹 𝕄)`. (4) *The order of an element* (3.17) (classical; Fulton, Appendix A). Let `O` be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field `E` and normalized valuation `v`, `B` a commutative `O`-algebra, `M` a `B`-module finitely generated over `O`, and `a ∈ B` acting injectively on `M ⊗_O E`. Then `M/aM` and `M[a] = ker(a : M → M)` have finite length over `O`, and `length_O(M/aM) − length_O M[a] = v(det_E(a | M ⊗_O E))`.

The roadmap extends DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra, whose relative cotangent spaces, multiplicities and Koszul parameter inequality, diagonal extraction, support statements and connectedness dimension it uses throughout; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §3.12a states the order of an element on a module free of finite rank over a discrete valuation ring, which is the case of 3.17 (1) for such modules. [DerivedModuleAlgebra](../../DerivedModuleAlgebra/README.md) gives the Koszul complexes of Layer 3, [GrothendieckEulerForms](../../GrothendieckEulerForms/README.md) the Euler characteristics, and [ProfiniteProPGroups](../../../Completed/ProfiniteProPGroups/README.md) the free profinite group on a finite set used for limit groups. In the family [RiemannHypothesisForDirichletLFunctions](../README.md), [UniformWildKernelBounds](../UniformWildKernelBounds/README.md) applies these statements to residually reducible deformation problems graded by a diagonal torus and to patching at dimension-one primes.

The layers carry the numbers of the layers of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra that they continue, and the targets continue its numbering. Layer 3 below defines the multigraded Koszul Euler characteristic `χ(ts; M)` with its calculus (additivity, symmetry, positivity, the product rule for a factor of degree zero, the peeling of powers and a lower bound) and the order `θ(a; M)` with its expressions by a determinant, by primes and by embeddings. Layer 4 sets up graded local algebras with a torus-fixed integral point (cotangent lattices, graded smooth points, two-signed points, the derivation bound, the graded formal implicit function theorem) and proves the graded cotangent bound with the monomial count it uses. Layer 6 proves that the generic determinantal incidence is irreducible and that determinantal sections are chained in a dimension, applies this to `ℤ/2`-graded algebras with a determinantal presentation, propagates a property along chains of adjacent components, constructs limit groups and modules patched up to an inverted element with their supports, and gives weighted presentations of coefficient rings: the coordinate grading, its graded part and the rescaling of an artinian coefficient ring.

## Conventions

Notation and conventions not restated here are those of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra (its Conventions; relative cotangent spaces, DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.9; Hilbert functions and multiplicities, DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§3.11–3.12; connectedness dimension and arithmetic rank, DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6). Rings are commutative with identity, and a local ring is nonzero. A chosen ultrafilter, basis or coefficient lift is part of the data and stays visible in every statement that uses it.

`O` is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer `π`, fraction field `E`, residue field `k` and normalized valuation `v`; each target says whether `O` is complete and whether `E` has characteristic `0`, and where an algebraic closure `Ē` occurs, `v` is extended to it. In 6.13 the coefficient ring is a complete Noetherian local ring `Λ` with finite residue field, and in 6.14–6.16 `O` is a complete local Noetherian ring with residue field `k`, over which 6.14 defines coefficient rings.

`length_B M` is the length of a `B`-module, a natural number or `∞`; a difference of lengths is written only where both are finite. For `a` acting on `M`, `M[a] = ker(a : M → M)`; `(0 :_M s)` is the annihilator of `s` in `M`; for a graded module, `M(−δ)` is the shift with `M(−δ)_d = M_{d−δ}`. `Ann_R M` is the annihilator and `Supp_R M` the support; `dim R/P` is a Krull dimension, with `dim ∅ = −1`; `c(R)` is the connectedness dimension and `ara(I)` the arithmetic rank of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6.

A `ℤ`-grading `A = ⊕_j A_j` is read as an action of the torus `𝔾_m` on the points of `A`, `A_j` being the part of weight `j`. Variables of a power series ring `E[[t_i]]` may carry weights; the weights of monomials are then defined, but such a ring is not graded. The Koszul Euler characteristic `χ(ts; M)` (3.16), the order `θ(a; M)` (3.17), the saturation `sat(x)`, the cotangent lattices `cot_j(x)`, the lengths `cotLen_j(F)`, two-signed points and graded smooth points (4.8), ideals chained in a dimension (6.10), the reducibility ideal `ired(N)` (6.11) and coefficient rings (6.14) are defined where they first appear.

## Layer 3: Multigraded Koszul Euler characteristics and orders of elements

These two targets continue Layer 3 of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra (homological dimension, local duality and multiplicity): a multigraded form of the Koszul Euler characteristic over a local base, and the order of an element on a module finite over a discrete valuation ring. Both feed the graded cotangent bound 4.9.

### 3.16 Koszul Euler characteristics of multigraded modules over a local base

*Origin:* adapted from Serre (1965), Chapter IV, the Koszul Euler characteristic and its positivity, and from the mixed
multiplicities of Teissier (1973), Chapter I, §2, Proposition 2.1, which refers also to Bhattacharya (1957): here for sections of a standard `ℕ^c`-graded algebra over a
Noetherian local base, as a function `χ(ts; M)` of large multidegrees defined for admissible lists, with the product rule
for a factor of degree `0` (8), the peeling of a power (10) and the lower bound (11).

*Method of the formal proof:* `χ` is defined by the recursion on the list of sections; positivity is proved by induction
on the number of sections and dévissage to quotients `R/𝔭` by homogeneous primes: if the first section is outside `𝔭` it
is a nonzerodivisor, and if it is inside it is replaced by a homogeneous element of the same degree outside `𝔭` (Serre's
argument); the lower bound for sections of equal degree peels one power of `β` at a time and ends with positivity.

Let `(B, m_B)` be a Noetherian local ring, `c ≥ 1`, and `R = ⊕_{μ∈ℕ^c}R_μ` a **standard `ℕ^c`-graded `B`-algebra**: `R_0 = B`
and `R` is generated over `B` by the finite `B`-modules `R_{e_1}, …, R_{e_c}`; let `R_+` be the ideal generated by the products
`R_{e_1}⋯R_{e_c}`. For a finite `ℤ^c`-graded `R`-module `M` and a finite list `ts = ((δ_1, s_1), …, (δ_m, s_m))` of
homogeneous elements `s_i ∈ R_{δ_i}`, the list is **admissible for `M`** if `(m_B·R_+)^N ⊆ Ann_R M + (s_1, …, s_m)` for some
`N`: the common zeros of the sections on the support of `M` in the multiprojective spectrum lie over the closed point of
`Spec B`. For an admissible list define the function `χ(ts; M)` of `d ∈ ℤ^c`, for `d` with all coordinates large, by
`χ(∅; M)(d) = length_B M_d` and

`χ((δ, s) :: ts; M) = χ(ts; M/sM) − χ(ts; (0 :_M s)(−δ))`

(the lists `ts` for `M/sM` and for `(0 :_M s)(−δ)` are again admissible, and all lengths are finite for such `d`; `χ` is
defined only on admissible data). For `f : ℤ^c → ℤ` and a degree `δ` put `(Δ_δf)(d) = f(d) − f(d − δ)`, `Δ_{ts} = ∏_iΔ_{δ_i}`
(it depends only on the degrees), and `H_M(d) = length_B M_d` when these lengths are finite. Prove, for admissible lists and
all `d` with all coordinates large:

1. `χ` is additive in short exact sequences of graded modules with a common admissible list;
2. `χ(ts; M)` does not depend on the order of the list;
3. if `s` is a nonzerodivisor on `M`, `χ((δ, s) :: ts; M) = χ(ts; M/sM)`;
4. `χ(ts; M)` depends on each `s_i` only modulo `Ann_R M`;
5. `χ(ts; M) ≥ 0`;
6. `χ(ts; M″) ≤ χ(ts; M)` for a graded quotient `M″` of `M`;
7. `χ(ts ++ us; M) ≤ χ(us; M/(ts)M)`;
8. (*product with a degree-zero factor*) for `β ∈ B = R_0` and `s ∈ R_δ`, if `(δ, βs) :: ts` is admissible for `M`, then so
   are `(0, β) :: ts` and `(δ, s) :: ts`, and `χ((δ, βs) :: ts; M) = χ((0, β) :: ts; M) + χ((δ, s) :: ts; M)`;
9. (*finite-length pieces*) if every `M_d` has finite length, then `χ(ts; M) = Δ_{ts}H_M` for every admissible list: the
   value depends only on the degrees, not on the sections;
10. (*peeling a power*) if `β ∈ B` is a nonzerodivisor on `M` and every graded piece of `M/βM` has finite length, then
    `χ((δ, β^as) :: ts; M) = a·Δ_{ts}H_{M/βM} + χ((δ, s) :: ts; M)` for every integer `a ≥ 0`;
11. (*lower bound*) under the hypotheses of (10), for an admissible list `L := ((δ_i, β^{a_i}s_i))_{i≤q} ++ us`,
    `χ(L; M) ≥ Σ_{i≤q} a_i·Δ_{L∖i}H_{M/βM}`, where `L∖i` is `L` without its `i`-th entry (`Δ_{L∖i}` depends only on its
    degrees); when `δ_1 = ⋯ = δ_q` this is `(Σ_ia_i)·Δ_{L∖1}H_{M/βM}`.

`χ(ts; M)(d)` is, for large `d`, the Euler characteristic of the degree-`d` part of the Koszul complex `K(s_1, …, s_m) ⊗ M`.
This description is classical, not a statement of [FormalProof], which defines `χ` by the recursion alone.

*Route.* Finiteness: admissibility puts a power of `m_B` on the relevant graded pieces for large `d`, and each piece of a
finite graded module over a standard graded algebra is finite over `B`. (1) and (2) are the long exact sequences of the
Koszul complex and the symmetry of the Koszul complex; (3) is the vanishing of `(0 :_M s)`; (4) the Koszul complex depends
on the action on `M` ([FormalProof],
`Formalism/SharpRestriction/ValuationFrobeniusEpsilon/LeastPrimeTwists/QuotientShiftIrreducible/NoetherianQuotientCalc/RuleCong.lean` (`Formalism.MainNotions.V.Pf.V2.Calc.Cong.chi_cong`)).
(5) by induction on the number of sections and dévissage of `M` to graded quotients `R/𝔭` by
homogeneous relevant primes: if `s ∉ 𝔭` use (3); if `s ∈ 𝔭`, then
`χ((δ, s) :: ts; R/𝔭) = χ(ts; R/𝔭) − χ(ts; (R/𝔭)(−δ)) = χ(ts; (R/𝔭)/σ)` for any `σ ∈ R_δ ∖ 𝔭`, by (1) on
`0 → (R/𝔭)(−δ) → R/𝔭 → (R/𝔭)/σ → 0`; such `σ` exists because `𝔭` is relevant and `R` is generated in the degrees `e_i`
(Serre's positivity argument). (6) and (7) are (5) applied to a kernel and to the annihilator terms, with (1) and (2).
(8) The exact sequences `0 → (0 :_M s) → (0 :_M βs) →^{s} (0 :_M β)` and `M/sM →^{β} M/βsM → M/βM → 0` have isomorphic
defect terms (`(0 :_M β)/s(0 :_M βs)` in both), so the classes of `M/βsM − (0 :_M βs)(−δ)` and of
`(M/βM − (0 :_M β)) + (M/sM − (0 :_M s)(−δ))` agree in the Grothendieck group of graded modules; apply `χ(ts; ·)` and (1).
Admissibility passes because `(βs) ⊆ (β) ∩ (s)`. (9) By induction on the list: `0 → (0 :_M s)(−δ) → M(−δ) → M → M/sM → 0`
gives `H_{M/sM} − H_{(0:_Ms)(−δ)} = Δ_δH_M`. (10) (8) and (3): `χ((0, β^a) :: ts; M) = χ(ts; M/β^aM) = a·χ(ts; M/βM)` (filter
`M/β^aM` by the `β^jM/β^{j+1}M ≅ M/βM` and use (1)), which is `a·Δ_{ts}H_{M/βM}` by (9). (11) Peel the powers one section
at a time with (10) and (2) (peeling does not change the degrees), then drop the last term by (5);
for equal degrees this is [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/LaDifPerm.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.la_holds`).
This is Serre's
multiplicity symbol in multigraded form over a local base (Serre, Ch. IV; mixed multiplicities in the sense of Teissier and
Bhattacharya); in geometric terms, intersection numbers of Cartier divisors on a scheme proper over `Spec B` whose common
zero locus lies over the closed point. *Needs:* DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§1.3–1.5, 3.11–3.13; DerivedModuleAlgebra §0.K.1 (Koszul complexes);
GrothendieckEulerForms §§5.1–5.2; Mathlib `GradedAlgebra`, `DirectSum`, `Module.length`.

**Checks.**

- `B = O`, `c = 1`, `R = M = O[T]`, one section `πⁿT` of degree `1`: `χ(d) = length_O(O/πⁿ) = n` for `d ≥ 1`, the length of
  the zero of `πⁿ` at the closed point; with the section `T` the value is `0` for `d ≥ 1`. By (10) with `β = π`, `a = n`,
  `s = T`: `n·Δ_∅H_{O[T]/π} + 0 = n`, since `H_{k[T]}(d) = 1`.
- Admissibility is needed for finiteness: the zero section on `O[T]` is not admissible, and `length_O O[T]_d` is infinite.
- (8) needs a factor of degree `0`: for two sections of positive degree the product rule shifts one term by the degree of the
  other, and for functions that are not eventually constant the shift matters.

### 3.17 The order of an element on a module finite over the coefficient ring

*Origin:* published: Fulton (1984), Appendix A, the order `θ(a; M)` of an element on a module finitely generated over a
discrete valuation ring, with its expressions by a determinant and by primes (1), of which the expression by embeddings
(2) is a rewriting through the norm. The case of (1) for `M` free of finite rank over `O` is stated in
DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §3.12a.

*Method of the formal proof:* for the inequality form of (2) recorded below, a dévissage of `M` over `B` along a prime
filtration: `length_O(M/aM) − length_O M[a]` vanishes on modules of finite length and is additive in short exact
sequences, as are the localised lengths, and for a quotient `B/𝔭` that is a domain finite over `O`,
`length_O((B/𝔭)/a(B/𝔭)) = v(N_{κ(𝔭)/E}(ā)) = Σ_σ v(σ(a))`, the norm being the product over the embeddings in
characteristic `0`. The route written here differs: it passes to `M ⊗_O E` as a module of finite length over the
Artinian algebra `B̄ ⊗_O E`, reads the factors `κ(𝔭)` off a composition series and uses the determinant, with Smith
normal form for `M` free over `O`.

Let `O` be a discrete valuation ring (any characteristic) with fraction field `E` and normalized valuation `v`, `B` a
commutative `O`-algebra, `M` a `B`-module which is finitely generated over `O`, and `a ∈ B` acting injectively on
`M ⊗_O E`. Prove:

1. `M/aM` and `M[a] = ker(a : M → M)` have finite length, and
   `θ(a; M) := length_O(M/aM) − length_O M[a] = v(det_E(a | M ⊗_O E)) = Σ_𝔭 length_{B_𝔭}(M_𝔭)·v(N_{κ(𝔭)/E}(ā))`,
   the sum over the finitely many primes `𝔭` of `B` in the support of `M` with `𝔭 ∩ O = 0` (their residue fields `κ(𝔭)` are
   finite over `E`, and `ā` is the image of `a`);
2. (*embeddings*) for any extension of `v` to an algebraic closure `Ē`,
   `θ(a; M) = Σ_σ length_{B_{𝔭_σ}}(M_{𝔭_σ})·[κ(𝔭_σ) : E]_i·v(σ(a))`, the sum over the `O`-algebra maps `σ : B → Ē` with
   `𝔭_σ = ker σ` as in (1), `[κ : E]_i` the inseparable degree; in characteristic `0` (or whenever the `κ(𝔭)/E` are
   separable) the factor is `1`.

The finiteness and the first equality of (1), with `v(d) = length_O(O/dO)` for `d ∈ O` (Mathlib's `Ring.ord O d`, the normalized
valuation of `d` by `Ring.ord_eq_addVal`), are stated in `Suggested.lean` as `length_quot_eq_length_ker_add_valuation_det`.

*The form of the formal proof.* Of the statements of 3.17, [FormalProof] proves and uses (2) in characteristic `0`, for `O`
complete and `B` a complete Noetherian local `O`-algebra with residue field `k`, in the form `θ(a; M) ≤ c·Σ_σ v(σ(a))` when every
`length_{B_{𝔭_σ}}(M_{𝔭_σ}) ≤ c` and every `v(σ(a)) ≥ 0`; (1) and the general form of (2) are published (Fulton, Appendix A),
not statements of [FormalProof].

*Route.* `B` acts on `M` through `B̄ := B/Ann_B(M)`, a subalgebra of `End_O(M)`, finite over `O`. (1) Both `θ(a; ·)` and
`v(det_E(a | · ⊗ E))` are additive in short exact sequences of such modules (snake lemma) and vanish on modules of finite
length; for `M` free over `O` the first equality is Smith normal form. `B̄ ⊗_O E ⊆ End_E(M ⊗ E)` is finite-dimensional over `E`, hence
Artinian (`B ⊗ E` itself need not be: `B = O[X]`, `M = O` with `XM = 0`), and `M ⊗ E` is a module of finite length over it, with a
composition series whose factors are the `κ(𝔭)`, `𝔭` as in (1) (the primes of `B̄ ⊗ E` are those of `B` containing `Ann_B(M)` and
meeting `O` in `0`), each occurring `length_{B_𝔭}(M_𝔭)` times;
on `κ(𝔭)`, `det_E(ā) = N_{κ(𝔭)/E}(ā)`. (2) `N_{κ/E}(ā) = ∏_σσ(ā)^{[κ:E]_i}` over the `E`-embeddings `σ : κ → Ē`, and `v` is
multiplicative on `Ē`. *Needs:* DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §3.12 (associativity and finite-length additivity) and §3.12a (the case of (1) for `M` free of finite rank over `O`); Mathlib `Algebra.norm`, `LinearMap.det`,
`Module.length`, `Ring.ord`, `Ring.ord_eq_addVal`; Tau Ceti `Matrix.length_quotient_range_mulVecLin_eq_ord_det` (the free case).

**Checks.**

## Layer 4: Torus-graded algebras and the graded cotangent bound

These two targets continue Layer 4 of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra (finiteness, integral points and completed local coordinates): `ℤ`-graded local algebras with a torus-fixed integral point, and a bound for the weight `−1` cotangent length at that point by valuations at the two-signed orbits.

### 4.8 Graded algebras with a torus-fixed integral point

*Origin:* particular to this proof: weighted forms of the cotangent space at an integral point and of the formal implicit
function theorem for an algebra graded by a torus with a fixed integral point, set up for the graded cotangent bound.

*Method of the formal proof:* for (2), a point that kills `A_{−2}` kills every negative weight, a coordinate of weight
`−1` entering through its square, and likewise for `A_2` and the positive weights, so a point is two-signed exactly when
it is nonzero on `A_{−2}A_2`; point derivations kill the saturation of `(ker x)²`, so the module `M` of (3) injects
into homomorphisms from a quotient of the cotangent lattice to `O/c`, and cardinalities give the length; and the graded
implicit function theorem is proved by successive approximation, each step of which has the right weights. Of (1),
[FormalProof] has the finite generation; the rank `n_{−1}` of `cot_{−1}(x)` at a graded smooth point is proved by the
route below, and no statement of it was found in [FormalProof]. The route written here obtains the weights in (4)
instead from the uniqueness of the solution and the invariance of the equations under the torus.

Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring whose fraction field `E` has characteristic `0`, with uniformizer `π`, residue
field `k`, and normalized valuation `v` extended to an algebraic closure `Ē`. A **graded local algebra with a torus-fixed
point** is a `ℤ`-graded `O`-algebra `A = ⊕_{j∈ℤ}A_j` such that `A_0` is a complete Noetherian local `O`-algebra with residue
field `k` and `A` is generated over `A_0` by finitely many homogeneous elements of nonzero weight, together with an
`O`-algebra map `x : A → O` killing every `A_j`, `j ≠ 0`. `𝔾_m` acts on `Ē`-points by `(u·ξ)(f) = u^jξ(f)` for `f ∈ A_j`. A point
is **negative-zero** if it kills every `A_j` with `j < 0`, **positive-zero** if it kills every `A_j` with `j > 0`, and
**two-signed** if it is neither. Give `E[[t_i : i ∈ I]]` (finitely many variables, `t_i` of weight `w_i ∈ ℤ`) its weights on
monomials; it is not a graded ring, and a **graded** map `φ : A → E[[t_i]]` is an `O`-algebra map such that the image of
`A_j` consists of series all of whose monomials have weight `j`. Define:

- `sat(x) = {f ∈ A : cf ∈ (ker x)² for some c ∈ O ∖ {0}}`, the saturation of `(ker x)²`;
- the **cotangent lattice of weight `j`**, `cot_j(x)`, the image of `A_j ∩ ker x` in `A/sat(x)`: a torsion-free `O`-module;
- for `F ⊆ A_j ∩ ker x`, `cotLen_j(F) = length_O(cot_j(x)/span_O F) ∈ ℕ ∪ {∞}`;
- `x` is a **graded smooth point** with coordinate counts `(n_j)_j` if there is a graded map `φ : A → E[[t_i : i ∈ I]]`, with
  `n_j` variables of weight `j`, constant term `x`, every `t_i` the linear part of some `φ(f)`, and every element of `ker x`
  whose image has zero linear part in `sat(x)` (a **chart**).

Prove: (1) when `A` is generated over `A_0` in weights `±1, ±2`, every `A_j` is a finitely generated `A_0`-module, and
`cot_{−1}(x)` is a finitely generated `O`-module, of rank `n_{−1}` at a graded smooth point; (2) an `Ē`-point is two-signed if
and only if it does not vanish on `A_{−2}A_2` when `A` is generated in weights `±1, ±2`; the two-signed points form a
`𝔾_m`-stable set, and an element of `A_0` has the same value along an orbit; (3) (*derivation bound*; *the form of the formal
proof*) let `O` be any discrete valuation ring with finite residue field, `A` an `O`-algebra graded by any abelian group,
`x : A → O` an `O`-algebra map killing `A_j`, `F ⊆ A_j`, `c ∈ O ∖ {0}`, and `M` an `O`-module with an injective **additive** map
`D : M → Hom_O(A, O/c)` whose values kill `F` and are reductions modulo `c` of `O`-valued point derivations of weight `j` at
`x` (`O`-linear maps `d : A → O` with `d(fg) = x(f)d(g) + x(g)d(f)` that vanish on every `A_i`, `i ≠ j`): then
`length_O M ≤ cotLen_j(F)`. With `D` **`O`-linear** the bound holds for any residue field, and, for `O` and `x` as at
the head of this section and any grading group, for every `F ⊆ A_j ∩ ker x` (this roadmap's own form, proved in the Route);
(4) (*graded formal implicit function theorem*) if `φ : A → E[[t_i : i ∈ I]]` is a chart at a
graded smooth point and `Z_1, …, Z_m ∈ A_0 ∩ ker x` are such that the linear parts of the `φ(Z_l)` in the `m = n_0`
variables of weight `0` are linearly independent, then there is a graded map `φ′ : A → E[[t_i : w_i ≠ 0]]` with constant term
`x` and `φ′(Z_l) = 0` for all `l`, whose linear parts in the nonzero-weight variables are those of `φ`; it is `φ` followed by
the substitution of the weight-`0` variables by the unique solution `g` of `φ(Z) = 0`.

*Route.* (1) from finite generation of `A` over the Noetherian `A_0` (each `A_j` is a finite `A_0`-module) and the chart's
linear parts, which embed `cot_{−1}(x)` in `E^{n_{−1}}` with image of full rank (the same argument applies in every weight).
(2) follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/SignHolds.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.sign_holds`).
A product `A_{−2}A_2` of weight `0` is nonzero
at `ξ` exactly when `ξ` is nonzero in both signs, a weight-`±1` coordinate entering through its square. (3) An `O`-valued
point derivation kills `(ker x)²`, hence `sat(x)` (its target is torsion-free), so `D` is an `O`-linear injection of `M` into
`Hom_O(cot_j(x)/span F, O/c)`; and `Hom_O(N, O/c)` has length at most that of a finitely generated `O`-module `N`
(`Hom_O(O/π^b, O/c) ≅ O/π^{min(b, v(c))}`). For a finite residue field an injective additive map already gives the bound:
`M` embeds in the finite set `Hom_O(cot_j(x)/span F, O/c)`, and for finite `O`-modules the cardinality determines the length;
this is the form of [FormalProof]. For `D` `O`-linear the argument above needs no finiteness; this form is the roadmap's own.
With `D` only additive and `k` infinite of characteristic `p` the bound fails: `k²` embeds additively in
`Hom_O(O/π, O/π) = k`. (4) is [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/IftSubstLow.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.ift_holds`), applied in
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/CutHolds.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.cut_holds`).
The formal
implicit function theorem over `E`: the equations `φ(Z_l) = 0` in `E[[s, u]]`
(`s` the weight-`0` variables, `u` the others) have invertible linear part in `s`, so successive approximation gives a
unique `g ∈ E[[u]]^m`, `g(0) = 0`, with `φ(Z)(g(u), u) = 0`. The torus acts on `E[[s, u]]` by scaling the variables by their
weights; the equations are invariant (weight `0`), so by uniqueness `g` is invariant, i.e. every monomial of `g` has weight
`0`, and the substitution preserves weights. *Needs:* DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§1.9, 4.2; Mathlib `GradedAlgebra`, `Derivation`, `MvPowerSeries`.

**Checks.**

- `A = O[[X]][b, c]/(bc − X)`, `b, c` of weights `−1, 1`: `X = bc ∈ (ker x)²`, so `cot_0(x) = 0` although `A_0 = O[[X]]` has a
  nonzero cotangent space at its own point: cotangent lattices are those of `A`, not of `A_0`.
- `A = O[[X]][b, c]/(bc − πX)`: `πX ∈ (ker x)²` but `X ∉ (ker x)²`; with the naive square, `cot_0` would be the torsion module

### 4.9 The graded cotangent bound

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the bound of the weight `−1` cotangent length at a torus-fixed point by twice the
valuations of `a` at the two-signed orbits (1), proved with mixed multiplicities of a multigraded Rees algebra, and the
monomial count (2) that this proof uses.

*Method of the formal proof:* after a Smith normal form `F_i ≡ π^{c_i}·y_i` of the weight `−1` equations and a chart
solving the weight `0` equations, the proof forms the `ℕ³`-graded Rees algebra over `A_0` on `A_{−2}`, `A_2` and
`A_0·F_r² + A_{−2}²·A_2` and bounds one multigraded Koszul Euler characteristic of the squared odd-weight equations, the
even-weight equations and `a` in two ways: from below by `(Σ c_i)·2^{r+r′}`, through the quotient by leading forms along
the fixed point and a count of monomials, and from above by `2^{r+1+r′}·Σ_ξ v(ξ(a))`, through the order of `a` on graded
pieces that are finite over `O`, supported at the two-signed orbits and of bounded length there.

Let `(A, x)` be a graded local algebra with a torus-fixed point over `O` (4.8), generated over `A_0` by homogeneous elements
of weights in `{±1, ±2}`. Let `r ≥ 1` and `s, r′, s′, m ≥ 0`, and let `I` be the ideal generated by homogeneous
`F_0, …, F_r ∈ A_{−1}`, `G_1, …, G_s ∈ A_{−2}`, `E_1, …, E_{r′} ∈ A_1`, `E′_1, …, E′_{s′} ∈ A_2` and
`Z_1, …, Z_m ∈ A_0 ∩ ker x`; let `a ∈ A_0`. Assume:

- (M1) `x` is a graded smooth point with `r` coordinates of weight `−1`, `s` of weight `−2`, `m` of weight `0`, `r′ + 1` of
  weight `1` and `s′ + 1` of weight `2`, and `F`, `G`, `Z` span `cot_{−1}(x)`, `cot_{−2}(x)`, `cot_0(x)` over `E`;
- (M2) the image of `A_0` in `A/I` is a finitely generated `O`-module;
- (M3) for every `Ē`-point `x₁ ≠ x` of `A/I` that is both negative-zero and positive-zero, either no point of `A/I` that is
  negative-zero but not positive-zero, or no point that is positive-zero but not negative-zero, has the same restriction to
  `A_0` as `x₁`;
- (M4) at every two-signed `Ē`-point of `A/I`, any two `Ē`-valued point derivations of `A/I` are proportional;
- (M5) `x(a) = 0`, and `ξ(a) ≠ 0` at every two-signed `Ē`-point `ξ` of `A/I`.

Prove:

1. the two-signed `Ē`-points of `A/I` form finitely many `𝔾_m`-orbits, `cotLen_{−1}(F)` is finite, and for every set `T` of
   orbit representatives `cotLen_{−1}(F) ≤ 2·Σ_{ξ∈T} v(ξ(a))`;
2. (*the monomial count*) let `N₋(n)` be the number of monomials of weighted degree `n` in `r` variables of weight `1` and
   `s` of weight `2`, `N₊(n)` the same for `r′ + 1` variables of weight `1` and `s′ + 1` of weight `2`, and
   `H(μ) := N₋(2μ₀ + 4μ₂)·N₊(2μ₁ + 2μ₂)` for `μ ∈ ℕ³`. Then for all `μ` with large coordinates the mixed difference
   `Δ_{e_0}^{r+s−1}Δ_{e_1}^{r′+s′}Δ_{e_2}H(μ)` equals `2^{r+r′−1}`.

The equations are square in weights `−2` and `0`, have one more equation than coordinates in weight `−1` and one fewer in
weights `1` and `2`, so `Spec(A/I ⊗ E)` has expected dimension one, the torus orbit; the equations do not form a regular
sequence at `x`, where the negative-zero locus (negative coordinates zero, positive coordinates free) has dimension larger than
the expected one.

*Route of (1).* (a) Smith normal form: after replacing `F` by a family with the same `O`-span, `F_i ≡ π^{c_i}y_i` modulo
`sat(x)` for `i < r` with `(y_i)` a basis of `cot_{−1}(x)`, `F_r ∈ sat(x)`, and `cotLen_{−1}(F) = Σ_ic_i`. (b) 4.8(4) solves
`Z = 0` for the weight-`0` coordinates: a chart `φ` in the nonzero-weight coordinates only, with `φ(Z_i) = 0`; `𝔓 = ker φ` is
the branch through `x`. Replacing `a` by `a + gF_k` (`g ∈ A_1`) makes `φ(a) ≠ 0` without changing its values on `A/I`.
(c) Finitely many orbits ([FormalProof] proves this from (M2) and (M4); the following argument uses (M2) only): every
two-signed orbit meets a slice `ξ(f) = 1`, `f` one of the generators of
`A_{−2}`; on it `ξ(h)²` is determined, for `h ∈ A_j`, by the value of `h²f^j` (or `h²g^{−j}`, `g ∈ A_2`), an element of `A_0`,
and the restrictions to `A_0` range over the finitely many `Ē`-points of the image of `A_0` in `A/I`, finite over `O` by (M2).

  (the part of weight `0` of `I` is `A_1F_0 + A_1F_1 ⊆ A_0·(πⁿ − f₀, f₁)`), so finite over `O` by (M2), hence free (a quotient of
  `O[[X, Y]]` by two elements that is finite over `O` is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension `1`, and `π` lies in none of its minimal primes);
  `(A/I)[1/b] = T[b^{±1}]`, so the two-signed orbits are the `Ē`-points of `T`, and (M4) makes each of them a reduced point of `T` (a
  nonzero point derivation of `T` would give a second one of `A/I`, besides `∂/∂b`). So
  `Σ_ξ v(ξ(a)) = length T/aT ≥ length T/(X, Y)T = n` (3.17 with (M5), and the surjection `T/aT → O/πⁿ`), and the bound holds there
  with the constant `1`. Equality at `a = X` holds for `f₀ = X`, `f₁ = Y` (`T = O`, `X = πⁿ`), not in general: for `f₀ = Y`,
  `f₁ = X − Y²`, `T = O` with `Y = πⁿ`, `X = π^{2n}`, so `Σ_ξ v(ξ(X)) = 2n` while `cotLen_{−1}(F) = n`.
- (*constant `1` when the negative side has one generator*) Let `r = 1`, `s = 0`, `A_{−j} = b^jA_0` for `j = 1, 2, 3` with `b`
  a nonzerodivisor, write `F_i = bψ_i` (`ψ_i ∈ A_0`), and assume that **every `Ē`-point of `A/I` with `b ≠ 0` is two-signed**. Then
  `(A/I)_{−1} ≅ A_0/𝔦₀ =: T″`, `𝔦₀ = (ψ_0, ψ_1, bE_j, b²E′_j, Z_l)`, is finite over `O` by (M2) (`I ∩ A_0 ⊆ 𝔦₀`), and flat when
  `A_0` is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension `m + r′ + s′ + 3`; then `cotLen_{−1}(F) = length O/(x(ψ_0), x(ψ_1)) = length T″/𝔭_xT″ ≤ length T″/aT″`,
  a surjection `T″/aT″ ↠ T″/𝔭_xT″` (as `x(a) = 0`), and `length T″/aT″ = Σ_σ v(σ(a))` over all `Ē`-points of
  `T″` (3.17 with (M4), (M5)), which are the orbits with `b ≠ 0`: by the hypothesis, the two-signed ones. So
  `cotLen_{−1}(F) ≤ Σ_ξ v(ξ(a))`. Without the hypothesis the sum over `T″` also counts the points that are positive-zero
  and not negative-zero, and the inequality is not obtained. The hypothesis holds in the universal ring
  `A_0 = O[[bw_i, b²d_j, z]]` by (M3): over every fixed point the `r′ + s′` homogeneous equations in the `r′ + s′ + 2` positive
  coordinates have a nonzero solution, so a negative-zero point lies over it; in the toy family it holds because `f₀ = πⁿ`
  forces `(X, Y) ≠ 0`. `T″` is a complete intersection when `A_0` is regular (Cohen–Macaulay `A_0` gives a Cohen–Macaulay
  `T″`).
- (*where the factor `2` is lost*) In the toy datum the upper bound of (g) is attained (local length `4`, total `4n`) and the
  lower bound of (h) is `2n`: the factor `2` is lost in (h), where the strict transform of `div(a)` is discarded by
  positivity.
- (*the extra equation*) Dropping `F_1` leaves the two-signed locus `bc = πⁿ` with `d` free: a two-parameter family, so the
  orbits are not finitely many, and (M2) and (M4) fail. The `(r + 1)`-st equation of weight `−1` is what cuts the locus down to orbits.

`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/OrbitVanishingLocal.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.LemmaM`) and
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/GradedModulesLength/MainCotSpan.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.main_holds`),
for the statement and its proof; Wiles 1995 (appendix) and Lenstra 1995 for the complete-intersection

## Layer 6: Determinantal sections, propagation and patching up to an element

These targets continue Layer 6 of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra (components, specialization cycles and quotient identification): the generic determinantal incidence and ideals chained in a dimension, finite `ℤ/2`-graded algebras with a determinantal presentation, propagation of a property along components, limit groups and patching up to an inverted element, and weighted presentations of coefficient rings.

### 6.10 Determinantal sections

*Origin:* adapted from Grothendieck's connectedness theorem (SGA 2, Exposé XIII), applied to the locus `V(m·y, det m)`
of a generic matrix and a generic column, which is a variety of complexes
(generic matrices with product zero, under rank conditions by minors): here that locus in power series over a
complete Noetherian local domain `R₀` (1), and its sections, chained in a dimension (2).

*Method of the formal proof:* the radical of `(m·y, det m)` is shown to be the kernel of an explicit parametrisation of
the matrices with a kernel vector by a polynomial ring, checked on field points, hence prime, and it passes to power
series because it is homogeneous; its height is at most `g + 1` by Cramer's rule and Krull's height theorem; the sections
then follow from catenarity and the connectedness theorem for ideals generated by few elements. The route written here
proves (1) instead through the incidence scheme over `ℤ`, its flatness and its closure, with the exact height from the
dimension formula.

Let `R₀` be a complete Noetherian local domain and `g ≥ 0`. In `S := R₀[[m_{ij}, y_j : 0 ≤ i, j ≤ g]]` write `m` for the generic
`(g + 1) × (g + 1)` matrix and `y` for the generic column. An ideal `J` of a Noetherian local ring `R` is *chained in dimension
`D`* if `c(R/J) ≥ D` (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6) and every minimal prime `P` of `J` has `dim R/P ≥ D + 1`. By the component-partition definition of
`c`, `c(R/J) ≥ D` says that `dim R/J ≥ D` and that any two minimal primes of `J` are joined by a chain of minimal primes of `J`
in which consecutive `P, P′` have `dim R/(P + P′) ≥ D` ([FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/FrobeniusSmoothVectors/Integral.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Fr.Chained`)). Prove:

1. (*the generic locus*) the radical of `(m·y, det m)` in `S` is a prime ideal of height at most `g + 1` (*the form of the
   formal proof*, which holds over any Noetherian domain `R₀`); its height is `g + 1`, and `V(m·y, det m)` is the closure of
   the locus where `m·y = 0` and `y ≠ 0` (classical, not a statement of [FormalProof]);
2. (*determinantal sections*; *the form of the formal proof*) let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring, `a ≥ b ≥ 0`,
   `R₀ := O[[X₁, …, X_a, Y₀, …, Y_g]]`, `f₁, …, f_b` in the maximal ideal of `R₀`, `M` a `(g + 1) × (g + 1)` matrix with entries
   in that maximal ideal, and `D := a − b` (which is `dim R₀ − b − g − 2`). Then `J := (f, M·Y, det M)` is chained in dimension
   `D`: `c(R₀/J) ≥ D`, and every minimal prime `P` of `J` has `dim R₀/P ≥ D + 1`. The Route gives the same for
   `R₀ = B[[Y₀, …, Y_g]]` over any complete Noetherian local domain `B`, with `D := dim R₀ − b − g − 2 ≥ 0` (this roadmap's own
   form).

*Route.* (1) Over `ℤ`, the incidence scheme `U := {(m, y) : m·y = 0, y ≠ 0}` is smooth with geometrically integral fibres of
relative dimension `(g + 1)²`: on the open set `y_j ≠ 0` the equations solve for the column `m_{·j}`, leaving an affine space over
`{y_j ≠ 0}`, and these open pieces meet. For the domain `R₀`, `U_{R₀}` is therefore integral (it is flat over `R₀`, so every
irreducible component meets the generic fibre, which is integral). Its closure in the affine space over `R₀` is `V(m·y, det m)`:
`V(m·y) = {y = 0} ∪ V(m·y, det m)`, and a pair `(m, 0)` with `det m = 0` over a point of `Spec R₀` is the limit of `(m, ty₀)`, `t → 0`,
for `y₀ ≠ 0` in the kernel of `m`, while the closure of `U_{R₀}` contains the closure of each of its fibres. So
`P := √(m·y, det m)` is prime in `R₀[m, y]`, and `R₀[m, y]/P` has dimension `dim R₀ + (g + 1)²` (dimension formula over the
universally catenary `R₀`). The ideal is bihomogeneous, so `A := R₀[m, y]/P` is graded with `A_0 = R₀` complete; its completion
at the vertex `𝔪_{R₀} + (m, y)` is `∏_nA_n`, in which the lowest homogeneous components of a product multiply, so it is a domain;
it is `S/PS` (completion is exact on finite modules), `PS` is the radical of `(m·y, det m)S` (a power of `P` lies in
`(m·y, det m)`), and `PS` has height `(g + 1)² + (g + 1) + dim R₀ − (dim R₀ + (g + 1)²) = g + 1` in the catenary local domain `S`.
Primality and the bound `≤ g + 1` are the statement of [FormalProof]; the equality and the description as a closure are
classical, proved above, and the Route of 6.11 (1) uses the equality.
(2) By (1) for the complete local domain `R₀`, `A := S/√(m·y, det m)S` is a complete local domain of dimension
`dim R₀ + (g + 1)²`, so `c(A) = dim A`. The `(g + 1)² + (g + 1) + b` elements `m_{ij} − M_{ij}`, `y_j − Y_j`, `f_k` of its maximal ideal
generate an ideal `𝔞` with `A/𝔞 ≅ R₀/J′` and `√J′ = √J`: the substitution `m ↦ M`, `y ↦ Y` is a surjection `S → R₀` carrying
`(m·y, det m)` onto `(M·Y, det M)`. The connectedness bound by arithmetic rank (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6) gives
`c(R₀/J) = c(A/𝔞) ≥ dim A − ara(𝔞) − 1 ≥ dim R₀ − g − b − 2 = D`; Krull's height theorem bounds the height of every minimal prime
of `𝔞` by `(g + 1)² + (g + 1) + b`, and catenarity of `A` gives `dim A/P ≥ D + 1`, i.e. `dim R₀/P ≥ D + 1` for the minimal primes
of `J`. *Needs:* DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6 (connectedness dimension, its invariance under radicals, the bound by arithmetic rank); catenarity
of complete local rings (SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.16, 0.21), Krull's height theorem and the dimension formula; Mathlib
`Matrix.det`, `MvPowerSeries`,
`Ideal.minimalPrimes`, `Ideal.height`.

**Checks.**

- (*the determinant is needed*) `V(m·y) = {y = 0} ∪ V(m·y, det m)` has two components of codimension `g + 1`; `det m` does not vanish
  identically on `{y = 0}`, so it removes that component, and keeps `{y = 0, det m = 0}`, which lies in the closure of the
  incidence. For `g = 0`, `(m₀₀y₀, m₀₀)` has radical `(m₀₀)`, prime of height one.
- (2) is sharp in the second clause: `B = O`, `g = b = 0`, `R₀ = O[[Y₀]]`, `M = (π)`: `J = (πY₀, π) = (π)`, `D = 0`, `c(R₀/J) = 1 ≥ 0`,
  and the minimal prime `(π)` has `dim R₀/(π) = 1 = D + 1`.
- Over a ring that is not a domain (1) fails: for `R₀ = k[[u, v]]/(uv)` the locus has a component over each of `u = 0`, `v = 0`.

### 6.11 Finite `ℤ/2`-graded algebras with a determinantal presentation

*Origin:* particular to this proof: for a finite `ℤ/2`-graded algebra `𝔸 = B ⊕ N` whose odd part has a determinantal
presentation, every prime of `B` not containing `ired(N)` and of coheight at least `1 + D₀` lies in every prime of
`Supp_B N` (1), with the case `N = 0` (2).

*Method of the formal proof:* Cramer's rule puts the determinant of the relation matrix in `Ann_B N`; `𝔸` is a quotient
of a power series ring in which the lifted entries and the generators are part of a regular system of parameters; the
ideal of the relations and the determinant has a least prime over it, obtained from a chart of the projectivised kernel
and shown least by passing to a valuation ring; and a prime of coheight at least `1 + D₀` over it is that least prime,
because the `g` relations cut the dimension by `g`. The route written here takes the irreducible locus `𝓛` and its
dimension from 6.10 (1) instead.

Let `O` be a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformizer `π` (its residue field need not be finite), `B` a complete
Noetherian local `O`-algebra whose residue field is that of `O`, and `𝔸 = B ⊕ N` a commutative `ℤ/2`-graded `B`-algebra, finite as a
`B`-module, with even part `B` and odd part a `B`-submodule `N` with `N·N ⊆ 𝔪_B` (`N` is an ideal of `𝔸` exactly when `N·N = 0`). Write `ired(N)` for
the ideal of `B` generated by the products `nn′`, `n, n′ ∈ N`, and `𝔧 := (π) + 𝔪_B² + ired(N)`. Assume that `N` is generated by
`m₁, …, m_g`, and that there are `X₁, …, X_{D₀} ∈ 𝔪_B` with `𝔪_B ⊆ (X) + 𝔧` and `O`-linear forms `λ_{ij}(X)` (`1 ≤ i, j ≤ g`) whose `g²`
coefficient vectors in `O^{D₀}` are linearly independent modulo `π`, with `Σ_jλ_{ij}(X)m_j ∈ 𝔧·N` for every `i`. Prove:

1. every prime `Z` of `B` not containing `ired(N)` with `dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀` is contained in every prime `Q` of `Supp_B N` (that
   is, `Ann_B N ⊆ Q`). As `Ann_B N·ired(N) = 0`, such a `Z` lies in `Supp_B N`, and the clause applies in particular to primes
   `Q` not containing `ired(N)`;
2. (*the case `N = 0`*) if `N = 0` (so that the hypothesis reads `𝔪_B ⊆ (X) + (π) + 𝔪_B²`), then `B` is a quotient of
   `O[[X₁, …, X_{D₀}]]`, and a prime `Z` with `dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀` exists only if `B = O[[X₁, …, X_{D₀}]]`; then `Z = 0`, which is
   contained in every prime.

Clause (1) is stated in `Suggested.lean` as `le_of_determinantalPresentation`. When `𝔸` comes from a two-block generalized
matrix algebra (IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §1.3) whose off-diagonal entry modules are identified by a polarization,
`N = A_12 ≅ A_21` and `ired(N)` is the reducibility ideal `I_red = A_12A_21` of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §1.4.

*Route.* (1) Write `Σ_jλ_{ij}(X)m_j = Σ_jc_{ij}m_j` with `c_{ij} ∈ 𝔧`: the `z_{ij} := λ_{ij}(X) − c_{ij}` give relations `Σ_jz_{ij}m_j = 0`,
and Cramer's rule (`adj(z)·z = det z·1`) gives `det z ∈ Ann_B N`. `𝔸` is local with maximal ideal `𝔪_B ⊕ N` (for `u ∈ B^×`,
`(u + n)(u − n) = u² − n²` is a unit of `B`) and residue field that of `O`, and `𝔪_𝔸² = (𝔪_B² + ired(N)) ⊕ 𝔪_BN`; so the `X_a` and the
`m_j` span `𝔪_𝔸/(𝔪_𝔸² + π𝔸)`, and complete Nakayama (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.9) makes `O[[X₁, …, X_{D₀}, Y₁, …, Y_g]] → 𝔸`, `Y_j ↦ m_j`, surjective, with `B`
the image of the even series (those in `X` and the `Y_iY_j`). Lift each `c_{ij}` to an even series `c̃_{ij}` in `(π) + (X, Y)²`
(`𝔪_B` is the image of `(X, Y_iY_j)` and `ired(N)` that of `(Y_iY_j)`) and put `z̃_{ij} := λ_{ij}(X) − c̃_{ij}`: then `z̃·Y` lies in the
kernel and `z̃ ≡ λ(X)` modulo `(π) + (X, Y)²`. The linear parts `λ_{ij}(X)` being independent modulo `π`, the `z̃_{ij}`, `D₀ − g²` of the
`X_a` and the `Y_j` are formal coordinates over `O` (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.9), in which `(z̃·Y, det z̃)` is the generic ideal of 6.10(1) (with `g − 1` in
place of `g`) over the complete local domain of power series in those `D₀ − g²` coordinates; so `𝓛 := V(z̃·Y, det z̃)` is
irreducible of dimension `(1 + D₀ + g) − g = 1 + D₀`. Every prime of `𝔸` over a prime of `Supp_B N` contains `det z` and the
relations, so it lies in `𝓛`. A prime `Z̃` of `𝔸` over `Z` (lying over: `𝔸` is finite over `B`) has `dim 𝔸/Z̃ = dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀`, so
it is the generic point of `𝓛`; every prime of `𝔸` over `Q` lies in `𝓛`, hence contains `Z̃`, and contracting gives `Z ⊆ Q`. Finally
`a·nn′ = (an)n′ = 0` for `a ∈ Ann_B N`. A step of this roadmap's own, not in [FormalProof]: a prime `Z ⊇ Ann_B N` with
`dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀` does not contain `ired(N)`. Otherwise `𝔪_B ⊆ (X) + (π) + 𝔪_B² + Z`, so `B/Z ≅ O[[X₁, …, X_{D₀}]]` as in (2);
modulo `Z` the relations become `Σ_j(λ_{ij}(X) − c_{ij})m̄_j = 0` with `c_{ij} ∈ (π) + 𝔪²`, and Cramer's rule gives
`det(λ(X) − c)·(N/ZN) = 0`, where the determinant is nonzero (modulo `π` its lowest form is the determinant of `g²` linearly
independent linear forms); so `N_Z = 0` by Nakayama, against `Ann_B N ⊆ Z`. So the hypothesis `Ann_B N ⊆ Z` and the
hypothesis `ired(N) ⊄ Z` of [FormalProof] select the same primes.
(2) follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PatchingModules/SelmerScaling/PresOut0.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Pa.presOut0`).
Complete Nakayama makes `O[[X]] → B` surjective, so `dim B ≤ 1 + D₀`; if
`dim B/Z ≥ 1 + D₀ = dim O[[X]]`, the kernel of `O[[X]] → B/Z` is a prime of the domain `O[[X]]` of full coheight, hence `0`. *Needs:*
DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.9; 6.10 (1); dimension under finite extensions (lying over, equality of dimensions); Mathlib `Matrix.adjugate`.

**Checks.**

- `B = O[[X]]`, `N = Bm` with `m² = X` (so `𝔸 = O[[m]]`), `D₀ = 1`, `λ₁₁ = X`: every hypothesis holds and `ired(N) = (X) ≠ 0`. Here
  `c₁₁ = X ∈ ired(N)` must be lifted to `Y²`, not to `X`, whose linear part would cancel that of `λ₁₁`; then `z̃ = X − Y²`, and `𝓛` is
  `{X = Y²}`, of dimension `2 = 1 + D₀`.
- `B = O[[x, u]]/(xu)`, `N = Bm` with `xm = 0`, `m² = u`, `D₀ = 1`, `λ₁₁ = x`: `Supp_B N = V(x)`, and the component `(u)` of `Spec B`,
  outside the support, is not concerned by the conclusion.
- Without the grading the statement is empty: if `N` were an ideal of `𝔸`, then `N·N ⊆ N ∩ B = 0`, and `ired(N) = 0`.

### 6.12 Propagation along components

*Origin:* adapted from the propagation of modularity along components in residually reducible automorphy lifting, in
Skinner and Wiles (1999), §4.3, Proposition 4.1, and Allen, Newton and Thorne (2020), §5, proof of Theorem 5.1
(arXiv:1912.11269v2): here an axiomatic form, local with
primes of two kinds `TI`, `TE` and thick components (1), and global on a Noetherian topological space (2).

*Method of the formal proof:* for (1), an invariant is carried along a chain of adjacent components, a component being
either good or thick and attached by a chain of links to a nice prime with a good generization, and each step passes
through a prime of coheight at least `D₁` containing two adjacent components; for (2), the components meeting `TI` are
split into two closed unions and preconnectedness of `Core` forces a point of `Cl` on both. Compared with the usual
propagation along components, the invariant has a second alternative for thick components.

Prove:

1. (*local*) Let `R` be a Noetherian ring with subsets `TI, TE, Good, Nice, Thick ⊆ Spec R`, an ideal `𝔰` and an integer `D₁ ≥ 0`.
   Write `dim 𝔭 := dim R/𝔭`. A *component* is a minimal prime of `𝔰`; components `P, Q` are *adjacent* if `dim R/(P + Q) ≥ D₁`;
   two nice primes in `TE` are *linked* if some thick component lies in both. Assume: `Good` is stable under specialization and
   `TI` under generization; a nice prime lies in `TI` or `TE`; every good prime `𝔭` with `dim 𝔭 ≥ D₁` lies in a nice prime; every
   prime `𝔭` with `dim 𝔭 ≥ D₁` containing a thick component lies in a nice prime of `TE`; the minimal primes of `R` in `TI` are
   components; any two components are joined by a chain of adjacencies; every component is in `TI` or thick; (P1) if a nice
   prime in `TI` is good, so are its generizations; (P23) if `p, q` are nice primes in `TE` joined by a chain of links and some
   generization of `p` in `TI` is good, every generization of `q` in `TI` is good. Then, if some good prime `𝔭` in `TI` has
   `dim 𝔭 ≥ D₁`, every minimal prime of `R` in `TI` is good.
2. (*global*) Let `𝒳` be a Noetherian topological space with subsets `TI, Good, Core, Cl ⊆ Core, EE`. Assume: `Core` is
   preconnected; every closed set meeting `Core` meets `Cl`; `Core ⊆ closure(Cl ∖ EE)`; every irreducible component meeting `TI`
   meets `Core`; at each `x ∈ Cl`, if one irreducible component through `x` meeting `TI` lies in `Good`, all do; each `x ∈ Cl ∖ EE`
   lies on a component meeting `TI`; some component meeting `TI` lies in `Good`. Then `TI ⊆ Good`.

For local `R`, the chain hypothesis of (1) holds when `c(R/𝔰) ≥ D₁` (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.6; by the component-partition definition of `c`, this says
that `dim R/𝔰 ≥ D₁` and that the adjacency graph on the components is connected), in particular when `𝔰` is chained in dimension
`D₁` (6.10), and trivially when `R/√𝔰` is irreducible. The same propagation is carried out for particular deformation rings by
ClassicalSerreModularity `I.1/pseudo-deformation-rings-and-pro-modularity` (the key proposition of Skinner–Wiles, from `(P1)`
and `(P2)`, for the pro-modular primes of `R_𝒟`), by PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §§8.4–8.5 (potentially
pro-automorphic minimal primes, with adjacency from the bound on `c(R₁)`) and by GL2ModularityLifting §§10.3 and 10.12 (Pan's
good components, by the partition argument of (2)).

*Route.* (1) is [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CurveDVRSpecialization/FlipTwoThree.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Pr.LocProp`),
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CurveDVRSpecialization/LocProp.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Pr.locProp`),
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CurveDVRSpecialization/FlipTwoThree.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Pr.LocData.Concl`).
For a component `Q` let `Inv(Q)` say: `Q ∈ TI` implies `Q` good, and either `Q` is good and in `TI`, or `Q` is thick and
lies in a nice prime of `TE` reached by a chain of links from a nice prime of `TE` with a good generization in `TI`. *Start:* let
`Q₀` be a minimal prime of `R` below the given good prime `𝔭₀`; it is in `TI`, hence a component; `𝔭₀` lies in a nice prime `n`,

### 6.13 Limit groups and patching up to an element

*Origin:* adapted from Pan (2022), §§4.4–4.8, and Newton and Thorne (2023), §4, after Skinner and Wiles (1999), §5, and
beside Thorne (2026), proof of Proposition 4.16, where the composites are a power of an element: ultrafilter patching of
modules of unbounded size whose comparison maps with the base level are inverse up to an element `f`, finiteness holding
only after `f` is inverted; here (2) states it for arbitrary modules over a Noetherian local ring, over the `(σ)`-adic
completion of `R[1/f]`, with quasi-regularity of `σ₁, …, σ_s` as conclusion, and (3) gives the supports of the patched
module. The limit group of (1) is adapted from Dotto and Thorne (2026), §3.4, Lemmas 3.4.1–3.4.2; (4) is a standard
fact on towers of modules, assembled in the Route from the Stacks project, Tags 09B8, 05GG and 031D.

*Method of the formal proof:* for each `k` the modules `M_N` are assembled along the ultrafilter modulo
`(σ)^k + 𝔪_Λ^j` for every `j`, with no bound on their ranks; maps to and from `𝒳` whose composites are `f` make the first
limit finite once `f` is inverted, and the tower of limits over `k` gives a finite module over the completion of
`R[1/f]` on which quasi-regularity passes from the levels. The limit group is the quotient of `Ph` by the closed normal subgroup that agrees
with the kernels of almost all levels modulo every open normal subgroup.

Fix an ultrafilter `𝒰` on `ℕ`; it is part of the data (Conventions above), and "for `𝒰`-almost all `N`" means "for `N` in a set of `𝒰`".
Prove:

1. (*limit group*) Let `Γ_N` (`N ≥ 1`) be profinite groups, `Ph` a topologically finitely generated profinite group (the Route
   uses only that `Ph` is profinite, and gives every clause of (1) for any profinite `Ph`) and `π_N : Ph ↠ Γ_N` surjections (for
   instance `Ph` the free profinite group on `ρ₀` generators when every `Γ_N` is topologically generated by `ρ₀` elements).
   There is a unique closed normal subgroup `K_∞ ⊆ Ph` with
   `ker(π_N)·V = K_∞·V` for `𝒰`-almost all `N`, for every open normal subgroup `V ⊆ Ph`; put `G_∞ := Ph/K_∞`. If `𝒰` is
   nonprincipal and `φ_N, τ_N ∈ Γ_N` satisfy `φ_N^{−1}τ_Nφ_N = τ_N^{q_N}` with positive integers `q_N ≡ 1` modulo `ℓ^N` and `τ_N`
   in a closed pro-`ℓ` subgroup of `Γ_N`, and `φ̃_N, τ̃_N ∈ Ph` are lifts, then the images in `G_∞` of `lim_𝒰 φ̃_N` and
   `lim_𝒰 τ̃_N` commute (*the form of the formal proof*; the Route gives the same for `q_N ∈ ℤ_ℓ` with `lim_𝒰 q_N = 1` and any
   `𝒰`, which is this roadmap's own form);
2. (*patching up to an element*) let `𝒰` be nonprincipal, `Λ` a complete Noetherian local ring with finite residue field,
   `S_∞ := Λ[[σ₁, …, σ_s]]`, `R` a Noetherian local ring with a ring map `S_∞ → R`, `f ∈ R`, `𝔦 ⊆ R` an ideal, `𝒳` an `R`-module, finite
   over `Λ` and killed by `(σ)`, and `M_N` (`N ≥ 1`) `R`-modules such that: for every `j, k ≥ 1`, for `𝒰`-almost all `N`,
   `M_N/(𝔪_Λ^j, (σ)^k)M_N` is free of finite rank over `S_∞/(𝔪_Λ^j, (σ)^k)` (the rank may depend on `N`); for every `j`, for `𝒰`-almost
   all `N`, there are `R`-linear maps `𝒳/𝔪_Λ^j𝒳 ⇄ M_N/((σ), 𝔪_Λ^j)M_N` whose two composites are multiplication by `f`; and there are `n`
   and a generating set of `𝔦` such that for every generator `t` and every `j`, for `𝒰`-almost all `N`, some `a ∈ R` with
   `(ft)^n − a ∈ 𝔪_R^j` kills `M_N`. Then the `(σ)`-adic completion `𝔹` of `R[1/f]` is Noetherian and there is a finite `𝔹`-module `𝕄`
   with `σ₁, …, σ_s` quasi-regular on `𝕄`, `𝕄/(σ)𝕄 ≅ 𝔹 ⊗_R 𝒳`, and `𝔦𝔹 ⊆ √(Ann_𝔹 𝕄)`. Here quasi-regularity is equivalent to
   `σ₁, …, σ_s` being a weakly regular sequence on `𝕄` (each `σ_i` a nonzerodivisor on `𝕄/(σ₁, …, σ_{i−1})𝕄`), because `𝕄` is
   finite over the Noetherian ring `𝔹`, which is `(σ)`-adically complete, so that `(σ)𝔹` lies in its Jacobson radical: Stacks,
   Tag 00LN gives one direction (by Nakayama, `𝕄/(σ)𝕄 ≠ 0` when `𝕄 ≠ 0`), and the proof of Tag 061S, with Tag 061R and with
   Tag 00IQ (2) in place of Krull's intersection theorem, the other;
3. (*supports*) in (2), assume moreover that `𝒳` is free of finite rank over `Λ`, and let `q_i` (`i` in any index set) be primes of `R` containing `𝔦 + (σ)` and not `f` (equivalently, primes of `𝔹`
   containing `𝔦𝔹 + (σ)`) such that the completion of `Λ` at `q_i ∩ Λ` is regular of dimension `δ`, `𝔹_i` the completion of `𝔹` at
   `q_i𝔹`, which is the completion of `R` at `q_i`, and `S_i ⊆ 𝔹_i` the annihilator of `𝔹_i ⊗_𝔹 𝕄`. Then: (dim) every minimal prime
   `P` of `S_i` has `dim 𝔹_i/P = δ + s`; (fib) for a prime `Q ⊇ (σ)` of `𝔹_i`, `S_i ⊆ Q` exactly when `Ann_R 𝒳 ⊆ Q ∩ R`; (move) if a prime
   `Z ⊇ S_i` of `𝔹_i` lies in a prime `Q ⊇ (σ)` of `𝔹_i` with `Q ∩ R ⊆ q_{i′}`, there is a prime `Z′ ⊇ S_{i′}` of `𝔹_{i′}` with
   `Z′ ∩ R = Z ∩ R` (a prime of `R` containing `𝔦`);
4. (*towers*) a standard fact on modules complete for an adic topology: let `S` be a ring with a
   finitely generated ideal `𝔶` and `(N_k)_{k≥1}` `S`-modules with surjections `N_{k+1} ↠ N_k` of kernel `𝔶^kN_{k+1}`, `𝔶^kN_k = 0` and
   `N₁` finite; then `lim_k N_k` is finite over the `𝔶`-adic completion of `S`, its quotient by `𝔶^k` is `N_k`, and an element dying
   in every `N_k` is zero (Stacks, Tags 09B8, 05GG and 031D).

### 6.14 Coordinate grading

*Origin:* adapted from Pinkham (1974), §2, Propositions 2.2–2.3, the grading of the tangent space and the `𝔾_m`-action on
a formal versal deformation of an isolated singularity with a `𝔾_m`-action: here a presentation with homogeneous kernel for
a coefficient ring on which a formal torus of any rank acts.

*Method of the formal proof:* separation on the dual numbers gives a surjection from a power series ring in finitely many
of the elements; its kernel is stable under weight components because it is stable under twisting the variables by
characters of the weight group, which is checked modulo each power of the maximal ideal, where a universal character
separates the finitely many weights.

Let `O` be a complete local Noetherian ring with residue field `k`, a *coefficient ring* a complete local Noetherian `O`-algebra with
residue field `k`, and `X` a free abelian group of finite rank. Let `R` be a coefficient ring and `(e_s)` a family in `𝔪_R` with
weights `w_s ∈ X` such that (sep) two homomorphisms from `R` to a coefficient ring that agree on all `e_s` are equal, and (tor) for
every homomorphism `f : R → R′` to a coefficient ring and every homomorphism `t : X → 1 + 𝔪_{R′}` there is `f′` with
`f′(e_s) = t(w_s)f(e_s)` for all `s`. Prove: finitely many `e_s` give a surjection `O[[x₁, …, x_n]] → R` with kernel stable under
taking weight components, and every `e_s` lies in the image of the power series of weight `w_s`.

*Route.* This is a theorem of [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CommutativeAlgebra/CoordGrading.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Alg.coord_grading`).
(sep) on `k[ε]` shows that the `e_s` span the relative cotangent space, so finitely many of them give a surjection
`π : O[[x₁, …, x_n]] → R` (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.9). Apply (tor) to `R → R[[u₁, …, u_ρ]]`, `ρ` the rank of `X`, and `t(w) = ∏(1 + u_i)^{w_i}`: for

`𝔪_R^N`), and the `t(w)`, `w ∈ X` distinct, are linearly independent over `R/𝔪_R^N` (they are the monomials in the `T_i = 1 + u_i`).
So `π(p) = 0` gives `π(p_w) ∈ ∩_N 𝔪_R^N = 0`: the kernel is homogeneous. The same comparison for `e_s = π(q)` and

### 6.15 The graded part of a weighted presentation

*Origin:* particular to this proof: in the setting of 6.14, the weight
parts of a weighted presentation form a graded subalgebra, generated over its part of weight `0` by the variables, each
weight part being finite over it.

*Method of the formal proof:* by Gordan's lemma the exponents of weight `0` form a finitely generated monoid, so the
weight-`0` part is a quotient of a power series ring in finitely many monomials and is a coefficient ring; the graded part
is generated over it by the variables, and a homomorphism to a coefficient ring is determined on the weight-`0` part by
its values on those monomials.

In 6.14's setting, let
`π : O[[x₁, …, x_n]] → R` be a surjection with weighted variables mapping into `𝔪_R` and kernel stable under weight components, and
`R_w` the image of the power series of weight `w`. Prove: `Σ_w R_w` is direct and is an `X`-graded subalgebra of `R`; `R_0` is a
coefficient ring whose maximal ideal is generated by `𝔪_O` and finitely many monomials of weight `0`; each `R_w` is a finite
`R_0`-module; `⊕_w R_w` is generated over `R_0` by the variables; and a homomorphism from `⊕_w R_w` to a coefficient ring is
determined by the images of the variables.

*Route.* This is a theorem of [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/WeakShimuraTheory/AdicExtensionMatrices/SxExtA.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Ws.sxExtA`).
The route is the method of the label above; each `R_w` is finite over `R_0` because the exponents of weight `w` have
finitely many minimal elements (Dickson's lemma), every other one exceeding one of them by an exponent of weight `0`.
*Needs:* 6.14 (the setting and coefficient rings); Mathlib `AddSubmonoid.fg_eqLocusM` (Gordan's lemma),
`Finsupp.wellQuasiOrderedLE` (Dickson's lemma) and `MvPowerSeries`.

### 6.16 Rescaling an artinian coefficient ring

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a ring of Laurent series over an artinian coefficient ring `C` whose coefficient of
`T^{−n}` lies in `𝔪_C^{⌈n/c⌉}`, itself a coefficient ring; for `c = 1` it is the extended Rees algebra of `𝔪_C`, with
`T^{−1}` as its variable, completed `T`-adically.

*Method of the formal proof:* the ring is the subalgebra of the Laurent series generated over `C[[T]]` by the elements
`T^{−c}x`, `x` in a finite generating set of `𝔪_C`; these are nilpotent, so the subalgebra is finite over `C[[T]]`, every
element is a power series plus a nilpotent, and it is a coefficient ring; a power of its maximal ideal lies in a high
power of `(T)`, where a given coefficient vanishes.

In 6.14's setting, for an artinian
coefficient ring `C` and `c ≥ 0`, prove: `S = C + T·C[[T]] + Σ_{n≥1} T^{−n}𝔪_C^{⌈n/c⌉} ⊂ C((T))` (`S = C + T·C[[T]]` for `c = 0`) is a
coefficient ring whose maximal ideal contains `T^nC` for `n > 0` and `T^n𝔪_C` for `−c ≤ n ≤ 0`, and for every `n` the coefficient
of `T^n` vanishes on a power of `𝔪_S`.

*Route.* This follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/WeakShimuraTheory/AdicExtensionMatrices/NearOfAdjoin.lean` (`UpperTheory.V.Ws.sxLaur`).
The route is the method of the label above. *Needs:* 6.14 (coefficient rings); a local ring finite over a complete
Noetherian local ring is complete and Noetherian; Mathlib `LaurentSeries`, `PowerSeries`, `Algebra.adjoin` and
`IsNilpotent`.

### Examples

The generic incidence `{m·y = 0, y ≠ 0}` has an irreducible closure of codimension `g + 1`; for `g = 0`, `(m₀₀y₀, m₀₀)` has radical `(m₀₀)` (6.10 (1)). A determinantal section by `b` further equations of a power series ring `R₀` over a complete local domain is chained in dimension `dim R₀ − b − g − 2`; for `B = O`, `g = b = 0` and `M = (π)` the minimal prime `(π)` has `dim R₀/(π) = 1 = D + 1` (6.10 (2)). For `B = O[[X]]` and `N = Bm` with `m² = X`, every hypothesis of 6.11 holds with `D₀ = 1`, and the locus `𝓛` is `{X = Y²}`, of dimension `2 = 1 + D₀` (6.11). In `k[[x, y]]/(xy)` with `D₁ = 1` the two components are not adjacent, and no property propagates across the node (6.12). For the ultrafilter of the sets containing a fixed `N₀`, the limit group is `Γ_{N₀}`; for a nonprincipal ultrafilter, with `f = 1`, `R = S_∞ = Λ[[σ]]`, `𝔦 = 0`, `𝒳 = Λ` and the modules `S_∞/((1 + σ)^{ℓ^N} − 1)`, patching up to `f` gives `𝔹 = S_∞` and `𝕄 = 𝔹` (6.13).
