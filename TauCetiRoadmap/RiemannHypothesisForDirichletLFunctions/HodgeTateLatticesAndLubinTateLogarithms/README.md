# Roadmap: Hodge–Tate lattices and Lubin–Tate logarithms

**Summary.** Let `C` be a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic `0` with `|p| < 1`, and `O_C` its ring of integers. The Hodge–Tate map of a `p`-divisible group `G` over `O_C`, `T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}`, sends a point of the Tate module to an invariant differential of the Cartier dual; it is onto after inverting `p`, and integrally its cokernel is a torsion module. This roadmap bounds that cokernel at every prime, uses the bound to build integral Hodge–Tate lattices on normal models of abelian varieties with full level structure and to read off Hodge–Tate sequences on their special fibres, and, separately, proves integrality statements for logarithms of Lubin–Tate formal group laws in several variables.

The headline theorems are these.

1. Let `p` be any prime and `H` a finite flat commutative group scheme of `p`-power order over `O_C`, with Hopf algebra `𝒜`. Every `κ ∈ O_C` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|` multiplies the cotangent module `ω_H` into the span of the classes `u − 1` of the group-like elements `u` of `𝒜`, and kills the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map of every `p`-divisible group over `O_C` (0.5b). For odd `p` this is Fargues' bound, which Fargues (2011) states with no hypothesis on `p`, with no printed proof at `p = 2`; at `p = 2` the factor is `κ = 2`.
2. Let `X` be a noetherian normal integral scheme, flat over `ℤ_(p)`, whose function field `Ω` contains the `p^R`-th roots of unity (`R ≥ 1`), and `G → X` a semi-abelian scheme, abelian over a dense open subset, whose generic `p^R`-torsion is rational over `Ω`. The Hodge–Tate lattice `ω^{HT}_R ⊆ ω_G`, generated by `p^R·ω_G` and by the differentials of the regular functions on neighbourhoods of the unit section of `G[p^R]` that restrict to characters of the generic `p^R`-torsion, is a coherent subsheaf, and `κ₀·ω_G ⊆ ω^{HT}_R ⊆ ω_G` at every codimension-one point over `p` and at every point over `p` where `ω^{HT}_R` is locally free, with `κ₀ = ζ_p − 1` for odd `p` and `κ₀ = 2` for `p = 2` (0.5a, 0.5b).
3. If moreover `R ≥ 2`, the generic fibre `A` is principally polarised and `ω^{HT}_R` is locally free near the special fibre, then, at any prime `p`, the Hodge–Tate map of the level induces a surjection `A[p^R](Ω) ⊗ O_X/p^{R−1} → ω^{HT}_R/p^{R−1}` of locally free `O_X/p^{R−1}`-modules whose kernel is locally a direct summand of rank `g`, equal to its own orthogonal for the Weil pairing and isomorphic to `Lie^{HT}_R ⊗ μ_{p^R}(Ω) ⊗ O_X/p^{R−1}`; on the reduced special fibre `S` this is a surjection of vector bundles with Lagrangian kernel. With an action of `O_E`, `p` unramified in `E` and the Rosati involution inducing the involution of `E`, it splits into split exact sequences of eigen-quotients modulo `p^{R−1}`, and on the bundle of pairs of full flags over `S`, with `x_0, …, x_{N−1}` the first Chern classes of the graded pieces numbered from the bottom of the flag, the power sums of the `x_i` vanish in the integral Chow ring and, for every `GL_N`-dominant (non-increasing) weight `λ`, the line bundle with first Chern class `−Σ_i λ_i x_i` is nef (0.17a). At primes ramified in `E`, if moreover the eigen-quotients of `ω^{HT}_R` are locally free near the fibre (as on the model of 0.5c (b)), the same holds with a twist by the different (0.17b).
4. Let `K` be a field of characteristic `0` with a nonarchimedean absolute value normalised by `|p| = p^{−1}`, `S` a finite set with a self-map `ς`, and `q ≥ 2`. The twisted system of logarithms `λ_x(T) = Σ_{e≥0} T_{ς^e(x)}^{q^e}/p^e` has a unique two-sided inverse `g` without constant term, and every coefficient of `g_x` in total degree `|n| ≥ 1` has `p`-adic valuation at least `−(|n| − 1)/(q − 1)`; the bound is attained (3.1).

The roadmap extends [HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups](../../HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups/README.md), whose finite-level Hodge–Tate map, Fargues' bound for odd `p` (its §0.5, of which 0.5f is, for odd `p`, a corollary), Raynaud extensions and Hodge–Tate sequences over `C` it uses, and [LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts](../../LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts/README.md), whose Honda theory and unramified Lubin–Tate logarithms `ℓ_s` it carries to several variables, and whose §§4.2a and 4.3a state the functional-equation lemma in several variables and the quasi-logarithms of the laws of the `ℓ_s`, of which parts of 3.2, 3.4 and 3.5 are corollaries, and whose §4.2b states the Dieudonné–Dwork lemma in several variables with a Frobenius lift on the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean field of characteristic zero and residue characteristic `p`, of which part of 3.3 is a corollary, its first statement being the case of a complete field. The integral models it works on are those of [ShimuraCompactifications](../../ShimuraCompactifications/README.md), modified by the Nash modifications of [NashBlowups](../../NashBlowups/README.md) (0.5c is a corollary of its §1.2); [FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory](../../FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory/README.md) gives the points and Cartier duality of finite flat group schemes.

Layer 0 holds the integral results: the cokernel bound at every prime, the Hodge–Tate lattice with its sandwich and a model on which it is locally free, colength bounds for the Hodge–Tate image on eigenspaces, the period matrix of a principally polarised abelian scheme over `O_C` and the functions additive modulo `p` on its `p`-torsion, the Hodge–Tate sequences on the fibres over a prime, the `p`-divisible group of a semi-abelian scheme over `O_C`, and, at every prime, the kernel of the Hodge–Tate map over `O_C`, which is the annihilator of the kernel of the dual map and, for a principal quasi-polarisation, a Lagrangian. Its numbering follows the places of these statements after HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.5, 0.17, 0.18 and 2.4. Layer 3 holds the logarithms: the sharp radius, the functional-equation lemma and Dwork's lemma in several variables, quasi-logarithms of the unramified Lubin–Tate laws, products of Lubin–Tate groups in twisted logarithmic coordinates, and sums of quasi-quadratic series under unitary substitutions.

## Conventions

- Notation and conventions not restated here are those of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups (its Conventions and §§0.1–0.4) for Layer 0, and of LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts (its Conventions and §§4.1–4.3) for Layer 3.
- In Layer 0, `C` is a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic `0` with `|p| < 1` and `O_C` its ring of integers; valuations are normalised by `v(p) = 1`, so that `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|` says `v(κ) ≥ 1/(p − 1)`. `G^D` is the Cartier dual, `T_pG` the Tate module, `ω_G` the invariant differentials (for a finite flat `H` with Hopf algebra `𝒜` and augmentation ideal `I`, `ω_H = I/I²`), `Lie` the Lie algebra, `α_H` and `α_G` the Hodge–Tate maps of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.2 and 0.4.
- In the setting of 0.5a, `X_{𝔽_p}` is the special fibre of `X` and `S = (X_{𝔽_p})_red` the reduced special fibre; `κ₀ = ζ_p − 1` for odd `p` and `κ₀ = 2` for `p = 2`; `Lie^{HT}_R` is the dual lattice of `ω^{HT}_R`; for an embedding `σ` of a number field with involution `c`, `σ̄ = σ∘c`.
- *Eigen-quotients.* Let a ring `O_E` with an involution `c` act on `G`, with the embeddings `σ : E → Ω`; `σ̄ = σ∘c`. `Lie_σ ⊆ Lie(A)` is the subspace on which `O_E` acts through `σ`. For a lattice `M ⊆ ω_A = ω_G ⊗ Ω` (at a point, or a subsheaf of `ω_G`), its *`σ`-eigen-quotient* `M_σ` is the image of `M` under restriction of forms, `ω_A → Hom(Lie_σ, Ω)`; so `ω_{G,σ}` and `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}` are lattices in `Hom(Lie_σ, Ω)`, of rank `rk Lie_σ`, and `(ω_{G,σ})^∨ = Lie_G ∩ Lie_σ`. Restriction identifies the eigen-subspace `(ω_A)_σ` of 0.5a (b) with `Hom(Lie_σ, Ω)`, and the eigen-sublattice `M ∩ (ω_A)_σ` with a sublattice of `M_σ`; they are equal at the points over the primes unramified in `E` (0.5a (b)) and differ in general. The symbol `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}` always denotes the eigen-quotient; the eigen-sublattice is written `ω^{HT}_R ∩ (ω_A)_σ`.
- *The level lattice and the components of the Weil pairing.* `T := A[ℓ^R](Ω)`, free of rank `N = 2g/[E:ℚ]` over `O_E/ℓ^R`; `T_σ := (T ⊗_ℤ O_X) ⊗_{O_E ⊗ O_X, σ} O_X`, the `σ`-eigen-quotient of `T ⊗ O_X`, a free `O_X/ℓ^R`-module of rank `N`. If the Rosati involution of the principal polarisation induces `c`, the Weil pairing `e_{ℓ^R} : T × T → μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω)` satisfies `e(ax, y) = e(x, c(a)y)`, and there is a unique pairing `ẽ : T × T → (𝔡_E^{−1}/ℓ^R𝔡_E^{−1}) ⊗ μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω)`, `O_E`-linear in the first variable and `c`-semilinear in the second, with `Tr_{E/ℚ}∘ẽ = e_{ℓ^R}` (`𝔡_E` the different of `E`). Its *`σ`-component* is the `O_X`-bilinear pairing `⟨·,·⟩_σ : T_σ × T_{σ̄} → (𝔡_σ^{−1}/ℓ^R𝔡_σ^{−1}) ⊗ μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω)` induced by `σ∘ẽ`, where `𝔡_σ := σ(𝔡_E)·O_X` is an invertible ideal, equal to `O_X` over the primes unramified in `E` (`𝔡_{σ̄} = 𝔡_σ`). It is perfect: `T_σ ≅ Hom(T_{σ̄}, 𝔡_σ^{−1}/ℓ^R) ⊗ μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω)`. The line `μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω)` is free of rank one over `ℤ/ℓ^R`; a primitive root of unity trivialises it.
- In Layer 3, `K` is a field of characteristic `0`, complete for a nonarchimedean absolute value with residue characteristic `p`, normalised by `|p| = p^{−1}`, with valuation ring `O` (3.1 drops completeness); "integral" means "with coefficients in `O`"; `v_p` is the valuation with `v_p(p) = 1`; for a ring endomorphism `σ` and a series `g`, `g^σ` applies `σ` to the coefficients; series in several variables are written in multi-index notation, `|n|` being the total degree of the multi-index `n`.

## Layer 0: Hodge–Tate lattices and cokernel bounds at finite level

The targets of this layer are numbered by their places after HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.5, 0.17, 0.18 and 2.4, not in construction order. The cokernel bound 0.5b at every prime comes first; the kernel statement 2.4, which needs only 0.5b, is used through its polarised case (b) by 0.5d, 0.5e, 0.5g, 0.18a and 0.17b; the Hodge–Tate sequence 0.17b holds at every prime on the thickened fibre, and 0.17a is its case at the primes unramified in `E`.

### 0.5a The Hodge–Tate lattice of the invariant differentials

*Origin:* adapted from Pilloni and Stroh (2016), the modified Hodge bundle `ω^mod` on normal admissible formal models of Siegel varieties at full level over `O_C`, for `p ≥ 3`, with `p^{1/(p−1)}·ω ⊆ ω^mod ⊆ ω` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §5.5; at `p = 2`, with `p^2` in place of `p^{1/(p−1)}`, conditionally on a bound that is not written up, their Remarque 1.10), and from the bound of Fargues (2010), (2011) behind it (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5): here the lattice on noetherian normal schemes flat over `ℤ_(p)` with semi-abelian reduction, with its coherence, the sandwich at codimension-one points and at points where it is locally free, the eigen-decomposition and the pull-back.

*Method of the formal proof:* with a principal polarisation of `A`: on an affine open `Spec B`, `G[p^R]_B` is flat and quasi-finite with constant generic fibre `A[p^R](Ω)`, and the functions on `A[p^R](Ω)` that are regular on it form a Hopf order (checked after a henselian normal flat local extension at each prime, where the group has a finite open subgroup, and descended); a Hopf order over the integrally closed `B` contains every character with values in the units of `B`, the dual order being a finite algebra under convolution; so the characters `e_{p^R}(x, ·)` are regular, finitely many of their differentials together with `p^R·ω_G` generate the lattice on `Spec B`, and the sandwich is tested at the height-one primes through an injective local homomorphism to `O_C` and the cokernel bound of 0.5b.

*Setting.* Let `p` be an odd prime, `R ≥ 1`, `X` a noetherian normal integral scheme, flat over `ℤ_(p)`, whose function
field `Ω` contains the `p^R`-th roots of unity, and `G → X` a semi-abelian scheme, abelian over a dense open subset, whose
generic fibre `A` has its `p^R`-torsion rational over `Ω`. For a point `q` of `X` let the *Hodge–Tate lattice*
`ω^{HT}_R(q) ⊆ ω_{G,q}` be the `O_{X,q}`-module generated by `p^R·ω_{G,q}` and by the differentials at the unit section of the
regular functions `f`, on open neighbourhoods `W ⊆ G[p^R]` of the unit section over `q`, for which there is a character `χ` of
`A[p^R](Ω)` with values in `μ_{p^R}(Ω)` such that `f(g) = χ(g)` at every `p^R`-torsion point `g` of the geometric generic fibre
lying in `W` (these differentials are defined modulo `p^R`, the cotangent space of `G[p^R]` along the unit section being
`ω_G/p^R`). With a principal polarisation of `A` the characters are the `e_{p^R}(x, ·)`, `x ∈ A[p^R](Ω)`, and the differentials
are the Hodge–Tate images `α(x)` of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.2. The *Hodge–Tate Lie lattice* is `Lie^{HT}_R(q) = {e ∈ Lie_A : w(e) ∈ O_{X,q}` for all
`w ∈ ω^{HT}_R(q)}`.

Prove: first, on every affine open `Spec B` of `X` each such character is the restriction of a unique regular function on
`G[p^R]_B`, so that the `ω^{HT}_R(q)` are the stalks of a coherent subsheaf `ω^{HT}_R ⊆ ω_G`, generated on `Spec B` by `p^R·ω_G`
and the differentials of these functions, stable under the endomorphisms of `G` and equal to `ω_G` where `p` is invertible.
Then: (a) at every codimension-one point `y` of `X` over `p`, of good or of semi-abelian reduction, and at every point `y`
over `p` at which `ω^{HT}_R` is locally free, `κ·ω_{G,y} ⊆ ω^{HT}_R(y) ⊆ ω_{G,y}` and `Lie_{G,y} ⊆ Lie^{HT}_R(y) ⊆ κ^{−1}Lie_{G,y}` for
every nonzero `κ ∈ O_{X,y}` whose valuation at each height-one prime of `O_{X,y}` containing `p` is at least `1/(p − 1)` times
that of `p`; these are exactly the nonzero elements of `κ₀·O_{X,y}`, `κ₀ = ζ_p − 1` (which lies in `O_{X,y}` by normality, and
has valuation `1/(p − 1)` times that of `p` at every such prime); at a codimension-one point `y`, the base change of `ω^{HT}_R(y)` along any injective local
homomorphism `O_{X,y} → O_C` (`C` complete, algebraically closed, of characteristic `0`) is the image of
`Hom(T_pA_C, ℤ_p(1)) ⊗ O_C` in `ω_G ⊗ O_C` under the map sending a compatible system of characters of the `A[p^n](C)` to the
limit of the differentials at the unit section of their regular functions on the finite parts of the `G[p^n]_{O_C}` (the
Hodge–Tate map of the dual of the `p`-divisible group of these finite parts); (b) let the ring of integers `O_E` of a number
field `E` act on `G` by endomorphisms, and let the function field `Ω` contain the images of all embeddings `σ : E → Ω` (no
local ring over `p` contains the field `E`, which contains `1/p`). Then `σ(O_E) ⊆ O_{X,y}` at every point `y`, since `O_{X,y}`
is integrally closed in `Ω`. Put `(ω_A)_σ = {w ∈ ω_A : a^*w = σ(a)w for all a ∈ O_E}` and consider the eigen-sublattices `ω_G ∩ (ω_A)_σ` and
`ω^{HT}_R ∩ (ω_A)_σ` (Conventions). At the points `y` of (a), `κ·(ω_G ∩ (ω_A)_σ)_y ⊆ (ω^{HT}_R ∩ (ω_A)_σ)(y) ⊆ (ω_G ∩ (ω_A)_σ)_y` for every `σ`. If `p` is
unramified in `E`, then at every point `y` over `p` the map `O_E ⊗ O_{X,y} → ∏_σ O_{X,y}`, `a ⊗ f ↦ (σ(a)f)_σ`, is an isomorphism
(its determinant squared is the discriminant of `E`, a unit at `y`), its idempotents `e_σ` split `ω_{G,y} = ⊕_σ (ω_G ∩ (ω_A)_σ)_y`, and
`ω^{HT}_R(y) = ⊕_σ (ω^{HT}_R ∩ (ω_A)_σ)(y)`; (c) for a dominant morphism `g : X′ → X` of schemes as above, the
Hodge–Tate lattice of `g^*G` is the *image* of `g^*ω^{HT}_R` in `g^*ω_G`, at every point (not the tensor pull-back `g^*ω^{HT}_R`,
which need not be locally free where the image is: 0.17a's Check); and for `1 ≤ R′ ≤ R`,
`ω^{HT}_{R′} = ω^{HT}_R + p^{R′}·ω_G`, which equals `ω^{HT}_R` at the points of (a).

*Route.* With a principal polarisation of `A`, the lattice and its first assertion follow from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/GeometryProofs/HodgeTateOmegaCoherentStatement.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.T.hodge_tate_omega_coherent`),
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Definitions/GlobalGeometry/MemEigSpOfMemHtDual.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.LieModel.htOm`).
*Characters are regular.* At a prime `P` of `B`, pass to the henselisation of `B_P`, a normal local domain flat over
`B`; there `G[p^R]` is the disjoint union of its finite part, an open and closed subgroup finite flat over the base and
containing the points over `P`, and a part with no point over the closed point (FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory
R07.1.3). The restriction of a character to the finite
part is a point of its Cartier dual over the fraction field, hence integral (the Cartier dual is finite and the base is
normal), so the character is regular at the points over `P`; regularity descends along the faithfully flat henselisation,
and uniqueness holds because `G[p^R]` is flat, so its generic fibre is schematically dense. Coherence: on `Spec B` finitely
many characters generate, and a function regular on `G[p^R]_B` serves every point of `Spec B`. Endomorphisms act on the
characters; where `p` is invertible `ω_G/p^R = 0`. (a) The sandwich follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Statements/AlgebraStatements/HodgeTateNormComparison.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.ChartSequence`).
It suffices to treat `κ = κ₀ = ζ_p − 1`: every qualifying `κ` lies in
`κ₀·O_{X,y}` (`κ/κ₀` has nonnegative valuation at every height-one prime, `κ₀` being a unit at those not containing `p`, and
the noetherian normal `O_{X,y}` is the intersection of its localisations at them), so `κ·ω ⊆ κ₀·ω` and `κ₀^{−1}Lie ⊆ κ^{−1}Lie`.
At a codimension-one point `y`, `O_{X,y}` is a discrete valuation ring containing `κ₀`; an injective local `O_{X,y} → O_C` is
faithfully flat, so membership can be tested after base change, and
the regular functions of the characters pull back to those of the base change. Over `O_C` let `H_n` be the finite part of
`G[p^n]_{O_C}`: an open and closed subgroup containing the unit section, so `ω_{H_n} = ω_G/p^n`; `H_n = G[p^n] ∩ H_{n+1}`, and
`(H_n)_n` is a `p`-divisible group `H` with `T_pH` a direct summand of `T_pA` (over the completion of `O_{X,y}`, `H` is the
`p`-divisible group of the Raynaud extension, HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.13, 0.18, and `0 → T_pH → T_pA → Y ⊗ ℤ_p → 0` is exact; at a point of good
reduction `H = A[p^∞]`). So restriction maps `Hom(T_pA, ℤ_p(1))` onto `T_p(H^D)`; by naturality of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.2 for the inclusion of
the finite parts, the differential of the regular function of a character is `α_{H^D}` of its restriction; the image of
`T_p(A^∨) ⊗ O_C` in `ω_G` is therefore the image of the Hodge–Tate map `T_p(H^D) ⊗ O_C → ω_H = ω_G`, and HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5 applied to the
`p`-divisible group `H^D` gives `κ₀·ω_G ⊆` image `⊆ ω_G` (`|κ₀|^{p−1} = |p|`). Since `p^R·ω ⊆ κ₀·ω` (`R ≥ 1 ≥ 1/(p − 1)` in units

`α_H` modulo `p^R` is the level-`R` map); descend. At a point `y` where `ω^{HT}_R` is locally free: a locally free submodule of
`ω_{G,y} ⊗ Ω` over the noetherian normal domain `O_{X,y}` is the intersection of its localisations at the height-one primes
(SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.6);
at those over `p` the codimension-one case applies, at the others `ω^{HT}_R = ω_G`; so `κ₀·ω_{G,y} ⊆ ω^{HT}_R(y)`. The Lie
statement is the dual. (b) `σ(O_E)` consists of elements of `Ω` integral over `ℤ`, hence lies in every `O_{X,y}`. The lattice

When `p` is unramified in `E`, the matrix `(σ(e_j))_{σ,j}` for a `ℤ`-basis `(e_j)` of `O_E` has determinant whose square is the
discriminant of `E`, invertible in `O_{X,y}`; so `O_E ⊗ O_{X,y} ≅ ∏_σ O_{X,y}`, and the idempotents `e_σ` decompose every

The torsion of `g^*G` over the function field of `X′` is that of `A`, and the regular function of a character
pulls back to the regular function of the same character (uniqueness); the differentials pull back accordingly. Every

### 0.5b The cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map at every prime

*Origin:* published: Fargues (2010), for odd `p`, the bound `p^{1/(p−1)}` for the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map of a finite flat group scheme over `O_C`, and Fargues (2011), which states it with no hypothesis on `p` (Théorèmes 2 and 3 with Remarque 2), with no printed proof at `p = 2`; the factor `κ₀ = 2` of (b), on the lattice of 0.5a, is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* in the dual of the Hopf algebra of a finite flat group scheme killed by `q`, a functional that is a derivation at the origin modulo `q` is divided by `q`; if `c` times it had norm above `1` for some `|c| < |p|^{1/(p−1)}`, the norms of its divided powers would attain their maximum at a last index `k`, and in the coproduct of the divided power of order `2k` the term `(k, k)` would be the only one of that norm, a contradiction; elementary divisors and the finite presentation of the cokernel then assemble the levels. The published proofs of Fargues (2010), (2011) pass through Faltings's integral comparison between the Tate module and crystalline periods and through the embedding of a finite flat group scheme into a `p`-divisible group.

Prove: (a) let `p` be any prime, `C` a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic `0` with
`|p| < 1`, `O_C` its ring of integers and `G` a `p`-divisible group over `O_C`. Then every `κ ∈ O_C` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`
kills the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map `T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}`. The same holds at finite level: for a finite flat
commutative group scheme `H` over `O_C` of `p`-power order, `κ·ω_H` lies in the image of
`α_{H^D} ⊗ 1 : H^D(O_C) ⊗ O_C → ω_H` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.2), the span of the classes of `u − 1` for the group-like elements `u` of the Hopf
algebra of `H`. For odd `p` this is Fargues' bound of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5; Fargues (2011) states it with no hypothesis on `p`, with no printed proof at `p = 2`; `κ = 2` qualifies at `p = 2`. (b) Consequently 0.5a (its setting, the
coherence of `ω^{HT}_R` and (a)–(c)) holds for `p = 2`, with `κ₀ = 2` (`= −(ζ₂ − 1)`): the qualifying `κ` of 0.5a (a) are the nonzero
elements of `2·O_{X,y}`.
The factor `2` in (b) is this roadmap's. [FormalProof] states the sandwich at `p = 2` with the factor `4`, and the sequence of 0.17b
modulo `2^{R−2}` for `R ≥ 4`, and uses nothing sharper; it proves (a) for every `κ` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`, and the factor `2`
follows from (a) by the route below.

`Suggested.lean` states the finite flat form of (a) as `smul_mem_span_cotangentMap_groupLike`.

*Route.* (a) for `p`-divisible groups is [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/DvdMulOfGroupLikeVal.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.coker_dlog_killed`), `Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/HodgeTatePoints/Farsharp.lean`
(`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_mem_range_dlogLinear`); the finite flat form follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/HodgeTatePoints/Farsharp.lean` (`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Farsharp.smul_dUnit_mem`).
(a) *One level.* Let `H` be a finite flat commutative group scheme over `O_C` killed by `q = p^n` (for the cokernel
of `α_G`, `H = G^D[p^n]`), and `𝒜` its Hopf algebra: a finite free `O_C`-module, commutative and cocommutative, with counit
`ε`, augmentation ideal `I` and cotangent module `I/I² = ω_H`, which `q` kills (for `H = G^D[p^n]`, `ω_H = ω_{G^D}/q`,
HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.25). The
`O_C`-points of the Cartier dual `H^D` are the group-like elements `u` of `𝒜` (`Δu = u ⊗ u`, `ε(u) = 1`), and `α_{H^D}` sends `u`
to the class of `u − 1` in `I/I²` (the value at the origin of `du/u`; HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.2). The claim is that `κ·(I/I²)` lies in the span of
these classes. Let `N ⊆ 𝒜` be the submodule spanned by the group-like elements, `I²` and `q𝒜`. It is finitely generated
and contains `q𝒜`, so `N = ⊕_i a_iO_C·e_i` for a suitable basis `(e_i)` of `𝒜` with `q ∈ a_iO_C` (elementary divisors over the
valuation ring), and `x ∈ 𝒜` lies in `N` iff `q | D(x)` for every `O_C`-linear `D : 𝒜 → O_C` with `D(N) ⊆ qO_C`. Such a `D` takes values in `qO_C` on `1`, on the group-like elements and
on `I²`, so it is a derivation at the origin modulo `q`: `D(ab) ≡ ε(a)D(b) + ε(b)D(a)` mod `q`. It therefore suffices to show
`q | κ·D(a)` for all `a ∈ 𝒜`: then `κ𝒜 ⊆ N`, and for `x ∈ I`, applying `ε` to `κx = Σ_uc_uu + y₂ + qz` (`y₂ ∈ I²`) gives
`κx = Σ_uc_u(u − 1) + q(z − ε(z)) + y₂`, which is the claim since `q` kills `I/I²`.

*The norms.* Put `y = q^{−1}D ∈ Hom_{O_C}(𝒜, C)`. With the convolution product `(f·g)(a) = (f ⊗ g)(Δa)` (unit `ε`) this is the
algebra `𝒜^∨ ⊗ C` of `C`-valued functions on the points of `H^D` (étale over `C`, which has characteristic `0`), which are all
`O_C`-rational (`C` is algebraically closed and `H^D` is finite over `O_C`), the value at `u` being `f(u)`; likewise
`Hom_{O_C}(𝒜 ⊗ 𝒜, C)` is the algebra of functions on `H^D × H^D`. Both algebras are commutative because `𝒜` is
cocommutative. Norm them by `‖f‖ = sup_{a∈𝒜}|f(a)|` on `Hom(𝒜, C)` and `‖f‖ = sup_{x∈𝒜⊗𝒜}|f(x)|` on `Hom(𝒜 ⊗ 𝒜, C)` (in a basis, the largest absolute value of a coordinate; `‖f‖ ≤ 1`
iff `f` takes integral values). These norms are ultrametric, homogeneous and submultiplicative, `‖ε‖ = 1`,
`‖f ⊗ g‖ = ‖f‖·‖g‖`, and the algebra map `Δ : Hom(𝒜, C) → Hom(𝒜 ⊗ 𝒜, C)`, `(Δf)(a ⊗ b) = f(ab)`, does not increase them.
(i) `y(u) = q^{−1}D(u) ∈ O_C` at every point `u`, so `y` is a root of the monic polynomial `∏_u(Y − y(u))`, whose
coefficients lie in `O_C`; hence all powers of `y` lie in the `O_C`-span of finitely many of them, and `‖y^k‖ ≤ K` for all
`k`. (ii) `e₀ = Δy − y ⊗ ε − ε ⊗ y` takes the value `q^{−1}(D(ab) − ε(b)D(a) − ε(a)D(b)) ∈ O_C` at `a ⊗ b`, so `‖e₀‖ ≤ 1`.

*Divided powers and the diagonal term.* (iii) Let `c ∈ C` with `|c| < |p|^{1/(p−1)}`, `z = cy`, `e = Δz − z ⊗ ε − ε ⊗ z = c·e₀`
(`‖e‖ ≤ |c|`), and `t(m) = ‖z^m/m!‖` (`t(0) = 1`). Since `|m!| = |p|^{v_p(m!)}` with `v_p(m!) ≤ m/(p − 1)`,
`t(m) ≤ K·(|c|·|p|^{−1/(p−1)})^m → 0` as `m → ∞`, and `‖e^l/l!‖ ≤ (|c|·|p|^{−1/(p−1)})^l < 1` for `l ≥ 1`. Suppose `t(1) > 1`.
Then `t` attains its maximum, which is `> 1`; let `k ≥ 1` be the last index at which it does. As `Δ` is an algebra map and the
algebras are commutative, `Δ(z^{2k}/(2k)!) = (z ⊗ ε + ε ⊗ z + e)^{2k}/(2k)! = Σ_{a+b+l=2k}(z^a/a! ⊗ z^b/b!)·e^l/l!`. The term
`(a, b, l) = (k, k, 0)` has norm exactly `t(k)²`. Every other term has smaller norm: for `l ≥ 1` its norm is at most
`t(a)t(b)·‖e^l/l!‖ < t(k)²`, as `t(a), t(b) ≤ t(k)`; for `l = 0` one of `a, b` exceeds `k`, where `t < t(k)`. So the sum has norm
`t(k)²`, while the left side has norm at most `t(2k) < t(k) < t(k)²`: a contradiction. Hence `‖cy‖ ≤ 1` whenever
`|c| < |p|^{1/(p−1)}`, and `‖κy‖ ≤ 1` whenever `|κ| ≤ |p|^{1/(p−1)}`, by letting `|c|` increase to `|κ|` (the value group of
`C` is divisible). This is `q | κ·D(a)` for all `a`, which proves the claim. It is the argument of [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/NormSmulLeOneOfPrimitiveBoundedPowers.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.NearlyPrimitive.norm_smul_le_one_of_lt`); nothing in
(i)–(iii) uses `p ≠ 2`.

*From the levels to `ω_{G^D}`.* Apply the claim to `H = G^D[p^n]`, `H^D = G[p^n]`. The image `R` of `T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}` is
finitely generated, and its reduction modulo `p^n` is the image at level `n` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.4: `T_pG → G[p^n](O_C)` is onto and
`α_G` modulo `p^n` is `α_{G[p^n]}`), so
`κ·ω_{G^D} ⊆ R + p^n·ω_{G^D}` for every `n`. The module `ω_{G^D}/R` is finitely presented, hence a finite direct sum of cyclic
modules `O_C/b_jO_C`; for `κ ≠ 0` and `n` with `|p^n| < |κ|`, `κ ∈ b_jO_C + p^nO_C` forces `κ ∈ b_jO_C`. So `κ` kills the
cokernel. (b) The route of 0.5a, which uses nothing about `p` except HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5, with (a) in place of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5, applied to the
`p`-divisible group `H^D` of the finite parts at codimension-one points (of good or semi-abelian reduction): `2^R·ω ⊆ 2·ω`
for `R ≥ 1`, so the image of `T_2(A^∨) ⊗ O_C`, reduced modulo `2^R` and lifted, together with `2^R·ω`, is the image itself, and it
contains `2·ω`; the passage to points where `ω^{HT}_R` is locally free and (b), (c) are as there.

*Needs:* HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.2, 0.4, 0.25; 0.5a.

### 0.5c The model where the Hodge–Tate lattice is locally free

*Origin:* adapted from Pilloni and Stroh (2016), the normalised blow-up of the minor ideal of the Hodge–Tate matrix, on which the modified Hodge bundle `ω^mod` of normal admissible formal models over `O_C` is locally free (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §5.5): here one normal projective model, a normalised Nash modification, on which the Hodge–Tate lattices of finitely many primes and all their eigen-quotients are locally free, with the pull-back of the eigen-quotients and the universal property of the model.

*Method of the formal proof:* models are projective closures of the generic point given by finite tuples of elements of the function field; for each of the finitely many coherent lattices, generated after a twist by finitely many sections, the tuple of the `r × r` minors of these sections (`r` the rank of the lattice; its Plücker coordinates) is adjoined, the model so joined is normalised, and the pulled-back lattice is free wherever one minor divides the others, which holds at every point of the normalised model and already held at the points where the lattice was free.

*Corollary of:* NashBlowups §1.2 (the Nash modification of a coherent sheaf, on which its pull-back has a locally free quotient of the generic rank, an isomorphism over the open set where the sheaf is locally free), for (b), with the normalisation of AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.4, and, with the universal property of NashBlowups §1.3, for (c); not for (a).

*Setting.* Let `X` be a noetherian normal integral scheme with function field `Ω` of characteristic `0`, `G → X` a semi-abelian scheme, abelian over a dense open subset, `ℓ` a prime and `R ≥ 1`, with `μ_{ℓ^R} ⊆ Ω` and the `ℓ^R`-torsion of the generic fibre `A` rational over `Ω`. The Hodge–Tate lattice `ω^{HT}_R(q) ⊆ ω_{G,q}` is defined at every point `q` of `X` by the formula of 0.5a (generated over `O_{X,q}` by `ℓ^R·ω_{G,q}` and the differentials at the unit section of the regular functions near the unit section over `q` that agree with a character of `A[ℓ^R](Ω)` at the `ℓ^R`-torsion points of the geometric generic fibre). The definition is local at `q`: at a point over `ℓ` it is the lattice of 0.5a for `X ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)`, which is in the setting of 0.5a; at every other point `ℓ` is a unit and `ω^{HT}_R(q) = ω_{G,q}`. So the `ω^{HT}_R(q)` are the stalks of a coherent subsheaf of `ω_G` with `ℓ^R·ω_G ⊆ ω^{HT}_R ⊆ ω_G`, stable under the endomorphisms of `G` (0.5a; 0.5b (b) at `ℓ = 2`). Let an order of a number field act on `G`, with all its embeddings `σ` in `Ω`, and let `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}` be the `σ`-eigen-quotient of `ω^{HT}_R` (Conventions), a coherent lattice in `Hom(Lie_σ, Ω)`.

Prove:

(a) (*pull-back of the eigen-quotients*) for a dominant morphism `g : X″ → X` of schemes as above, with function field `Ω″`, the Hodge–Tate lattice of `g^*G` is the image of `g^*ω^{HT}_R` in `g^*ω_G` (0.5a (c)), and its `σ`-eigen-quotient is the `O_{X″}`-submodule of `Hom(Lie_σ, Ω) ⊗ Ω″` generated by the pull-back of `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}`;

(b) (*the model*) let `X` be flat and projective over the ring of integers of a number field, and `Σ` a finite set of primes with levels `R(ℓ) ≥ 1`, `ℓ ∈ Σ`, each as in the setting. The normalisation `X′` of the Nash modification of `X` for the finitely many sheaves `ω^{HT}_{R(ℓ)}` and `ω^{HT}_{R(ℓ),σ}` (`ℓ ∈ Σ`, all `σ`), taken one after the other (NashBlowups §1.2), is a normal projective scheme with a projective birational morphism to `X` on which, for every `ℓ ∈ Σ`, the Hodge–Tate lattice of the pulled-back semi-abelian scheme and all its eigen-quotients are locally free; `X′ → X` is an isomorphism over every point at which all these sheaves are locally free, in particular over the generic fibre, and over the points above the primes outside `Σ` at which the order is étale over `ℤ`;

(c) (*universal property*) every normal `X″` with a dominant morphism to `X` on which the lattices of (b) are locally free factors uniquely through `X′`.
(c) is not a statement of [FormalProof], which proves the existence of the model; it follows from the universal properties
of the Nash modification (NashBlowups §1.3) and of normalisation, with (a).

Any finite family of coherent lattices can be made locally free on a normal projective birational model; what is special to the eigen-quotients is that the model does not change the generic fibre, the eigen-quotient of `ω_G` being a direct summand in characteristic `0`.
On normal admissible formal models of Siegel varieties over `O_C` at full level `p^n`, for `p ≥ 3`, the counterpart of (b) is the
modified Hodge bundle of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §5.5: the lattice generated by the Hodge–Tate images and `p^{1/(p−1)}·ω`
becomes locally free on the normalised blow-up of the minor ideal of the Hodge–Tate matrix, with `p^{1/(p−1)}·ω ⊆ ω^mod ⊆ ω`.

*Route.* (a) The image under a linear map of the module generated by a set is generated by the image of the set, and the eigen-projections commute with the extension `Ω → Ω″`; with 0.5a (c). (b)
follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/GeometryProofs/HodgeCptProjectiveModelStatement.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.T.hodge_cpt_projective_model`),
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/GeometryProofs/FlagModelCommonRefinementStatement.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.T.flag_model_common_refinement`).
After a twist each lattice is generated by finitely many global sections; the Nash modification is the blow-up of the ideal generated by their Plücker coordinates (NashBlowups §§1.2, 1.4), on which the image of the pulled-back lattice is locally free; do this for the finitely many lattices in turn (a locally free image stays locally free under further pull-back) and normalise (finite: AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.4). By (a) the locally free sheaves so obtained are the Hodge–Tate lattice of the pulled-back scheme and its eigen-quotients. Where all the lattices are already locally free the Plücker ideals are invertible and, `X` being normal, nothing changes; on the generic fibre `ω^{HT}_R = ω_G` and the eigen-quotients are direct summands; over a prime `ℓ′ ∉ Σ` at which the order is étale, `ω^{HT}_{R(ℓ)} = ω_G` for all `ℓ ∈ Σ` and the idempotents exist. (c) The universal property of the Nash modification (NashBlowups §1.3), (a), and the universal property of normalisation (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.4). The coherence and the pull-back of the lattice are used as 0.5a states them, and the local freeness of the image of a pulled-back lattice on the Nash modification and the isomorphism where the lattices are already locally free as NashBlowups §§1.2–1.4 state them; their proofs are not repeated here.

*Needs:* 0.5a, 0.5b; NashBlowups §§1.2–1.5; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §§1.3, 5.4.

**Checks.**

- The Katz–Mazur model of `X(Nℓ^R)`, `N ≥ 3` prime to `ℓ`, as in the first Check of 0.17a: at a supersingular point all Hodge–Tate forms vanish in the fibre and `ω^{HT}_R` is not locally free there; `X′ → X` modifies the model over these points.
- For `Σ = {ℓ₁, ℓ₂}` the model is obtained by modifying over `ℓ₁` and then over `ℓ₂`, in either order: the result is normal, the lattices of both primes are locally free on it (a locally free image stays locally free under pull-back), and (c), applied to each step, gives it the universal property of (c) for `Σ`, which determines it up to unique isomorphism over `X`. If the order is étale over `ℤ` at `ℓ₁`, the second modification is moreover an isomorphism over `ℓ₁`, by the last clause of (b).

### 0.5d The colength of the Hodge–Tate image at an unramified place

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the exact relation `x(τ) − x(τ∘c) = r(τ)/(p − 1) − Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}·r(Frob^{i+1}τ)` between the colengths of the Hodge–Tate image at conjugate embeddings, for a principally polarised abelian scheme with an action of `𝒪` unramified at the odd prime `p`, with no condition on Hasse invariants, and the bound `S(τ_s)` of (a); nearest published result: Hernandez (2019), an exact formula for the degree of the cokernel of the `τ`-part of the Hodge–Tate map near the `μ`-ordinary locus for `p` large, which with the duality of the partial Hasse invariants gives the relation of (b) in that range.

*Method of the formal proof:* a duality relates the colengths at conjugate embeddings through the colengths of an auxiliary lattice of forms regular modulo `p`, whose product over the embeddings is fixed by the determinant of the period matrix; expanding `p`-th powers of isotypic lifts in the Hopf algebra of the `p`-torsion gives an inequality between consecutive embeddings of a Frobenius cycle, and the global count forces each inequality to be an equality.

Let `p ≠ 2`, `O` the `p`-adically complete valuation ring of an algebraically closed valued field of characteristic `0` and residue characteristic `p`, `T` a complete noetherian local domain of characteristic `0` in which `p` is not a unit, `T → O` a local homomorphism, and `G` a semi-abelian scheme over `T` with a uniformisation (degeneration data over `T` whose abelian part is a polarised abelian scheme `B`, with the extension `G̃` of `B` by a torus `T_0` that they define; the generic fibre of `G` a polarised abelian variety; these are the degeneration data of ShimuraCompactifications §6.4, which take `T` normal); assume that the polarisation of `B` is principal (for an abelian scheme `G`, `B = G`, no degeneration data are needed and `T` need not be normal). Let `𝒪` be the ring of integers of a number field with an involution `c`, `p ∤ disc(𝒪)`, acting on `G`, on the degeneration data and on the generic fibre compatibly, `a` and `c(a)` being adjoint for the polarisations. Let `w` be a place of `𝒪` above `p`, `τ_0, …, τ_{n−1}` the embeddings `𝒪 → O` at `w` in Frobenius order (`τ_{s+1} ≡ τ_s^p` modulo the maximal ideal of `O`, indices modulo `n`), and for every embedding `τ : 𝒪 → O` let `r(τ)` be the rank of the `τ`-eigenspace `Lie_τ` of `Lie(G) ⊗ O`. Prove:

(a) for every `s` there are finitely many homomorphisms of formal groups `Ĝ_O → Ĝ_m` whose differentials restricted to `Lie_{τ_s}` span a submodule `L_s ⊂ Hom(Lie_{τ_s}, O)` with `Hom(Lie_{τ_s}, O)/L_s ≅ ⊕_iO/(c_i)` and `∏_i|c_i| ≥ |p|^{S(τ_s)}`, where `S(τ_s) := Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}·r(τ_{s+i+1}∘c)`;

Then `x(τ) − x(τ∘c) = r(τ)/(p − 1) − Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}·r(Frob^{i+1}τ)` and `x(τ∘c) ≤ r(τ∘c)/(p − 1)`.

The inequality `x(τ∘c) ≤ r(τ∘c)/(p − 1)` is Fargues' bound (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5).

*Route.* (a) is [FormalProof], `ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/ArithmeticGeometry/PlaceDivisors/StChartF.lean`
(`ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Pdiv.ChartF.UpFSt`), and (b) is [FormalProof],

So

`ξ(τ) = r(τ)/(p − 1) − x(τ) + x(τ∘c)`. (∗)

*Frobenius.* For an embedding `σ` at a place `v` above `p`, `p·ξ(σ) − ξ(Frob σ) ≥ r(Frob σ)`. The group `(𝒪/p)^× = ∏_v𝔽_v^×` acts on `G[p]`, hence on its Hopf algebra `𝒜`, a ring of functions on `N = T_pG/p`; for `σ` at `v` let `χ_σ` be the character `a ↦ σ([a_v])` (`[a_v]` the Teichmüller representative of the component at `v`); as `p` is odd, the `χ_σ` are nontrivial and pairwise distinct. Choose bases `(ε_{σ,l}b_{σ,l})_l` of the `𝔄_σ`, with `(b_{σ,l})_l` a basis of `Hom_σ` and `v(ε_{σ,l}) ≤ v(c_p) < 1` (0.5h (2)), and lifts `y_{σ,l} ∈ 𝒜` that are `χ_σ`-isotypic (project any lift; the order of `(𝒪/p)^×` is prime to `p`). The chain of 0.5h is a chain of equalities, so the monomials `y^m` with all exponents `< p` span `𝒜`, and those with `m ≠ 0` span the augmentation ideal `I` (each `y_{σ,l}` vanishes at `0`). The `z_{σ,l} = y_{σ,l}/ε_{σ,l}` reduce modulo `𝔪` to the `b̄_{σ,l}`, so the `z^m` form a basis of the functions `N → O` (the monomials of degree `< p` in a basis of `Hom_{𝔽_p}(N, k)`, `k` the residue field, span the functions `N → k`, because the `p`-th power of such a linear form is again one, and they are as many as its dimension). Modulo `𝔪`, `b̄_{σ,l}^p` is additive and `Frob σ`-semilinear, so `(b̄_{σ,l}^p)_l` is a basis of the `Frob σ`-semilinear forms and the matrix `M_σ` of the coefficients of the `z_{Frob σ,l′}` in the `z_{σ,l}^p` is invertible. Expanding `y_{σ,l}^p ∈ 𝒜` in the `y^m`, the coefficients of the `y_{Frob σ,l′}` are `(B_σ)_{l,l′} = ε_{σ,l}^p(M_σ)_{l,l′}/ε_{Frob σ,l′}` and every other monomial that occurs has degree `≥ 2`; as `y_{σ,l}^p ∈ I²`, the rows of `B_σ` are relations among the classes of the `y_{Frob σ,l′}` in `I/I² = ω/p`, whose `χ_{Frob σ}`-part, free of rank `r(Frob σ)` over `O/p`, these classes generate. So the cokernel of `B_σ` modulo `p`, of degree at most `v(det B_σ)`, maps onto `(O/p)^{r(Frob σ)}`, and `p·ξ(σ) − ξ(Frob σ) = v(det B_σ) ≥ r(Frob σ)`. *Equality.* Summing over the embeddings at each place `v`, `(p − 1)·Σ_{σ at v}ξ(σ) ≥ Σ_{σ at v}r(σ)`; summed over all places both sides are `g` (`Σ_σξ(σ) = g·v(c_p)` by 0.5h (2), `Σ_σr(σ) = g`). So every inequality is an equality, `ξ(Frob σ) = p·ξ(σ) − r(Frob σ)`, and going round the cycle, `ξ(τ) = Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}r(Frob^{i+1}τ)`; with (∗) this is the identity of (b). The cokernel of `𝔥` on `T_τ` is killed by `c_p` (0.5g (c)) and generated by `r(τ∘c)` elements, so `x(τ∘c) ≤ r(τ∘c)/(p − 1)`.

(a) for an abelian scheme: `T_τ` is an extension of `𝔥(T_τ)`, of rank `r(τ∘c)`, by `K_τ`, of rank `r(τ)`, so `r(τ) + r(τ∘c)` is the rank of `T_w` over `𝒪_w` for every `τ` at `w`, and (b) gives `x(τ_s) ≤ (r(τ_s) + r(τ_s∘c))/(p − 1) − ξ(τ_s) = Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)}(r(τ_{s+i+1}) + r(τ_{s+i+1}∘c) − r(τ_{s+i+1})) = S(τ_s)`; the characters of a `ℤ_p`-basis of `T_p(G^∨)` have differentials spanning `L(τ_s)`. (a) in general: by 0.5f's route `Ĝ_O = Ĝ̃_O`, an `𝒪`-equivariant extension of `B̂_O` by `T̂_0`, and `Lie_τ(G)` is an extension of `Lie_τ(B)` by `Lie_τ(T_0)`. Over `O` the torus `T_0` is split, with character group `X`. Cartier duality turns `0 → T_0[p^∞] → G̃[p^∞] → B[p^∞] → 0` into an exact sequence `0 → T_p(B^∨) → T_p(G̃[p^∞]^D) → X ⊗ ℤ_p → 0` (FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory R07.1.7; exactness is checked on the generic fibre), so every `χ ∈ X` is the restriction to `T̂_0` of a character of `Ĝ_O`. The restrictions to `Lie_τ(G)` of the differentials of these characters and of the characters of `B̂_O` pulled back to `Ĝ_O` span a submodule containing `L_τ(B)` and mapping onto `Hom(Lie_τ(T_0), O)`; so `Hom(Lie_τ(G), O)/L_τ(G)` is a quotient of `Hom(Lie_τ(B), O)/L_τ(B)`, and (a) for `B`, with `r_B ≤ r_G`, gives (a) for `G`.

**Checks.**

- A one-dimensional formal group of height `2` with `𝒪_w = ℤ_{p²}` (`r = 1, 0` at the two embeddings, `0, 1` at their conjugates): `S = 1/p + 1/p³ + ⋯ = p/(p² − 1)`, and (b) gives `x = p/(p² − 1)` at the embedding of the differential and `0` at the other.
- `S` depends only on the ranks `r`, not on the reduction. It vanishes exactly when `r(τ∘c) = 0` for every `τ` at `w`, and then the differentials span every `Hom(Lie_{τ_s}, O)`; for an abelian scheme this is the case in which the part of `G[p^∞]` at `w` is of multiplicative type, its dual being the part at `c(w)`, of dimension `Σ_{τ at w}r(τ∘c)`. For an ordinary elliptic curve with `𝒪 = ℤ` and `c = 1`, `r = 1` and `S = Σ_{i≥0}p^{−(i+1)} = 1/(p − 1)`, while the Hodge–Tate image is all of `ω`, of colength `0` (the étale quotient `ℚ_p/ℤ_p` of `G^∨[p^∞]` already maps onto it: 0.5b, first Check): the bound of (a) is not sharp there.
- The Lubin–Tate group of height `h`: `S = 1/(p − 1) − 1/(p^h − 1)`, the valuation of the period of its dual (Schneider–Teitelbaum, Appendix, Theorem (c), with `e = 1` and `q = p^h`).
- Always `S(τ_s) ≤ max_τr(τ∘c)/(p − 1)`.

### 0.5e The colength bound at a place disjoint from its conjugate

*Origin:* adapted from Fargues' bound for the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map, Fargues (2010), (2011): at a place `w ≠ w̄` the total colength of the character differentials on the embeddings at `w` is bounded through the rank `r⁻` of the Lie algebra at the conjugate place `w̄`, by duality, with a term `vd/2` for the different.

*Method of the formal proof:* the semi-abelian scheme and the action are read in formal charts, the formal group law is split equivariantly into its abelian and toric parts, and the bound becomes a statement on linear data: the colength of the Hodge–Tate image of the abelian part at `w` is bounded through the part at `w̄` by the relation between the period matrices and the Weil form, the ring `O_{E,w} = ℤ_p[α]` is compared with the product of `n` copies of `O` through the discriminant of the power basis, and an inequality between the colength of an `O[α]`-span of `h` elements and the product of the colengths of its `n` projections gives the exponent `n·r⁻·(1/(p−1) + vd/2)`.

Let `p` be any prime and `T`, `G`, `O`, `T → O` as in 0.5f, the polarisation of the abelian part `B` of the uniformisation being principal (as in 0.5d); `|·|` the absolute value of the fraction field of `O`. Let the ring of integers `O_E` of a number field `E` with an involution `c` act on `G`, on the degeneration data and on the generic fibre compatibly, `a` and `c(a)` being adjoint for the polarisations (as in 0.5d). Let `w` be a place of `E` above `p` with `w ≠ w̄` (`w̄ = c(w)`), `n = [E_w:ℚ_p]`, `τ_1, …, τ_n` the embeddings `O_E → O` at `w`, and `vd := v_p(disc(E_w/ℚ_p))/n`, the valuation (`v_p(p) = 1`) of a generator of the different of `E_w/ℚ_p`. For an embedding `τ : O_E → O` let `Lie_τ ⊆ Lie(G) ⊗ O` be the submodule on which `O_E` acts through `τ`; it is free of finite rank. Assume that `Lie_τ` has the same rank `r⁺` at all embeddings `τ` at `w`, and the same rank `r⁻` at all embeddings at `w̄`. Prove: there are finitely many homomorphisms of formal groups `Ĝ_O → Ĝ_m` such that, `L_j ⊆ Hom(Lie_{τ_j}, O)` denoting the span of the restrictions of their differentials and `Hom(Lie_{τ_j}, O)/L_j ≅ ⊕_i O/(c_{j,i})`,

`∏_{j=1}^{n} ∏_i |c_{j,i}| ≥ |p|^{n·r⁻·(1/(p−1) + vd/2)}`.

(Equivalently `Σ_{j,i} v(c_{j,i}) ≤ n·r⁻·(1/(p − 1) + vd/2)`. When `r⁻·(1/(p − 1) + vd/2) < r⁺/(p − 1)`, for instance when `r⁻ < r⁺` and `E_w/ℚ_p` is unramified, this improves the bound `n·r⁺/(p − 1)` that 0.5f gives; nothing is asserted about a single embedding.)

*Route.* The bound follows from [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/UnramifiedDepth/ColengthBoundSplitPlaceChart.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.ChF.colength_bound_split_place_chart`).
*Reduction.* As in the route of 0.5d (a), `Ĝ_O = Ĝ̃_O` is an `O_E`-equivariant extension of `B̂_O` by `T̂_0`; the character group `X` of `T_0` is a projective `O_E`-module of some rank `ρ`, so `Lie_τ(T_0)` has rank `ρ` at every `τ` and `r^±_G = r^±_B + ρ`; the characters of `B̂_O` pull back to `Ĝ_O`, and every `χ ∈ X` is the restriction to `T̂_0` of a character of `Ĝ_O`. *The abelian part.* Let `𝔥` and `c_p` (`|c_p|^{p−1} = |p|`) be the period data of the principally polarised `B_O` (0.5g), `𝔄` the module of 0.5h, and `T_v`, `ω_{B,v}`, `Lie_{B,v}`, `𝔄_v` the parts of `T_pB ⊗ O`, `ω_B ⊗ O`, `Lie_B ⊗ O`, `𝔄` cut out by the idempotent of a place `v` (`𝔄` is `O_E`-stable, 0.5h (5)); by adjointness `𝔥` maps `T_{w̄}` into `ω_{B,w}` and `T_w` into `ω_{B,w̄}`, and `K = ker 𝔥` is a Lagrangian (2.4 (b)). Write `γ_w`, `γ_{w̄}` for the colengths (products of absolute values of elementary divisors) of these two images. (1) `ω_{B,w̄}/𝔥(T_w)` is killed by `c_p` (0.5g (c)) and generated by `rk ω_{B,w̄} = n·r⁻_B ≤ n·r⁻` elements, so `γ_{w̄} ≥ |p|^{n·r⁻/(p−1)}`. (2) `γ_w ≥ γ_{w̄}`. Let `d_w = rk Lie_{B,w}` and `X_w = |p|^{ξ_w}`, `ξ_w` the degree (0.5h) of `Hom(T_w, O)/𝔄_w`. The argument of (∗) in the route of 0.5d (b), with the idempotents of `w`, `w̄` in place of those of `τ`, `τ∘c` (the Weil form pairs `T_w` with `T_{w̄}` perfectly, as `w ≠ w̄`), gives `X_w·γ_w = |c_p|^{d_w}·γ_{w̄}`. And `X_w ≤ |c_p|^{d_w}`: `𝔄_w` is the module of 0.5h formed for the factor `B[p]_w` of `B[p]`, and the first two steps of the chain of 0.5h for that factor (the monomials of degree `< p` in lifts of a basis adapted to `𝔄_w` lie in its Hopf algebra; the discriminant formula) give `(p − 1)·ξ_w ≥ deg(ω_{B[p]_w}) = d_w`. Hence `γ_w ≥ |p|^{n·r⁻/(p−1)}`. *The order at `w`.* `O_{E,w} = ℤ_p[α]` (the ring of integers of a finite extension of `ℚ_p` is monogenic), `f` the minimal polynomial of `α`; the `a_j = τ_j(α)` are distinct and `D = ∏_{i<j}(a_i − a_j)²` has `|D| = |p|^{n·vd}`. In `Õ = ∏_jO ⊇ O[α] = O_{E,w} ⊗ O` (`α ↦ (a_j)_j`), `Õ/O[α]` has degree `n·vd/2` (compare discriminants), and `f′(α)·Õ ⊆ O[α]` (the trace dual of `O[α]` is `f′(α)^{−1}O[α]`), `Õ/f′(α)Õ` having degree `n·vd`. Let `M ⊆ S = ∏_jHom(Lie_{τ_j}, O)` be the image of `ω_G ⊗ O` under restriction: an `O[α]`-module (`α` acting by `a_j` on the `j`-th factor) that maps onto every factor (`Lie_{τ_j}` is saturated). *Generators.* The `w̄`-part of `T_pB` is free over the discrete valuation ring `O_{E,w̄}`, of rank `r⁺_B + r⁻_B` (`T_{w̄}` is an extension of `𝔥(T_{w̄})`, of rank `n·r⁺_B`, by `K_{w̄}`, of rank `n·r⁻_B`), and `𝔥(ax) = c(a)^*𝔥(x)`; so `𝔥(T_{w̄})` is generated over `O[α]` by the differentials of the characters of `r⁺_B + r⁻_B` elements of `T_pB`. The `w`-part of `ω_{T_0} ⊗ O = X ⊗ O` is generated over `O[α]` by `dχ/χ` for `ρ` elements `χ ∈ X`. With the lifts of these `χ`, `h = r⁺_B + r⁻_B + ρ` character differentials of `Ĝ_O` generate an `O[α]`-submodule `M′ ⊆ M`, and `M` is `M′` plus the image of `ω_{B,w}`; so `M/M′` is a quotient of `ω_{B,w}/𝔥(T_{w̄})`, of degree at most `n·r⁻/(p − 1)` by (1) and (2), and `r⁺ ≤ h ≤ r⁺ + r⁻`. Put `S′ = ∏_jpr_j(M′)`; each `pr_j(M′)` has rank `r⁺`, `M/M′` being torsion. *Two colength facts.* (I) `deg(S/M) ≤ r⁺·n·vd/2`: `M` contains `r⁺` elements whose `j`-th components form a basis of `Hom(Lie_{τ_j}, O)` for every `j` (for each `j` a nonempty open condition modulo `𝔪`, and the residue field is infinite), and their `O[α]`-span is the image of `O[α]^{r⁺}` under an isomorphism `Õ^{r⁺} → S`, of colength `r⁺·deg(Õ/O[α])` in `S`. (II) `deg(S′/M′) ≥ (r⁺ − h/2)·n·vd`: `M′` is the image of `O[α]^h` under `Φ : Õ^h → S′`, the product over `j` of the surjections `Φ_j : O^h → pr_j(M′)` given by the generators; so `S′/M′ ≅ Õ^h/(O[α]^h + ker Φ)`, and `ker Φ = ∏_jker Φ_j`, of rank `h − r⁺` in each factor, satisfies `f′(α)·ker Φ ⊆ ker Φ ∩ O[α]^h`, whence `deg(S′/M′) ≥ h·n·vd/2 − (h − r⁺)·n·vd`. *Conclusion.* `deg(S/S′) = deg(S/M) + deg(M/M′) − deg(S′/M′) ≤ deg(M/M′) + (h − r⁺)·n·vd/2 ≤ n·r⁻·(1/(p − 1) + vd/2)`, and `L_j ⊇ pr_j(M′)`, so `Σ_{j,i}v(c_{j,i}) ≤ deg(S/S′)`.

*Needs:* 0.5d, 0.5f, 0.5g, 0.5h, 2.4.

**Checks.**

- `E_w = ℚ_p` (`n = 1`, `vd = 0`): the colength at `w` is at most `r⁻/(p − 1)`. For `r⁻ = 0` the differentials span `Hom(Lie_τ, O)`: the part of the `p`-divisible group at `w` is of multiplicative type, as its dual at `w̄` has dimension `0`.
- Signature `(1, 1)` at a split place of a quadratic field (`n = 1`, `r⁺ = r⁻ = 1`), connected reduction: the part at `w` is a one-dimensional formal group of height `2`, and the bound is `1/(p − 1)`, the bound of 0.5f; for the Lubin–Tate group of `ℤ_{p²}` the colength is `p/(p² − 1)` (0.5d, Checks), so the bound is not attained there.
- `w` ramified quadratic over an odd `p` (`n = 2`, `vd = 1/2`), `r⁻ = 0`: the bound is `0`, although `O_{E,w} ⊗ O` is not the product of two copies of `O`.
- `r⁻ < r⁺` alone does not make the bound better than that of 0.5f: for `p = 3` and `E_w = ℚ_3(ζ_9)` (`n = 6`, `v_3(disc) = 9`, `vd = 3/2`; a place `w ≠ w̄` of `ℚ(ζ_9, √−2)`, `3` being split in `ℚ(√−2)`) with `r⁺ = 2`, `r⁻ = 1`, the bound is `6·(1/2 + 3/4) = 15/2`, while 0.5f gives `6·2/2 = 6`.

### 0.5f The cokernel bound in chart form

*Origin:* adapted from Fargues (2010), (2011): the bound for the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map of a `p`-divisible group over `O_C`, proved for odd `p` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5) and stated with no hypothesis on `p` in Fargues (2011), with no printed proof at `p = 2`, here read on the formal group of a uniformised semi-abelian scheme, at every prime.

*Method of the formal proof:* for a uniformisation whose abelian part `B` is principally polarised (the hypothesis that 0.5d and 0.5e state and that the statement here does not make): the formal group law of `G` is a split extension of that of `B` by a formal torus; the period relation of 0.5g with the constant `c_p`, `|c_p|^{p−1} = |p|`, the columns of the period matrix being differentials of characters, puts `c_p` times the cotangent module of `B` in the span of the differentials of its characters; linear algebra on the split extension then puts `c_p·(ω_G ⊗ O)` in the span of the differentials of the torus characters and of the pulled-back characters of `B`. The route written here differs: it applies 0.5b (a) to the `p`-divisible group `H^D`, `H = G̃[p^∞]_O`, and reads the characters off a basis of `T_p(H^D)`, with no polarisation of `B`.

*Corollary of:* HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5 (Fargues's bound: for odd `p`, every `a ∈ O_C` with `v(a) ≥ 1/(p − 1)` kills the cokernel of the Hodge–Tate map of a `p`-divisible group over `O_C`), for odd `p` only.

Let `p` be a prime, `T` a complete noetherian local domain of characteristic `0` in which `p` is not a unit, `G` a semi-abelian scheme over `T` with a uniformisation (as in 0.5d), `O` the `p`-adically complete valuation ring of an algebraically closed valued field of characteristic `0`, and `T → O` a local homomorphism. Prove: there are finitely many homomorphisms of formal groups `Ĝ_O → Ĝ_m` whose differentials span a submodule of `ω_G ⊗ O` containing `κ·(ω_G ⊗ O)` for every `κ ∈ O` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`. This holds at every prime.

*Route.* The absolute value has rank one and `O` is `p`-adically complete, so `O = O_C` for the complete algebraically closed field `C = Frac O`. The uniformisation identifies the formal completion of `G` along its closed fibre with that of the extension `G̃` of `B` by the torus `T_0` that its degeneration data define (ShimuraCompactifications §§6.3–6.5); so `Ĝ_O` is the formal group of the `p`-divisible group `H = G̃[p^∞]_O`, and `ω_H = ω_G ⊗ O`. ### 0.5g The period matrix of a principally polarised abelian scheme over `O_C`

*Origin:* adapted from Colmez (1992), Théorème 5.2: his `p`-adic Riemann relation between the periods of an abelian variety over a finite extension of `ℚ_p` and its Weil pairing, after inverting `p`, here stated integrally over `A_inf/(ker θ)²` for a principally polarised abelian scheme over `O_C`, with the constant `c`, `|c| = |ζ_p − 1|`, `det Π = c^g` up to a unit, and both rows `𝔥`, `𝔟` of `Π` regular modulo `p` on `A[p]`.

*Method of the formal proof:* the abelian scheme is lifted with a symmetric line bundle to the square-zero thickening `A_inf/(ker θ)²` of `O_C`; the Weil pairing is a ratio of two trivialisations in the biextension given by the theorem of the cube, the Hodge–Tate functional is the pairing with tangent vectors, and taking `N`-th multiples of a point above two lifted torsion points in the two orders gives `z̃^N = 1 + ⟨𝔥(t), ξV_s⟩ − ⟨𝔥(s), ξV_t⟩`, which along the lifts of the `p^n`-th roots of unity becomes `𝔟^T𝔥 − 𝔥^T𝔟 = c·E`. The computation is that of Colmez's proof, carried out integrally on a lift of the abelian scheme instead of after inverting `p` over a finite extension of `ℚ_p`.

Let `p` be any prime, `C` complete algebraically closed of characteristic `0`, `A` a principally polarised abelian scheme over `O_C` of dimension `g`, `E` the matrix of the Weil pairing in a basis of `T_pA`, and `𝔥 : T_pA ⊗ O_C → ω_A` the Hodge–Tate map of HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.4 composed with the polarisation. Let `O_C^{(2)} → O_C` be the square-zero thickening `A_inf/(ker θ)²`, `I ≅ O_C` its kernel, and `Ã` a lift of `A` with its polarisation. For `x = (x_n) ∈ T_pA` and lifts `x̃_n ∈ Ã(O_C^{(2)}/p^n)` of the `x_n`, the element `p^n·x̃_n ∈ Lie(A) ⊗ I/p^n` does not depend on the choice of the lifts `x̃_n` (it depends on `Ã`); its limit is `𝔟(x) ∈ Lie(A) ⊗ I`. Prove:

(a) `𝔟` is `ℤ_p`-linear and functorial for homomorphisms that lift (the functoriality of `𝔟` is not used below);

(b) the same construction for `𝔾_m` sends a generator of `ℤ_p(1)` to `c` times a generator of `I`, with `|c| = |ζ_p − 1|`;

(c) `⟨𝔟(x), 𝔥(y)⟩ − ⟨𝔥(x), 𝔟(y)⟩ = c·E(x, y)`, that is `𝔟^T𝔥 − 𝔥^T𝔟 = c·E` (`Π^TJΠ = c·E` for `Π = (𝔥; 𝔟)` and `J = [[0, −1], [1, 0]]`); hence `det Π` is `c^g` times a unit, the kernel of `𝔥` is Lagrangian (2.4 (b), `A[p^∞]` having height `2g` and dimension `g`), and `c` kills the cokernel of `𝔥` (0.5b (a), at every prime; for odd `p`, HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.5);

(d) the functions `x ↦ 𝔥(x)` and `x ↦ 𝔟(x)` on `A[p](O_C) = T_pA/p`, with values in `O_C/p`, are restrictions of regular functions on `A[p]`.

*Route.* (b)–(d) follow from [FormalProof],
`Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Statements/AlgebraStatements/HodgeTateDatumStatement.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.HodgeTateDatumStatement`), `ReciprocityTheory/DualLattices/CotangentSurjective.lean`
(`ReciprocityTheory.PGsh.Up.HodgeTateDatum`).
Lift `A` with its polarisation to the thickening (deformation of a principally polarised abelian scheme along a square-zero extension). (c) *The tangent pairing formula*: for a square-zero thickening `R′ → R` with kernel `(ξ)` free of rank one over `R`, `N = 0` in `R′`, `N`-torsion points `s`, `t` with lifts `s̃`, `t̃`, `Ns̃ = ξV_s`, `Nt̃ = ξV_t`, and a lift `z̃` of `e_N(s, t)`: `z̃^N = 1 + ⟨𝔥(t), ξV_s⟩ − ⟨𝔥(s), ξV_t⟩`, `𝔥(t)` being the differential at the origin of `e_N(·, t)`. ### 0.5h Functions additive modulo `p` on `A[p]`

*Origin:* particular to this proof: over `O_C` the Hopf algebra of the `p`-torsion of a principally polarised abelian scheme is generated by functions additive modulo `p`, and the linear forms congruent modulo `p` to its elements form the row span of the period matrix `Π` of 0.5g.

*Method of the formal proof:* the row span of `Π` lies in `𝔄(A)` and has colength `g·v(c)` by the determinant of `Π`; the monomials of degree `< p` in lifts of a Smith basis of `𝔄(A)` lie in `𝒜`, and the valuation of the determinant of their values shows that `p^{2g}(p−1)·deg(Hom/𝔄) ≥ 2·colength(𝒜)`, and `2·colength(𝒜) ≥ p^{2g}·deg(ω_{A[p]}) = p^{2g}(p−1)·g·v(c)`; the chain of inequalities closes, so the two lattices are equal and the monomials span `𝒜`.

In the setting of 0.5g let `𝒜` be the Hopf algebra of `A[p]`, identified with functions on `N = T_pA/p`, and `𝔄(A) ⊆ Hom(T_pA, O_C)` the module of linear forms congruent modulo `p` to elements of `𝒜`.
For a finitely presented torsion `O_C`-module `M ≅ ⊕_i O_C/(c_i)` put `deg M := Σ_i v(c_i)` (`v(p) = 1`; the degree of
HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.24); for a lattice `L′ ⊆ L` the *colength* of `L′` in `L` is `deg(L/L′)`. (The ordinary
length of such a module is infinite.) Prove:

(1) `𝔄(A)` is the row span of `Π`;

(2) `c·Hom(T_pA, O_C) ⊆ 𝔄(A)`, and `Hom(T_pA, O_C)/𝔄(A)` has degree `g·v(c)`;

(3) `𝔄(A)^∨ = {x : 𝔥(x), 𝔟(x) integral}`, `𝔥(𝔄(A)^∨) = ω_A`, and `𝔄(A)^∨ ∩ ker 𝔥 = c^{−1}E^{−T}𝔥^T(O_C^g)`;

(4) `𝒜` is generated as an algebra by its elements additive modulo `p`, and `2·colength(𝒜) = p^{2g}(p − 1)·g·v(c) = p^{2g}·deg(ω_{A[p]})`, the colength of `𝒜` being taken in the algebra of all `O_C`-valued functions on `N`;

(5) `𝔄(A)` is stable under the endomorphisms of `A`;

(4′) for every finite flat subgroup `H ⊆ A[p]` of an abelian scheme `A` over `O_C` carrying a relatively ample line bundle (no principal polarisation assumed), the Hopf algebra of `A[p]/H` is generated as an algebra by its elements additive modulo `p`;

(6) (*semi-abelian reduction*) let `G` be a semi-abelian scheme over `O_C` with principally polarised abelian generic fibre `A`, obtained by base change along a local homomorphism `T → O_C` from a semi-abelian scheme with a uniformisation over a complete noetherian normal local domain `T` of characteristic `0` (the setting of 0.5d), `B` the abelian part of the uniformisation over `O_C`, `Y` the cocharacter module of its torus part, and `𝔊_1` the `p`-torsion of the group of 0.18a. Then `𝔊_1 ≅ (B × ℰ ⊗ Y)[p]` for an ordinary elliptic curve `ℰ` over `O_C`; the lattice `𝔄(𝔊_1)` of forms regular modulo `p` has colength `g·v(c)` and contains `c·Hom(T_pA, O_C)`; the Hopf algebra of `𝔊_1` is generated by its elements additive modulo `p`; and for the map `𝔥_G : T_pA ⊗ O_C → ω_G` of 0.18a (c) composed with the polarisation, `𝔥_G(𝔄(𝔊_1)^∨) = ω_G` and `𝔄(𝔊_1)^∨ ∩ ker 𝔥_G = c^{−1}E^{−T}𝔥_G^T(O_C^g)`.

*Route.* (1)–(3) are [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Statements/AlgebraStatements/PolarisedAFStatement.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.GR.PolarisedAFStatement`).
(1)–(4) from 0.5g by the chain `p^{2g}(p−1)·deg(Hom/𝔄) ≥ 2·colength(𝒜) ≥ p^{2g}·deg(ω) = p^{2g}(p−1)·g·v(c) ≥ p^{2g}(p−1)·deg(Hom/𝔄)`.   `O_S`-module and `(T/p) ⊗ O_S → ω/pω` is not a map of `O_S`-modules. The statement of (a) on `S` concerns `ω ⊗ O_S = ω/ϖω`, a line
  over the residue field, onto which `(T/p) ⊗ O_S` (of dimension `2`) maps with a one-dimensional kernel. The sequence of (a) modulo `p^{R−1}` is a sequence of
  `O/p^{R−1}`-modules, `O/p^{R−1} = O/ϖ^{e(R−1)}`, and `ω/p^{R−1}` is one of its terms; what is not an `O_S`-module is `ω/p`.

### 0.17b The Hodge–Tate sequence modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`, at every prime

*Origin:* adapted from Pilloni and Stroh (2016) and Scholze (2015), as for 0.17a (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§5.5, 2.5, with §2.4): the sequence of eigen-quotients modulo `ℓ^{R−1}` at every prime `ℓ`, `ℓ = 2` and the primes ramified in `E` included, with the twist by the different at the ramified primes, and the flags, power sums and nefness of (d).

*Method of the formal proof:* for odd `ℓ` with `κ = ζ_ℓ − 1` and the modulus `ℓ^{R−1}`, and at `ℓ = 2` with `κ = 4` and the modulus `2^{R−2}`: the statement is proved for an abstract chart over a noetherian normal domain, with a test point over the valuation ring of an algebraically closed field at each height-one prime containing `ℓ`; the sandwich and the vanishing of `α_σ∘β_σ` modulo `ℓ^m` are tested at the height-one primes and descended from the test points, where the kernel of the Hodge–Tate map is its own orthogonal; `β_σ` is defined through the Gram matrix of the Weil pairing corrected by a generator of the different and has a retraction; and exactness follows because a map between projective modules of the same rank that is onto modulo `ℓ^m` is injective modulo `ℓ^m`.

Let `E` be a number field with an involution `c` (the identity is allowed), `ℓ` any prime, `R ≥ 2`, and `X`, `G` as in 0.5c, with: an action of `O_E` on `G`; a principal polarisation of `A` whose Rosati involution induces `c` on `O_E`; and the embeddings of `E` in `Ω`. Let `T`, `T_σ`, `⟨·,·⟩_σ`, `𝔡_σ` be as in the Conventions, and `α : T → ω^{HT}_R/ℓ^R·ω_G` the Hodge–Tate map of the level (`α(x)` is the differential at the unit section of the regular function of the character `e_{ℓ^R}(x, ·)`, 0.5a); it satisfies `α(ax) = c(a)^*α(x)`. Assume that `ω^{HT}_R` and all its eigen-quotients `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}` are locally free at the points over `ℓ`. All statements are on a neighbourhood of the fibre over `ℓ` in `X ⊗ ℤ_(ℓ)`. Prove:

(a) `rk ω^{HT}_{R,σ} + rk ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄} = N` for every `σ`;

(b) `α` induces `O_X`-linear maps `α_σ : T_σ → ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄}/ℓ^{R−1}` (the projection of `α` to the `σ̄`-eigen-quotient factors through `T_σ`), and there is a unique `O_X`-linear `β_σ : Hom(ω^{HT}_{R,σ}, 𝔡_σ^{−1}) ⊗ μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω) → T_σ/ℓ^{R−1}` with `⟨β_σ(w), y⟩_σ = w(α_{σ̄}(y))` for all `y ∈ T_{σ̄}` (the transpose of `α_{σ̄}` through the `σ`-component of the Weil pairing);

(c) the reductions modulo `ℓ^{R−1}` form a split short exact sequence of locally free `O_X/ℓ^{R−1}`-modules

`0 → Hom(ω^{HT}_{R,σ}, 𝔡_σ^{−1}) ⊗ μ_{ℓ^R}(Ω) → T_σ → ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄} → 0`,

of ranks `rk Lie_σ`, `N`, `rk Lie_{σ̄}`; consequently the same sequence is an exact sequence of vector bundles on the scheme `X ⊗ ℤ/ℓ^{R−1}`, on the fibre `X_{𝔽_ℓ}` and on the reduced fibre `S = (X_{𝔽_ℓ})_red`, where `T_σ` becomes the trivial bundle `(T/ℓ) ⊗_{O_E/ℓ, σ} O_S` of rank `N` and the twist `μ_ℓ(Ω)`;

(d) hence, for each `σ`, a full flag of `Hom(ω^{HT}_{R,σ}, 𝔡_σ^{−1})` together with a full flag of `ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄}` is, on `S`, a full flag `0 = F_0 ⊂ F_1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ F_N` of a trivial bundle of rank `N`. On the bundle of such pairs of flags over `S` put `x_{σ,i} = c₁(F_{i+1}/F_i)` for `0 ≤ i < N` (first the graded pieces of the first flag, then those of the second), and for a weight `λ = (λ_0, …, λ_{N−1}) ∈ ℤ^N` let `L_λ = ⊗_i (F_{i+1}/F_i)^{⊗(−λ_i)}`, so that `c₁(L_λ) = −Σ_i λ_i x_{σ,i}`; `λ` is dominant for `GL_N` when `λ_0 ≥ λ_1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ λ_{N−1}`. Then the power sums `Σ_i x_{σ,i}^k`, `k ≥ 1`, vanish in the integral Chow ring, and `L_λ` is nef for every dominant `λ`.

The prime `ℓ` may be `2` and may ramify in `E` and in the base. More generally (c) holds modulo `ℓ^m` for every `m` and every `κ` with `κ·ℓ^m ∣ ℓ^R` whose absolute value satisfies `|κ|^{ℓ−1} ≤ |ℓ|` at the height-one primes over `ℓ`; `m = R − 1` is the case `κ = κ₀`. [FormalProof] has the statement for `κ = ζ_ℓ − 1` at odd `ℓ`, where `m = R − 1` is allowed, and for `κ = 4` at `ℓ = 2`, where it gives the modulus `2^{R−2}`; the modulus `2^{R−1}` and the statement for other `κ` rest on the factor of 0.5b (b). For `E = ℚ` (c) is 0.17a (a), and at a prime unramified in `E` it is 0.17a (b).

*Route.* (a) follows from [FormalProof], `Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/HodgeTatePoints/FinrankDualEqOfProjective.lean`
(`PositivityTheory.HtPt.Ge.chart_sequence`).
Work on an affine open `Spec B`, `B` a noetherian normal domain in which `ℓ` is not a unit; by local freeness `ω^{HT}_R`, `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}`, `ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄}` are projective over `B`, and `𝔡_σ` is principal, generated by some `d`. *The maps.* `ℓ^R·ω_G ⊆ ℓ^{R−1}·κ₀·ω_G ⊆ ℓ^{R−1}·ω^{HT}_R` (0.5a (a), 0.5b (b)), so `α` is defined modulo `ℓ^{R−1}·ω^{HT}_R`, and its projection to the `σ̄`-eigen-quotient is `σ`-linear in `x` because `α(ax) = c(a)^*α(x)`. In a basis of `T` over `O_E/ℓ^R` the matrix of `d·⟨·,·⟩_σ` has entries in `B` and is invertible modulo `ℓ^R` (the pairing is perfect, and `d` clears the denominators of the trace-dual basis); `β_σ(w)` is the row `(w(α_{σ̄}(e_j)))_j` times the inverse matrix, unique modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`. Since `ω^{HT}_{R,σ̄}` is spanned by `α_σ(T_σ)` and the projection of `ℓ^R·ω_G`, `α_σ` is onto modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`; the same for `α_{σ̄}`, and projectivity of `ω^{HT}_{R,σ}` then gives `β_σ` a retraction modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`. *Reduction to points.* Identities of projective lattices over `B`, and divisibility by `ℓ^{R−1}` in them, are tested at the height-one primes `𝔮` of `B`; at `𝔮 ∌ ℓ` there is nothing to prove; at `𝔮 ∋ ℓ`, `B_𝔮` is a discrete valuation ring and for a complete algebraic closure `C` of its fraction field with a valuation above `𝔮`, `O_C` is faithfully flat over `B_𝔮`, so the tests are made after base change to `O_C`. There 0.18a applies to the pulled-back semi-abelian scheme (of good reduction or not; the hypothesis of 0.18a (d) holds by NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §3.6): the image of `T ⊗ O_C` is the base change of `ω^{HT}_R` up to `ℓ^R·ω_G`, and its kernel `W` is saturated, `O_E`-stable and equal to its own orthogonal for the Weil pairing (2.4 (b)). *The composite vanishes.* For a functional `f` on the image of `T ⊗ O_C` in the `σ̄`-eigen-quotient, the vector `w` with `e(w, y) = f(α(y)_{σ̄})` lies in `T ⊗ O_C` (perfectness), in the `σ`-eigenspace (adjointness) and in `W` (it is orthogonal to `W`, and `W^⊥ = W`). The `σ`-eigen-sublattice of `T ⊗ O_C` is `𝔡_σ` times its image in `T_σ`; so the transpose of `α_{σ̄}`, which is `β_σ` after division by `d`, has image in the kernel of `α_σ`: `α_σ∘β_σ ≡ 0` modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`. *Ranks.* Over `C` the eigenspaces of `T ⊗ C` have dimension `N`, the pairing puts those at `σ` and `σ̄` in duality, and `W^⊥ = W` makes `W_σ` and `W_{σ̄}` exact annihilators; so the ranks of the two images add up to `N`: (a). *Exactness.* A split injection `i : P₁ → B^N` and a surjection `q : B^N → P₂` modulo `a`, with `q∘i ≡ 0` and `rk P₁ + rk P₂ = N`, are exact in the middle modulo `a`: `(r, q) : B^N → P₁ × P₂` (`r` a retraction) is onto modulo `a` between projective modules of the same rank, hence injective modulo `a`. *Gluing.* On overlaps two liftings of `α` differ by `ℓ^R·ω_G ⊆ ℓ^{R−1}·ω^{HT}_R`, and `β_σ` is unique modulo `ℓ^{R−1}`. (c) on `X ⊗ ℤ/ℓ^{R−1}`, `X_{𝔽_ℓ}` and `S`: a split exact sequence of projective modules remains exact after base change. (d) The pair of flags is a full flag
of the trivial bundle `T_σ` with graded pieces of first Chern classes `x_{σ,0}, …, x_{σ,N−1}`, so the Whitney formula (SchemeAndStackFoundations §5.4) gives
`∏_i(1 + x_{σ,i}) = c(T_σ) = 1` in the integral Chow ring: every elementary symmetric function `e_j` of the `x_{σ,i}`, `j ≥ 1`,
vanishes. Newton's identities `p_k = Σ_{j=1}^{k−1}(−1)^{j−1}e_jp_{k−j} + (−1)^{k−1}k·e_k` (`e_k = 0` for `k > N`) have integer
coefficients, so by induction `p_k(σ) = 0` for every `k ≥ 1`, also where the Chow ring has torsion (the Chern character
would give the identity only with rational coefficients). *Nefness.* `L_λ ≅ ⊗_{k=1}^{N−1} (det F_k)^{⊗(λ_k − λ_{k−1})} ⊗ (det F_N)^{⊗(−λ_{N−1})}`; `det F_N` is trivial, and `(det F_k)^{−1} = ∧^k(F_k^∨)` is a quotient of the trivial bundle `∧^k((O^N)^∨)` (dualise the inclusion `F_k ⊂ O^N`), hence generated by global sections. For dominant `λ` the exponents `λ_{k−1} − λ_k` of the `(det F_k)^{−1}` are `≥ 0`, so `L_λ` is generated by global sections, hence nef. At `ℓ = 2` the same steps apply with `κ₀ = 2` and the modulus `2^{R−1}`, and for a general admissible `κ` with the modulus `ℓ^m`; they are not carried out separately here, and the steps at the points rest on 0.18a.

*Needs:* 0.5a, 0.5b, 0.5c, 0.18a, 2.4; NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §3.6; SchemeAndStackFoundations §5.4.

**Checks.**

The eigen-quotient `T_σ` of `O_E ⊗ B` is `B`, while the eigen-sublattice is `𝔡_σ·B`: with eigen-sublattices in place of eigen-quotients, or without the twist, the statement would be wrong by the different.

The flags are trivial, the bundle of pairs of flags is `S`, `F_1 = Lie^{HT}_R ⊗ O_S ⊗ μ_p(Ω)` and `F_2/F_1 = ω^{HT}_R ⊗ O_S`. The dominant weight `(1, 0)` gives `L_{(1,0)} = F_1^{−1} ≅ ω^{HT}_R ⊗ O_S`, a quotient of the trivial bundle `(T/p) ⊗ O_S`, hence nef. With the opposite sign `c₁ = +Σ_i λ_i x_{σ,i}` the same weight would give `Lie^{HT}_R ⊗ O_S`, which has negative degree on every curve of `S` contracted by `g` to a supersingular point (such curves exist, `𝓘` not being invertible there): on it `ω^{HT}_R = 𝓘O_{X′}·g^*ω` has positive degree, `𝓘O_{X′}` being ample on the fibres of `g` and `g^*ω` trivial on them.

### 0.18a The `p`-divisible group of a semi-abelian scheme over `O_C`

*Origin:* adapted from Grothendieck (1972), SGA 7 I, exposé IX, and Faltings and Chai (1990), chapter III: the finite and toric parts of the torsion of a semi-abelian scheme over a henselian discrete valuation ring, with their orthogonality, and the Raynaud extension, carried to `O_C`, where the finite parts give a `p`-divisible group `𝔊` with the full Tate module `T_pA` and `ω_𝔊 = ω_G`, its Hodge–Tate map and, under a principal polarisation, its Lagrangian kernel. Scholze and Weinstein (2013), Theorem B, give a `p`-divisible group over `O_C` with this Tate module abstractly, not its description by the finite parts nor the equality of differentials.

*Method of the formal proof:* the finite flat groups `𝔊_n` are treated as Hopf orders in the algebra of functions on `T_n`: the functions all of whose translates are regular near the finite open subgroup of `G[p^n]` form a Hopf order induced from that subgroup, the orders of successive levels restrict onto one another, their cotangent spaces are `ω_G/p^n`, and characters and regular pairings lie in them; for (d) the Hodge–Tate map is compared with its transpose through the polarisation, and a submodule of at most half the rank that contains its orthogonal for a perfect alternating form is equal to it.

Let `p` be a prime, `C` a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic `0` with `|p| < 1`, and `G` a semi-abelian scheme over `O_C` whose generic fibre `A` is an abelian variety of dimension `g`; let `r` be the toric rank of the special fibre, `T_n = A[p^n](C)`, `G[p^n]^f` the finite part of the quasi-finite flat separated group scheme `G[p^n]`, `H_n = G[p^n]^f(C)`, and `𝒜_n` the algebra of functions `f : T_n → O_C` such that for every `t ∈ T_n` the function `h ↦ f(t + h)` on `H_n` is the restriction of a regular function on `G[p^n]^f`. Prove:

(a) `G[p^n]^f` is an open and closed subgroup scheme, finite free of order `p^{n(2g−r)}`; `H_n` is the set of points of `T_n` that extend to sections of `G`; `H_n = H_{n+1} ∩ T_n` and `p·H_{n+1} = H_n`; `𝒜_n` is free of rank `p^{2gn}` and a commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebra for `(Δf)(s, t) = f(s + t)`; `𝔊_n = Spec 𝒜_n` is the unique finite flat group scheme over `O_C` with generic fibre `A[p^n]` containing `G[p^n]^f` as an open subgroup scheme; `𝔊_n(O_C) = T_n`, and `𝔊_n/G[p^n]^f` is the constant group `T_n/H_n ≅ (ℤ/p^n)^r`;

(b) `𝔊_{n+1}[p^n] = 𝔊_n`; the `𝔊_n` form a `p`-divisible group `𝔊` of height `2g` and dimension `g` with `T_p𝔊 = T_pA` and `ω_𝔊 = ω_G` (compatibly with `ω_{𝔊_n} = ω_G/p^n`), functorial in `G`, equal to `G[p^∞]` when `G` is an abelian scheme; it is an extension of `(ℚ_p/ℤ_p)^r` by the `p`-divisible group `(G[p^n]^f)_n` of height `2g − r`, which is the `p`-divisible group of the Raynaud extension when `G` comes by base change from a complete discrete valuation ring (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.18);

(c) every character of `T_n` with values in `μ_{p^n}(C)` lies in `𝒜_n`; so `T_p(𝔊^D) = T_p(A^∨)`, the Hodge–Tate map of `𝔊^D` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.4) is the map `HT_G : T_p(A^∨) ⊗ O_C → ω_G` sending a compatible system of characters to the limit of their differentials at the origin, it has the same image as the Hodge–Tate map of the dual of `(G[p^n]^f)_n`, and `κ·ω_G ⊆ HT_G(T_p(A^∨) ⊗ O_C) ⊆ ω_G` for every `κ ∈ O_C` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`;

(d) let `λ` be a principal polarisation of `A` whose Weil pairings, restricted to `H_n × H_n`, are restrictions of regular functions on `G[p^n]^f × G[p^n]^f` for all `n`; this holds when `G` and `λ` come by base change from a henselian discrete valuation ring (NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §§3.3, 3.5, 3.6). Then `e_n ∈ 𝒜_n ⊗ 𝒜_n`, the pairings define a homomorphism `𝔊 → 𝔊^D` inducing `λ` on Tate modules, and the kernel of `HT_G∘λ : T_pA ⊗ O_C → ω_G` is saturated of rank `g` and equal to its own orthogonal for the Weil pairing.

*Route.* (a) `O_C` is a henselian local ring, and `G[p^n]` is quasi-finite, flat, separated and of finite presentation over it, hence quasi-affine (Zariski's main theorem: Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.toNormalization` is then an open immersion into the relative normalisation, which is affine over `O_C`); on an affine open neighbourhood of its finite closed fibre SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.14 splits it into a part finite over `O_C` and a part with empty closed fibre. So `G[p^n]` is the disjoint union of an open and closed subscheme `G[p^n]^f`, finite over `O_C` and containing the closed fibre, and an open subscheme with empty closed fibre. Every connected component of a scheme finite over `O_C` meets the closed fibre, so the group law, the inverse and the unit section map into `G[p^n]^f`, which is a subgroup scheme, finite free over the local ring `O_C`; its closed fibre is `G_s[p^n]`, of order `p^{n(2(g−r)+r)}` for the extension `G_s` of an abelian variety of dimension `g − r` by a torus of rank `r`. A point of `T_n` that extends to a section of `G` extends to one of `G[p^n]` (`G` is separated), which lies in `G[p^n]^f` because `Spec O_C` is connected and its closed point maps to the closed fibre; conversely the `C`-points of the finite `G[p^n]^f` extend. Hence `H_n = H_{n+1} ∩ T_n`, `|H_n| = p^{n(2g−r)}` (the generic fibre is étale over the algebraically closed `C`), and `p : H_{n+1} → H_n` has kernel `H_{n+1} ∩ T_1 = H_1`, so it is onto by counting. Translation by a point of `H_n` is an automorphism of `G[p^n]^f`, so the condition defining `𝒜_n` depends only on the coset of `t`, and `𝒜_n ≅ ∏_{T_n/H_n} O(G[p^n]^f)` is free of rank `p^{nr}·p^{n(2g−r)}`; on each product of cosets `(s, t) ↦ f(s + t)` is the pull-back of a translate of `f` along the group law of `G[p^n]^f`, so `Δ` maps `𝒜_n` to `𝒜_n ⊗ 𝒜_n`. `𝒜_n ⊗ C` is the algebra of all functions on `T_n`, so `𝔊_n` has generic fibre `A[p^n]`, and `G[p^n]^f` is its open and closed subgroup scheme of the coset `H_n`; the quotient is the constant group `T_n/H_n`, of order `p^{nr}`, killed by `p^n`, with `p`-torsion `(T_1 + H_n)/H_n ≅ T_1/H_1` of order `p^r` (if `pt ∈ H_n` then `pt ∈ H_{n−1} = pH_n`), hence `≅ (ℤ/p^n)^r`. Uniqueness: in a finite flat `𝔊′` with generic fibre `A[p^n]`, an open subgroup scheme identified with `G[p^n]^f` is also closed (it is finite); its translates by the points of `T_n = 𝔊′(O_C)` are open and closed, and two of them are equal or disjoint (an open and closed subscheme of the flat `𝔊′` with empty generic fibre is empty), and their union contains the generic fibre, so it is `𝔊′`; hence `O(𝔊′)` and `𝒜_n` are the same algebra of functions on `T_n`. (b) The finite part of `G[p^n]` is `G[p^n] ∩ G[p^{n+1}]^f`, a closed subgroup scheme of `G[p^{n+1}]^f`, and the cosets of `H_{n+1}` meet `T_n` in cosets of `H_n`; so restriction maps `𝒜_{n+1}` onto `𝒜_n`, and `𝔊_n` is a closed subgroup scheme of `𝔊_{n+1}`. On the coset of `t ∈ T_{n+1}` the equation `p^n x = 0` has no solution unless `p^n t ∈ H_{n+1} ∩ T_1 = H_1 = p^nH_{n+1}`, and then the coset contains a point `t′ ∈ T_n` and the solutions are `t′ + G[p^n]^f`; so `𝔊_{n+1}[p^n] = 𝔊_n`. Multiplication by `p` maps `𝔊_{n+1}` into `𝔊_n` with kernel `𝔊_1`, and the induced closed immersion `𝔊_{n+1}/𝔊_1 → 𝔊_n` is an isomorphism, both being finite free of rank `p^{2gn}`: the `𝔊_n` form a `p`-divisible group of height `2g`. As `G[p^n]^f` is an open subgroup scheme of `𝔊_n` containing the unit section, `ω_{𝔊_n} = ω_{G[p^n]} = ω_G/p^n`, so `ω_𝔊 = ω_G` and `𝔊` has dimension `g`. Homomorphisms of semi-abelian schemes preserve the finite parts and the condition defining `𝒜_n`; for an abelian scheme `G[p^n]` is finite and `H_n = T_n`; the same steps for the finite parts give the `p`-divisible group `(G[p^n]^f)_n` of height `2g − r`, with quotient `(ℚ_p/ℤ_p)^r` by (a). Over a complete discrete valuation ring the finite part of `G[p^n]` is the `p^n`-torsion of the Raynaud extension (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.13, 0.18), and finite parts commute with the base change to `O_C`. (c) The restriction of a character `χ` of `T_n` to `H_n` is a `C`-point of the Cartier dual of `G[p^n]^f` (FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory R07.1.7), which is finite over `O_C`, hence an `O_C`-point: a group-like element of `O(G[p^n]^f)`; on a coset `t + H_n`, `h ↦ χ(t + h)` is `χ(t)` times that element. So `𝔊_n^D(O_C)` is the group of all characters of `T_n`, which the Weil pairing identifies with `A^∨[p^n](C)`; the Hodge–Tate map of `𝔊^D` sends `χ` to the class of `χ − 1` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.2, 0.4), which depends only on the restriction of `χ` to the open subgroup `G[p^n]^f`; and the inclusions are 0.5b (a) for `𝔊^D`, as `ω_{(𝔊^D)^D} = ω_G`. (d)
follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/TateMapDualHomBidual.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.dlog_lagrangian`).
On cosets `e_n(s + h, t + h′) = e_n(s, t)·e_n(s, h′)·e_n(h, t)·e_n(h, h′)`: the middle factors are characters of `H_n` in one variable, regular by (c), and the last is regular by hypothesis, so `e_n ∈ 𝒜_n ⊗ 𝒜_n`. Its bimultiplicativity holds on `C`-points, hence in the flat `𝒜_n ⊗ 𝒜_n ⊗ 𝒜_n`, so it is a pairing `𝔊_n × 𝔊_n → μ_{p^n}`, that is a homomorphism `𝔊_n → 𝔊_n^D`, compatible in `n` and inducing `λ` on Tate modules; the form `B_λ` on `T_pA` is the Weil pairing, perfect and alternating, and 2.4 (b) applied to `𝔊`, of height `2g` and dimension `g` by (b), gives the kernel.

*Needs:* HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.2, 0.4, 0.13, 0.18; 0.5b, 2.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.14; NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties §§3.3, 3.5, 3.6; FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory R07.1.7 (Cartier duality).

**Checks.**

### 2.4 The kernel of the Hodge–Tate map over `O_C` at every prime

*Origin:* published: Fargues (2011), Théorème 2, the integral Hodge–Tate sequence of a `p`-divisible group over `O_C` is a complex whose cohomology is killed by `p^{1/(p−1)}`, stated with no hypothesis on `p`, with no printed proof at `p = 2`; (a), the kernel of the Hodge–Tate map as the annihilator of the kernel of the dual map, and (b), its Lagrangian property for a principal quasi-polarisation, are drawn from it.

*Method of the formal proof:* the first assertion of (a) is deduced from a Dieudonné module over `A_inf` (as in 0.5g), finite free of rank the height, with a Frobenius, its base change to `O_C` carrying the Hodge filtration, obtained by splitting the group into its constant part, its multiplicative part and a part that is connected with connected dual and, for the last, taking on the Tate module the periods of a basis of quasi-logarithms lifted to the frame `A_inf[X]/(pX − ([ε] − 1), X²)`, `ε` a compatible system of primitive `p`-power roots of unity, in which `x^p ∈ p·ker θ` for every `x ∈ ker θ`; from the resulting sandwich, `x` lies in the kernel of the Hodge–Tate map exactly when a nonzero multiple of the Cartier pairing `⟨x, ·⟩` factors through the Hodge–Tate map of the dual. (b) compares the Hodge–Tate map with its transpose through `λ` and counts ranks. The route written here for (a) differs: it argues from the vanishing of the composite of the two Hodge–Tate maps, the cokernel bound of 0.5b and a count of dimensions, then by saturation. Fargues' proof (2011) passes through Faltings's integral comparison between the Tate module and crystalline periods.

This target extends HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §2.4. For a `p`-divisible group `G` over `O_C`, that target states that `0 → Lie(G) ⊗ C(1) → T_pG ⊗ C → ω_{G^D} ⊗ C → 0` is exact, the second map being `α_G ⊗ C` and the first the `C`-linearisation of the transpose of `α_{G^D}` through the Cartier pairing `T_pG × T_p(G^D) → ℤ_p(1)`, and that integrally the composite of the two maps is zero. Prove, at every prime `p`, `p = 2` included:

(a) for every `p`-divisible group `G` over `O_C`, the kernel of `α_G ⊗ 1 : T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}` is the annihilator of the kernel of `α_{G^D} ⊗ 1 : T_p(G^D) ⊗ O_C → ω_G` for the `O_C`-linear extension of the Cartier pairing `T_pG × T_p(G^D) → ℤ_p(1)`; it is a saturated submodule of rank `dim G`, and over `C` it is the image of the first map of the sequence. No polarisation is involved: the kernel and its annihilator lie in the Tate modules of `G` and of `G^D`;

(b) (*the polarised case*) let moreover `ht G = 2·dim G`, and let `λ : G → G^D` be a homomorphism inducing an isomorphism on Tate modules for which the form `B_λ(x, y) = ⟨y, λ(x)⟩` on `T_pG` is alternating (for instance the homomorphism defined by a principal polarisation of an abelian scheme, or that of 0.18a (d); in both `ht G = 2g` and `dim G = g`). Then for `f = (α_{G^D} ⊗ 1)∘λ : T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_G`: `κ·ω_G ⊆ im f` for every `κ` with `|κ|^{p−1} ≤ |p|`, and `ker f` is a saturated submodule of rank `dim G` equal to its own orthogonal for `B_λ` (a Lagrangian). The condition on the height does not follow from the others: over `O_C` a homomorphism that is bijective on Tate modules need not be an isomorphism. For `G = (ℚ_p/ℤ_p)²` and `λ : G → G^D = μ_{p^∞}²` with matrix `[[0, ε], [−ε, 0]]`, `ε` a generator of `ℤ_p(1)`, the form `B_λ` is alternating and perfect, `ω_G = 0`, and `ker f` is all of `T_pG ⊗ O_C`.
For the principal polarisation of an abelian scheme `A` over `O_C`, `ker f ⊗ C` is the Hodge–Tate filtration
`Lie A ⊗ C(1) ⊂ T_pA ⊗ C`, which HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §2.5 states to be Lagrangian for the Weil pairing; (b) is the
integral form of that statement.

*Route.* The first assertion of (a), the kernel as the annihilator, is [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/PublishedTheorems/Algebra₂/FarguesExact.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.FarguesExact`),
`Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/PrismaticDieudonne/PrismaticDieudonneHodge.lean`
(`UpperTheory.Lit.farguesExact`); the second, that the kernel is saturated of rank `dim G` and over `C` the image of the
first map, is not part of that statement.
*The composite is zero.* Integrally `α_G∘(α_{G^D})^∨ = 0`, and the sequence is exact over `C`: HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §2.4, which states both with no restriction on `p`. *Exactness over `C`* also follows from the vanishing of the composite: by 0.5b (a) applied to `G` and to `G^D`, `α_G ⊗ C` and `α_{G^D} ⊗ C` are onto; so the transpose of `α_{G^D}` has rank `dim G`, the kernel of `α_G ⊗ C` has dimension `ht G − dim G^D = dim G` (HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §0.25), and a complex with these dimensions is exact. *The kernel.* Over `C` the kernel of `α_G` is the image of the transpose of `α_{G^D}`, which is the annihilator of the kernel of `α_{G^D}`; integrally, `ker(α_G ⊗ 1)` and the annihilator of `ker(α_{G^D} ⊗ 1)` are both saturated in `T_pG ⊗ O_C`, being kernels of maps to torsion-free modules, so they are equal.

(b) is [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicSpectrum/Proofs/AlgebraProofs/TateMapDualHomBidual.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.CompanionTheory.Ar.HT.dlog_lagrangian`).
The cokernel: 0.5b (a) for `G^D`, `λ` being bijective on Tate modules. By (a), `ker(α_G ⊗ 1) = (ker f)^⊥` for `B_λ`. Naturality of the Hodge–Tate map for `λ` and the antisymmetry of `B_λ` give `λ^*∘(α_G ⊗ 1) = −f`, so `ker(α_G ⊗ 1) ⊆ ker f`, that is `(ker f)^⊥ ⊆ ker f`. As `κ·ω_G ⊆ im f`, `rk ker f ≤ ht G − dim G = ht G/2`; and a submodule of rank at most half the rank that contains its orthogonal for a nondegenerate alternating form is equal to it.

*Needs:* 0.5b; HodgeTateAndCanonicalSubgroups §§0.4, 0.25, 2.4.

## Layer 3: Logarithms of Lubin–Tate laws in several variables

### 3.1 Sharp radius for inverses of twisted systems of logarithms

*Origin:* adapted from Lang (1978), section 8.6, Lemma 4, as used by Schneider and Teitelbaum (2001), section 3: the logarithm of a Lubin–Tate group with residue cardinality `q` over an unramified base is an isomorphism of each disc of radius below `p^{−1/(q−1)}` onto itself; here twisted systems in several variables, permuted by a self-map `ς`, with the coefficient bound for their inverse, for every integer `q ≥ 2`.

*Method of the formal proof:* in the case `q = p²`: the inverse family is built by successive approximation in the degree filtration, and it is a left inverse because substitution into a system that is the identity modulo degree two is injective; the bound is an induction on the degree, in which `λ_x(g) = T_x` expresses a coefficient of `g_x` through coefficients of lower degree of the powers `g_y^{q^e}/p^e`, with `(q^e − 1)/(q − 1) ≥ e`.

Here `K` may be any field of characteristic `0` with a non-archimedean absolute value normalised by `|p| = p^{−1}` (completeness is
not used). Let `S` be a finite set with a self-map `ς : S → S` and `q ≥ 2` an integer (in the applications `q = p^h`). The
*twisted system* of logarithms is
`λ_x(T) := Σ_{e ≥ 0} T_{ς^e(x)}^{q^e}/p^e` (`x ∈ S`), in the variables `T_y`, `y ∈ S`. Prove: there is a unique family
`g = (g_x)_{x∈S}` of series without constant term with `λ_x(g) = T_x` for all `x`, it is also a left inverse, `g_x(λ) = T_x`, and
`v_p(coeff_n g_x) ≥ −(|n| − 1)/(q − 1)` for every multi-index `n ≠ 0`. Equivalently, `g_x(p^{1/(q−1)}·T)/p^{1/(q−1)}` is
integral after adjoining `p^{1/(q−1)}`. `Suggested.lean` states this target as `exists_twistedLog_inverse_norm_coeff_le`, with the twisted system
`twistedLog`. For `q = p²` this is the statement of [FormalProof], which every use in this family takes; for the other `q`
the route below applies unchanged. At `q = p²` the twisted system is [FormalProof],
`Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GenLogInverse.lean` (`Formalism.DworkPowerSeriesWeights.genLog`).

*Route.* The linear part of `λ` is the identity, so `g` exists and is unique and two-sided by the formal inverse
function theorem. From `λ_x(g) = T_x`: `g_x = T_x − Σ_{e≥1}p^{−e}g_{ς^e x}^{q^e}`. Induction on the degree `N`: a coefficient
of degree `N` of `g_{ς^e x}^{q^e}` is a sum of products of `q^e` coefficients of degrees `m_i` with `Σm_i = N`, of valuation
`≥ −Σ(m_i − 1)/(q − 1) = −(N − q^e)/(q − 1)`; after division by `p^e` it is `≥ −(N − 1)/(q − 1) + (q^e − 1)/(q − 1) − e`,
and `(q^e − 1)/(q − 1) ≥ e` for every integer `q ≥ 2`. The pattern `ς` plays no role.

**Checks.**
- `S = {x}`, `ς = id`, `h = 2`: the inverse of `Σ_eT^{p^{2e}}/p^e` has coefficient `−1/p` at `T^{p²}`, of valuation
  `−1 = −(p² − 1)/(p² − 1)`: the bound is attained.
- `h = 1`, `ς = id`: `λ = Σ_eT^{p^e}/p^e`, the logarithm of the Lubin–Tate group of `p` over `ℤ_p`; the bound
  `−(n − 1)/(p − 1)` is the classical radius `|x| < p^{−1/(p−1)}` of the exponential. For an integral commutative formal group of
  dimension `g` over a finite extension of `ℚ_p` with ramification index `e`, HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §1.16 gives
  that the logarithm and its inverse identify `(𝔪^r)^g` with its additive group for every integer `r > e/(p − 1)`; for twisted
  systems the bound of 3.1 gives the convergence of the inverse on `|x| < p^{−1/(q−1)}`.

### 3.2 The functional-equation lemma in several variables

*Origin:* published: Hazewinkel (1978), chapter I, section 2, and chapter II, section 10, the functional-equation lemma, after Honda (1970); (a) is its case for the type `p^{−1}σ²`, and (b) and (c), the module over the integral `c` with `σ²c = c` and the combinations of twisted letter logarithms, are particular to this proof.

(b) is the linearity of the condition; (c), for odd `p`, is the shift of the letters by two.

*Corollary of:* LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.2a (Hazewinkel's functional-equation lemma in several variables), for (a), which is its clause (ii) for `f = ℓ`, as in its first Check; not for (b) and (c).

with `g(0) = 0` is *of type `p^{−1}σ²`* if `g − p^{−1}g^{σ²}(X^{p²})` is integral (`g^{σ²}` the series with `σ²` applied to the

### 3.3 Dwork's lemma in several variables, and the relation `≃`

*Origin:* adapted from Krattenthaler and Rivoal (2011), Lemma 1, the Dieudonné–Dwork lemma in several variables over `ℤ_p`, which gives the case `K = ℚ_p`, `σ = id` of the first statement, and from the lemma of Dieudonné and Dwork in one variable, as in Lang (1980), chapter 14: here several variables with a Frobenius lift `σ`; the general statement, for a Frobenius lift on the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean field of characteristic zero and residue characteristic `p`, is LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.2b, of which the first statement is the case of a complete field; the relation `≃`, with its compatibility with addition, its stability under integral substitution and under division by an integer prime to `p`, is particular to this proof.

*Method of the formal proof:* with `Φ` the substitution `X ↦ X^p` combined with `σ` on the coefficients, `Φ(exp Ψ) = (exp Ψ)^p·exp(ΦΨ − pΨ)`; if `F = exp Ψ` is integral, `Φ(F) ≡ F^p (mod p)` makes the last factor congruent to `1`, which bounds `ΦΨ − pΨ`, and conversely a series `F` with constant term `1` and `Φ(F)/F^p ≡ 1 (mod p)` is integral, by induction on the degree; `h/N ≃ 0` for `p ∤ N` follows from the criterion, which is linear in `Ψ`, `1/N` being a unit fixed by `σ`. For the last clause the route written here differs: it takes `exp(h/N)` as the `N`-th root of `exp(h)` with constant term `1` and uses the integrality of the binomial series `(1 + y)^{1/N}`.

*Corollary of:* LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.2b (the Dieudonné–Dwork lemma in several variables with a Frobenius lift), for the first statement, which is its exponential form for complete `K`, and for the last clause, which follows from the first statement because the criterion is linear in `Ψ` and `1/N` is a unit of `O` fixed by `σ`; not for the other properties of `≃`.

Let `K`, `O`, `σ` be as in 3.2 (`p` may be any prime here). For `Ψ` in finitely many variables with `Ψ(0) = 0` prove:
`exp(Ψ)` is integral iff `Ψ^σ(X^p) − pΨ ∈ p·O[[X]]`. For series `f, g` without constant term write `f ≃ g` if `exp(f − g)`
is integral. Prove: `≃` is an equivalence relation, compatible with addition, and stable under substitution of integral
series without constant term; if `h ≃ 0` and `p ∤ N`, then `h/N ≃ 0`.

*Route.* The statement is [FormalProof],
`Formalism/ThetaLogCoordinates/UniformThetaSubstitution/CyclicPowerClassOfExpEquiv.lean`
(`Formalism.InvariantFactorPaths.G7.DworkMv`), which does not assume `K` complete.
Dwork's lemma: `exp(Ψ)^σ(X^p)/exp(Ψ)^p = exp(Ψ^σ(X^p) − pΨ)`, and a series `F = 1 + (higher)` is integral iff the quotient
`F^σ(X^p)/F^p` of its Frobenius twist by its `p`-th power is `≡ 1 (mod p)` (Dieudonné–Dwork; the one-variable proof works coefficient by
coefficient in several variables). `≃`: `exp` turns sums into products of integral series, and an integral series without
constant term may be substituted in an integral series. For `p ∤ N`: `exp(h/N)` is the unique `N`-th root of `exp(h)` with
constant term `1`, and the binomial series `(1 + y)^{1/N}` is integral.

**Checks.**
- `Ψ = X`: `Ψ^σ(X^p) − pΨ = X^p − pX ∉ pO[[X]]`, and `exp(X)` is not integral.
- `Ψ = Σ_eX^{p^e}/p^e` (the Artin–Hasse exponent): `Ψ(X^p) − pΨ = −pX`, and `exp(Ψ)` is integral.

*Needs:* LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.1; Mathlib `PowerSeries.exp`, `MvPowerSeries`.

### 3.4 Quasi-logarithms of the unramified Lubin–Tate laws

*Origin:* adapted from Gross and Hopkins (1994), Proposition 13.8, as quoted by Boyarchenko and Weinstein (2016), Lemma 2.5: over the integers `W(𝔽̄_p)` of the completion of the maximal unramified extension of `ℚ_p`, the classes of `ℓ_s` and `g₀` form a basis of the quasi-logarithms of `F_s` with integral derivative modulo integral series, which gives (a) and the case of (b) of series with integral derivative; here (b) over `O`, with `β ∈ K` and no hypothesis on the derivative, is particular to this proof, and the two-variable clause (c), (d) and (e) are consequences stated in the form this proof uses: (c) is derived from the basis, (d) is an expansion by Hasse derivatives and (e) a computation with `ℓ_s`; the induction on the degree in (b) is that of Lazard (1955), Lemme 3.

*Method of the formal proof:* for the sign `s = 1`: (d) expands `Φ(φ + pY·e_x) − Φ(φ)` monomial by monomial and bounds each term by `|p|`; (a) follows from (d) and `F_s(x^p, y^p) ≡ F_s(x, y)^p` modulo `p`; (b) is an induction on the degree in which a coefficient of the defect controls the `n`-th coefficient of `f` through a binomial coefficient, prime to `p` unless `n` is a power of `p`, the non-integral parts at the degrees `p^{2j}` and `p^{2j+1}` being removed by multiples of `ℓ_s` and `g₀` that converge `p`-adically; (c) applies (b) to the coefficient slices in each variable and compares the two expansions. For (c) the route written here differs: it applies (b) in each variable to the derivative and integrates.

*Corollary of:* LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3a (quasi-logarithms and the Dieudonné module; its clause (ii), the basis `ℓ_s`, `g₀` of the quasi-logarithms of `F_s` with integral derivative over `W(𝔽̄_p)`), for (a). That clause also gives the case of (b) over `W(𝔽̄_p)` of series with integral derivative, for which the condition `f([a](X)) − a·f(X)` integral, `a ∈ ℤ_p`, of that section follows from the integrality of `δ_F f`; (b) as stated here is the more general statement.

Let `p` be odd, `K` as in 3.2 and containing `W(𝔽_{p²})`, `s ∈ {1, −1}` ([FormalProof] has the sign `s = 1`; the sign `−1` follows by the substitution of the second Check, which carries `ℓ_{−1}`, its law and `g₀` to unit multiples of those of `ℓ_1`), `ℓ_s` the logarithm of LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3, `F_s` its law,
`g₀ := ℓ_s(T^p)/p`, and for a series `f` without constant term `δ_Ff(x, y) := f(F_s(x, y)) − f(x) − f(y)`. Prove:
(a) `δ_Fg₀` is integral (the Frobenius twist of the logarithm is a quasi-logarithm);
(b) if `f(0) = 0` and `δ_Ff` is integral, then `f = α·g₀ + β·ℓ_s + R` with `α ∈ O`, `β ∈ K` and `R` integral;
(c) if `H ∈ xyK[[x, y]]` and `H(F_s(x₀, x₁), y) − H(x₀, y) − H(x₁, y)` and the same in the second variable are integral, then
`H = β·ℓ_s(x)ℓ_s(y) + R` with `β ∈ K` and `R` integral;
(d) if `∂_xΦ` is integral, `φ` is an integral substitution and `Y` is integral without constant term, then
`Φ(φ + pY·e_x) − Φ(φ) ∈ p·O[[…]]`;
(e) for `π` in an extension of `K` with `π^{p²−1} = p`: `ℓ_s(πt)/π ≡ t + s·t^{p²} (mod p)`, and its inverse is integral with `≡ Σ_{l≥0}(−s)^lt^{p^{2l}} (mod p)`.

*Route.* (a) `p·δ_Fg₀ = ℓ_s(F(x, y)^p) − ℓ_s(F(x^p, y^p))` and `F(x, y)^p ≡ F(x^p, y^p) (mod p)`, with (d) applied to `ℓ_s`, whose
derivative is integral. (b) Induction on the order with corrections: at a degree that is not a power of `p` some binomial
coefficient prime to `p` shows that the term is integral; at the degrees `p^{2i}` and `p^{2i+1}` the non-integral parts are
those of `ℓ_s` and `g₀`; completeness gives the limit (the rank of the quotient module is `1` for height two: the quotient
of the series with integral `δ_F` by `K·ℓ_s + (integral)` is free of rank `1` over `O`, generated by `g₀`). (c) apply (b) in each
variable to the derivative and integrate. (d) Hasse derivatives: `Φ(φ + pY) − Φ(φ) = Σ_{k≥1}p^kY^k(∂^{(k)}Φ)(φ)` and
`p^k∂^{(k)} = (p^k/k!)∂^k` with `v_p(p^k/k!) ≥ 1`. (e) `ℓ_s(πt) = Σ_jπ^{p^{2j}}t^{p^{2j}}/(sp)^j`, and
`v_p(π^{p^{2j}−1}/p^j) = (p^{2j} − 1)/(p² − 1) − j`, which is `0` for `j = 0, 1` and `≥ 1` for `j ≥ 2`; the inverse telescopes
modulo `p` because `t ↦ t^{p²}` is additive modulo `p`.

**Checks.**
- (b) is sharp in both directions: `g₀ = T^p/p + ⋯` has `δ_Fg₀` integral but is not of the form `β·ℓ_s + (integral)` (its
  coefficient of `T^p` is `1/p`, while `ℓ_s` has no term of degree `p`), so `g₀` cannot be dropped; and every `β·ℓ_s`,
  `β ∈ K`, has `δ_F = 0`, so the series with integral `δ_F` modulo integral series do not form a finitely generated
  `O`-module (the statement "free of rank `2` modulo integral series" is false); modulo `K·ℓ_s + (integral)` they are free
  of rank `1`.
- (e) for `s = −1` follows from `s = 1` over `W(𝔽_{p⁴})` by `t ↦ εt` with `ε^{p²−1} = −1`, since `ℓ_{−1}(εY) = εℓ_1(Y)`
  (`ε^{p^{2j}} = (−1)^jε`).

*Needs:* 3.3; LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §§4.2, 4.3; Mathlib `PowerSeries`, `Nat.choose` (Lucas), Hasse derivatives.

### 3.5 The letter group: products of Lubin–Tate groups in twisted logarithmic coordinates

*Origin:* adapted from Hazewinkel (1978), chapter II, section 10, the functional-equation lemma in several variables, after Honda (1970): the law of a system of logarithms of functional-equation type is integral, and so is the comparison of two systems of the same type; here an integral change of variables, with the support condition, from the twisted logarithmic coordinates `λ_s`, `s ∈ ℤ/d`, to the product of `d/2` and `d/2` copies of the height-two Lubin–Tate law of `ℓ`, through trace-dual bases of `W(𝔽_{p^d})` over `W(𝔽_{p²})`.

*Method of the formal proof:* the letter logarithms `λ_s` and the system `z_s` of the right-hand sides (`Σ_rσ^s(ω_r)ℓ(X_r)` for `s` even, `Σ_rσ^s(ω*_r)ℓ(Y_r)` for `s` odd) satisfy the same functional equation, with the shift `s ↦ s − 2`, so the functional-equation lemma in vector form gives an integral system `T` with `λ_s(T) = z_s`; truncating `T_s` to the variables of the parity of `s` keeps this identity and gives the support condition; the inverse system `Ξ` is `ℓ^{−1}` of the even and odd combinations of the `λ_s` with the dual basis, integral by 3.2 (a) and (c), and the two systems are shown to be mutually inverse after applying the logarithms, by trace duality. The route written here differs: it takes the integral series `X_r(T)`, `Y_r(T)` from 3.2 (c) and (a) and obtains `T` as their inverse by the inverse function theorem over `O`.

*Corollary of:* LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.2a (Hazewinkel's functional-equation lemma in several variables: the law of a tuple of logarithms of functional-equation type, and the comparison of two such tuples, are integral), for the integrality of the law with logarithms `(λ_s)_s` and of the change of variables `T`.

Let `p` be odd, `d ≥ 2` even, `K ⊃ W(𝔽_{p^d})` with `σ` the Frobenius, `ℓ := ℓ_1` of LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3, and `ω_r, ω*_r` (`r < d/2`) integral
elements fixed by `σ^d` with `Σ_{s even}σ^s(ω_rω*_{r′}) = δ_{rr′}` (bases of `W(𝔽_{p^d})` over `W(𝔽_{p²})` dual for the trace).
Let `λ_s(T) := Σ_{e≥0}T_{s−2e}^{p^{2e}}/p^e` (`s ∈ ℤ/d`). Prove: there are integral systems `T = (T_s(X, Y))_{s∈ℤ/d}` and
`Ξ = (Ξ_r)` (its inverse), without constant terms, with
`λ_s(T(X, Y)) = Σ_rσ^s(ω_r)ℓ(X_r)` for `s` even and `= Σ_rσ^s(ω*_r)ℓ(Y_r)` for `s` odd, the linear part of `T_s` being
`Σ_rσ^s(ω_r)X_r`, resp. `Σ_rσ^s(ω*_r)Y_r`, and `T_s` involving only the variables `X` for `s` even and only the variables
`Y` for `s` odd (the *support condition*). Consequently the formal group law with logarithms `(λ_s)_s` is integral and
isomorphic, by an integral change of variables with that linear part, to the product of `d/2` copies of the law of `ℓ`
(the even letters) and `d/2` copies (the odd letters).

*Route.* The statement is [FormalProof], `Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/GaussSumParity/StatementA.lean`
(`Formalism.L2.Th1.D.BMinus1`).
By 3.2 (c) the combinations `Σ_{s even}σ^s(ω*_r)λ_s` and `Σ_{s odd}σ^s(ω_r)λ_s` (in the variables `T`) are of type
`p^{−1}σ²`, so by 3.2 (a) they are `ℓ` of integral series `X_r(T)`, `Y_r(T)`; trace duality inverts the linear parts; the
support condition holds because the even (odd) combinations involve only the even (odd) letters, which the shift
`s ↦ s − 2` preserves. The integral inverse comes from the inverse function theorem over `O`.

**Check.** `d = 2`: one even and one odd letter, `ω = ω* = 1`, `λ_0 = Σ_eT_0^{p^{2e}}/p^e`, `λ_1 = Σ_eT_1^{p^{2e}}/p^e`, and
`T_0 = X`, `T_1 = Y`: the product of the law of `ℓ` with itself.

*Needs:* 3.2; LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §§4.2, 4.3; Mathlib `Algebra.traceForm`, `WittVector`.

### 3.6 Sums of quasi-quadratic series under unitary substitutions; hyperbolic frames

*Origin:* adapted from the theorem of the cube for formal groups (Mumford (1969), biextensions of formal groups; Breen (1983), cubical structures): sums of a series `φ` whose defect `β₀` is biadditive and Hermitian up to `≃` are invariant under unitary substitutions (a) and reduce to the pairings `β₀(X_e, Y_e)` on a hyperbolic frame (c), up to series with integral exponential.

*Method of the formal proof:* the points of the formal group form a module over the scalars fixed by `σ²`, and `φ` induces a map from it to the group of all series modulo those with integral exponential; by (C), (N) and (o) this map has a biadditive Hermitian polar map and satisfies `φ(αu) = ασ(α)·φ(u)`, and (a) and (c) are the expansion of such a map along the columns of a unitary matrix and along a hyperbolic pair. The computation is the usual one for Hermitian forms, organised as an abstract lemma applied in a quotient where `≃` is equality.

Let `p` be odd, `K ⊃ W(𝔽_{p²})` complete and absolutely unramified, `σ` the Frobenius, `F` the law of `ℓ_s` (LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3) with
`[α] := ℓ_s^{−1}(α·ℓ_s)` for integral `α` with `σ²α = α`, and `φ` a series without constant term such that
`β₀(x, y) := φ(x +_F y) − φ(x) − φ(y)` satisfies: (C) `β₀(x₀ +_F x₁, y) ≃ β₀(x₀, y) + β₀(x₁, y)` and the same in `y` (3.3); (N)
`φ([α]x) ≃ ασ(α)·φ(x)` for every unit `α` with `σ²α = α`. For a matrix `δ = (δ_{ij})` of integral elements fixed by `σ²` write
`δ^* := σ(δ)^T` and `(δ·u)_i := Σ^F_j[δ_{ij}]u_j` (sums for `F`). Prove: (o) `β₀([α]x, y) ≃ β₀(x, [σα]y)` and `φ([α]x) ≃ ασ(α)·φ(x)` for
every integral `α` with `σ²α = α` (not only units); and
(a) *Unitary invariance.* If `δ^*δ = 1`, then `Σ_iφ((δ·u)_i) ≃ Σ_jφ(u_j)`.
(b) *Isotropic families.* If the columns `f_e` of an integral matrix fixed by `σ²` satisfy `Σ_iσ(f_{e,i})f_{e′,i} = 0` for all `e, e′`,
then `Σ_iφ(Σ^F_e[f_{e,i}]X_e) ≃ 0`. (b) is not used in this family.
(c) *Hyperbolic frames.* A *hyperbolic pair* is a pair of finite families `f = (f_{e,i})`, `f′ = (f′_{e,i})` of integral elements fixed
by `σ²` with `Σ_iσ(f_{e,i})f_{e′,i} = 0`, `Σ_iσ(f′_{e,i})f′_{e′,i} = 0` and `Σ_iσ(f_{e,i})f′_{e′,i} = δ_{ee′}`. With
`t_i := Σ_e([f_{e,i}]X_e +_F [f′_{e,i}]Y_e)`, `Σ_iφ(t_i) ≃ Σ_eβ₀(X_e, Y_e)`.

*Route.* The relation `≃` is stable under integral substitutions, under multiplication by elements of `ℤ_p` (`(1 + X)^c` is
integral for `c ∈ ℤ_p`), under halving (`p` odd: 3.3 with `N = 2`) and under coefficientwise limits. (i) `φ(Σ^F_ix_i) ≃ Σ_iφ(x_i) + Σ_{i<j}β₀(x_i, x_j)`
by induction from the definition of `β₀` and (C). (ii) `β₀(x, [c]y) ≃ c·β₀(x, y)` for `c ∈ ℤ_p` (integers by (C),
then limits). (o) follows from [FormalProof],
`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/SecondCoefficientNorms/IsotropicVanish.lean` (`Formalism.L2.Th1.C.NormAdjAll`).
Applying (N) to `x +_F y`: `β₀([α]x, [α]y) ≃ ασ(α)β₀(x, y) ≃ β₀(x, [σ(α)α]y)` by (ii), and substituting `[α]^{−1}y` gives
`β₀([α]x, y) ≃ β₀(x, [σα]y)` for units `α`, hence for every integral `α` fixed by `σ²` (a sum of two units, and (C)). With `α = 2`,
(N) gives `φ([2]x) ≃ 4φ(x)`, i.e. `β₀(x, x) ≃ 2φ(x)`; so `2φ([a]x) ≃ β₀([a]x, [a]x) ≃ β₀(x, [σ(a)a]x) ≃ 2aσ(a)φ(x)`, and halving gives
`φ([a]x) ≃ aσ(a)φ(x)` for every integral `a` fixed by `σ²`. (a) follows from [FormalProof],
`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/SecondCoefficientNorms/IsotropicVanish.lean` (`Formalism.L2.Th1.C.UnitaryInv`).
By (i), `Σ_iφ((δ·u)_i)` is `≃` to the sum of the pure terms
`Σ_iφ([δ_{ij}]u_j) ≃ (Σ_iσ(δ_{ij})δ_{ij})φ(u_j)` and the cross terms `Σ_iβ₀([δ_{ij}]u_j, [δ_{ij′}]u_{j′}) ≃ β₀(u_j, [Σ_iσ(δ_{ij})δ_{ij′}]u_{j′})`
(by (o)); `δ^*δ = 1` leaves `Σ_jφ(u_j)`. (c) follows from [FormalProof],
`Formalism/LocalToGlobal/TorsionIntegrality/SecondCoefficientNorms/IsotropicVanish.lean` (`Formalism.L2.Th1.C.HyperbolicSum`).
(b) and (c) are the same computation with the Gram relations of the family: an isotropic
family gives `≃ 0`, a hyperbolic pair `Σ_eβ₀(X_e, Y_e)`.

**Check.** One variable, `δ = (ζ)` with `ζσ(ζ) = 1` (`ζ` a root of unity of order dividing `p + 1`): (a) reads `φ([ζ]u) ≃ φ(u)`, which
is (N) for `α = ζ`. The hypothesis "`p` odd" is used: halving is not available at `p = 2`.

*Needs:* 3.3, 3.4; LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts §4.3.

- §0.1: the conormal module `ω_H = I/I²` of a finite locally free commutative group scheme `H`, with its functoriality and base change.
- §0.2: the finite-level Hodge–Tate map `α_H : H(S) → ω_{H^D}`, additive and natural in `H`, sending a point to the class of `g − 1` for the corresponding group-like element `g` of the Hopf algebra of `H^D`.
- §0.3: functoriality, endomorphisms and polarisations of `α`.
- §0.4: the Hodge–Tate map `α_G` of a `p`-divisible group over `O_C`, with `α_G` modulo `p^n` equal to `α_{G[p^n]}`, and its linearisation `α_G ⊗ 1 : T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}`.
- §0.5: for a `p`-divisible group over `O_C` the complex `Lie(G) ⊗ O_C(1) → T_pG ⊗ O_C → ω_{G^D}` has zero composite and is exact over `C`, at every prime; for odd `p` its cohomology is killed by every `a` with `v(a) ≥ 1/(p − 1)`, also for finite flat `H` (the general theorem of which 0.5f is, for odd `p`, a corollary).
- §§0.6, 0.7: the degree `deg G = deg ω_G` of a finite flat group scheme over `O_C`, and `deg G[p^n] = n·dim G` (0.5h).
- §0.13: the finite part of `G[p^n]` on degeneration charts.
- §0.17: the finite-level Hodge–Tate map over the toroidal boundary of the Siegel models of ShimuraCompactifications §7.19.
- §0.18: the Raynaud extension `G̃` of an abelian variety over a complete discretely valued field, the height `2g − r` of `G̃[p^∞]` and the exact sequence `0 → T_pG̃ → T_pA → Y ⊗ ℤ_p → 0`.
- §0.20: the different of the Hopf algebra of a generically étale finite flat group scheme is the pull-back of `Fitt₀ ω_G`, and
  its norm is the trace discriminant (0.5h).
- §0.24: the degree of a finitely presented torsion module over a rank-one valuation ring (0.5h).
- §0.25: for a `p`-divisible group `G` over `O_C`, `ω_{G[p^n]} ≅ ω_G/p^n` and `dim G + dim G^D = ht G` (0.5b, 2.4).
- §2.4: the exact Hodge–Tate sequence of a `p`-divisible group over `O_C` after inverting `p`, and the vanishing of the integral composite, at every prime (the route of 2.4 (a)).
- §2.5: the Hodge–Tate sequence of an abelian variety over `C`, whose filtration is Lagrangian for the Weil pairing of a principal polarisation (2.4 (b); the published form named in the labels of 0.17a and 0.17b).
- §§5.5–5.7: named in Scope; §5.5, the modified Hodge bundle `ω^mod` for `p ≥ 3`, also in the comparison of 0.5c and as the published form named in the labels of 0.5a, 0.5c, 0.17a and 0.17b.

### From TauCetiRoadmap.LubinTateFormalModulesAndQuasiCanonicalLifts

- §4.1: Honda type operators, used as notation.
- §4.2: Honda's functional-equation integrality for normalised logarithms in one variable, with the criterion that `ℓ_v^{−1}(C·ℓ_u)` is integral if and only if `vC = tu`, which gives the integrality of `[c] = ℓ^{−1}(c·ℓ)` for `σ²c = c`.
- §4.2a: Hazewinkel's functional-equation lemma in several variables, at every prime: for a tuple `f` of functional-equation type the law `f^{−1}(f(X) + f(Y))` is integral, and so is `f^{−1}(f̄)` for every tuple `f̄` of the same type (the general theorem of which 3.2 (a) and the integrality in 3.5 are corollaries).
- §4.2b: the Dieudonné–Dwork lemma in several variables with a Frobenius lift on the valuation ring of a nonarchimedean field of characteristic zero and residue characteristic `p` (the general theorem of which two clauses of 3.3 are corollaries: its first statement, which is the case of a complete field, and its last clause, `h/N ≃ 0` for `p ∤ N`).
- §4.3: the unramified Honda–Lubin–Tate comparison over `W(𝔽_{p²})`, with the logarithms `ℓ_s = Σ_j (sp)^{−j}X^{p^{2j}}`, `s = ±1`.
- §4.3a: quasi-logarithms and the Dieudonné module; its clause (ii), the basis `ℓ_s`, `g₀` of the quasi-logarithms of `F_s` with integral derivative over `W(𝔽̄_p)` (the general theorem of which 3.4 (a) and the case of 3.4 (b) over `W(𝔽̄_p)` of series with integral derivative are corollaries).
- §0.3: named in Scope only.
