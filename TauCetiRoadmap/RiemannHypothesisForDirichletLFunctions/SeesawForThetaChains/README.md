# Roadmap: the seesaw for theta chains

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the seesaw of the chains of theta lifts between definite unitary groups of [ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups](../ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups/README.md): the identity that turns the top period of a chain, a pairing of a lifted column form with a form on the top definite group, into a Bessel period at the bottom of the row tower, and then into a pairing with a Bessel–Poincaré series. Its objects are unitary groups of Hermitian spaces over a CM extension `E/F` of number fields, their automorphic forms, the Weil representations and theta kernels of unitary dual pairs, and Bessel and Fourier–Jacobi periods. The unfolding statements 6.2a–6.2d hold for general unitary pairs; every other statement is made for the data of the chain: a word `u`, the tuples `ξ` of twisting characters, and the coefficient field `𝕃`, read in `ℂ` through a fixed embedding `σ₀`.

The headline theorems are these. (1) *Unfolding.* Let `E/F` be a quadratic extension of number fields, `V = V_A ⊥ ⟨ζ⟩ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` and `W = W₀ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` Hermitian spaces with `U(V_A)`, `U(W₀)` anisotropic and `ζ` anisotropic, with Weil data for the pairs of `V`, `W` and of their cores with the same splitting characters. For a continuous automorphic `φ` on `U(V_A)` and a rapidly decreasing `Υ′` on `[U(W)]`, the Bessel period of the theta lift `θ_Ψ(Υ′)` along the flag of `ℍ^ρ` and the line `ζ` is an integral over `R_J(𝔸)\U(W)(𝔸)`, `R_J` the Jacobi group of `W ⊃ W₀`, of Fourier–Jacobi periods against Jacobi theta functions (6.2b, 6.2c); and a Fourier–Jacobi period of a theta lift is an integral of Bessel periods for a space with one hyperbolic plane fewer (6.2d). Along a double step of the chain the two compose (6.2). (2) *The seesaw identity.* Let `m ≥ 4` be even, let the data of the chain satisfy the gap size `SizeGap` of the row gap and the hypotheses of the row steps, and let the Bessel–Poincaré series `P_u` be nonzero on the new forms `M_u` for every word `u`. Then the top period of the base tuple vanishes on the kernel of the descent; for every tuple `ξ` there is a root of unity `ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)` (in the formal proof: a nonzero number, a unit at `𝔭` for `ξ = ξ_𝔭`; 6.12), `ζ_ℂ(u, 1) = 1`, with `P̃_ξ(φ, Υ) = ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)·B_u(φ)(ι_ξ Υ)` for all columns `φ ∈ M_u ⊗ ℂ` and rows `Υ` of the relevant part `ℋ^{rt}_ξ`; and `B_u(φ)(x) = a_u·⟨V x, P_u(U_u φ)⟩_ℋ` with `a_u ∈ ℂ^×`, an isometry `U_u` of `M_u ⊗ ℂ` and one isometric embedding `V : 𝔼 → ℋ` for all words (6.14). (3) *Rationality.* If moreover the hypotheses of the rational structure of the grid hold, then `σ₀^{−1}B_u` is `𝕃`-linear in the column and `c_𝕃`-semilinear in the row on the rational structures, and either `B_u = 0` or every seesaw ratio `ζ(u, ξ)` is a root of unity of `𝕃` with `σ₀(ζ(u, ξ)) = ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)` (6.15).

The roadmap rests on [MetaplecticAutomorphicForms](../../MetaplecticAutomorphicForms/README.md) for the Weil representations of unitary pairs, global theta lifts and Kudla's seesaw, and for the Fock model, its `K`-finite vectors and the compact dual pairs (its §§1.5, 2.5a, 3.7a), of which the finite expansion of the double lift in 6.9 is a corollary, and on its family companion [ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs](../ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs/README.md) for the global Weil representation and the Fock model; on GanGrossPrasadUnitaryGroups for Bessel data and global Bessel periods; on [AdelicAlgebraicGroups](../../AdelicAlgebraicGroups/README.md), whose §4.5a states the integration over a group compact at infinity as a weighted sum over its classes, of which 6.10 is a corollary; on [ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups](../ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups/README.md) for the data of the chain, the column chain and the top period; on [RowTowerAndRallisIdentities](../RowTowerAndRallisIdentities/README.md) for the rows, the descent and its section; and on [BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds](../BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds/README.md) for the Bessel–Poincaré series and their nonvanishing. The pairing and the seesaw identity are used by [GluedTargetAndDivisionInequality](../GluedTargetAndDivisionInequality/README.md) and [ThetaChainsAndFrobeniusPrimeSums](../ThetaChainsAndFrobeniusPrimeSums/README.md).

The roadmap has one layer, Layer 6 of the chain. Its introduction fixes the Weil data of the steps, the rows and descents, the top period, the column functions and the Bessel periods. 6.2a–6.2d unfold Bessel and Fourier–Jacobi periods of theta lifts for general unitary pairs; 6.1 defines the period pairing and the seesaw ratio; 6.2 and 6.3 apply the unfolding to the double steps and to the last double step of the chain; 6.4–6.7 are the local laws at the places of odd residue characteristic or split, at the type primes, at the level prime and at the twist places, and 6.8 assembles them into the evaluation of the outer integrals; 6.9 and 6.10 expand the double lift and the top period; 6.11 transports linear relations among top periods to bottom Bessel periods with one archimedean factor, and 6.12 shows that the constants it carries change only by roots of unity from tuple to tuple; 6.13 identifies the bottom Bessel periods with pairings against Bessel–Poincaré series; 6.14 is the seesaw identity and 6.15 its rationality.

## Conventions

The layer number and the target ids of this roadmap are those of the chain of theta lifts of the family [RiemannHypothesisForDirichletLFunctions](../README.md): this roadmap holds Layer 6, and cites the other layers of the chain in the roadmaps that hold them.

**Fields.** `E` is the host (FrobeniusRestrictedRieszSums §0.1): finite abelian over `ℚ` with an imaginary quadratic subfield `K`; `F = E⁺` its maximal totally real subfield, `f = [F:ℚ]`; `c ∈ G` complex conjugation. Under `SplitRamification d` (FrobeniusRestrictedRieszSums §0.3) `K` is ramified only at the prime `d`; on the cyclic hosts (FrobeniusRestrictedRieszSums §0.4) `d ≡ 7 (mod 8)` and `K = ℚ(√−d)`. `𝕃` is the coefficient field of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.6: a number field containing `E`, Galois over `ℚ` and totally complex, read in `ℂ` through the embedding `σ₀` fixed in ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.6, with the conjugation `c_𝕃 = σ₀^{−1}∘(complex conjugation)∘σ₀` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.6.

**CM type and places.** An embedding `ι₀ : E ↪ ℂ` is fixed; `Φ = Gal(E/K)` is identified with the CM type `{ι₀∘g : g ∈ Φ}` induced from `K` (`|Φ| = f`). Elements of `Φ` are called places. When `K = ℚ(√−d)`, `√−d ∈ K` denotes the root with `Im ι₀(√−d) > 0`, so that `Im τ(√−d) > 0` for every `τ ∈ Φ`.

**Primes of the construction.** The *type primes* `g₀, g₁` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.7) carry the depth-zero types; the *auxiliary primes* `p_δ, p′_δ` carry the characters `δ, δ′` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.4, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.7); the *level prime* `T(u) = N(π_u)` of a word `u` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.8) carries the new forms. The *twist places* `Q(ξ)` of a tuple `ξ` of twisting characters are its full ramification set (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.5).

**Hermitian forms, definiteness and measures.** Hermitian forms are conjugate-linear in the first variable (the convention of `HermitianFormsAndLattices` and `ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups`). Definiteness is that of `HermitianFormsAndLattices`: a space is *definite at* a real place if its form has one sign there, *totally definite* if it is definite at every real place (the sign may depend on the place), and *totally positive definite* if every sign is `+`; a *definite unitary group* is the unitary group of a totally definite space. The column spaces `X_j`, the model spaces `V_j` and the hyperplane `X^h ⊂ X_m` are totally positive definite (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2). On `U(V)(𝔸_f)` the stabiliser of the chosen self-dual lattice has volume `1`; for totally definite `V`, `∫_{[U(V)]} φ = Σ_𝒸 |Γ_𝒸|^{−1} φ(g_𝒸)` over the classes `𝒸` (representatives `g_𝒸`, stabilisers `Γ_𝒸`), so the total volume is the mass; for indefinite `V` the archimedean measure is transferred from the compact inner form, and formal degrees are taken for this measure.

**Theta lifts between definite groups.** The step of the column chain from level `j − 1` to level `j` is a definite dual pair in the homomorphism model (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2): the symplectic space is `𝕎_j := Hom_E(X_{j−1}, X_j)`, on which `(g, h)` acts by `x ↦ h∘x∘g^{−1}`, with the alternating form `Tr_{E/F}(δ_T·tr(x^†y))`, the scaling element `δ_T := √−d/d = −1/√−d` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2), and the standard additive character of `𝔸_F/F` (equivalently the form `Tr_{E/F}(δ_T·tr(y^†x))` with the complex conjugate character). Every pair of spaces of the chain (column steps, row steps, model steps, the arrows of the grid of RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.8a) is a homomorphism pair in this normal form (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a; skew element `δ_T`, or a positive rational multiple of it for the grid); lifts integrate against the theta kernel, and the row descents integrate against its complex conjugate at the complex conjugate vector, `θ̄_{S,T}(·; Φ) = conj θ_{S,T}(·; conj Φ)` for the test vector `Φ` (equivalently, they are linear theta integrals of the conjugate datum: skew element `−δ_T`, inverse splitting characters, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a (b)). `x^†` is the adjoint of a homomorphism of Hermitian spaces. In the conventions of `ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups` (standard character, symplectic form built from the two Hermitian forms and a fixed purely imaginary element, here taken to be `δ_T`, which is not the `√−d` of this roadmap) this is the homomorphism pair of ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups §0.1 whose first space is the dual `X_{j−1}^∨` (on which `U(X_{j−1})` acts contragrediently) and whose second space is `X_j` with the skew form `δ_T⟨,⟩`, read through its dictionary ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.5.

**Indices of the chain.** Step `j` is the column lift `U(X_{j−1}) → U(X_j)` with Hecke character `μ_j` and step weight `w_j = e_{j−1}` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.3), the row descent `U(Y_j) → U(Y_{j−1})` (against the conjugate kernel `θ̄`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a), and the model lift between `U(V_j)` and `U(W_j)`. Columns grow by `t + 1`, rows by `t`.

**Row spaces.** The row forms on `U(Y_0)` are formed at principal level at the type primes, where the finite group `G₀` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a acts (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.3): `ℋ^♮` denotes the rows at principal level and `𝔼^♮ = Π_good·ℋ^♮` the good ones; `ℋ` and `𝔼 = Π_good·ℋ` are their parts of the row type level `(P_1, χ_{P_1})` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a, on which the Poincaré series, the seesaw and the target rows live; `𝔼_0` is the part of the base level `(P_0, χ_{P_0})`.

**Characters and levels at the type primes.** For a type prime `g ∈ {g₀, g₁}` the prime `π_g𝒪_K` of `K` above `g` is fixed, and for a place `v` of `F` above `g`, `w_v` is the place of `E` above `v` and `π_g` (residue field `𝔽_g`). The *type characters* of step `j` are `χ_{v,j} := ω_g^{2j}` on `𝒪_{E_{w_v}}^×`, `ω_g` a fixed generator of the characters of `𝔽_g^×`, the same for all `v | g` and all `j` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.4 (b)): squares, of depth zero, read as elements of `𝔽_g^×` through the identification of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a (its tame condition). The step character `μ_j` is tamely ramified there, with residue `χ_{v,j}^{−1}` at `w_v` and `χ_{v,j}` at `w̄_v`. The levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a carry these characters: on `U(V_j)` the character `χ_{v,j}^{−1}` of the determinant of the `t`-block, and on the row stage of `Y_j` the character `χ^{fl}_j = ∏_{i≤j} χ_{v,i}^{−1}` of the determinants of the added blocks. A *level* at the type primes is a pair `(P, χ_P)` of a subgroup `P` of the finite group `G₀` and a character `χ_P` of `P`; the rows are formed at principal level and the levels cut out their parts (base level `(P_0, χ_{P_0})`, row type level `(P_1, χ_{P_1})`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a). At the twist places the levels carry `c_v(det of the lower block)`, `c_v = (ξ_{w_v}|_{𝒪^×})^{s}`, with exponent `+1` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c); the rule for all bad places is that of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9 (the residues `c_{i,v}` of the step characters). The splitting character `η̃` and the twisting characters are unramified at the type places, and `μ_j`, `δ`, `δ′` are unramified outside the *bad places* `S₀`, the places above `g₀, g₁, p_δ, p′_δ` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.4 (b)).

**Periods and pairings.** The top period `P̃_ξ(φ, Υ)` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9) is linear in the column `φ` and conjugate-linear in the row form `Υ`. The period pairing `B_u(φ)(x)` of 6.1 is linear in the column `φ` and conjugate-linear in the row `x ∈ 𝔼` (6.14 (d)); for a tuple `ξ` and a row form `Υ` of the relevant part `ℋ^{rt}_ξ` of the row type level (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4), `P̃_ξ(φ, Υ) = ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)·B_u(φ)(ι_ξΥ)` with a root of unity `ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)` (6.14 (c)). The steps of the descent of a tuple are normalised by the volume of its finite level group (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4).

## Layer 6: The seesaw and the Bessel pairing

The pairing of a source form with a row is defined as the top period composed with the section of the descent. The top period of the chain against a form on `U(X^h)` is unfolded two steps at a time (Bessel to Fourier–Jacobi to Bessel) down to a Bessel period on `U(Y_0)`; each double step is the composite of three exchanges under one theta lift, stated for general unitary pairs in 6.2a–6.2d. Place by place the outer integrals reduce to explicit nonzero local constants (vacua at the unramified places, a finite Fourier transform at the level prime, single cells with characters at the type primes, finite chart sums at the twist places) and to one factor depending only on the archimedean test data; so linear relations among top periods are transported to bottom Bessel periods of the unprojected descent, and the bottom Bessel periods are pairings with the Bessel–Poincaré series of BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds. The archimedean factor is not computed: it is nonzero because the row whose evaluation is a nonzero Poincaré series `P_uφ₁` (BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.21) pairs with it to `‖P_uφ₁‖² ≠ 0`, and that row is a descent. The descent intertwines the finite group of the type primes, so on the relevant part of the forms on `U(X^h)` the unprojected descent lands in the good rows and agrees with the projected one. The result: the top period kills the kernel of the descent, it factors through the descent for every twisting tuple with a root of unity as the only change, and the pairing is rational over the coefficient field. Of the seesaw scalar `a_u` only `a_u ≠ 0` enters the argument; its size is never needed (it is absorbed into an invertible matrix).

The local statements need `E/F` to be unramified at every finite place (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups, introduction of Layer 2) and every dyadic place of `F` to split in `E` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2 (g)), so that every finite place outside the type primes, the level prime and the twist places is split in `E`, or inert of odd residue characteristic. The seesaw runs only for the row type level `(P_1, χ_{P_1}) = (K₂, χ₂)` (the Bessel–Whittaker level of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a), at every stage: the descent is defined once, at principal level at the type primes (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4), and types are moved by its equivariance under the finite group `G₀`; the Gelfand–Graev level `(P_0, χ_{P_0})` enters only through counts of labels. The Poincaré series and the descents of the relevant forms land in the rows of level `(K₂, χ₂)`.

*The Weil data.* Every step of the chain, a column step, a row step or a model step, is a homomorphism pair in the normal form of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a, with the skew element `δ_T` (Conventions; ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups, introduction of Layer 3); the row descent `io_j` integrates against the complex conjugate of the theta kernel of the row step, that is, it is the linear theta integral of the conjugate datum of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(b) (the same pair with `−δ_T` and the inverse splitting characters; ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(c)), with the finite test vectors of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §§2.9–2.9c. The unfolding of 6.2–6.10 is carried out for the row steps in this conjugate datum; the data that it induces on the column pairs (6.2) are therefore the conjugate data of the column steps of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3(v)): this is why the column forms enter through their conjugate readings `φ̄[y]` below and why 6.9 yields `conj((K_{j+2}K_{j+1}φ)[y′])` (see *Column functions*). In particular the column vectors that the unfolding produces at the bad places (`𝔨_j` of 6.5, the chart vectors `d_Q(α, α′)` of 6.7) are vectors of the conjugate data, and the chain's finite data of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 are their complex conjugates `𝔨̄_j`, `d̄_Q(α, α′)` (6.8 (a)). At the twist places the column chain is a matrix of steps indexed by charts, and the last two steps at the type primes and twist places carry the line variable as a sum over cosets (6.5, 6.7; ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.5).

Throughout this layer the coefficient field `𝕃` is read in `ℂ` through the embedding `σ₀` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.6, and `c_𝕃` is the conjugation of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.6. Local fields are completions `F_v` of `F` at a place `v`; at a place split in `E` the unitary groups are general linear groups over `F_v = E_w`.

*Rows* (the dictionary of RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.3). `ℋ^♮` is the row space at principal level at the type primes, a `ℂ[G₀]`-module, and `𝔼^♮ = Π_good·ℋ^♮`; for a level `(H, χ_H)`, `H ⊂ G₀`, `e_{H,χ_H} := |H|^{−1}Σ_{h∈H}χ_H(h)^{−1}h ∈ ℂ[G₀]` cuts out the parts `ℋ_{(H,χ_H)}`, `𝔼_{(H,χ_H)}` of that level. `ℋ := ℋ_{(P_1,χ_{P_1})}` is the space of the row type level `(K₂, χ₂) = (P_1, χ_{P_1})`, the range of `R(e_𝒟 ⊗ 𝟙_{K₂,χ₂})`, where the Poincaré series take values (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.3, BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14), and `𝔼 := Π_good·ℋ` are the target rows of the pairing. *Descents.* `D_ξ := io_1(ξ) ∘ ⋯ ∘ io_m(ξ) : 𝒰^♮_ξ ⊗ ℂ → ℋ^♮` is the descent, defined at principal level (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4), and `ι_ξ := Π_good ∘ D_ξ`; `D_ξ` commutes with `G₀`, and on the relevant part `ℋ^{rt}_ξ = Π_good·e_{P_1,χ_{P_1}}·𝒰^♮_ξ` one has `D_ξ = ι_ξ`, with values in `𝔼` (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4). `𝒰^{♮,rt}_ξ := e_{P_1,χ_{P_1}}(𝒰^♮_ξ ⊗ ℂ)` are the forms of row type level. *Period.* The top period is `P̃_ξ(φ, Υ) = P̃(col_ξ φ, Υ)` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9 (its only definition): linear in the column `φ` and conjugate-linear in the row form `Υ` on `U(X^h)`; on rational structures `σ₀^{−1}P̃_ξ` is `𝕃`-linear in `φ` and `c_𝕃`-semilinear in `Υ` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9(d)). *Column functions.* A column form `φ` of level `j` is a function `φ(𝒸)(y)` of a class `𝒸` and a frame `y` (an invertible matrix at each `τ ∈ Φ`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.1). For a frame `y`, `φ[y](g) := χ_lev(κ)·φ(𝒸)(𝔤_∞^{−1}g_∞y)` if the finite part of `g` is `𝔤·y_𝒸·κ` with `𝔤` rational, `y_𝒸` the representative of the class `𝒸` and `κ` in the level group (`χ_lev` the level character), and `φ[y](g) := 0` otherwise; `φ ↦ φ[y]` is linear. The unfolding identities 6.2–6.10 are analytic identities between integrals linear in both arguments; they are applied to the conjugate reading `φ̄[y] := conj(φ[y])` of a column, and 6.10 identifies the result with the complex conjugate of the top period. So the bottom periods and the constants of 6.11–6.14 are the complex conjugates of those integrals, and every relation of 6.11–6.15 has the variance of `P̃`: linear in the column, conjugate-linear in the row. The column theta lifts enter through the Weil data that the row steps induce on the column pairs, which are the complex conjugates of the data of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (the rows being integrated against the conjugate kernel, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(c)); for them the kernel identity is ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3(v), with every scalar conjugated and `ρ^ξ_j`, `ρ′^ξ_j` inverted, and `c′_K(j)` is the complex conjugate of the constant `c_K(j)` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3(iii). *Bessel periods.* For a Bessel datum `V ⊃ V_A` (6.2b) with `U(V_A)` anisotropic, `φ` continuous automorphic on `U(V_A)` and `f` continuous automorphic on `U(V)`, `Bes_{V⊃V_A}(φ, f) := ∫_{[U(V_A)]}∫_{[N_V]} φ(h)·f(nh)·conj(ψ_B(n)) dn dh`, an integral over a compact set (`[U(V_A)]` and `[N_V]` are compact; the first by AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.4, an anisotropic group having a compact quotient), linear in both arguments. On cusp forms it is the global Bessel period `P(f ⊗ φ)` of GanGrossPrasadUnitaryGroups §4.1 for the pair `V_A ⊂ V` and the character `ψ_B`; the form here admits continuous automorphic functions.

### 6.2a Unfolding along a flag unipotent radical

*Origin:* adapted from Wang (2025), Theorem 4.5, written for symplectic–orthogonal pairs and cusp forms (unitary pairs by a footnote), with Furusawa and Morimoto (2025), Proposition 3.1, for the pair `(U(2n), U(1))`, and the earlier cases of Furusawa (1995) and Ginzburg, Rallis and Soudry (1997): the unfolding of a unipotent period of a global theta lift to an integral over the adelic points of the other group modulo the rational stabiliser of a frame of the open orbit; here one lemma for unitary pairs along the unipotent radical of a flag, for an arbitrary continuous function `Θ` on `[N]` with (T1)–(T2), a rapidly decreasing function on `[U(W)]` with `U(W)` possibly isotropic, the growth bound (i) and the functional `𝔣_Θ`.

*Method of the formal proof:* for the CM host of the chain, (ii): the theta kernel is expanded in the mixed model over the rational frames of `W`; the frames with another Gram matrix than `x₀` are eigenfunctions of `N_C` with eigenvalue `≠ 1` and integrate to `0` over `[N]`, the linearly dependent frames are killed by a transvection that fixes the first vectors of the flag and multiplies `Θ` by `c_a ≠ 1`, and the remaining frames form one orbit of `N(F) × U(W)(F)`, over which the sum is unfolded against a product fundamental domain.

Let `E/F` be a quadratic extension of number fields, `ψ_F` a nontrivial character of `F\𝔸_F`, `W` a Hermitian space and `V = V₀ ⊕ I ⊕ I^∨` a Hermitian space over `E` with `I = ⟨e_1, …, e_ρ⟩` and `I^∨` totally isotropic, in duality and orthogonal to `V₀`, with Weil data for `(U(V), U(W))` and `(U(V₀), U(W))` with the same splitting characters (MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §3.3 and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §4.4a); `θ` and `θ₀` their theta functionals, `ω`, `ω₀` their Weil representations, `ρ₀` the Heisenberg action for `(V₀, W)`, and for a function `Υ′` on `[U(W)]` and a Schwartz function `Ψ`, `θ_Ψ(Υ′)(g) := ∫_{[U(W)]} θ(ω(g, h)Ψ)Υ′(h) dh`. A function `f` on `[U(W)]` (or on `[U(V′)]` for another Hermitian space `V′`) is *rapidly decreasing* if it is continuous and `|f(h)|·‖h‖^n` is bounded for every `n`, `‖h‖ := inf_{𝔤∈U(W)(F)} height(𝔤h)`. Let `N` be the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of the flag `⟨e_1⟩ ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ I` in `U(V)`, with elements `n_C(c)·n_B(b)·m(u)` (`u` upper unitriangular in `GL(I)`, `b ∈ Hom(V₀, I)`, `n_C(c)` in the subgroup `N_C` of the elements acting trivially on `I^⊥` and on `V/I`), and `Ψ ↦ Ψ^I` the mixed model: `Ψ^I(x)` lies in the Schwartz space of `(V₀, W)` for `x ∈ W(𝔸)^ρ`, `θ(Ψ) = Σ_{x∈W(F)^ρ}θ₀(Ψ^I(x))`, `(ω(n_C(c)n_B(b)m(u), 1)Ψ)^I(x) = ψ_F(q_c(x))·ρ₀(x∘b)Ψ^I(xu)` (`q_c(x)` linear in the Gram matrix of `x`) and `(ω(1, h)Ψ)^I(x) = ω₀(1, h)Ψ^I(h^{−1}x)`. Let `x₀ ∈ W(F)^ρ` be linearly independent with Gram matrix `q₀·E_{ρρ}` (`q₀ ∈ F`), `R_{x₀}(F) := {h ∈ U(W)(F) : hx₀ = x₀u for some upper unitriangular u}`, and `Θ` a continuous function on `N(F)\N(𝔸)` with (T1) `Θ(n_C(c)n) = conj ψ_F(q_c(x₀))·Θ(n)`; (T2) for every `1 ≤ j ≤ ρ` and every `a = e_j + Σ_{i<j}a_ie_i ∈ I(F)` with `Gram(x₀)·a = 0` there are a transvection `u_a ∈ N(𝔸)` of `V` along the isotropic vector `a` fixing `e_1, …, e_j` and a number `c_a ≠ 1` with `Θ(n u_a n′) = c_aΘ(nn′)` for all `n, n′ ∈ N(𝔸)`. Then for every rapidly decreasing `Υ′` on `[U(W)]` and every `Ψ`: (i) there are `C_Ψ`, `A₀` with `Σ_x|θ₀((ω(n, h)Ψ)^I(x))| ≤ C_Ψ‖h‖^{A₀}` for all `n ∈ N(𝔸)`, so `Θ(n)Υ′(h)Σ_x|…|` is bounded on `[N] × [U(W)]`; (ii) `∫_{[N]} Θ(n)·θ_Ψ(Υ′)(n) dn = ∫_{R_{x₀}(F)\U(W)(𝔸)} Υ′(h)·𝔣_Θ(ω(1, h)Ψ) dh`, where `𝔣_Θ(Ψ) := ∫_{U_ρ(𝔸)}∫_{[Hom(V₀,I)]} Θ(n_B(b)m(u))·θ₀(ρ₀(x₀∘b)Ψ^I(x₀u)) db du`, all integrals absolutely convergent (Haar measures on `N(𝔸)`, `U_ρ(𝔸)`, `Hom(V₀, I)(𝔸)` giving the rational quotients volume one; any Haar measure on `U(W)(𝔸)`). `U(W)` may be isotropic.

*Route.* Expand the kernel in the mixed model. (i) follows from MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §5.2 (uniform moderate growth of the theta kernel), applied through the splittings of MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §3.3 to a positive Schwartz majorant of `Ψ`, with `[N]` compact; with `Θ` bounded on the compact `[N]` and `Υ′` rapidly decreasing it gives absolute convergence and Fubini, the only estimate. The terms whose Gram matrix differs from that of `x₀` integrate to `0` over `[N]` by (T1). A linearly dependent term (`x_j` in the span of `x_1, …, x_{j−1}`) has a relation vector `a` with `Gram(x₀)·a = 0`; integrate first over the compact quotient of the subgroup `M_j ⊂ N` fixing `e_1, …, e_j` (normal in `N`): the sum over the class of `a` is unchanged by `u_a ∈ M_j(𝔸)`, which multiplies `Θ` by `c_a ≠ 1`, so it integrates to `0` (T2). The remaining terms form one orbit of `N(F) × U(W)(F)` (Witt's theorem, HermitianFormsAndLattices §1.1 (b)), whose stabiliser meets `N(F)` in its intersection with the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of `I` and projects onto `R_{x₀}(F)`; unfold, with the fundamental domain `U_ρ(𝔸) × [Hom(V₀, I)] × [N_C]` for functions invariant under `N_C`. *Sources:* (the Fourier coefficient of a theta lift along the open orbit); (ii): [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/UnfoldOverFlagUnipotentThetaLiftStatement.lean` (`GlobalTheory.T.unfold_over_flagUnipotent_thetaLift`). [FormalProof] states 6.2a (ii), 6.2b, 6.2c and 6.2d for the CM host `E/F` of the chain only (its data `D7`), and 6.2a, 6.2b and 6.2d under the hypothesis `GlobalTheory.Unf.Descent` (a Weil datum of a pair descends, with the same splitting characters, to the core of a Witt decomposition of either space), which it proves for that host (`GlobalTheory.Desc.weil_data_descend`, `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/WeilDatumDescendsToWittCore.lean`); for every quadratic extension of number fields 6.2a–6.2d are this roadmap's own. The same statement and proof hold for the other dual pairs of type I.

*Needs:* MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §§3.3, 5.2, 5.6 and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §4.4a; AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.5 (heights); AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §0.4 (moderate growth); HermitianFormsAndLattices §1.1 (b) (Witt extension).

### 6.2b Bessel to Fourier–Jacobi

*Origin:* adapted from Wang (2025), Theorem 4.5 with the hook-type orbits of its §5.2: the exchange of periods attached to nilpotent orbits under the global theta lift, written there for symplectic–orthogonal pairs and cusp forms (unitary pairs by a footnote); here for a unitary pair with the Bessel datum `V = V_A ⊥ ⟨ζ⟩ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` and the Jacobi datum `W = W₀ ⊕ ℍ^ρ`, anisotropic cores, continuous forms on `U(V_A)`, a rapidly decreasing `Υ′`, an explicit flat map and absolute convergence.

*Method of the formal proof:* the Bessel period of the theta lift is unfolded (6.2a) to an integral over the rational points of the Jacobi group, passed to the adelic Jacobi group through its product Haar measure and compact quotient, and the inner mean is identified with the Fourier–Jacobi integral of the flat map of the translated Schwartz function.

In the notation of 6.2a, let `V = V_A ⊥ ⟨ζ⟩ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` with `U(V_A)` anisotropic (`ζ` anisotropic; `ℍ^ρ` with the isotropic basis `e_1, …, e_ρ` and its dual) and `W = W₀ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` with `U(W₀)` anisotropic; *the Bessel datum* `V ⊃ V_A`: `N_V` the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of the flag of `⟨e_1, …, e_ρ⟩` in `U(V)`, `ψ_B` the character of `N_V` given by `ψ_F` on the chain `e_1 → ⋯ → e_ρ → ζ`, and `R_B = U(V_A) ⋉ N_V` the Bessel subgroup; *the Jacobi datum* `W ⊃ W₀`: `N_J` the unipotent radical of the stabiliser of the isotropic flag of `W`, `ψ_U` its generic character, `R_J = U(W₀) ⋉ N_J` the Jacobi group. Let `V′₀ := V_A ⊥ ⟨ζ⟩`, `x₀` the isotropic frame of `W`, `y₀ := (0, …, 0, −ζ)`, `Ψ^♮(x, y)` the doubly mixed model, and the *flat map* `♭_BF(Ψ) := ∫_{U_ρ(𝔸)} conj ψ_U(u)·Ψ^♮(x₀u, y₀) du`, a Schwartz function for `(V′₀, W₀)` (absolutely convergent). For `φ` continuous on `[U(V_A)]`, a function `G′` on `[U(W)]` and a Schwartz function `Ψ′` for `(V′₀, W₀)` put `FJ♭(φ, G′; Ψ′) := ∫_{[U(W₀)]}∫_{[N_J]} G′(nh)·conj ψ_U(n)·∫_{[U(V_A)]} φ(h₀)·θ₀(ω₀(h₀, 1)·J_ζ(n)·ω₀(1, h)Ψ′) dh₀ dn dh`, `J_ζ` the Heisenberg action of `N_J` through the vector `−ζ ⊗ e_ρ`. Then for every rapidly decreasing `Υ′` on `[U(W)]` the function `h ↦ FJ♭(φ, R(h)Υ′; ♭_BF(ω(1, h)Ψ))` on `U(W)(𝔸)` is left `R_J(𝔸)`-invariant, it is the `R_J(𝔸)`-average of an integrable function on `U(W)(𝔸)`, and

`Bes_{V⊃V_A}(φ, θ_Ψ(Υ′)) = ∫_{R_J(𝔸)\U(W)(𝔸)} FJ♭(φ, R(h)Υ′; ♭_BF(ω(1, h)Ψ)) dh`.

*Route.* 6.2a with `q₀ = 0` and `Θ = conj ψ_B` (then `R_{x₀} = R_J`, and the inner integral over `b` is the second mixing at `y₀`); Fubini over the compact `[U(V_A)]`, using a bound of the inner integral by a power of the height; pass from `R_J(F)\U(W)(𝔸)` to `R_J(𝔸)\U(W)(𝔸)` through the closed subgroup `R_J(𝔸)` (whose Haar measure is the product measure; the quotient `[U(W₀)] × [N_J]` is compact); the flat map intertwines `R_J(𝔸)` with the Jacobi action. [FormalProof] (in the form recorded at 6.2a), `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/IsQuasiFam.lean` (`GlobalTheory.Unf.UnfBF`), proved as `GlobalTheory.T.besselPeriod_thetaLift_unfolding` (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/BesselPeriodThetaLiftUnfoldingStatement.lean`). *Needs:* 6.2a; MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §§3.3, 5.6 and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §4.4a; AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.4.

### 6.2c The link between the two Fourier–Jacobi integrals

*Origin:* adapted from Gan, Gross and Prasad (2012): the Fourier–Jacobi models and global Fourier–Jacobi periods of skew-Hermitian spaces; here the inner integral `FJ♭` of the Bessel unfolding of 6.2b is the Fourier–Jacobi period of an explicit Jacobi theta function `Y_{φ,Ψ′}`, the two Heisenberg actions of `N_J` being matched under the similitude `τ`, with inverse central characters.

*Method of the formal proof:* the kernel of the inner integral is a central character value times a value of the Weil representation of the pair `(V′₀, W₀)` at a point of the Heisenberg variable; under the map `w ↦ (τ∘w)^*` of the lines the points of the two Heisenberg actions of `N_J` correspond and the central coordinates are opposite, so the central characters are inverse, and the identity follows by rewriting the two integrals term by term.

In the notation of 6.2b, let `L = ⟨ζ⟩`, `L′ = ⟨ζ′⟩` a line and `τ : L′ → L`, `τζ′ = −ζ`, a similitude with factor `δ₂/δ₁` (`⟨τa, τb⟩ = (δ₂/δ₁)·⟨a, b⟩`), where `δ₁`, `δ₂` are the skew elements (trace-zero elements of `E^×`) of the homomorphism pairs `(U(L), U(W₀))` and `(U(W₀), U(L′))`: an isometry when `δ₁ = δ₂`, as for every pair of the chain (Conventions). The Heisenberg action `J_ζ` of `N_J` factors through the projection `n ↦ n̄` of `N_J` onto `Heis(Hom(W₀, L))`, identified with `Heis(Hom(W₀, L′))` under `w ↦ (τ∘w)^*`. Since `conj δ₁ = −δ₁`, this identification carries the symplectic form of the second pair to the negative of that of the first, so it inverts central characters: this is why `Y` below and the theta functional of 6.2d have inverse central characters (with an anti-isometry and `δ₁ = δ₂`, the integrand of 6.2d would transform under the centre by `ψ_F²`). For a Schwartz function `Ψ′` for `(V′₀, W₀)` put `Y_{φ,Ψ′}(n̄·h) := conj ∫_{[U(V_A)]} φ(h₀)·θ₀(ω₀(h₀, 1)·J_ζ(n)·ω₀(1, h)Ψ′) dh₀` (`h ∈ U(W₀)(𝔸)`, `n ∈ N_J(𝔸)`; `θ₀` the theta functional of `(V′₀, W₀)`, so that `Y` is a theta function in the remaining variables, not an evaluation of `Ψ′`). *Route.* On the factor `ω_{L,W₀}` of `ω₀ = ω_{V_A,W₀} ⊗ ω_{L,W₀}` (ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §3.3b (a), with the roles of the two spaces exchanged: `V′₀ = V_A ⊥ L` decomposed, `U(W₀)` acting), `(n̄, h) ↦ J_ζ(n)·ω₀(1, h)` is the Schrödinger–Weil representation of the Jacobi group of MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §6.17 for the symplectic space `Hom(W₀, L)`, with `U(W₀)` acting through the splitting of MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §3.3 (on the factor `ω_{V_A,W₀}` only `h` acts). `θ₀` is left invariant under the rational points of `U(V′₀) × (U(W₀) ⋉ Heis)` (ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §4.4a), which makes `Y` a Jacobi form; continuity and moderate growth by 6.2a (i); the identity is the definition of `FJ♭` rewritten, with `ω_{V_A⊥L,W₀} = ω_{V_A,W₀} ⊗ ω_{L,W₀}` and the correspondence of the two Heisenberg actions of `N_J` under `w ↦ (τ∘w)^*`. ### 6.1 The period pairing and the seesaw ratio

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the period pairing of a source form with a row, defined through a section of the descent, and the ratios that compare twisted chains with the base chain.

*Method of the formal proof:* the pairing `B_u` and the section `sec` are definitions of the formal proof, which states the API with them; of the seesaw ratios it states that a family exists (6.15 (b)).

*Definitions.* For a word `u`, a column `φ ∈ M_u ⊗ ℂ` (the `T(u)`-new forms of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.8) and a row `x ∈ 𝔼`, the *period pairing* is

`B_u(φ)(x) := P̃_1(φ, sec x)`,

with `P̃_1` the top period of the base tuple (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9) and `sec : 𝔼 → Q_1` the section of the descent (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4: the inverse of `ι_1` on `Q_1 = ℋ^{rt}_1 ∩ (ℋ^{rt}_1 ∩ ker ι_1)^⊥`, composed with the orthogonal projection of `𝔼` onto `ι_1(Q_1)`). Since the column level at a twist place is the hyperspecial stabiliser at stage `0` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c), `ℳ_0(u, ξ) = ℳ_0(u)` and `col_ξ φ` is defined for `φ ∈ M_u`. For a tuple `ξ ≠ 1` the *seesaw ratio* `ζ(u, ξ) ∈ 𝕃` is defined as follows: if the pairing `(φ, Υ) ↦ B_u(φ)(D_ξ Υ)` on `(M_u ⊗ ℂ) × ℋ^{rt}_ξ` is not identically zero and there is `z ∈ 𝕃` with

`P̃_ξ(φ, Υ) = σ₀(z) · B_u(φ)(D_ξ Υ)` for all `φ ∈ M_u ⊗ ℂ`, `Υ ∈ ℋ^{rt}_ξ`,

then `ζ(u, ξ) := z` (it is unique); otherwise `ζ(u, ξ) := 1`. Put `ζ(u, 1) := 1`.

*The seesaw ratios.* A family `ζ(u, ξ) ∈ 𝕃^×`, `ζ(u, 1) = 1`, is a family of seesaw ratios if `P̃_ξ(φ, Υ) = σ₀(ζ(u, ξ)) · B_u(φ)(D_ξΥ)` for all rational `φ` and all rational `Υ ∈ ℋ^{rt}_ξ`; 6.15 (b) shows that one exists, and it is unique whenever `B_u ≠ 0`. The definition by cases above is this roadmap's own; [FormalProof] proves only that such a family exists.

*API.* `B_u(φ)(x)` is linear in `φ` and conjugate-linear in `x` (as `P̃_1` is in `Υ`, `sec` being linear). For `Υ ∈ ℋ^{rt}_1` one has `sec(ι_1 Υ) = proj_{Q_1} Υ`, and for every tuple `ι_ξ Υ = ι_ξ(proj_{Q_ξ} Υ)` on `ℋ^{rt}_ξ` (the difference lies in `ℋ^{rt}_ξ ∩ ker ι_ξ`); on `ℋ^{rt}_ξ`, `D_ξ = ι_ξ` (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4). Hence `B_u(φ)(ι_1 Υ) = P̃_1(φ, proj_{Q_1} Υ)`, and `B_u = 0` iff every `P̃_1(φ, ·)` vanishes on `Q_1`. The definitions involve no choice: `sec` is determined by `ι_1` and the Petersson products, and `ζ(u, ξ)` by `B_u` and `P̃_ξ`.

*Check.* The section is needed: defining `B_u(φ)(x) := P̃_1(φ, Υ)` for an arbitrary `Υ ∈ ℋ^{rt}_1` with `ι_1 Υ = x` is well defined only once the top period is known to vanish on `ℋ^{rt}_1 ∩ ker ι_1`, which is the content of 6.14(b); the definition through `Q_1` is unconditional, and 6.14(b) says the two agree.

### 6.2 The double step of the chain

*Origin:* adapted from Wang (2025), Theorem 4.5, and Furusawa and Morimoto (2025), Proposition 3.1, the exchange of Bessel and Fourier–Jacobi periods under one theta lift, in the settings recorded at 6.2b and 6.2d: here the composite of two exchanges along a double step `Y_j → Y_{j+1} → Y_{j+2}` of the row tower of the chain, with the double lift `Θ²`, the integrability statements, one hyperbolic plane per double step and the Bessel line `⟨z_m⟩` with the vector `z_{m,u}` of the word.

*Method of the formal proof:* the double step is the composite of the Bessel to Fourier–Jacobi unfolding (6.2b), the link law (6.2c) and the Fourier–Jacobi to Bessel unfolding (6.2d), using that the flat maps preserve theta functions and that right translation of a theta lift is the lift of the translated Schwartz function.

For even `j < m − 2` let `V = Y_j`, `W = Y_{j+1}`, `V′ = Y_{j+2}`, with `V_A = X_j`, `W₀ = X_{j+1}`, `V_{A′} = X_{j+2}`, and for the word `u` the vector of the Bessel datum `ζ = ζ′ = z_{m,u}`, of norm `−T(u)`, which spans the line `⟨z_m⟩` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §§2.2, 2.8), so that `V = V_A ⊥ ⟨z_m⟩ ⊕ ℍ^{ρ+1}`, `W = W₀ ⊕ ℍ^{ρ+1}` and `V′ = V_{A′} ⊥ ⟨z_m⟩ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` (`ρ = h_{j+2}`; one hyperbolic plane fewer), with the Weil data of the row steps (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.2) and the data they induce on `(U(V_A), U(W₀))` and `(U(W₀), U(V_{A′}))` by descent through the hyperbolic planes. The groups `U(V_A)`, `U(W₀)`, `U(V_{A′})` are anisotropic (the column spaces are totally definite). Let `φ` be a continuous automorphic function on `U(V_A)`, `Υ′` a rapidly decreasing automorphic function on `U(V′)` whose theta lift `θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′` to `U(W)` is again rapidly decreasing, and `Ψ₁`, `Ψ₂` Schwartz functions for the two pairs. Then for every `g ∈ U(V)(𝔸)` there is an integrable function `f₁` on `U(W)(𝔸)` with `∫_{U(W)(𝔸)} f₁ = Bes_{V⊃V_A}(φ, R(g)θ_{Ψ₁}θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′)`, and for every `h₁` the function `h₂ ↦ Bes_{V′⊃V_{A′}}(Θ²(Ψ₁, Ψ₂, φ; g, h₁, h₂), R(h₂)Υ′)` is the `R_{B′}(𝔸)`-average of an integrable function on `U(V′)(𝔸)` whose integral is the `R_J(𝔸)`-average `F₁(h₁) := ∫_{R_J(𝔸)} f₁(r h₁) dr` of `f₁` (a function on `R_J(𝔸)\U(W)(𝔸)`, integrable there; `f₁` itself is not left `R_J(𝔸)`-invariant, `R_J(𝔸)` having infinite volume); in particular

`Bes_{V⊃V_A}(φ, R(g) θ_{Ψ₁}θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′) = ∫_{R_J(𝔸)\U(W)(𝔸)} ∫_{R_{B′}(𝔸)\U(V′)(𝔸)} Bes_{V′⊃V_{A′}}(Θ²(Ψ₁, Ψ₂, φ; g, h₁, h₂), R(h₂)Υ′) dh₂ dh₁`,

where `R_J`, `R_{B′}` are the Jacobi group of `W ⊃ W₀` and the Bessel subgroup of `V′ ⊃ V_{A′}` (6.2b, 6.2d), the Bessel periods are those of the conventions of this layer (for cusp forms they are the periods of GanGrossPrasadUnitaryGroups §4.1), and `Θ²(Ψ₁, Ψ₂, φ; g, h₁, h₂) := Θ_Y(♭_FB(ω(h₁, h₂)Ψ₂))` with `Y := Y_{φ, ♭_BF(ω(g, h₁)Ψ₁)}` the Jacobi form of 6.2c and `Θ_Y`, `♭_FB`, `♭_BF` the maps of 6.2b, 6.2d: a continuous automorphic function on `U(V_{A′})`, the double theta lift `V_A → W₀ → V_{A′}` of `φ` against the flat maps (partial Fourier transforms in the isotropic variables) of the two Schwartz functions.

Route: 6.2b applied to `(φ, θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′, ω(g, 1)Ψ₁)`, which needs `θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′` rapidly decreasing; 6.2c with `ζ′ = ζ = z_{m,u}`, which turns the inner period into the Fourier–Jacobi period of the Jacobi form `Y`; 6.2d applied to `(Y, Υ′, ω(h₁, 1)Ψ₂)`. The flat maps preserve theta functions and converge for a linearly independent base point (6.2a). In the chain `θ_{Ψ₂}Υ′` is rapidly decreasing because it is a multiple of the descent of a row form, a cusp form (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.2); a theta lift of a rapidly decreasing function is in general only of moderate growth, so this hypothesis is where cuspidality enters. What is owned here is the instance: the ranks rise by `t` and `t + 1` along the double step, one hyperbolic plane is used per double step, and the negative line `⟨z_m⟩` is the Bessel line at every even stage, with the vector `z_{m,u}` of the word, the one that defines the Bessel character `ψ_u` of the Poincaré series in 6.13.

### 6.3 The seesaw of the last double step

*Origin:* adapted from Kudla (1984): the seesaw identity of global theta lifts, for the Weil representations of unitary pairs attached to splitting characters of Kudla (1994) and Harris, Kudla and Sweet (1996); here (a) the identity of kernels for the pairs of the last double step and (b) the codimension-one Bessel period of a double descent as the period of a double lift, for totally definite spaces over a CM extension and decomposable test vectors.

*Method of the formal proof:* (b): the Bessel period without unipotent part is an integral over the compact quotient `[U(V_A)]`, the two theta kernels are continuous and bounded on the compact quotients, and the identity is the exchange of the order of integration in a triple integral over `[U(V_A)]`, `[U(V_S)]` and `[U(V_T)]`.

Let `V_A`, `V_S`, `V_T` be totally definite Hermitian spaces over a CM extension, `L = ⟨ζ⟩` and `L″ = ⟨z⟩` lines with `⟨ζ, ζ⟩ = −⟨z, z⟩`, `V₀ = V_A ⊥ L` and `V_X = V_T ⊥ L″`, with Weil data for the pairs `(U(V_A), U(V_S))`, `(U(V_S), U(V_T))`, `(U(V_S), U(L″))`, `(U(V₀), U(V_S))` and `(U(V_S), U(V_X))` whose characters satisfy the seesaw compatibility of MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §6.15.

(a) *Seesaw identity of kernels.* For Schwartz functions `Ψ` (for `(V_S, V_T)`) and `Ψ_z` (for `(V_S, L″)`), and `g ∈ U(V_S)(𝔸)`, `h′ ∈ U(V_T)(𝔸)`: `θ_{V_S,V_X}(g, (h′, 1); Ψ ⊗ Ψ_z) = θ_{V_S,V_T}(g, h′; Ψ) · θ_{V_S,L″}(g, 1; Ψ_z)`, i.e. the Weil datum of `(U(V_S), U(V_X))` restricted to `U(V_S) × (U(V_T) × U(L″))` is the tensor product.

(b) *The last double step.* For `φ` on `U(V_A)`, `Υ` on `U(V_T)` and Schwartz functions `Ψ₁` (for `(V₀, V_S)`) and `Ψ₂` (for `(V_S, V_T)`):

`Bes_{V₀⊃V_A}(φ, θ_{V_S→V₀}(Ψ₁; θ_{V_T→V_S}(Ψ₂; Υ))) = ∫_{[U(V_T)]} θ_{V_S→V_X}(Ψ′; θ_{V_A→V_S}(Ψ₀; φ))(h′, 1) · Υ(h′) dh′`,

where `Bes_{V₀⊃V_A}` is the codimension-one Bessel period `∫_{[U(V_A)]}` (no unipotent part), for decomposable `Ψ₁ = Ψ₀ ⊗ a` (`ω_{V₀,V_S} = ω_{V_A,V_S} ⊗ ω_{L,V_S}`; `Ψ₀` for `(V_A, V_S)`, `a` a Schwartz function of the line variable) and `Ψ′ := Ψ₂ ⊗ a′`, `a′` the function `a` read as a Schwartz function for `(V_S, L″)` through `⟨ζ, ζ⟩ = −⟨z, z⟩`; both sides are continuous and linear in `Ψ₁`, so the identity extends to all `Ψ₁` with the right side the corresponding sum (splitting the line variable of a general `Ψ₁` does not produce one pair of test vectors). In the chain: `V_A = X_{m−2}`, `V_S = X_{m−1}`, `V_T = X^h ≅ Y_m`, `V₀ = Y_{m−2} = X_{m−2} ⊥ ⟨z_m⟩`, `V_X = X_m = X^h ⊥ ⟨z⟩`, with `⟨z, z⟩ = −⟨z_m, z_m⟩ = T(u_*)` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2). So the bottom Bessel period of the double descent of `Υ` is the top period of the double lift of `φ`.

Route: (a) is Kudla's seesaw at the level of Weil representations, `ω_{V_S,V_T} ⊗ ω_{V_S,L″} = ω_{V_S,V_T⊕L″}` restricted to `U(V_T) × U(L″)`, which at every place is the compatibility of the splittings with orthogonal sums, ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §3.3b (a), (b) (MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §6.15 takes the compatibility of the see-saw characters as a hypothesis and does not state this identity). For (b), write the left side as a triple integral over `[U(V_T)]`, `[U(V_S)]`, `[U(V_A)]` of `φ · θ_{V₀,V_S} · θ_{V_S,V_T} · Υ` (all quotients are compact, AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.4, and the kernels continuous, so every integral is a finite class sum and Fubini applies); for decomposable `Ψ₁` rewrite the product of kernels by (a) and the splitting of `ω_{V₀,V_S}` along `V₀ = V_A ⊥ L`; integrate over `[U(V_A)]` and `[U(V_S)]` first; extend by linearity and continuity in `Ψ₁`.

*Sources:* (Kudla's seesaw). (b): follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/IsQuasiFam.lean` (`GlobalTheory.Unf.LastLaw`), proved as `GlobalTheory.T.doubleLiftPeriod_rankZero_fubini_compactQuotient` (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/DoubleLiftPeriodRankZeroFubiniCompactQuotientStatement.lean`). *Needs:* MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §§3.3, 5.6, 6.15 and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §§3.3b, 4.4a; ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups §§0.4, 1.2; AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.4.

### 6.4 Local laws at the places of odd residue characteristic or split

*Origin:* adapted from Mœglin, Vignéras and Waldspurger (1987): the lattice model of the Weil representation at a place of odd residue characteristic, in which the vector of the self-dual lattice is fixed by the lattice stabilisers (a); here, for the double-step configuration of 6.2 with adapted lattices and at the split places, the dyadic ones included, the flat maps of vacua (b), the support of the unfolded integrands on one cell (c), the lattice lemma (d) and the contraction of two vacua (e), without the scalars.

*Method of the formal proof:* a vacuum is characterised by its invariance under the Heisenberg translations of the self-dual lattice; the flat maps intertwine the translations by `Hom(V₀, W₀)`, and for adapted lattices the lattice of the core, extended by zero, lies in the lattice of the whole, so invariance passes through the flat maps and through the integral over the line variable; the support statement is reduced by the Iwasawa decomposition to a diagonal element whose entries are forced to be units.

Let `F_v` be the completion of `F` at a finite place `v` outside the type primes, the level prime and the twist places, with `E_v/F_v` unramified quadratic or split and, if `v | 2`, split. Let `(V, W)` be a unitary pair over `E_v` in the double-step configuration of 6.2, with self-dual lattices adapted to the decompositions `V = V₀ ⊕ ℍ^ρ`, `W = W₀ ⊕ ℍ^ρ` (the lattice of each hyperbolic plane spanned by a hyperbolic pair, the lattices the direct sums of their parts) and, on the Bessel side, `V₀ = V_A ⊥ ⟨ζ⟩` with `𝒪ζ` a direct summand and `⟨ζ, ζ⟩` a unit; the scales of the isotropic bases adapted to the conductor of the additive character, and the characters unramified. Call *vacuum* any vector of the Weil representation fixed by the Heisenberg translations of the self-dual lattice of the configuration space (MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §2.6a and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §4.4a). Then:

(a) a vacuum is fixed, up to the values of the splitting characters on determinants, by the stabilisers of the lattices; (b) the flat map of a vacuum for `(V, W)` is a vacuum for `(V₀, W₀)` (possibly zero); (c) the flat map of `ω(1, h)vac` is nonzero only for `h ∈ R_J(F_v) · Stab(L_W)` (one cell), and the unfolded Bessel integrand of `Θ²` is nonzero only for `h₂ ∈ R_{B′}(F_v) · Stab(L_{V′})`; (d) if `𝔤 ∈ U(V₀)(F_v)` keeps the multiples of `ζ` that lie in `L_{V₀}` inside `𝔤 L_{V₀}`, then `𝔤 ∈ U(V_A)(F_v) · Stab(L_{V₀})`; (e) the contraction of two vacua (flat maps followed by integration over the line variable) is a bi-vacuum for the column lattices (possibly zero).

No scalar is asserted in (b), (e): the scalars of the vacua at the unramified places are part of the common archimedean and unramified datum of 6.8, and their product is nonzero as a consequence of 6.14(a). At the places above `T(u)` statements (a)–(c) hold for the self-dual lattices of the row spaces, while (d) and (e) are replaced by 6.6 (there the Bessel vector `ζ = z_{m,u}` of the word (6.2) has `⟨ζ, ζ⟩ = −T(u)`, of valuation 1, and the lattice of `V₀` is glued).

Route: at such a place the norm and trace are surjective on units and `2` is a unit, or the place is split and an integral idempotent replaces `1/2`; so a unimodular Hermitian matrix is congruent to `1` and the self-dual lattices of a fixed space form one orbit (HermitianFormsAndLattices §4.2), and the stabiliser of an adapted self-dual lattice admits an Iwasawa decomposition for the parabolic of the isotropic flag. (a) is the lattice model of the Weil representation (MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §2.6a at the places of odd residue characteristic; at the split places, the dyadic ones included, the type-II model of MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §3.2a). (b), (e): the flat map intertwines the Heisenberg translations by `Hom(V₀, W₀) ⊂ Hom(V, W)`, and the lattice of the core lies in the lattice of the whole when the lattices are adapted, so invariance is transported; (e) adds the integration of an invariant function over the line variable. (c): by the Iwasawa decomposition and the equivariance of the flat map under `R_J` and the lattice stabiliser, reduce to a diagonal element `m(t)`; the support of the vacuum makes `t_i⁻¹` integral, the conductor of the generic character makes `t_i/t_{i+1} − 1` integral, the scale of `ζ` makes `t_ρ − 1` integral, so `t` is an integral unit and `m(t)` lies in the stabiliser; the same for the Fourier–Jacobi-to-Bessel flat map. (d): `𝔤L₀` is self-dual and contains the unimodular line lattice `𝒪ζ`, hence splits it off; its complement is a self-dual lattice of `V_A`, which is `a·L_{V_A}` with `a ∈ U(V_A)(F_v)`, and `a⁻¹𝔤` stabilises `L₀`.

### 6.5 Local laws at the type primes

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the symmetric level `J_j` with its character `ch_j`, a depth-zero level at the split type primes, with explicit test vectors and four laws (o)–(c) by which the unfolded integrals are supported on one cell and contract to a positive constant, and the equivariance of the column vectors.

*Method of the formal proof:* a direct computation with the test function of the symmetric level: a block-triangular Iwasawa decomposition with the support, periodicity and conductor conditions gives the one-cell law, an induction on the position (one row of entries exchanged for one column per step, each contributing the volume of a ball) gives the exchange, and the contraction of the double step is evaluated by the Fourier transform of the indicator of a box, which yields `c_G(j)` as a product of four positive constants.

Let `v` be a place of `F` above a type prime `g ∈ {g₀, g₁}`, split in `E`, so that `F_v` has residue field `𝔽_g`, every unitary group of the chain is `GL_n(F_v)` there and the Weil representations act on Schwartz functions of matrices. Fix an additive character `ψ_g` of `F_v` trivial on `𝒪` and not on `ϖ⁻¹𝒪` (on the residue field it is the `ψ_g` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9 read through `ϖ⁻¹`), a uniformiser `ϖ`, and the components `μ_j` of the step characters of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.4(b) at the place `w_v` (tamely ramified, with `μ_j|_{𝒪^×} = χ_{v,j}^{−1}∘(reduction)`, `χ_{v,j} = ω_g^{2j}` the type characters of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.4(b)).

*Level data* (the row stage levels of `(P_1, χ_{P_1})` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a, recalled in the coordinates of the laws). In the basis `b_v = (e_1, …, e_{r_S}, z_m, β_1, …, β_k, f_{r_S}, …, f_1)` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a (`r_S = m/2 − 1`), extended at stage `j` by `j` added blocks of size `t`, the level of the row stage `Y_j` at `v` is `J_j := K^{(P_1)}_j(v)`, the row stage level of `(P_1, χ_{P_1})` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a (the preimage of `P_1(v)` in the flag group `K^{fl}_j(v)`), with its character `ch_j := χ_{P_1}·χ^{fl}_j`. So `J_0 = K₂(v)`: the elements of `GL_r(𝒪)` whose reduction is unitriangular in `b_v` with the `k` entries in the row of `z_m` and the columns of `β_1, …, β_k` equal to `0`, with `ch_0(M) = ψ_g(ϖ⁻¹·Σ(the 2r_S entries of the chain e_1 → ⋯ → e_{r_S} → z_m → f_{r_S} → ⋯ → f_1))·ψ_g(ϖ⁻¹·Σ_{i<k} M_{β_i,β_{i+1}})`; and for `j ≥ 0`, `J_{j+1}` consists of the block matrices with upper left block in `J_j`, upper right block integral, lower left block `≡ 0 (mod ϖ)` and lower right block in `GL_t(𝒪)`, with `ch_{j+1}(h) = ch_j(upper left block)·μ_{j+1}(det of the lower right block)` (the residue `c_{j+1,v} = χ_{v,j+1}^{−1}` of `μ_{j+1}`, the rule of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9). Equivalently, `J_j` consists of the integral matrices congruent to `1` modulo `ϖ` outside the *free positions*: the strictly upper triangular positions of `b_v` other than the `k` positions (row `z_m`, column `β_i`); from the coordinates of `Y_0` to the added blocks; inside an added block and from an added block to a later one. The row test function of the step `(Y_j, Y_{j+1})` at `v` is the row type vector `Ψ_j := Φ^{rt}_{j+1,v}` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a: in coordinates adapted to the lattices, `Ψ_j(x) = ch_j(g)·μ_{j+1}(det g)⁻¹` if the upper square block `g` of `x` lies in `J_j` and the lower block is `≡ 0 (mod ϖ)`, and `0` otherwise.

*Operators.* For a position `1 ≤ p ≤ c + 1` (`c = c(j) := m − 1 − j`, the rank of the orthogonal complement of `X_j` in `Y_j`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2 (b)) let `E_p(Ψ)(x₀) := ∫ Ψ(x₀ + e)·ψ_g(−Σ superdiagonal of e) de`, the integral over the entries `e` that are free at position `p` (added rows before `p` against old columns, old rows against added columns after `p`, and the strictly upper part of the added block); `♭_{col,p}`, `♭_{row,p}` the same with one further column, respectively row, at position `p` (the line variable); `R_p` the subgroup generated by the block `GL` and the unipotent group of position `p`. Put `𝔨_j := V_j⁻¹·E_1(Ψ_j)`, `V_j` the volume ratio of the unipotent parts of the level group and of the lattice stabiliser. Write `ω̄_v(κ, κ′)` for the local action of the conjugate datum of the column step (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(b)), and `K^{fn}_j(v)`, `χ^{fn}_j` for the fine levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a with their characters. `𝔨_j` (`0 ≤ j ≤ m − 3`) is a finite Heisenberg combination of lattice vectors, a function of `x` modulo `g` supported on the integral matrices at `w_v`, and a vector of the conjugate datum of the column step `(X_j, X_{j+1})` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(b)): for `κ ∈ K^{fn}_j(v)`, `κ′ ∈ K^{fn}_{j+1}(v)`, `ω̄_v(κ, κ′)𝔨_j = χ^{fn}_j(κ)·χ^{fn}_{j+1}(κ′)^{−1}·𝔨_j`. Hence its complex conjugate `𝔨̄_j` is an admissible finite datum of that step (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (A3)) for the fine levels and for the column levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a. The last two steps carry the line variable of 6.3: for a coset `y` of `g^{−1}L_{m−1,w_v}` modulo `L_{m−1,w_v}` put `𝔨^{(y)}_{m−2}(x) := Ψ_{m−2}([x | y])` (the row vector of the step `(Y_{m−2}, Y_{m−1})` at the map equal to `x` on `X_{m−2}` and sending the one added coordinate to `y`) and `𝔨^{(y)}_{m−1}(x) := Ψ_{m−1}(x_h)·ψ_g(−η_x(y))` if `η_x` is integral and `0` otherwise, where `x_h` is the part of `x` with values in `X^h` and `η_x := π_u^{−1}·(the last row of x)` (`z_u = π_ue_{N_m}`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.8). Then `ω̄_v(κ, κ′)𝔨^{(y)}_{m−2} = χ^{fn}_{m−2}(κ)·χ^{fn}_{m−1}(κ′)^{−1}·𝔨^{(κ′y)}_{m−2}` for `κ ∈ K^{fn}_{m−2}(v)`, `κ′ ∈ K^{rt}_{m−1}(v)`, and `ω̄_v(κ, κ′)𝔨^{(y)}_{m−1} = χ^{fn}_{m−1}(κ)·χ^{fn}_m(κ′)^{−1}·𝔨^{(κy)}_{m−1}` for `κ ∈ K^{rt}_{m−1}(v)`, `κ′ ∈ K^{fn}_m(v)`: the coset moves with the element, so a single coset datum is equivariant only under the stabiliser of its coset, and the common level of stage `m − 1` is the principal congruence subgroup (the intersection of these stabilisers, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a). The chain's finite data at the type primes are the `𝔨̄_j` (`j ≤ m − 3`) and the families `𝔨̄^{(y)}_{m−2}`, `𝔨̄^{(y)}_{m−1}`, a system of data in the sense of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2, each member admissible for the column levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a; the composite of the last two steps is the sum over the cosets `y` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.5).

*Laws.* (o) *One cell and exchange:* `E_p(h·Ψ_j) ≠ 0 ⟹ h ∈ R_p·J_{j+1}` for every `p`; `E_p(Ψ_j) = C_p·E_1(Ψ_j)`, `C_p > 0`, for `p ≤ r_S + 1`. (a) For `c(j) + 1 = 2r′`: `♭_{col,r′+1}(h·Ψ_j) ≠ 0 ⟹ h ∈ U·R_{r′+1}·J_{j+1}`, `U` the column unipotent group of position `r′ + 1`. (b) For `c(j) + 1 = 2r′`: `♭_{col,r′+1}(Ψ_j)(x; y) = C·𝔨_j(x)·1[y ∈ ϖ^{−r′}𝒪^n]`; for `c(j) = 2r′`: `♭_{row,r′+1}(Ψ_j)(x; u) = C′·𝔨_j(x)·1[u ∈ ϖ^{r′+1}𝒪^n]`; `C, C′ > 0`. (c) For `c(j) = 2r′ + 1`, so that `c(j + 1) = 2r′` (the case of every double step: `j` and `m` even, the column law (b) applied to the first step with parameter `2r′ + 1` and the row law to the second with parameter `2r′`): the contraction of `♭_{col,r′+2}(Ψ_j)` with `♭_{row,r′+1}(h·Ψ_{j+1})` vanishes unless `h ∈ R_{r′+1}·J_{j+2}`, and at `h = 1` equals `c_G(j)·𝔨_j ⊗ 𝔨_{j+1}` with `c_G(j) = C·C′·V̂·vol(ϖ^{r′+1}𝒪^n) > 0`, `C, C′` the constants of (b) and `V̂` the constant in "the Fourier transform of the indicator of `ϖ^{−(r′+1)}𝒪^n` is `V̂` times the indicator of `ϖ^{r′+1}𝒪^n`". Consequently, in the unfolding of 6.2 at these places the integrand is supported on one cell, the level group acts by a character, and the contraction of the two Heisenberg combinations of the rows is `c_G(j) ≠ 0` times the tensor product of the combinations of the columns.

Route: the description by free positions is proved by induction on `j`. (o): a block-triangular Iwasawa decomposition, the support and periodicity of `Ψ_j` and the conductor of `ψ_g` force the Levi part into the lattice stabiliser, then the pattern refines the stabiliser to `J_{j+1}`; the exchange is an induction on `p`, one exchange of roots per step. (b), column: for `y` in the box, adding the line variable at that position does not change `Ψ_j` (the position is free at that scale); otherwise every value vanishes; then (o). (b), row: the row unipotent of a vector of the box lies in the source level and the level character is trivial on it at the symmetric position. (a) is the support statement behind (b). (c): Fourier transform of the indicator of a box (AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactors §AL.0.1.7, so that `V̂ = vol(ϖ^{−(r′+1)}𝒪^n)`), (b), and (o) for the support. *Equivariance of the column vectors* (the level laws). There are six: the law of the steps `j ≤ m − 3`, the source and target laws of the step `m − 1` with its coset, the source law of the step `m`, its target law, and the containment of the principal congruence subgroups with trivial characters. Steps `j ≤ m − 3`: for `r ∈ R_1 ∩ J_j` restricting to `κ` on `X_j` and `r′ ∈ R_1 ∩ J_{j+1}` restricting to `κ′` on `X_{j+1}`, the law follows from three facts: the row test vector is a two-sided eigenfunction, `Ψ_j(x∘k)·μ(det k) = ch_j(k)·Ψ_j(x)` for `k ∈ J_j` and `Ψ_j(k∘x)·μ(det k) = ch_{j+1}(k)·Ψ_j(x)` for `k ∈ J_{j+1}` (from the formula for `Ψ_j`); `R_1` acts on the completions of `x₀` by the free entries through an affine bijection of the parameters which shifts the argument of `ψ_g` by the sum of the superdiagonal entries of the added block (and `det(h^{−1}rh) = det κ`, `r` being unipotent on the complement); and a change of variables in the finite sum over the free entries. Step `m − 1`: the target law is that of the row test vector of `(Y_{m−2}, Y_{m−1})` at the map `[x | y]`, the source law the source law above. Step `m`: the Fourier factor `ψ_g(−η_x(y))` is unchanged under `k` in the level of stage `m − 1` with `ky″ ≡ y′` because `η(ky″ − y′)` is integral, and `Ψ_{m−1}` obeys the source law (source); for `κ′ ∈ K^{fn}_m(v)` the last row of `κ′^{−1}x` is congruent to that of `x` modulo `g` (the last row of `κ′` is standard), and `Ψ_{m−1}` of the `X^h`-rows obeys the law whose hypothesis is that the last column of `κ′` is standard (target); both `c ≡ 0, d ≡ 1` and `b ≡ 0` are used, so the law fails for `κ′ = (1 b; 0 1)` with `b ≢ 0 (mod g)`. The containment: `κ ≡ 1 (mod g)` extended by `1` lies in the row level and the row character is `1` on `1 + g·End`. The sign of `ψ_g` in `𝔨^{(y)}_{m−1}` follows the conjugation of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a(b).

*Check.* At `j = 0`, in the basis `b_v` the reduction of `J_0` is the group of unitriangular matrices whose `k` entries linking the line vector `z_m` to the block of `β` vanish: it has index `g^k` in the Gelfand–Graev level `P_0` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a, and `ch_0` (which involves the entry linking `z_m` to `f_{r_S}`) is not the restriction of `χ_{P_0}` (which involves the entries linking `z_m` to `β_1` and `β_k` to `f_{r_S}`); a test function built on `(P_0, χ_{P_0})` is a different chain. Nor is `ch_0` the restriction of any character of the full upper unitriangular group (its entry `(z_m, f_{r_S})` is not a simple-root entry of `b_v`): the laws above are computed directly, and no statement on Gelfand–Graev characters, regular or degenerate, enters.

### 6.6 Local laws at the level prime

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the local laws at the level prime in this form: the expansion of the indicator of the glued lattice into characters, the three orbits, and on forms new at the prime the passage of the unfolding to the adjoint finite Fourier transform, with an explicit nonzero constant.

*Method of the formal proof:* the expansion (a) is orthogonality of the characters of `Λ′/ϖΛ′`, applied to the vector `z = ϖA·vec + a`; (b) is a case distinction on the lattices containing `e` on which `ϖ` times the last coordinate is integral; and the operators `S_j` are brought to the front of the column chain by an iteration that moves them down the chain to its first step at the cost of one nonzero constant, where each of them is a linear bijection of the columns multiplying norms by a positive constant.

Let `v` be a place of `F` above `T(u)`, split in `E`, `𝔽_v` the residue field of `F_v` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9b), with `q_v` elements, and `χ` a nontrivial additive character of `𝔽_v`. Use the forms on pairs of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9b and ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.7: a pair `𝔡 = (Λ, fn)` is a lattice `Λ ⊂ F_vⁿ` with a nonzero `𝒪`-linear map `fn : Λ → 𝔽_v`, `lat 𝔡 = Λ`, `ker 𝔡 = ker fn`; the diamond operators `⟨a⟩_v`, `a ∈ 𝔽_v^×`, the trace `Tr_v`, the new forms `ker Tr_v`, and the adjoint finite Fourier transform `FF^* = FF_v^*` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.7 (the inputs have as kernel the lattice of the output). The *glued lattice* `L^{gl}_𝔡 ⊂ F_vⁿ ⊕ F_v` of `𝔡` is that of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9b (defined there for pairs of level `0`; for a pair of any level by the same formula, with the unit vector `e` of the last coordinate, the coordinate of the line vector `z_{m,u}`, in place of `z_{m,u}`): unimodular, independent of the choices, with projection `Λ` to `F_vⁿ` and intersection `ker fn` with `F_vⁿ`. It is the local form of the self-dual lattice of `V₀ = X_j ⊕ ⟨ζ⟩` when `⟨ζ, ζ⟩` has valuation `1`.

(a) *Expansion.* Let `Λ′ ⊂ F_v^{n′}` be a lattice (`n′ := rk Λ′`, the rank of the target; in the chain `n = N_j`, `n′ = N_{j+1}`), write `x = (A | a)` with `A` the matrix on `F_vⁿ` and `a` the last column, and fix `vec ∈ Λ` with `fn(vec) = 1`, so that `L^{gl}_𝔡 = (ker fn ⊕ 0) + 𝒪(vec + ϖ^{−1}e)`. Then `1_{Hom(L^{gl}_𝔡, Λ′)}(x) = q_v^{−n′}·1[A(ker fn) ⊂ Λ′]·1[z ∈ Λ′]·Σ_ϑ ϑ(z)`, `z := ϖA·vec + a`, the sum over the characters `ϑ` of `Λ′/ϖΛ′`: one term, the trivial character, in which the last column enters only through the support `z ∈ Λ′`, and for each pair `Q` with `lat Q = Λ′` a term `⊗ χ ∘ fn_Q` (the nontrivial `ϑ` are the `χ ∘ fn_Q`). (b) *Three orbits.* If `𝔤 ∈ U(V₀)(F_v) ≅ GL_{n+1}(F_v)` keeps the multiples of the line vector in `𝔤 · L^{gl}_𝔡`, then `L := 𝔤·L^{gl}_𝔡` contains `e` and `ϖ` times the last coordinate is integral on it, and every lattice `L` of `F_vⁿ ⊕ F_v` with these two properties is of one of three shapes, with `L₁ := L ∩ (F_vⁿ ⊕ 0)`: `L = L₁ ⊕ 𝒪e`; `L = L₁ ⊕ ϖ^{−1}𝒪e`; or `L = L₁ + 𝒪(y + ϖ^{−1}e)` with `y ∉ L₁`, `ϖy ∈ L₁`, the glued lattice of the pair `(L₁ + 𝒪y, fn)`, `fn` the functional with kernel `L₁` and `fn(y) = 1`. In the third case `𝔤·L^{gl}_𝔡 = g·L^{gl}_𝔡` for some `g ∈ GL_n(F_v)` in the block, and `𝔤 ∈ U(V_A)(F_v)·Stab(L^{gl}_𝔡)`; the first two shapes are determined by the lattice `L₁` of `F_vⁿ` alone. (c) *Two orbits die on new forms.* For a form `φ` on pairs that is new at this place, the contribution of `φ(𝔡)` to the unfolded lift against the indicator of `Hom(L^{gl}_𝔡, Λ′)` (`Λ′` of rank `n′` as in (a)) equals `q_v^{−n′/2} · χ_W(ϖ_v) · Σ_{Q : lat Q = Λ′} (contribution of (FF^* φ)(𝔡) against 1_{Maps(𝔡, Q)} ⊗ χ̄ ∘ fn_Q)`, `χ̄ ∘ fn_Q` being the function `y ↦ χ(−fn_Q(y))` on `Λ′`, zero outside (the same character `χ̄` as in the kernel of `FF^*`), `χ_W` the target character of the step as in (e) (the constant is `q_v^{−n′}` times the scalar by which an element `g ∈ GL_n(F_v)` with `g·ker 𝔡 = lat 𝔡` acts on the Schwartz functions; the same holds at any other pair `𝔡₁` in place of `𝔡` on the right, with a constant depending on the two pairs), and the contributions of the lattices of the first two shapes of (b) vanish. ### 6.7 Local laws at the twist places

*Origin:* particular to this proof: at the places where the twisting characters ramify, the unfolded integrals are organised by finitely many charts, and the column chain becomes a matrix of steps indexed by them. That the chart levels contain the principal congruence subgroup of level `p^{j+1}` is this roadmap's own formulation; [FormalProof] has level `p^{2m}`.

*Method of the formal proof:* the laws are proved for data in a normal form that is a product of local data over the twist places, by induction on the number of twist places down to a local double integral, which is a finite sum over the cells of the double coset representatives (the charts) with the volumes of the two level groups as constant; they are then transferred to the data of the chain, which differ from the normal form by a root of unity on each chart, by absorbing these scalars into the family of column forms.

Let `v` be a twist place of a twisted tuple `ξ` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.5, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c: a degree-one place of `F` split in `E`), so that the groups are `GL_n(F_v)`, and let `ξ_v` be the component of the twisting character, of finite order, whose restriction to `𝒪^×` is nontrivial and factors through the residue field, and `c_v` its residue as in ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c. The level of the row stage of rank `n` is the parahoric `J_Q ⊂ GL_n(𝒪)` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c, stabilising the standard lattice and the lattice scaled by `ϖ` on the last `n − r` coordinates (type `(r, n − r)`), with the character `c_v(det of the lower block)`; the row test function of the step `(Y_j, Y_{j+1})` is `Ψ_j^Q := Φ^Q_{j+1,v}` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c (the indicator of the integrality conditions there times `c_v(det of the r × r block)^{−1}`); `idx_v(j)` is the index of the level group of the row stage on `Y_j` in `GL_{n_j}(𝒪)` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c).

*Charts.* `Ch_0 := {1}`; `Ch_{j+1}` is a set of representatives of the double cosets `R_1\GL/J_Q` on which `E_1(g_α, h; Ψ_j^Q)` (the operator of 6.5 at position `1`) is nonzero for some `α ∈ Ch_j`; it is finite. For `α ∈ Ch_j`, `α′ ∈ Ch_{j+1}`: `d_Q(α, α′) := w_j(α)·V⁻¹·E_1((g_α, g_{α′})·Ψ_j^Q)`, with the chart weight `w_j(α)` the inverse of the volume of the stabiliser of the chart `α` in the column level group (for the Haar measure giving the lattice stabiliser volume `1`). At stage `m − 1` the index is the line-variable coset: the charts of stage `m − 1` are the orbits of the cosets of `p^{−m}L_{m−1,w_v}` modulo `p^mL_{m−1,w_v}` under the row level of stage `m − 1` (one representative `g = 1`), with weight `idx_v(m − 1)` times the length of the orbit, that is, the sum over all cosets with the weight `idx_v(m − 1)`; stage `m` has one chart, of weight `idx_v(m)`. For a chart `β` of a stage `j` with `h_j ≥ 0` hyperbolic planes let `ε_β` be the finite combination of right translations by elements of `U(Y_j)(F_v)` that carries the Bessel functional of the stage, at the representative `g_β`, to position one (a single translation when `h_j = 0`), multiplied by `w_j(β)`. *Chart levels.* For a chart `α` of stage `j` (`1 ≤ j ≤ m − 2`) with representative `g_α` let `K_{j,α}` be the group of the `κ ∈ Stab(L_{j,w_v})` that are restrictions to `X_j` of elements `r` of `R_1 ∩ g_αJ_Qg_α^{−1}` (`R_1` of 6.5, `J_Q` the row level of stage `j`), with the character `χ_α(κ) := ψ_v(n(r))^{−1}·c_v(det of the lower block of g_α^{−1}rg_α)`, `ψ_v` the additive character of `E_1` at `v` and `n(r)` the sum of the superdiagonal entries of the unipotent part of `r`; it does not depend on `r` because the chart is live (two lifts of the same `κ` give the same value wherever the sum defining the chart is nonzero). Then `K_{j,α}` contains the principal congruence subgroup of level `p^{j+1}`, `χ_α` is trivial on it (this roadmap's own sharpening; [FormalProof] uses level `p^{2m}`), and the chart vector of step `j + 1` is a vector of the conjugate datum: `ω̄_v(κ, κ′)d_Q(α, α′) = χ_α(κ)·χ_{α′}(κ′)^{−1}·d_Q(α, α′)` for `κ ∈ K_{j,α}`, `κ′ ∈ K_{j+1,α′}` (`ω̄_v` as in 6.5); so its complex conjugate `d̄_Q(α, α′)` is an admissible finite datum (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (A3)) for the column levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c. At stage `m − 1` the representative is `g = 1`, with the row level of stage `m − 1`; the last two steps are as in 6.5, with the cosets of `p^{−m}L_{m−1,w_v}` modulo `p^mL_{m−1,w_v}`, which the principal congruence subgroup of level `p^{2m}` fixes, weighted as above, and with `K^X_m(v)` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c at the top. The `d̄_Q(α, α′)` and these coset families are the chain's finite data at the twist places, a system of data in the sense of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (indexed by charts and cosets), each member admissible for the column levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c. Level `p` is not asserted for the chart levels: in a normal form of a chart representative with first denominator `p^{−a}` and shear vector `z ∈ L_{j,w_v}`, every `κ ∈ K_{j,α}` has `(κ − 1)z ∈ p^aL_{j,w_v}`. *The chart matrix.* At a twisted tuple the column forms of level `j` are families `(φ_α)_{α∈Ch_j}` (one chart at the other places), the step `j + 1` is the matrix `(K_{j+1}(d̄_Q(α, α′)))_{α,α′}` of operators of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 with the chart vectors as data at `v`, and a composite of steps is the product of these matrices, the sum over chart paths (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.5), the row forms being acted on by the combinations `ε_β` of (i) at each stage. The double step at the entry `(α, β)` is `Σ_{α′} K_{j+2}(d̄_Q(α′, β)) ∘ K_{j+1}(d̄_Q(α, α′))`, the intermediate chart summed once: composing independently summed single-step data would add the cross terms `d(α, α′) ⊗ d(α″, β)`, `α′ ≠ α″`, which no law of this target removes.

`c_Q(j) · Σ_β Bes( Σ_{α, α′} Θ²(φ_α; (d_Q(α, α′) ⊗ d_Q(α′, β)) · 𝒞), ε_β·f )`,

### 6.8 Evaluation of the outer integrals

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the assembly of the local laws into one nonzero constant and a common datum independent of the twisting characters.

*Method of the formal proof:* the integral over the finite adelic quotients is collapsed to the twist places by invariance and support of the integrand away from them (one cell, with a ratio of volumes), the law of 6.7 is applied there, and the finite data are then identified place set by place set, using that Heisenberg combinations at disjoint sets of places commute and the laws of 6.5 and 6.6; the common datum is the bi-operator of 6.6 applied to the contraction of the two vacua.

For the data of the chain (levels of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §§2.9–2.9c, the row test vectors of RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.2, the column test vectors of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §§3.2–3.3) and each even `j`:

(a) *Normal form of the test vectors.* For archimedean `a, a′` and the fixed finite adelic base points `b_j` (common to all tuples), `ω_j(a b_j, a′ b_{j+1}) Ψ^{row}_j(P) = s_tv(j) · e_G(j) ∘ e_Q(j)(Ψ^{vac}_j(P; a, a′))`, where `Ψ^{vac}_j` is a vacuum for the self-dual lattices at all finite places, `e_G(j)`, `e_Q(j)` are finite Heisenberg combinations supported at the type primes and the twist places, and `s_tv(j) ≠ 0`; on the column side the finite test vector is `d_Q(j) ∘ d_G(j) ∘ d_T(j)` applied to a vacuum, where, in the conjugate datum in which the unfolding is carried out (introduction of this layer), `d_G(j)` is the `𝔨_j` of 6.5 at the type primes (the coset families `𝔨^{(y)}` at the last two steps), `d_Q(j)` the family of the chart vectors `d_Q(α, α′)` of 6.7 at the twist places, and `d_T(j)` at the places above `T(u)` the finite Heisenberg combination that carries every vacuum of the column lattices there to the vector whose function, in the model on `Hom(X_{j,w}, X_{j+1,w})` (`w` above `π_u`), is the indicator of the maps between the pairs of the two lattices; applied to the vacuum of the standard lattices it gives the datum `φ_{T,v}` of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 (A2), real-valued, and it is reached from the contraction of the vacua there by `op_j` (6.6(e)). The chain's data of ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.2 are the complex conjugates: `𝔨̄_j`, `d̄_Q(α, α′)` and `φ_{T,v}`. (b) *Finiteness.* The integrand of 6.2 is supported on finitely many cells `R · U(Y)(F_∞) · t · K`. (c) *Evaluation.* For a column form `φ` of level `j` and a frame `y`, the double integral of 6.2 with `φ̄[y]` in place of the function on `U(V_A)` at these data equals `c_V(j) · Bes_{Y_{j+2}⊃X_{j+2}}(Θ²_C(conj((S_jφ)[y])), R(a′)Υ′)` (at a twisted tuple: for a family `(φ_α)` of 6.7, the sum over the target charts `β` of the same expression with the entry `(α, β)` of the chart matrix of 6.7 summed over `α` and the row form acted on by `ε_β`), where `Θ²_C` is the double lift `X_j → X_{j+1} → X_{j+2}` with a *common datum* `C`: a function of the two configuration variables that is a bi-vacuum for the column lattices at all finite places and smooth at the archimedean places, depending on `j`, on the two Fock polynomials and on the archimedean translations, not on the tuple; it is not asserted to be nonzero; and

`c_V(j) = v_cell(j) · s_tv(j) · s_tv(j+1) · c_Q(j) · c_G(j) · c_T(j)`,

`v_cell(j)` the product of the two volume ratios `vol(K ∩ U(F_f)) / vol(K ∩ R)` of the single cells. In the last double step (6.3) the same holds without `v_cell(j)`. Every factor of `c_V(j)` is nonzero.

Route: after the normal form (a), the finite part of the integrand is a product of local functions; at each place apply 6.4 (one cell, vacua, contraction), 6.5 (one cell and a character, `c_G(j)`), 6.6 (the operator `S_j` and `c_T(j)`) or 6.7 (charts, `c_Q(j)`); the archimedean part and the scalars of the vacua are left in the datum `C`.

### 6.9 The double lift as a sum of composites of single lifts

*Origin:* adapted from Howe (1979) and Weil (1964), the commutation of the Weil actions of the two members of a dual pair, and Kashiwara and Vergne (1978), the `K`-finite vectors of the Fock model are the polynomials: here (a) on the bi-theta functions of two definite pairs, (b) the archimedean Weil data of the column pairs are the same for all tuples, and (c) the double lift with a common datum is a finite sum of composites of single lifts, which ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3 (v) turns into the step operators of the chain.

*Method of the formal proof:* commutation is proved by duality for theta functions given by their properties: the difference of the two composites is orthogonal to every theta function, by unitarity of one action and commutation of the other with superposition. The datum is then averaged over the real centre, so that it is a finite sum of products of polynomial Fock vectors, and the double lift of each product is a composite of two single lifts. The route written here instead quotes for (a) the commutation of the actions of the two members of a dual pair in each variable, and for (c) the `K_∞`-isotypic decomposition of the datum.

*Corollary of:* MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §2.5a (i) (in the Fock model of its §1.5 the vectors finite under the maximal compact subgroup are the polynomials, and the central circle of that subgroup acts on the polynomials of each degree by one character, a different one for each degree) and §3.7a (iii) (for two definite spaces the polynomials of each degree are a finite sum of products of irreducible representations of the two unitary groups), for the expansion of the double lift as a finite sum of composites of single lifts in (c).

(a) For totally definite `V_A`, `V_S`, `V_{A′}` and Weil data of `(U(V_A), U(V_S))` and `(U(V_S), U(V_{A′}))`, the two Weil actions on functions on `Hom(V_A, V_S) × Hom(V_S, V_{A′})` commute and preserve the space of bi-theta functions. (b) Two tuples differ by finite-order Hecke characters, so the archimedean components of the Weil data of the column pairs `(U(X_{j−1}), U(X_j))` are the same for all tuples. (c) For column forms with a central character at infinity, the double lift `Θ²_C(φ̄[y])` with a datum `C` as in 6.8(c) is a finite sum `Σ_i θ_{P′_i} ∘ θ_{P_i}` of composites of single lifts with polynomial Fock test vectors of bounded degree; with ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3(v) (the kernel identity for the Weil data induced on the column pairs, the conjugate data of the column steps),

`Θ²_C(φ̄[y]) = c′_K(j+1) c′_K(j+2) · Σ_{y′} a(y′) · conj((K_{j+2} K_{j+1} φ)[y′])`,

the finitely many `y′` and the coefficients `a(y′)` depending neither on `φ` nor on the tuple; at a twisted tuple `K_{j+2}K_{j+1}` is the double step of the chart matrix (6.7), applied to the family `(φ_α)`, at each target chart.

Route: (a) is the commutation of the actions of the two members of a dual pair, applied in each variable; (b): a finite-order Hecke character has trivial components on the connected groups `ℂ^×`; (c): the central character makes the archimedean `K_∞`-isotypic decomposition of `C` finite, and in the Fock model each isotypic piece is a finite sum of tensor products of polynomial Fock vectors; then ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3 for each single lift.

(a): [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/IsBiSmooth.lean` (`GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.BiAct`), proved as `GlobalTheory.T.seeSawWeilBiAction_commutes` (`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/SeeSawWeilBiActionCommutesStatement.lean`). *Needs:* ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.2a, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3, 6.7, 6.8; MetaplecticAutomorphicForms §3.3 and ThetaKernelsForUnitaryDualPairs §§3.45, 3.46, 4.4a.

### 6.10 The top period as a class sum

*Origin:* adapted from Gross (1999): the class set of a group compact at infinity, with the weights `|Γ_𝒸|^{−1}` and the mass; here the integral over `[U(X^h)]` of the pairing of a top row form with the conjugate reading of a column form, as a sum over the classes evaluated by Schur orthogonality at infinity, with the constant `c_P(ξ)` and its dependence on the tuple.

*Method of the formal proof:* the integral over `[U(X^h)]` is a sum over the classes of `vol_f(J_ξ)/|Γ_𝒸|` times integrals over the compact group `U(X^h)(F_∞)`; at each class the average over that group of the coefficient pairing is equivariant, hence by multiplicity one of the branching from `U(X_m)` to `U(X^h)` a constant of the archimedean data times the pairing of the restriction of the column at the class with the row form.

*Corollary of:* AdelicAlgebraicGroups §4.5a (class-set integration on a group compact at infinity: the integral of an integrable function invariant on the right under a compact open subgroup is the volume of that subgroup times the sum over the classes `𝒸` of `|Γ_𝒸|^{−1}` times its integral over the archimedean group at the representative of `𝒸`), with Schur orthogonality on `U(X^h)(F_∞)` and the evaluation of `c_P(ξ)` as in the route.

For a column form `φ′` of level `m` (on `U(X_m)`, weight `Λ_m`), a vector `y` of its coefficient space, a top row form `Υ` on `U(X^h)` (`Y_m ≅ X^h` is definite) and a vector `v` of its archimedean coefficient space:

`∫_{[U(X^h)]} Υ(v)(h′) · φ̄′[y](h′, 1) dh′ = c_P(ξ) · a(y, v) · conj(P̃(φ′, Υ))`,

with `a(y, v)` a function of the archimedean data alone, independent of `φ′`, `Υ` and the tuple, and `c_P(ξ) ≠ 0` with `ι_top(ξ)·c_P(ξ)/c_P(1)` a root of unity, `ι_top(ξ)` the terminal multiplier of 6.7(ii) (the index of the level `J_ξ` of the forms on `U(X^h)` for `ξ` in that of the base tuple; `c_P(ξ)/c_P(1)` alone is `[J_1 : J_ξ]^{−1}` times a root of unity). (Both sides are linear in `Υ` and conjugate-linear in `φ′`; `conj(P̃(φ′, Υ)) = ⟨res φ′, Υ⟩`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9.)

Route: the class-set integration formula on the definite group `U(X^h)` (the adelic integral is a sum over classes of `vol_f(J_ξ)/|Γ_𝒸|` times integrals over the compact `U(X^h)(F_∞)`, for the common Haar measure, DiscreteSeriesCoefficientsAndAutomorphicMetrics §7.5a); at each class the integral over `U(X^h)(F_∞)` of the coefficient pairing is, by Schur orthogonality (RepresentationTheory/CompactGroups §§4.1, 4.2, on the compact group `U(X^h)(F_∞)`), the pairing of the restriction of `φ′` at the class (`res φ′`, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9, with the weights `1/|Γ_𝒸|`) with `Υ`, times a factor of the archimedean data; the finite volume stays. `c_P(ξ) = V_T·vol_f(J_ξ)·s(ξ)`, with `V_T` an archimedean volume common to all tuples, `vol_f(J_ξ) = vol_f(J_1)/ι_top(ξ)`, and `s(ξ)` the scalar of the unique top chart, a value of the twisting characters at the fixed base points, hence a root of unity.

*Sources:* (class sums on definite groups); follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/ValLastX.lean` (`GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.PerX`, `GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.Tup.perT`). *Needs:* ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9c, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.9, 6.7; ThetaLiftsForDefiniteUnitaryGroups §1.2; DiscreteSeriesCoefficientsAndAutomorphicMetrics §7.5a; RepresentationTheory/CompactGroups §§4.1, 4.2.

### 6.11 Transport of relations and the archimedean factor

*Origin:* particular to this proof: linear relations among top periods are transported to bottom Bessel periods, which yields a single archimedean factor that is never computed.

*Method of the formal proof:* induction on the number of double steps; in one double step a relation among periods two levels up is tested, by density of the images of the archimedean intertwiners in the smooth model, on vectors on which the composite of two descents is the double theta lift, and is then carried down by the unfolding of 6.2, the evaluation of 6.8, the expansion of 6.9 and the passage of the archimedean translation into the model vector; no archimedean integral is evaluated.

Let `m ≥ 4` be even, fix a word `u` and the data of the chain (unramified outside the type primes, the level prime `T(u)` and the twist places), and a finite set `Ξ` of tuples. For `ξ ∈ Ξ` let `col^S_ξ = (K_m K_{m−1} S_{m−2}) ∘ ⋯ ∘ (K_2 K_1 S_0)` be the column chain of the tuple interleaved with the operators of 6.6(e) (at a twisted tuple the product of the chart matrices of 6.7, and the sum over the cosets of the last two steps, 6.5, 6.7, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.5), and put

`c_top(ξ) := conj( ι_top(ξ) · c_P(ξ) · ∏_{l=0}^{m/2−1} c_V(2l)(ξ) c′_K(2l+1)(ξ) c′_K(2l+2)(ξ) )`, `c_bot(ξ) := ∏_{j=1}^{m} c_I(j)(ξ)`,

where `c′_K` are the constants of 6.9, `c_V` those of 6.8, `c_P` that of 6.10, `ι_top(ξ)` the terminal multiplier of 6.7(ii), and `c_I(j)(ξ) > 0` the constant of RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4 relating the theta integral of the row step `j` for the common measure to the descent `io_j(ξ)` on forms of row type level. For a column `φ ∈ M_u ⊗ ℂ`, a row form `e` (a function of the archimedean model vector `v`) and archimedean data `(y, v)` let the *bottom period* be the complex conjugate of the Bessel period of the conjugate reading, `𝒫_bot(φ, e)(y, v) := conj(Bes_{Y_0⊃X_0}(φ̄[y], e(v))) = ∫_{[U(X_0)]} φ[y](h)·conj(∫_{[N_{Y_0}]} e(v)(nh)·conj(ψ_B(n)) dn) dh`, linear in `φ` and conjugate-linear in `e`, as `P̃` is in the column and the row form.

(a) *Transport of relations.* For every finitely supported family `(α_x)` indexed by triples `x = (ξ_x, φ_x, Υ_x)` with `Υ_x ∈ 𝒰^{♮,rt}_{ξ_x}`: if `Σ_x α_x c_top(ξ_x) P̃(col^S_{ξ_x} φ_x, Υ_x) = 0`, then `Σ_x α_x c_bot(ξ_x) 𝒫_bot(φ_x, D_{ξ_x} Υ_x)(y, v) = 0` for all `(y, v)`. (b) *The archimedean factor.* Equivalently: either all top periods `P̃(col^S_ξ φ, Υ)` and all bottom periods `𝒫_bot(φ, D_ξΥ)` (`Υ ∈ 𝒰^{♮,rt}_ξ`) vanish, or there is a function `Λ_u(y, v)` of the archimedean data alone, independent of `ξ`, `φ`, `Υ`, with

`c_bot(ξ) · 𝒫_bot(φ, D_ξΥ)(y, v) = Λ_u(y, v) · c_top(ξ) · P̃(col^S_ξ φ, Υ)`.

Route: induction on the number of double steps. One double step (6.2 with 6.8 and 6.9): for archimedean vectors of the form `I_{P₁} I_{P₂} v″` (images of the archimedean intertwiners of the row steps), write the composite of two descents as the double theta lift (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.2), unfold by 6.2, evaluate by 6.8, expand the double lift by 6.9 and move the archimedean translation into the vector; a relation among the periods at level `j + 2` gives the same relation at level `j` on these vectors, and on all vectors because their span is dense in the smooth model (Harish-Chandra modules and the Casselman–Wallach globalisation). The last double step is the same with 6.3 and 6.10. These identities are between integrals linear in the row form and conjugate-linear in the column (the conjugate readings `φ̄[y]`), and 6.10 turns the top one into `c_P(ξ)·a(y, v)·conj(P̃)`; taking complex conjugates of the resulting relation (with the coefficients `α_x` conjugated, which is harmless as the family is arbitrary) gives (a) with the conjugated constants `c_top` and the bottom periods `𝒫_bot` as defined; `c_bot` is positive. The unfolding computes the descent without the good projector, which is why the statement is for `D_ξ`; the identity of RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4 between the theta integral of a row step with the test vector of row type level and `c_I(j)(ξ)·io_j(ξ)`, and the laws of 6.5, hold on forms of row type level, which is why `Υ` is taken there; the datum `C` of 6.8 may vanish, in which case both sides vanish. (b) is linear algebra: by (a) the assignment `c_top · P̃ ↦ c_bot · 𝒫_bot` is a well-defined linear map from a subspace of `ℂ`, hence multiplication by one function `Λ_u`.

(a): [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/ValLastX.lean` (`GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.MainX`, `GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.Tup.cTop`, `GlobalTheory.SeeSaw.Tup.cBot`); (b): follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/FormsAtZero.lean` (`GlobalTheory.MiTop.BottomData.Rel`). *Needs:* RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.2, RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4, 6.2–6.10; AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §§2.1, 2.7 (density of `K`-finite vectors, Casselman–Wallach).

### 6.12 The seesaw constants up to roots of unity

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the comparison of the constants of the seesaw for a twisted tuple with those of the base tuple; [FormalProof] shows that a positive power of their ratio is a nonzero rational number supported at the residue characteristics of the twist places, and the root-of-unity form stated here is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* the relation "some positive power of `x/y` is a nonzero rational number supported at the given primes" is shown to be reflexive, to hold when `xⁿ = yⁿ`, and to be closed under products and quotients; the quotient `c_top(ξ)/c_bot(ξ)` is then factored double step by double step into factors each of which satisfies the relation against the base tuple.

For every tuple `ξ`

`(c_top(ξ)/c_bot(ξ)) / (c_top(1)/c_bot(1))` is a root of unity,

and so is `c_T(j)(ξ)/c_T(j)(1)` for every `j`.

*The form of the formal proof.* A positive power of `(c_top(ξ)/c_bot(ξ)) / (c_top(1)/c_bot(1))` is a nonzero rational number whose numerator and denominator are divisible only by residue characteristics of the twist places `Q(ξ)`; [FormalProof], `Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/FamAllQ.lean` (`GlobalTheory.MiTop.FamilyRatioCondition`, `GlobalTheory.MiTop.qPrime_alt`), `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/LatticeFrames/UnitRatio.lean` (`GlobalTheory.Fam.UnitRatio`).

Route: factor by factor. (i) `s_tv(j)(ξ)/s_tv(j)(1)` is a root of unity: by the twisting rule of the Weil representation the two test vectors differ by values of the finite-order character `ξ` at determinants, which are `1` on the connected archimedean groups and roots of unity at the fixed finite base points. (ii) `c_T(j)(ξ)/c_T(j)(1)` is a root of unity: the `r_{j,v}` of 6.6(e) are common and `χ_W(ξ)ⁿ = χ_W(1)ⁿ` for some `n`. (iii) `c′_K(j)(ξ)` and `ι_top(ξ)·c_P(ξ)` are a common constant times a root of unity (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3; 6.10: the terminal multiplier cancels the finite volume of the top level, while `c_P(ξ)` alone carries `[J_1 : J_ξ]^{−1}`), and complex conjugation (in the definition of `c_top`) keeps a root of unity a root of unity. (iv) For the double step at even `j`, `c_Q(j)(ξ)/(c_I(j+1)(ξ) c_I(j+2)(ξ))` does not depend on `ξ`: `c_I(i)(ξ) = 𝔪_i·vol_f(K^{rt}_i(ξ))` is the volume of the row-type level group `K^{rt}_i(ξ)` of `ξ` at stage `i` (ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.9a) times a constant independent of `ξ` (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4), and that group has index `idx_ξ(i)` in the one of the base tuple; and `c_Q(j)` is the volume of the level groups at stages `j + 1`, `j + 2` at the places of `F` below `Q(ξ)` for the measures giving the lattice stabilisers volume one (one factor per conjugate pair of places of `E`, 6.7). (v) `v_cell(j)` and `c_G(j)` do not depend on the tuple.

*Check.* The volumes must match: for the Iwahori-type parahoric of `GL_2(𝒪_v)` the index is `q_v + 1`; with `q_v = 5` a ratio in which the level volume of one side were left uncompensated would carry `6`, which is not a unit at `2` and `3` although `Q(ξ)` lies above `5`.

*Needs:* ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §2.5, ThetaChainsOnDefiniteUnitaryGroups §3.3, RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4, 6.6, 6.7, 6.8, 6.10, 6.11.

### 6.13 Bottom Bessel periods as pairings with Poincaré series

*Origin:* this roadmap's own formulation: the unfolding of a Poincaré series over a Bessel subgroup of `U(Y_0)` with a discrete series at infinity: the rows as evaluations of intertwiners (o), the bottom Bessel period of a row as its pairing with the Bessel–Poincaré series of a column (a), (b), and the transport of relations (c).

*Method of the formal proof:* rows are evaluations of intertwiners at a unit vector; their values there and at the vector conjugate to the Bessel functional are proportional with a constant independent of the row (from the classification of discrete series and the rapid decay of cusp forms), and since the operator of BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14 applied to a coordinate vector is the Poincaré series of the column, the inner product is the unfolded integral `b(e, x)` up to that constant.

Let `u` be a word, `ℋ ⊃ 𝔼` the rows of level `(K₂, χ₂)` and `P_u` the Bessel–Poincaré series of BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14, with Bessel subgroup `S_u ⊂ U(Y_0)` and character `ψ_u`.

(o) *Rows as evaluations.* Let `t_S` be a finite adelic element conjugating the level of the rows to `(K₂, χ₂)` (trivial when both are written in the same coordinates). Then `T ↦ (g ↦ T(⊗_τ w_τ)(g·t_S))` is a linear bijection from the continuous intertwiners of the smooth model of `⊗_τ 𝒟_τ` into cusp forms of that level onto `ℋ`; and if `T ≠ 0` and `T(v′)(·t_S) ∈ ℋ` for a vector `v′` of the model, then `v′ ∈ ℂ·⊗_τ w_τ`. (If the rows are modelled on the complex conjugate representation, as is convenient for the Bessel functional, the same holds with the antiunitary image `v₁` of `⊗_τ w_τ` and a conjugate-linear bijection; this `v₁` is the vector of (b).) (a) *Unfolding.* For `e ∈ ℋ` and a column `x`: `⟨e, P_u x⟩_ℋ = ∫_{N_S(𝔸)U(X_0)(𝔸)\U(Y_0)(𝔸)} 𝔅_x(e)(g) dg`, absolutely convergent (Hermitian products are conjugate-linear in the first variable), where `𝔅_x(e)(g) := ∫_{[U(X_0)]} Ph_x(hg)·conj(∫_{[N_S]} e(nhg)·conj ψ_u(n) dn) dh` is the Bessel period of the right translate `R(g)e` along `(N_S, ψ_u)` weighted by the column inside the `U(X_0)`-integral (`Ph_x` the function of the column of BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14, `S_u = N_S ⋊ U(X_0)`). The column cannot be taken outside that integral: `Ph_x` is `(N_S(𝔸), ψ_u)`-equivariant, but under `h ∈ U(X_0)(𝔸_f)` it involves `x(h)`, which has no scalar law, so `Ph_x` does not descend to `S_u(𝔸)\U(Y_0)(𝔸)`. (b) *Pairing form.* There is `κ_PF ≠ 0`, the constant of the normalisation of the products (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.4), such that `⟨e, P_u x⟩_ℋ = κ_PF · b(e, x)` for every `e ∈ ℋ` and every column `x`, where `b(e, x) := ∫_{N_S(𝔸)U(X_0)(𝔸)\U(Y_0)(𝔸)} 𝔅_x(e(v₁))(g) dg`, conjugate-linear in `e` and linear in `x` (`Ph_x` and `P_u` are linear in the column, BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14), as `𝒫_bot` is in the row and the column. (c) *Relations.* There is a central finite adelic element `c_u`, which may depend on the word, such that a linear relation `Σ_i α_i 𝒫_bot(x_i, e_i)(y, v) = 0` valid for all archimedean data implies `Σ_i α_i b(e_i, c_u·x_i) = 0`; in particular `b(e, x) ≠ 0` implies that some `𝒫_bot(c_u⁻¹·x, e)(y, v)` is nonzero.

Route: (o): values are square-integrable by rapid decay; injectivity because an equivariant map from an irreducible module that vanishes at a nonzero vector is zero; surjectivity: an element of `ℋ` is the value at `⊗w` of a continuous intertwiner into `L²` (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.3(i)); its values at smooth vectors are smooth vectors fixed by an open compact subgroup, hence continuous functions (Sobolev), `Z(𝔤)`-finite, of uniform moderate growth (a class of `L²` functions closed under derivatives is bounded by a power of the height), and cuspidal because `ℋ` consists of cusp forms (RowTowerAndRallisIdentities §5.3(i)) and the cuspidal subspace is closed and invariant; uniqueness of the vector: `R(e_𝒟)T(v′) = ⟨⊗w, v′⟩·T(⊗w)` by Schur orthogonality, and `T` is injective. (a): unfold the Poincaré series over `S_u(F)\U(Y_0)(F)` to an integral over `S_u(F)\U(Y_0)(𝔸)` (BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14), integrate over `[N_S]` first, using the `(N_S(𝔸), ψ_u)`-equivariance of `Ph_x` (`U(X_0)` normalises `N_S` and fixes `ψ_u`), and disintegrate over `N_S(𝔸)U(X_0)(𝔸)\U(Y_0)(𝔸)` with the compact fibre `[N_S] × [U(X_0)]` (`U(X_0)` anisotropic); the integral over `[U(X_0)]` of a right translate is right invariant under `U(X_0)(𝔸)`, so `𝔅_x(e)` is a function on that quotient. (b): by (o) the map from rows to `ℋ` is evaluation at a unit vector; rows evaluated at it and at the vector `v₁` conjugate to the Bessel functional are proportional with a constant independent of the row (classification of discrete series, rapid decay); the operator of BesselPoincareSeriesAndGramBounds §5.14 applied to a coordinate vector is the Poincaré series of the column. (c): the pairing `b` is, after a central translation that matches the base point of the unfolding with the base point `x_u` of the Poincaré series, the integral over the archimedean variable of the Poincaré datum of bottom Bessel periods at the vector `v₁`.

### 6.14 The seesaw identity

*Origin:* particular to this proof: the period of the `m`-fold theta lift equals, up to a constant, a pairing of the `m`-fold descent with a Bessel–Poincaré series, the nonvanishing coming from an `L²` norm; that the constant `ζ_ℂ(u, ξ)` of (c) is a root of unity is this roadmap's own formulation, [FormalProof] having the form recorded below.
