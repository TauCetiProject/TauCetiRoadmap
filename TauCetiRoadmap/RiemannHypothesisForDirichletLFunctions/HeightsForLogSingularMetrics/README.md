# Roadmap: heights for log-singular metrics

**Summary.** This roadmap extends arithmetic intersection theory on projective arithmetic varieties to hermitian line bundles whose metrics are smooth only off a boundary divisor and degenerate there at a log-log rate, as the Hodge metric does at the cusps of a modular curve; it computes heights of projective bundles of Hermitian vector bundles, of `ℙ¹`-bundles and of flag varieties over `ℤ`; and it bounds the maximal slope of the tangent lattice of an abelian variety by the logarithm of its height. The setting is a projective arithmetic variety `X` of absolute dimension `d` (integral, projective and flat over `Spec ℤ`), a dense open subscheme `U`, flat over `ℤ` with `U_ℚ` smooth, and a log-smooth model `p : X′ → X`: a projective birational morphism, an isomorphism over `U`, with `X′_ℚ` smooth and boundary `D = X′_ℚ ∖ U_ℚ` a simple normal crossings divisor. A metric on `L(ℂ)` over `U(ℂ)` is log-log singular semipositive if its weight `φ` relative to a smooth metric on `X′(ℂ)` satisfies `−C·ℓ_s ≤ φ ≤ C`, where `ℓ_s` grows like `log log(1/|s|²)` near `D`, and its curvature is semipositive on `U(ℂ)`.

The headline theorems are these. (1) For log-log singular semipositive `L̄₁, …, L̄_d` with regularisations `L̄_{i,t}` on a common log-smooth model (smooth metrics below the singular one that converge to it monotonically and locally uniformly on `U(ℂ)` as `t → 0`, with a curvature loss tending to `0`), the arithmetic intersection numbers `I_t = (L̄_{1,t}⋯L̄_{d,t})` converge to a real number `L̄₁⋯L̄_d` that depends neither on the regularisations nor on the log-smooth model; it is symmetric and multilinear, equals the usual number for smooth metrics, and satisfies the projection formula for generically finite maps; and if the boundary rates of the regularisations are at most `C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)`, then `|I_t − L̄₁⋯L̄_d| ≤ C′·(log log(1/t))³/log(1/t)`. (2) Let `Ē` be a Hermitian vector bundle of rank `n ≥ 1` over the ring of integers `O_K` of a number field `K`, with normalised slope `μ̂(Ē) = deĝ(Ē)/([K:ℚ]·n)`, and let `Ō(1)` be the dual of the tautological line on the scheme `P` of lines in `E`, with the metric induced from `Ē`. Then `Ō(1)^n = [K:ℚ]·n·(−μ̂(Ē) + ½(H_n − 1))`, where `H_n = Σ_{k≤n} 1/k`; and for every `η > 0` there are a finite extension `L/K` and a nonzero `x ∈ E ⊗ O_L` with `mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ −μ̂(Ē) + ½(H_n − 1) + η`, the mean taken over the complex embeddings of `L`: an absolute Siegel lemma whose constant grows like `½ log n`. (3) Let `Z` be a projective arithmetic variety, `V̄` a Hermitian vector bundle of rank two on `Z`, `π : Y = ℙ(V) → Z` with relative tangent bundle `Ξ̄` (of degree `2` on the fibres), `D = dim Y_ℚ`, and `M̄` a hermitian `ℚ`-line bundle on `Z` with smooth metric. Then for every `k ≥ 1` with `2k ≤ D + 1`, `(Ξ̄^{2k}·π^*M̄^{D+1−2k}) = 2κ(k)·deg_{Y_ℚ}(Ξ^{2k−1}·π^*M^{D+1−2k})` with `κ(k) = ½Σ_{j<k} 1/(2j + 1)`: the even part in `u` of `(π^*M̄ + (u/2)Ξ̄)^{D+1}` is determined by geometric degrees. The same holds for the limit numbers of (1) when the metrics are log-log integrable along a normal crossings boundary and the metric of `Ξ` is induced, over almost every point of `Z(ℂ)`, by a hermitian form on the fibre of `V` depending smoothly on the point. (4) There is an absolute constant `C` such that for every number field `L` and every semi-abelian scheme `G` over `O_L` whose generic fibre `A` is a principally polarised abelian variety of dimension `g`, the tangent lattice `Lie(G)` with the Hodge inner product at every complex embedding satisfies `μ̂_max(Lie Ḡ) ≤ C·log(2 + g + max(h(A), 0))`, `h(A)` being the height of `A` in Deligne's normalisation for this metric; Bost's bound for the same maximal slope is linear in the height.

The roadmap extends ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII, whose continuous and adelic hermitian line bundles, intersection numbers, Deligne pairings and volumes are the objects that Layers 0, 4 and 14 extend to log-log singular metrics, and FaltingsFinitenessAndIsogenyTheoremsPartII, whose bound for the maximal slope of the tangent lattice, linear in the height, Layer 16 complements with a bound logarithmic in the height. It builds on HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions for arithmetic intersection numbers on possibly singular models, with the Cartier recursion, the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel formula and Zhang's theorem of successive minima; on [ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights](../../ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights/README.md) for Hermitian vector bundles over `O_K`, heights of points and the Moret–Bailly models of abelian varieties; on [SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods](../SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods/README.md), which regularises log-log singular metrics and bounds the boundary mass of their mixed Monge–Ampère measures; and on [SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles](../SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles/README.md) for maximal slopes and absolute minima. In the family [RiemannHypothesisForDirichletLFunctions](../README.md), [ArithmeticVolumesAndIntegralFormsOnUnitaryShimuraVarieties](../ArithmeticVolumesAndIntegralFormsOnUnitaryShimuraVarieties/README.md) applies these statements to the Hodge line bundles of unitary Shimura varieties and to the heights of points on their flag bundles.

Layer 0 defines log-smooth models, log-log singular semipositive metrics and their regularisations, attaches an adelic line bundle to such a metric, and constructs the limit intersection numbers, with a bound for the boundary mass of the mixed measures of the regularisations and two rates of convergence. Layer 4 compares the arithmetic volumes of these bundles with their regularised volumes and their limit self-intersections, proves the two successive-minima statements for bundles that are nef on the fibres without assuming ampleness, and computes the height of the projective bundle of a Hermitian vector bundle, from which the absolute Siegel lemma and a bound for the first absolute minimum follow. Layer 14 proves the reflection formula for `ℙ¹`-bundles, for metrics induced from the rank-two bundle, for arbitrary smooth metrics on the relative tangent bundle and for limit numbers, and computes arithmetic intersection numbers of products of flag varieties over `ℤ`, with a bound for Grassmannians. Layer 16 proves the logarithmic bound for the maximal slope of the Lie algebra, by a reduction to one tangent vector through the absolute Siegel lemma and a jet argument with theta sections.

## Conventions

Logarithms are natural. A projective arithmetic variety is an integral scheme, projective and flat over `Spec ℤ`, as in ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.1, and `d` is the absolute dimension of `X`, as in ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.3. A hermitian line bundle `L̄ = (L, ‖·‖)` carries a metric on `L(ℂ)` invariant under complex conjugation; its Chern current is `c₁(L̄) = (1/πi)∂∂̄ log‖s‖ + δ_div(s)`, in the Green-function convention of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII, so that for `‖·‖ = e^{−φ/2}‖·‖₀` it is `c₁(L, ‖·‖₀) + (i/2π)∂∂̄φ`. Line bundles are written additively where convenient (`L̄ + mĀ` is `L̄ ⊗ Ā^{⊗m}`). Products such as `L̄^d`, `(L̄₁⋯L̄_d)` and `(Ξ̄^{2k}·π^*M̄^r)` are arithmetic intersection numbers in the sense of HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a, extended by multilinearity to hermitian `ℚ`-line bundles; on a variety of absolute dimension `d` they take `d` factors. `vol^` is the arithmetic volume: on projective models that of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.3, for adelic line bundles that of its §4.2. In Layer 0, `C`, `C′` and `C″` are positive constants independent of the parameter `t`, which may change from one occurrence to the next; their dependence is stated where it matters.

A Hermitian vector bundle `Ē` over `O_K` is that of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.1, with the degree `deĝ` of its §1.3, the normalised degree `deĝ_n = deĝ/[K:ℚ]` and, for positive rank, the normalised slope `μ̂(Ē) = deĝ_n(Ē)/rk E`, which is invariant under finite extension of `K`; `μ̂_max(Ē)` is the maximal slope of a nonzero sub-bundle (SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §2.1). For a number field `L`, `mean_σ` is the mean `(1/[L:ℚ])Σ_σ` over the complex embeddings `σ` of `L`. `h_{L̄}(x)` is the height of an algebraic point for a hermitian line bundle on a projective flat model (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.12). `H_n = Σ_{k=1}^{n} 1/k` is the harmonic number. For a vector bundle `V`, `ℙ(V)` is the bundle of lines in `V` (`Proj Sym V^∨`), with tautological line `O(−1) ⊂ π^*V` and its dual `O(1)`; a hermitian metric on `V` induces metrics on both.

Notation and conventions not restated here are those of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII (Conventions and Layer 0), HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a, ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights (Conventions and Layer 1) and FaltingsFinitenessAndIsogenyTheoremsPartII (Conventions).

## Layer 0: Hermitian line bundles with log-log singular metrics

This layer defines log-smooth models and log-log singular semipositive metrics on projective arithmetic varieties, with their regularisations, and constructs the adelic line bundle and the limit intersection numbers of such metrics, with a bound for the boundary mass of the mixed measures of the regularisations and two rates of convergence.

### 0.4 Hermitian line bundles with log-log singular metrics

*Setting.* Let `X` be a projective arithmetic variety (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.1) of absolute dimension `d` and `U ⊂ X` a dense open subscheme, flat over `ℤ`, with `U_ℚ` smooth. A *log-smooth model* of `(X, U)` is a projective birational morphism `p : X′ → X` of projective arithmetic varieties, an isomorphism over `U`, such that `X′_ℚ` is smooth and the reduced complement `D := X′_ℚ ∖ U_ℚ` is a simple normal crossings divisor (possibly empty). Let `L` be a line bundle on `X` and `‖·‖` a smooth conjugation-invariant metric on `L(ℂ)` over `U(ℂ)`. On a log-smooth model choose a smooth conjugation-invariant metric `‖·‖₀` on `p^*L(ℂ)` over `X′(ℂ)`, with Chern form `θ`, a Kähler form `ω` on `X′(ℂ)`, and conjugation-invariant metrised canonical sections `s_j` of the components `D_j` of `D` with `|s_j| < 1/2`; put `|s|² = ∏_j|s_j|²` (so `|s|² = 1` if `D = ∅`), `λ_s = 1 + log(1/|s|²) ≥ 1`, `ℓ_s = 1 + log(1 + log(1/|s|²)) ≥ 1`, and `φ = −log(‖·‖²/‖·‖₀²)` on `U(ℂ)`, so that `c₁(L, ‖·‖) = θ + (i/2π)∂∂̄φ` there. When `D ≠ ∅`, `λ_s` and `ℓ_s` are comparable, up to constants, with `log(1/|s|²)` and `log log(1/|s|²)`.

Define: `‖·‖` is *log-log singular semipositive* along `D` if, for a constant `C`, `−C·ℓ_s ≤ φ ≤ C` and `θ + (i/2π)∂∂̄φ ≥ 0` on `U(ℂ)`. Equivalently: `‖·‖` has logarithmic singularities along `D` relative to `p^*L` in the sense of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.1, with the bound for the inverse frame norm taken with exponent `0` (`‖e‖^{−1}` bounded for a local frame `e`), and `c₁(L, ‖·‖) ≥ 0` on `U(ℂ)`. A quotient `L̄₁ ⊗ L̄₂^{−1}` of two such is *log-log integrable*. A *regularisation* of `L̄ = (L, ‖·‖)` on `X′` is a family of smooth conjugation-invariant metrics `‖·‖_t = e^{−φ_t/2}‖·‖₀` on `p^*L(ℂ)` over `X′(ℂ)`, `0 < t < t₀`, with `φ ≤ φ_t ≤ C`, `φ_t ≥ −C·log log(1/t)`, `φ_t` non-increasing as `t` decreases, `θ + (i/2π)∂∂̄φ_t ≥ −ε(t)ω` with `ε(t) → 0`, and `φ_t → φ` locally uniformly on `U(ℂ)`; its *boundary rate* is `δ(t) = sup_{U(ℂ)} (φ_t − φ)/λ_s`. It is *`C²`* if `φ_t → φ` in `C²` on compact subsets of `U(ℂ)`, and *close* if moreover `φ_t ≤ φ + C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)` on `{|s|² ≥ t}`. For `D ≠ ∅`, SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (with its `γ = 2πθ` and its weight `φ`) constructs `C²` regularisations with `ε(t) ≤ C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)`, made conjugation-invariant by averaging `φ_t` with its transform under complex conjugation (all data are conjugation-invariant, so every property survives); they are close, with `φ_t ≤ φ + C·t^{1/3}` on `{|s|² ≥ t}`, when `|dφ| ≤ C·(log(1/|s|²))^C/|s|` near `D` (SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (c); for instance for good metrics); for `D = ∅`, `φ` is smooth on `X′(ℂ)` and `φ_t = φ` is a close `C²` regularisation.

Prove:

(o) log-smooth models exist; the predicate does not depend on the log-smooth model, on `‖·‖₀`, `ω` or the metrics of the `s_j`; every smooth semipositive metric on `L(ℂ)` over `X(ℂ)` qualifies; it is preserved by tensor products and by pull-back along every morphism `f : X″ → X′` from a log-smooth model `X″` (of another pair `(X₂, U₂)`, with boundary `D″`) that maps no component of `X″_ℚ` into `D` and the complement of `D″` into the complement of `D`, generically finite or not: for instance a generically finite morphism, a projective bundle over `X′`, a fibre product of projective bundles over `X′` (where `f^{−1}(D) = D″`), or the bundle of partial or full flags of a vector bundle on `X′`, which is smooth over `X′`, with `f^{−1}(D) = D″` and `m = 1` in the reparametrisation below. Regularisations pull back to regularisations, `C²` ones to `C²` ones, with the loss and the boundary rate multiplied by at most a constant (SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (d)); when `f^{−1}(D) ≠ ∅`, closeness is kept after the reparametrisation `t ↦ c₀t^m`, `m ≥ 1` the largest multiplicity of a component of `D″` in `f^*D` (`m = 1` for instance when `f_ℚ` is smooth and `D″ = f^{−1}(D)`), and without it when `m = 1` and the metrised canonical sections of `D″` are pulled back from `X′`, so that `|s″|² = |s∘f|²` (otherwise `m = 1` still needs `t ↦ c₀t`); when `f^{−1}(D) = ∅` (for instance a map into `U`), closeness is kept with the original parameter, the compact image of `X″(ℂ)` lying in `{|s|² ≥ t₁}` for some `t₁ > 0` (the formula `c₀t^m` would give the constant `c₀`; Check "a map into the interior");

(a) (*adelic line bundle*) for every regularisation `δ(t) → 0` as `t → 0`; hence, after blowing up `X′` along the reduced closed subscheme `X′ ∖ U` (which changes neither `U` nor `X′_ℚ`, where `X′_ℚ ∖ U_ℚ = D` is already a Cartier divisor), the model hermitian line bundles `(p^*L, ‖·‖_t)` form a boundary-Cauchy sequence for a boundary divisor `E₀ = (Ē, g₀)` in the sense of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §1.2 (`Ē` Cartier with support exactly `X′ ∖ U` and `Ē ≥ D̄`, the closure of `D`; `g₀` a Green function of `Ē` with `g₀ ≥ 1` and `g₀ − ½log(1/|s|²)` bounded near `D`), and define an adelic line bundle `L̄^{ad} ∈ P̂ic(U/ℤ)` (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §1.3) with metric `‖·‖` on `U(ℂ)`, independent of the regularisation and of the log-smooth model;

(b) (*intersection numbers*) for log-log singular semipositive `L̄₁, …, L̄_d` with regularisations on a common log-smooth model, the arithmetic intersection numbers `I_t = (L̄_{1,t}⋯L̄_{d,t})` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a) converge as `t → 0` to a real number `L̄₁⋯L̄_d` independent of the regularisations and of the log-smooth model of `(X, U)` (with the `L_i` pulled back); extended by multilinearity to log-log integrable bundles it is symmetric and multilinear, equals the model number when the metrics are smooth on `X(ℂ)`, and satisfies the projection formula `f^*L̄₁⋯f^*L̄_d = deg(f)·L̄₁⋯L̄_d` for generically finite morphisms `f : X″ → X′` of log-smooth models that map the complement of the boundary into the complement of the boundary, `deg f` being the degree of `f_ℚ` on generic fibres. The number is attached to the integral model and the integral line bundles, not to the generic fibre with its singular metrics: for generically finite morphisms `f_1 : X″ → X′_1`, `f_2 : X″ → X′_2` from one log-smooth model, the numbers on `X′_1` and `X′_2` satisfy `deg(f_1)·L̄^{(1)}_1⋯L̄^{(1)}_d = deg(f_2)·L̄^{(2)}_1⋯L̄^{(2)}_d` when, for each `i`, the pull-backs of `L^{(1)}_i` and `L^{(2)}_i` to `X″` are isomorphic by an isomorphism carrying one singular metric to the other; agreement on the generic fibre alone does not suffice (third Check). No identification of this number with the intersection numbers of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.1 of the adelic line bundles of (a) is asserted (nefness of the regularisations on curves inside the boundary is not guaranteed, so ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.1's convergence along nef approximations is not available for them in general), nor with the arithmetic intersection numbers of the theory of Burgos Gil, Kramer and Kühn for metrics with log-log singularities, whose Green objects are also subject to growth conditions on their derivatives;

(c) (*boundary mass and rate*) In (b), let `Ā` be an ample line bundle on `X′` with a smooth conjugation-invariant metric whose Chern form `ω_A = c₁(Ā)` is `≥ ω`, and `m` an integer `≥ sup_t ε_i(t)` for the given regularisations `L̄_{i,t}` (with losses `ε_i(t)`), so that every `N̄_{i,t} = L̄_{i,t} + mĀ` has a smooth semipositive metric. (i) (*boundary mass bound*) There are `C, ρ₀ > 0`, depending on `X′`, `D`, the metrics of the `s_j`, the `θ_i`, `Ā`, `m`, the constants of the predicate and an upper bound `C_reg` for the given regularisations (`φ_{i,t} ≤ C_reg` for all `i` and `t`), but not on `t`, such that every measure `μ = c₁(N̄₁) ∧ ⋯ ∧ c₁(N̄_{d−1})` on `X′(ℂ)`, each `N̄_j` being `Ā` or some `N̄_{i,t}` (any `i`, any `t`, independently for each `j`), satisfies `μ({|s|² < ρ}) ≤ C·log log(1/ρ)/log(1/ρ)` for `0 < ρ < ρ₀`. (ii) (*rate*) If the given regularisations `L̄_{i,t}` (those with which `I_t` is formed) satisfy `δ(t) ≤ C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)`, then `|I_t − L̄₁⋯L̄_d| ≤ C′·(log log(1/t))³/log(1/t)`. (iii) (*rate for close regularisations*) If the given regularisations are close, then `|I_t − L̄₁⋯L̄_d| ≤ C′·(log log(1/t))²/log(1/t)`. In (ii) and (iii), at `t = exp(−1/s²)` the bound is at most `C″·s` (at that `t`, `(log log(1/t))³/log(1/t) = 8s²log³(1/s) ≤ 10.8·s` and `(log log(1/t))²/log(1/t) = 4s²log²(1/s) ≤ 2.2·s` for `0 < s < 1`), so for a bound `C″·s` (ii) suffices and closeness is a refinement. `C″` depends only on the complex data (the complex manifold `X′(ℂ)`, `D`, the reference metrics, the weights and their regularisations), not on the integral model, and is uniform when the `L̄_i` range over sums of bounded multiples of finitely many given bundles. It is not uniform over covers: for a generically finite `f : X″ → X′` as in (b), with the bundles and regularisations pulled back, `I_t` and the limit on `X″` are `deg(f)` times those on `X′`, so `|I_t − lim|` is multiplied by `deg(f)`; over a tower of covers of growing degree the bound is uniform for the numbers divided by the degree. SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7(c) gives the bound on `δ(t)` and closeness for good metrics (SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §5.3), and more generally under `|dφ| ≤ C·(log(1/|s|²))^C/|s|` near `D`.

*Route.* (o) Apply the SNC compactification of ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §6.2 to `U_ℚ` with the closure `X_ℚ` (its local-isomorphism resolution §5.5, isomorphic over `U_ℚ ⊂ Reg(X_ℚ)`, then the principalisation §5.1 of the ideal of the reduced complement): a sequence of blow-ups of `X_ℚ` along coherent ideals with support in `X_ℚ ∖ U_ℚ`. Blowing up `X` along their closures, which lie in `X ∖ U`, gives `X′`. Two log-smooth models are dominated by a third (resolve the closure of the graph); `λ_s` and `ℓ_s` change under pullback and under a change of the metrics of the `s_j` by bounded factors, and the comparison with ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.1 is the one-variable model of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.1 with `n = 0` on the inverse side. Pull-back of the predicate and of regularisations is SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (d) on complex points, applied to `γ = 2πθ` and `h = f(ℂ)` with `D′ = D″`. Its pull-back clauses hold for EVERY family of smooth functions with `φ ≤ φ_t ≤ C` and some loss `ε(t)`, so they apply to the regularisations of this section, which converge only locally uniformly and whose loss tends to `0` at an arbitrary rate (not only to the families of its (b)): the pulled-back family satisfies `φ∘f ≤ φ_t∘f ≤ C`, `φ_t∘f ≥ −C·log log(1/t)`, has loss `≥ −C·ε(t)ω″` (`f^*ω ≤ Cω″`), is non-increasing, converges locally uniformly (in `C²` for `C²` regularisations) on compact subsets of the complement of `D″` (they map to compact subsets of `U(ℂ)`), and has boundary rate `≤ C·δ(t)` (`λ_{s∘f} ≤ C·λ_{s″}`, from `|s∘f|² ≥ c₀|s″|^{2m}`); and `φ∘f ≥ −C·ℓ_{s″} − C`. Closeness in the sense of this section, with the error `E(t) = C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)` on `{|s|² ≥ t}` (weaker than the error `C·t^{1/3}` of SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (c)), is kept after `t ↦ c₀t^m` by the closeness clause of SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7 (d): `{|s″|² ≥ t} ⊆ {|s∘f|² ≥ c₀t^m}`, and `E(c₀t^m) ≤ E(t)` for `m ≥ 1`, `0 < c₀ ≤ 1` and `t ≤ e^{−e}` (`log y/y` decreases on `[e, ∞)`); the reparametrised family is again a regularisation (loss `C·ε(c₀t^m) → 0`, lower bound `−C·log log(1/t) − C`). It needs only that `f(ℂ)` is holomorphic, maps no component into `D` and has `f^{−1}(D) ⊆ D″`, not that `f` is generically finite; the pulled-back data stay conjugation-invariant since `f` is defined over `ℚ`. (a) On `{|s|² < τ}`, `0 ≤ φ_t − φ ≤ C + C·ℓ_s`, so the ratio is at most `(C + C·log(1 + log(1/τ)))/(1 + log(1/τ))`; on the compact set `{|s|² ≥ τ}` it tends to `0` with `t` by local uniform convergence; choose `τ` small, then `t`. Compare directly with the Cartier boundary divisor `E₀`: the model divisor `(0, (φ_t − φ_{t′})/2)` has zero finite part and `Ē` is effective, so `−εE₀ ≤ (0, (φ_t − φ_{t′})/2) ≤ εE₀` as soon as `|φ_t − φ_{t′}| ≤ 2ε·g₀` on `U(ℂ)`. Since `g₀ ≥ 1` and `g₀ − ½log(1/|s|²)` is bounded near `D`, `g₀ ≥ c·λ_s` on `U(ℂ)` for a constant `c > 0`, while `0 ≤ φ_t − φ ≤ δ(t)λ_s` and `0 ≤ φ_{t′} − φ ≤ δ(t′)λ_s` give `|φ_t − φ_{t′}| ≤ max(δ(t), δ(t′))·λ_s`; so the boundary norm of the difference is at most `C·max(δ(t), δ(t′))`, and the sequence is Cauchy for `E₀`. The closure `D̄` of `D` is not used as a model divisor: it need not be Cartier, even after the blow-up (ninth Check); two regularisations differ by a boundary-null sequence; pullback to a dominating model is a regularisation there. (b) Fix an ample `A` on `X′` with a smooth metric of curvature `≥ ω`, and an integer `m ≥ max(sup_t ε(t), m₀)`, `m₀` chosen below. The bundles `N̄_{i,t} = L̄_{i,t} + mĀ` are smooth and semipositive, and `I_t` is a fixed linear combination, by multilinearity, of the numbers `J_t = (N̄_{i_1,t}⋯N̄_{i_k,t}·Ā^{d−k})`. *Monotonicity:* each `J_t` is non-increasing as `t` decreases: for `t′ < t` the difference telescopes into terms `((0, (φ_{i,t} − φ_{i,t′})/2)·N̄⋯N̄·Ā⋯Ā)`, each a positive multiple of `∫(φ_{i,t} − φ_{i,t′})·c₁(N̄)⋯c₁(N̄)c₁(Ā)⋯ ≥ 0`. Only the `J_t` are monotone; `I_t`, a combination of them with coefficients of both signs, need not be: on `ℙ¹_ℤ` the trivial bundle with limit weight `0` and the weights `2^{−j}f_j` (`f_j = 1` for even `j`, a fixed smooth nonconstant conjugation-invariant `f` with `1 ≤ f ≤ 2` for odd `j`) has decreasing weights with losses tending to `0`, and its self-intersections are `0` for even `j` and `−(4^{−j}/4π)∫i∂f ∧ ∂̄f < 0` for odd `j` (Cartier recursion along the section `1`). *Finiteness* (for `D ≠ ∅`; for `D = ∅` the weights are smooth and there is nothing to prove): put `P_τ = log(1/(|s|² + τ))` and `ψ_τ = −C·log P_τ` for `0 ≤ τ ≤ 1/4` (as `|s|² < 1/4`, `|s|² + τ < 1/2` and `P_τ > log 2`, so `ψ_τ` is defined and the division by `P_τ` below is uniform; for `|s|² + τ ≥ 1` it would not be), with `C` so large that `ψ₀ ≤ φ` (`φ ≥ −C·ℓ_s`, and `ℓ_s ≤ 6·log log(1/|s|²)` where `|s|² < 1/4`). Then `i∂∂̄ψ_τ = C·i∂∂̄log(|s|² + τ)/P_τ + C·i∂P_τ ∧ ∂̄P_τ/P_τ²`, and the first term is `≥ −C′ω` uniformly in `τ`; so `ψ_τ` is `(θ + mω)`-plurisubharmonic for `m ≥ m₀(θ, C, s)`, and `ψ_τ` decreases to `ψ₀ ≤ φ` as `τ ↓ 0`. For fixed `t` and `η > 0`, the closed sets `{ψ_τ ≥ φ_t + η}` decrease with `τ` and have empty intersection, so `ψ_τ ≤ φ_t + η` on the compact `X′(ℂ)` for small `τ` (Dini), and monotonicity bounds each `J_t` below by the limit as `τ → 0` of the numbers with every weight replaced by `ψ_τ`, up to `O(η)`. These are bounded below uniformly in `τ`: their difference from the number for a fixed `τ₀` is a sum of integrals of `ψ_τ − ψ_{τ₀} ≥ −C·ℓ_s − C` against mixed measures in which `i∂∂̄ψ_τ` is a bounded form plus a rank-one form `C·(f_τ + g_τ)·iξ ∧ ξ̄`, with `ξ = ∂log|s|²` independent of `τ`: locally `|s|² = |σ|²e^{−a}` with `σ` a local equation of `D` and `a` smooth, and off `D` `i∂∂̄log(|s|² + τ) = (|s|²τ/(|s|² + τ)²)·iξ ∧ ξ̄ − (|s|²/(|s|² + τ))·i∂∂̄a`, so the two terms of `i∂∂̄ψ_τ` contribute `g_τ = (|s|²τ/(|s|² + τ)²)/P_τ` (from the first; `g_τ ≤ 1/(4P_τ)` is bounded, and at `|s|² = τ` it equals `1/(4·log(1/(2τ)))`, but the form `g_τ·iξ ∧ ξ̄` is not bounded: near a point where `D = {z₁ = 0}`, its coefficient of `i dz₁ ∧ dz̄₁` at `|s|² = τ` is `≍ 1/(τ·log(1/τ))`, since `|ξ|²` is of the order of `1/|s|²` there) and `f_τ = (|s|²/(|s|² + τ))²/P_τ²` (from the second), the rest being bounded. Since `ξ ∧ ξ = 0`, each mixed measure contains that rank-one form at most once. Near a point where `D = {z₁⋯z_k = 0}`, `iξ ∧ ξ̄ ≤ C·Σ_j i dz_j ∧ dz̄_j/|z_j|² + Cω`, and `ℓ_s ≤ C + C·log log(1/|s|²)`. For the integral along one `z_j`, write `u = |s|²`, which for fixed other coordinates is a constant multiple of `|z_j|²` up to a bounded factor, so that `i dz_j ∧ dz̄_j/|z_j|²` becomes a multiple of `du/u`. Where `u ≥ τ`: `f_τ ≤ C/log²(1/u)` and `g_τ ≤ τ/(uP_τ)`, and `∫ ℓ·du/(u log²(1/u)) < ∞`, `∫_τ^{c} ℓ·τ du/(u²P_τ) ≤ C` (`ℓ/P_τ` is bounded); where `u < τ`: `f_τ ≤ (u/τ)²/P_τ²` and `g_τ ≤ u/(τP_τ)`, and `(τP_τ)^{−1}∫_0^τ ℓ du ≤ C` (with `ℓ ≤ C·log log(1/u)`). So each integral is bounded uniformly in `τ`. So every `J_t` converges to a real number, and so does `I_t`. *Independence:* the `φ_t` are quasi-plurisubharmonic with a uniform bound for the loss, so they decrease to the quasi-plurisubharmonic extension of `φ` everywhere on `X′(ℂ)` (SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7(a)); for two regularisations `φ_t`, `φ′_{t′}` and `η > 0`, the closed sets `{φ′_{t′} ≥ φ_t + η}` decrease with `t′` and have empty intersection, so `φ′_{t′} ≤ φ_t + η` for small `t′` (Dini), and monotonicity gives `lim J′ ≤ J_t + O(η)`; exchange the roles. Multilinearity, the smooth case and the projection formula pass to the limit from the model statements (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a; ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.1 for the degree factor). (c)(i) is SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.8(b) on `X′(ℂ)` for the log-log weights `(θ_i, φ_i)` (`ℓ_s ≤ C·log R_s` with `R_s = e + log(1/|s|²)`): its families of regularisations are the `φ_{i,t}` (`φ_i ≤ φ_{i,t} ≤ C`, `θ_i + (i/2π)∂∂̄φ_{i,t} ≥ −ε_i(t)ω ≥ −mω_A`), taken with the Kähler form `ω_A` and with `m` in place of its `m′`, so that its mixed measures are the `μ` of (i); its constant does not depend on the parameters. The proof there is an energy inequality for bounded potentials, `∫log(1/|s|²)·∏(ω_i + (i/2π)∂∂̄v_i) ≤ C(1 + M)` for `sup v_i − inf v_i ≤ M` (Poincaré–Lelong, one factor at a time), followed by truncation of the potentials at the level `C₁·log R + 2` and Markov's inequality. (c)(ii) `|I_t − lim| ≤ Σ|J_t − lim J|`, and by monotonicity each `J_t − lim J ≥ 0` is the limit as `t′ → 0` of sums of integrals of `φ_{i,t} − φ_{i,t′} ≤ φ_{i,t} − φ_i` against mixed measures of the kind in (i). Split at `|s|² = t`. Where `|s|² ≥ t` the integrand is at most `δ(t)·λ_s`, and the layer-cake formula with (i) gives `∫_{|s|² ≥ t} λ_s dμ ≤ C·(log log(1/t))²`, so this part is `≤ C·δ(t)·(log log(1/t))²`. Where `|s|² < t` the integrand is at most `C + C·ℓ_s`, and the same formula gives `∫_{|s|²<t}(C + C·ℓ_s)dμ ≤ C·(log log(1/t))²/log(1/t)`. With the bound on `δ(t)` the total is `≤ C′·(log log(1/t))³/log(1/t)`, which is `O(s²log³(1/s))` at `t = exp(−1/s²)`. (c)(iii) The same splitting: where `|s|² ≥ t`, closeness gives `0 ≤ φ_{i,t} − φ_{i,t′} ≤ φ_{i,t} − φ_i ≤ C·log log(1/t)/log(1/t)`, against a mixed measure of bounded mass; where `|s|² < t`, `R_s ≥ log(1/t)` and the integrand is at most `C·log R_s`, and the second bound of SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.8(b) gives `∫_{R_s ≥ log(1/t)} log R_s dμ ≤ C·(log log(1/t))²/log(1/t)`. Without closeness the bulk costs `δ(t)·∫_{|s|²≥t}λ_s dμ`, one factor `log log(1/t)` more, which is (ii).

## Layer 4: Arithmetic volumes and heights of projective bundles

This layer compares the arithmetic volume of a log-log singular semipositive bundle with its regularised volumes and with its limit self-intersection, and computes the height of the projective bundle of a Hermitian vector bundle over `O_K`, with the absolute Siegel lemma as a consequence.

### 4.3a Arithmetic volumes of log-log singular line bundles

Prove, in the setting of 0.4 with `L̄` log-log singular semipositive, `vol^(L̄)` the volume of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §4.2 of the adelic line bundle `L̄^{ad}` of 0.4(a), `Ā` as in the route of 0.4(b) and `L̄^d` the number of 0.4(b):

(a) for every regularisation, `vol^(L̄) = lim_{t→0} vol^(L̄_t)`, the model volumes (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.3) being non-increasing as `t` decreases; in particular `vol^(L̄) ≤ vol^(L̄_t)` for every `t`;

(b) if `p^*L` is nef on every closed fibre `X′_{𝔽_p}`, `p` prime (then `p^*L_ℚ` is nef on `X′_ℚ`: a curve in `X′_ℚ` has a closure in `X′` flat over an open of `Spec ℤ`, whose special fibres are `1`-cycles of the same degree), then `vol^(L̄) ≥ L̄^d` (Moriwaki's inequality, the generalised Hodge index theorem);

(c) (a) and (b) hold on every log-smooth model and for every integral structure: in particular when the lattice of `L` at finitely many primes is replaced by another lattice with the same generic fibre, provided the hypotheses hold on the fibres of that model;

(d) (*the two minima statements without ampleness*) let `M̄` be a hermitian line bundle with a smooth semipositive metric on a projective arithmetic variety `X` of dimension `d`, with `M` nef on every closed fibre, `deg = deg_{X_ℚ}(M^{d−1})` and `e ∈ ℝ`. *Lower:* if every closed point `x` of `X_ℚ` has height `h_{M̄}(x) ≥ e`, then `M̄^d ≥ d·e·deg`. *Upper:* if the points with `h_{M̄}(x) ≤ e` are Zariski dense in `X_ℚ`, then `M̄^d ≤ d·e·deg`. Neither assumes `M_ℚ` ample or big. The lower half is the nef form of Zhang's theorem; the upper half is a consequence of the volume inequality (b) (for `M_ℚ` ample both are in HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17f, Zhang's theorem).

The number of 0.4(b) is not claimed to be a volume: no hypothesis making the regularisations nef along the boundary is available for the metrics of 0.4 with unbounded weights (along a regularisation, the height of a boundary point tends to `−∞` when the quasi-plurisubharmonic extension of the weight is `−∞` at one of its complex points, as at the cusps for the Hodge line of a modular curve; at boundary points near which the weight is bounded it can have a finite limit, for instance on `ℙ¹` with boundary `{0, ∞}` and a weight with a log-log pole at `0` and smooth near `∞`).

*Route.* (a) The regularisations converge to `L̄^{ad}` in the boundary topology (0.4(a)), so ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §4.2 (volume as the limit of the model volumes along any representing sequence) gives the limit; `φ_t` decreases with `t`, so the metrics `‖·‖_t` increase, the sets of sections of sup norm `≤ 1` shrink, and the model volumes decrease. (b) First for a smooth semipositive `M̄` with `M` nef on every fibre: for rational `ε > 0`, `M + εA` is ample, HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17d gives `χ̂(H⁰(n(M̄ + εĀ))) = (M̄ + εĀ)^d·n^d/d! + o(n^d)`, Minkowski's theorem gives `ĥ⁰ ≥ χ̂ + o(n^d)`, so `vol^(M̄ + εĀ) ≥ (M̄ + εĀ)^d`, and `ε → 0` by ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.3(3). For `L̄`: `L̄_t + εĀ` is smooth, semipositive once `ε(t) ≤ ε`, and nef on every fibre, so `vol^(L̄_t + εĀ) ≥ (L̄_t + εĀ)^d`; by (a) for `L̄ + εĀ` and 0.4(b), `vol^(L̄^{ad} + εĀ) = lim_t vol^(L̄_t + εĀ) ≥ lim_t (L̄_t + εĀ)^d = (L̄ + εĀ)^d`; let `ε → 0` through rationals, with the continuity of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §4.3(3) on the left and multilinearity on the right. (c) The statements and routes use only the model through its fibres and the generic fibre. (d) Write `M̄(−c)` for `M̄` with its metric multiplied by `e^{c}`; `M̄(−c)^d = M̄^d − d·c·deg`. *Lower:* `M̄(−e)` is semipositive, of nonnegative degree on vertical curves (nef on the fibres) and on horizontal curves (heights `≥ 0`), hence nef in the sense of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.1, and nef bundles have nonnegative self-intersection (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.1). *Upper:* if `M̄^d > d·e″·deg` for some `e″ > e`, then `M̄(−e″)` has positive self-intersection and satisfies the hypotheses of (b), so `vol^(M̄(−e″)) > 0` and some power has a non-zero section `s` of sup norm `≤ 1`; every point off `div s` then has `h_{M̄(−e″)} ≥ 0`, i.e. `h_{M̄} ≥ e″ > e`, against density. The order matters: the upper half rests on the volume inequality.

*Needs:* 0.4; ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §§0.1, 0.3, 3.1, 4.2–4.3; HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §§5.17d, 5.17f.

**Checks.**

- `X = Spec ℤ` (`d = 1`, no boundary), `L̄ = O(c)`: `vol^ = max(c, 0)`, and (b) reads `max(c, 0) ≥ c`; equality fails for `c < 0`, where `O(c)` is semipositive and not nef, so a statement "`vol^ = L̄^d`" would need nefness.

### 4.3b Heights of projective bundles and the absolute Siegel lemma

*Setting.* Let `K` be a number field and `Ē` a hermitian vector bundle of rank `n ≥ 1` over `O_K` (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.1), with normalised slope `μ̂(Ē) = deĝ(Ē)/([K:ℚ]·n)` (the slope `deĝ(Ē)/n` of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.3, divided by `[K:ℚ]`). Let `π : P → Spec O_K` be the scheme of lines in `E` (`P = Proj Sym E^∨`), `O(−1) ⊂ π^*E` the tautological line with the metric induced from `Ē`, and `Ō(1)` its dual: a smooth semipositive metric, relatively ample. A closed point of `P_K` is a line `ℓ ⊂ E_L` over a finite extension `L/K`, and `h_{Ō(1)}(ℓ) = −deĝ_n(ℓ̄)`, `ℓ̄ = ℓ ∩ E_{O_L}` with the induced metrics (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.12). `H_n = Σ_{k=1}^{n} 1/k`.

Prove: (a) (*height of the projective bundle*) `Ō(1)^n = [K:ℚ]·n·(−μ̂(Ē) + ½(H_n − 1))` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a); equivalently, the normalised height of `P_K` for `Ō(1)` is `−μ̂(Ē) + ½Σ_{k=2}^{n} 1/k`;

(b) (*the absolute Siegel lemma*) for every `η > 0` there are a finite extension `L/K` and `x ∈ E ⊗ O_L`, `x ≠ 0`, with `mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ −μ̂(Ē) + ½Σ_{k=2}^{n} 1/k + η`, the mean `(1/[L:ℚ])Σ_σ` over the complex embeddings of `L`; in particular `−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≥ μ̂(Ē) − ½log n − η`. The constant grows like `½log n`, while the product form of DiophantineApproximationAndTranscendence §2.5 gives `((n − 1)/2)·log 2` for the first minimum;

(c) (*the first absolute minimum*) in the notation of SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §§2.1 and 4.3 (`μ̂_max(Ē)`, the maximal destabilising sub-bundle, of rank `s₁`, and the first absolute minimum `λ^abs(Ē)`): `log λ^abs(Ē) ≤ −μ̂_max(Ē) + ½(H_{s₁} − 1)`. For `s₁ ≥ 2` this is smaller than the constant `((s₁ − 1)/2)·log 2` of SlopesOfHermitianVectorBundles §4.3 (`½(H_s − 1) ≤ (s − 1)/4 < ((s − 1)/2)·log 2`); for `s₁ = 1` both are `0`.

*Route.* (a) Under a finite extension `K′/K` both sides of the displayed identity are multiplied by `[K′:K]` (the left by the `[K′:K]` scaling of HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a, the right because `μ̂` is invariant, ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §1.6, and `[K′:ℚ] = [K′:K][K:ℚ]`), so their quotients by `n·[K:ℚ]`, the normalised height of `P_K` and `−μ̂ + ½(H_n − 1)`, are invariant (for `Ē = ℤ²` over `ℚ` the quotient by `[K:ℚ]` alone is `½`, the normalised height `¼`), and it suffices to prove (a) after a finite extension. After a finite extension `E` is free: `E ≅ O_K^{n−1} ⊕ I`, and if `I^h = (a)` with `h` the class number, `I·O_{K′}` is generated by `a^{1/h}` in `K′ = K(a^{1/h})`. With a basis, `P = ℙ^{n−1}`, the metrics are `‖x‖_σ = |A_σx|` with `A_σ ∈ GL_n(ℂ)` (`A_{σ̄}` the complex conjugate of `A_σ`), and the metric of `Ō(1)_A` is `e^{−f}` times the standard one, `f = log(|A_σx|/|x|)` at `σ`. *Standard metric:* for `n = 1`, `P = Spec O_K` with `Ō(1)` the trivial metrised line, `Ō(1)^1 = 0`. For `n ≥ 2` the Cartier recursion along the coordinate section `x_n` of `O(1)` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a, with `−∫log‖x_n‖`) gives `Ō(1)^n_{ℙ^{n−1}} = Ō(1)^{n−1}_{ℙ^{n−2}} − Σ_σ∫_{ℙ^{n−1}(ℂ)} log(|x_n|/|x|)·c₁(Ō(1))^{n−1}`, and the integral is `−½H_{n−1}` (for a uniform unit vector `u` of `ℂ^n`, `|u_n|²` has the Beta law `B(1, n − 1)`, with mean logarithm `−H_{n−1}`); so `Ō(1)^n_{std} = [K:ℚ]·½Σ_{m=1}^{n−1}H_m = [K:ℚ]·(n/2)(H_n − 1)`. *Change of metric:* the archimedean term `Ō(1)_A^n − Ō(1)^n_{std}` is a sum over `σ` of terms depending only on `(A_σ^*A_σ)^{1/2}` and invariant under unitary conjugation, so it suffices to take `A_σ = exp(T)`, `T` real diagonal; along `exp(sT)` the derivative of the term at `σ` is `n·∫ Σ_i t_i|y_i|²/|y|²·ω_FS(y)^{n−1} = tr T` (`y = exp(sT)x`, each `|y_i|²/|y|²` having integral `1/n`), so the term is `log|det A_σ|`. Since `deĝ(Ē) = −Σ_σ log|det A_σ|`, `Ō(1)_A^n = [K:ℚ]·(n/2)(H_n − 1) − deĝ(Ē)`, which is (a). (b) Put `e = −μ̂(Ē) + ½(H_n − 1) + η`. If every closed point of `P_K` had `h_{Ō(1)} ≥ e`, then `Ō(1)(−e)`, which is ample and semipositive, would have only points of nonnegative height, and `pointwiseNonnegative_cycleNonnegative` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17f) would give `Ō(1)^n − n·e·[K:ℚ] ≥ 0`, against (a). So some line `ℓ ⊂ E_L` has `−deĝ_n(ℓ̄) < e`; after a further finite extension `ℓ̄ = O_{L′}·x` (as in (a)), and then `−deĝ_n(ℓ̄) = mean_σ log‖x‖_σ`. Finally `H_n − 1 ≤ log n`. (c) Apply (b) to the maximal destabilising sub-bundle `F̄` of `Ē` (rank `s₁`, normalised slope `μ̂_max(Ē)`, with the restricted metrics): its vectors have the same height in `F̄` and in `Ē`, the height of an integral vector is at most `mean_σ log‖x‖_σ` (the finite places contribute `≤ 0`), and `η` is arbitrary.

## Layer 14: Reflection formula and arithmetic flag varieties over ℤ

This layer proves the reflection formula for `ℙ¹`-bundles, which determines the even part of the arithmetic self-intersection along the fibre by geometric degrees, for metrics induced from the rank-two bundle, for arbitrary smooth metrics on the relative tangent bundle and for the limit numbers of Layer 0, and computes arithmetic intersection numbers of products of flag varieties over `ℤ`.

### 14.1 The reflection formula for arithmetic projective-line bundles

*Objects.* A *hermitian vector bundle* `V̄ = (V, h)` on a projective arithmetic variety `Z` is a locally free sheaf `V` of rank `r` with a smooth conjugation-invariant hermitian metric on `V(ℂ)` over `Z(ℂ)` (smooth in the sense of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.1: locally the restriction of a smooth metric under local embeddings of `Z(ℂ)`); sub-bundles and quotients carry the induced metrics, `det V̄` the determinant metric. For `rk V = 2`, `π : Y = ℙ(V) → Z` is the bundle of lines in `V`, `S = O(−1) ⊂ π^*V` the tautological line with the induced metric, `Q = π^*V/S` with the quotient metric, and `Ξ̄ = T_{Y/Z} = Hom(S, Q)` with the induced metric; `Ξ ≅ S^{−2} ⊗ π^*det V` isometrically, of degree `2` on the fibres. For a vector bundle `E` of rank `N ≥ 2` on `Z` and `1 ≤ a < N`, the bundle of full flags `0 = F_0 ⊂ F_1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ F_N = E` of sub-bundles is the bundle `ℙ(F_{a+1}/F_{a−1})` of lines over the bundle of partial flags with the step `a` omitted, with tautological line `F_a/F_{a−1}`; the lines `F_i/F_{i−1}` (`i ∉ {a, a+1}`) and `det(F_{a+1}/F_{a−1})` are pulled back from that base, and the dual of the root line `(F_a/F_{a−1}) ⊗ (F_{a+1}/F_a)^{−1}` is the relative tangent line `Ξ = Hom(F_a/F_{a−1}, F_{a+1}/F_a)`. Provide this as API: the hermitian bundles, `ℙ(V̄)` with `S̄`, `Q̄`, `Ξ̄`, base change and the isometry `Ξ̄ ≅ S̄^{−2} ⊗ π^*det V̄`.

Prove: (a) let `Z` be a projective arithmetic variety, `V̄` a hermitian vector bundle of rank two on `Z`, `π : Y = ℙ(V) → Z`, `D = dim Y_ℚ`, `M̄` a hermitian `ℚ`-line bundle on `Z` with smooth metric, and for real `u` put `ĥ(u) = (π^*M̄ + (u/2)Ξ̄)^{D+1}` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a). Let `g_n` be the coefficients of the geometric degree polynomial, `Σ_n g_n X^n = deg_{Y_ℚ}((π^*M + (X/2)Ξ)^D)`. Then `ĥ(u) + ĥ(−u) = Σ_{k≥1} (D + 1)·(κ(k)/k)·g_{2k−1}·u^{2k}`, `κ(k) = ½Σ_{j=0}^{k−1} 1/(2j + 1)`; equivalently, for every `k ≥ 1` with `2k ≤ D + 1`, `(Ξ̄^{2k}·π^*M̄^{D+1−2k}) = 2κ(k)·deg_{Y_ℚ}(Ξ^{2k−1}·π^*M^{D+1−2k}) = 4κ(k)·deg_{Z_ℚ}(disc(V)^{k−1}·M^{D+1−2k})`, `disc(V) = c₁(V)² − 4c₂(V)`: the even part of the arithmetic self-intersection along the fibre is determined by geometric degrees. The orientation is part of the statement: `Ξ` has degree `+2` on the fibres; replacing `Ξ̄` by its dual leaves the left side unchanged and changes the sign of the right side. The same holds, with the same proof, for `M̄` replaced by a product of `D + 1 − 2k` hermitian line bundles on `Z`.

(b) (*arbitrary smooth metrics on the relative tangent bundle*) Keep `Z`, `V`, `π`, `D` and `M̄` as in (a), and give `Ξ` any smooth conjugation-invariant metric `‖·‖′`, not necessarily induced by a metric on `V`. On `W = Y ×_Z Y`, with projections `q₀, q₁` and diagonal `Δ_Y`, put `A′ = q₀^*Ξ̄′`, `B′ = q₁^*Ξ̄′`; the section `v` of the route below satisfies `v^{⊗2} ∈ H⁰(W, A ⊗ B)` with divisor `2Δ_Y`, and `log‖v‖′ = ½log‖v^{⊗2}‖_{A′⊗B′}`. Then for every `k ≥ 1` with `2k ≤ D + 1` and `r = D + 1 − 2k`, `(Ξ̄′^{2k}·π^*M̄^r) = −Σ_{i<k}∫_{W(ℂ)} log‖v‖′·c₁(A′ − B′)∧c₁(A′)^{2i}∧c₁(B′)^{2k−1−2i}∧c₁(M̄)^r`. For the metric induced by `V̄` the right side is the value computed in (a); in general it is an explicit archimedean integral.

(c) (*the reflection formula for limit numbers*) Keep `Z`, `V`, `π`, `D`, `W`, `v` as in (a), (b), let `U ⊂ Z` be a dense open subscheme, flat over `ℤ`, with `U_ℚ` smooth, and suppose that `Z` is a log-smooth model of `(Z, U)` in the sense of 0.4: `Z_ℚ` is smooth and its boundary `D_Z = Z_ℚ ∖ U_ℚ` is a simple normal crossings divisor. Then `Y` and `W` are log-smooth models of their pairs with the preimages of `U`, their boundaries `π^{−1}D_Z` and its preimage in `W` are simple normal crossings divisors, and `Δ_Y ⊂ W` meets the boundary with normal crossings. Let (i) `M̄` be a hermitian `ℚ`-line bundle on `Z` whose metric is log-log integrable along `D_Z` (0.4); (ii) `Ξ` carry a metric `‖·‖` on `Y(ℂ) ∖ π^{−1}D_Z`, log-log integrable along `π^{−1}D_Z`; (iii) on an open subset of `Z(ℂ) ∖ D_Z` whose complement has measure zero, the restriction of `‖·‖` to each fibre `ℙ(V_z)` be induced (through `Ξ ≅ S^{−2} ⊗ π^*det V`) by a positive definite hermitian form on `V_z` depending smoothly on `z`. No hypothesis on regularisations is made: the numbers of 0.4(b) do not depend on them. Then for every `k ≥ 1` with `2k ≤ D + 1` and `r = D + 1 − 2k`, the numbers of 0.4(b) satisfy `(Ξ̄^{2k}·π^*M̄^r) = 2κ(k)·deg_{Y_ℚ}(Ξ^{2k−1}·π^*M^r)`, and the formula of (a) for `ĥ(u) + ĥ(−u)` holds for these numbers; the same holds for `M̄` replaced by a product of `r` such hermitian line bundles on `Z`. This is the form in which the reflection formula applies to metrics that are log-log integrable along a boundary and fibrewise round, for instance Hodge metrics on toroidal models; no arithmetic intersection theory for log-singular metrics beyond the limit numbers of 0.4 is used.

*Route.* (a) Fibre square. Let `W = Y ×_Z Y`, of absolute dimension `D + 2`, with projections `q₀, q₁` and diagonal `Δ_Y ⊂ W`; write `I_Y`, `I_W` for the intersection numbers, `A = q₀^*Ξ̄`, `B = q₁^*Ξ̄`, `M̄_W` the pullback of `M̄`. (i) `I_W` vanishes on `D + 2` classes all pulled back by `q₁`, and (ii) `I_W(B, q₀^*ℓ̄_1, …, q₀^*ℓ̄_{D+1}) = 2·I_Y(ℓ̄_1, …, ℓ̄_{D+1})`, since `B` has degree `2` on the fibres of `q₀`: both are the composition and projection formulas for the Deligne pairing along the `ℙ¹`-bundles `q₁, q₀ : W → Y` (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.3(3), in the form for the composite pairing, which does not need `W` regular), with ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §3.1 identifying the numbers with those of HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a. (iii) The section `v = x ∧ y` of `q₀^*S^{−1} ⊗ q₁^*S^{−1} ⊗ det V` vanishes exactly on `Δ_Y` (this needs `V` locally free on `Z`), so `O(Δ_Y)²` is isometric to `A ⊗ B` with `‖v‖` the sine of the angle between the two lines, `‖v‖ ≤ 1`; `A` and `B` agree isometrically on `Δ_Y`; the Cartier recursion along `v` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a) gives, for every `D` classes `T` each pulled back by `q₀` or by `q₁`, `I_W(A², T) − I_W(B², T) = I_W((A − B)(A + B), T) = −2∫_{W(ℂ)} log‖v‖·c₁(A − B)∧c₁(T)` (the term on `Δ_Y` vanishes since `(A − B)|_{Δ_Y}` is trivial). (iv) By (ii), then (iii) applied `k` times with `T = A^{2i}B^{2k−1−2i}M̄_W^{r}`, `r = D + 1 − 2k`, then (i): `I_Y(Ξ̄^{2k}, π^*M̄^r) = ½I_W(B·A^{2k}·M̄_W^r) = −Σ_{i<k} ∫_{W(ℂ)} log‖v‖·c₁(A − B)∧c₁(A)^{2i}∧c₁(B)^{2k−1−2i}∧c₁(M̄)^r`. (v) Fibre integration. At a point of `Z(ℂ)` write the trace-free part of `(i/2π)` times the curvature of `V̄` in a normal frame as `Σ_j a_jσ_j` (Pauli matrices, `a` a triple of real `(1,1)`-forms; `disc(V)` is represented by `4⟨a, a⟩`); on the fibre `ℙ¹ × ℙ¹ = S² × S²` the Chern forms of `A`, `B` are `2ω_FS − 2⟨a, n⟩`-type expressions in the points `n₁, n₂ ∈ S²` of the two lines, `log‖v‖ = ½log((1 − ⟨n₁, n₂⟩)/2)`, and the fibre integral of the sum in (iv) is an `SO(3)`-invariant polynomial of degree `2k − 2` in `a`, hence `c·⟨a, a⟩^{k−1}`; the constant is computed by expanding `½log((1 − z)/2)` in Legendre polynomials (Funk–Hecke), or, for all `k` at once, on split bundles `V̄ = O ⊕ N̄`, where `∫_{−1}^{1}log((1 − u)/2)·u^{2k−2}du = −(2/(2k − 1))Σ_{j<k}1/(2j + 1)`. The result, after integration against the closed form `c₁(M̄)^r` over `Z(ℂ)` (when `Z(ℂ)` is singular, the fibre integrals and this integral are taken on a resolution of `Z_ℚ`, ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §5.5, to which `V̄` and `M̄` pull back), is `4κ(k)·deg_{Z_ℚ}(disc(V)^{k−1}·M^r) = 2κ(k)·deg(Ξ^{2k−1}·π^*M^r)` (Chern–Weil for `V̄` on that resolution). (vi) Summing the even terms of the binomial expansion of `(π^*M̄ ± (u/2)Ξ̄)^{D+1}` and using `C(D+1, 2k)/C(D, 2k−1) = (D+1)/(2k)` gives the formula. The square uses only that the fibration is a `ℙ¹`-bundle with a metric on the relative tangent bundle that is round on each fibre and that the diagonal is cut out exactly on the integral model. (b) Steps (i)–(iv) use no property of the metric on `Ξ`: (i), (ii) hold for any metrics; in (iii), `v^{⊗2}` is a section of `A ⊗ B` (as `Ξ ≅ S^{−2} ⊗ π^*det V`) with divisor `2Δ_Y`, the Cartier recursion along it with the metric of `A′ ⊗ B′` gives `I_W((A′ − B′)(A′ + B′), T) = −2∫_{W(ℂ)} log‖v‖′·c₁(A′ − B′)∧c₁(T)`, and the term on `Δ_Y` vanishes because `A′` and `B′` both restrict to `Ξ̄′` on `Δ_Y ≅ Y`; (iv) is then the same telescoping. Only (v) uses the round metric. (c) The numbers of 0.4(b) do not depend on the regularisations, so any may be used. Write `M̄` and `Ξ̄` as differences of log-log singular semipositive metrics; regularise the pieces of `M̄` on `Z(ℂ)` and those of `Ξ̄` on `Y(ℂ)` by SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7(b) (monotone, `C²`), and pull the former back to `Y` by `π`, and all of them to `W` by `q₀`, `q₁`, by 0.4(o) and SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.7(d): `π`, `q₀`, `q₁` and `W → Z` are smooth and the boundaries are the preimages of `D_Z`, so `m = 1`. Take the metrised canonical sections of the boundary components of `Y` and `W` pulled back from `Z`; then `|s|`, and so `R_s`, on `W` is the same function whether a weight comes through `q₀` or through `q₁`, and all pulled-back families are monotone `C²` families of regularisations of log-log weights for one boundary divisor of `W(ℂ)`. Fix first the reference: regard `v` as a section of `N = q₀^*S^{−1} ⊗ q₁^*S^{−1} ⊗ det V`, whose square is `A ⊗ B`; choose the fixed smooth metric `‖·‖₀` of `Ξ` so small (multiply it by a constant) that the smooth metric `‖·‖_N` of `N` whose square is the metric of `A₀ ⊗ B₀` has `‖v‖_N ≤ 1` on the compact `W(ℂ)`, and only then define the weights `ψ` of `Ξ̄` and their regularisations `ψ_t` relative to this `‖·‖₀`. For each `t` apply (b) to the smooth metrics `‖·‖_t = e^{−ψ_t/2}‖·‖₀` of `Ξ` (`ψ_t` a difference of two monotone families) and `M̄_t`. The left sides converge to the numbers of 0.4(b). On the right, the divisor of `v` is `Δ_Y`, smooth and reduced (the section `v^{⊗2}` of `A ⊗ B` has divisor `2Δ_Y`, which is not), and, the reference being normalised before `ψ_t` is defined, `log‖v‖′_t = log‖v‖_N − ¼(ψ_t∘q₀ + ψ_t∘q₁)` exactly (a constant rescaling of `‖·‖_N` after the fact would add a constant to the right side, whose integral against `c₁(A′ − B′)∧⋯` need not vanish: for `k = 1` over `Spec ℤ`, `∫_{ℙ¹×ℙ¹}c₁(A − B)c₁(B) = 4`), and the forms are signed mixed forms of the pulled-back regularisations on `W(ℂ)`. SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.8(d) applies to `log‖v‖_N` (`Δ_Y` meets the boundary with normal crossings) and SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.8(c) to the second summand: `|ψ_t∘q_j| ≤ C·log R_s`, since each piece `φ` of `ψ` has `φ ≤ φ_t ≤ C` and `φ ≥ −C·log R_s`, and the `ψ_t∘q_j` converge locally uniformly off the boundary. So the right sides converge to the same integral for the singular metrics, and its integrand is integrable against each positive piece of the signed singular form (SingularHermitianMetricsAndL2Methods §7.8(c), (d)). Over the open set of (iii) the singular metrics are smooth and fibrewise round; by this integrability Fubini's theorem applies to the fibration `W → Z` there (the complement has measure zero and the singular forms have densities), and step (v) of (a) applies fibre by fibre: the fibre integral of the integrand over `W_z` is `2κ(k)` times the fibre integral over `ℙ(V_z)` of `c₁(Ξ̄)^{2k−1}`, wedged with `c₁(M̄)^r` (both are `SO(3)`-invariant expressions `c·⟨a, a⟩^{k−1}` in the curvature of the hermitian form, with constants fixed by the split case). The identity for `ĥ(u) + ĥ(−u)` follows as in (vi).

### 14.2 Arithmetic intersection numbers of flag varieties over ℤ

*Setting.* For `N ≥ 1` let `Fl_N → Spec ℤ` be the scheme of full flags `0 = F_0 ⊂ F_1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ F_N = O^N` of sub-bundles (SchubertGeometry S1.1), with the metrics induced by the standard hermitian form of `ℤ^N`; `x_i = ĉ₁(F_{i+1}/F_i)`, `0 ≤ i < N`, and `O(1)_j = (det F_j)^{−1}`, the pullback of the Plücker `O(1)` of `ℙ(Λ^j ℤ^N)` with its Fubini–Study metric. For a finite set `Ψ`, `Fl = ∏_{τ∈Ψ} Fl_N` over `Spec ℤ`, of absolute dimension `D + 1`, `D = |Ψ|N(N−1)/2`, with classes `x_{τ,i}`. For a polynomial `F` of degree `D + 1` in the `x_{τ,i}` let `ĥ_{Fl}(F)` be its arithmetic intersection number on `Fl` (multilinear extension of HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17a); for `λ ∈ ℝ^{Ψ×N}` put `ℓ_λ = −Σ_{τ,i} λ_{τ,i}x_{τ,i}` (the class of the line `L_λ`; `λ` dominant when each `λ_τ` is non-increasing), and `p_k(τ) = Σ_i x_{τ,i}^k`.

Prove: (a) `ĥ_{Fl}(p₁(τ)·Q) = 0` for every `Q` of degree `D`; (b) `ĥ_{Fl}(ℓ_λ^{D+1}) = 0` for every `λ` on a wall `λ_{τ,a} = λ_{τ,a+1}`; (c) `ĥ_{Fl}(ℓ_λ^{D+1}) ≥ 0` for dominant `λ`; (d) for `1 ≤ q < N`, the Grassmannian `Gr(q, N) ⊂ ℙ(Λ^qℤ^N)` over `ℤ`, of relative dimension `e = q(N − q)`, with the Fubini–Study `Ō(1)` (`= (det S̄)^{−1}`, `S` the tautological rank-`q` sub-bundle) satisfies `Ō(1)^{e+1} ≤ (e + 1)·deg(Gr(q, N))·(q/2)·log N`.

*Route.* (a) `p₁(τ) = Σ_i x_{τ,i} = ĉ₁(det O^N)`, and `det ℤ^N` with the standard metric is trivial with a generator of norm one, so `p₁(τ) = 0` as a class. (b) `ℓ_λ = Σ_{j=1}^{N−1}(λ_{j−1} − λ_j)·ĉ₁(Ō(1)_j) − λ_{N−1}·ĉ₁(det ℤ^N)` at each `τ`; on the wall the coefficient of `Ō(1)_{a+1}` vanishes, so `L_λ` is pulled back isometrically from the partial flag scheme forgetting `F_{a+1}`, of smaller absolute dimension, and the projection formula gives `0`. (c) For dominant `λ`, `L_λ` is a nonnegative combination of Plücker lines, semipositive, generated by the Plücker coordinates, whose norms are `≤ 1`; so every point has height `≥ 0`. For integral regular dominant `λ` (strictly decreasing at every `τ`) `L_λ` is ample, and `pointwiseNonnegative_cycleNonnegative` (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17f, stated for ample semipositive bundles) gives `ĥ_{Fl}(ℓ_λ^{D+1}) ≥ 0`; by homogeneity the same holds for rational regular dominant `λ`, and for all dominant real `λ` by continuity of the polynomial `λ ↦ ĥ_{Fl}(ℓ_λ^{D+1})`, regular dominant weights being dense in the dominant ones. (d) Points spanned by the rows of `q × N` matrices with roots of unity as entries are Zariski dense in `Gr(q, N)`; their Plücker coordinates are algebraic integers, so the finite places contribute `≤ 0` to the height, and at every complex embedding `Σ_I |det A_I|² = det(AA^*) ≤ N^q` (Cauchy–Binet and Hadamard), so their heights are `≤ (q/2)·log N`; the upper half of the successive-minima inequality (HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §5.17f) gives the bound.

*Needs:* HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions §§5.17a, 5.17f; SchubertGeometry S1.1 (flags of finite locally free modules), S3.5 (Plücker embeddings).

**Checks.**

## Layer 16: Maximal slope of the Lie algebra of an abelian variety

This layer bounds the maximal slope of the tangent lattice of a semi-abelian scheme with principally polarised generic fibre logarithmically in the height.

### 16.1 A logarithmic bound for the maximal slope of the Lie algebra

*Metric.* For a principally polarised abelian variety `A` over `ℂ` with Riemann form `H` on `Lie A` (the hermitian form whose imaginary part is the polarisation on the period lattice), the *Hodge inner product* on `Lie A` is `π·H`. Its dual on `ω_A` is `‖α‖² = (2π·(g − 1)!)^{−1}∫_A iα ∧ ᾱ ∧ c₁(λ)^{g−1}` (`λ` the principal polarisation), and its determinant on `det Lie A` is the dual of Deligne's metric `‖α‖² = (2π)^{−g}|∫_A α ∧ ᾱ|` on `det ω_A` (top forms `α`); for `A = ℂ/(ℤ + τℤ)`: `H(z, w) = zw̄/Im τ`, `‖∂/∂z‖² = π/Im τ`, `‖dz‖² = Im τ/π`.

Prove: there is an absolute constant `C` such that for every number field `L` and every semi-abelian scheme `G` over `O_L` whose generic fibre `A` is a principally polarised abelian variety of dimension `g`, the tangent lattice `Lie(G)` with the Hodge inner product at every complex embedding satisfies `μ̂_max(Lie Ḡ) ≤ C·log(2 + g + max(h(A), 0))`, `μ̂` the normalised slope and `h(A) = −deĝ_n(Lie Ḡ)` the height of `A` in Deligne's normalisation for this metric (the stable height when `G` is the identity component of the Néron model after a semistable extension). Every saturated sub-lattice of `Lie(G)`, in particular every eigen-lattice for an action of the ring of integers of a number field, then has normalised slope at most the same bound.

*Route.* Write `h⁺ = max(h(A), 0)`.

(1) *Reduction to one tangent vector.* For a saturated sub-lattice `F ⊂ Lie(G)` of rank `k ≤ g` with the restricted metrics, the absolute Siegel lemma 4.3b (b) gives, for every `η > 0` and over a finite extension, `x ∈ F ∖ 0` with `−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≥ μ̂(F) − ½(H_k − 1) − η ≥ μ̂(F) − ½log g − η` (`H_k` the harmonic number). So the bound follows, with `C` replaced by `C + 1`, once every non-zero integral tangent vector `x` over every finite extension satisfies `−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ C·log(2 + g + h⁺)`. Steps (2) and (3) concern this bound.

(2) *The theta lattice.* After a finite extension, ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.8 gives a Moret–Bailly model of `(A, L, A[r²])` for a symmetric `L` defining the principal polarisation and an even `r`: a semistable group scheme whose identity component is `G` (so the tangent lattice is unchanged), a cubist hermitian extension `ℒ` of `L`, and the key degree identity for `π_*ℒ` (rank `ρ = 1`): `deĝ_n(π_*ℒ̄) = −½h(A) − (g/4)·log 2π`. A generator `θ` of `π_*ℒ` (free after a further finite extension) therefore has mean logarithmic `L²`-norm `½h(A) + (g/4)·log 2π ≤ h⁺/2 + (g/4)·log 2π`. ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.8 also gives the lattice `F_r ⊂ π_*ℒ^{⊗r²}` spanned by the `r^{2g}` good translates of `θ`, each translation map being integral and isometric, so every translate is an integral section with the same `L²`-norms (and sup norms) as `θ` at every complex place. These sections and their norms are what step (3) uses; it needs no bound for the slopes of `F_r`.

(3) *The jet step*, which is the argument of [FormalProof]. For an even `r` of the order of `2 + g² + h⁺` and `n = r²`: for every order `i ≤ n/3` some section of `F_r` vanishes to order exactly `i` along `x` at the origin; jets along an integral tangent vector of integral sections are integral in a rigidified basis, so a triangular determinant of jets is a non-zero algebraic integer; at each complex place the `i`-th jet is bounded by the Cauchy estimate for functions of Gaussian growth on the universal cover, in terms of `‖x‖_σ^i` and the sup norms of the sections, which are compared with their `L²`-norms by the matrix lemma in logarithmic mean of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §5.8. The product formula for the determinant then gives `−mean_σ log‖x‖_σ ≤ ½log(e·n·n/3) + O((h⁺ + g·log(2 + g + h⁺))/n)`, which is `O(log(2 + g + h⁺))` since `n ≍ (2 + g² + h⁺)²`. The height `h⁺/2` of `θ` is paid once while the number of vanishing orders grows like `r²`; this is the source of the logarithm.

*Needs:* 4.3b; ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §§1.1–1.6, 2.7, 5.8 (the Moret–Bailly model, the cubist hermitian extension, the key degree identity and the good translation maps); AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §§A5.1–A5.4 (uniformisation, for the Cauchy estimates of step (3)).

**Checks** (degenerate cases, rank one and a product that reduces to rank one: they test the normalisations, not the logarithmic dependence on the height).

- `g = 1`: `Lie Ḡ` is a line of normalised slope `−h(E)`, and the bound reads `−h(E) ≤ C·log(2 + 1 + max(h(E), 0))`, a consequence of the lower bound for the height (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §6.5).
- Products: for `A = E₁ × E₂` with `h(E₁)` large, `μ̂_max(Lie) = max(−h(E₁), −h(E₂))` is bounded, while the linear bound of FaltingsFinitenessAndIsogenyTheoremsPartII §2.3 allows a slope of the order of `h(A)`.

### Examples

For `g = 1` the tangent lattice is a line of normalised slope `−h(E)`, and the bound reads `−h(E) ≤ C·log(2 + 1 + max(h(E), 0))`, a consequence of the lower bound for the height (ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §6.5). For `A = E₁ × E₂` with `h(E₁)` large, `μ̂_max(Lie) = max(−h(E₁), −h(E₂))` stays bounded (16.1, Checks).

### Dependencies

Layer 4 (4.3b); ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeights §§1.1–1.6, 2.7, 5.8, 6.5; AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §§A5.1–A5.4.
