# Roadmap: multiplier ideals of pairs and point separation

**Summary.** A pair `(X, Δ)` is a normal variety `X` over a field of characteristic zero with a `ℚ`-Weil divisor `Δ` such that `K_X + Δ` is `ℚ`-Cartier. It is klt (resp. lc) when every discrepancy, the coefficient of `K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)` along a prime divisor of a normal birational model `f : Y → X`, is `> −1` (resp. `≥ −1`). For effective `Δ`, the multiplier ideal `J(X, Δ)` is the push-forward of `O_Y(⌈K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)⌉)` from a log resolution; it is the unit ideal exactly on the klt locus. This roadmap develops pairs and their multiplier ideals from log resolutions and Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing, proves Nadel vanishing for pairs, and applies it, by the method of Angehrn and Siu (concentrating multiplicity at a point until it carries an isolated non-klt centre), to global generation and separation of points for adjoint bundles. It also proves a Bertini theorem for the common zeros of general members of a base-point-free birational linear system, a division theorem for sections vanishing along the simple common zeros of `D` sections on a variety of dimension `D`, and a bound for the sections of a line bundle by intersection numbers with a generated line bundle.

The headline theorems are these. (1) Let `X` be a normal projective variety over a field of characteristic zero, `Δ` an effective `ℚ`-Weil divisor with `K_X + Δ` `ℚ`-Cartier, and `L` a Cartier divisor with `L − (K_X + Δ)` nef and big. Then `H^i(X, O_X(L) ⊗ J(X, Δ)) = 0` for every `i > 0` (Nadel vanishing). In (3), `k` is algebraically closed of characteristic zero, and in (2) and (4) it is `ℂ`; in (2)–(4), `n = dim X`, and a subvariety is closed, integral and of positive dimension. (2) Let `(X, Δ)` be a projective klt pair with `Δ` effective, `N` a nef `ℚ`-Cartier divisor, `M` a line bundle numerically equivalent to `K_X + Δ + N`, `x ∈ X` a closed point, and `c_1, …, c_n > 0` with `Σ_d d/c_d ≤ 1`. If `(N^d · Z) > c_d^d` for every subvariety `Z ∋ x` of dimension `d`, then `M` is generated by global sections at `x`; the constant `c_d = ½n(n + 1)` is admissible. (3) Let `X` be smooth and projective, `L` an ample line bundle and `x_1, …, x_ℓ ∈ X` distinct closed points. If `(L^d · Z) > (½n(n + 2ℓ − 1))^d` for every subvariety `Z` of dimension `d`, then `H⁰(X, K_X + L) → ⊕_i (K_X + L) ⊗ k(x_i)` is surjective (the wider form of 9.12c, this roadmap's own formulation; over `ℂ` it is the theorem of Angehrn and Siu, AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.8, and 9.12c itself states the two-point form over `ℂ` that [FormalProof] has). (4) Let `(X, Δ)` be a projective klt pair over `ℂ` with `Δ` effective, `N` a nef and big `ℚ`-Cartier divisor, `x₁ ≠ x₂` closed points, and `c(1), …, c(n) > 0` with `(N^e · Z) > c(e)^e` for every subvariety `Z` of dimension `e` through `x₁` or `x₂` (`Z = X` included) and `Σ_e 2^{1/e}·e/c(e) ≤ 1`. Then every line bundle `M` numerically equivalent to `K_X + Δ + N` has a global section vanishing at exactly one of `x₁`, `x₂` (Kollár's two-point separation).

Log resolutions come from [ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero](../../ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero/README.md), intersection theory and simple normal crossings divisors from [SchemeAndStackFoundations](../../SchemeAndStackFoundations/README.md), and `ℚ`-Cartier divisors, nefness and Kleiman's nonnegativity of intersection numbers from [AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII](../../AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII/README.md), whose §§6.1–6.3 and 6.5–6.7 state the general theorems of which 9.10a–9.10c and 9.12–9.12c here are corollaries, whose §1.6a (Kodaira's lemma) and §1.7a (asymptotic Riemann–Roch for ample sheaves) state those of which 9.10d is a corollary, and whose §6.8 states over `ℂ` the wider form of 9.12c. ## Conventions

Every target names its base field. 9.12, 9.12a, 9.12b and 9.12c are stated over `ℂ`; in the wider form of 9.12c, `k` is algebraically closed of characteristic zero; in 9.12–9.12c, `n = dim X`, and "subvariety" means a closed integral subvariety of positive dimension. In 9.12–9.12c the boundary `Δ` of a pair `(X, Δ)` is effective. `K_X` is the canonical divisor of `X`, `≡` is numerical equivalence, and `h⁰(Λ)` is the dimension of the space of global sections of `Λ`. For invertible sheaves `L_1, …, L_d` on a proper `k`-scheme and a closed subscheme `Z` of dimension `d`, `(L_1 ⋯ L_d · Z)` is the degree of `c_1(L_1) ∩ ⋯ ∩ c_1(L_d) ∩ [Z]` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §§5.2–5.3, the intersection number of AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.7), written `(L^d · Z)` when every `L_i` is `L`, and `(B · L^{d−1})`, `(L^d)` when `Z` is the whole integral projective scheme; for a `ℚ`-Cartier divisor `N` with `rN` Cartier, `(N^d · Z) = r^{−d}(O_X(rN)^d · Z)` (also written `N^d·Z`). This number is the numerical intersection of CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger §4.3 (DynamicalDegreesOfRationalMaps §2.2): `m ↦ χ(Z, L^m)` is a polynomial of degree at most `d` whose coefficient of `m^d` is `(L^d · Z)/d!`, so `(L^d · Z) = Σ_{j=0}^{d} (−1)^{d−j} binom(d, j) χ(Z, L^j)`, the form in which `selfIntersection` computes it. For a real number `t`, `⌈t⌉` is its round-up, applied coefficientwise to divisors.

### 9.10a Discrepancies of pairs, klt and lc pairs, and the non-klt locus

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Definition 3.5, Lemmas 3.10–3.11 and Corollary 3.12, and Lazarsfeld (2004), §9.3:
discrepancies, klt and lc pairs, the non-klt locus and their test on one log resolution ((a), (b) and the first clause
of (c)); the invariance under étale morphisms and extension of the base field in (c) is this roadmap's own
formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* the discrepancy is defined on the divisorial valuation rings of `k(X)` as the order of a
rational top form minus `1/r` times the order of a local equation of `r(K_X + Δ)`, and its independence of the choices
follows from normality, a function of order zero at every codimension-one point through a given point being a unit at
that point.
On a log resolution of a projective `X` (the form of (b) recorded below) the centre of a valuation is found by the
valuative criteria, and in étale coordinates adapted to
the boundary the fact that a logarithmic top form has at most a simple pole along any divisorial valuation bounds the
discrepancy by the coefficients.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.1 (discrepancies of pairs, klt and lc pairs and the non-klt
locus: (a), (b) and the first clause of (c)).

Let `X` be a normal integral variety over a field `k` of characteristic zero and `Δ` a `ℚ`-Weil divisor on `X` such
that `K_X + Δ` is `ℚ`-Cartier; here `K_X` is the canonical Weil divisor of a nonzero rational top form, the closure of
the canonical divisor of the smooth locus. For a proper birational morphism `f : Y → X` from a normal variety and a
prime divisor `E ⊂ Y`, define the discrepancy `a(E; X, Δ)` as the coefficient of `E` in `K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)`, where
`K_Y` and `K_X` are the divisors of the same rational top form (so `f_*K_Y = K_X`) and `f^*` is the pullback of
`ℚ`-Cartier Weil divisors: if `rD` is the Weil divisor of a Cartier divisor `A` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII
§0.7), then `f^*D = (1/r)·[f^*A]`, where `f^*A` pulls back the local equations of `A`, which stay nonzero because `f` is
dominant and `X`, `Y` are integral (Stacks, Lemma 31.14.13 (02OO)), and `[·]` is the Weil divisor on `Y`. Define: `(X, Δ)` is *klt* if `a(E; X, Δ) > −1` and *lc* if `a(E; X, Δ) ≥ −1` for
every such `f` and `E` (all normal birational models, exceptional or not); the *non-klt locus* `Nklt(X, Δ)` is the
union of the centres `f(E)` of the divisors with `a(E; X, Δ) ≤ −1`.

Prove: (a) `f^*D` does not depend on `(r, A)`, is `ℚ`-linear in `D`, is effective when `D` is, and satisfies
`f_*f^*D = D`; `a(E; X, Δ)` depends only on the valuation of `k(X)` defined by `E`, not on `f`, nor on the top form, nor
on the Cartier index used to pull back; for a non-exceptional `E` (a prime divisor of `X`), `a(E; X, Δ) = −coeff_E Δ`;
(b) if `f : Y → X` is a log resolution of `(X, Δ)` (`Y` smooth, `Exc(f) ∪ f^{−1}_*Supp Δ` a simple normal crossings
divisor; one exists by ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §§5.5–5.6 with §5.1: resolve `X`, then principalise the
ideal of the exceptional locus together with the strict transform of `Supp Δ`, or by §7.7's total-transform form), then `(X, Δ)` is klt (resp. lc) iff every coefficient
of `K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)` is `> −1` (resp. `≥ −1`), and `Nklt(X, Δ)` is the image of the union of the components with
coefficient `≤ −1`, a closed subset; (c) a smooth `X` with `Δ = 0` is klt; if `g : X′ → X` is an étale morphism from a
normal variety `X′` (so `K_{X′} + g^*Δ = g^*(K_X + Δ)`), then `Nklt(X′, g^*Δ) = g^{−1}Nklt(X, Δ)`, and `(X′, g^*Δ)` is klt
(resp. lc) iff the restriction of `(X, Δ)` to the open set `g(X′)` is; for a field extension `k′/k`, `X_{k′}` is normal,
and for each connected component `X′` of `X_{k′}` (a normal variety over `k′`) and the pullback `Δ′` of `Δ` to it,
`(X′, Δ′)` is klt (resp. lc) iff `(X, Δ)` is.
The form of the definitions that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/Divisors₂/IsLogCanonicalMultipleCanonical.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.discrep`, `GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.KltAt`, `GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.LcAt`). There
`discrep` is defined on the discrete valuation rings of `k(X)` that contain `k` and are essentially of finite type over
`k` (`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.DivOver`), with no model among its arguments, so that independence of `f` holds by
definition; in the same file, independence of the top form and of the Cartier index is
`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.discrep_eq`, and `a(E; X, Δ) = −coeff_E Δ` for a non-exceptional `E` is
`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.IsLogEqn.discrep_eq_neg`. For projective `X`, the form of (b) that the family uses is
[FormalProof],
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PublishedTheorems/NadelVanishingPairs/NadelVanishingLemmas/LogResolutionMultiplierLocal.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.kltAt_lcAt_iff_of_isLogRes`), with
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PublishedTheorems/NadelVanishingPairs/NadelVanishingLemmas/LogResolutionMultiplierOpen.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.isOpen_kltAt_and_lcAt_of_isLogRes`) and
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ExistsIsLogMult.lean` (`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.IsLogRes`). The form
of the first clause of (c) that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/Divisors₂/IsLogCanonicalMultipleCanonical.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.kltAt_zero_of_smooth`).

*Route.* (a) If `rD = [A]` and `r′D = [A′]`, then `r′A` and `rA′` have the same Weil divisor, hence are equal because the
Cartier-to-Weil map of the normal `X` is injective (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §0.7), and their pullbacks agree;
`f` is an isomorphism over the generic point of every prime divisor of `X`, where `O_X` is a discrete valuation ring, which
gives `f_*f^*D = D`. Two models are dominated by a third, where the discrepancy of the strict transform of `E` is the same
coefficient. (b) Define `Δ_Y` by `K_Y + Δ_Y = f^*(K_X + Δ)`; then `a(E; X, Δ) = a(E; Y, Δ_Y)` for every divisor
`E` over `Y` (Kollár 1997, Lemma 3.10, p. 18), and for a smooth `Y` with a simple normal crossings `Δ_Y` of coefficients
`≤ 1` (resp. `< 1`) every divisor over `Y` has discrepancy `≥ −1` (resp. `> −1`), by a computation in local coordinates in
which the components of `Δ_Y` are coordinate hyperplanes (Kollár 1997, Lemma 3.11 and Corollary 3.12, pp. 18–19). (c) On
smooth `X` the pullback of a local generator of `ω_X` vanishes along every exceptional divisor. An étale `g`, and the
extension of the base field, carry a log resolution of `(X, Δ)` to log resolutions (of `(X′, g^*Δ)`, of each component
`(X′, Δ′)`) whose divisors have the coefficients of the divisors they lie over, and (b) applies.

*Needs:* ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §§5.1, 5.5–5.6, 7.7; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §0.7
(`ℚ`-Cartier Weil divisors, injectivity of the Cartier-to-Weil map on normal schemes); SchemeAndStackFoundations §3.4
(`picardToClassGroup`: the Weil divisor of a rational section, here of the canonical sheaf of the smooth locus) and §4.17
(simple normal crossings); Tau Ceti `SchemeWeilDivisor`, `Scheme.CartierDivisor`.

**Checks.**

- `X = 𝔸²`, `Δ = c·L` for a line `L`: klt iff `c < 1`, lc iff `c ≤ 1`.
- `X = 𝔸²`, `Δ = c·C` for the cusp `C = {y² = x³}`: klt iff `c < 5/6` (the log resolution has exceptional
  divisors with discrepancies `1 − 2c`, `2 − 3c`, `4 − 6c`); a test on the first blow-up only would give `c < 1`.
- The cone over a smooth plane cubic, `Δ = 0`: the exceptional curve of the blow-up of the vertex has discrepancy
  `−1`, so the pair is lc and not klt; the cone over a smooth conic has discrepancy `0` and is klt.

### 9.10b Multiplier ideals of pairs

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Theorem 2.16 and its proof, and Lazarsfeld (2004), 9.2.19, 9.1.22 and §9.3: the
multiplier ideal of a pair as the push-forward of the round-up from a log resolution, its independence of the
resolution, local vanishing, the klt criterion and the cosupport; the monotonicity in `Δ` and the comparison with
`algebraicMultiplierIdeal` are consequences of the definition, stated here.

*Method of the formal proof:* for projective `X` (the form recorded below), `J(X, Δ)` is characterised by
valuations: a local function `g` lies in it exactly when `ord_E(g) + a(E; X, Δ) > −1` for every divisorial valuation
`E` with centre in the open set considered, so that its independence of the resolution is built into the
characterisation; for any log resolution `f : Y → X` this ideal is shown to be the push-forward of
`O_Y(⌈K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)⌉)`, by lifting a valuation to its centre on `Y` and using that a smooth variety with a simple
normal crossings boundary of coefficients in `[0, 1)` is klt. The route written here
differs: it defines `J(X, Δ)` from one log resolution and proves its independence by comparing two log resolutions
through a common one.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.2 (the multiplier ideal of a pair: independence of the log
resolution, local vanishing, the klt criterion, the cosupport and the comparison with `algebraicMultiplierIdeal`; the
monotonicity in `Δ` is not stated there: it follows from the definition on a common log resolution of `(X, Δ)` and
`(X, Δ′)`, since `f^*(Δ − Δ′)` is effective (9.10a (a))).

For `X`, `Δ` as in 9.10a with `Δ` effective, and a log resolution `f : Y → X` of `(X, Δ)`, define
`J(X, Δ) = f_*O_Y(⌈K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)⌉) ⊆ O_X`. Prove: it is a coherent ideal sheaf independent of `f`; the higher
direct images `R^i f_*O_Y(⌈K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)⌉)` vanish for `i > 0` (local vanishing); `J(X, Δ) = O_X` iff `(X, Δ)`
is klt; the cosupport of `J(X, Δ)` is `Nklt(X, Δ)`; `J(X, Δ) ⊆ J(X, Δ′)` when `Δ′ ≤ Δ`; and for smooth `X` and an
effective Cartier divisor `D` with ideal `O(−D)`, `J(X, c·D) = algebraicMultiplierIdeal (O(−D)) c`, the multiplier ideal of
AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10.
For smooth `X` and `Δ = c·D` as in the last clause, the independence of `f` and the local vanishing are
`algebraicMultiplierIdeal.resolutionIndependent` and `algebraicMultiplierIdeal.localVanishing` of
AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10, on a log resolution, which is a principalisation of `O(−D)`; the form here
adds normal `X`.
For projective `X`, the form of the definition of `J(X, Δ)`, its coherence and independence of `f`, the klt criterion and
the cosupport that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PublishedTheorems/NadelVanishingPairs/NadelVanishingLemmas/MultiplierIdealIndependent.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.isMultIdeal_iff_regularAt`), with
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ExistsIsLogMult.lean` (`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.IsMultIdeal`) and
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PublishedTheorems/NadelVanishingPairs/NadelVanishingLemmas/MultiplierIdealSupportNonKlt.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Discrepancy.exists_isMultIdeal_support_iff_not_kltAt`).

*Route.* Independence: compare two log resolutions through a common one and use that the round-up of an
`f`-exceptional divisor with simple normal crossings support pushes forward to `O_X` (Lazarsfeld 9.2.19). Local
vanishing: the relative Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing `R^i f_*O_Y(K_Y + ⌈A⌉) = 0` for an `f`-nef and `f`-big `A` with
simple normal crossings fractional part, deduced from `algebraicAdjointVanishing` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10) after compactifying, twisting by
the pullback of a sufficiently ample divisor on `X` and using Serre vanishing and the Leray spectral sequence
(Lazarsfeld 9.1.22), applied to `A = −f^*(K_X + Δ)`. The klt criterion and the cosupport from 9.10a
(b). The smooth case is the definition of `algebraicMultiplierIdeal` with a principalisation that is also a log
resolution.

*Needs:* 9.10a; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10 (`algebraicAdjointVanishing`, `algebraicMultiplierIdeal`);
ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero §§5.1, 5.5–5.6, 7.7; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.2 (`leray_spectral_sequence`)
and §2.6 (`ample_serre_vanishing`).

**Checks.**

- `X = 𝔸²`, `Δ = c·L`: `J(X, Δ) = O(−⌊c⌋L)`; for the cusp `C`, `J(X, c·C) = O` for `c < 5/6` and the maximal ideal
  of the cusp point for `5/6 ≤ c < 1`.
- On the cone over a conic, `J(X, 0) = O` (klt), while on the cone over a cubic `J(X, 0)` is the maximal ideal of
  the vertex.

### 9.10c Nadel vanishing for pairs

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Theorem 2.16, and Lazarsfeld (2004), 9.4.17: Nadel vanishing for pairs.

*Method of the formal proof:* on a log resolution on which Kodaira's lemma writes a multiple of the pullback of the nef
and big class `L − (K_X + Δ)` as ample plus an effective simple normal crossings divisor, the vanishing needed is that
of the canonical class plus an ample class plus a fractional simple normal crossings boundary; this case is proved by
spreading out to a fibre in a characteristic `p` larger than the dimension that lifts modulo `p²`, and by descending
Čech vanishing from a high Frobenius twist through the round-downs of the boundary; twisting by powers of an ample
bundle of `X` then gives the vanishing of the higher direct images and the comparison with
`H^i(X, O_X(L) ⊗ J(X, Δ))`. The route written here differs: it takes the vanishing on the log resolution from
`algebraicAdjointVanishing`, Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing for nef and big classes (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry
§9.10), and local vanishing from 9.10b.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.3 (Nadel vanishing for pairs).

Let `X` be a normal projective variety over a field of characteristic zero, `Δ` an effective `ℚ`-Weil divisor with
`K_X + Δ` `ℚ`-Cartier, and `L` a Cartier divisor such that `L − (K_X + Δ)` is nef and big. Then
`H^i(X, O_X(L) ⊗ J(X, Δ)) = 0` for every `i > 0`.
For smooth `X` this is the case `a = O_X(−qΔ)` (`q ≥ 1` with `qΔ` integral), `c = 1/q`, `M = O_X(qΔ)`, with `L − K_X` in
place of `L`, of the vanishing clause of `algebraicMultiplierIdeal` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10), through
the comparison of 9.10b; the form here adds normal `X`.
The form that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/PublishedTheorems/NadelVanishingPairs/NadelVanishingLemmas/NadelVanishingTheorem.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.nadelVanishing`).

*Route.* On a log resolution `f`, `⌈K_Y − f^*(K_X + Δ)⌉ + f^*L = K_Y + ⌈A⌉` with `A = f^*(L − K_X − Δ)` nef and big
and with simple normal crossings fractional part; `algebraicAdjointVanishing` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10) gives `H^i(Y, K_Y + ⌈A⌉) = 0`; local
vanishing and the projection formula identify it with `H^i(X, O_X(L) ⊗ J(X, Δ))` by Leray (Lazarsfeld 9.4.17;
Kollár 1997, Theorem 2.16 and its proof, p. 13, where `J` is this push-forward).

*Needs:* 9.10b; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.10 (`algebraicAdjointVanishing`).

**Checks.**

- `X = ℙ²`, `Δ = c·L₀` for a line `L₀`, `0 ≤ c < 1`, `L = O(k)`: `L − (K + Δ)` has degree `k + 3 − c`, nef and big for
  `k ≥ −2`; the conclusion `H^i(O(k)) = 0` for `i > 0`, `k ≥ −2`, holds. For `c = 1`, `J = O(−1)` and the hypothesis
  holds for `k ≥ −1`, with `H^i(O(k − 1)) = 0`.
- For `L − (K + Δ)` nef and not big the statement fails: `X` an elliptic curve, `Δ = 0`, `L = O`: `H¹(O) ≠ 0`.

### 9.10d Kodaira's lemma and asymptotic Riemann–Roch

*Origin:* published: The Stacks Project, Proposition 33.45.13 (0BJ8), asymptotic Riemann–Roch for an ample invertible
sheaf (a), and Esnault and Viehweg (1992), Properties 5.4 d), Kodaira's lemma (b).

*Method of the formal proof:* (a) is proved for a nef invertible sheaf in place of `A`: `χ(Z, A^m)` is a polynomial in
`m` with leading coefficient `(A^d · Z)/d!`, and `h^j(Z, A^m) = O(m^{d−1})` for `j > 0` by dévissage of coherent sheaves
and induction on the dimension; (b): on the zero scheme `H` of a section of a power of an ample sheaf,
`h⁰(H, L^m|_H) = O(m^{d−1})` while `h⁰(Z, L^m) ≥ c·m^d` for infinitely many `m`, so some `L^m` has a nonzero section
vanishing on `H`. For (a) the route written here differs: it proves the ample case only, where Serre vanishing makes
`h⁰(Z, A^m)` the value of the Hilbert polynomial for large `m`.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.7a (asymptotic Riemann–Roch for ample sheaves) and §1.6a
(Kodaira's lemma).

Let `k` be a field and `Z` an integral projective `k`-scheme of dimension `d ≥ 1`. Prove:
(a) (asymptotic Riemann–Roch) for an ample invertible sheaf `A` on `Z`, `(A^d · Z) > 0` and
`h⁰(Z, A^m) = ((A^d · Z)/d!)·m^d + O(m^{d−1})` as `m → ∞` (Stacks, Proposition 33.45.13 (0BJ8)); its leading term,
`vol(A) = (A^d · Z)`, is the ample case of ArakelovGeometryAndAbelianHeightsPartII §0.3 (1′), and (a) adds the
error term `O(m^{d−1})`;
(b) (Kodaira's lemma) for a big invertible sheaf `L` on `Z` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.6) there are
`ν ≥ 1` and an effective Cartier divisor `C` on `Z` such that `L^ν(−C)` is ample (Esnault–Viehweg 1992, Properties 5.4 d)).
In particular, if `N` is a nef `ℚ`-Cartier divisor on a normal projective variety `X` of dimension `n` with `(N^n · X) > 0`,
then `N` is big (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.6) and some multiple `aN` that is Cartier is linearly
equivalent to `A + E` with `A` an ample and `E` an effective Cartier divisor.

*Route.* (a) For large `m`, `H^i(Z, A^m) = 0` for `i > 0` (Serre vanishing), so `h⁰(Z, A^m)` is the value at `m` of
`hilbertPolynomial` of `O_Z` (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.5), whose coefficient of `m^d` is `(A^d · Z)/d!` by the
Conventions; the positivity is that of intersection numbers of ample sheaves (CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger §4.3).
(b) Let `H` be an effective Cartier divisor with `O_Z(H)` ample. From `0 → L^m(−H) → L^m → L^m|_H → 0`,
`h⁰(H, L^m|_H) = O(m^{d−1})` (embed `L|_H` in a power of an ample sheaf and use `hilbertPolynomial` on `H`), while
`h⁰(Z, L^m) ≥ c·m^d` along sufficiently divisible `m`; so `L^m(−H)` has a nonzero section, with divisor `C`, and
`L^m(−C) ≅ O_Z(H)`.

*Needs:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.5 (`hilbertPolynomial`); AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.6
(bigness, and bigness of a nef sheaf with positive top intersection); CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger §4.3 (numerical
intersections, positivity for ample sheaves); DynamicalDegreesOfRationalMaps §2.2 (agreement with the degrees of
`c_1(L)^d ∩ [Z]`); SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 2 (Serre vanishing).

**Checks.**

- `Z = ℙ^d`, `A = O(1)`: `h⁰(O(m)) = binom(m + d, d) = m^d/d! + O(m^{d−1})` and `(O(1)^d · ℙ^d) = 1`; without the `1/d!` the
  formula would already be wrong for `d = 2`, where `h⁰(O(m)) = (m + 1)(m + 2)/2`.
- On the blow-up of `ℙ²` at a point, with `h` the pullback of a line and `e` the exceptional curve, `O(h)` is big and not
  ample (`h · e = 0`), and `O(2h)(−e) = O(2h − e)` is ample (`(2h − e) · e = 1`, `(2h − e) · (h − e) = 1`, `(2h − e)² = 3`).
- Bigness is needed in (b): on `ℙ¹ × ℙ¹`, `O(1, 0)` is nef with `(O(1, 0)²) = 0`, and `O(ν, 0)(−C) = O(ν − a, −b)` for `C` of
  type `(a, b)` with `a, b ≥ 0` is never ample, its degree on a fibre of the first projection being `−b ≤ 0`.

### 9.12 Isolated non-klt centres

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Theorem 6.4 and Steps 6.6–6.8, by the method of Angehrn and Siu (1995): the
isolated non-klt centre theorem.

*Method of the formal proof:* Kollár's induction on the dimension of the non-klt locus through `x`, with the divisor
carried in the class `λN − μA` (`A` ample, `μ > 0`) that Kodaira's lemma provides: at each step a non-klt centre `Z`
of the largest dimension `e` is cut by a divisor `B` obtained by specialising a section, defined over an extension of
the function field of `Z`, that vanishes to high order along the graph of `Z ↪ X`, and `(1 − δ)D + cB`, for a small
`δ > 0` and with `c` the log canonical threshold at `x`, has non-klt locus of dimension `< e` near `x`. That
`(X, Δ + (1 − δ)D + B)` is not klt at `x` follows from inversion of adjunction along the parameters of the family and a
weighted valuation over the graph; a last ample divisor missing `x` brings the class to `N`. The route written here
differs: it first reduces to an ample
`N` by the perturbation `(1 − t)N + tH`, treats a singular point `x` by specialising from nearby smooth points along a
curve, and makes the minimal lc centre through `x` unique by tie-breaking.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.5 (Kollár's isolated non-klt centre theorem, with `D ≡ N`).

Let `(X, Δ)` be a projective klt pair of dimension `n ≥ 1` with `Δ` effective, `N` a nef `ℚ`-Cartier divisor, `x ∈ X` a
closed point, and `c_1, …, c_n > 0` rationals with `Σ_{d=1}^{n} d/c_d ≤ 1` such that `(N^d · Z) > c_d^d` for every subvariety `Z ∋ x` of
dimension `d`. Then there is an effective `ℚ`-Cartier divisor `D` numerically equivalent to `λN` for some `0 < λ < 1` such that
`(X, Δ + D)` is lc in a neighbourhood of `x`, klt on a punctured neighbourhood of `x`, and not klt at `x`. By 9.10b the last two conditions say that `x` is an isolated point of the cosupport of `J(X, Δ + D)`;
the first (lc near `x`) is a separate conclusion, not expressed by the multiplier ideal. Moreover `D` may be taken
numerically equivalent to `N` itself (Kollár 1997, Theorem 6.4, p. 35; the form with `λ < 1` is that theorem for `(1 − ε)N`,
as in the proof of Kollár 1997, Theorem 5.8). The form of this last statement that the family uses, with real `c_d` and `N`
nef and big, is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/Divisors₂/IsLogCanonicalMultipleCanonical.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Published.IsolatedNonKltDivisor`), proved in
`Development/WeilBound/CoreStatements/AutomorphicKernels/NadelVanishing/TopIsolated.lean`
(`NadelVanishingSeparation.NSI.isolatedNonKltDivisor`).

*Route.* Kollár's form of the Angehrn–Siu argument. First reduce to ample `N` (Kollár 1997, 6.6.1): with `rN` Cartier the
numbers `(N^d · Z)` lie in `r^{−d}ℤ`, so the strict inequalities hold with a margin independent of `Z`; `N` is big because
`(N^n · X) > 0`, and Kodaira's lemma (9.10d (b)) writes `N ≡ H + E` with `H` an ample and `E` an effective `ℚ`-divisor. For
small `t > 0`, `N_t = (1 − t)N + tH` is ample with `(N_t^d · Z) ≥ (1 − t)^d (N^d · Z)` by Kleiman's nonnegativity
(AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §1.7 (ii)), and `(X, Δ + tE)` is klt (9.10a (b)); since `λ′N ≡ λ′N_t + λ′tE`, it
suffices to find `D′ ≡ λ′N_t` for the klt pair `(X, Δ + λ′tE)` and to put `D = D′ + λ′tE`. For ample `N`, asymptotic
Riemann–Roch (9.10d (a)) gives, since `(N^n) > c_n^n`, for large `m` a divisor in `|mN|` with multiplicity `> m·c_n` at `x`
(there are more sections than conditions); scaled by `n/(m c_n)` it is a `ℚ`-divisor `D_n ≡ (n/c_n)N` with multiplicity
`> n` at `x`. At a smooth point `x` of `X` this makes `(X, Δ + D_n)` not lc at `x` (the exceptional divisor of the blow-up of
`x` has discrepancy `n − 1 − mult_x < −1`); at a singular point `x` such a divisor is obtained by specialising from nearby
smooth points (Kollár 1997, 6.7.1): along a curve through `x` whose other points are smooth, the divisors at those points lie
in one linear system `|mN|`, and their limit at `x` is not lc at `x` (Kollár 1997, 7.8). Take the log canonical threshold and
perturb so that the minimal lc centre `Z` through `x` is unique (tie-breaking). If `d = dim Z > 0`, `(N^d · Z) > c_d^d` gives,
in the same way on `Z` and lifted to `X` by Serre vanishing, a `ℚ`-divisor `≡ (d/c_d − ε)N` with high multiplicity along `Z`
at `x`, which cuts the minimal centre down (Kollár 1997, 6.8.1). The dimension drops at each step and the total coefficient
stays below `Σ_d d/c_d ≤ 1`. The induction gives `D′ ≡ λN − μA` with `λ < Σ_d d/c_d`, `μ > 0` and `A` ample; adding a general effective
`ℚ`-divisor in the ample class `(λ′ − λ)N + μA` (for any `λ ≤ λ′ ≤ 1`), which does not pass through `x` and preserves klt and lc
near `x`, gives `D ≡ λ′N`.

*Needs:* 9.10a, 9.10b, 9.10d; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §§1.5–1.7 (nef `ℚ`-Cartier divisors, bigness of a nef
divisor with positive top intersection, Kleiman's nonnegativity, and §1.7 (v): a nef plus an ample `ℚ`-Cartier divisor is
ample, and ampleness is numerical); SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 2 (Serre vanishing).

### 9.12a Kollár's two-point separation for klt pairs

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Theorem 5.9 with Theorem 6.5: two-point separation for klt pairs.

*Method of the formal proof:* Kollár's induction: after rational budgets `h_e` are fixed and Kodaira's lemma has made
`N` ample, a divisor `D ≡ dN` with `d < 1` is built by cutting non-klt centres through the two points, each cut being
the specialisation of a section over an extension of the function field of the centre that vanishes to high order
along the graph, until one point is an isolated point of the non-klt locus and the other lies in it. Nadel vanishing
for `(X, Δ + D)` then lifts a section of `M` that is nonzero at the isolated point and vanishes at the other.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.7 (Kollár's two-point separation for klt pairs).

Let `(X, Δ)` be a projective klt pair of dimension `n ≥ 1` over `ℂ` (`X` normal and integral, `Δ` effective, `K_X + Δ`
`ℚ`-Cartier), `N` a nef and big `ℚ`-Cartier divisor on `X`, `x₁ ≠ x₂` closed points of `X`, and `c(1), …, c(n)` positive real
numbers such that `(N^e · Z) > c(e)^e` for every subvariety `Z` of dimension `e` (`1 ≤ e ≤ n`, `Z = X` included) that contains
`x₁` or `x₂`, and `Σ_{e=1}^{n} 2^{1/e}·e/c(e) ≤ 1`. Prove that every line bundle `M` on `X` with
`(M · C) = ((K_X + Δ) · C) + (N · C)` for all complete integral curves `C ⊂ X` has a global section `s` with `s(x₁) ≠ 0` and
`s(x₂) = 0`, or with `s(x₂) ≠ 0` and `s(x₁) = 0` (Kollár 1997, Theorem 5.9, pp. 30–31, with Theorem 6.5).
The form that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/Divisors₂/PointSeparationPair.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.Published.PointSeparationPair`), proved in
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/PointSeparationPairOfPrincipalisation.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.pointSeparationPair_of_principalisation`).

*Route.* Kollár's induction on the dimension of the non-klt locus through the points. (1) *Budgets.* Write `N = N₀/r` with
`N₀` a line bundle. The numbers `(N^e · Z)` lie in `r^{−e}ℤ`, so there are rationals `h_e > 0` with `2e^e < h_e^e·(N^e · Z)` for
all `Z` as above and `Σ_e h_e < 1`. (2) *Reduction to ample `N`.* By Kodaira's lemma (9.10d (b)) `aN₀ ∼ A + E` with `A` ample and `E`
effective; `(X, Δ + cE)` is klt for small `c > 0` (9.10a (b)); for a large multiple `m′` of `r`, with `q = m′/r`, replace
`(Δ, N)` by `(Δ + E/m′, (A + (q − a)N₀)/m′)`, which keeps the numerical class of `K_X + Δ + N` and makes the second term
ample; since `((A + bN₀)^e · Z) ≥ b^e(N₀^e · Z)` by Kleiman's nonnegativity (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII
§1.7 (ii)), the inequalities of (1) hold for it with the budgets `h_e·q/(q − a)`, whose
sum is still `< 1` when `q` is large. (3) *The cutting divisor.* Let
`D ≡ dN` be effective, `P ⊂ {x₁, x₂}`, `Z ∋ P` a subvariety of dimension `e` that is `X` or the closure of the centre of a
divisor of discrepancy `≤ −1` for `(X, Δ + D)`, and `h > 0` rational with `|P|·e^e < h^e(N^e · Z)`. Then there are an effective
`B ≡ hN` not containing `Z` and `δ₀ > 0` such that `(X, Δ + (1 − δ)D + B)` is not klt at the points of `P` for `0 ≤ δ ≤ δ₀`,
while off `Z` adding `B` preserves klt. For large `l` the sections of `lN` restricted to `Z` have dimension `> |P|·binom(M + e − 1, e)`
for some `M` with `hM > el` (9.10d (a) on `Z`; after (2) `N` is ample, and for large `l` every section on `Z` extends
to `X` by Serre vanishing), so one of them vanishes to order `≥ M` at each point of `P`;
extend it to `X` and add a general section vanishing on `Z`; `B` is `h/l` times its divisor. That `B` destroys klt at a point
`x ∈ P`, also where `Z` is singular: the section is a specialisation of sections over the function field of `Z` vanishing
to order `M` along the graph of `Z ↪ X`; klt passes from the special member of a family to the generic one; and over the
generic point of `Z` (a smooth point) there is a divisor `V` with order `≥ M` on the section and log discrepancy at most
`e + M·(a(E; X, Δ + (1 − δ)D) + 1) ≤ e + Mδ·ord_E(D)`, `E` a divisor over `X` with centre `Z` and `a(E; X, Δ + D) ≤ −1` (for
`Z = X`: at most `e`, the blow-up of the point); its log discrepancy for the pair with `B` is at most
`e + Mδ·ord_E(D) − hM/l`, which is `≤ 0` for small `δ` because `hM > el`. (4) *One step.* If `(X, Δ + D)` is lc at both points and `Z` contains both, take `B` for
`P = {x₁, x₂}`; `(X, Δ + (1 − δ₀)D)` is klt at both; let `c_i` be the log canonical threshold of `B` at `x_i` and suppose
`c₂ ≤ c₁`: then `D′ = (1 − δ₀)D + c₁B ≡ d′N`, `d′ ≤ d + h`, is lc and not klt at `x₁`, not klt at `x₂`, and its non-klt locus
near `x₁` is a proper closed subset of `Z`. (5) *Induction.* Start with `Z = X`, `D = 0`. While `x₁` (say) is not an isolated
non-klt point, take a non-klt centre `Z′ ∋ x₁` of smaller dimension `e′`: if the pair is lc at `x₂` and a centre through `x₂`
contains `x₁`, apply (4) on it; if it is lc at `x₂` and no centre through `x₂` contains `x₁`, first add a small ample divisor
through such a centre and not through `x₁`, which makes the pair not lc at `x₂`; if it is not lc at `x₂`, apply (3), (4)
with `P = {x₁}` on `Z′`. Each step lowers the dimension and costs at most `h_{e′} + ε`; the roles of the points may swap.
The result is `D ≡ dN`, `d < 1`, with one of the points an isolated point of the non-klt locus and the other in it.
(6) *Sections.* 9.10c for `(X, Δ + D)` and `M`, `M − (K_X + Δ + D) ≡ (1 − d)N` ample, gives
`H¹(X, M ⊗ J(X, Δ + D)) = 0`; lift the section of `M ⊗ O/J` that generates at the isolated point and vanishes elsewhere.

*Needs:* 9.10a, 9.10b, 9.10c, 9.10d; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §§1.5–1.7 (nef and big `ℚ`-Cartier
divisors, Kleiman's nonnegativity, and §1.7 (v): a nef plus an ample `ℚ`-Cartier divisor is ample, and ampleness is
numerical); SchemeAndStackFoundations Layer 2 (Serre vanishing); ResolutionOfSingularitiesCharacteristicZero
(log resolutions).

**Checks.**

- `X = ℙ¹`, `Δ = 0`, `N = O(a)`: the hypothesis is `a > c(1)` and `2/c(1) ≤ 1`, that is `a ≥ 3`, and `K + N = O(a − 2)` has a
  section vanishing at exactly one of two points iff `a − 2 ≥ 1`. For `a = 2` both fail (`O` has only constant sections).
- A uniform constant: since `2^{1/e} ≤ 1 + 1/e`, `Σ_{e=1}^{n} 2^{1/e}e ≤ Σ_{e=1}^{n}(e + 1) = binom(n + 2, 2) − 1`, so
  `c(e) = binom(n + 2, 2) − 1 = ½n(n + 3)` is admissible: the hypothesis `(N^e · Z) > (½n(n + 3))^e` is that of 9.12c, and
  of its wider form for `ℓ = 2`.
- The conclusion names no point: it is not asserted that the section can be chosen to vanish at `x₂` rather than at `x₁`.

### 9.12b Global generation of adjoint bundles

*Origin:* published: Kollár (1997), Theorem 5.8 and 5.10: global generation of adjoint bundles for klt pairs, by the
method of Angehrn and Siu (1995).

*Method of the formal proof:* for the case recorded below (smooth `X`, `Δ = 0`, `M = K_X ⊗ N₁^m ⊗ N′`), with
`N₀ = (2m − 1)N₁ + 2N′`, the isolated non-klt centre theorem for the constant
`c_d = ½n(n + 1)` gives an effective `D ≡ ½N₀` with `x` an isolated non-klt point of `(X, D)`; Nadel vanishing applies
because `N₁^m ⊗ N′ − D ≡ ½N₁` is nef and big, and a section of `M` not vanishing at `x` is lifted from the isolated
point.

Let `(X, Δ)` be a projective klt pair with `Δ` effective, `N` a nef `ℚ`-Cartier divisor, `M` a line bundle on `X`

`(N^d · Z) > c_d^d` for every subvariety `Z ∋ x` of dimension `d`, then `M` is generated by global sections at `x`
(Kollár 1997, Theorem 5.8, p. 30; its proof uses the effectivity of `Δ` through Kollár 1997, Theorem 2.16).
[FormalProof] has the case of a smooth `X` of dimension `n ≥ 1` with `Δ = 0` and `M = K_X ⊗ N₁^m ⊗ N′`, where `N₁`
is a nef and big line bundle with `(N₁^e · Z) ≥ 1` for every subvariety `Z ∋ x` of dimension `e`, `N′` is a nef line
bundle and `m > ½n(n + 1)`:
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/EffectiveGenerationCanonicalNefNumberField.lean`

*Route.* For `n = 0`, `X` is a point and `M ≅ O_X`. For `n ≥ 1`, 9.12 (after replacing each `c_d` by a slightly larger rational, possible because the values `(N^d · Z)` lie in

### 9.12c Point separation for adjoint bundles

*Origin:* adapted from Kollár (1997), Theorem 5.9 with 5.10: two-point separation at `Δ = 0` on a smooth complex
projective variety, for a nef and big line bundle, with the constant `½n(n + 3)` in every dimension. The wider form below
is this roadmap's own formulation: the separation of `ℓ` points of Angehrn and Siu (1995), Theorem 0.3, which is
stated over `ℂ`, here over every algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §6.7 (Kollár's two-point separation for klt pairs, at `Δ = 0`

### 9.13 Bertini for a birational base-point-free linear system

*Origin:* adapted from Chow and van der Waerden (1937), the factorisation of the associated form of a variety at the
points where it meets a general linear space, and from Jouanolou (1983), Bertini's theorems: the statement that
packages them for the image of a birational base-point-free linear system over a field of characteristic zero, with
one polynomial `Q` over `k` valid over every extension, the open set `U`, the degree `δ = (L^D)` and the injectivity
of `φ_V` on `Z(s)`, is this roadmap's own formulation.

*Method of the formal proof:* over a number field, one elimination form excludes the tuples `s` whose linear section
meets `φ_V(R ∖ U)` or the locus over which `φ_V` has infinite fibres, and a discriminant excludes those for which
`ξ ↦ f_X(s, ξ)` has a square factor; for the remaining tuples `Z(s)` has length `(L^D) = δ`, while the squarefree form
`f_X(s, ·)` of degree `δ` gives `δ` distinct image points, so `Z(s)` is étale and `φ_V` is injective on it: étaleness
is deduced by counting points against the length.

Let `k` be a field of characteristic zero, `R` a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over `k` of dimension
`D ≥ 1`, `L` a line bundle on `R`, `V ⊂ H⁰(R, L)` a subspace of finite dimension without common zeros such that
`φ_V : R → ℙ(V^∨)` is birational onto its image `X`, and `U ⊂ R` a dense open subset. Let `δ` be the degree of `X` and `f_X`
its Chow form: the form on `V^{D+1}`, of degree `δ` in each argument, irreducible over the algebraic closure, that vanishes at
`(s_0, …, s_D)` exactly when the hyperplanes `s_i = 0` have a common point on `X` (over the algebraic closure it is the form
of `chowParameters`, AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.6, unique up to a scalar; `X` being defined over `k`, a scalar
multiple has coefficients in `k`). Prove that `δ = (L^D)` and that there is
a nonzero polynomial `Q` on `V^D`, with coefficients in `k`, depending on `(R, V, U)`, such that for every field extension
`E/k` and every `s = (s_1, …, s_D) ∈ (V ⊗ E)^D` with `Q(s) ≠ 0`:
(i) the common zero scheme `Z(s) ⊂ R_E` of `s_1, …, s_D` is finite and étale over `E`, of degree `δ`, and contained in `U_E`;
(ii) `φ_V` is injective on the `δ` geometric points of `Z(s)`;
(iii) `f_X(s_1, …, s_D, ξ) = c·∏_y ξ(y)` for `ξ ∈ V`, the product over the geometric points `y` of `Z(s)`, `ξ(y)` the value in
a trivialisation of `L` at `y`, and `c ≠ 0`.
When `φ_V` is an immersion, SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.13 `bertini_smoothness`, applied to one section at a time, gives
the étaleness in (i); the form here adds birational systems, the open set `U`, the degree, (ii) and (iii).
The form that the family uses, for `k` a number field, is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/HermSysHypOneLeLambdaZero.lean` (`GlobalTheory.HSShort.Bertini`),
proved in `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/BertiniSimpleZerosGenericSections.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.BertiniTop.bertini`) from the published inputs collected in `GlobalTheory.HSCore.Quoted`.

*Route.* `δ = (L^D)`: the Hilbert polynomial of the image of a birational base-point-free system has leading coefficient
`(L^D)/D!`, and the degree of the Chow form is the degree of the image. Choose a form `g₁` not vanishing identically on `X`
and vanishing on `φ_V(R ∖ U)`, and a form `g₂` not vanishing identically on `X` such that `φ_V` has finite fibres over
`{g₂ ≠ 0}`. The cone over `X ∩ {g₁g₂ = 0}` has dimension `≤ D`, so elimination gives `Q₁ ≠ 0` such that for `Q₁(s) ≠ 0` the
linear space `s = 0` misses it. Because `f_X` is irreducible and the characteristic is zero, there is `Q₂ ≠ 0` such that for
`Q₂(s) ≠ 0` the form `ξ ↦ f_X(s, ξ)` is nonzero and has no square linear factor. Put `Q = Q₁Q₂`. Then `Z(s)` is finite and
inside `U`; `f_X(s, ξ)` vanishes exactly when `ξ` vanishes at the image of a point of `Z(s)`, so it is `c` times the product
of `δ` distinct linear forms. `Z(s)` has length `(L^D) = δ`: the Koszul complex of `s_1, …, s_D`, with terms
`(L^{−i})^{⊕binom(D, i)}` for `0 ≤ i ≤ D`, resolves `O_{Z(s)}` on `R_E`: it is exact off `Z(s)` because some `s_i` is a unit there
and multiplication by `s_i` is null-homotopic on the complex, and at a point of `Z(s)` because the local ring there is regular
of dimension `D`, hence Cohen–Macaulay, and `s_1, …, s_D` generate an ideal primary to its maximal ideal, hence form a
regular sequence; so `dim_E Γ(Z(s), O) = Σ_{i=0}^{D} (−1)^i binom(D, i) χ(R_E, L^{−i})`, the `D`-th difference of
`m ↦ χ(R, L^m)`, which is `(L^D)` by the Conventions (Euler characteristics do not change from `k` to `E`). With at least `δ`
geometric points, `Z(s)` is étale with exactly `δ` points, in bijection with the linear factors.

*Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations §5.3 (`proper_pushforward`, `eq_cartier_divisor`);
AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §1.5 (`hilbertPolynomial`) and §1.6 (`chowParameters`, the Chow form); for the length of
`Z(s)`: Tau Ceti `TauCeti.koszulComplex.exactAt_of_isWeaklyRegular`, `TauCeti.koszulComplex.smulHomotopy`,
`TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.isRegularLocalRing_stalk_of_smooth`, `TauCeti.IsRegularLocalRing.isCohenMacaulay`,
`Module.IsCohenMacaulay.isRegular_of_le_radical` and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.finrank_cohomology_pullback_eq_of_isPullback`.

**Check.** `R = ℙ¹`, `L = O(d)`, `V` all binary forms of degree `d`, `U` the complement of the point `[1 : 0]`: `X` is the
rational normal curve, `δ = d = deg L`, `f_X(s, ξ)` is the resultant of `s` and `ξ`, and `Q(s)` = (discriminant of `s`)·(the
coefficient of `x^d`): for `Q(s) ≠ 0`, `s` has `d` simple roots in `U` and the resultant is `c·∏_{s(y)=0} ξ(y)`.

### 9.13a Division by `D` sections with simple common zeros

*Origin:* adapted from Ein and Lazarsfeld (1999), Proposition 1.2 (ii), global division by the monomials in sections
generating a twisted ideal, over `ℂ`: (i) follows from its case of the ideal of `Z` by the identity
`I_{D+a−1} = I_Z^a`, which that proposition does not state (Ein, Lazarsfeld and Smith (2001), proof of Theorem 2.2);
(i) over the fields other than `ℂ`, beyond the forms of [FormalProof], and (ii) beyond the form of [FormalProof] are
this roadmap's own formulations.

*Method of the formal proof:* over a number field and in the forms recorded below, after a linear change of the
sections that makes them a regular sequence, a section vanishing on `Z` is locally a combination of the sections, and
the combination is made global one section at a time through
`0 → F ⊗ L^l → F ⊗ L^{l+1} → (F/t_1F) ⊗ L^{l+1} → 0`, the obstruction being an `H¹` that vanishes by
Kawamata–Viehweg; higher orders follow by induction on the order, the symbol of the section in `I_Z^a/I_Z^{a+1}`
being the restrictions of its coefficients to `Z`; surjectivity of restriction to `Z` by gluing local lifts along the
same sequences.

*Corollary of:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.13 (Ein and Lazarsfeld's global division by sections
generating a twisted ideal: (i) over `ℂ` for `a ≥ 1`, through the identity `I_{D+a−1} = I_Z^a` stated below; not (ii),
and not the other fields).

Let `k` be a field of characteristic zero, `R` a smooth projective geometrically connected variety over `k` of dimension
`D ≥ 1` with canonical bundle `K_R`, `L` a line bundle on `R` generated by its global sections, and `s_1, …, s_D ∈ H⁰(R, L)`
sections whose common zero scheme `Z` is finite and étale over `k`. A section of a line bundle `B` *vanishes to order `≥ a`
along `Z`* if its germ at every point of `Z` lies in `I_Z^a·B`. Prove:
(i) for a nef and big line bundle `L_A` and integers `a ≥ 0`, `j ≥ D − 1`, every `f ∈ H⁰(R, K_R ⊗ L_A ⊗ L^{j+a})` vanishing
to order `≥ a` along `Z` is `Σ_{|α|=a} s^α·m_α` with `m_α ∈ H⁰(R, K_R ⊗ L_A ⊗ L^j)`, the sum over the multi-indices `α ∈ ℕ^D`
of length `a`, `s^α = s_1^{α_1} ⋯ s_D^{α_D}`;
(ii) for a nef and big line bundle `L₂`, the restriction `H⁰(R, K_R ⊗ L₂ ⊗ L^D) → H⁰(Z, K_R ⊗ L₂ ⊗ L^D|_Z)` is surjective.
The form that the family uses is [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/WellChosen.lean` (`GlobalTheory.HSCore.FilMono`: (i) for `a ≥ 0`
and `j ≥ D`; `GlobalTheory.HSCore.KerRes`: (i) for `a = 1` and `j ≥ D − 1`), proved in
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/FilMonoOfKerRes.lean` (`GlobalTheory.HSCore.filMono_of_kerRes`)
and `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/RestrictedSectionChartCoords.lean`

`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/VanishesToOneOfStalkMap.lean` (`GlobalTheory.HSResOnto.res_onto`),

`L_A ≅ L₂ ⊗ L^D ⊗ O(E)` with `E` an effective divisor disjoint from `Z`, and `L₂` is generated by its global sections.
Over `ℂ`, (i) for every `a ≥ 1`, `j ≥ D − 1` and every nef and big `L_A` is the case of the ideal of `Z`, generated by
`s_1, …, s_D`, of AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.13 (Ein and Lazarsfeld 1999, Proposition 1.2 (ii)), with
the monomials of degree `a`, its hypothesis of nefness and bigness being that of `L_A ⊗ L^{j−D+1}`. This deduction

Over the other fields of characteristic zero, (i) beyond the forms of [FormalProof], and (ii) beyond the form of

by the route below.

*Route.* `L` is nef, so `L_A ⊗ L^i` is nef and big for `i ≥ 0` and Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing (AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry

### 9.13b A bound for sections by intersection numbers with a generated line bundle

*Origin:* particular to this proof: a binomial bound for `h⁰(X, B)` by the intersection numbers `(B · L^{d−1})` and
`(L^d)` with a line bundle `L` generated by its global sections (a), and the comparison `(Λ · L^{d−1}) ≤ n·(L′^d)` for
`L′ ≅ L ⊗ O(P)` with `P` effective (b); nearest published result: Kollár and Matsusaka, bounds for the dimension of
spaces of sections by intersection numbers.

*Method of the formal proof:* induction on the dimension and on `a`, restricting to the generic member `H` of the
system generating `L`, taken over the field `k(t_1, …, t_N)`: `h⁰(B) ≤ h⁰(B ⊗ L^{−1}) + h⁰(H, B|_H)`, intersection
numbers on `H` are those on `X` with one more factor `L`, a bundle with a section has nonnegative intersection with
nef bundles, and Pascal's rule adds the two bounds; for (b), the entries of an intersection number are replaced one at
a time, each replacement adding a nonnegative intersection number of a nef or effective class with nef classes.

Let `k` be a field, `X` an integral projective `k`-scheme of dimension `d ≥ 1`, `L` a line bundle on `X` generated by
finitely many global sections, `B` a line bundle on `X` and `a ≥ 0` an integer. Prove:
(a) if `(B · L^{d−1}) < (a + 1)·(L^d)` then `h⁰(X, B) ≤ binom(a + d, d)·(L^d)`;
(b) if `L′` is a nef line bundle with `L′ ≅ L ⊗ O(P)` for an effective Cartier divisor `P`, `Λ` a nef line bundle and `n ≥ 0`
an integer with `L′^{⊗n} ⊗ Λ^{−1}` nef, then `(Λ · L^{d−1}) ≤ n·(L′^d)`; hence `n·(L′^d) < (a + 1)·(L^d)` implies
`h⁰(X, Λ) ≤ binom(a + d, d)·(L^d)`.
In the application `L′` carries a linear system that is birational onto its image, `X` is a modification on which its
base ideal is invertible, `L` is the moving part, and `(L^d)` is the degree `δ` of the image (9.13).
(a) follows from [FormalProof], `Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/RankComponentWeightInvariance.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.JetRank.globalSections_dim_le_of_intersection_bound`), and (b) from [FormalProof],
`Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/RankComponentWeightInvariance.lean`
(`GlobalTheory.JetRank.interNum_cons_le`).

*Route.* (a) by induction on `d` and on `a`. Let `H` be the generic member of the system generating `L`, over the field
`k(t_1, …, t_N)`: it is integral or empty, of dimension `d − 1`, and `(P_1 ⋯ P_{d−1})_H = (L · P_1 ⋯ P_{d−1})_X`. The sequence
`0 → B ⊗ L^{−1} → B → B|_H → 0` gives `h⁰(B) ≤ h⁰(B ⊗ L^{−1}) + h⁰(H, B|_H)`. If `(B · L^{d−1}) < 0` then `h⁰(B) = 0`, because `L`
is nef. Otherwise `((B ⊗ L^{−1}) · L^{d−1}) < a·(L^d)` and the induction on `a` bounds the first term by
`binom(a − 1 + d, d)·(L^d)`, while on `H` the hypothesis holds with the same `a` and the induction on `d` bounds the second by
`binom(a + d − 1, d − 1)·(L^d)`; add by Pascal's rule. (b): `(Λ · L^{d−1}) ≤ n·(L′ · L^{d−1})` since `nL′ − Λ` and `L` are
nef; then replace the factors `L` by `L′` one at a time, each step adding an intersection number of `P` with nef bundles,
which is `≥ 0`.

*Needs:* AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometryPartII §§1.5, 1.7 (nef invertible sheaves, Kleiman's nonnegativity);
SchemeAndStackFoundations §§5.2–5.3 (intersection numbers, `c_1(O(D)) ∩ [V] = [D ∩ V]`).

**Checks.**

- `X = ℙ^d`, `L = O(1)`, `B = O(b)` with `0 ≤ b`: the hypothesis with `a = b` is `b < b + 1`, and
  `h⁰(O(b)) = binom(b + d, d)`: equality in (a).
- `X = ℙ¹`, `L′ = O(2)` with the system `⟨x², xy⟩` (fixed point `x = 0`, moving part `L = O(1)`, image of degree `1`),
  `Λ = O(b)`, `b ≤ 2n`: (b) reads `b ≤ 2n`, and `2n < a + 1` gives `h⁰(O(b)) = b + 1 ≤ a + 1`.

### Examples

On `X = 𝔸²` with `Δ = c·L` for a line `L`, the pair is klt iff `c < 1` and lc iff `c ≤ 1`, and `J(X, c·L) = O(−⌊c⌋L)`; for the cusp `C = {y² = x³}` the exceptional divisors of the log resolution have discrepancies `1 − 2c`, `2 − 3c`, `4 − 6c`, so `(𝔸², c·C)` is klt iff `c < 5/6`, and `J(𝔸², c·C)` is `O` for `c < 5/6` and the maximal ideal of the cusp point for `5/6 ≤ c < 1` (9.10a, 9.10b). The cone over a smooth plane cubic is lc and not klt, with `J(X, 0)` the maximal ideal of the vertex, while the cone over a smooth conic is klt, with `J(X, 0) = O` (9.10a, 9.10b). On `ℙ²` with `Δ = c·L₀` for a line `L₀` and `L = O(k)`, Nadel vanishing reads `H^i(O(k)) = 0` for `i > 0` and `k ≥ −2` when `0 ≤ c < 1`, and `H^i(O(k − 1)) = 0` for `k ≥ −1` when `c = 1`; on an elliptic curve with `Δ = 0` and `L = O`, where `L − K_X` is nef and not big, `H¹(O) ≠ 0` (9.10c). On `ℙ¹` with `N = O(a)`, the hypothesis of 9.12b with `c_1 = 1` is `a > 1` and `K + N = O(a − 2)` is globally generated, while for `a = 1` it has no section; for `ℓ = 2` points the threshold of the wider form of 9.12c is `2`, so `a ≥ 3`, and `K + L = O(a − 2)` separates two points iff `a − 2 ≥ 1` (9.12b, 9.12c); the same bound `a ≥ 3` is the hypothesis of 9.12a for two points on `ℙ¹`, and since `2^{1/e} ≤ 1 + 1/e` the constant `c(e) = ½n(n + 3)` is admissible in 9.12a, which is the hypothesis of 9.12c, and of its wider form for `ℓ = 2` (9.12a). For `R = ℙ¹`, `L = O(d)` and all binary forms of degree `d`, the image is the rational normal curve of degree `δ = d`, its Chow form is the resultant, and `Q(s)` can be taken to be the discriminant of `s` times its coefficient of `x^d` (9.13). On `ℙ¹` with `L = L_A = O(1)`, a form of degree `j` vanishing at a point `P` is a linear form vanishing at `P` times a form of degree `j − 1`; with `L₂ = O(1)` the space `H⁰(O) = k` maps onto the value at `P`, while for `L₂ = O`, nef and not big, the source `H⁰(O(−1))` is zero (9.13a). On `ℙ^d` with `L = O(1)` and `B = O(b)`, `b ≥ 0`, the bound of 9.13b (a) with `a = b` is the equality `h⁰(O(b)) = binom(b + d, d)` (9.13b).
