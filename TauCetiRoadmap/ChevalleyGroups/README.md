# Roadmap: Chevalley groups and the Chevalley–Demazure group schemes

This roadmap develops, for every valid Dynkin type `t`, the explicit realization and
point-group theory of **the simply connected Chevalley–Demazure group scheme
`G_sc(t)` over `ℤ`**. Its canonical constructor is the Chevalley–Demazure construction of
ReductiveGroups Layer 9 (9A and 9B) at `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum t`; `G_sc(t)` here is
that specialization. It develops the theory of its points that the finite groups of Lie type are
built from and studied with:

- its root subgroups `x_α` for every root, its split maximal torus, and the Chevalley commutator
  formula;
- over a field, the Bruhat decomposition, the Tits system, the centre, and Steinberg's presentation;
- the endomorphisms a Steinberg map is made of: field Frobenius, graph automorphisms, and the
  exceptional isogenies of `B₂` and `F₄` in characteristic two and `G₂` in characteristic three,
  together with their action on the character lattice;
- flatness of `G_sc(t)` over `ℤ`, its big cell, and the identification of its points over an
  algebraically closed field with the group generated by the root subgroups;
- Chevalley's comparison theorem, which identifies any two Chevalley group schemes of the same
  type and weight lattice by an isomorphism matching root subgroups.

The construction is the classical one: Chevalley's, via Kostant's `ℤ`-form of the enveloping
algebra acting on an admissible lattice in a faithful representation, taken as the closed subgroup
scheme of `GLₙ` over `ℤ` generated by the root subgroups and a weight torus. The simply connected
form uses the direct sum of the fundamental representations, whose weights span the full weight
lattice for every type at once. Suggested home: `TauCeti/Algebra/AlgebraicGroup/Chevalley/` for the
group schemes and their points, with the representation-theoretic inputs in the existing homes
`TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/HighestWeight/` and `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping/Kostant/`.

## Scope and boundaries

In scope: everything about the explicitly constructed Chevalley group schemes and their points
that is proved from the construction, by the methods of Chevalley, Steinberg, and Demazure. The
general theory is not in scope, and the dividing line is one question: *is the statement about a
specified integral Chevalley model or its explicit realization, proved using the constructor/comparison interface?*

- The [reductive-groups roadmap](../ReductiveGroups/README.md) owns abstract affine group schemes:
  reductive and semisimple groups, maximal tori and Borel subgroups of an abstract group, the root
  datum of a reductive group, central isogenies and simple connectivity as properties, and the
  isomorphism and existence theorems. Its Layer 9 (9A and 9B) owns the canonical integral Lie/group constructor, proves split reductivity
  and the specified root datum, and supplies the native model used here. This roadmap constructs
  the realization adapters and transports that certification, including
  `TauCeti.simplyConnectedSemisimpleCommHopfAlgProperty`, to its displayed point API.
- The [root-systems roadmap](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md) owns `DynkinType`, the
  Bourbaki numbering, `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum`, diagram automorphisms of root data,
  and root-datum isogenies.
- The [Lie highest-weight roadmap](../RepresentationTheory/LieHighestWeight/README.md) owns Verma
  modules, the irreducible quotients `L(λ)`, PBW, finite-dimensionality of `L(λ)` for dominant
  integral `λ` (its Layers 3 and 4), and Serre's theorem (its Layer 7). The split-field finite-dimensionality and rational descent adapter used here is
  constructed once in ReductiveGroups Layer 9A, item 5, beside that existing native highest-module API; C1
  consumes it and computes its fundamental-module and symmetry specializations.
- The [CFSG statement roadmap](../CFSGStatement/README.md) owns the classification list, the
  choice of Steinberg map for each index and the transport of its family-specific
  realizations to the common canonical carrier. Reusable represented models are retained. The [CFSG basic-properties roadmap](../CFSGBasicProperties/README.md) owns
  finite fixed-point groups: Steinberg descent, twisted root groups, and simplicity. Everything in
  this roadmap is about the ambient group and holds over an arbitrary field or ring, not only for
  fixed points of an endomorphism.

## Constructor ownership and comparison order

C0–C2 below retain every explicit Geck, fundamental-module, admissible-lattice,
root-index and torus calculation. Their generic producers live **once** in
ReductiveGroups Layer 9A (item 1, the integral Chevalley lattice; item 5, the admissible
lattices of the highest-weight modules) and Layer 9B (the Chevalley–Demazure construction and its
base change). C0 supplies the concrete normalization adapter; C1 computes the fundamental modules,
weight bases and numbered symmetries; C2 identifies `G_D`, the Chevalley–Demazure group of
ReductiveGroups 9B at the root datum with character lattice `Λ(D)`, with the displayed native
toral closure. Thus saying “construct” below includes these actual adapters and inverse/value
equations, not a second canonical group. `DynkinType.chevalleyGroupScheme` and
`DynkinType.ChevalleyGroup` name that group scheme and its points; no independent carrier is
introduced beside them.

C3 proves the presentation and point-group formulas on this model. C4 instantiates the native
generic flatness theorem and proves the integral big cell and its point consequences; it does not
repeat a generic flatness construction. C5's exceptional **point** map and character compatibility
come from the field presentation, independently of algebraicity. ReductiveGroups 9B ("Special
isogenies in characteristics two and three") owns the exceptional isogenies as morphisms of group
schemes; C7 compares that morphism with C5 on all roots, the torus and points. C6 owns the
Chevalley-datum comparisons and their extension from the big cell to the whole scheme. There is no
C5↔C7 or C3↔ReductiveGroups 9B existence cycle.

For a general reductive datum, including central tori or empty roots, the full construction is
ReductiveGroups 9B. C0–C7's single-type notation denotes the semisimple specialization and does not
narrow it. For every represented datum, its actual `Λ(D)` torus is used: the full-`P`
parametrization can have `μ₂` kernel already for adjoint `A₁`. No point-image saturation is
silently substituted.

## Standing conventions

- **Numbering.** Simple roots, fundamental weights, and simple coroots are indexed by `Fin t.rank`
  in the Bourbaki numbering fixed by `DynkinType.cartanMatrix`, and all roots by `Fin t.numRoots`
  as in `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum`. No second enumeration of the roots is introduced.
- **The Lie algebra.** The Lie algebra is `DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht` over `ℚ`, with its numbered
  Chevalley generators `DynkinType.lieBasis`, Cartan subalgebra `DynkinType.cartanSubalgebra`, and
  the instances `DynkinType.instIsKillingLieAlgebra` and
  `DynkinType.instIsTriangularizableLieAlgebra`. A representation of the Serre algebra
  `Matrix.ToLieAlgebra ℚ t.cartanMatrixᵀ` is read as a representation of `DynkinType.lieAlgebra`
  through the inverse of `DynkinType.serreLift`, which is an isomorphism by Serre's theorem
  (LieHighestWeight Layer 7, specialized as the target `DynkinType.serreLift_injective` of C2).
- **Root subgroups.** `x_α(u) = Σₙ uⁿ ρ(e_α)⁽ⁿ⁾` for the root vector `e_α` of the Chevalley system
  of C0, as in `kostantRootSubgroupParam`. For a root `α` and a unit `s`,
  `n_α(s) = x_α(s) x_{-α}(-s⁻¹) x_α(s)` and `h_α(s) = n_α(s) n_α(1)⁻¹`, following Steinberg,
  *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, Chapter 3, Lemma 19, who writes `w_α` for `n_α`. The Weyl
  representative `n_α(1)` agrees with `kostantWeylPoints`.
- **Characters.** The torus of a Chevalley datum `D` is the split torus whose character lattice is
  the lattice `Λ(D)` spanned by the weights of `D` (C2), and it acts on the weight-`μ` part of the
  module through the character `μ`. For `G_sc(t)`, `Λ = Fin t.rank → ℤ`, identified with the weight
  lattice of `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum t` through the fundamental weights. A character
  map of an endomorphism `F` preserving the torus is a pullback `χ ↦ χ ∘ F`, in the same direction
  as `RootPairingIsogeny.weightMap`. It is data attached to `F` together with a proof of the torus
  compatibility equation (C5). It is not extracted from an arbitrary abstract homomorphism.
- **Exceptional isogenies.** The exponent `e_i` is `1` on a long simple root and `p` on a short
  one, long and short as in `DynkinType.IsLongSimpleRoot`, and `σ` is the length-exchanging
  permutation of `DynkinType.IsSpecialNodePerm`, so that `τ (x_{±α_i}(u)) = x_{±α_{σ i}}(u ^ e_i)`
  up to the signs recorded in C5. This is the convention of the root-datum isogenies
  `DynkinType.b2SpecialIsogeny`, `DynkinType.f4SpecialIsogeny`, and `DynkinType.g2SpecialIsogeny`.
- **Generality.** The group schemes are over `ℤ` and their points are taken over every commutative
  ring. The group theory of C3 and C5 is over a field. Results that need an algebraically closed
  field, or a field of characteristic `p`, say so in their hypotheses.

### C0: the Chevalley basis

Beside the existence statement `DynkinType.exists_isChevalleySystem`, construct a named Chevalley
system `DynkinType.chevalleySystem t ht` of `DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht` for the involution
`DynkinType.chevalleyInvolution`, with root vectors `e_α` for every `α : Fin t.numRoots`, built from
Geck's basis rather than chosen from the existence theorem. `LieModule.Weight ℚ H L` contains the
zero weight of the Cartan action (for `A₁` its elements are `0, α, -α`), so the roots are the subtype
`{α : LieModule.Weight ℚ H L // α.IsNonZero}`. Transport the root enumeration `Fin t.numRoots` to
that subtype by a named equivalence, with `e_{α_i}` and `e_{-α_i}` the numbered generators of
`DynkinType.lieBasis`. `IsChevalleySystem` takes a family indexed by all of `Weight ℚ H L`, so
provide a separate adapter that extends the root-vector family by a chosen value at the zero
weight before calling that API. Prove that it is an
`IsChevalleySystem` for the signed Geck involution, that its roots are those of
`DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum` under the fixed enumeration, and the structure-constant
theorem: for roots `α, β` with `α + β` a root, `⁅e_α, e_β⁆ = N_{α,β} e_{α+β}` with
`N_{α,β} = ±(r + 1)`, where `r` is the largest integer with `β - rα` a root (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 1, Theorem 1).
Record `N_{α,β}` as an integer-valued function `DynkinType.structureConstant`, with the symmetries
`N_{β,α} = -N_{α,β}` and the Chevalley relation `N_{-α,-β} = -N_{α,β}` for the involution-compatible
choice.

### C1: the simply connected module

1. **Finite-dimensionality over a split field.** Prove that `irreducibleQuotient b λ` is finite
   dimensional for dominant integral `λ` over a field `K` of characteristic zero under
   `[IsTriangularizable K H L]`, with no `IsAlgClosed K` hypothesis. Either remove the algebraically
   closed hypothesis from the weight-space files the existing proof uses, replacing it by
   triangularizability, or prove that base change to an algebraic closure carries `L_K(λ)` to
   `L_{K̄}(λ)` and deduce finite-dimensionality from the closed case. Its home is beside
   `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/HighestWeight/FiniteDimensional.lean`.
2. **The fundamental modules.** For `i : Fin t.rank`, let `ϖ_i` be the fundamental weight of
   `DynkinType.lieAlgebra` with respect to the pinned base. Define `DynkinType.fundamentalModule t ht
   i` as `irreducibleQuotient` at `ϖ_i` over `ℚ`, and `v⁺_i` its highest-weight generator. Prove it
   finite dimensional and nonzero; nonvanishing of the Verma module `M(ϖ_i)` is PBW, from
   LieHighestWeight Layer 3.
3. **Admissible lattices.** Define `DynkinType.fundamentalLattice t ht i` as the `U_ℤ`-orbit
   `U_ℤ · v⁺_i` of the highest-weight vector, for `U_ℤ` the simple-generator Kostant form. Prove it
   is stable under `U_ℤ`, finitely generated over `ℤ`, and spans the module over `ℚ`
   (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 2, Corollary 1 to Theorem 2, whose part (b) also gives the
   weight decomposition), and that it lies in the span of the integral
   weight spaces, so that `kostantWeightBasis` gives it a weight basis. Its weights are those of
   `L(ϖ_i)`, and its `ϖ_i`-weight space is `ℤ · v⁺_i`.
4. **The full-weight module.** Define `DynkinType.scModule t ht` as the direct sum of the
   fundamental modules and `DynkinType.scLattice t ht` as the direct sum of their lattices, with the
   product weight basis. Prove that its weights span the weight lattice
   `Fin t.rank → ℤ` (each `ϖ_i` is a weight) and that the representation of
   `DynkinType.lieAlgebra` on it is faithful. This is the analogue for every type of
   `span_range_basisWeight_eq_top` for the spin carriers; that the sum of the fundamental
   representations realizes the full weight lattice is Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3, the remark
   after Lemma 27.
5. **Numbered symmetries.** For `σ` in `DynkinType.diagramSymmetry t`, construct the
   `ℚ`-linear automorphism `θ_σ` of `scModule` that maps `L(ϖ_i)` to `L(ϖ_{σ i})`, sends `v⁺_i` to
   `v⁺_{σ i}`, intertwines `e_{±α_i}` with `e_{±α_{σ i}}`, and preserves `scLattice`. Construct it
   from the universal property of `irreducibleQuotient` applied to the twist of `L(ϖ_i)` by the
   diagram automorphism of `DynkinType.lieAlgebra`, and prove `θ_σ ∘ θ_τ = θ_{στ}`. Package
   `(σ, θ_σ)` as a symmetry of the numbered Kostant data in the sense of the module
   `TauCeti.Algebra.Lie.UniversalEnveloping.Kostant.RootSubgroup.NumberedSymmetry`. The direct sum
   of all fundamental modules is stable under every diagram automorphism, so one module carries all
   of them.

   `θ_σ` maps the weight-`μ` component of `scLattice` onto the weight-`σ·μ` component, but it need
   not permute a weight basis: fundamental modules have weight spaces of dimension greater than one,
   and `θ_σ` can act on a `σ`-fixed weight space by a non-monomial matrix. The scheme-level descent
   `kostantToralNumberedSymmetryIso` asks for a monomial action. Generalize it to a lattice
   automorphism carrying each weight component `M_μ` onto `M_{σ·μ}`: such an automorphism
   conjugates the weight torus to itself, which is what the descent uses monomiality for.

### C2: Chevalley data, their group schemes, and root data

**Serre's theorem for the pinned algebra.** `DynkinType.serreLift_injective`: specialize Serre's
theorem from LieHighestWeight Layer 7 to `DynkinType.serreLift`, so that it is an isomorphism and a
representation of the Serre algebra is one of `DynkinType.lieAlgebra`.

A **Chevalley datum** of type `t`, `DynkinType.ChevalleyDatum t ht`, is a finite-dimensional
faithful representation of `DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht` over `ℚ` together with a lattice spanning
it that is stable under the simple-generator Kostant form, and a weight basis of that lattice. Its
**weight lattice** `Λ(D)` is the sublattice of `Fin t.rank → ℤ` spanned by its weights; it contains
the root lattice and is free of rank `t.rank`. Fix a `ℤ`-basis `b_D : Basis (Fin t.rank) ℤ Λ(D)` and
write each weight of the weight basis in `b_D`-coordinates. These coordinate weights span
`Fin t.rank → ℤ` by the definition of `Λ(D)`. Its group scheme `ChevalleyDatum.groupScheme` is
`kostantToralGroupScheme` on the module, lattice, and weight basis, with the coordinate weights as
`wt`. Its points are `ChevalleyDatum.Points D A`. `scModule` with `scLattice` is a Chevalley datum
with `Λ = Fin t.rank → ℤ` and `b_D` the standard basis, so the coordinate weights are the weights
themselves. The Geck module is a datum with `Λ` the root lattice and `b_D` the simple roots.
Everything in C2 and C3, except the two items marked as simply connected, is stated for every
Chevalley datum.

Do not use the weights themselves, read in `Fin t.rank → ℤ`, as `wt` for a datum with
`Λ(D) ≠ Fin t.rank → ℤ`. That parametrizes the torus by `Hom(P, -)` for `P` the full weight lattice,
and the resulting map to `GLₙ` has kernel the diagonalizable group with character group `P / Λ(D)`.
For adjoint `A₁`, `Λ = 2ℤϖ`, the weights are `2ϖ, 0, -2ϖ`, and the map is
`s ↦ diag(s², 1, s⁻²)`, with kernel `μ₂`. That map is not a closed immersion, and in characteristic
two its kernel is nontrivial as a group scheme although its points over a field are trivial.

Define

```text
DynkinType.chevalleyGroupScheme t ht := kostantToralGroupScheme (scModule, scLattice, weight basis)
```

over `ℤ`, and `DynkinType.ChevalleyGroup t ht A`, the group of its points over a commutative ring
`A`, as a subgroup of `GLₙ(A)` through `kostantToralPointsSubgroup`. Write `G_sc(t)` for the scheme.
Also name `DynkinType.adjointGroupScheme t ht := DynkinType.geckGroupScheme t ht`, the adjoint
form, whose comparison with `G_sc(t)` is C6.

- **Root subgroups for every root.** For every `α : Fin t.numRoots`, the root subgroup
  `DynkinType.rootSubgroup α : Multiplicative A →* ChevalleyGroup t ht A`, `u ↦ x_α(u)`, as a closed
  immersion `𝔾ₐ → G_sc(t)`. For a non-simple root, prove that `e_α` acts on `scLattice` with divided
  powers preserving it: `α = w α_i` for a Weyl word `w` and a simple root `α_i`, and
  `e_α = ± Ad(n_w) e_{α_i}` for the Weyl representative `n_w`, so `x_α(u) = n_w x_{α_i}(± u) n_w⁻¹`.
  Record that identity, with its sign, as a theorem.
- **The torus.** For a datum `D`, `ChevalleyDatum.torus D`, the split torus `T_D` with character
  lattice `Λ(D)`, realized as `TauCeti.GeneralLinear.weightTorus` on the coordinate weights above.
  It is a closed immersion into `GLₙ`, hence into `G_D`, by
  `TauCeti.GeneralLinear.isClosedImmersion_weightTorus`, because the coordinate weights span. Its
  points over `A` are `Hom(Λ(D), Aˣ)`, which `b_D` identifies with `Fin t.rank → Aˣ`. Reuse the
  existing diagonalizable-group and weight-torus API for this. No second abstract torus theory is
  introduced. For `G_sc(t)` this is `DynkinType.chevalleyTorus`, a closed immersion by the span
  statement of C1.
- **Points of the torus against the image of the full-weight parametrization.** State the
  difference between `T_D(A) = Hom(Λ(D), Aˣ)` and the range of `kostantTorusPoints` with the
  weights read in `Fin t.rank → ℤ`. That range is the image of the restriction map
  `Hom(P, Aˣ) → Hom(Λ(D), Aˣ)`, a proper subgroup in general. For adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ`, the
  root subgroups and the full torus `T_D(ℚ) = ℚˣ` generate `PGL₂(ℚ)`, while the root subgroups and
  that range generate only the image of `SL₂(ℚ)`, which misses the class of `diag(2, 1)`. For
  `G_sc(t)` the two agree.
- **The pinning equation** `t x_α(u) t⁻¹ = x_α(t(α) u)` for every root `α` and `t ∈ T_D(A)`, with
  `α` read in `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum` and lying in the root lattice, hence in `Λ(D)`.
  `h_α(s)` is the point `χ ↦ s ^ ⟨χ, α^∨⟩` of `T_D`.
- **Generation of the torus (simply connected).** For `G_sc(t)`, over every commutative ring, the
  torus lies in the subgroup generated by the root subgroups: the simple coroots are a basis of the
  cocharacter lattice in the simply connected datum, so `t = ∏ h_{α_i}(s_i)`. This is the simply
  connected case of the existing coroot-generation theorem.
- **The Frobenius and scalar extension.** Functoriality of the points in `A`, and the `q`-power
  Frobenius of `ChevalleyGroup t ht A` for `A` of characteristic `p`, from
  `kostantElementaryFrobenius`.

### C3: the group over a field

Let `k` be a field, `D` a Chevalley datum, and `E(k)` the subgroup of its points generated by the
root subgroups and the torus `T(k) = T_D(k) = Hom(Λ(D), kˣ)` of C2, not the smaller range of the
full-weight parametrization. For `G_sc(t)` the torus is already in the subgroup generated by root
subgroups, by C2. Following Steinberg's *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, Chapters 3 and 6,
which treat every admissible lattice. Steinberg's Chevalley group is the subgroup generated by the
root subgroups alone; the torus normalizes it, and each statement below extends to `E(k)` by that
normalization:

- **The Chevalley commutator formula** for every pair of linearly independent roots `α, β`, in
  Steinberg's normalization, which is also Mathlib's `commutatorElement` and the one the existing
  Kostant commutator lemmas use:
  `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆ = x_α(u) x_β(v) x_α(u)⁻¹ x_β(v)⁻¹ = ∏ x_{iα+jβ}(c_{ijαβ} u^i v^j)`, the product
  over `i, j > 0` with `iα + jβ` a root, in increasing `i + j` (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3,
  Lemma 15). Write `M_{α,β,i}` for the integer with `(ad e_α)^i e_β / i! = M_{α,β,i} e_{iα+β}`, so
  `M_{α,β,i} = (1/i!) N_{α,β} N_{α,α+β} ⋯ N_{α,(i-1)α+β}`. In this convention the coefficients are
  `c_{11} = N_{α,β}`, `c_{i1} = M_{α,β,i}` and `c_{1j} = -M_{β,α,j}`, and in type `G₂` the two
  remaining ones are `c_{32} = (2/3) M_{α+β,α,2}` (`α` short, `β` long) and
  `c_{23} = (1/3) M_{α+β,β,2}` (`α` long, `β` short). The sign of `c_{1j}` is constant: the part
  of the commutator linear in `u` is `X - exp(v ad e_β) X` for `X = e_α`, that is
  `-Σ_{j ≥ 1} v^j (ad e_β)^j e_α / j!`, and products of two nonconstant factors have degree at
  least two in `u`. Carter's convention `[x, y] = x⁻¹y⁻¹xy` with `(-t)^i` gives other signs; do not
  mix the two. The existing class-two (`x_α(t) x_β(u) x_α(t)⁻¹ = x_β(u) x_{α+β}(c t u)`),
  multiply-laced, and `G₂` relations are the cases, and the formula is proved for all pairs of the
  root system, not only for the simple ones.

  **Checks.**
  - `A₂`, `e_α = E₁₂`, `e_β = E₂₃`, `N_{α,β} = 1`: `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆ = x_{α+β}(uv)`. A formula
    with `c_{11} = -N_{α,β}` gives `x_{α+β}(-uv)`.
  - `C₂ = Sp₄` over `ℚ` in the standard symplectic basis, `α = 2e₂` long, `β = e₁ - e₂` short,

    agrees with the correct formula only for `j = 1`, so the `A₂` test cannot see it.

    -2α - β` with `α` short, `e_α = E₁₂ + 2E₃₄ + E₄₅ + E₆₇`, `e_β = E₂₃ + E₅₆`: the commutator
    `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆` equals the ordered product with exponents `uv [e_α, e_β]`,

    being `c_{32}` with `c_{32} = (2/3) M_{α+β,α,2}`; the same matrices with the roles exchanged
    confirm `c_{23} = (1/3) M_{α+β,β,2}`. Negating either coefficient makes the two sides differ,
    since the root vector it multiplies acts nontrivially on this module.
- **The unipotent radical and Borel subgroup.** `U(k)` generated by the positive root subgroups,
  with unique expression `∏_{α > 0} x_α(u_α)` in any fixed order of the positive roots;
  `B(k) = U(k) T(k)` with `U(k)` normal; the opposite `U⁻(k)`; `U(k) ∩ B⁻(k) = 1`.
- **The Weyl group.** `N(k)` generated by `T(k)` and the `n_α(1)`, with `N(k) / T(k)` isomorphic to
  the Weyl group of `DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum` by `n_α ↦ s_α`, and `N(k) ∩ B(k) = T(k)`.
- **Bruhat decomposition and the Tits system.** `E(k) = ⊔_w B(k) n_w B(k)`, with unique normal form
  `u n_w b` for `b ∈ B(k)` and `u` in `U(k) ∩ n_w U⁻(k) n_w⁻¹` (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3,
  Theorem 4 and Theorem 4′), and a `TauCeti.TitsSystem` on `E(k)` with `B = B(k)`, `N = N(k)`, and

- **Centralizer and centre.** For `k` infinite, the centralizer of `T(k)` is `T(k)` and its
  normalizer is `N(k)`. For every field, the centre of `E(k)` is `{h ∈ T(k) : α(h) = 1 for every
  root α}` (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3, Lemma 28(d), there for the subgroup generated by the
  root subgroups), which is `Hom(Λ(D)/Q, k^×)` for `Q` the root lattice, and `Hom(P/Q, k^×)` in the
  simply connected datum, `P` the weight lattice.
- **Steinberg's presentation (simply connected).** For `G_sc(t)`, `E(k)` is presented by the
  generators `x_α(u)`, `α` a root and `u ∈ k`, subject to additivity in `u`, the commutator formula
  for `α ≠ -β`, and, writing `h_α(s)` for the word above, `h_α(st) = h_α(s) h_α(t)` for
  `s, t ∈ k^×` (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 6, Theorem 8, for an irreducible root system). State
  it as
  a `MulEquiv` from `PresentedGroup` on that relator set, and prove the consequence used in C5: a map
  on the generators `x_α(u)` into any group that respects these relations extends uniquely to a
  homomorphism out of `E(k)`. In rank one the commutator formula is empty and the presentation uses
  Steinberg's relation `n_α(s) x_α(u) n_α(s)⁻¹ = x_{-α}(-s⁻² u)` instead; include it.

### C4: flatness, the big cell, and points over an algebraically closed field

The toral closure is defined over `ℤ`, and without further proof its base change to a field `k`
only contains the closed subgroup scheme that the root subgroups and torus generate over `k`. This
milestone proves equality and identifies the points.

Statements are for a Chevalley datum `D` with group scheme `G_D` and torus `T_D` of C2, and
`G_sc(t)` is the case `D = (scModule, scLattice)`.

- **Flatness.** Instantiate native
  `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isTorsionFree_kostantToralCoordinateHopfAlgebra` at D's actual
  e,h,ρ,M,hM,hnil,b,wt. The quotient coordinate Hopf algebra is torsion-free,
  hence flat over ℤ and the schematic closure of its generic fibre. Preserve the
  actual parameter equality through C2's realization isomorphism. This consumes
  the existing theorem; it supplies neither specialization nor smoothness by itself.
- **The big cell.** Order the weights of `D` linearly, refining the dominance order (`μ` before `ν`
  when `μ - ν` is a nonzero sum of positive roots), and order the weight basis compatibly, so the
  weight spaces are consecutive blocks. Then `U` acts by block upper unitriangular matrices, `U⁻`
  by block lower unitriangular ones, and `T_D` by diagonal ones. Let `GLₙ^{LDU}` be the open
  subscheme of `GLₙ` over `ℤ` where the leading principal minors at the block boundaries are
  invertible; block `LDU` factorization identifies it with (block lower unitriangular) ×
  (block diagonal) × (block upper unitriangular). The big cell is `Ω_D = G_D ∩ GLₙ^{LDU}`, and the
  multiplication morphism `U⁻ × T_D × U → Ω_D` is an isomorphism over `ℤ`, so `Ω_D` is open in
  `G_D`. On points over a field the content is
  `E(k) ∩ GLₙ^{LDU}(k) = U⁻(k) T_D(k) U(k)`: by the Bruhat decomposition of C3, conjugated by a
  representative of the longest element, `g ∈ U⁻(k) T_D(k) n_w U(k)`. For `l` block lower
  triangular and `u` block upper unitriangular, the leading block minors of `l n_w u` are invertible
  exactly when those of `n_w` are. `n_w` maps the weight-`μ` block onto the weight-`wμ` block, so
  its leading block minor at an initial segment `S` of weights is invertible
  only if `wS = S`. If that holds for every initial segment, `w` fixes every weight. The weights
  of a faithful representation span `Λ(D) ⊗ ℚ`, so `w = 1`. Over an algebraically closed field
  this is Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 5, Theorem 7. Over `ℤ`, prove that the multiplication
  morphism is an isomorphism by writing its inverse on coordinate rings: the block `LDU` factors
  are polynomials in the matrix entries and the inverses of the leading block minors, and the
  inverse identities, their compatibility with base change, and the covering of `G_D` by the Weyl
  translates of `Ω_D` with their overlaps are part of this item. A fibrewise isomorphism criterion
  may replace that proof only after its full finite-presentation, flatness and fibre hypotheses
  have been proved for the actual morphism; flatness of the source alone is not such a criterion
  (Jantzen, *Representations of Algebraic Groups*, Part II, Chapter 1, and SGA 3 guide this proof). `U` and `U⁻` are the closed subgroup
  schemes whose points are the unique products of C3, so they are isomorphic to affine space of
  dimension the number of positive roots.

  For `G_sc(t)` the same open set is cut out by the product of the highest-weight matrix
  coefficients `g ↦ ⟨v⁺_i*, g v⁺_i⟩` of the fundamental summands. That formula is specific to
  `G_sc(t)` and is not used for a general datum: on `SL₂` the coefficient is the entry `a`, and
  `g ↦ -g` changes its sign, so it does not descend to `PGL₂`. The block minors above are
  polynomials in the matrix entries of `GLₙ` for every datum, so no descent is needed.
- **Fibres.** `G_D` is covered by the translates of the big cell by the Weyl representatives,
  and is smooth over `ℤ` with geometrically connected fibres of dimension `t.rank + t.numRoots`.
- **Points.** For every field `k`, every point over `k` lies in some translate `n_w Ω_D(k)` of the
  big cell, and `Ω_D(k) = U⁻(k) T_D(k) U(k)`; hence the points over `k` are `E(k)`, generated by
  root subgroups and `T_D(k) = Hom(Λ(D), kˣ)`. For adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ` this is `PGL₂(ℚ)`. For
  `G_sc(t)` they are generated by root subgroups alone: `ChevalleyGroup t ht k = E(k)` for every
  field `k`. The base change of the group scheme to a field `k` equals the closed subgroup scheme
  of `GLₙ` over `k` generated by the reduced root subgroups and torus.
- **Points of the adjoint form over an algebraically closed field.** The pinned Geck carrier
  `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckPoints`, the points of `DynkinType.adjointGroupScheme`, has root
  subgroups `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckRootSubgroupPoints` only for the simple roots and their
  negatives. Prove `closure_geckRootSubgroupPoints_eq_top`: for every valid `t` and every
  algebraically closed field `k`, these subgroups generate all of `geckPoints t ht k`. Over an
  algebraically closed field the restriction `Hom(P, kˣ) → Hom(Q, kˣ)` is surjective because `kˣ`
  is divisible, so the full-weight parametrization `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckWeightTorusPoints`
  reaches every torus point; the torus is generated by the `h_{α_i}(s)`, the other root subgroups
  are conjugates of the simple ones by the Weyl representatives `n_{α_i}(1)`, and the previous item
  finishes. For `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂` the Geck weights span the full weight lattice, so this is also
  the statement for `G_sc(t)`.

  **Checks.**
  - Adjoint `A₁` over `𝔽̄_p`: the points are `PGL₂(𝔽̄_p)`, and the images of the upper and lower
    unipotent matrices generate it, since `SL₂(𝔽̄_p) → PGL₂(𝔽̄_p)` is surjective (every determinant
    is a square). A statement without the algebraic closure fails over `ℚ`: the class of
    `diag(2, 1)` in `PGL₂(ℚ)` is not in the image of `SL₂(ℚ)`.
  - Over `𝔽₂` in type `B₂`, the torus and the simple positive root subgroups generate a group
    generated by two involutions modulo a trivial torus, hence dihedral, while the unipotent radical
    of `Sp₄(𝔽₂) ≅ S₆` has order 16 and is not dihedral. So the subgroup generated by the simple
    positive root subgroups is not the Borel subgroup over small fields, and the theorem uses
    positive and negative simple root subgroups together.

### C5: endomorphisms and their character maps

Work on `DynkinType.ChevalleyGroup t ht k` for a field `k`, which is `E(k)` by C4. Write
`torus s = ∏ h_{α_i}(s_i)` for `s : Fin t.rank → kˣ`, and `χ(s) = ∏ s_i ^ χ_i` for a character
`χ : Fin t.rank → ℤ` in fundamental-weight coordinates, so `χ(torus s) = χ(s)`; define `charEval χ s`
as this product, for `s` with values in any commutative group. The items below are about
`G_sc(t)`, except the three that name the adjoint form `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckPoints`: the
predicate `IsCharacterMap` with the torus parametrization
`TauCeti.DynkinType.geckWeightTorusPoints`, which is indexed the same way and is not injective in
general, its kernel being `Hom(P/Q, kˣ)`; `exists_specialIsogeny`; and
`exists_geckWeightTorusPoints_eq_mul_inv`, the last two over an algebraically closed field. They do
not extend to the adjoint points over an arbitrary field, where the torus `T_D(k) = Hom(Q, kˣ)` of C2
is larger than the image of that parametrization and is not generated by root subgroups.

**Character maps are data.** An abstract endomorphism of `ChevalleyGroup t ht k` that maps the torus
into itself need not act on it through characters, even over an algebraically closed field.
Complex conjugation, applied entrywise to `SL₂(ℂ)`, preserves the diagonal torus and every root
subgroup, and acts on the torus coordinate by `z ↦ z̄`. If that were `z ↦ z^m`, `z = 2` would force
`m = 1` and `z = i` would contradict it. The same happens for the ambient groups the CFSG roadmap
uses: `Gal(𝔽̄_p / 𝔽_p) ≅ Ẑ`, and an element of `Ẑ` outside `ℤ` gives an automorphism of
`𝔽̄_pˣ` that is not an integer power. The integer-matrix theorem is about morphisms of split tori as
group schemes (Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, proof of Theorem 1.1.19), not about homomorphisms
of their groups of points. So a character map is supplied with `F` and checked.

Define, over every field `k`, for a group `G` with a torus parametrization
`T : (Fin r → kˣ) →* G`, an endomorphism `F` of `G` and `φ : AddMonoid.End (Fin r → ℤ)`, the
predicate

```text
IsCharacterMap T F φ  :↔  ∀ s, F (T s) = T (fun i => charEval (φ (Pi.single i 1)) s),
```

used with `T = torus` on `G_sc(t)` and `T = geckWeightTorusPoints` on the adjoint form. Equivalently
`χ(F (torus s)) = (φ χ)(s)` for every character `χ`, which is the pullback `χ ↦ χ ∘ F`. Prove:

- **Composition.** `IsCharacterMap T F φ → IsCharacterMap T G ψ → IsCharacterMap T (F ∘ G) (ψ ∘ φ)`.
- **Uniqueness over an infinite field.** If `k` is infinite, `IsCharacterMap torus F φ` and
  `IsCharacterMap torus F φ'` give `φ = φ'`, since distinct characters are distinct functions on
  `(kˣ)^{rank}`. Over a finite field `φ` is not determined by `F`, which is why it is data rather
  than a function of `F`.
- **Surjectivity on the torus.** If `k` is algebraically closed and `φ` is injective, `F` maps the
  torus onto itself. `kˣ` is divisible, hence an injective `ℤ`-module, so the dual of an injective
  `φ` is surjective on `Hom(Fin t.rank → ℤ, kˣ)`.

Each of the three families below comes with its character map and a proof of `IsCharacterMap`,
computed from its action on the root subgroups through `h_α(s) = n_α(s) n_α(1)⁻¹`. None of them
uses C7.

- **Field maps.** A field endomorphism `φ` of `k` induces an endomorphism of `ChevalleyGroup t ht k`
  by functoriality, with `φ (x_α(u)) = x_α(φ u)` for every root. Such a map has no character map
  in general, and none is asserted. The `q`-power Frobenius over a field of characteristic `p`
  satisfies `IsCharacterMap torus Frob_q (q • id)`, since `Frob_q (torus s) = torus (s ^ q)`.
- **Graph automorphisms.** For `σ` in `DynkinType.diagramSymmetry t`, the automorphism
  `DynkinType.chevalleyGraphAut σ` of `G_sc(t)` induced by the numbered symmetry `θ_σ` of C1,
  with `γ_σ (x_{±α_i}(u)) = x_{±α_{σ i}}(u)` on the positive and negative simple roots and
  `γ_σ (x_α(u)) = x_{σ α}(ε_{σ,α} u)` with signs `ε_{σ,α} = ±1` on every root, the signs computed
  from the structure constants of C0 (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 10, Theorem 29);
  `γ_σ γ_τ = γ_{στ}`; `γ_σ` commutes with every field map; and
  `IsCharacterMap torus γ_σ (DynkinType.diagramAut σ)⁻¹.weightMap`, from
  `γ_σ (h_{α_i}(s)) = h_{α_{σ i}}(s)`. The inverse is the orientation fixed in the standing
  conventions.
- **Exceptional isogenies.** For `(t, p)` one of `(B₂, 2)`, `(F₄, 2)`, `(G₂, 3)` and `k` of
  characteristic `p`, the endomorphism `DynkinType.chevalleySpecialIsogeny` of `E(k)` with
  `τ (x_α(u)) = x_{ᾱ}(ε_α u^{q(α)})`, where `ᾱ` is the image of `α` under the length-exchanging map
  of root systems, `q(α)` is `1` for `α` long and `p` for `α` short, and `ε_α = ±1`. Define it from
  the presentation of C3, checking that the relations are respected (Steinberg, *Lectures*,
  Chapter 10, Theorem 28, which also gives bijectivity for perfect `k`). Prove `τ ∘ τ = Frob_p`,
  that `τ` is bijective when `k` is perfect, the pinned equations on the positive and negative
  simple root subgroups in the convention above, and `IsCharacterMap torus τ φ_X` for `φ_X` the weight
  map of `DynkinType.b2SpecialIsogeny`, `DynkinType.f4SpecialIsogeny`, or
  `DynkinType.g2SpecialIsogeny` respectively. The proof is the computation
  `τ (h_{α_i}(s)) = h_{ᾱ_i}(s ^ {q(α_i)})`, read from the images of `n_{α_i}(s)`, and then the
  comparison of the resulting integer matrix with that weight map.

  On the adjoint form, prove `exists_specialIsogeny`: for `(t, p)` as above, `σ` with
  `TauCeti.DynkinType.IsSpecialNodePerm t σ`, and `k` algebraically closed of characteristic `p`,
  there is an endomorphism `τ` of `geckPoints t ht k` with `τ ∘ τ` equal to
  `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckFrobenius t ht p 1 k` and, for every `i`, signs `ε₊, ε₋ = ±1` with
  `τ (x_{±α_i}(u)) = x_{±α_{σ i}}(ε_± u ^ {e_i})` on the numbered root subgroups. For `F₄` and `G₂`
  the Geck carrier is `G_sc(t)`. For `B₂` the central isogeny `G_sc(B₂) → G_ad(B₂)` matches root
  subgroups and its kernel `μ₂` has only the trivial point over a field of characteristic two, so
  it is an isomorphism on points over an algebraically closed field compatible with `Frob_2`, and
  `τ` transports.

  The exponents are forced and are not a free convention. Write `α` long and `β` short in `B₂`,
  so `⟨α, β^∨⟩ = -2` and `⟨β, α^∨⟩ = -1`. Applying `τ` to
  `h_β(s) x_α(u) h_β(s)⁻¹ = x_α(s ^ ⟨α, β^∨⟩ u)` gives
  `q(β) ⟨ᾱ, β̄^∨⟩ = q(α) ⟨α, β^∨⟩` as exponents of `s`, that is `q(short) = 2 q(long)` for
  `p = 2`. `(1, 2)` satisfies it. The reversed assignment `(2, 1)` would need `s⁻¹ = s⁻⁴` for every
  `s ∈ kˣ`, false for `s` of order seven in `𝔽₈ˣ`, even though its formal square is also
  `Frob_2`.
- **Determination by simple root subgroups.** For a Chevalley datum `D` and a field `k`, two
  homomorphisms from `E(k)` to a group that agree on the positive and negative simple root
  subgroups and on `T_D(k)` are equal: the `n_{α_i}(1) = x_{α_i}(1) x_{-α_i}(-1) x_{α_i}(1)` lie in
  the subgroup generated by the simple root subgroups, every root is a Weyl conjugate of a simple
  one, and `x_α(u) = n_w x_{α_i}(± u) n_w⁻¹` by C2. Agreement on the simple root subgroups alone
  suffices for `G_sc(t)` over every field, and for every datum over an algebraically closed field:
  in the first case `T(k)` is generated by the `h_{α_i}(s)` (C2), in the second the restriction
  `Hom(P, kˣ) → Hom(Λ(D), kˣ)` is surjective because `kˣ` is divisible, and its image is generated
  by the `h_{α_i}(s)`. With the presentation, this gives the uniqueness that pins `γ_σ` and `τ` by
  their simple-root equations.

  **Checks.**
  - Adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ`, `E(ℚ) = PGL₂(ℚ)`: `δ([A]) = v₂(det A) mod 2` is a well-defined
    homomorphism `PGL₂(ℚ) → ℤ/2`, since rescaling `A` by `λ` changes `v₂(det A)` by `2 v₂(λ)`. With
    `h = [diag(-1, 1)]`, the trivial endomorphism and `g ↦ h^{δ(g)}` agree on every root subgroup
    (unipotent representatives have determinant one) and on the image `[diag(s², 1)]` of the
    full-weight parametrization, and differ at `[diag(2, 1)] ∈ T_D(ℚ)`. So without the torus
    `T_D(k)` the statement is false over `ℚ`, and agreement on the full-weight parametrization does
    not repair it.
  - `G_sc(A₁) = SL₂` over `ℚ`: composed with `SL₂(ℚ) → PGL₂(ℚ)` the same two maps are both trivial,
    consistent
    with root-only determination there: `diag(2, 1/2) = h_α(2)` is a product of root elements.
- **Standard and based endomorphisms.** For `k` algebraically closed, an endomorphism `F` with an
  injective `φ` satisfying `IsCharacterMap torus F φ` is *standard* if it maps each root subgroup onto a
  root subgroup, and *based* if moreover it maps each positive root subgroup onto a positive root
  subgroup, that is, it preserves the Borel subgroup `B(k)` as well as the torus. For standard `F`
  with `F (x_α(u)) = x_β(f(u))`, torus compatibility gives `f(α(t) u) = (φ β)(t) f(u)` for all
  `t`. Taking `t` in the kernel of `α` shows `φ β` is trivial there, hence a rational multiple of
  `α`. Since `α(T(k)) = kˣ`, `f = f(1) · g` for the field endomorphism `g` of `k` determined by
  `g(α(t)) = (φ β)(t)`. `φ` is injective and the root system is reduced, so `φ` determines `β` up
  to sign. It determines the sign too: if a second standard map with the same `φ` sent `x_α` into
  `x_{-β}`, its field endomorphism `g'` would satisfy `g g' = 1` on `kˣ`, and expanding
  `g(1 + c) g'(1 + c) = 1` gives `g(c)² + g(c) + 1 = 0` for every `c ≠ 0, -1`, so `g` takes at
  most two values there, contradicting injectivity of `g` on the infinite field `k`. So the root
  permutation of a standard map, and the field endomorphisms on its root subgroups, are read from
  `φ`.
- **Regression for basedness.** On `SL₂` over `𝔽̄_p`, let `n = [[0, 1], [-1, 0]]` and
  `F = Int(n) ∘ Frob_p`. It preserves the diagonal torus, exchanges the two root subgroups, and
  `F² = Frob_{p²}` because `n²` is central. `IsCharacterMap torus F (-p • id)` holds, so `F` is standard
  and not based. Record this as an example. On the root datum it is the isogeny `s ∘ (p · id)` of the
  simply connected `A₁` datum, `s` the reflection, with weight map `-p · id` and square `p² · id`;
  the automorphism group `{±id}` of that datum is abelian, so no conjugation moves it to a positive
  scaling, and a normal form for such isogenies holds only for based ones, those preserving the
  positive system.
- **Rigidity up to the torus.** For `k` algebraically closed, two standard endomorphisms `F`, `F'`
  with `IsCharacterMap torus F φ` and `IsCharacterMap torus F' φ` for the same `φ` satisfy `F = Int(s) ∘ F'`
  for a torus point `s`. By the previous item, they send `x_{±α_i}(u)` to
  `x_{±β_i}(c_{±i} g_{±i}(u))` and `x_{±β_i}(c'_{±i} g_{±i}(u))`, with `β_i` and the field
  endomorphisms `g_{±i}` read from `φ`. The `β_i` are linearly independent because `φ` is
  injective, and `kˣ` is divisible, so there is `s` with
  `β_i(s) = c_i / c'_i` for all `i`. `F` and `F'` map the torus onto itself (surjectivity above),
  so they send `n_{α_i}(1)` into the normalizer of the torus, and the rank-one computation
  `x_β(c) x_{-β}(-c₋) x_β(c) = [[1 - c c₋, c (2 - c c₋)], [-c₋, 1 - c c₋]]` shows that this forces
  `c₋ = c⁻¹`, for `F` and for `F'`. Determination by the simple root subgroups finishes. Also
  record how conjugation by a Weyl representative `n_w` and by `γ_σ` changes the character map: by
  `w` and by the diagram automorphism of `σ`.
- **Lang's equation on the torus.** Rigidity gives `F = Int(s) ∘ F'`, which is not yet
  `F = Int(t) ∘ F' ∘ Int(t)⁻¹`. Since `Int(t) ∘ F' ∘ Int(t)⁻¹ = Int(t F'(t)⁻¹) ∘ F'`, the missing
  step is solving `s = t F'(t)⁻¹` modulo the centre. Prove, as a named lemma, that for `k`
  algebraically closed and `IsCharacterMap torus F' φ` with `φ ^ n = p ^ r • id` for some `n, r > 0`,
  the homomorphism `t ↦ t F'(t)⁻¹` of `T(k)` is surjective. Its character map is `1 - φ`. Every
  complex eigenvalue of `φ` has absolute value `p ^ (r / n) > 1` when `p > 0`, and `φ` is nilpotent
  when `p = 0`; in both cases `1 - φ` is injective, and
  `T(k) = Hom(Fin t.rank → ℤ, kˣ)` with `kˣ` divisible makes its dual surjective. This is the torus
  case of the Lang–Steinberg theorem and needs nothing beyond that linear algebra. State it on the
  adjoint form as `exists_geckWeightTorusPoints_eq_mul_inv`: for `k` algebraically closed of
  characteristic `p`, an endomorphism `F` of `geckPoints t ht k` with
  `IsCharacterMap geckWeightTorusPoints F φ` and `φ ^ n = p ^ r • 1`, `n, r > 0`, every torus point
  `T s` is `T u · F(T u)⁻¹` for some `u`; the surjection on parameters maps onto the image of `T`.

  **Checks.**
  - `F = Frob_q` on `SL₂(𝔽̄_p)`, `φ = q • id`: the equation `s = u · u^{-q}` is solvable for every
    `s ∈ 𝔽̄_pˣ` because `x^{q-1} = s` has a root.
  - Characteristic zero, `p = 0`: then `φ ^ n = 0`, `1 - φ` is unipotent, and the conclusion still
    holds. The hypothesis is needed to exclude `p = 1`: `F = id` with `φ = id` satisfies
    `φ ^ 1 = 1 ^ 1`, and `u F(u)⁻¹ = 1` misses every nontrivial torus point; a field has no
    characteristic one.
- **Steinberg endomorphisms.** For `F` a composite `γ_σ ∘ Frob_q` with `q = p^m`, `m > 0`, or an
  odd power of `τ`, some positive power of `F` is a Frobenius `Frob_{p^m}` with `m > 0`, `F` is
  based, and its character map is the composite of the root-datum maps above. This is the
  group-level form of `ValidLieTypeIndex.exists_pow_datumSteinberg_eq_smulId`.

### C6: Chevalley's comparison theorem

For Chevalley data `D` and `D'` of the same type `t` whose weight lattices satisfy
`Λ(D') ⊆ Λ(D)`, construct a homomorphism of group schemes `G_D → G_{D'}` over `ℤ` with
`x_α(u) ↦ x_α(u)` for every root, whose kernel on points over a field is central and finite. When
the lattices are equal, it is an isomorphism. Prove the group-level statement on `E(k)` over every
field first, in two steps.

- **On the elementary subgroup.** Let `E'_D(k)` be the subgroup generated by the root subgroups
  alone, Steinberg's Chevalley group. Construct `φ' : E'_D(k) → E'_{D'}(k)` with `x_α(u) ↦ x_α(u)`,
  following Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3, Corollary 5 to Theorem 4′: the relations (R) of that
  chapter together with the relations among the `h_α(t)` define `E'_D(k)` (Corollaries 3 and 4
  there, consequences of the Bruhat decomposition of C3), and `Λ(D') ⊆ Λ(D)` gives the required map
  between the tori (Lemma 28(c) there). These words `h_α(t)` lie in `E'_D(k)` and do not reach all
  of `T_D(k)`: for adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ` they miss `[diag(2, 1)]`.
- **Extension by the actual torus.** On the tori of C2 the map is restriction of characters,
  `res : T_D = Hom(Λ(D), -) → Hom(Λ(D'), -) = T_{D'}`; on points over a field it need not be
  surjective, and for `G_sc(A₁) → PGL₂` over `ℚ` its cokernel is `ℚˣ / ℚˣ²`. Prove that
  `E(k) = E'_D(k) T_D(k)`, that `E'_D(k) ∩ T_D(k)` is the subgroup `H_D(k)` generated by the
  `h_α(s)` (from the uniqueness in the Bruhat decomposition of C3), that `res` sends `h_α(s)` to
  `h_α(s)`, and that the map on `E'_D(k)` intertwines conjugation by `t ∈ T_D(k)` with conjugation
  by `res(t)`, which on generators is the pinning equation of C2 with `α(res(t)) = α(t)` because
  `α` lies in the root lattice, contained in `Λ(D')`. Then `e t ↦ φ'(e) res(t)` is a well-defined
  homomorphism `E_D(k) → E_{D'}(k)`: it is the homomorphism `E'_D(k) ⋊ T_D(k) → E_{D'}(k)` given
  by the two maps, which kills the antidiagonal copy of `H_D(k)`, and `E(k)` is the quotient by
  that copy.

Then extend the map to the schemes using the big cell of C4.

Consequences to prove: every Chevalley datum of type `t` with full weight lattice has group scheme
isomorphic to `G_sc(t)` by an isomorphism matching root subgroups, tori, and Frobenius, and
intertwining the graph automorphisms and exceptional isogenies where the datum carries them; and
there is a homomorphism `G_sc(t) → DynkinType.adjointGroupScheme t` matching root subgroups, whose
kernel on points over every field is the centre of C3. That it is a central isogeny in the
scheme-theoretic sense belongs to the reductive-groups roadmap's Layer 9.

### C7: exceptional isogenies of group schemes

For the three pairs of C5, construct the endomorphism `τ` of the `𝔽_p`-group scheme
`G_sc(t)_{𝔽_p}` whose map on points over every perfect field of characteristic `p` is the
endomorphism of C5, and prove `τ ∘ τ = Frob_p` as morphisms of schemes. By C4 the fibre is smooth,
so a morphism out of it is determined by its points over `𝔽̄_p`. On the big cell `τ` is given by
polynomials over `𝔽_p` in the coordinates of `U⁻ × T × U`; glue over the translates of the big
cell, as the abstract homomorphism of Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 10, Theorem 28, is given on
the big cell of Chapter 5, Theorem 7. That `τ` is an isogeny with infinitesimal kernel belongs to the reductive-groups roadmap's
Layer 9.

## Worked examples

- `A₁`: the Chevalley datum of the standard representation of `sl₂` gives `SL₂`; prove
  `G_sc(A₁) ≅ SL₂` by C6, with root subgroups the upper and lower unipotent matrices; identify the
  adjoint form with `PGL₂`, whose points over `k` contain the image of `SL₂(k)` with index
  `|k^× / k^{×2}|`. That index is the cokernel of `Hom(P, kˣ) → Hom(Q, kˣ)`, and it is visible only
  because the adjoint torus of C2 has character lattice `Q`, so its points are all of `kˣ`.
- `G₂`: the seven-dimensional module and the adjoint module are both full-weight, so C6 identifies
  their group schemes; check the commutator formula of C3 against the `G₂` relations already in
  `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping/Kostant/RootSubgroup/Commutator/G2/`.
- `B₂` in characteristic two: `τ` of C5 agrees with `TauCeti.SpStd.specialIsogeny` under the
  isomorphism `G_sc(B₂) ≅ Sp₄` of C6.
