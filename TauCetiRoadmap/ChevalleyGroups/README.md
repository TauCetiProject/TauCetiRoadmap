# Roadmap: Chevalley groups and the Chevalley–Demazure group schemes

**Summary.** For every valid Dynkin type `t`, this roadmap builds the simply connected
Chevalley–Demazure group scheme `G_sc(t)` over `ℤ` in Chevalley's explicit form, with the theory
of its points from which the finite groups of Lie type are made. Take the split simple Lie algebra
of type `t` over `ℚ` with a Chevalley basis `e_α`, the direct sum `V_sc(t)` of its fundamental
representations, and the lattice that Kostant's `ℤ`-form generates there from the highest weight
vectors: `G_sc(t)` is the closed subgroup scheme of `GLₙ` over `ℤ` generated by the root subgroups
`x_α(u) = Σₙ uⁿ e_αⁿ / n!` and a split torus acting on that lattice. More generally a *Chevalley
datum* `D`, a faithful representation with a Kostant-stable lattice and a basis of weight vectors,
gives a group scheme `G_D` with a split torus `T_D` whose character lattice `Λ(D)` is spanned by
the weights: the weight lattice `P` for `G_sc(t)`, the root lattice `Q` for the adjoint group.

The headline theorems are these, for a field `k` and the group `E(k)` generated by the root
subgroups of `G_D` and the torus `T_D(k) = Hom(Λ(D), kˣ)`.

- `E(k)` has a Bruhat decomposition and a Tits system with the Weyl group of `t`, and its centre is
  the group `Hom(Λ(D)/Q, kˣ)` of torus points on which every root is `1`. For `G_sc(t)`, `E(k)` is
  presented by Steinberg's relations among the `x_α(u)`.
- `G_D` is smooth over `ℤ` with geometrically connected fibres of dimension the rank plus the
  number of roots, covered by the Weyl translates of its big cell `U⁻ × T_D × U`, and its points
  over every field `k` are exactly `E(k)`; for `G_sc(t)` they are generated by the root subgroups
  alone. Over an algebraically closed field the points of the adjoint group are generated by the
  root subgroups of the simple roots and their negatives.
- For `(t, p)` one of `(B₂, 2)`, `(F₄, 2)`, `(G₂, 3)` and `k` of characteristic `p`, the points of
  `G_sc(t)` over `k` have an endomorphism `τ` with `τ(x_α(u)) = x_ᾱ(±u^q(α))`, where `α ↦ ᾱ`
  exchanges long and short roots and `q(α)` is `1` on long and `p` on short roots; `τ ∘ τ` is the
  Frobenius, `τ` is bijective for perfect `k`, and over `𝔽_p` it is an endomorphism of the group
  scheme with square the Frobenius.
- Chevalley's comparison theorem: for Chevalley data `D`, `D′` of type `t` with `Λ(D′) ⊆ Λ(D)`
  there is a homomorphism `G_D → G_D′` over `ℤ` that is the identity on every root subgroup, with
  central finite kernel on points over a field; it is an isomorphism when the lattices are equal.

[ReductiveGroups](../ReductiveGroups/README.md) Layer 9 owns the canonical Chevalley–Demazure
construction; `G_D` and `G_sc(t)` are its specializations, made explicit so that root subgroups,
torus and points can be computed with, and its integral open cell, central isogenies and special
isogenies are transported to them. The Tau Ceti library supplies Geck's split Lie algebras with
a named Chevalley system, the fundamental modules and their lattices, the generic Kostant root
subgroups and toral closures, and character maps.
[LieHighestWeight](../RepresentationTheory/LieHighestWeight/README.md) Layer 7 supplies Serre's
theorem and [RootSystems](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md) the root lengths.
CFSGStatement and CFSGBasicProperties build the finite groups of Lie type as fixed points of the
endomorphisms constructed here.

## How to read the build

- Layer 0: integer structure constants for the named Chevalley basis.
- Layer 1: the generators and the diagram symmetries on `V_sc(t)`.
- Layer 2: Serre's theorem for the pinned algebra, Chevalley data and their group schemes,
  `G_sc(t)` and the adjoint form, root subgroups for every root, the torus, the pinning equation,
  and the comparison with ReductiveGroups §9B.
- Layer 3: the commutator formula, Bruhat decomposition, Tits system, centre and Steinberg's
  presentation over a field.
- Layer 4: flatness, the integral big cell, smooth connected fibres, and the points over a field.
- Layer 5: character maps, Frobenius, graph automorphisms, the exceptional isogenies on points,
  rigidity and Lang's equation on the torus, and Steinberg endomorphisms.
- Layer 6: Chevalley's comparison theorem.
- Layer 7: the exceptional isogenies as morphisms of `𝔽_p`-group schemes, compared with the point
  maps of Layer 5.

Layers 0 to 2 are one lane, and Layers 3 to 7 build on it. Layer 4 needs Layer 3 (the big cell is
proved from the Bruhat decomposition); Layers 5 and 6 both need Layer 4; Layer 7 needs Layers 4
and 5. Layer 5 does not use Layer 7: the character maps of the exceptional isogenies are computed
in Layer 5 from their action on the root subgroups. ReductiveGroups §9B owns the exceptional
isogenies as morphisms of group schemes and §7.1 below compares that morphism with the point map
of §5.4, so there is no cycle between this roadmap and ReductiveGroups. Each *Needs:* line lists
what its statement and its route use, so that a target proved by another route is not blocked
behind an unrelated section.

## Conventions

1. **Types and numbering.** `t : TauCeti.DynkinType` with `ht : t.Valid`. Simple roots,
   fundamental weights and simple coroots are indexed by `Fin t.rank` in the Bourbaki numbering
   fixed by `TauCeti.DynkinType.cartanMatrix`, and all roots by `Fin t.numRoots` as in
   `TauCeti.DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum`; the roots of the Lie algebra are matched with
   these numbers by `TauCeti.DynkinType.rootEquiv`. No second enumeration of the roots is
   introduced. Characters of a torus are written in fundamental-weight coordinates, so the weight
   lattice is `P = Fin t.rank → ℤ`, and `Q ⊆ P` is the root lattice.
2. **The Lie algebra.** The Lie algebra is `TauCeti.DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht` over `ℚ`, Geck's
   explicit matrix realization, with its numbered Chevalley generators
   `TauCeti.DynkinType.lieBasis`, its Cartan subalgebra `TauCeti.DynkinType.cartanSubalgebra`,
   and the instances `TauCeti.DynkinType.instIsKillingLieAlgebra` and
   `TauCeti.DynkinType.instIsTriangularizableLieAlgebra`. For a root `α`, `e_α` is
   `TauCeti.DynkinType.chevalleySystem t ht α`, the named Chevalley system for the signed
   Chevalley involution `TauCeti.DynkinType.chevalleyInvolution`; at `±α_i` it is the numbered
   generator. A representation of the Serre algebra `Matrix.ToLieAlgebra ℚ t.cartanMatrixᵀ`
   (`TauCeti.DynkinType.SerreLieAlgebra`) is read as a representation of the Lie algebra through
   the inverse of `TauCeti.DynkinType.serreLift`, which is an isomorphism by Serre's theorem
   (§2.1).
3. **Root subgroups and Weyl elements.** For a representation `ρ` with a Kostant-stable lattice,
   `x_α(u) = Σₙ uⁿ ρ(e_α)⁽ⁿ⁾`, with `ρ(e_α)⁽ⁿ⁾ = ρ(e_α)ⁿ / n!`, as in
   `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantRootSubgroupParam`. For a root `α` and a unit `s`,
   `n_α(s) = x_α(s) x_{−α}(−s⁻¹) x_α(s)` and `h_α(s) = n_α(s) n_α(1)⁻¹`, following Steinberg,
   *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, Chapter 3, Lemma 19, who writes `w_α` for `n_α`. The Weyl
   representative `n_α(1)` of a simple root agrees with
   `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantWeylPoints`.
4. **Commutators.** `⁅g, h⁆ = g h g⁻¹ h⁻¹`, Mathlib's `commutatorElement` and Steinberg's
   normalization, which the Kostant commutator relations of the supplier contracts use. Carter's convention
   `[x, y] = x⁻¹ y⁻¹ x y` gives other signs and is not mixed in.
5. **The torus of a Chevalley datum.** The torus `T_D` of a Chevalley datum `D` is the split torus
   whose character lattice is `Λ(D)` (§2.2), acting on the weight-`μ` part of the module through
   the character `μ`; its points over `A` are `T_D(A) = Hom(Λ(D), Aˣ)`. For `G_sc(t)`, `Λ = P`,
   identified with the weight lattice of `TauCeti.DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum` through
   the fundamental weights. The full-weight parametrization `Hom(P, Aˣ) → Hom(Λ(D), Aˣ)`, by
   restriction of characters, is never used in place of `T_D`: it has kernel `μ₂` already for
   adjoint `A₁`, and over a field that is not algebraically closed its image can be smaller than
   `T_D(k)` (for adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ` it misses `[diag(2, 1)]`, §2.5). No image of points is
   silently replaced by its saturation.
6. **Character maps.** A character map of an endomorphism `F` that preserves the torus is the
   pullback `χ ↦ χ ∘ F`, in the same direction as `TauCeti.RootPairingIsogeny.weightMap`. It is
   data attached to `F` together with a proof of the torus compatibility `TauCeti.IsCharacterMap`
   (§5.1), never extracted from an abstract homomorphism. The value of a character `χ` at a torus
   parameter `s : Fin t.rank → kˣ` is `χ(s) = ∏ᵢ sᵢ ^ χᵢ`, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.torusCharacter s χ`.
7. **Exceptional isogenies.** The exponent `e_i` is `1` on a long simple root and `p` on a short
   one, long and short as in `TauCeti.DynkinType.IsLongSimpleRoot`, and `σ` is the
   length-exchanging permutation of `TauCeti.DynkinType.IsSpecialNodePerm`, so that
   `τ(x_{±α_i}(u)) = x_{±α_σi}(u ^ e_i)` up to the signs recorded in §5.4. This is the convention
   of the root-datum isogenies `TauCeti.DynkinType.b2SpecialIsogeny`,
   `TauCeti.DynkinType.f4SpecialIsogeny` and `TauCeti.DynkinType.g2SpecialIsogeny`.
8. **Generality.** The group schemes are over `ℤ` and their points are taken over every
   commutative ring. The group theory of Layers 3 and 5 is over a field. Results that need an
   algebraically closed field, or a field of characteristic `p`, say so in their hypotheses. For a
   general reductive datum, with central tori or no roots, the construction is ReductiveGroups §9B;
   the single-type notation here denotes its semisimple specialization and does not narrow it. The
   acceptance statements in `Suggested.lean` take their fields in `Type`; nothing depends on the
   universe.
9. **Layers and names.** Layer `k` is the milestone that other roadmaps cite as `Ck` (table
   below), and its sections keep the item titles they cite, such as "The big cell", "Points" and
   "Exceptional isogenies". The acceptance statements are declared in `Suggested.lean` in the
   namespace `TauCetiRoadmap.ChevalleyGroups`; a target without a declaration there is named in
   italics by the Lean name it is to carry, such as *DynkinType.chevalleyGroupScheme*. The group schemes and their points are to live under
   `TauCeti/Algebra/AlgebraicGroup/Chevalley/`, the representation-theoretic inputs beside
   `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/HighestWeight/` and `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping/Kostant/`.

Citations of this roadmap by a former milestone resolve to the layers in the right column; a cited
item title, such as "§C4 / The big cell", is the title of a section of that layer, and a cited
"Generality" is Convention 8.

## Layer 0: the Chevalley basis

The root vectors `e_α = TauCeti.DynkinType.chevalleySystem t ht α` are built from Geck's basis by
recursion on the height of `α`; extended by zero at the zero weight
(`TauCeti.DynkinType.extendRootFamily`), they form a Chevalley system for the signed Chevalley
involution (`TauCeti.DynkinType.isChevalleySystem_chevalleySystem`). The weights
`LieModule.Weight ℚ H L` of the Cartan action include the zero weight (for `A₁` they are `0`, `α`
and `−α`), so the roots are the nonzero weights `(t.cartanSubalgebra ht).root`, numbered by
`TauCeti.DynkinType.rootEquiv`, and the extension at the zero weight is a separate adapter.

### 0.1 Structure constants on the root enumeration

Define *DynkinType.structureConstant* `t ht : Fin t.numRoots → Fin t.numRoots → ℤ`, written
`N_{α,β}`: when `α + β` is a root `γ` of `TauCeti.DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum`, it is the
integer with `⁅e_α, e_β⁆ = N_{α,β} e_γ`, and otherwise it is `0`. It is the integer structure
constant `TauCeti.IsChevalleySystem.intStructureConstant` of the named Chevalley system, read
through the root enumeration, which carries sums of roots of the datum to sums of weights
(`TauCeti.DynkinType.coe_rootEquiv_eq_add`).

- For roots `α`, `β` with `α + β = γ` a root, `⁅e_α, e_β⁆ = N_{α,β} e_γ`.
- **The structure-constant theorem.** For roots `α`, `β` with `α + β` a root,
  `N_{α,β} = ±(r + 1)`, where `r` is the largest integer with `β − rα` a root (Steinberg,
  *Lectures*, Chapter 1, Theorem 1). In particular `N_{α,β} ≠ 0`.
- For roots `α`, `β` with `α + β` a root, `N_{β,α} = −N_{α,β}`.
- **The Chevalley relation.** For roots `α`, `β` with `α + β` a root,
  `N_{−α,−β} = −N_{α,β}`; this is where the compatibility of the system with the Chevalley
  involution enters.
- For roots `α`, `β` with `α + β` neither a root nor zero, `⁅e_α, e_β⁆ = 0`.

*Route.* The bracket equation and the value `±(r + 1)` are
`TauCeti.IsChevalleySystem.lie_eq_intStructureConstant_zsmul` and
`TauCeti.IsChevalleySystem.intStructureConstant_eq_natCast_or_eq_neg_natCast`, whose `r` is
Mathlib's `LieModule.chainBotCoeff`; the two symmetries are `TauCeti.IsSl2System.structureConstant_skew`
and `TauCeti.IsSl2System.structureConstant_neg_neg_eq_neg_iff`; the last item holds because
`α + β` is then not a weight. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.DynkinType.chevalleySystem`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.isChevalleySystem_chevalleySystem`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.extendRootFamily`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.rootEquiv`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.coe_rootEquiv_eq_add` and the integer
structure constants above; Mathlib `LieModule.chainBotCoeff`.

**Checks.**
- Type `B₂ = C₂`, with `α` long and `β` short simple roots. For the pair `(β, α)`, `α − β` is not
  a root, so `r = 0` and `N_{β,α} = ±1`; for `(β, α + β)`, `(α + β) − β = α` is a root and `α − β`
  is not, so `r = 1` and `N_{β,α+β} = ±2`. In `Sp₄` with `e_α = E₂₄`, `e_β = E₁₂ − E₄₃`, `e_{α+β} = E₁₄ + E₂₃`
  and `e_{α+2β} = E₁₃` (the matrices of the Checks of §3.1), `⁅e_β, e_α⁆ = e_{α+β}` and
  `⁅e_β, e_{α+β}⁆ = 2 e_{α+2β}`. A normalization by the number `q` of
  steps up the `β`-string instead of down (`q = 2` from `α`, `q = 1` from `α + β`) would give
  `±2` and `±1`, wrong in both places.

### Examples

- In `sl₃` with `e_{α₁} = E₁₂`, `e_{α₂} = E₂₃`, `e_{α₁+α₂} = E₁₃` and `e_{−γ} = E_{ji}` for
  `e_γ = E_{ij}` (the root vectors compatible with the involution `x ↦ −xᵀ`):
  `⁅E₁₂, E₂₃⁆ = E₁₃` and `⁅E₂₁, E₃₂⁆ = −E₃₁`, so `N_{α₁,α₂} = 1`, `N_{α₂,α₁} = −1` and
  `N_{−α₁,−α₂} = −1`, as the Chevalley relation requires.

### Dependencies

Tau Ceti: the named Chevalley system and its root enumeration, and the integer structure
constants of a Chevalley system with their symmetries. Mathlib: root strings
(`LieModule.chainBotCoeff`).

## Layer 1: the simply connected module

`V_sc(t)` is `TauCeti.DynkinType.scModule t ht`, the direct sum over `i : Fin t.rank` of the
fundamental modules `L(ϖᵢ) = TauCeti.DynkinType.fundamentalModule t ht i`, each the irreducible
quotient of the Verma module of the fundamental weight `ϖᵢ`, finite dimensional over `ℚ`, with
highest weight vector `v⁺ᵢ`. Its lattice `TauCeti.DynkinType.scLattice t ht` is the direct sum of
the orbits `U_ℤ · v⁺ᵢ` under the simple-generator Kostant form `U_ℤ`; it is stable under `U_ℤ`, it
is a full lattice, and its weight basis `TauCeti.DynkinType.scWeightBasis` has weights
`TauCeti.DynkinType.scWeight`, in fundamental-weight coordinates, which span `P`. The Lie algebra
acts faithfully on `V_sc(t)`. For `σ` in `TauCeti.DynkinType.diagramSymmetry t`,
`θ_σ = TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv` is the `ℚ`-linear automorphism of `V_sc(t)` that
maps `L(ϖᵢ)` to `L(ϖ_σi)` and `v⁺ᵢ` to `v⁺_σi`, intertwines the action of `x` with that of its
image under the diagram automorphism of the Lie algebra, satisfies `θ_σ θ_τ = θ_στ`, and preserves
`scLattice`. The direct sum of all fundamental modules is stable under every diagram automorphism,
so one module carries all of them.

### 1.1 The numbered generators on the full-weight module

Prove that each numbered generator `e_{±α_i}` (`(t.lieBasis ht).rootGenerator`) acts nilpotently on
`V_sc(t)` through the representation of the enveloping algebra. This is the nilpotency hypothesis
with which the Kostant root subgroups, the elementary group and the toral closure of `V_sc(t)` with
`scLattice` and `scWeightBasis` are defined, so it is the last input of `G_sc(t)` in §2.3. The
statements about `G_sc(t)` in `Suggested.lean` carry it as a hypothesis for that reason.
*Route.* `e_{±α_i}` lies in the root space of a nonzero root, and `V_sc(t)` is finite dimensional
and the direct sum of its weight spaces for the Cartan subalgebra, so the action is nilpotent by
Mathlib's `LieModule.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_mem_rootSpace`; the enveloping algebra acts on the image
of `x` as `x` does. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.DynkinType.scModule`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.lieBasis_e_mem_rootSpace_rootEquiv`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.lieBasis_f_mem_rootSpace_rootEquiv`; Mathlib
`LieModule.isNilpotent_toEnd_of_mem_rootSpace`, `LieModule.IsTriangularizable`.

### 1.2 Numbered symmetries

For `σ` in `TauCeti.DynkinType.diagramSymmetry t`, package `(σ, θ_σ)` as a symmetry of the
numbered Kostant data of `V_sc(t)` in the sense of the module
`TauCeti.Algebra.Lie.UniversalEnveloping.Kostant.RootSubgroup.NumberedSymmetry`, with `σ` acting
on the generator index `Fin t.rank ⊕ Fin t.rank` on both summands: `θ_σ` preserves `scLattice`
and carries the action of `e_{±α_i}` to that of `e_{±α_σi}`. Prove:

- `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantElementaryNumberedSymmetryAut` gives an automorphism of
  the elementary group of `V_sc(t)` over every commutative ring, with
  `x_{±α_i}(u) ↦ x_{±α_σi}(u)`, commuting with base change of the ring and with the Frobenius;
  its order divides that of `σ`, since `(θ_σ)ⁿ = θ_{σⁿ}` and `θ_1 = 1`.
- `θ_σ` maps the weight-`μ` component of `scLattice` onto the weight-`σ·μ` component, where
  `(σ·μ)_σj = μ_j`, that is `σ·μ = μ ∘ σ⁻¹`. It need not permute a weight basis: fundamental
  modules have weight spaces of dimension greater than one, and `θ_σ` can act on a `σ`-fixed weight
  space by a non-monomial matrix. Weight components are what
  `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralNumberedSymmetryIso` asks for, so `θ_σ` induces
  an automorphism of the toral closure of `V_sc(t)`, which is `G_sc(t)` of §2.3, acting on root
  subgroups by `x_{±α_i} ↦ x_{±α_σi}` and on the weight torus by relabelling its coordinates by
  `σ⁻¹`. This is the graph automorphism of §5.3.

*Needs:* §1.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv` with
`TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv_lie`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv_lof`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv_mul` and
`TauCeti.DynkinType.scModuleDiagramEquiv_mem_scLattice_iff`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.diagramLieEquiv`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.scWeightBasis`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantElementaryNumberedSymmetryAut`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralNumberedSymmetryIso`.

**Checks.**
- Type `A₂`, `σ` the exchange of the two nodes: `V_sc(A₂) = L(ϖ₁) ⊕ L(ϖ₂)` is the standard
  representation of `sl₃` plus its dual, with weights `(1, 0)`, `(−1, 1)`, `(0, −1)` and `(0, 1)`,
  `(1, −1)`, `(−1, 0)`. `μ ↦ μ ∘ σ⁻¹` carries the first list onto the second, as `θ_σ` must, since
  it maps `L(ϖ₁)` onto `L(ϖ₂)`. The relabelling `μ ↦ −μ` also exchanges the two lists as sets but
  sends the highest weight `(1, 0)` to `(−1, 0)`, the lowest weight of `L(ϖ₂)`, so it is not the
  action of `θ_σ` on weights.

### Examples

- Type `A₁`: `V_sc(A₁) = L(ϖ)` is `ℚ²` with lattice `ℤ²` and weights `1` and `−1`; the diagram
  symmetry group is trivial.
- Type `D₄`: the symmetry group is `S₃`, permuting `L(ϖ₁)`, `L(ϖ₃)` and `L(ϖ₄)` and fixing the
  adjoint summand `L(ϖ₂)`, on whose four-dimensional zero weight space `θ_σ` acts through the
  diagram automorphism of the Cartan subalgebra.

### Dependencies

Tau Ceti: the fundamental modules, the full-weight module, its lattice and weight basis, the
diagram automorphisms of the Lie algebra and of `V_sc(t)`, and the numbered-symmetry interfaces of
the Kostant elementary group and toral closure. Mathlib: nilpotency of root vectors on a finite
dimensional module.

## Layer 2: Chevalley data, their group schemes, and root data

### 2.1 Serre's theorem for the pinned algebra

Prove *DynkinType.serreLift_injective*: `TauCeti.DynkinType.serreLift` is injective, so that with
`TauCeti.DynkinType.serreLift_surjective` it is an isomorphism of Lie algebras from the Serre
algebra `TauCeti.DynkinType.SerreLieAlgebra` onto `TauCeti.DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht`, and a
representation of the Serre algebra is one of the Lie algebra. It is the specialization of
LieHighestWeight §7.1, `serreLift_injective_of_base`, to the pinned rational lift. *Needs:*
LieHighestWeight §7.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.DynkinType.serreLift`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.serreLift_surjective`.

### 2.2 Chevalley data

A **Chevalley datum** of type `t`, *DynkinType.ChevalleyDatum* `t ht`, is a finite-dimensional
faithful representation of `TauCeti.DynkinType.lieAlgebra t ht` over `ℚ`, together with a lattice
spanning it that is stable under the simple-generator Kostant form, and a weight basis of that
lattice: a `ℤ`-basis of vectors on which each Cartan generator acts by an integer, the weights
being read in `Fin t.rank → ℤ`. Its **weight lattice** `Λ(D)` is the sublattice of
`Fin t.rank → ℤ` spanned by its weights.

- `Λ(D)` contains the root lattice `Q` and is free of rank `t.rank`.
- Fix a `ℤ`-basis `b_D : Basis (Fin t.rank) ℤ Λ(D)` and write each weight of the weight basis in
  `b_D`-coordinates. These coordinate weights span `Fin t.rank → ℤ`, by the definition of `Λ(D)`.
- The numbered generators act nilpotently on the module, which is the direct sum of its weight
  spaces (as in §1.1).
- Its group scheme *ChevalleyDatum.groupScheme*, written `G_D`, is
  `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralGroupScheme` on the module, the lattice and the
  weight basis, with the coordinate weights as `wt`. Its points over a commutative ring `A`,
  *ChevalleyDatum.Points* `D A`, form a subgroup of `GLₙ(A)` through
  `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralPointsSubgroup`.
- `V_sc(t)` with `scLattice` and `scWeightBasis` is a Chevalley datum with `Λ = Fin t.rank → ℤ`

## Layer 3: the group over a field

Let `k` be a field, `D` a Chevalley datum, and `E(k)` the subgroup of its points generated by the
root subgroups and the torus `T(k) = T_D(k) = Hom(Λ(D), kˣ)` of §2.5, not the range of the
full-weight parametrization, which can be smaller. For `G_sc(t)` the torus already lies in the
subgroup generated by the root subgroups, by §2.7. The source is Steinberg's *Lectures on Chevalley Groups*, Chapters 3 and
6, which treat every admissible lattice. Steinberg's Chevalley group `E′(k)` is the subgroup
generated by the root subgroups alone; the torus normalizes it, and each statement below extends
to `E(k)` by that normalization.

### 3.1 The Chevalley commutator formula

Prove, for every pair of linearly independent roots `α`, `β`, in Steinberg's normalization
(Convention 4),

```text
⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆ = x_α(u) x_β(v) x_α(u)⁻¹ x_β(v)⁻¹ = ∏ x_{iα+jβ}(c_{ijαβ} u^i v^j),
```

the product over `i, j > 0` with `iα + jβ` a root, in increasing `i + j` (Steinberg, *Lectures*,
Chapter 3, Lemma 15). Write `M_{α,β,i}` for the integer with
`(ad e_α)^i e_β / i! = M_{α,β,i} e_{iα+β}`, so that
`M_{α,β,i} = (1/i!) N_{α,β} N_{α,α+β} ⋯ N_{α,(i−1)α+β}`. In this convention the coefficients are
`c_{11} = N_{α,β}`, `c_{i1} = M_{α,β,i}` and `c_{1j} = −M_{β,α,j}`, and in type `G₂` the two
remaining ones are `c_{32} = (2/3) M_{α+β,α,2}` (`α` short, `β` long) and
`c_{23} = (1/3) M_{α+β,β,2}` (`α` long, `β` short). The sign of `c_{1j}` is constant: the part of
the commutator linear in `u` is `X − exp(v ad e_β) X` for `X = e_α`, that is
`−Σ_{j ≥ 1} v^j (ad e_β)^j e_α / j!`, and products of two nonconstant factors have degree at least
two in `u`. The class-two relation `x_α(t) x_β(u) x_α(t)⁻¹ = x_β(u) x_{α+β}(c t u)`, the
multiply-laced relations and the `G₂` relations of the Kostant root subgroups (supplier contracts)
are cases of it, and
the formula is proved for all pairs of the root system, not only for simple ones. *Needs:* §0.1,
§2.4; RootSystems §6.2 (long and short roots); Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.commutatorElement_kostantRootSubgroupMatrix_of_lie_eq`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.commutatorElement_kostantRootSubgroupMatrix_of_lie_lie_eq`,
the `G₂` relations under `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping/Kostant/RootSubgroup/Commutator/G2/`;
Mathlib `commutatorElement`.

**Checks.**
- `A₂`, `e_α = E₁₂`, `e_β = E₂₃`, `N_{α,β} = 1`: `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆ = x_{α+β}(uv)`. A formula with
  `c_{11} = −N_{α,β}` gives `x_{α+β}(−uv)`.
- `C₂ = Sp₄` over `ℚ` in the symplectic basis `e₁, e₂, f₁, f₂`, `α = 2e₂` long, `β = e₁ − e₂`
  short, `e_α = E₂₄`, `e_β = E₁₂ − E₄₃`, so that `⁅e_β, e_α⁆ = E₁₄ + E₂₃ = e_{α+β}` and
  `⁅e_β, e_{α+β}⁆ = 2E₁₃`, hence `M_{β,α,2} = 1`: `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆ = x_{α+β}(−uv) x_{α+2β}(−uv²)`,
  whose `(1, 3)` entry at `u = v = 1` is `−1`. The sign `(−1)^j` in `c_{1j}` gives `+1` there, and
  agrees with the correct formula only for `j = 1`, so the `A₂` test cannot see it.
- `G₂` in its seven-dimensional representation, with weights `2α + β, α + β, α, 0, −α, −α − β,
  −2α − β`, `α` short, `e_α = E₁₂ + 2E₃₄ + E₄₅ + E₆₇` and `e_β = E₂₃ + E₅₆`: the commutator
  `⁅x_α(u), x_β(v)⁆` equals the ordered product of the exponentials of `uv [e_α, e_β]`,
  `u²v (ad e_α)² e_β / 2`, `u³v (ad e_α)³ e_β / 6` and `u³v² (ad [e_α, e_β])² e_α / 3`, the last
  being `c_{32}` with `c_{32} = (2/3) M_{α+β,α,2}`; the same matrices with the roles exchanged
  confirm `c_{23} = (1/3) M_{α+β,β,2}`. Negating any one coefficient makes the two sides differ,
  since the root vector it multiplies acts nontrivially on this module.

### 3.2 The unipotent radical and Borel subgroup

`U(k)` is the subgroup generated by the positive root subgroups. Prove that every element of
`U(k)` has a unique expression `∏_{α > 0} x_α(u_α)` in any fixed order of the positive roots (for
a split reductive group over any nonempty base, Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Theorem 5.1.13,
p. 143); that `B(k) = U(k) T(k)` with `U(k)` normal in `B(k)`; and, for the opposite group `U⁻(k)`
generated by the negative root subgroups and `B⁻(k) = U⁻(k) T(k)`, that `U(k) ∩ B⁻(k) = 1`.
*Needs:* §3.1, §2.6.

### 3.3 The Weyl group

`N(k)` is the subgroup generated by `T(k)` and the `n_α(1)`. Prove that `N(k)/T(k)` is isomorphic to
the Weyl group of `TauCeti.DynkinType.simplyConnectedRootDatum` by `n_α ↦ s_α`, and that
`N(k) ∩ B(k) = T(k)`. For a split reductive group, `N_G(T)/T` is the constant group of the Weyl
group and `n_α(1)` maps to `s_α` (Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Proposition 5.1.6, p. 138, and
Corollary 5.1.11, p. 142); over an algebraically closed field the Borel subgroups containing `T`
correspond bijectively to the positive systems (Proposition 1.4.4, p. 34), on which the Weyl group
acts simply transitively, so an element of `N ∩ B` lies in `T`. *Needs:* §§2.4–2.6, §3.2.

### 3.4 The Bruhat decomposition and the Tits system

Prove `E(k) = ⊔_w B(k) n_w B(k)`, with unique normal form `u n_w b` for `b ∈ B(k)` and `u` in
`U(k) ∩ n_w U⁻(k) n_w⁻¹` (Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3, Theorem 4 and Theorem 4′), and
construct a `TauCeti.TitsSystem` on `E(k)` with `B = B(k)`, `N = N(k)` and the simple reflections
`s_{α_i}`. *Needs:* §§3.1–3.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TitsSystem`.

**Checks.**
- `SL₂` over `𝔽_q`: the cells are `B` and `U n B`, of sizes `q(q − 1)` and `q · q(q − 1)`, and
  `q(q − 1) + q²(q − 1) = q(q² − 1) = |SL₂(𝔽_q)|`. For `w = 1` the group `U ∩ U⁻` is trivial;
  allowing `u` to range over all of `U(k)` there would represent each element of `B(k)` `q` times
  and break uniqueness.

### 3.5 Centralizer and centre

For `k` infinite, prove that the centralizer of `T(k)` is `T(k)` and its normalizer is `N(k)`. For
every field `k`, prove that the centre of `E(k)` is `{h ∈ T(k) : α(h) = 1 for every root α}`
(Steinberg, *Lectures*, Chapter 3, Lemma 28(d), there for the subgroup generated by the root
subgroups), which is `Hom(Λ(D)/Q, kˣ)`, and `Hom(P/Q, kˣ)` for `G_sc(t)`. *Needs:* §3.4.

**Checks.**
- `SL₂ = G_sc(A₁)`: over `ℚ` the centre is `{±1} = Hom(P/Q, ℚˣ)`, the torus points `diag(s, s⁻¹)`
  with `α = s² = 1`. Over `𝔽₂` it is trivial: `Hom(P/Q, 𝔽₂ˣ) = μ₂(𝔽₂) = 1`, and
  `SL₂(𝔽₂) ≅ S₃` has trivial centre. A statement giving a centre of order `|P/Q|` over every field
  fails there.
- For `k = 𝔽₂` the torus `T(𝔽₂)` is trivial, so the centralizer of `T(k)` is all of `E(k)`; this is
  why the centralizer statement assumes `k` infinite.

### 3.6 Steinberg's presentation in the simply connected case

For `G_sc(t)`, prove that `E(k)` is presented by the generators `x_α(u)`, `α` a root and `u ∈ k`,
subject to additivity in `u`, the commutator formula of §3.1 for `α ≠ −β`, and, writing `h_α(s)`
for the word of Convention 3, `h_α(st) = h_α(s) h_α(t)` for `s, t ∈ kˣ` (Steinberg, *Lectures*,
Chapter 6, Theorem 8, for an irreducible root system). In rank one the commutator formula is empty
and the presentation uses Steinberg's relation `n_α(s) x_α(u) n_α(s)⁻¹ = x_{−α}(−s⁻² u)` instead;
include it. State it as a `MulEquiv` from the `PresentedGroup` on that relator set, and prove the
consequence used in Layer 5: a map on the generators `x_α(u)` into any group that respects these
relations extends uniquely to a homomorphism out of `E(k)`. *Needs:* §§3.1–3.5, §2.7; Mathlib
`PresentedGroup`.

### Examples

- For `G_sc(A₁)` over a field, the relation `n_α(s) x_α(u) n_α(s)⁻¹ = x_{−α}(−s⁻² u)` holds with

## Layer 4: flatness, the big cell, and points

The toral closure is defined over `ℤ`, and without further proof its base change to a field `k`
only contains the closed subgroup scheme that the root subgroups and the torus generate over `k`.
This layer obtains equality and identifies the points: the integral open cell, smoothness, the
fibres and that equality are ReductiveGroups §9B.1's, transported along §2.9, and the big cell and
the points over fields are identified here. Statements are for a Chevalley datum `D` with group
scheme `G_D` and torus `T_D` of Layer 2; `G_sc(t)` is the case `D = (scModule, scLattice)`.

### 4.1 Flatness

Instantiate `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isTorsionFree_kostantToralCoordinateHopfAlgebra`
at the actual representation, lattice, generators, weight basis and coordinate weights of `D`:
the quotient coordinate Hopf algebra is torsion free, hence flat over `ℤ` and the schematic
closure of its generic fibre. Preserve the actual parameter equality through the realization
isomorphism of §2.9. This consumes that theorem; it supplies neither specialization nor
smoothness by itself. *Needs:* §2.2, §2.9; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.isTorsionFree_kostantToralCoordinateHopfAlgebra`.

### 4.2 The big cell

Order the weights of `D` linearly, refining the dominance order (`μ` before `ν` when `μ − ν` is a
nonzero sum of positive roots), and order the weight basis compatibly, so that the weight spaces
are consecutive blocks. Then `U` acts by block upper unitriangular matrices, `U⁻` by block lower
unitriangular ones, and `T_D` by diagonal ones. Let `GLₙ^LDU` be the open subscheme of `GLₙ` over
`ℤ` where the leading principal minors at the block boundaries are invertible; block `LDU`
factorization identifies it with (block lower unitriangular) × (block diagonal) × (block upper
unitriangular). The big cell is `Ω_D = G_D ∩ GLₙ^LDU`. Prove that the multiplication morphism
`U⁻ × T_D × U → Ω_D` is an isomorphism over `ℤ`, so that `Ω_D` is open in `G_D`.

On points over a field the content is `E(k) ∩ GLₙ^LDU(k) = U⁻(k) T_D(k) U(k)`. Translating the
Bruhat decomposition of §3.4 on the left by a representative `n₀` of the longest element, which
conjugates `U` onto `U⁻` and normalizes `T_D`, gives
`E(k) = n₀ E(k) = ⊔_w U⁻(k) T_D(k) n₀ n_w U(k)`, so `g ∈ U⁻(k) T_D(k) n_w U(k)` for some `w`. For
`l` block lower triangular and `u` block upper unitriangular, the leading block minors of `l n_w u`
are invertible exactly when those of `n_w` are. `n_w` maps the weight-`μ` block onto the weight-`wμ`
block, so its leading block minor at an initial segment `S` of weights is invertible only if
`wS = S`. If that holds for every initial segment, `w` fixes every weight; the weights of a faithful
representation span `Λ(D) ⊗ ℚ`, so `w = 1`. Over an algebraically closed field this is Steinberg,
*Lectures*, Chapter 5, Theorem 7.

Over `ℤ`, the isomorphism is ReductiveGroups §9B.1's integral open cell, transported along the
isomorphism of §2.9, which matches root subgroups and tori: §9B.1 constructs, for the pinned group,
the open immersion `U⁻ × T × U → G` with its inverse from the leading block minors in the weight
basis (for a split reductive group over any nonempty base, Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*,
Theorem 5.1.13, p. 143), and the cover of the group by the Weyl translates of its image (on
geometric fibres, from the Bruhat decomposition over an algebraically closed field, Conrad,
Corollary 1.4.14, p. 39). The transported image is `Ω_D`. It lies in `GLₙ^LDU`, because the leading
block minors of `l t u`, for `l` block lower and `u` block upper unitriangular and `t` diagonal, are
those of `t`. An open subscheme of `G_D` is determined by its points over fields; every point of
`G_D` over a field `k` lies in `E(k)` by the transported cover, and there the two open subschemes
have the same points by the equality above. `U` and `U⁻` are the closed subgroup schemes whose
points are the unique products of §3.2, so they are isomorphic to affine space of dimension the
number of positive roots.

For `G_sc(t)` the same open set is cut out by the product of the highest-weight matrix coefficients
`g ↦ ⟨v⁺ᵢ*, g v⁺ᵢ⟩` of the fundamental summands. That formula is specific to `G_sc(t)` and is not
used for a general datum. The block minors above are polynomials in the matrix entries of `GLₙ` for
every datum, so no descent is needed. *Needs:* §§3.2–3.4, §2.9; ReductiveGroups §9B.1 (the
integral open cell and the cover by its Weyl translates); Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.IsOpenImmersion`.

**Checks.**
- `SL₂`: the big cell is `{a ≠ 0}`, and `[[1, 0], [c, 1]] diag(t, t⁻¹) [[1, b], [0, 1]]` has top left
  entry `t`, so the factorization is unique with `t = a`. The highest-weight coefficient is the entry

## Layer 5: endomorphisms and their character maps

Work on `ChevalleyGroup t ht k` for a field `k`, which is `E(k)` by §4.4. Write
`torus s = ∏ᵢ h_{α_i}(s_i)` for `s : Fin t.rank → kˣ`; for a character `χ : Fin t.rank → ℤ` in
fundamental-weight coordinates, `χ(torus s) = χ(s) = TauCeti.torusCharacter s χ`. The items below
are about `G_sc(t)`, except three that name the adjoint form `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckPoints`: the
character maps for the torus parametrization `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckWeightTorusPoints`, which is
indexed the same way and is not injective in general, its kernel being `Hom(P/Q, kˣ)`; the
exceptional isogenies `TauCetiRoadmap.ChevalleyGroups.exists_specialIsogeny`; and Lang's equation
on the torus, the last two over an algebraically closed field. Over an algebraically closed field
that parametrization reaches every point of the torus `T_D(k) = Hom(Q, kˣ)` of §2.5, since `kˣ` is
divisible, and for `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂`, where `P = Q`, it does so over every field. For the other
types its image can be smaller: for adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ` it misses `[diag(2, 1)]`, which does not
lie in the group generated by the root subgroups (§2.5).

### 5.1 Character maps

An abstract endomorphism of `ChevalleyGroup t ht k` that maps the torus into itself need not act on
it through characters, even over an algebraically closed field, so a character map is supplied with
`F` and checked. The integer-matrix theorem is about morphisms of split tori as group schemes
(Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, proof of Theorem 1.1.19), not about homomorphisms of their
groups of points.

For a group `G` with a torus parametrization `T : (Fin r → kˣ) →* G`, an endomorphism `F` of `G` and
`φ : Module.End ℤ (Fin r → ℤ)`, the predicate is Tau Ceti's

```text
TauCeti.IsCharacterMap T F φ  :↔  ∀ s, F (T s) = T (torusPointMap φ s),
```

where the `i`-th coordinate of `torusPointMap φ s` is `TauCeti.torusCharacter s (φ (Pi.single i 1))`;
equivalently `χ(F (T s)) = (φ χ)(s)` for every character `χ`, the pullback `χ ↦ χ ∘ F`. It is used
with `T = torus` on `G_sc(t)` and `T = TauCeti.DynkinType.geckWeightTorusPoints` on the adjoint form.
Its composition law (`IsCharacterMap T F φ` and `IsCharacterMap T F′ ψ` give
`IsCharacterMap T (F ∘ F′) (ψ ∘ φ)`), uniqueness over an infinite field for an injective `T`, and
surjectivity on the torus for injective `φ` when `kˣ` is divisible are Tau Ceti's (supplier
contracts). Define the parametrization *DynkinType.chevalleyTorusPoints* `t ht k`, the
homomorphism `s ↦ torus s` into `ChevalleyGroup t ht k`, and prove:

- it is the weight torus of the datum `V_sc(t)`, `s` acting on a weight vector of weight `μ` by
  `TauCeti.torusCharacter s μ`, because `⟨ϖ_j, α_i^∨⟩ = δ_ij`;
- it is injective, because the weights of `V_sc(t)` span `P`; so over an infinite field a character
  map of an endomorphism of `ChevalleyGroup t ht k` for `torus` is unique, while over a finite field
  `φ` is not determined by `F`, which is why it is data rather than a function of `F`;
- if `k` is algebraically closed and `φ` is injective, `F` maps the torus onto itself: `kˣ` is
  divisible, hence an injective `ℤ`-module, so the dual of an injective `φ` is surjective on
  `Hom(Fin t.rank → ℤ, kˣ)`.

*Needs:* §2.5, §2.7, §4.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCharacterMap`, `TauCeti.torusPointMap`,
`TauCeti.torusCharacter`, `TauCeti.IsCharacterMap.comp`, `TauCeti.IsCharacterMap.unique`,
`TauCeti.IsCharacterMap.map_range_eq`, `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralWeightTorusPoints`,
`TauCeti.IsAlgClosed.unitsRootableByNat`.

**Checks.**
- Complex conjugation, applied entrywise to `SL₂(ℂ)`, preserves the diagonal torus and every root
  subgroup and acts on the torus coordinate by `z ↦ z̄`. It has no character map: `z̄ = z^m` at
  `z = 2` forces `m = 1`, and `z = i` contradicts it. The same happens for the ambient groups of
  the CFSG roadmaps: `Gal(𝔽̄_p/𝔽_p) ≅ Ẑ`, and an element of `Ẑ` outside `ℤ` gives an automorphism of
  `𝔽̄_pˣ` that is not an integer power.
- Over `𝔽_q`, `Frob_q` is the identity of `SL₂(𝔽_q)`, and both `id` and `q • id` are character maps
  of it, since `s^q = s` on `𝔽_qˣ`; uniqueness needs an infinite field.

### 5.2 Field maps and the Frobenius

### 5.5 Determination by simple root subgroups

For a Chevalley datum `D` and a field `k`, prove that two homomorphisms from `E(k)` to a group that

- Adjoint `A₁` over `ℚ`, `E(ℚ) = PGL₂(ℚ)`: `δ([A]) = v₂(det A) mod 2` is a well-defined homomorphism

### 5.6 Standard and based endomorphisms

For `k` algebraically closed, an endomorphism `F` with an injective `φ` satisfying
`IsCharacterMap torus F φ` is *standard* if it maps each root subgroup onto a root subgroup, and
*based* if moreover it maps each positive root subgroup onto a positive root subgroup, that is, it
preserves the Borel subgroup `B(k)` as well as the torus. Prove that the root permutation of a
standard map, and the field endomorphisms on its root subgroups, are read from `φ`. For an
isogeny of split reductive group schemes carrying the split torus into the split torus, each root
group goes onto a root group by `u ↦ c u^q`, and the map on characters determines the bijection of
roots and the exponents (Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, p. 187, with Definition 6.1.8, p. 191).
*Route.* For standard `F` with `F(x_α(u)) = x_β(f(u))`, torus compatibility gives
`f(α(t) u) = (φ β)(t) f(u)` for all `t`. Taking `t` in the kernel of `α` shows that `φ β` is trivial there, hence a rational multiple
of `α`. Since `α(T(k)) = kˣ`, `f = f(1) · g` for the field endomorphism `g` of `k` determined by
`g(α(t)) = (φ β)(t)`. `φ` is injective and the root system is reduced, so `φ` determines `β` up to
sign. It determines the sign too: if a second standard map with the same `φ` sent `x_α` into
`x_{−β}`, its field endomorphism `g′` would satisfy `g g′ = 1` on `kˣ`, and expanding
`g(1 + c) g′(1 + c) = 1` gives `g(c)² + g(c) + 1 = 0` for every `c ≠ 0, −1`, so `g` takes at most two
values there, contradicting injectivity of `g` on the infinite field `k`. *Needs:* §5.1, §2.6, §3.2.

**Checks.**
- On `SL₂` over `𝔽̄_p`, let `n = [[0, 1], [−1, 0]]` and `F = Int(n) ∘ Frob_p`. It preserves the
  diagonal torus, exchanges the two root subgroups, and `F² = Frob_{p²}` because `n² = −1` is
  central. `IsCharacterMap torus F (−p • id)` holds, so `F` is standard and not based; record this as
  an example. On the root datum it is the isogeny `s ∘ (p · id)` of the simply connected `A₁` datum,
  `s` the reflection, with weight map `−p · id` and square `p² · id`; the automorphism group `{±id}`
  of that datum is abelian, so no conjugation moves it to a positive scaling, and a normal form for
  such isogenies holds only for based ones, those preserving the positive system.

### 5.7 Rigidity up to the torus, and Lang's equation on the torus

For `k` algebraically closed, prove that two standard endomorphisms `F`, `F′` with
`IsCharacterMap torus F φ` and `IsCharacterMap torus F′ φ` for the same `φ` satisfy `F = Int(s) ∘ F′`
for a torus point `s`; for isogenies of reductive group schemes that agree on a maximal torus this
is Conrad, *Reductive group schemes*, Corollary 6.1.15, p. 195. *Route.* By §5.6, they send
`x_{±α_i}(u)` to `x_{±β_i}(c_{±i} g_{±i}(u))` and `x_{±β_i}(c′_{±i} g_{±i}(u))`, with `β_i` and the field endomorphisms `g_{±i}` read from `φ`. The `β_i`
are linearly independent because `φ` is injective, and `kˣ` is divisible, so there is `s` with
`β_i(s) = c_i / c′_i` for all `i`. `F` and `F′` map the torus onto itself (§5.1), so they send
`n_{α_i}(1)` into the normalizer of the torus, and the rank-one computation
`x_β(c) x_{−β}(−c₋) x_β(c) = [[1 − c c₋, c (2 − c c₋)], [−c₋, 1 − c c₋]]` shows that this forces
`c₋ = c⁻¹`, for `F` and for `F′`. Determination by the simple root subgroups (§5.5) finishes. Also
record how conjugation by a Weyl representative `n_w` and by `γ_σ` changes the character map: by `w`
and by the diagram automorphism of `σ`.

Rigidity gives `F = Int(s) ∘ F′`, which is not yet `F = Int(t) ∘ F′ ∘ Int(t)⁻¹`. Since
`Int(t) ∘ F′ ∘ Int(t)⁻¹ = Int(t F′(t)⁻¹) ∘ F′`, the missing step is solving `s = t F′(t)⁻¹` modulo the
centre. Prove, as a named theorem, Lang's equation on the torus of `G_sc(t)`: for `k` algebraically
closed of characteristic `p` and `IsCharacterMap torus F′ φ` with `φ ^ n = p ^ r • id` for some
`n, r > 0`, the homomorphism `t ↦ t F′(t)⁻¹` of `T(k)` is surjective. Its character map is `1 − φ`.
Every complex eigenvalue of `φ` has absolute value `p ^ (r / n) > 1` when `p > 0`, and `φ` is
nilpotent when `p = 0`; in both cases `1 − φ` is injective, and `T(k) = Hom(Fin t.rank → ℤ, kˣ)`
with `kˣ` divisible makes its dual surjective. This is the torus case of the Lang–Steinberg theorem
and needs nothing beyond that linear algebra: it is
`TauCeti.IsCharacterMap.exists_eq_mul_inv` for `T = torus`, with
`TauCeti.injective_one_sub_of_pow_eq_smul_one` supplying injectivity of `1 − φ`. On the adjoint form
it is `TauCeti.DynkinType.exists_geckWeightTorusPoints_eq_mul_inv`: over a ring of characteristic `p`
with divisible units, such as an algebraically closed field, an endomorphism `F` of `geckPoints t ht k`
with `IsCharacterMap geckWeightTorusPoints F φ` and `φ ^ n = p ^ r • 1`, `r > 0`, has every torus point
`T s` of the form `T u · F(T u)⁻¹`, the surjection on parameters mapping onto the image of `T`.

*Needs:* §§5.1, 5.5, 5.6, §2.6; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCharacterMap.exists_eq_mul_inv`,
`TauCeti.injective_one_sub_of_pow_eq_smul_one`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.exists_geckWeightTorusPoints_eq_mul_inv`.

**Checks.**
- `F = Frob_q` on `SL₂(𝔽̄_p)`, `φ = q • id`: the equation `s = u · u^(−q)` is solvable for every
  `s ∈ 𝔽̄_pˣ` because `x^(q−1) = s` has a root.
- Characteristic zero, `p = 0`: then `φ ^ n = 0`, `1 − φ` is unipotent, and the conclusion still
  holds. The hypothesis `r > 0` cannot be dropped: `F = id` with `φ = id` satisfies
  `φ ^ 1 = p ^ 0 • id`, and `u F(u)⁻¹ = 1` misses every nontrivial torus point.

### 5.8 Steinberg endomorphisms

For `F` a composite `γ_σ ∘ Frob_q` with `q = p^m`, `m > 0`, or an odd power of `τ`, prove that some
positive power of `F` is a Frobenius `Frob_{p^m}` with `m > 0`, that `F` is based when `k` is
algebraically closed (§5.6), and that its character map is the composite of the root-datum maps
above. This is the group-level form of
`TauCeti.ValidLieTypeIndex.exists_pow_datumSteinberg_eq_smulId`. *Route* (this roadmap's own
argument). `γ_σ` commutes with `Frob_q` and `γ_σ γ_τ = γ_στ` (§5.3), so `F^n = Frob_{q^n}` for `n` the order of `σ`; for
`F = τ^{2j+1}`, `F² = (τ ∘ τ)^{2j+1} = Frob_{p^{2j+1}}` (§5.4). `γ_σ`, `Frob_q` and `τ` carry positive
root subgroups to positive ones, and the character map of a composite is the composite of the
character maps (§5.1). *Needs:* §5.1, §§5.2–5.4, §5.6; Tau Ceti

## Layer 6: Chevalley's comparison theorem

For Chevalley data `D` and `D′` of the same type `t` whose weight lattices satisfy
`Λ(D′) ⊆ Λ(D)`, the comparison theorem is a homomorphism of group schemes `G_D → G_D′` over `ℤ`
with `x_α(u) ↦ x_α(u)` for every root, whose kernel on points over a field is central and finite.
When the lattices are equal it is an isomorphism. The group-level map on `E(k)` is constructed over
every field in two steps (§§6.1–6.2); the scheme map is the central isogeny of ReductiveGroups
§9B.2, which §6.3 identifies with it on points.

### 6.1 On the elementary subgroup

Let `E′_D(k)` be the subgroup generated by the root subgroups alone, Steinberg's Chevalley group.
Construct `φ′ : E′_D(k) → E′_D′(k)` with `x_α(u) ↦ x_α(u)`, following Steinberg, *Lectures*,
Chapter 3, Corollary 5 to Theorem 4′: the relations (R) of that chapter together with the relations
among the `h_α(t)` define `E′_D(k)` (Corollaries 3 and 4 there, consequences of the Bruhat
decomposition of §3.4), and `Λ(D′) ⊆ Λ(D)` gives the required map between the tori (Lemma 28(c)
there). These words `h_α(t)` lie in `E′_D(k)` and do not reach all of `T_D(k)`: for adjoint `A₁`
over `ℚ` they miss `[diag(2, 1)]`. *Needs:* §§3.1–3.4, §2.6.

### 6.2 Extension by the actual torus

On the tori of §2.5 the map is restriction of characters,
`res : T_D = Hom(Λ(D), −) → Hom(Λ(D′), −) = T_D′`; on points over a field it need not be surjective,
and for `G_sc(A₁) → PGL₂` over `ℚ` its cokernel is `ℚˣ/ℚˣ²`. Prove that `E(k) = E′_D(k) T_D(k)`,
that `E′_D(k) ∩ T_D(k)` is the subgroup `H_D(k)` generated by the `h_α(s)` (from the uniqueness in
the Bruhat decomposition of §3.4), that `res` sends `h_α(s)` to `h_α(s)`, and that the map on
`E′_D(k)` intertwines conjugation by `t ∈ T_D(k)` with conjugation by `res(t)`, which on generators is
the pinning equation of §2.6 with `α(res(t)) = α(t)` because `α` lies in the root lattice, contained
in `Λ(D′)`. Then `e t ↦ φ′(e) res(t)` is a well-defined homomorphism `E_D(k) → E_D′(k)`: it is the
homomorphism `E′_D(k) ⋊ T_D(k) → E_D′(k)` given by the two maps, which kills the antidiagonal copy of
`H_D(k)`, and `E(k)` is the quotient by that copy. The argument is this roadmap's own. *Needs:*
§6.1, §2.6, §3.4.

### 6.3 Extension to the group schemes and consequences

The homomorphism of group schemes `G_D → G_D′` over `ℤ` is ReductiveGroups §9B.2's central
isogeny for the inclusion `Λ(D′) ⊆ Λ(D)` of weight spans, with scheme kernel `D(Λ(D)/Λ(D′))` and an
isomorphism when the lattices are equal (for split reductive groups over any nonempty base, Conrad,
*Reductive group schemes*, Theorem 6.1.16(1), p. 196). Prove that on points over every field `k`,
which are `E_D(k)` and `E_D′(k)` by §4.4, it is the map of §6.2: both are the identity on every root
subgroup and restriction of characters on `T_D(k)`, which generate `E_D(k)`. Prove the
consequences: every Chevalley datum of type `t` with full weight lattice has group scheme isomorphic
to `G_sc(t)` by an isomorphism matching root subgroups, tori and Frobenius, and intertwining the
graph automorphisms and exceptional isogenies where the datum carries them; and the homomorphism
from `G_sc(t)` to the adjoint form *DynkinType.adjointGroupScheme* `t` matches root subgroups and
has kernel on points over every field the centre of §3.5. Over `ℤ` the existence of that
homomorphism is `TauCetiRoadmap.ChevalleyGroups.exists_scGroupScheme_hom_geckGroupScheme`: for every
valid `t`, given the nilpotency of §1.1, there is a morphism of group schemes over `ℤ` from the
toral closure of `V_sc(t)` (§2.3) to `geckGroupScheme t ht` whose composite with each numbered root
subgroup `kostantRootSubgroupToToral` is the Geck root subgroup `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckRootSubgroup`
of the same index. On points this last consequence is
`TauCetiRoadmap.ChevalleyGroups.exists_scToGeckPoints`: for every valid `t`, given the nilpotency
of §1.1, and every field `k`, there is a homomorphism from the points over `k` of the toral closure
of `V_sc(t)` (§2.3) to `geckPoints t ht k` that sends each numbered root subgroup
`kostantToralRootSubgroupPoints` to the Geck root subgroup `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckRootSubgroupPoints`
of the same index, and whose kernel is the centre of the source. *Needs:* §6.2, §1.1, §2.3, §3.5,
§4.4, §§5.2–5.4 (for the intertwining); ReductiveGroups §9B.2 (the central isogeny for an inclusion
of weight spans); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantToralRootSubgroupPoints`,
`TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.kostantRootSubgroupToToral`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckGroupScheme`,
`TauCeti.DynkinType.geckRootSubgroup`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.geckRootSubgroupPoints`; Mathlib
`MonoidHom.ker`, `Subgroup.center`.

**Checks.**
- `SL₂ → PGL₂` over `𝔽₂`: the kernel on points is `μ₂(𝔽₂) = 1`, the centre of `SL₂(𝔽₂)`, and both
  groups have order six, so the map is an isomorphism on points over `𝔽₂` although the scheme map is
  not an isomorphism. Over `ℚ` its kernel is `{±1}` and its cokernel `ℚˣ/ℚˣ²` (§6.2).

## Layer 7: exceptional isogenies of group schemes

### 7.1 The exceptional isogenies over 𝔽_p

For the three pairs of §5.4, the endomorphism `τ` of the `𝔽_p`-group scheme `G_sc(t)_𝔽p` with
`τ ∘ τ = Frob_p` as morphisms of schemes is the special isogeny of ReductiveGroups §9B ("Special
isogenies in characteristics two and three"), transported along the base change to `𝔽_p` of the
isomorphism of §2.9. Prove that on points over every field `k` of characteristic `p` it is the
endomorphism of §5.4 when the two have the same signs on the simple root subgroups, and in general
up to conjugation by a point of the torus. Both send `x_{±α_i}(u)` to `x_{±α_σi}(±u ^ e_i)`, with
the same sign at `α_i` and at `−α_i`. The signs are trivial in characteristic two. In
characteristic three, write `β = α_σi` and `c`, `c₋` for the signs at `α_i` and `−α_i`; since `e_i`
is odd, `n_{α_i}(1)` goes to `x_β(c) x_{−β}(−c₋) x_β(c)`, the rank-one matrix of §5.7, which for
`c₋ = −c` is `[[−1, 0], [c, −1]]`, with square `x_{−β}(c)` of order three, whereas
`n_{α_i}(1)² = h_{α_i}(−1)` has square `1`, so no homomorphism has `c₋ = −c`. In type `G₂` the
simple roots are a basis of `P`, so a torus point removes the difference between the signs of the
two maps; two homomorphisms out of `E(k) = G_sc(t)(k)` (§4.4) that agree on the simple root
subgroups are equal (§5.5). That `τ` is an isogeny with infinitesimal kernel belongs to
ReductiveGroups Layer 9. *Needs:* §2.9, §4.4, §§5.4–5.5, §5.7; ReductiveGroups §9B (special
isogenies).

**Checks.**
- Over `𝔽̄_p` the map of points of `τ` is bijective, the field being perfect, but `τ` is not an
  isomorphism of schemes: `τ ∘ τ = Frob_p`, and the Frobenius of a group scheme of positive dimension
  over `𝔽_p` is not an isomorphism. A bijection on points over `𝔽̄_p` does not determine an
  isomorphism.

### Examples

- `B₂` in characteristic two: `τ` agrees with `TauCeti.SpStd.specialIsogeny` under the isomorphism
  `G_sc(B₂) ≅ Sp₄` of §6.3 (Worked examples).

## Scope and ownership

**This roadmap owns** everything about the explicitly constructed Chevalley group schemes and their
points that is proved from the construction, by the methods of Chevalley, Steinberg and Demazure. The
general theory is not in scope, and the dividing line is one question: *is the statement about a
specified integral Chevalley model or its explicit realization, proved through the
constructor and comparison interface?* By layer: the integer structure constants on the root

### From TauCetiRoadmap.ReductiveGroups

- §9B, the Chevalley–Demazure construction: for each root datum, the pinned split reductive group
  scheme over `ℤ` built as the Kostant toral closure of an admissible lattice, with its base change, its
  root subgroup maps, its change-of-data isomorphisms (§§9B.2–9B.3) and its certification as split
  reductive with the specified root datum. Consumed by §2.9.
- §9B.1, integral open cells: the open immersion `U⁻ × T × U⁺ → G` over `ℤ` with its inverse from the
  leading block minors in the weight basis, the cover of `G` by its Weyl translates, smoothness,
  relative dimension `rank X + |Φ|`, geometrically connected fibres, and over every field the equality
  of the base change with the subgroup scheme generated by the reduced root maps and the torus.
  Consumed, transported along §2.9, by §§4.2–4.4.
- §9B.2, central quotients and realizations: for an inclusion of weight spans of two realizations, the
  central isogeny between their group schemes, with scheme kernel the diagonalizable group of the
  quotient lattice, identifying root subgroup parameters and restricting characters on the torus, and
  an isomorphism when the lattices are equal. Consumed by §6.3.

  `F₄` and `G₂` as endomorphisms of the pinned simply connected group scheme over `𝔽_p`, with
