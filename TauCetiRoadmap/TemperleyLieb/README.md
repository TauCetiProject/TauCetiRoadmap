# Roadmap: Temperley–Lieb diagrams, categories, and algebras

**Summary.** The Temperley–Lieb category is the smallest interesting diagram category. A morphism
`n → m` is a planar matching of the `n + m` boundary points of a rectangle, `n` on the bottom and
`m` on the top, together with a number of closed circles. Composition stacks one diagram on
another and adds the circles closed up in the middle; the tensor product places diagrams side by
side. Over a commutative ring `R` with a loop parameter `δ ∈ R`, the linear Temperley–Lieb
category `TL(R, δ)` has the free `R`-modules on planar matchings as hom-modules, and a closed
circle evaluates to `δ`. Its endomorphism algebras are the Temperley–Lieb algebras `TL_n(R, δ)`,
presented by generators `e_1, …, e_(n−1)` with `e_i² = δ e_i`, `e_i e_(i±1) e_i = e_i` and
commutation at distance. Writing `δ = q + q⁻¹`, the quantum integers are `[0] = 0`, `[1] = 1`,
`[n + 2] = δ[n + 1] − [n]`, the values at `δ` of the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.
The category is the skein theory of the Kauffman bracket, Jones's tower of algebras, and the
representation category of quantum `sl₂`, seen without a quantum group.

The layers below complete the quantum integers and planar matchings (Layers 0 and 1), then build
the diagram category and its presentation (Layer 2), the linear category with its pivotal,
spherical and braided structure (Layer 3), the algebras with their trace (Layer 4), cell modules and
semisimplicity (Layer 5), the Jones–Wenzl projections and the tensor ideals (Layer 6), and the
Karoubi envelope with its quotients at roots of unity (Layer 7). The headline theorems are these.

- Strong monoidal functors from the diagram category to a monoidal category `C` correspond, up to
  monoidal isomorphism, to objects `V` of `C` with a self-duality `η : 𝟙 → V ⊗ V`,
  `ε : V ⊗ V → 𝟙` satisfying the two snake equations, on which the circle `η ≫ ε` acts in the same
  way from the left and from the right. For `TL(R, δ)`, the `R`-linear strong monoidal functors
  into an `R`-linear monoidal category correspond to the self-dualities with `η ≫ ε = δ`.
- For every commutative ring `R` and every `δ ∈ R`, the algebra on `n` strands presented by the
  Temperley–Lieb relations is a free `R`-module with a basis indexed by the planar matchings of
  `2n` points, of rank the Catalan number `catalan n`, and it carries exactly one family of
  unnormalized Markov traces.
- Over a field `K`, `TL_n(K, δ)` is semisimple when `[1], …, [n]` are nonzero. If `K` has
  characteristic zero, it is semisimple exactly when `[1], …, [n]` are nonzero, or `δ = 0` and `n`
  is odd.
- Over a field of characteristic zero, let `ℓ` be the least positive integer with `[ℓ] = 0`. The
  negligible morphisms form the unique proper nonzero tensor ideal of `TL(K, δ)`, generated by the
  Jones–Wenzl projection on `ℓ − 1` strands (Goodman–Wenzl, over `ℂ`). Over an algebraically
  closed such field, the quotient of the Karoubi envelope by it is a fusion category with simple
  objects `X_0, …, X_(ℓ−2)` and truncated Clebsch–Gordan fusion rules.

The roadmap builds on Tau Ceti's presented Temperley–Lieb algebra, with its Jones–Wenzl projections
and the uniqueness of its Markov trace, and on Tau Ceti's quantum integers, planar matchings and
diagrams, cellular bases, matrix and Karoubi envelopes and tensor-ideal quotients.
[PivotalSpherical](../PivotalSpherical/README.md) owns the theory of pivotal and fusion
categories: this roadmap gives it its first diagrammatic examples, and its root-of-unity quotients
meet that roadmap's fusion hypotheses. The Schur–Weyl roadmap of
[RepresentationTheory](../RepresentationTheory/SchurWeyl/README.md) builds the Brauer algebra, of
which the Temperley–Lieb algebra is the planar part, and its Brauer diagrams supply the
fixed-size stacking used here. [TemperleyLiebOperatorAlgebras](../TemperleyLiebOperatorAlgebras/README.md)
and [QuantumGroups](../QuantumGroups/README.md) take the diagram algebra, its trace, the cell forms,
the negligible ideal and the envelope from here.

## How to read the build

The layers are in dependency order. Layers 0 and 1 are independent of everything else and of each
other, and are where the encodings are fixed. Layer 2 carries the load-bearing combinatorics: the
associativity of gluing (§2.1) and the presentation theorem (§2.6). Layer 3 is wide, each structure
a separate target once the linear category (§3.1) exists; its pivotal, spherical and ribbon
targets are stated against Tau Ceti's pivotal vocabulary. Layer 4 sits on Layer 3. In Layer 5 the
general cellular theory (§5.1) can start at any time, while the Temperley–Lieb cell structure needs
Layer 4. Layer 6 needs Layers 0 and 4 for the recursions and traces, and Layer 5's pairings for the
tensor ideals. Layer 7 consumes everything except its first two sections, which are general
category theory, depend on nothing else here, and can start at once; they are on the critical path
to the fusion quotients. Each layer ends with its examples and its dependencies, and each target's
`*Needs:*` line lists what its statement and its route use, so that a target proved by another
route is not blocked behind an unrelated one.

## Conventions

- **The loop parameter is `δ = q + q⁻¹`**: a closed circle evaluates to `δ`. Everything is stated
  in terms of `δ` and quantum integers wherever possible; `q` appears only where a statement needs
  it (root-of-unity phrasing, the braiding). The Kauffman variable `A` has `δ = −A² − A⁻²`, so
  `q = −A²`; the relation between the three is a classical trap. Tingley's note explains where the
  sign comes from (a square root of the quantum-group parameter is chosen both in the braiding and
  in the ribbon element) and how a non-standard ribbon element removes it (Tingley, Introduction
  and Corollary 4.5). With `δ = q + q⁻¹` every statement through Layer 6 is sign-free; the sign
  enters only with the braiding (§3.5). Tau Ceti's spin-model trace
  `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.markovTrace q hδ` is stated for `δ = −(q + q⁻¹)`, the case `q ↦ −q` of
  this convention.
- **Quantum integers are Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.qInt R δ n`**, the value at `δ` of Mathlib's rescaled
  Chebyshev polynomial `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S R (n − 1)` at the integer index `n − 1`, which
  absorbs the off-by-one between `S_k(δ)` and `[k + 1]` once. `[n]` is prose shorthand for
  `TauCeti.qInt R δ n`; there is no bracket notation in Lean. No `q` is needed, so genericity
  hypotheses are ring-level statements about `δ`. Since `[0] = 0` always, a genericity hypothesis
  reads "`TauCeti.qInt R δ k ≠ 0` for `1 ≤ k ≤ n`" (or `IsUnit`), never "for all `k`". The
  **quantum order** of `δ` is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.quantumOrder δ : ℕ∞`, the least `ℓ ≥ 1` with
  `[ℓ] = 0` (`⊤` if there is none); root-of-unity hypotheses are phrased as `[ℓ] = 0` and
  `[k] ≠ 0` for `1 ≤ k < ℓ`, that is `TauCeti.quantumOrder δ = ℓ`. Identities of quantum integers
  are identities of Chebyshev polynomials: they are proved in `ℤ[X]` (or `ℤ[T;T⁻¹]`) about `S`, at
  that generality, and evaluated afterwards.
- **Boundary points live on a circle.** A diagram `n → m` is drawn in a rectangle and read bottom
  to top, but its boundary datum is the cyclic order: points `0, …, n − 1` along the bottom from
  left to right, then `n, …, n + m − 1` along the top from right to left (counterclockwise around
  the rectangle). A planar matching is then a fixed-point-free involution of `Fin (n + m)` whose
  pairs do not interleave, a condition invariant under rotation; the rectangle matters only for
  composition. This is Tau Ceti's numbering in `TauCeti.TLDiagram`, and it makes rotation,
  reflection and corner-dragging relabellings.
- **Composition order:** `f ≫ g` stacks `g` on top of `f`. Two composites pin this down, and every
  cup and cap composite of the roadmap is checked against them: `cup ≫ cap = δ · 𝟙` on the unit
  object (the closed circle) and `cap ≫ cup = e₁` (the endomorphism of `2`).
- **Algebra multiplication is Mathlib's.** The diagram algebra on `n` strands is Mathlib's
  `End` of the object `n`, so it inherits Mathlib's product on `End`, which reverses composition:
  `x * y = y ≫ x` (Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.End`, which "agrees with `Function.comp`"). So `x * y`
  stacks `x` on top of `y`. The relations `e_i² = δ e_i`, `e_i e_(i±1) e_i = e_i` and commutation
  at distance are symmetric enough not to notice; the Jones normal form, the Markov property and
  the Jones–Wenzl recursions are not.
- **Generators.** In prose the generators on `n` strands are one-based, `e_1, …, e_(n−1)`, with
  `e_i` capping the adjacent points `i`, `i + 1` on both boundaries. In Lean,
  `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.e δ i` with `i : Fin (n − 1)` is `e_(i+1)`. The generators of the algebra
  on `n + 1` strands are indexed by `Fin (n + 1 − 1)`, which reduces to `Fin n`; on `n + 2` strands
  the last one is `e δ (Fin.last n)`, and no truncated subtraction appears.
- **Closed circles are data, then coefficients.** In the diagram category a morphism carries its
  circle count as a natural number: discarding it would silently impose `δ = 1`, and the universal
  property needs the circle to be a nontrivial endomorphism of the unit so that linearization at
  every `δ` factors through this one category. In `TL(R, δ)` the hom-modules are free on matchings
  alone and composition multiplies by `δ^(#circles)`. The linearization functor relates the two;
  neither is quotiented silently into the other.
- **The trace is unnormalized.** The diagrammatic (Markov) trace closes a diagram to the right and
  evaluates the circles: `tr̂(1_n) = δⁿ`. Normalized traces divide by `δⁿ` and exist only when `δ`
  is invertible; they are kept separate and never silently identified.
- **Objects are one-field structures.** The objects of the diagram category and of `TL(R, δ)` wrap
  a natural number of boundary points in a one-field structure (constructor `of`), so category and
  monoidal instances hang on a dedicated type, never on `ℕ`; the tensor product adds the underlying
  numbers.
- **Universes.** The objects of the diagram category, of the free self-duality category and of
  `TL(R, δ)` live in `Type`; the hom-types of the first two are in `Type`, those of `TL(R, δ)` in
  the universe of `R`.
- **Semisimple and nondegenerate are different words.** "Nondegenerate" refers to the trace
  pairings `Hom(n, m) × Hom(m, n) → R`; "semisimple" to the algebras `TL_n`. Both classifications
  are targets, and they do not coincide statement for statement (§5.4).
- **Computability.** The combinatorial carriers (planar matchings, diagrams, half-diagrams, their
  operations and counts) are coefficient-free and decidable, so their small values are checked by
  `decide`. This does not extend to the linear objects: a bare `[CommRing R]` gives no
  `DecidableEq R`, and already `TL_0 ≅ R`.
- **Vocabulary.** Mathlib's names are used where Mathlib has the notion: `catalan`, `DyckWord`,
  `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S`, `ExactPairing`, `RigidCategory`, `BraidedCategory`,
  `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi`, `CategoryTheory.Mat_`, `IsSemisimpleRing`,
  `TwoSidedIdeal`, `StarRing`, `CategoryTheory.Simple`. Where Mathlib has none, Tau Ceti's general
  notions are used, never a Temperley–Lieb-private one: `TauCeti.PivotalCategory`,
  `TauCeti.SphericalCategory`, `TauCeti.RibbonCategory`, `TauCeti.CellularBasis`,
  `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal`, `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`, `TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory`.
- **Names.** Lean names in code font in the prose are Mathlib's, Tau Ceti's, or declared in
  `Suggested.lean`. Names proposed for new declarations are written in plain text (the diagram
  category TLDiagCat, the linear category TLCat R δ, the algebra TLAlg R δ n, the comparison
  presentedTLEquiv) and appear in code only in the intended signatures of §§2.6 and 3.1a; formulas
  use the notation `TL(R, δ)`, `TL_n`, `Φ`, `ι`, `tr̂`, `𝔼` and `f_n` introduced with them.

## Layer 0: quantum integers

The quantum integers and their identities, the `q`-side characterization, the root-of-unity
dictionary, the computable evaluator and the quantum order are Tau Ceti's (supplier contracts
below). This layer adds their expansion in powers of `q` over an arbitrary commutative ring.

### 0.1 The expansion in powers of `q`

Prove that in the Laurent polynomial ring `ℤ[T;T⁻¹]` the Laurent quantum integer
`TauCeti.laurentQuantumInteger n` is `T^(n−1) + T^(n−3) + ⋯ + T^(1−n)`, that is
`∑_(j < n) T^(2j+1−n)`, for every `n : ℕ`; then evaluate it with
`TauCeti.qInt_add_inv_eq_laurentEval`: for every commutative ring `R` and unit `q ∈ Rˣ`,
`TauCeti.qInt R (q + q⁻¹) n = ∑_(j < n) q^(2j+1−n)`, with integer powers of the unit `q`. This is
the `q`-side form of `[n]` over every commutative ring, without the hypothesis `q − q⁻¹ ≠ 0` of
Tau Ceti's field form `TauCeti.quantumInteger_natCast_eq_sum`; with Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.sub_inv_mul_qInt_add_inv` (`(q − q⁻¹)[n] = qⁿ − q⁻ⁿ`) and the root-of-unity dictionary
`TauCeti.exists_qInt_eq_zero_iff` it completes the dictionary between the `δ`-language of the
roadmap and the "`q` a root of unity" language of the literature (Tingley's note for the
convention). *Route.* Induction on `n` with the recurrence `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S_add_two` in
`ℤ[T;T⁻¹]`. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.laurentQuantumInteger`, `TauCeti.laurentEval`,
`TauCeti.qInt_add_inv_eq_laurentEval`; Mathlib `LaurentPolynomial`, `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S_add_two`.

**Checks.**

- At `q = 1` the sum has `n` terms equal to `1`, giving `[n] = n` at `δ = 2`, which is Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.qInt_two_left`. A sum in steps of one, `q^(n−1) + q^(n−2) + ⋯ + q^(1−n)`, has `2n − 1`
  terms and would give `2n − 1`.
- At `q = −1` every term is `(−1)^(n−1)`, giving `(−1)^(n−1) n` at `δ = −2`, which is
  `TauCeti.qInt_neg_two_left`.
- For `n = 3`, `q⁻² + 1 + q² = (q + q⁻¹)² − 1 = δ² − 1 = [3]`.

### Examples

- `[3] = δ² − 1`, `[4] = δ³ − 2δ`, `[5] = δ⁴ − 3δ² + 1`.
- Quantum orders: `δ = 0` has quantum order `2` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.quantumOrder_zero`), since
  `[2] = 0`; `δ = 1` has quantum order `3`; the golden ratio `δ = (1 + √5)/2` has
  `[2] = [3] = δ`, `[4] = 1`, `[5] = 0`, so quantum order `5`; `δ = ±2` has infinite quantum order
  in characteristic zero (`TauCeti.quantumOrder_two`, `TauCeti.quantumOrder_neg_two`). In
  characteristic `p`, `[n] = n` at `δ = 2` vanishes at `n = p`, so there `δ = 2 = 1 + 1⁻¹` has
  quantum order `p` although `1` is excluded from the root-of-unity dictionary.

### Dependencies

Tau Ceti's quantum integers (`TauCeti.qInt`, `TauCeti.laurentQuantumInteger` and their API);
Mathlib's Chebyshev and Laurent polynomials. Nothing else in this roadmap.

## Layer 1: planar matchings, encodings, counting, operations

The carrier and its operations are Tau Ceti's: `TauCeti.PlanarMatching k`, the subtype of
`TauCeti.PerfectMatching (Fin k)` cut out by `Equiv.Perm.IsNoncrossingMatching`, so that the
perfect-matching API (finiteness, decidability, transport, arc restriction) applies unchanged; its
rotation and reflection with the dihedral relations; the innermost cup lemma; the Dyck-word
equivalence with the Catalan count; the equivalence with noncrossing pair partitions; the
half-diagrams `TauCeti.HalfDiagram n k` with their count `TauCeti.halfCount n k`; and the
Temperley–Lieb diagrams `TauCeti.TLDiagram n m`, a planar matching of `n + m` points with a circle
count, with rotation, corner-dragging and the two reflections and the laws relating them. This
layer adds the compatibility of the pairing equivalence with rotation and reflection, and the
count of diagrams by their through-strands.

### 1.1 Planar matchings as noncrossing pair partitions

For `α = Fin k`, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.PerfectMatching.noncrossingPairingEquiv` identifies
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching k` with the noncrossing partitions of `Fin k` (`TauCeti.NoncrossingPartition`)
all of whose blocks are pairs, sending a matching `x` to the partition with blocks `{i, x i}`, and
`TauCeti.PerfectMatching.card_isNoncrossingMatching_eq_catalan` transports the Catalan count.
Prove that it intertwines the operations: the partition of `TauCeti.PlanarMatching.rotate k x` is
`TauCeti.NoncrossingPartition.rotate k` of the partition of `x`, and the partition of
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.reflect k x` is `TauCeti.NoncrossingPartition.reverse k` of the partition of
`x`; in particular both operations on noncrossing partitions preserve the pair partitions. The
involution and Dyck models, and the diagram constructions on them, stay with planar matchings; this
needs nothing analytic. *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PerfectMatching.noncrossingPairingEquiv`, `TauCeti.PlanarMatching.rotate`,
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.reflect`, `TauCeti.NoncrossingPartition.rotate`,
`TauCeti.NoncrossingPartition.reverse`.

**Checks.**

- For `k = 6` and the matching with arcs `{0, 1}`, `{2, 5}`, `{3, 4}`, rotation gives the arcs
  `{1, 2}`, `{3, 0}`, `{4, 5}`, and the rotated partition is `{0, 3}`, `{1, 2}`, `{4, 5}` on both
  sides. The inverse rotation would give `{0, 5}`, `{1, 4}`, `{2, 3}`, so the check fixes the
  direction of the rotation.
- The crossing pairing `{0, 2}`, `{1, 3}` of `Fin 4` is a perfect matching, but neither a planar
  matching nor, as a partition, noncrossing. The two planar matchings of `Fin 4`, the nested
  `{0, 3}`, `{1, 2}` and the side-by-side `{0, 1}`, `{2, 3}`, are exchanged by rotation, and so are
  the two noncrossing pair partitions.

### 1.2 Through-strands and the refined count

Define the through-strand count of a diagram `D : TauCeti.TLDiagram n m`, the number of arcs of its
matching joining a bottom point (`< n`) to a top point (`≥ n`): a declaration we call through,
written `t(D)` here. Prove that `t(D) ≤ min n m` with `n − t(D)` and `m − t(D)` even, and that `t(D)`
is invariant under the two reflections. Prove the **product formula**: the number of diagrams
`n → m` without circles and with exactly `k` through-strands is
`TauCeti.halfCount n k * TauCeti.halfCount m k`, by cutting a diagram along its through-strands into
a half-diagram on the bottom and one on the top, each with `k` defects, the pair determining the
diagram because planarity joins the defects in order. Summing over `k`,
`∑_k TauCeti.halfCount n k * TauCeti.halfCount m k = catalan ((n + m) / 2)` for `n + m` even, and in
particular `∑_k (TauCeti.halfCount n k)² = catalan n`. The vanishing of
`TauCeti.halfCount n k` off the parity `n ≡ k (mod 2)` and for `k > n`
(`TauCeti.halfCount_eq_zero_iff`) is used by name here and in every count and Gram statement below
(Westbury; for half-diagrams, the link states of Ridout–Saint-Aubin, §2, counted in their
equation (2.9), p. 8). *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TLDiagram`,
`TauCeti.HalfDiagram`, `TauCeti.halfCount`, `TauCeti.halfCount_eq_zero_iff`,
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.card_planarMatching`; Mathlib `catalan`.

**Checks.**

- `TauCeti.halfCount 4 k` is `2, 0, 3, 0, 1` for `k = 0, …, 4`, and `4 + 9 + 1 = 14 = catalan 4`.
- The diagrams `3 → 1` without circles and with one through-strand number
  `TauCeti.halfCount 3 1 * TauCeti.halfCount 1 1 = 2 · 1 = 2`, which is all the planar matchings of
  four points, since by parity every diagram `3 → 1` has exactly one through-strand.
- At `n = 4`, `k = 1` the binomial formula `C(4, 1) − C(4, 0) = 3`, read without its parity
  condition, would count three half-diagrams; there are none, and the product formula would then
  give `9` diagrams `4 → 4` with one through-strand, which do not exist.

### Examples

- `Nat.card (TauCeti.PlanarMatching 6) = catalan 3 = 5`, and `TauCeti.PlanarMatching.rotate 6 ^ 6 = 1`
  (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.PlanarMatching.rotate_pow_self`).
- Rotating `e₁ ∈ TauCeti.TLDiagram 2 2`, with arcs `{0, 1}` and `{2, 3}`, one step gives the arcs
  `{1, 2}` and `{3, 0}`: the identity diagram `TauCeti.TLDiagram.id 2`, which joins `a` to `3 − a`.
- On three strands the horizontal reflection `TauCeti.TLDiagram.reflect 3 3` exchanges `e₁` (arcs
  `{0, 1}`, `{4, 5}`, through-strand `{2, 3}`) and `e₂` (arcs `{1, 2}`, `{3, 4}`, through-strand
  `{0, 5}`), while the vertical reflection `TauCeti.TLDiagram.verticalReflect 3 3` fixes both.

### Dependencies

Tau Ceti's perfect and planar matchings, noncrossing partitions, half-diagrams and Temperley–Lieb
diagrams; Mathlib's `catalan` and `DyckWord`. Layer 0 is not used.

## Layer 2: the diagram category and its presentation

### 2.1 The diagram category

Define a category we call TLDiagCat. Its objects are a one-field structure wrapping the number of
boundary points (constructor `of`), and the morphisms `of n ⟶ of m` are Tau Ceti's diagrams
`TauCeti.TLDiagram n m`. The composite `f ≫ g` of `f : n → m` and `g : m → p` glues the bottom
boundary of `g` to the top boundary of `f` (the top point of `f` in position `j` from the left to
the bottom point `j` of `g`), joins two outer boundary points when a path along arcs of `f` and `g`
connects them, and has as circles the circles of `f`, those of `g`, and the closed components
formed in the middle. The middle points have degree at most two in the union of the two matchings,
so its components are paths and cycles, and the new circles are the cycles. The identity is
`TauCeti.TLDiagram.id n`. Prove the category axioms. **Associativity is the hard theorem of this
layer**: the matchings of `(f ≫ g) ≫ h` and `f ≫ (g ≫ h)` agree, and the total circle counts agree,
`c(f, g) + c(f ≫ g, h) = c(g, h) + c(f, g ≫ h)` for the numbers `c` of circles closed at each
gluing. *Route.* A finite-graph argument, with Mathlib's `SimpleGraph` connectivity or a
direct path-following induction on the Dyck encoding; on endomorphisms the comparison of §2.2
supplies both halves from Tau Ceti. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TLDiagram`,
`TauCeti.TLDiagram.id`, `TauCeti.PlanarMatching`; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Category`, `SimpleGraph`.

**Checks.**

- `cup ≫ cap` (with `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cup : 0 → 2` and `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cap : 2 → 0`) is the
  empty diagram `0 → 0` with one circle, and `cap ≫ cup` is `e₁ : 2 → 2` with none.
- `e₁ ≫ e₁` is `e₁`'s matching with one circle. A composition that forgot the circles formed in
  the middle would make `e₁` idempotent, which is the diagram category at `δ = 1`.

### 2.2 The comparison with Brauer stacking

For diagrams `n → n`, relabel the cyclic boundary `Fin (n + n)` as `Fin n ⊕ Fin n` by sending the
bottom point `i < n` to `Sum.inl i` and the top point `n + j` to `Sum.inr (n − 1 − j)` (the top is
read right to left in the cyclic convention and left to right in `TauCeti.BrauerDiagram`, whose
`Sum.inl` points are its bottom), and transport the matching along this equivalence with
`TauCeti.PerfectMatching.congr`. Prove that the resulting map from `TauCeti.TLDiagram n n`
(forgetting the circles) to `TauCeti.BrauerDiagram n` is injective; that its image is exactly the
Brauer diagrams whose matching, carried back along the relabelling, satisfies
`Equiv.Perm.IsNoncrossingMatching`; and that it intertwines composition with stacking in the
reverse order: the matching of `f ≫ g` is sent to `TauCeti.composeDiagram` of the image of `g`
(above) and the image of `f` (below), and the number of circles closed in forming `f ≫ g` is
`TauCeti.middleLoopCount` of the same pair. Then associativity on endomorphisms follows: its
matching part from `TauCeti.composeDiagram_assoc`, its circle part from
`TauCeti.middleLoopCount_add_middleLoopCount_composeDiagram`. The Brauer stacking is for diagrams
of one fixed size, so gluing between different sizes `n → m → p` stays with §2.1, by reduction to
the fixed-size case through corner-dragging or directly. *Needs:* §2.1; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.BrauerDiagram`, `TauCeti.PerfectMatching.congr`, `TauCeti.composeDiagram`,
`TauCeti.composeDiagram_assoc`, `TauCeti.middleLoopCount`,
`TauCeti.middleLoopCount_add_middleLoopCount_composeDiagram`, `TauCeti.permToBrauer`.

**Checks.**

- `e₁ : 2 → 2` maps to the Brauer diagram with the cap `{inl 0, inl 1}` and the cup
  `{inr 0, inr 1}`; `e₁ ≫ e₁` maps to the stack of that diagram on itself, with middle-loop count
  `1`, matching the one closed circle.
- The identity `TauCeti.TLDiagram.id n` (joining `a` to `2n − 1 − a`) maps to
  `TauCeti.permToBrauer 1`: the bottom point `i` is joined to the top point `2n − 1 − i = n + (n − 1 − i)`,
  which is `inr i`. Reading the top boundary in the same direction on both sides would send it to
  the reversal permutation instead.
- The crossing Brauer diagram on two strands (`inl 0 ↔ inr 1`, `inl 1 ↔ inr 0`) is not in the
  image: carried back it is `{0, 2}`, `{1, 3}`, whose arcs interleave. A comparison that silently
  allowed crossings would be detected here.

### 2.3 Monoidal structure

Juxtaposition makes TLDiagCat monoidal: `of n ⊗ of m = of (n + m)`, and `f ⊗ g` places `g` to the
right of `f`, shifting the indices of the matchings and adding the circles. Strictness is handled
once: the tensor product adds the underlying numbers, and the associators and unitors are
equality-induced isomorphisms. *Needs:* §2.1; Mathlib `MonoidalCategory`, `eqToIso`.

**Checks.**

- `cap ⊗ 𝟙 (of 1)` and `𝟙 (of 1) ⊗ cap : 3 → 1` are distinct morphisms (the cap on the left two
  points, respectively the right two).
- `(cup ≫ cap) ⊗ 𝟙 (of 1) = 𝟙 (of 1) ⊗ (cup ≫ cap)`: both are the identity strand with one circle.
  This equality of the circle's left and right actions, forced by storing a count, is exactly the
  relation of §2.6.

### 2.4 Rigidity

Every object is self-dual via nested cups and caps: the coevaluation `of 0 → of n ⊗ of n` and the
evaluation `of n ⊗ of n → of 0` are the diagrams without circles whose matching is
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.nested n`, and the zigzag identities are diagram computations. This gives
`ExactPairing (of n) (of n)` and a `RigidCategory` instance on TLDiagCat. *Needs:* §2.3; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.nested`, `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cup`, `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cap`; Mathlib
`ExactPairing`, `RigidCategory`.

**Checks.**

- On one strand the coevaluation and evaluation are `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cup` and
  `TauCeti.TLDiagram.cap`, and `(cup ⊗ 𝟙) ≫ (𝟙 ⊗ cap)` is the identity strand with no circle.

### 2.5 Monoidal presentations (reusable infrastructure)

Build, in general, the monoidal category presented by generating objects (a type), generating
morphisms between tensor words, and relations, with its universal property: strong monoidal
functors out of it correspond to interpretations of the generators satisfying the relations.
Mathlib's free monoidal category is free on a type of objects only; this is the missing piece, and
Brauer, partition and symmetric-group diagram categories reuse it. Three choices are fixed here.
The presented category is **strict**, as TLDiagCat is: its objects are tensor words and the
tensor product of objects is concatenation. "Functors out" means **strong** monoidal functors into
an arbitrary, not necessarily strict, monoidal target; no strictification theorem is needed, and
Mathlib has the shape in `CategoryTheory.FreeMonoidalCategory.project`, a monoidal functor out of
the free monoidal category into any monoidal category. Relations are imposed as a **congruence** on
the hom-sets of the free monoidal category on the generating morphisms, closed under composition
and whiskering on both sides; these are the closure conditions of Tau Ceti's tensor ideals
`TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`, transposed from ideals of a preadditive category to congruences of an
arbitrary one. *Needs:* Mathlib `CategoryTheory.FreeMonoidalCategory`,
`CategoryTheory.FreeMonoidalCategory.project`, `CategoryTheory.Quotient`, `Functor.Monoidal`.

### 2.6 The presentation theorem for TLDiagCat (the universal property)

The monoidal category freely presented (§2.5) by one generating object `g` with `cup : 𝟙 → g ⊗ g`,
`cap : g ⊗ g → 𝟙` and exactly the two zigzag relations is a category we call
FreeSelfDualityCat; TLDiagCat is its **quotient** by one further relation, and the universal
property says so. Storing only a circle count makes a circle to the left of the identity strand
equal, as a morphism `1 → 1`, to a circle to its right. Writing `d = cup ≫ cap : 𝟙 → 𝟙`, this
forces the left and right actions of `d` on the generating object to agree, and the snake
equations do not imply this in an arbitrary monoidal target: `End (𝟙_ C)` is commutative in every
monoidal category, but it need not act centrally on the other hom-sets.

So: strong monoidal functors from TLDiagCat to `C` correspond to objects `V` with `η : 𝟙 → V ⊗ V` and
`ε : V ⊗ V → 𝟙` satisfying the snake equations **and**
`ρ_V⁻¹ ≫ (𝟙_V ⊗ d) ≫ ρ_V = λ_V⁻¹ ≫ (d ⊗ 𝟙_V) ≫ λ_V` with `d = η ≫ ε`, which is Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.CirclesCommuteAt d V`, while FreeSelfDualityCat is presented by the snakes alone. A self-duality satisfying the snakes is Mathlib's `ExactPairing V V`,
so both universal properties take one as input. They consist of existence of a strong monoidal
functor lift with an isomorphism from the image of the generator to `V` along which the images of
`cup` and `cap` are `η_ V V` and `ε_ V V`, and uniqueness of such a functor up to monoidal natural
isomorphism compatible with that identification. The intended signatures, for an arbitrary monoidal
category `C` (`[Category C] [MonoidalCategory C]`, binders of every declaration), are

```lean
-- `TauCeti.CirclesCommuteAt d X` is Tau Ceti's:
-- `(ρ_ X).inv ≫ (X ◁ d) ≫ (ρ_ X).hom = (λ_ X).inv ≫ (d ▷ X) ≫ (λ_ X).hom`
def IsCircleCentral {V : C} (η : 𝟙_ C ⟶ V ⊗ V) (ε : V ⊗ V ⟶ 𝟙_ C) : Prop :=
  TauCeti.CirclesCommuteAt (η ≫ ε) V
-- `F` sends the generating self-duality of `D` to the given one on `V`, along `e`
def SendsSelfDuality {D : Type*} [Category D] [MonoidalCategory D] {g : D} [ExactPairing g g]
    (F : D ⥤ C) [F.Monoidal] (V : C) [ExactPairing V V] (e : F.obj g ≅ V) : Prop :=
  ε F ≫ F.map (η_ g g) = η_ V V ≫ (e.inv ⊗ₘ e.inv) ≫ μ F g g ∧
    (e.inv ⊗ₘ e.inv) ≫ μ F g g ≫ F.map (ε_ g g) ≫ η F = ε_ V V
def TLDiagCat.lift (V : C) [ExactPairing V V] (hc : IsCircleCentral (η_ V V) (ε_ V V)) :
    TLDiagCat ⥤ C
instance : (TLDiagCat.lift V hc).Monoidal
def TLDiagCat.liftObjIso : (TLDiagCat.lift V hc).obj (TLDiagCat.of 1) ≅ V
theorem TLDiagCat.lift_sendsSelfDuality :
    SendsSelfDuality (TLDiagCat.lift V hc) V (TLDiagCat.liftObjIso V hc)
theorem TLDiagCat.lift_unique (G : TLDiagCat ⥤ C) [G.Monoidal]
    (e : G.obj (TLDiagCat.of 1) ≅ V) (he : SendsSelfDuality G V e) :
    ∃ i : G ≅ TLDiagCat.lift V hc, NatTrans.IsMonoidal i.hom ∧
      i.hom.app (TLDiagCat.of 1) = e.hom ≫ (TLDiagCat.liftObjIso V hc).inv
```

and the same four declarations for FreeSelfDualityCat, without `hc`. Prove that the circle is
central on the generator of TLDiagCat (the necessity half). Centrality at `V` propagates to the
tensor powers of `V` by Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.circlesCommuteAt_isMonoidal` (the objects at which a
fixed scalar is central contain `𝟙_ C` and are closed under `⊗`) and
`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.prop_projectObj` (such a property, holding on the generators, holds
on every interpreted tensor word).

The condition is at `V`, not at every object of `C`. The image of TLDiagCat consists of the tensor
powers of `V` and the unit, so centrality at `V`, propagated to tensor powers inside the proof, is
what the interpretation needs, and requiring the same equation at every `X : C` is strictly
stronger (second Check). The stronger condition may be kept under a separate name as a sufficient
hypothesis, but the characterization is the one at `V`. This also settles the two presentations:
the presented category has trivial composition and hard normal forms, the matching model the
reverse, and the equivalence, through the innermost-cup normal form (Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.exists_val_apply_eq_val_add_one`), lets each do what it is good at.
*Needs:* §§2.1–2.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CirclesCommuteAt`, `TauCeti.circlesCommuteAt_isMonoidal`,
`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.prop_projectObj`,
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.exists_val_apply_eq_val_add_one`; Mathlib `ExactPairing`,
`Functor.Monoidal`, `NatTrans.IsMonoidal`.

**Checks.**

- The negative test for TLDiagCat is the category of `A`-`A`-bimodules over `A = ℚ × ℚ × ℚ` with
  `⊗_A`. Let `M` have a one-dimensional summand `M_ij` for each directed edge `12`, `21`, `23`,
  `32` of the path on three vertices, let `ε` send a reverse-edge pair `m_ij ⊗ m_ji` to `e_i` and
  kill the paths that do not close, and let `η` send `e_i` to `∑ m_ij ⊗ m_ji` over the neighbours
  `j` of `i`. Both snakes hold, because in either composite only the matching reverse edge
  survives. But `d` is multiplication by `(1, 2, 1)`, so on `M_12` it acts from the left by `1` and
  from the right by `2`: no monoidal functor out of TLDiagCat sends the generator to this

### 2.7 Through-strand factorization

Every diagram `D : n → m` factors as a **surjection** `n → k` (all `k` top points on
through-strands) followed by an **injection** `k → m` (all `k` bottom points on through-strands),
with `k = t(D)`, uniquely once the circles of `D` are put on the surjective factor. Prove the
re-factorization lemma: if `f = f_s ≫ f_i` and `g = g_s ≫ g_i` are factored and the middle
composite factors as `f_i ≫ g_s = h_s ≫ h_i`, then `f ≫ g` factors as `(f_s ≫ h_s) ≫ (h_i ≫ g_i)`,
the first a surjection and the second an injection. *Needs:* §§1.2, 2.1.

**Checks.**

- `e₁ : 2 → 2` has no through-strand: it factors as `cap : 2 → 0` (vacuously surjective) followed
  by `cup : 0 → 2` (vacuously injective), which is the convention `cap ≫ cup = e₁`; the identity on
  `n` strands factors through `n` with both factors identities.

### Examples

- On three strands `e₁ ≫ e₂ ≫ e₁ = e₁` with no circle, while `e₁ ≫ e₁ ≫ e₁` is `e₁` with two
  circles.
- In finite-dimensional `ℚ`-vector spaces every self-duality is circle-central, since `End (𝟙)` is
  `ℚ` acting by scalars. `ℚ²` with the evaluation matrix `[[0, 1], [−1, 0]]` and the coevaluation
  matrix `[[0, −1], [1, 0]]` satisfies the snakes with circle value `−2`, and §2.6 gives a monoidal
  functor out of TLDiagCat sending a diagram with `c` circles to `(−2)^c` times a matrix.

## Layer 3: the linear category `TL(R, δ)`

### 3.1 The category

For a commutative ring `R` and `δ ∈ R`, define a category we call TLCat R δ, written `TL(R, δ)`:
its objects are a one-field structure (constructor `of`) wrapping a natural number, the hom-module
`of n ⟶ of m` is the free `R`-module `TauCeti.PlanarMatching (n + m) →₀ R` on matchings (no circle
data), and composition is the bilinear extension of the composition of §2.1 with each new circle
replaced by the factor `δ`. Define the identity-on-objects linearization functor from TLDiagCat,
sending a diagram with `c` circles to `δ^c` times its matching. Instances: `Preadditive`,
`Linear R`, `MonoidalCategory`, `MonoidalPreadditive`, `MonoidalLinear R`, `RigidCategory`. The
basis of each hom-module indexed by matchings is a declaration we call diagBasis (over `ℂ(q)`:
Chen, Remark 2.1.3, p. 8). *Needs:*
§§2.1–2.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlanarMatching`; Mathlib `Finsupp`, `Preadditive`, `Linear`,
`MonoidalPreadditive`, `MonoidalLinear`, `RigidCategory`, `Module.Basis`.

**Checks.**

- `cup ≫ cap = δ • 𝟙 (of 0)`, `cap ≫ cup = e₁` and `e₁ ≫ e₁ = δ • e₁`. A composition that dropped
  the factor `δ` would make `e₁` idempotent at every `δ`.

### 3.1a The `R`-linear universal property

For the linear category the useful form names the loop value: monoidally `R`-linear functors
`TL(R, δ) ⥤ C` into an `R`-linear monoidal target correspond to self-dualities with
`η ≫ ε = δ • 𝟙 (𝟙_ C)`. It is what the evaluation representation (§3.6) consumes. Its
declarations, which we call TLCat.lift and TLCat.lift_unique, are stated among `R`-linear strong
monoidal functors. The target binders are `[Category C] [Preadditive C] [Linear R C]
[MonoidalCategory C] [MonoidalPreadditive C] [MonoidalLinear R C]`, each carried by every
declaration that mentions `C`; an `R`-linear strong monoidal functor out of `TL(R, δ)` forces the
left action of `δ • 𝟙 (𝟙_ C)` on `V` to be `δ • 𝟙 V`, which needs the last two. With them:

```lean
def TLCat.lift (V : C) [ExactPairing V V] (hδ : η_ V V ≫ ε_ V V = δ • 𝟙 (𝟙_ C)) :
    TLCat R δ ⥤ C
instance : (TLCat.lift R δ V hδ).Monoidal
instance : (TLCat.lift R δ V hδ).Linear R
def TLCat.liftObjIso : (TLCat.lift R δ V hδ).obj (TLCat.of 1) ≅ V
theorem TLCat.lift_unique (G : TLCat R δ ⥤ C) [G.Monoidal] [G.Linear R]
    (e : G.obj (TLCat.of 1) ≅ V) (he : SendsSelfDuality G V e) :
    ∃ i : G ≅ TLCat.lift R δ V hδ, NatTrans.IsMonoidal i.hom ∧
      i.hom.app (TLCat.of 1) = e.hom ≫ (TLCat.liftObjIso R δ V hδ).inv
```

where SendsSelfDuality (§2.6) is taken for the generating self-duality of the object `of 1` (cup and
cap, with `η ≫ ε = δ • 𝟙`). No centrality hypothesis is needed, since in a monoidally `R`-linear
category `δ • 𝟙 (𝟙_ C)` acts as `δ • 𝟙 V` from both sides (Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.circlesCommuteAt_smul_id`). *Needs:* §§2.6, 3.1; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.circlesCommuteAt_smul_id`; Mathlib `MonoidalLinear`, `Functor.Linear`, `ExactPairing`,
`NatTrans.IsMonoidal`.

### 3.2 Pivotal and spherical instances

### 3.3 The reflection involutions

Vertical reflection is a contravariant monoidal involution of `TL(R, δ)`; on endomorphism algebras
it is the `StarRing` structure of §4.1, and it fixes every `e_i`. Horizontal (left–right)
reflection, which we call reflect, sends `e_i` to `e_(n−i)` on `n` strands; it is covariant on
composition but **reverses juxtaposition**, `reflect (f ⊗ g) = reflect g ⊗ reflect f`, so it is not
a monoidal involution of the category: state it as a monoidal functor into the monoidal opposite
(Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.MonoidalOpposite`), or give the anti-monoidal comparison explicitly.
Rotation, the two reflections and the rigidity data satisfy the dihedral compatibility laws, stated
once at category level from the diagram-level laws of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TLDiagram`
(`TauCeti.TLDiagram.reflect_rotate`, `TauCeti.TLDiagram.verticalReflect_rotate`,
`TauCeti.TLDiagram.reflect_verticalReflect`), so that Layer 4 does not restate them per algebra
(the two reflections and the half-turn on diagrams: Chen, Definitions 2.1.5–2.1.6, p. 8, and
Remark 2.3.5, pp. 15–16). *Needs:* §§3.1, 3.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TLDiagram.reflect`,
`TauCeti.TLDiagram.verticalReflect` and their laws; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.MonoidalOpposite`.

**Checks.**

- The distinct diagrams `cap ⊗ 𝟙` and `𝟙 ⊗ cap : 3 → 1` are exchanged by reflect, so a reflect
  that preserved the order of tensor factors would fix a diagram it moves.
- On three strands reflect exchanges the distinct basis diagrams `e₁` and `e₂`, while vertical

### 3.6 The evaluation representation

Two constructions, kept apart because only one of them needs a square root. For any unit `q ∈ R`
there is a rank-two self-duality with circle value `q + q⁻¹`: on `R²` take the cap matrix
`B = [[0, q], [1, 0]]` (the evaluation sends `e_i ⊗ e_j` to `B_ij`) and the cup coefficient matrix
`B⁻¹ = [[0, 1], [q⁻¹, 0]]`; the two snakes are `B B⁻¹ = B⁻¹ B = 1` and the circle is
`∑_ij (B⁻¹)_ij B_ij = q + q⁻¹`. Through §3.1a this gives a monoidally `R`-linear functor
`TL(R, q + q⁻¹) ⥤ ModuleCat R`, hence algebra maps `TL_n → End((R²)^⊗n)`. The square root is needed
only for compatibility with the Kauffman braiding (§3.5), a separate statement: for a unit `A ∈ R`
and `δ = −A² − A⁻²`, `V = R²` with the cap matrix `[[0, A], [−A⁻¹, 0]]` and the cup matrix
`[[0, −A], [A⁻¹, 0]]` (entries `0, ±A^(±1)`, circle value `δ`) gives through §3.1a a monoidal
functor `TL(R, δ) ⥤ ModuleCat R`, and hence algebra maps `TL_n → End((R²)^⊗n)`. This is quantum
`sl₂` Schur–Weyl duality with no quantum group in sight and the first real test of the universal
property; its faithfulness is §5.6 (over `ℂ(q^(1/2))`, with Tingley's `q^(1/2)` as `A`, the
`A`-form is his Theorem 4.2 with his equation (18), up to multiplying the cap by `A` and the cup
by `A⁻¹`). *Needs:* §3.1a; Mathlib `ModuleCat`, `Matrix`, `TensorProduct`.

**Checks.**

- `B B⁻¹ = B⁻¹ B = 1` and `∑_ij (B⁻¹)_ij B_ij = q + q⁻¹` for `B = [[0, q], [1, 0]]`: the self-duality
  exists without `A`, so a statement requiring a square root for the cup and cap would be too
  strong.
- For the cap matrix `[[0, A], [−A⁻¹, 0]]` and its inverse `[[0, −A], [A⁻¹, 0]]` the circle is
  `A · (−A) + (−A⁻¹) · A⁻¹ = −A² − A⁻²`.

### 3.7 Monos and epis

For a diagram `D : TauCeti.TLDiagram n m` let its image in `TL(R, δ)` be `δ^c` times its matching,
`c` its circles. Prove: if `R` is nontrivial and the image of `D` is a monomorphism, then all bottom
points of `D` lie on through-strands; conversely, if `δ` is a non-zero-divisor and all bottom points
of `D` lie on through-strands, the image of `D` is a monomorphism. Dually for epimorphisms and top
points. *Route.* Forward, by contraposition: an arc of `D` joining two bottom points gives by
planarity an innermost one, joining adjacent points `i`, `i + 1`, and `e_i ≫ D = δ D = (δ • 𝟙) ≫ D`
with `e_i ≠ δ • 𝟙`; dually `D ≫ e_i = δ D` with an arc among the top points. Converse:
`D ≫ D̄ = δ^c' • 𝟙`, where `D̄` is the vertical reflection and `c'` counts the arcs among `D`'s top
points and twice its circles. The hypothesis on `δ` is real, but not for the reason one first
guesses: `cup : 0 → 2` is mono for every `δ`, since precomposition adjoins a disjoint arc and closes
no circle; what `δ` a non-zero-divisor rules out is the stored circle count (Checks). *Needs:*
§§2.7, 3.1, 3.3.

**Checks.**

- The empty diagram `0 → 0` with one circle has all (zero) bottom points on through-strands, yet

## Layer 4: the algebras, the tower, and the Markov trace

### 4.1 The algebras

Define a type we call TLAlg R δ n, written `TL_n`, as Mathlib's `End` of the object `of n` of
`TL(R, δ)`, literally, so that its
`Ring` and `Algebra R` structures come from `CategoryTheory.Preadditive` and
`CategoryTheory.Linear` rather than being rebuilt (see the multiplication convention). Prove that it
is a free `R`-module whose `Module.finrank` over a nontrivial `R` is `catalan n`, give it
the `StarRing` structure of vertical reflection, and prove `star (r • x) = r • star x`, since
`StarRing` alone constrains only the ring structure and the reflection is `R`-linear (the algebra
and its diagram basis over `ℂ(q)`: Chen, Definition 2.2.1, p. 9, and Remark 2.1.3, p. 8).
*Needs:* §§3.1, 3.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlanarMatching.card_planarMatching`; Mathlib `End`,
`Module.finrank`, `StarRing`, `StarModule`.

**Checks.**

- Over the trivial ring Mathlib's `Module.finrank` is `1` (`Module.finrank_subsingleton`), not
  `catalan n`: the nontriviality hypothesis is needed.
- On three strands `star (e₁ * e₂) = e₂ * e₁`, a different basis diagram from `e₁ * e₂`, while
  `star e_i = e_i`: a `star` that fixed every diagram would not be anti-multiplicative.

### 4.2 Generators and the presentation theorem

Define the generators `e_i := cap_i ≫ cup_i` of TLAlg R δ (n + 1), for one-based `i ≤ n`, with
`cap_i : n + 1 → n − 1` joining the adjacent points `i`, `i + 1` and `cup_i : n − 1 → n + 1` its
reflection, indexed by `Fin n` so that the last generator, the one the Markov property and the
Wenzl recursion need, is `Fin.last`. In the other order, `cup_i ≫ cap_i` is the composite
`δ • 𝟙` on `n − 1` strands, not `e_i`. The relations are `e_i² = δ e_i`,
`e_i e_(i±1) e_i = e_i` and commutation at distance. The presented algebra is Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb R δ n`; no second one is built, and its generators on `n + 1` strands, indexed
by `Fin (n + 1 − 1)`, agree with these with no reindexing map. The target is the comparison, which
we call presentedTLEquiv and write `Φ`, an `R`-algebra isomorphism from `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb R δ n`
to `TL_n`, for `R : Type u` with both
sides in `Type u` (at `n = 0` both are `R`), built with `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.lift` from the
diagram relations, together with the generator comparison
(`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.e δ i ↦ e_i`) and the tower compatibility
(`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl ↦` the inclusion of §4.3). Bijectivity forces the **Jones
normal form** for reduced words in the `e_i` and the spanning argument (Tau Ceti's tower normal
form `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.span_strandIncl_union_strandIncl_mul_e_last_mul_eq_top` spans; the
add-a-cap and drag-a-corner generation of diagrams is the diagram side), with the Catalan count of
Layer 1 and diagBasis pinning the dimension. Its headline content, stated on the native algebra, is
`exists_basis_planarMatching`: for every commutative ring `R` and every `δ`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb R δ n` is a free `R`-module with a basis indexed by
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching (2 * n)` (the diagrams `n → n`), so of rank `catalan n`. Prove also that
under this comparison Tau Ceti's augmentation `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.aug` is the coefficient of the
identity diagram (both are the algebra map killing every generator, and a product of diagrams is
the identity diagram only if both factors are). This is the canonical comparison between the two
algebras: TemperleyLiebOperatorAlgebras (its Layer 1) and the Markov-trace comparison of §4.7 take
it rather than constructing their own (over `ℂ`: Ridout–Saint-Aubin, Proposition 2.3, p. 7, for
the Jones normal form and Theorem 2.4, p. 9, for the comparison; Chen, Remark 2.2.3, p. 10).
*Needs:* §§1.2, 3.1, 4.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.e`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.lift`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.hom_ext`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.aug`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.span_strandIncl_union_strandIncl_mul_e_last_mul_eq_top`,
`TauCeti.PlanarMatching.card_planarMatching`.

**Checks.**

- On three strands the five diagrams `1, e₁, e₂, e₁e₂, e₂e₁` are the basis (`catalan 3 = 5`), and
  the word `e₁ e₂ e₁` is the diagram `e₁`, not a sixth basis element.
- On two strands `aug 1 = 1` and `aug e₁ = 0`, and the identity diagram has coefficient `1` in `1`
  and `0` in `e₁`: the augmentation is the identity coefficient, not, for instance, the sum of all
  coefficients, which would send `e₁` to `1`.

### 4.3 The tower

Define the inclusions, which we call incl and write `ι : TL_n → TL_(n+1)`, as `R`-algebra maps
(add a through-strand on the right, `x ↦ x ⊗ 𝟙 (of 1)`), and prove them injective for every `R` and `δ`:
they send diagBasis injectively into diagBasis, with no invertibility needed. *Needs:* §§3.1, 4.1.

### 4.4 The diagrammatic Markov trace

Define the `R`-linear trace `tr̂ : TL_n → R` by closing every strand to the right and evaluating the
circles. Prove `tr̂(1) = δⁿ`, traciality `tr̂(x y) = tr̂(y x)`, the compatibility
`tr̂(ι x) = δ tr̂(x)`, the **Markov property** `tr̂(ι x · e_n) = tr̂(x)` with `e_n` the last
generator on `n + 1` strands, and `tr̂(star x) = tr̂(x)`, all division-free in the unnormalized
convention. Define the pairing `⟨x, y⟩ = tr̂(star x · y)` and prove it symmetric and invariant,
`⟨a x, y⟩ = ⟨x, star a · y⟩`; its relation to the cell forms is in §5.3 (the closure and its
traciality: Chen, Definition 2.1.7, p. 8, and Lemma 5.1.5, p. 39). *Needs:* §§4.1–4.3.

**Checks.**

- On two strands `tr̂(1) = δ²` and `tr̂(e₁) = δ`; on three, `tr̂(e₁ e₂) = δ`.
- The Markov property at `n = 1`: `tr̂(e₁) = δ = tr̂(1_1)`. At `n = 2` with `x = e₁`,
  `ι e₁ · e₂ = e₁ e₂` has trace `δ = tr̂(e₁)`, while `ι e₁ · e₁ = δ e₁` has trace `δ³`: the Markov
  property is for the last generator, on the side where `ι` adds the strand.

### 4.5 The conditional expectation

Define the conditional expectation, which we call condExp and write `𝔼 : TL_(n+1) → TL_n`, an
`R`-linear map closing the last strand only. Prove that it is a bimodule map over `TL_n`
(`𝔼(ι a · x · ι b) = a · 𝔼(x) · b`), that `𝔼(ι x) = δ x`, that `𝔼(e_n) = 1` for the last
generator, and that `tr̂ ∘ 𝔼 = tr̂`. *Needs:* §§4.3, 4.4.

**Checks.**

- On three strands `𝔼(e₂) = 1_2` and `𝔼(ι e₁) = δ e₁`; `𝔼(e₁ e₂)`, closing the last strand of the
  diagram `e₁ e₂`, is `e₁`.

### 4.6 Uniqueness and existence of the Markov trace

On the diagram algebra: any family of `R`-linear forms on TLAlg R δ n satisfying traciality, the
compatibility with incl, the Markov property, and `tr 1 = 1` in degree `0` equals `tr̂`. On the
native algebra the predicate is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace R δ tr`, for a family
`tr : ∀ n, TauCeti.TemperleyLieb R δ n →ₗ[R] R`: `tr n 1 = δ ^ n` for every `n` (needed in every
degree, because `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl` starts at one strand), traciality,
`tr (n + 2) (strandIncl x) = δ * tr (n + 1) x`, and the Markov property
`tr (n + 2) (strandIncl x * e δ (Fin.last n)) = tr (n + 1) x`. Uniqueness is Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.unique`, from the tower normal form. The headline
`existsUnique_isMarkovTrace` says that over every commutative ring and at every `δ` exactly one such
family exists: prove that `tr̂ ∘ Φ` satisfies `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace`. *Needs:*
§§4.2, 4.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.unique`.

### 4.7 Comparison with Tau Ceti's trace

Whenever `δ = −(q + q⁻¹)` for a unit `q`, `tr̂ ∘ Φ` is
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.markovTrace q hδ`: by §4.6 it is a Markov trace, and Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.eq_markovTrace` identifies every Markov trace at such `δ` with
the spin-model trace (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.isMarkovTrace_markovTrace` proves that it
is one). Mind the sign: in the convention `δ = q' + q'⁻¹` of this roadmap, Tau Ceti's unit is
`q = −q'`. *Needs:* §4.6; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.markovTrace`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.eq_markovTrace`.

### Examples

- On two strands TLAlg R δ 2 has basis `1, e₁` with `e₁² = δ e₁`, `tr̂(1) = δ²`, `tr̂(e₁) = δ`, and
  the pairing has Gram matrix `[[δ², δ], [δ, δ²]]` in this basis, of determinant `δ²(δ² − 1)`.

## Layer 5: cellular algebras, Gram determinants, semisimplicity

### 5.1 Cellular algebras (reusable infrastructure)

The definition is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.CellularBasis R A M` (Graham–Lehrer's cell datum over a
commutative ring, with the poset of cells, the basis `C μ s t`, the anti-involution, the span
`TauCeti.CellularBasis.lowerSpan` of the lower cells, the cell representations
`TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep` and the Gram matrices `TauCeti.CellularBasis.gramMatrix`, symmetric
and invariant). Build on it, over a commutative ring: the **cell module** `W(μ)`, the free
`R`-module on `M μ` as an `A`-module through `TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep`; the bilinear form
`φ_μ` on it (a `LinearMap.BilinForm` with matrix `TauCeti.CellularBasis.gramMatrix c μ`) with the
invariance `φ_μ(a* x, y) = φ_μ(x, a y)`; its radical, an `A`-submodule, and the quotient
`L(μ) = W(μ) / rad(μ)`; and **base change** along `R → S`, giving a cellular basis of `S ⊗_R A`
with the Gram matrices mapped entrywise. **Over a field** `K`, prove the two general theorems the
roadmap consumes (Graham–Lehrer, §3, where the base ring is a field): the classification of simple
modules, that the `L(μ)` with `φ_μ ≠ 0` are simple and pairwise non-isomorphic and that every simple
`A`-module is isomorphic to one of them; and the semisimplicity criterion
`isSemisimpleRing_iff_forall_det_gramMatrix_ne_zero`, that for a finite poset of cells `A` is
semisimple iff the Gram matrix of every cell has nonzero determinant. Neither is ring-general.
These are built in general, beside Tau Ceti's cellular bases: Hecke, Brauer and partition algebras
reuse them, and the general proofs are no harder than the Temperley–Lieb ones. *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.CellularBasis`, `TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep`, `TauCeti.CellularBasis.gramMatrix`,
`TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep_transpose_mul_gramMatrix`; Mathlib `LinearMap.BilinForm`,
`IsSemisimpleRing`, `IsSimpleModule`, `TensorProduct`.

**Checks.**

- `ℤ` is cellular with one cell, one basis vector and the perfect form `(u, v) ↦ uv`, yet `ℤ` is not
  a semisimple ring: the criterion is false over `ℤ`, and this is its negative test.
- `K[x]/(x²)` with cells `0 < 1`, `C_1 = 1` and `C_0 = x` has Gram matrices `1` and `0`
  (`x · x = 0`), and it is not semisimple; the matrix algebra `M_2(K)` with its matrix units has the
  identity as Gram matrix and is semisimple.

### 5.2 The Temperley–Lieb cell structure

Construct a cellular basis (`TauCeti.CellularBasis`) of `TL_n` over `R` whose cells are the through-strand counts
`k : Fin (n + 1)`, ordered as numbers, with `M k` the half-diagrams on `n` points with `k` defects
(empty unless `k ≡ n (mod 2)`). Fix the orientation once, consistently with
`TauCeti.halfCount`: a half-diagram is a **surjective** diagram `n → k` (§2.7: all `k` top points on
through-strands), and these correspond to Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.HalfDiagram n k`, the defects being
the bottom ends of the through-strands. For half-diagrams `s`, `t : n → k`, with `s̄ : k → n` the
vertical reflection of `s`, the basis vector is `C_k(s, t) = t ≫ s̄`, the diagram with bottom half
`t` and top half `s`, the anti-involution is `star`, and `star C_k(s, t) = C_k(t, s)`. Since
`a * C_k(s, t) = t ≫ (s̄ ≫ a)`, left multiplication acts on the first index modulo diagrams with fewer
through-strands, which is Tau Ceti's cellular axiom. The cell form, which we call cellForm, is
defined by `ū ≫ v = ⟨u, v⟩ · 1_k + (diagrams with fewer through-strands)` for half-diagrams `u`,
`v : n → k`, an endomorphism of `k` as the right-hand side requires, and it is the Gram matrix of
this cellular basis. With injective half-diagrams `k → n` the composable pairing would be `u ≫ v̄`
instead and the module action would have to be restated to match; with that orientation `ū ≫ v` is
an endomorphism of `n` and does not typecheck against `1_k`.

Define the cell module, which we call CellModule R δ n k, as the cell module of this basis, free
with the half-diagram basis (`Module.finrank = TauCeti.halfCount n k` over a nontrivial `R`), and
pin the action formula as well as its orientation. With half-diagrams `n → k` and Mathlib's
reversed multiplication on `End`, plain precomposition `u ↦ a ≫ u` is a right action; the left
`Module` instance of `TL_n` on CellModule R δ n k is the star-twisted precomposition
`a • u = (star a) ≫ u`, with the terms of fewer through-strands discarded, which is what the cell
representation `TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep` computes. Prove the `IsScalarTower` instance for
`R`, `TL_n` and CellModule R δ n k: without it the two actions are formally
unrelated and the `R`-bilinearity of the cell form says less than intended (Westbury;
Graham–Lehrer; over `ℂ`, the cell modules are the standard modules of Ridout–Saint-Aubin, §3,
pp. 11–13, with the invariance of their form in Lemma 3.1). *Needs:* §§1.2, 2.7, 4.1, 4.2, 5.1;
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CellularBasis`, `TauCeti.HalfDiagram`, `TauCeti.halfCount`.

**Checks.**

- `n = 3`, `k = 1`: the half-diagrams are the cap on the left pair and the cap on the right pair,
  the Gram matrix is `[[δ, 1], [1, δ]]`, of determinant `δ² − 1 = [3]`.
- With the unpinned action `a • u = a ≫ u` read as a left action, `(e₁ * e₂) • u` and
  `e₁ • (e₂ • u)` differ on the half-diagram `u` of the right cap on three points, so the module
  axiom fails (the first is `u` itself, the second `δ` times the half-diagram of the left cap): the
  twist by `star` is needed.

### 5.3 The Gram determinant formula, universally

With `G_(n,k)` the Gram matrix of cellForm in the half-diagram basis, `det G_(n,k)` is the evaluation
at `δ` of an explicit polynomial `P_(n,k) ∈ ℤ[X]`, which we call gramPoly n k, characterized in
`ℤ[X]` (a domain, where every quantum integer `S_(j−1)(X)` with `j ≥ 1` is nonzero) by
`P_(n,k) · ∏_(j=1)^((n−k)/2) [j]^(m_j) = ∏_(j=1)^((n−k)/2) [k+j+1]^(m_j)` with
`m_j = TauCeti.halfCount n (k + 2j)`, and transported to every `R` by base change (Westbury;
Ridout–Saint-Aubin, Theorem 4.7). **The universal form is the target, not the specialized one.**
Cross-multiplied and then evaluated at a particular `δ`, the identity says nothing when some `[j]`
with `j ≤ (n − k)/2` vanishes, since then both sides are `0` and every `m_j` in range is positive;
that is not a corner case, it is where the classification below lives. It already bites at
`δ = 0`, `n = 4`, `k = 0`, whose denominator is `[1]³[2]`. Prove also the relation of the cell forms
to the trace pairing of §4.4: under the bijection sending a diagram `n → n` to the cap diagram
`2n → 0` with the same planar matching (Tau Ceti's cyclic numbering), `tr̂(star x · y) = ⟨x, y⟩` for
diagrams `x`, `y`, with the cell form of `TL_(2n)` at `k = 0` on the right; so the pairing of §4.4
has Gram matrix `G_(2n,0)` and determinant `P_(2n,0)(δ)`. *Needs:* §§4.4, 5.2; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.halfCount`, `TauCeti.qInt`; Mathlib `Polynomial`, `Matrix.det`, `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S`.

**Checks.**

- Anchors: `det G_(2,0) = [2]`, `det G_(3,1) = [3]`, `det G_(4,2) = [4]`,
  `det G_(4,0) = [2]²[3] = δ²(δ² − 1)`, `det G_(5,1) = [3]⁴ (δ² − 2)`.
- `det G_(4,0) = [2]²[3]` is not recoverable from the cross-multiplied identity at `δ = 0`, where it
  reads `0 = 0`; and `det G_(5,1)` at `δ = 0` is `−2`, which the universal form sees and the
  cross-multiplied identity, with denominator `[1]⁴[2]`, does not.
- The trace pairing needs the `star`: on three strands `tr̂(x · y)` differs from the cap form for some
  pair of diagrams, while `tr̂(star x · y)` agrees with it for all of them; on two strands both agree,
  so a test there would not see the difference.

### 5.4 Semisimplicity

Over a field `K`, `TL_n(K, δ)` is a semisimple ring (`IsSemisimpleRing`) iff `det G_(n,k) ≠ 0` for every `k` with
`k ≤ n` and `k ≡ n (mod 2)` (§§5.1–5.3); in particular it is semisimple whenever `[k] ≠ 0` for
`1 ≤ k ≤ n`. Over a field of **characteristic zero** this becomes the closed classification:
`TL_n(K, δ)` is semisimple iff `TauCeti.quantumOrder δ > n`, or `δ = 0` and `n` is odd. Over `ℂ`
these are Ridout–Saint-Aubin's Gram determinant (Theorem 4.7) and the semisimplicity remarks after
their Corollary 4.8, with the Graham–Lehrer criterion; the extension to every field of
characteristic zero is part of the target. On the native algebra the two headlines are
`isSemisimpleRing_of_qInt_ne_zero` (any field `K`, `TauCeti.qInt K δ k ≠ 0` for
`k ∈ Finset.Icc 1 n` implies `IsSemisimpleRing (TauCeti.TemperleyLieb K δ n)`) and
`isSemisimpleRing_iff_of_charZero` (the closed classification under `[CharZero K]`). **The `δ = 0`
trap:** `[2] = 0` at `δ = 0`, yet `TL_3(0)` is semisimple (its Gram determinants are
`det G_(3,1) = [3] = −1` and `det G_(3,3) = 1`), while `TL_2(0)` is not (`det G_(2,0) = 0`); so
"semisimple iff `[k] ≠ 0` for `k ≤ n`" is false as an iff, which the clause on `δ = 0` and odd `n`
repairs. **That clause is characteristic zero:** `det G_(5,1) = [3]⁴(δ² − 2)` is `−2` at `δ = 0`,
nonzero in characteristic zero and zero in characteristic `2`, so `TL_5(𝔽̄₂, 0)` is not semisimple
and the parity rule fails there. The modular classification in general is out of scope (Scope and
ownership). *Needs:* §§5.1–5.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.quantumOrder`; the comparison of §4.2.

**Checks.**

- `TL_2(0)` is not semisimple, `TL_3(0)` is, and `TL_5(0)` is over a field of characteristic zero but
  not over `𝔽̄₂`.
- At `δ = 0` on seven strands the Gram determinants are `−64`, `3`, `−1`, `1` for `k = 1, 3, 5, 7`:
  `TL_7(0)` is semisimple in characteristic zero and fails in characteristics `2` and `3`, so no
  single small characteristic is the only exception.

### 5.5 Nondegeneracy of the category

The closure pairings `Hom(n, m) × Hom(m, n) → R`, `(f, g) ↦ tr̂(f ≫ g)`, are nondegenerate for all
`n`, `m` iff `[k] ≠ 0` for all `k ≥ 1` (over `ℂ`, in terms of negligible morphisms: Goodman–Wenzl,
Corollary 3.2 and Theorem 3.3, p. 10). This `δ`-side statement holds over any field; it is the clean
category-level one, with no parity exception, and it is the form to state and prove. Over an
algebraically closed field of **characteristic zero** it translates to "`δ ≠ ζ + ζ⁻¹` for every root
of unity `ζ ≠ ±1`" (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.exists_qInt_eq_zero_iff`), which already excludes `δ = 0`
(the case `ζ = ±i`). The characteristic hypothesis is not decoration: in characteristic `p`,
`δ = 2 = 1 + 1⁻¹` has `[p] = 0` while `ζ = 1` is excluded, so the translated form is false there. At
`m = 0` the pairing of `Hom(n, 0)` with `Hom(0, n)` is, in the diagram bases, the cell form of §5.2
at `k = 0`, pairing `v` with `ū`. *Route.* For "only if", let `ℓ` be least with `[ℓ] = 0`: Tau
Ceti's projection `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl δ (ℓ − 1)`, transported by §4.2, is nonzero and
pairs with every `g` to `aug(g) · [ℓ] = 0` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_mul` and
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.map_jonesWenzl` through §4.6); over `ℂ` at `q = e^(πi/ℓ)` this
is the argument of Chen, Lemma 5.3.2, p. 41. *Needs:* §§3.2, 4.2, 4.4, 4.6, 5.2, 5.3; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.exists_qInt_eq_zero_iff`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_mul`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.map_jonesWenzl`.

**Checks.**

- At `δ = 0` the pairing on `Hom(1, 1) = R` is `(a, b) ↦ a b δ = 0`, degenerate although `TL_1` is
  semisimple: nondegeneracy and semisimplicity differ already on one strand.

### 5.6 Faithfulness of the evaluation representation

Over a field `K`, with `A ∈ Kˣ`, `δ = −A² − A⁻²` and `[k] ≠ 0` for `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, the algebra map
`TL_n(K, δ) → End((K²)^⊗n)` of §3.6 is injective, by the Gram machinery: the tensor space contains
every cell module of §5.2 and the algebra is semisimple (§5.4). *Needs:* §§3.6, 5.2, 5.4.

**Checks.**

- On two strands `e₁` acts by a rank-one map of trace `δ`, linearly independent from the identity,
  so `TL_2 → End(K² ⊗ K²)` is injective for every `δ`, including `δ = 0` where `[2] = 0`: the
  hypothesis is sufficient, not necessary.

### Examples

- `G_(3,1) = [[δ, 1], [1, δ]]` with determinant `[3]`; `det G_(4,2) = [4]`; `det G_(4,0) = δ²(δ² − 1)`;

## Layer 6: Jones–Wenzl projections

The projections themselves are Tau Ceti's: `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl δ n`, defined by
Wenzl's quadratic recursion, with `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.existsUnique_jonesWenzl` (when
`[1], …, [n]` are units, exactly one element of the algebra on `n` strands is idempotent, has
augmentation `1` and is killed on both sides by every generator), the absorption identities, the
capping identity and the quantum dimension `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.map_jonesWenzl`.
This layer adds the remaining characterizations, the single-clasp recursion, the coefficient
formula, the partial trace, and the tensor-ideal theory. Write `f_n` for the projection.

### 6.1 Characterization of the Jones–Wenzl projection

Prove: `f_n` is fixed by the anti-involution, both on the native algebra (the `R`-algebra
anti-automorphism of `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb R δ n` fixing every generator, built with
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.lift` into the opposite algebra) and, through presentedTLEquiv, by `star`;
over a field with `[k] ≠ 0` for `1 ≤ k ≤ n`, `f_n` is the unique **nonzero** idempotent killed on
both sides by every `e_i`; and its coefficient on the identity diagram is `1` (Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.aug_jonesWenzl` with §4.2). Over any commutative ring the characterization
uses the coefficient instead of nonvanishing, which is Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.existsUnique_jonesWenzl` (Wenzl; over `ℂ(q)`, Chen, Theorem 2.3.2, p. 12,
and Remarks 2.3.4–2.3.5, p. 15). *Needs:* §4.2; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.existsUnique_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_mul`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.mul_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.lift`.

**Checks.**

- `f_2 = 1 − [2]⁻¹ e₁` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_two`) is fixed by the
  anti-involution, and so is `f_3 = 1 − ([2]/[3])(e₁ + e₂) + [3]⁻¹(e₁e₂ + e₂e₁)`, whose two words of
  length two are exchanged by it with equal coefficients.
- Over a field the idempotent `0` is killed by every `e_i`: the nonvanishing hypothesis is what
  excludes it.

### 6.2 The single-clasp recursion

Wenzl's quadratic recursion `f_(n+1) = f_n − ([n]/[n+1]) f_n e_n f_n` (with `f_n` included on the
first `n` strands) is Tau Ceti's definition, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_add_two`. Prove the
**single-clasp (linear) recursion**: when `[1], …, [n + 1]` are units,
`f_(n+1) = f_n + ∑_(i=1)^n (−1)^(n−i+1) ([i]/[n+1]) · f_n · e_n e_(n−1) ⋯ e_i`, with `f_n` included
on the first `n` strands by `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl`, the product that of the algebra,
and division by `Ring.inverse`; and the mirror form with `e_i ⋯ e_(n−1) e_n · f_n`. It expands
`f_(n+1)` as `f_n` plus a weighted sum of once-capped terms, uses only one copy of `f_n`, and is the
route to the coefficients (Morrison, Proposition 3.3, p. 6, in his loop convention `−[2]`).
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_add_two`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.isIdempotentElem_jonesWenzl`.

**Checks.**

- At `n = 1` it reads `f_2 = 1 − ([1]/[2]) e₁`. At `n = 2` it gives
  `f_2 − ([2]/[3]) f_2 e₂ + ([1]/[3]) f_2 e₂ e₁`, which expands to the `f_3` of §6.1; with all signs
  negative it would give the coefficient `−[2]⁻¹ ([3] − 1)/[3] ≠ −[2]/[3]` on `e₁`.

### 6.3 The coefficient formula

Prove the closed formula for the coefficient of any diagram in `f_n`, in the basis of §4.2,
following S. Morrison, *A formula for the Jones–Wenzl projections*, Proposition 5.1, p. 9. That
formula is a **sum, over the admissible removal sequences** of the diagram, of products of quantum
integers, divided by the quantum factorial `[n]!` (its precursor is the recursion of
Proposition 4.1, which writes a coefficient in `f_(n+1)` as a sum of ratios `[i]/[n + 1]` times
coefficients in `f_n`); it is not a single product read off the diagram. The statement therefore
needs the finite set of admissible sequences of a diagram, their reindexing, and the sum; a
different product formula would have to be identified and justified separately. The paper takes
the loop value to be `−[2]` where this roadmap takes `+[2]`, so its Wenzl recursion carries the
opposite displayed sign; the translation
of diagram generators and coefficient signs is recorded with the statement. The formula is a
rational expression in the quantum integers, so each coefficient can be evaluated with Tau Ceti's
computable `TauCeti.qIntAux`; its acceptance tests are `f_2`, `f_3` and a diagram admitting two
different admissible removal sequences, which detects a missing summation rather than only a wrong
constant. *Needs:* §§4.2, 6.2; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.qInt`, `TauCeti.qIntAux`.

**Checks.**

- The formula reproduces `f_2 = 1 − [2]⁻¹ e₁` and
  `f_3 = 1 − ([2]/[3])(e₁ + e₂) + [3]⁻¹(e₁e₂ + e₂e₁)`.
- The coefficient of `e_i` in `f_n` is `−[i][n − i]/[n]`; on four strands that of `e₂` is
  `−[2]²/[4]`, which differs from that of `e₁`, `−[3]/[4]`, so a formula depending only on the number
  of caps fails here.

### 6.4 Traces, partial traces and absorption

On the diagram algebra `tr̂(f_n) = [n + 1]` when `[1], …, [n]` are units, from §4.6 and Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.map_jonesWenzl`, even when `[n + 1]` vanishes. Prove the
**partial trace** `𝔼(f_(n+1)) = ([n+2]/[n+1]) f_n` when `[1], …, [n + 1]` are units. Tau Ceti has
the absorption `f_(n+1) · ι(f_n) = f_(n+1) = ι(f_n) · f_(n+1)`
(`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_mul_strandIncl_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl_jonesWenzl_mul_jonesWenzl`); prove its tensor variants
`f_n · (𝟙_a ⊗ f_k ⊗ 𝟙_b) = f_n = (𝟙_a ⊗ f_k ⊗ 𝟙_b) · f_n` for `a + k + b = n`, in TLAlg through
presentedTLEquiv and the monoidal structure of §3.1 (Wenzl; over `ℂ(q)`, Chen, Remark 2.3.3,
p. 15, for the absorption, and the relation before Proposition 2.3.6, p. 16, for the partial
trace). *Needs:* §§3.1, 4.2,
4.5, 4.6; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.IsMarkovTrace.map_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl_mul_strandIncl_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.strandIncl_jonesWenzl_mul_jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.e_last_mul_strandIncl_jonesWenzl_mul_e_last`.

**Checks.**

- `tr̂ f_2 = δ² − 1 = [3]` and `tr̂ f_3 = [4]`.
- `𝔼(f_2) = δ − δ⁻¹ = [3]/[2]`, and `𝔼(f_1) = δ = [2]/[1]`: the ratio is `[n+2]/[n+1]`, not
  `[n+1]/[n]`.

### 6.5 Tensor ideals and the negligible ideal

A tensor ideal of a monoidal preadditive category is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`: a
two-sided `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal` closed under whiskering on both sides. Over an `R`-linear category
its hom-groups are automatically `R`-submodules (`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.smul_mem`), so no separate
linear notion is introduced; the same closure conditions impose the relations of §2.5. Define the
negligible ideal of `TL(R, δ)`, which we call negligibleIdeal: the morphisms `f : X → Y` with
`tr̂(f ≫ g) = 0` for every `g : Y → X`, the trace being Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.trace` of the spherical
structure of §3.2. Prove that it is a `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal` (closure under composition by
traciality, under whiskering by sphericality) (Chen, Lemma 5.2.2, p. 40; Goodman–Wenzl, §1.3,
p. 5). *Needs:*
§§3.2, 4.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`, `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal`, `TauCeti.trace`,
`TauCeti.SphericalCategory`.

**Checks.**

- At `δ = 0` every `g : of 1 → of 1` is a scalar `r • 𝟙`, with trace `r δ = 0`, so the identity of
  one strand is negligible: this is the quantum order `ℓ = 2`, with `f_(ℓ−1) = f_1 = 1`.

### 6.6 Constancy of dimension under specialization

Let `𝒪` be the local ring of rational functions in the generic parameter that are regular at the
specialization point, with fraction field the generic coefficients and residue field the specialized
ones. Prove: for an `𝒪`-linear idempotent endomorphism `E` of a finite free `𝒪`-module `L`, the
image of `E` is a direct summand of `L`, hence free, so the rank of `E` after extending to the
fraction field equals the rank of `E` after reducing to the residue field. Applied to `L` the
`𝒪`-span of the diagrams `m → n` and `E(x) = e x f` for evaluable idempotents `e`, `f`, it gives
`dim e·Hom(m,n)·f = dim e(τ)·Hom(m,n)(τ)·f(τ)`, and likewise for any finite free `𝒪`-lattice that `e`
and `f` preserve on the left and on the right. Goodman–Wenzl's Proposition 1.1 (p. 4) asserts this
for the span `A` of an **arbitrary** set of diagrams, and that is false: its proof sums the four
corners `eAf`, `eA(1−f)`, `(1−e)Af`, `(1−e)A(1−f)` to `A`, which needs `eA ⊆ A` and `Af ⊆ A`.
*Needs:* §3.1; Mathlib `IsLocalRing`, `Module.Free`, `Module.finrank`.

**Checks.**

- In `TL₃` at generic `δ = t + t⁻¹`, specialized at `τ = exp(πi/3)` so that `δ(τ) = 1`, put
  `p = e₁/δ`, `r = 1 − p` and `x = p e₂ r = e₁e₂/δ − e₁/δ²`. The relations give `px = x = xr` and
  `xp = x² = 0`, so `E = p + (t − τ)x` and `F = r` are evaluable idempotents. With `A` the span of the
  identity diagram, `EAF` is spanned by `(t − τ)x ≠ 0` (dimension `1`), while
  `E(τ)A(τ)F(τ) = p(τ)r(τ)·ℂ = 0` (dimension `0`). This is the acceptance test of the lemma's
  hypotheses.

### 6.7 Specialization-injectivity of the critical-block tower map

In the proof of Goodman–Wenzl's Theorem 3.3 the element `b ≠ 0` lies in `e(τ)T_n(τ)f(τ)` for
evaluable idempotents `e`, `f` with `e T_n f` inside the block of a diagram `μ`, and for a diagram
`α` on the first critical line, of size `n + m` and connected to `μ` in the generic Bratteli
diagram, one needs `x ↦ z_α(τ)·(x ⊗ 1_m)` to be injective on `e(τ)T_n(τ)f(τ)`. Generically
`x ↦ z_α·(x ⊗ 1_m)` is injective on `e T_n f`, and §6.6 gives `dim e T_n f = dim e(τ)T_n(τ)f(τ)`.
The first equality of the source's chain is not an instance of §6.6: it is about the image of the
`𝒪`-lattice `e T_n^𝒪 f` under `x ↦ z_α (x ⊗ 1_m)` inside `T_(n+m)^𝒪`, and multiplication by `z_α`
need not preserve `T_n ⊗ 1_m`. Reduction only bounds the specialized rank above by the generic one;
equality needs the image to be saturated (a direct summand of `T_(n+m)^𝒪`), for example from an
`𝒪`-linear left inverse built from an evaluable adapted basis of the block. State the injectivity
at `τ` as its own lemma, with that saturation input as a named step, or replace it by another proof
of the ideal theorem; either way the Goodman–Wenzl classification does not rest on the unrestricted
Proposition 1.1 (Goodman–Wenzl, §§1–3). *Needs:* §§3.1, 6.6.

### 6.8 The Goodman–Wenzl theorem

Over a field of **characteristic zero** at quantum order `ℓ` (`TauCeti.quantumOrder δ = ℓ`),
`f_(ℓ−1)` exists, is negligible (`tr̂ f_(ℓ−1) = [ℓ] = 0`), and **generates the negligible ideal,
which is the unique proper nonzero tensor ideal**. Generic complement, over the same fields: when
`[k] ≠ 0` for all `k ≥ 1`, the only tensor ideals are `0` and everything. Over `ℂ` these are
Goodman–Wenzl's Proposition 2.1 (p. 8) for the generation, Theorem 3.3 (pp. 10–11) for the
uniqueness and Corollary 3.2 (p. 10) for the generic complement; the extension to every field of
characteristic zero is part of the target. Follow Goodman–Wenzl's §§1–3 and expose its inputs as
named lemmas rather than inside one proof: the path and block idempotents it evaluates (§2.4
there), and the two specialization
statements §§6.6–6.7. Its Hom-to-End retractions divide by `δ`, so the included case `δ = 0`
(quantum order `ℓ = 2`) is a separate target with its own argument, not a verbatim specialization.
The characteristic hypothesis is carried deliberately: in characteristic `p` both the Jones–Wenzl
projections and the lattice of ideals genuinely differ (Scope and ownership). *Needs:* §§4.6, 5.4,
6.4, 6.5, 6.6, 6.7; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.quantumOrder`, `TauCeti.TemperleyLieb.jonesWenzl`,
`TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`.

**Checks.**

- At `δ = 1` (quantum order `3`), `f_2 = 1 − e₁` has `tr̂ f_2 = δ² − 1 = 0`, and it is nonzero; at
  generic `δ` no `f_n` is negligible since `tr̂ f_n = [n + 1] ≠ 0`.

### Examples

- `f_2 = 1 − [2]⁻¹ e₁`, `f_3 = 1 − ([2]/[3])(e₁ + e₂) + [3]⁻¹(e₁e₂ + e₂e₁)`, `tr̂ f_2 = [3]`,
  `tr̂ f_3 = [4]`; the single-clasp recursion and the coefficient formula reproduce both.

## Layer 7: the Karoubi envelope and the root-of-unity quotient

### 7.1 The envelope (reusable infrastructure)

For a monoidal preadditive (respectively `S`-linear) category `C`, Tau Ceti provides the monoidal
structure of `CategoryTheory.Mat_ C` (`CategoryTheory.Mat_.instMonoidalCategory`, with
`[MonoidalPreadditive C]`, and the strong monoidal embedding
`CategoryTheory.Mat_.embeddingMonoidal`) and of `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi C`
(`CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi.instMonoidalCategory`, `(X, p) ⊗ (Y, q) = (X ⊗ Y, p ⊗ q)`, with
`CategoryTheory.Idempotents.toKaroubiMonoidal`), their `MonoidalPreadditive`, `Linear S` and
`RigidCategory` instances, so that `Karoubi (Mat_ C)` is rigid monoidal for rigid monoidal
preadditive `C`. Prove the compatibility of the two lifts: `MonoidalLinear S (Mat_ C)` and
`MonoidalLinear S (Karoubi C)` for `[Linear S C] [MonoidalLinear S C]`, with the source monoidal and
linear structures binders of each instance. Define the envelope, which we call TLKar K δ, as
`Karoubi (Mat_ TL(K, δ))`, rigid, monoidal and monoidally `K`-linear. Only the finite biproducts
come free, from `Mat_` and Mathlib's `Idempotents/Biproducts.lean`; everything this layer says
about tensor products, duals or quantum dimensions in the envelope rests on this, and every diagram
category that wants a semisimplified quotient needs exactly it. *Needs:* §3.1 for TLKar; Tau Ceti
`CategoryTheory.Mat_.instMonoidalCategory`, `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi.instMonoidalCategory`
and their linear and rigid instances; Mathlib `MonoidalLinear`, `CategoryTheory.Mat_`,
`CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi`.

### 7.2 Quotients by tensor ideals (reusable infrastructure)

Tau Ceti provides the quotient of a monoidal preadditive category by a tensor ideal:
`TauCeti.MonoidalQuotient I` (an abbreviation of `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.Quotient`) and
`TauCeti.toMonoidalQuotient I`, with the descended `MonoidalCategory`, the strong monoidal quotient
functor, `MonoidalPreadditive` and `MonoidalLinear S` instances, zero detection
(`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.quotientFunctor_map_eq_zero_iff`), the factorization
`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.lift` with its monoidal structure, zero objects and finite biproducts. Layer
6's negligible ideal is a tensor ideal of `TL(K, δ)`, but the quotient below is taken in TLKar K δ,
and the passage is not automatic. Prove: the extension of a tensor ideal of `C` along `Mat_` (the
matrices with entries in the ideal) and along `Karoubi` (the morphisms whose underlying morphism lies
in the ideal), each again a `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`; the descent of the rigid, pivotal, spherical,
braided and ribbon structures to `TauCeti.MonoidalQuotient I`; and that the layer uses
`Karoubi (TauCeti.MonoidalQuotient I)`, since a quotient need not be idempotent complete (Checks).
*Needs:* §7.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MonoidalIdeal`, `TauCeti.MonoidalQuotient`,
`TauCeti.toMonoidalQuotient`, `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.quotientFunctor_map_eq_zero_iff`,
`TauCeti.PivotalCategory`, `TauCeti.SphericalCategory`, `TauCeti.RibbonCategory`; Mathlib
`RigidCategory`, `BraidedCategory`, `CategoryTheory.IsIdempotentComplete`.

**Checks.**

- The additivity of whiskering is used, which is why Tau Ceti's quotient assumes
  `[MonoidalPreadditive C]`: descent asks for `W ◁ f − W ◁ g ∈ I` when `f − g ∈ I`, and ideal
  closure only gives `W ◁ (f − g) ∈ I`. In the `ℤ`-linear category with objects `ℕ`, `End n = ℤ` and
  no other nonzero morphisms, tensor `n + m` on objects and `f ⊗ g = f^(2^m) g^(2^n)` on
  endomorphisms (monoidal, not monoidal preadditive), the ideal `I(n, n) = 3^(2^n) ℤ` is closed under
  composition and both whiskerings; `1 = 4` in `End 0 / I = ℤ/3`, while whiskering by `1` gives
  `1 ≠ 16` in `End 1 / I = ℤ/9`, so the tensor product does not descend.
- Finite-rank free abelian groups modulo the morphisms divisible by `6` have the matrices over `ℤ/6`
  as morphisms, and the idempotent `3` of the rank-one object does not split: its image would have
  ranks `(1, 0)` under `ℤ/6 ≅ 𝔽₂ × 𝔽₃`, while every object has ranks `(n, n)`. So there is no
  instance `CategoryTheory.IsIdempotentComplete (TauCeti.MonoidalQuotient I)` in general.

### 7.3 From Hom computations to an abelian, Hom-finite category

Tau Ceti's finite semisimple categories `TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory` are defined on an
abelian category, and PivotalSpherical's fusion results carry `[Abelian C]` and finite-dimensional
Hom spaces. The envelope and the quotient above are additive and, after `Karoubi`, idempotent
complete; that does not make them abelian. Prove, in general: a `K`-linear additive
idempotent-complete category with a family of objects `X_i` such that `End(X_i) ≃ₐ[K] K`,
`Hom(X_i, X_j) = 0` for `i ≠ j`, and every object a finite biproduct of the `X_i`, is abelian and
has finite-dimensional Hom spaces: morphisms between two decompositions are block matrices of
scalars, so kernels and cokernels are computed by finite-dimensional linear algebra in each block,
and each `X_i` is simple. For the Temperley–Lieb envelope the inputs are the Jones–Wenzl Hom
computations of §§7.4–7.5: `Hom(X_a, X_b) = 0` for `a ≠ b`, `End(X_a) ≃ₐ[K] K`, and the finite
Jones–Wenzl decomposition of every object (for every `a` generically, and for `0 ≤ a ≤ ℓ − 2` in the
root-of-unity quotient). *Needs:* Mathlib `Abelian`, `Linear`, `HasFiniteBiproducts`,
`CategoryTheory.IsIdempotentComplete`, `CategoryTheory.Simple`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory`.

**Checks.**

- `Mat_` of the one-object category `K` (finite-dimensional vector spaces with chosen bases)
  satisfies the hypotheses with one `X_i` and is abelian.
- Dropping `Hom(X_i, X_j) = 0` fails on the finitely generated projective modules over the path
  algebra of the quiver `• → •`: both indecomposable projectives have endomorphism ring `K` and every
  object is a finite biproduct of them, but the nonzero map between them is mono and epi in that
  category without being an isomorphism, so the category is not abelian.
- Dropping `End(X_i) ≃ₐ[K] K` fails on the finite free modules over `K[ε]/(ε²)` (additive, and
  idempotent complete since the ring is local), where `ε : X → X` has no kernel: `ε ≠ 0` and
  `ε ≫ ε = 0`, while every morphism into `X` killed by `ε` has entries in `(ε)` and so is not mono
  unless its source is `0`.

### 7.4 The generic classification

When `[k] ≠ 0` for all `k ≥ 1`: the images `X_a` of the Jones–Wenzl projections `f_a` in TLKar K δ
are simple and pairwise non-isomorphic, every simple object is one of them, and every object is a
finite biproduct of them. Feed exactly these facts (off-diagonal Hom vanishing,
`End(X_a) ≃ₐ[K] K`, finite decompositions) into §7.3 to obtain `Abelian` and finite-dimensional Hom
spaces on TLKar K δ, and only then state semisimplicity as `TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory`,
whose ambient hypothesis is an abelian category. The predicate and its decomposition API are Tau
Ceti's, from PivotalSpherical; what this layer owns is the Temperley–Lieb content: that the
Jones–Wenzl images are the simples, and the decomposition and classification proofs for them.
**Clebsch–Gordan:** `X_(n+1) ⊗ X_1 ≅ X_n ⊞ X_(n+2)` and in general
`X_a ⊗ X_b ≅ ⊞ X_c` over `c = |a − b|, |a − b| + 2, …, a + b`; quantum dimensions `[n + 1]`; the
fusion ring is `ℤ[X]` with `X_n ↦ S_n`. This is the semisimple representation theory of `U_q(sl₂)`
with no quantum group defined (over `ℂ(q)`, for the subcategory generated by the projections under
`⊞` and `⊗`: Chen, Corollary 2.4.7, p. 19, Lemma 4.0.7, p. 33, and Theorem 4.0.8, p. 35).
*Needs:* §§6.4, 7.1, 7.3; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory`, `TauCeti.SimpleClasses`, `TauCeti.quantumDim`; Mathlib
`CategoryTheory.Simple`, `Polynomial.Chebyshev.S`.

**Checks.**

- `X_1 ⊗ X_1 ≅ X_0 ⊞ X_2` when `[2] ≠ 0`, with dimensions `[2]² = 1 + [3]`.
- The rule `X_a ⊗ X_b ≅ ⊞ X_c` and the dimensions `[c + 1]` agree with `[a + 1][b + 1] = ∑_c [c + 1]`,
  for instance `[3]² = [1] + [3] + [5]` for `a = b = 2`.

### 7.5 The root-of-unity quotient

pp. 44 and 47), spherical with dimensions `[k + 1]`, and braided and ribbon when the braiding
descends. Fusion is stated, following
PivotalSpherical's convention (no bundled fusion class), as explicit instance hypotheses, so that
that roadmap's results apply to these categories: `[RigidCategory C]`, `[Abelian C]`, `[Linear K C]`,
`[MonoidalPreadditive C]`, `[MonoidalLinear K C]`, `TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory C`,
`[Fintype (TauCeti.SimpleClasses C)]`, `[Simple (𝟙_ C)]`, and finite-dimensional Hom spaces over
`K`. `Abelian` and Hom-finiteness come from §7.3, applied to `X_0, …, X_(ℓ−2)` with the root-of-unity
Hom computations (the images of `f_a` for `a ≤ ℓ − 2` are non-negligible, pairwise orthogonal, with
endomorphism ring `K`, and every object decomposes into them); `MonoidalPreadditive` and
`MonoidalLinear` come from §§7.1–7.2; `Simple (𝟙_ C)` from `End 𝟙 ≃ₐ[K] K` in a semisimple category
(a unit with two nonzero summands would have an endomorphism algebra of dimension at least `2`).
Prove that the quotients satisfy every one of these hypotheses, which makes them fusion categories;
they are the entry point to the categories with `Aₙ` fusion rules surveyed by Edie-Michell and
Morrison, whose classification is context here, not a target. *Needs:* §§6.8, 7.1–7.4; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.IsFiniteSemisimpleCategory`, `TauCeti.SimpleClasses`, `TauCeti.SphericalCategory`,
`TauCeti.RibbonCategory`; Mathlib `Abelian`, `MonoidalLinear`, `CategoryTheory.Simple`.

**Checks.**

- At `δ = 1` (quantum order `3`) the quotient has two simple objects `X_0`, `X_1`, with
  `X_1 ⊗ X_1 ≅ X_0` and `dim X_1 = [2] = 1`.
- At `δ = (1 + √5)/2` (quantum order `5`) the simples are `X_0, …, X_3` with dimensions
  `1, δ, δ, 1`, and `X_2 ⊗ X_2 ≅ X_0 ⊞ X_2` with `δ² = 1 + δ`: the quotient contains the Fibonacci
  fusion rules. The untruncated rule would also produce `X_4`, which is negligible (`[5] = 0`).

### Examples

  `X_1 ⊗ X_1 ≅ X_0`; at `δ = (1 + √5)/2` it contains the Fibonacci fusion rules.
