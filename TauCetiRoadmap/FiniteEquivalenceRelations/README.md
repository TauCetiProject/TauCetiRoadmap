# Roadmap: quotients by finite equivalence relations and their effectivity

**Summary.** For a commutative ring `k` and a commutative `k`-algebra `A`, a closed subscheme
`R ⊆ X ×_k X` of `X = Spec A` is an ideal `I ⊆ A ⊗[k] A`. Define when `I` is an equivalence
relation (`IsEquivIdeal`) and prove that this is exactly Mathlib's internal equivalence relation
`CategoryTheory.IsEquivalenceRelation` on the two projections `R ⇉ X` in
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme`, and that it restricts to every closed subscheme. Define the ring of
invariants `A^R = {a | a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a ∈ I}` and the ideal `J(B)` generated by the `b ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ b`,
`b ∈ A^R`. The end theorems are: for `A` finite-dimensional over a field `k`, `Spec A^R` is the
coequalizer of `R ⇉ X` in the category of all schemes; `R` is effective in Mathlib's sense
(`CategoryTheory.IsEffectiveEquivalenceRelation`) iff `I = J(B)`, iff `dim_k I = dim_k J(B)`; an
equivalence relation with a flat projection is effective and `A` is flat over `A^R`; Raicu's
finite equivalence relation on `𝔸²_k` is not `X ×_Y X` for `Y = Spec A^R`, over every field;
its restriction to the finite local subscheme `Z = V(f₁², f₂², f₃²)`, `f₁ = x₁²`,
`f₂ = x₁x₂ - x₂²`, `f₃ = x₂³`, is a closed equivalence relation on a finite local `k`-scheme which
is not effective, over every field, with an explicit obstruction over `𝔽₃`; and toric equivalence
relations on affine toric varieties are effective (Raicu 2010). For modules, along any map of
commutative rings `A → B`: base change is a bijection from `Hom_A(M, N)` onto the morphisms of
descent data `B ⊗ M → B ⊗ N` for every `M` iff `N → B ⊗ N ⇉ (B ⊗ B) ⊗ N` is an equalizer; this
holds for every `N` iff `A → B` is pure, iff extension of scalars is comonadic (the Joyal–Tierney
theorem, Mesablishvili 2000); the quotient map `A^R → A` satisfies the equalizer for `N = A^R`
(Mathlib's `Algebra.IsEffective`) for every `I`, and in the flat case modules on `Spec A^R` are
the modules on `X` with descent data along the quotient map; and `Spec B → Spec A` is a universal
effective epimorphism of affine schemes iff `A → B` is pure. The question whether every closed
equivalence relation on a finite scheme over a field is effective is Grothendieck's (FGA III, §2,
1961).

Suggested home: `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Quotient/FiniteEquivalenceRelation.lean`, beside
Tau Ceti's `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Quotient/EquivalenceRelation.lean`.

## Scope and ownership

**Owned here.** The ideal-theoretic description of closed equivalence relations on affine
schemes over a ring `k` and its comparison with Mathlib's internal equivalence relations in
`Scheme`; the ring of invariants and the difference ideal `J(B)`; the categorical quotient and
the effectivity criterion for `A` finite over a field; the restriction to closed subschemes;
Raicu's examples (the non-effective relation on `𝔸²` and the toric theorem); the non-effective
relation on a finite local scheme of Layer 3; the Amitsur condition, descent of morphisms of
modules and the Joyal–Tierney theorem for pure ring maps, with their application to the quotient
map `A^R → A` and to the universal effective epimorphisms of affine schemes (Layer 4). No roadmap

## Conventions

- `X = Spec A`, `X ×_k X = Spec (A ⊗[k] A)`; a closed subscheme `R ⊆ X ×_k X` is `Spec` of
  `(A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I`. The first projection is `a ↦ a ⊗ 1` (`relFst`), the second `a ↦ 1 ⊗ a`
  (`relSnd`); in `Scheme` they are `specFst` and `specSnd`, and Mathlib's
  `IsEquivalenceRelation p₁ p₂` is applied with `p₁ = specFst`, `p₂ = specSnd`. Witness:
  `relFst I (a) = [a ⊗ 1]`, so for `I = ⊥`, `relFst (a) - relSnd (a) = a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a`, which is `0`
  iff `a ∈ k · 1` when `k` is a field.
- `X ×_k X ×_k X = Spec (A ⊗[k] (A ⊗[k] A))`, with `incl₁₂ (a ⊗ b) = a ⊗ (b ⊗ 1)`,
  `incl₂₃ (a ⊗ b) = 1 ⊗ (a ⊗ b)` and `incl₁₃ (a ⊗ b) = a ⊗ (1 ⊗ b)`. The ideal of `R ×_X R` (pairs
  `(r, r')` with the second projection of `r` equal to the first of `r'`) is `I₁₂ + I₂₃`, the sum of
  the images of `I` by `incl₁₂` and `incl₂₃`. Witness: for `A = k[t]`,
  `incl₁₃ (t ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ t) = t ⊗ 1 ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ 1 ⊗ t` is the sum of `incl₁₂` and `incl₂₃` of
  `t ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ t`.
- **Effective** means Mathlib's `IsEffectiveEquivalenceRelation`: there is `π : X ⟶ Y` such that
  `R` is the kernel pair of `π` and the square `R ⇉ X ⟶ Y` is a pushout. For a reflexive pair a
  pushout with equal legs is a coequalizer. Kollár 2012, ¶50, and Raicu 2010, §1, call a relation
  effective when `R → X ×_Y X` is an isomorphism for some `Y`; for `A` finite over a field the two
  agree by 1.2 and 1.3. Witness: the diagonal `I = ker μ` is effective with `Y = X`, and `I = ⊥`
  (all of `X ×_k X`) is effective with `Y = Spec k` when `A ≠ 0`.
- Gradings: `k[x₁, x₂] ⊗[k] k[y₁, y₂]` is graded by total degree; Raicu's ideal and the ideal of
  `Z` are homogeneous, so `I`, `A^R` and `J(B)` are graded and compared degree by degree. Witness:
  the cocycle `(x₁y₂ - x₂y₁)y₂³` has degree `5`, the degree in which the comparison of 3.1 fails.
- **Toric.** For a commutative monoid `σ`, the diagonal action of the torus on
  `Spec k[σ] ×_k Spec k[σ]` is encoded by the coaction `diagonalCoaction`,
  `χ^s ⊗ χ^t ↦ (χ^s ⊗ χ^t) ⊗ χ^(s + t)`, with values in `k[σ]` rather than in the group algebra
  of the torus: for `σ` cancellative the map `k[σ] → k[σ^gp]` is injective, and over a field
  invariance under either coaction says that `I` is homogeneous for the grading by `s + t`.
  Witness: for `σ = ℕ`, `x - y ↦ (x - y) ⊗ χ¹`.
- **Descent of modules** (Layer 4). For `A → B`, tensor products are over `A`, with `B` on the
  left: the unit is `n ↦ 1 ⊗ n`, the cofaces are `coface₁ (b ⊗ n) = (b ⊗ 1) ⊗ n` and
  `coface₂ (b ⊗ n) = (1 ⊗ b) ⊗ n`, and a descent datum is a coaction `M → B ⊗ M` as in
  Mesablishvili 2000, p. 39; these are the coalgebras of the comonad of
  `ModuleCat.extendRestrictScalarsAdj` by AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §3.2
  (`overlapCoalgebraEquivalence`). Witness: for `B = A × A`, `B ⊗ B = A⁴` indexed by
  `(1, 1), (1, 2), (2, 1), (2, 2)`, and for `N = A`, `coface₁ (u, v) = (u, u, v, v)` and
  `coface₂ (u, v) = (u, v, u, v)`.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 translates closed equivalence relations on `Spec A` into ideals of `A ⊗[k] A` and back, over
any commutative ring `k`. Layer 1 builds the invariants and the quotient and proves the effectivity
criterion `I = J(B)` for `A` finite over a field, with its linear-algebra form. Layer 2 derives the
flat case from Tau Ceti's quotient by a finite locally free equivalence relation. Layer 3 gives the
examples: Raicu's non-effective relation on `𝔸²`, its restriction to a finite local scheme, which
answers Grothendieck's question over a field in the negative, an explicit obstruction over `𝔽₃`, and
Raicu's toric theorem. Layer 4 turns to modules: the Amitsur condition and descent of morphisms
along any ring map, the Joyal–Tierney theorem for pure maps, its application to the quotient
map `A^R → A`, and the universal effective epimorphisms of affine schemes. The hardest targets are
1.2 (coequalizers in all schemes, not only affine ones) and 3.4; 3.2 and 3.3 are finite
computations.

## Layer 0: equivalence relations as ideals

### 0.1 Equivalence ideals

Define `incl₁₂`, `incl₂₃`, `incl₁₃` and `IsEquivIdeal I` with three fields:
`IsEquivIdeal.le_ker_mul` (`I ⊆ ker (A ⊗[k] A → A)`, the diagonal factors through `R`),
`IsEquivIdeal.map_comm` (`I` is stable under `a ⊗ b ↦ b ⊗ a`) and `IsEquivIdeal.map_incl₁₃_le`
(`incl₁₃ (I) ⊆ incl₁₂ (I) + incl₂₃ (I)`, the outer projection sends `R ×_X R` into `R`). These are
conditions (1)–(3) of Kollár 2012, ¶50 (Raicu 2010, §2.1, for `X = 𝔸ⁿ_k`). Define the projections
`relFst`, `relSnd` and their spectra `specFst`, `specSnd`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `Algebra.TensorProduct.map`, `Algebra.TensorProduct.lmul'`,
`Algebra.TensorProduct.comm`, `Ideal.map`, `Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ`, `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.map`.

**Checks.**

- The diagonal `I = ker μ` and the whole `X ×_k X` (`I = ⊥`) satisfy the three fields. `I = ⊤`
  (the empty relation) satisfies `map_comm` and `map_incl₁₃_le` and fails `le_ker_mul` as soon
  as `A ≠ 0`, since `1 ∉ ker μ`: reflexivity is not implied by the other two.
- `map_incl₁₃_le` is needed: on `A = k[t]/(t²)`, `char k ≠ 2`, the scheme-theoretic union of the
  graphs of `t ↦ t` and `t ↦ -t` has ideal `(x - y) ∩ (x + y) = (xy)` in
  `k[x, y]/(x², y²)`, with `x = t ⊗ 1`, `y = 1 ⊗ t`; it is reflexive (`μ(xy) = t² = 0`) and
  symmetric, but `incl₁₃ (xy) = xz` is not in `(xy, yz)` in `k[x, y, z]/(x², y², z²)` (degree 2:
  `xz ∉ span {xy, yz}`). A definition by reflexivity and symmetry, or by unions of graphs of a
  group action, would accept it.
- `map_comm` is needed: on `A = k × k` with points `p, q`, `A ⊗[k] A = k⁴` has the four points
  `(p, p), (p, q), (q, p), (q, q)`, and the closed subscheme `{(p, p), (q, q), (p, q)}`, with ideal
  generated by the idempotent of `(q, p)`, is reflexive and transitive and not symmetric.

### 0.2 Restriction to a closed subscheme

Prove `IsEquivIdeal.map_quotient`: if `I` is an equivalence ideal on `A` and `𝔞 ⊆ A` is an ideal,
the image of `I` in `(A ⧸ 𝔞) ⊗[k] (A ⧸ 𝔞)` is an equivalence ideal on `A ⧸ 𝔞`: it is the ideal
of `R ∩ (Z ×_k Z)`, `Z = Spec (A ⧸ 𝔞)`, and the restriction of an equivalence relation on
`X(T)` to the subset `Z(T)` is one (Kollár 2012, ¶50, used with `X = 𝔸ⁿ`). No stability of `Z`
under `R` is assumed. *Needs:* 0.1, Mathlib `Ideal.map_map`, `Algebra.TensorProduct.map`.

**Checks.**

- `𝔞 = ⊤`: `A ⧸ 𝔞 = 0`, and every ideal of the zero ring satisfies the three fields.
- `𝔞 = (t)` on `A = k[t]` with `I = ⊥`: the image is `⊥` in `k ⊗[k] k = k`, the total relation
  on a point.
- For the relation `x² = y²` on `𝔸¹` (`I = (x² - y²)`) and `𝔞 = (t - 1)`, the image is `0` in
  `k ⊗ k`: `R ∩ (Z × Z)` is the point `(1, 1)`, although `R` contains `(1, -1)`, which does not
  lie in `Z × Z`.

### 0.3 Comparison with Mathlib's equivalence relations

Prove `isEquivalenceRelation_iff`: `IsEquivIdeal I` iff
`IsEquivalenceRelation (specFst I) (specSnd I)` in `Scheme`. Route: `Spec` is fully faithful on
rings, so a section `X ⟶ R` of both projections is a ring map `(A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I → A` equal to `μ` on
pure tensors (reflexivity); a swap `R ⟶ R` is `Algebra.TensorProduct.comm` modulo `I` (symmetry);
the pullback of `specSnd` and `specFst` is
`Spec ((A ⊗[k] A ⧸ I) ⊗[A] (A ⊗[k] A ⧸ I)) = Spec (A ⊗[k] (A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ (I₁₂ + I₂₃))` (transitivity);
joint monicity holds since `R → X ×_k X` is a closed immersion and `X ×_k X → X × X` is a
monomorphism. The definition is Stacks, Definition 39.3.1 (Tag 022P), for the base `Spec k`.
Prove `isEquivIdeal_iff_isSpecEquivRel`: `IsEquivIdeal I` iff
`(relFst I).toRingHom.IsSpecEquivRel (relSnd I).toRingHom`, the ideal conditions against Tau
Ceti's conditions on `T`-points. Route: a `T`-point of `Spec ((A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I)` is a ring map
`A ⊗[k] A → T` killing `I`, determined by its two restrictions to `A`; reflexivity at `T = A`,
`u = id`, is `I ⊆ ker μ`, symmetry at `T = (A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I` is the swap, transitivity at the ring
of `R ×_X R` is `map_incl₁₃_le`, and conversely each ideal condition gives the `T`-point condition
for every `T` by composition. One direction of `isEquivalenceRelation_iff` is then Tau Ceti's
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isSpecEquivRel_appTop` transported along
`Γ(Spec A, ⊤) ≅ A`. *Needs:* 0.1, Mathlib `CategoryTheory.IsEquivalenceRelation`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.map`, pullbacks of affine schemes; Tau Ceti `RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isSpecEquivRel_appTop`.

**Checks.**

- For `I = ker μ`, `(A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I ≅ A` and `specFst = specSnd`, the same isomorphism `R ≅ X`;
  a pair of equal isomorphisms is an equivalence relation in any category.
- For `I = ⊥`, the pair is the two projections of `X ×_k X`, the kernel pair of `X ⟶ Spec k`
  (Mathlib `IsKernelPair.equivalenceRelation`).
- The graph union of 0.1 (`I = (xy)` on `k[t]/(t²)`) has no transitivity morphism: there is no
  `Spec` of a ring map from `(A ⊗ A) ⧸ I` to the pullback ring compatible with the outer
  projections, because `xz` would have to vanish there.

### Examples

- The diagonal, the total relation `X ×_k X`, and the kernel pair `A ⊗[C] A` of any subalgebra
  `C ⊆ A`, whose ideal is generated by the `c ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ c`; on `T`-points this is Tau Ceti's
  `RingHom.isSpecEquivRel_tensorProduct`.
- `x² = y²` on `𝔸¹` (`char k ≠ 2`): the kernel pair of `t ↦ t²`.

## Layer 1: invariants, the quotient and effectivity

### 1.1 Invariants and the difference ideal

Define `invariants I : Subalgebra k A`, the equalizer `AlgHom.equalizer (relFst I) (relSnd I)`, that
is `A^R = {a | a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a ∈ I}` (Stacks, Section 39.23, Tag 03BE, equation 39.23.0.1), and
`differenceIdeal I`, the ideal of `A ⊗[k] A` generated by the `b ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ b`, `b ∈ A^R` (Kollár
2012, ¶50). Then `differenceIdeal I ≤ I`, and `(A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ differenceIdeal I` is `A ⊗[A^R] A`, the
ring of `X ×_Y X` for `Y = Spec A^R`. Define `specQuotientMap I : X ⟶ Spec A^R` and prove
`specFst_comp_specQuotientMap`: it equalizes `specFst` and `specSnd` (proved in `Suggested.lean`).
*Needs:* 0.1, Mathlib `AlgHom.equalizer`, `Ideal.span`, `CommRingCat.ofHom_comp`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.map_comp`.

**Checks.**

- `I = ker μ`: every `a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a` lies in `ker μ`, so `A^R = A` and `differenceIdeal = ker μ`.
- `I = ⊥`, `k` a field, `A ≠ 0`: `a ⊗ 1 = 1 ⊗ a` forces `a ∈ k`, so `A^R = k` and
  `differenceIdeal = ⊥`. A definition of invariants as `{a | μ (a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a) = 0}` would give
  `A`.
- Raicu's relation on `𝔸²` (3.1), over `ℚ`: the invariant forms have dimensions `0, 2, 1, 3, 2` in
  degrees `1` to `5`, spanned in degrees `2` and `3` by `f₁ = x₁²`, `f₂ = x₁x₂ - x₂²` and
  `f₃ = x₂³`, and in degrees `4` and `5` by `f₁², f₁f₂, f₂²` and `f₁f₃, f₂f₃`; `differenceIdeal`
  is strictly smaller than `I` in degree `5`, of dimension `43` against `44`.

### 1.2 The categorical quotient

Prove `isColimit_cofork`: for `k` a field and `A` finite-dimensional, `Spec A^R` with
`specQuotientMap` is a coequalizer of `specFst` and `specSnd` in the category of all schemes, for
every ideal `I`. In the category of affine schemes this is the anti-equivalence with rings and the
definition of `A^R` as an equalizer (Stacks, Section 39.23, Tag 03BE). For all schemes the
statement is Grothendieck's: in the category of finite schemes over a locally noetherian base,
finite colimits computed on algebras are colimits in the category of all schemes (FGA III, §2,
p. 212-04). Route: `A` is integral over `A^R` (Stacks, Lemma 39.23.4, Tag 03BJ, in the flat case;
here `A` is finite over `k ⊆ A^R`), so `|Spec A| → |Spec A^R|` is surjective with finite fibres;
an invariant morphism `X ⟶ T` sends each fibre into one point of `T`, hence factors locally
through affine opens of `T`, where the affine case applies. *Needs:* 1.1, Mathlib
`AlgebraicGeometry.Spec`, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom` gluing, `Cofork.ofπ`.

**Checks.**

- `I = ⊤` (`R = ∅`): `A^R = A`, and `Spec A` is the coequalizer of the empty pair, as it should
  be.
- `A = k × k`, `I = ⊥`: `A^R = k`, and the coequalizer of the two projections of
  `X ×_k X ⇉ X` is the point `Spec k`.
- `A = k[t]/(t²)`, `I = ker μ`: `A^R = A`, so the coequalizer of the identity pair is `X`; a
  quotient computed on reduced rings would give `Spec k`.

### 1.3 The effectivity criterion

Prove `isEffectiveEquivalenceRelation_iff`: for `k` a field and `A` finite-dimensional,
`IsEffectiveEquivalenceRelation (specFst I) (specSnd I)` iff `I = differenceIdeal I`, that is iff
`R → X ×_Y X` is an isomorphism for `Y = Spec A^R` (Kollár 2012, ¶50, « R is effective iff
equality holds »). Prove `isEffectiveEquivalenceRelation_iff_finrank`: iff
`finrank k I = finrank k (differenceIdeal I)`, two subspaces of the finite-dimensional
`A ⊗[k] A`, the second contained in the first. Route: by 1.2 any `π` making `R` effective is
`specQuotientMap` up to isomorphism; the kernel pair of `Spec A → Spec A^R` is
`Spec (A ⊗[A^R] A)`, whose ideal in `A ⊗[k] A` is `differenceIdeal I`; the pushout clause follows
from 1.2 and reflexivity (0.3). The finrank form is `Submodule.eq_of_le_of_finrank_eq`. No
hypothesis on `I`: if `I = differenceIdeal I`, `I` is the ideal of a kernel pair, hence an
equivalence ideal. *Needs:* 0.3, 1.1, 1.2, Mathlib `IsEffectiveEquivalenceRelation`,
`IsKernelPair`, `Submodule.eq_of_le_of_finrank_eq`.

**Checks.**

- `I = ⊥` on `A = k × k`: `finrank k I = 0 = finrank k (differenceIdeal I)`, effective with
  `Y = Spec k`.
- `I = (x² - y²)` on `A = k[t]/(t⁴)`, `char k ≠ 2`: `A^R` contains `k[t²]/(t⁴)`, so `I` is the
  ideal of the kernel pair of `t ↦ t²`, and both sides of the criterion hold.
- The restriction of Raicu's relation to `Z` (3.2): `finrank` differs by one in degree `5`
  (`52` against `51` for the lifts to `k[x, y]`), so the criterion fails.

### Examples

- The quotient of `k × k` by its total relation is `Spec k`.
- The kernel pair of a subalgebra `C ⊆ A`: `A^R ⊇ C`, and the relation is effective, with
  `Y = Spec A^R`. The inclusion can be strict: for `C = k[x³, x⁵] ⊆ A = k[x]`, put
  `u = x ⊗ 1` and `v = 1 ⊗ x` in `A ⊗[C] A`, where `u³ = v³` and `u⁵ = v⁵`; the identity
  `u⁷ − v⁷ = (u⁴ + u³v + uv³ + v⁴)(u³ − v³) − uv(u⁵ − v⁵)` gives `x⁷ ⊗ 1 = 1 ⊗ x⁷`, so
  `x⁷ ∈ A^R` and `x⁷ ∉ C`; the finite epimorphism `Spec A → Spec C` is not strict (FGA I, §A,
  n° 2, b).

## Layer 2: the flat case

### 2.1 Flat relations are effective

Prove `isEffectiveEquivalenceRelation_of_flat`: for `k` a field, `A` finite-dimensional and `I` an
equivalence ideal such that `(A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I` is flat over `A` through the first factor
(`Algebra.TensorProduct.leftAlgebra`), the relation is effective and `A` is flat over `A^R`
(SGA 3, Exp. V, Théorème 4.1 (iv); FGA III, Théorème 5.1 (iv), p. 212-12; Stacks,
Proposition 39.23.9, Tag 03BM). Route: `B = (A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I` is finite over `A` through `relFst`
(`A` is finite over `k`), finitely presented since `A` is noetherian, and flat by hypothesis, hence
projective. By 0.3, `relFst`, `relSnd` satisfy `RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel`, and the ring of invariants
`(relFst I).toRingHom.eqLocus (relSnd I).toRingHom` has the carrier of `invariants I`. Tau Ceti's
`RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.isPullback_SpecMap` makes `R` the kernel pair of `specQuotientMap`,
`RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.isColimitCofork` makes it the coequalizer, whence the pushout clause by
reflexivity as in 1.3, and `RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.flat` gives `A` flat over `A^R`. In scheme
form, the same follows from 0.3 with `specFst` finite, flat and locally of finite presentation, by
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isKernelPair_affineCoequalizerπ`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isColimitAffineCoequalizerCofork` and
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.flat_affineCoequalizerπ`, transported along
`affineCoequalizer ≅ Spec A^R`. *Needs:* 0.3, 1.1, 1.3, Tau Ceti
`RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.isPullback_SpecMap`, `RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.isColimitCofork`,
`RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.flat`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isKernelPair_affineCoequalizerπ`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.isColimitAffineCoequalizerCofork`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.flat_affineCoequalizerπ`, Mathlib `Module.Flat`, `Module.Finite`,
`Module.Flat.projective_of_finitePresentation`.

**Checks.**

- The free action of `ℤ/2` swapping the factors of `A = k × k`: `R = Δ ∪ σ` is `I = ⊥`, free of
  rank `2` over `A`, and `A^R = k`, with `A` free of rank `2` over `k`.
- `I = ker μ`: `(A ⊗ A) ⧸ I = A` is free of rank `1` over `A`, and `A^R = A`.
- The flatness hypothesis is needed: the relation of 3.2 is a closed equivalence relation on a
  finite scheme which is not effective, so its projection is not flat.

### Examples

## Layer 3: examples

### 3.1 Raicu's relation on the affine plane

Define `raicuCocycle k = (x₁y₂ - x₂y₁) y₂³` and `raicuIdeal k`, the ideal of
`k[x₁, x₂] ⊗[k] k[y₁, y₂]` generated by `x₁² - y₁²`, `x₁x₂ - x₂² - (y₁y₂ - y₂²)`, `x₂³ - y₂³` and
the cocycle, with `xᵢ = Xᵢ ⊗ 1` and `yᵢ = 1 ⊗ Xᵢ`. Prove `raicuIdeal_isEquivIdeal`,
`raicuIdeal_finite` (the quotient is a finite `k[x]`-module) and `differenceIdeal_raicuIdeal_ne`:
over every field, `R` is not `X ×_Y X` for `Y = Spec A^R` (Kollár 2012, ¶50, the example and the
claim that it stays non-effective after every base change; Raicu 2010, Example 2.1). Route: the
cocycle condition `f(x, y) + f(y, z) - f(x, z) ∈ J(x, y) + J(y, z)` for the ideal `J` generated by
the `fᵢ(x) - fᵢ(y)` gives transitivity; by homogeneity only invariants of degree `≤ 5` matter in
degree `5`, where `I` and `J(B)` agree below degree `5`, so non-effectivity is the statement that
the cocycle is not congruent modulo `J` to a difference `g(x) - g(y)` of degree `5`, a rank
computation over `ℤ` whose quotient is free (Kollár 2012, ¶50). *Needs:* 0.1, 1.1, Mathlib
`MvPolynomial`.

**Checks.**

- The three generators `fᵢ(x) - fᵢ(y)` lie in `ker μ`, and so does the cocycle (`x = y` kills
  `x₁y₂ - x₂y₁`); finiteness: `y₁² ≡ x₁²` and `y₂³ ≡ x₂³` modulo `I`.
- Symmetry: the swap sends the cocycle to `-(x₁y₂ - x₂y₁)x₂³`, and `(x₁y₂ - x₂y₁)x₂³` is the
  cocycle plus `(x₁y₂ - x₂y₁)(x₂³ - y₂³) ∈ I`, so `map_comm` holds although the cocycle is not
  antisymmetric on the nose.
- Over `ℚ`, in degree `5`, `dim I₅ = 44` and `dim (J(B))₅ = 43`, inside the `56`-dimensional
  space of quintic forms in `x₁, x₂, y₁, y₂`, and `raicuCocycle` is not in `(J(B))₅`; in degrees
  `1` to `4` the dimensions agree (`0, 2, 9, 23`).

### 3.2 A non-effective relation on a finite local scheme

Define `raicuArtinIdeal k = (f₁², f₂², f₃²) ⊆ k[x₁, x₂]` and `raicuArtinRelation k`, the image of
`raicuIdeal k` in `O(Z) ⊗[k] O(Z)`, `O(Z) = k[x₁, x₂] ⧸ raicuArtinIdeal k`: by 0.2 and 3.1 it is a
closed equivalence relation on `Z = Spec O(Z)`, with finite projections since `O(Z)` is
finite-dimensional. Since `f₁² = x₁⁴` and `f₃² = x₂⁶`, `Z` is supported at the origin, and `O(Z)` is
a local artinian `k`-algebra with residue field `k`. Prove
`not_isEffectiveEquivalenceRelation_raicuArtin`: over every field `k`, this relation is not
effective. This answers negatively, over a field, the question of FGA III, §2, p. 212-05: whether
every closed equivalence relation on a finite scheme over a field is effective. The restriction to
`Z` is this roadmap's own; it is not in Raicu's or Kollár's text. It is not formal: the invariants
`B(Z)` of `R ∩ (Z × Z)` are computed on `O(Z)` and can be more than the images of the invariants on
`𝔸²`, so the difference ideal can grow. Route: by 1.3 it suffices that `I(Z) ≠ J(B(Z))`,
`I(Z) = raicuArtinRelation k`; both are graded, and in degree `5` their lifts to `k[x, y]` satisfy
`dim (I + K)₅ = 52 > 51 = dim (J + K)₅`, `K` the ideal of `Z × Z` and `J` the lift of `J(B(Z))`, a
rank computation needing the invariants of `O(Z)` of degree `≤ 5` only. Over `ℚ` the eliminations
divide only by integers whose prime factors are 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 41 and 61; for every other prime `p`
the same elimination runs over `𝔽_p` with the same ranks, the seven listed primes are checked
directly, and ranks do not change under field extension from the prime field. *Needs:* 0.2, 1.3,
3.1, Mathlib `Ideal.Quotient`, `MvPolynomial`, `Module.finrank`.

**Checks.**

- `dim_k O(Z) = 15` when `char k ≠ 2`, with Hilbert function `1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 2`; when `char k = 2`,
  `f₂² = x₁²x₂² + x₂⁴` and `dim_k O(Z) = 16`, Hilbert function `1, 2, 3, 4, 3, 2, 1`. In degree `6`,
  the relations `x₁²f₂², x₁x₂f₂², x₂²f₂²` on `x₁³x₂³, x₁²x₂⁴, x₁x₂⁵` have determinant
  `det [[-2, 1, 0], [1, -2, 1], [0, 1, -2]] = -4`.
- The exponents matter: for `Z = V(f₁ᵃ, f₂ᵇ, f₃ᶜ)` over `𝔽₃`, comparing `I(Z)` with `J(B(Z))` in
  degrees `≤ 14`, with the invariants of degree `≤ 7`, finds no gap for `(1, 1, 1)`, `(2, 1, 1)`,
  `(1, 2, 1)`, `(1, 1, 2)`, `(2, 2, 1)`, `(2, 1, 2)` and `(1, 2, 2)`; `(2, 2, 2)` is the smallest
  triple with a gap, and `(3, 3, 2)`, of length `27`, has gaps in degrees `5` and `6`
  (`44 > 43`, `77 > 75`).
- `raicuArtinRelation` is neither `⊥` nor the diagonal `ker μ`: it contains `x₁² - y₁²`, nonzero in
  `O(Z) ⊗ O(Z)`, and its degree-`1` part is `0` (Raicu's generators have degree `≥ 2` and the ideal
  of `Z × Z` starts in degree `4`), so it does not contain `x₁ - y₁ ∈ ker μ`.

### 3.3 The obstruction over 𝔽₃

Prove `raicuCocycle_not_mem_differenceIdeal`: over `𝔽₃`, the image of `raicuCocycle` in
`O(Z) ⊗ O(Z)` lies in `raicuArtinRelation` (it is a generator) and not in its `differenceIdeal`.
Everything is finite over `𝔽₃`, and a certificate is: a basis of the invariants of `O(Z)` of

form on `(O(Z) ⊗ O(Z))₅` that vanishes on `(J(B(Z)))₅` and not on the cocycle. Checking it is matrix
arithmetic over `ZMod 3`, by `decide` or by a verified elimination. The same holds over `ℚ` and
`𝔽₂`. *Needs:* 3.2, 1.1, Mathlib `ZMod`.

**Checks.**

- The cocycle has degree `5`, and `(O(Z) ⊗ O(Z))₅` has dimension `∑ h_i h_{5-i}` over the Hilbert

- Membership in `raicuArtinRelation` is by the definition of `raicuIdeal`; non-membership in the

### 3.4 Toric relations are effective

Define `diagonalCoaction k σ` (Conventions) and `IsToric k σ I`: the image of `I` by the coaction
lies in the ideal generated by `I` in `(k[σ] ⊗ k[σ]) ⊗ k[σ]`, so `R` is invariant under the
diagonal action of the torus (Raicu 2010, §1, Definition). Prove `eq_differenceIdeal_of_isToric`:
for `σ` an affine monoid (finitely generated, cancellative, torsion-free) and `I` a toric
equivalence ideal on `k[σ]`, `I = differenceIdeal I` (Raicu 2010, Theorem 4.1, giving an affine
toric `Y` with `R ≅ X ×_Y X`, then `A^R ⊇ O(Y)` and `J(O(Y)) ⊆ J(B) ⊆ I`; Kollár 2012,
Theorem 51). Route: the Amitsur complex of a map of monoid algebras is exact (Raicu 2010,
Theorem 3.1). *Needs:* 0.1, 1.1, Mathlib `AddMonoidAlgebra`, `Bialgebra.comulAlgHom`, `IsAddFG`,
`IsCancelAdd`, `IsAddTorsionFree`.

**Checks.**

- `σ = ℕ`, `I = (x² - y²)`, `char k ≠ 2`: homogeneous of degree `2` for `s + t`, toric, and equal
  to the difference ideal of `k[t²]`.
- Raicu's relation of 3.1 is not toric for `σ = ℕ²`: `x₁x₂ - x₂²` mixes the degrees `(1, 1)` and
  `(0, 2)`, so 3.4 and 3.1 do not conflict.
- `σ = ℕ`, `I = ker μ = (x - y)`: `x - y ↦ (x - y) ⊗ χ¹`, toric, with `A^R = A`.

## Layer 4: descent of modules along the quotient map

### 4.1 The Amitsur condition and descent of morphisms

For a map of commutative rings `A → B` and an `A`-module `N`, define the cofaces
`coface₁ (b ⊗ n) = (b ⊗ 1) ⊗ n` and `coface₂ (b ⊗ n) = (1 ⊗ b) ⊗ n` from `B ⊗[A] N` to
`(B ⊗[A] B) ⊗[A] N`, and `IsAmitsurEqualizer A B N`: `N → B ⊗[A] N`, `n ↦ 1 ⊗ n`, is injective
and its image is the equalizer of the two cofaces. For `N = A`, `B ⊗[A] A = B` and
`IsAmitsurEqualizer A B A` is Mathlib's `Algebra.IsEffective A B` together with the injectivity
of `A → B`. This is the unit of the adjunction between `A`-modules and descent data at `N`
(Mesablishvili 2000, p. 39), and condition (1) of Stacks, Lemma 35.13.2 (Tag 0EUA), for one
morphism. For a `B`-linear `g : B ⊗[A] M → B ⊗[A] N` define
`pullLeft g` and `pullRight g`, its base changes along `b ↦ b ⊗ 1` and `b ↦ 1 ⊗ b`, both written
on `(B ⊗[A] B) ⊗[A] M`, and `IsCompatible g : pullLeft g = pullRight g`: `g` is a morphism of
the canonical descent data on `B ⊗[A] M` and `B ⊗[A] N` (Stacks, Definition 35.3.1 (2),
Tag 023G; Mesablishvili 2000, p. 39). Prove `bijOn_baseChange`: if `IsAmitsurEqualizer A B N`,
then `f ↦ f.baseChange B` is a bijection from `M →ₗ[A] N` onto the compatible `g`, for every
`A`-module `M`; and `isAmitsurEqualizer_iff_bijOn`: bijectivity for `M = A` alone gives
`IsAmitsurEqualizer A B N`. This is the per-module form of Mesablishvili 2000, Proposition 2.
Route: a `B`-linear `g` is determined by the `A`-linear `m ↦ g (1 ⊗ m)`, and `g` is compatible
exactly when this map takes values in the equalizer of the two cofaces, which is `N`; no
presentation of `M` is needed. Conversely, for `M = A` a compatible `g` is `x ↦ x • g (1 ⊗ 1)`
with `g (1 ⊗ 1)` in the equalizer. *Needs:* Mathlib `Algebra.TensorProduct.includeLeft`,
`Algebra.TensorProduct.includeRight`, `LinearMap.rTensor`, `LinearMap.lTensor`,
`TensorProduct.mk`, `TensorProduct.assoc`, `TensorProduct.comm`, `LinearMap.baseChange`,
`Set.BijOn`, `Algebra.IsEffective`.

**Checks.**

- `ℤ → ℚ`, `N = ℤ`: `ℤ → ℚ` is injective, but `ℚ ⊗ ℚ = ℚ` and the two cofaces agree on all of
  `ℚ`, so the equalizer is `ℚ ≠ ℤ` and `IsAmitsurEqualizer` fails. Correspondingly every
  `ℚ`-linear `g : ℚ → ℚ` is compatible, and base change from `Hom(ℤ, ℤ) = ℤ` onto these is not
  surjective. A definition by injectivity of `N → B ⊗ N` alone would accept it.
- `ℤ → ℚ`, `N = ℤ/2`: `ℚ ⊗ ℤ/2 = 0`, so every element of `B ⊗ N` is `1 ⊗ 0` and the equalizer
  clause holds, while injectivity fails. A definition by the equalizer clause alone would accept
  it.
- `B = A × A`, `M = N = A`: `B ⊗ B = A⁴` (Conventions), the equalizer of the cofaces is the
  diagonal `A ⊆ A × A`, and `IsAmitsurEqualizer` holds. A `B`-linear `g` is multiplication by
  some `(u, v)`; `pullLeft g` and `pullRight g` are multiplication by `(u, u, v, v)` and
  `(u, v, u, v)`, so `g` is compatible iff `u = v`, that is iff `g` is the base change of
  multiplication by `u`. A definition of morphisms of descent data by `B`-linearity alone would
  accept `(1, 0)`.

### 4.2 Pure maps: the Joyal–Tierney theorem

Prove `forall_isAmitsurEqualizer_iff`: `IsAmitsurEqualizer A B P` holds for every `A`-module `P` iff
`A → B` is universally injective, or pure: `P → B ⊗[A] P` is injective for every `A`-module `P`
(Stacks, Definition 35.4.5, Tag 08WL). Prove `full_faithful_comparison_iff`: the comparison functor
from `A`-modules to the coalgebras of the comonad of `ModuleCat.extendRestrictScalarsAdj` (the
descent data of Mesablishvili 2000, p. 39, by AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §3.3,
`moduleCanonicalComparison`) is full and faithful, that is `A → B` is a descent morphism for modules
(Stacks, Definition 35.4.15, Tag 08WY), iff `A → B` is pure (Mesablishvili 2000, Proposition 2;
Stacks, Theorem 35.4.22, Tag 08XA, (a) ⇔ (c)). Prove `nonempty_comonadicLeftAdjoint_iff`:
`ModuleCat.extendScalars (algebraMap A B)` is comonadic, that is `A → B` is an effective descent
morphism for modules, iff `A → B` is pure (Mesablishvili 2000, Theorem 4; Stacks, Theorem 35.4.22,
Tag 08XA, (b) ⇔ (c)). This is the theorem of Joyal and Tierney, unpublished, announced by Olivier;
Mesablishvili 2000 gives the first published proof (Mesablishvili 2000, abstract; Stacks, Section
35.4, Tag 08WE). The modules are those of the universe of `A` and `B`, which contains every finitely
presented module. Route: « pure ⇒ Amitsur » for `P` applies purity to `P` and to the cokernel `Q` of
`P → B ⊗ P`: the map `(b₁ ⊗ b₂) ⊗ p ↦ b₂ ⊗ [b₁ ⊗ p]`, `(B ⊗ B) ⊗ P → B ⊗ Q`, sends
`coface₁ x - coface₂ x` to `1 ⊗ [x]`, so an equalized `x` has `[x] = 0` in `Q`; the converse is the
injectivity clause. Full faithfulness is then 4.1 for every `N`, and effectivity is Mesablishvili's
split-coequalizer argument (Mesablishvili 2000, Lemma 1, Proposition 3 and Theorem 4; Stacks, Lemma
35.4.20, Tag 08X7). *Needs:* 4.1, Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Comonad.comparison`,
`CategoryTheory.ComonadicLeftAdjoint`, `ModuleCat.extendScalars`,
`ModuleCat.extendRestrictScalarsAdj`, `CategoryTheory.Functor.Full`,
`CategoryTheory.Functor.Faithful`, and for the Checks `Algebra.IsEffective.of_faithfullyFlat`,
`Algebra.IsEffective.of_section`; Tau Ceti `Module.FaithfullyFlat.exact_mk_one_lTensor_sub_mk_one`
for the faithfully flat Checks.

**Checks.**

- `ℤ → ℤ[x]` is free, hence faithfully flat, hence pure (Stacks, Lemma 35.4.8, Tag 08WP); the
  Amitsur condition holds for every `P` by Tau Ceti's
  `Module.FaithfullyFlat.exact_mk_one_lTensor_sub_mk_one`, and for `P = ℤ` it is Mathlib's
  `Algebra.IsEffective.of_faithfullyFlat`.
- `ℤ → ℚ` is flat, injective and an epimorphism of rings, and not pure: `ℤ/2 → ℚ ⊗ ℤ/2 = 0`. Its
  comparison functor is not faithful: the zero map and the identity of `ℤ/2` become equal.
- `ℤ → ℤ × ℤ/2` is split by the first projection, hence pure (Stacks, Example 35.4.6, Tag 08WM;
  for `P = ℤ`, `Algebra.IsEffective.of_section` with the projection as a ring map), and not flat,
  since `ℤ/2` is not a flat `ℤ`-module: effective descent of modules holds without flatness, where
  Mathlib's `comonadicExtendScalars` asks for faithful flatness.

### 4.3 The quotient map

Prove `isEffective_invariants`: for every commutative ring `k`, `k`-algebra `A` and ideal
`I ⊆ A ⊗[k] A`, `Algebra.IsEffective (invariants k A I) A`: `A^R → A ⇉ A ⊗[A^R] A` is an equalizer.
With the injectivity of `A^R ⊆ A` (`Subtype.val_injective`) this is `IsAmitsurEqualizer` for the
module `A^R` (4.1), and `Algebra.codRestrictEqLocusPushoutCocone.surjective_of_isEffective` makes
`A^R` the equalizer of `A ⇉ A ⊗[A^R] A` in `CommRingCat`. By 4.1, base change from
`Hom_{A^R}(M, A^R)` onto the compatible maps `A ⊗[A^R] M → A` is bijective for every `A^R`-module
`M`. Route: `A ⊗[A^R] A^R` is `A`; the map `A ⊗[k] A → (A ⊗[k] A) ⧸ I` factors through `A ⊗[A^R] A`,
since `differenceIdeal I ≤ I` (1.1); so `a ⊗ 1 = 1 ⊗ a` in `A ⊗[A^R] A` gives `a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a ∈ I`,
that is `a ∈ A^R`. In the language of 1.2, `Spec A^R` is the coequalizer of the kernel pair of
`Spec A → Spec A^R` in affine schemes; when `A` is a finite `A^R`-module, `Spec A → Spec A^R` is a
strict epimorphism in all schemes, since a finite `Spec A' → Spec A` is one iff `A` is the equalizer
of `A' ⇉ A' ⊗[A] A'` (FGA I, §A, n° 2, b). Prove `nonempty_comonadicLeftAdjoint_invariants`: under
the hypotheses of 2.1, extension of scalars along `A^R → A` is comonadic, so the modules on
`Y = Spec A^R` are the `A`-modules with descent data along `X → Y` (in the language of
AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §3.2), whose kernel pair is `R` by 2.1 (FGA I, §B, Théorème 1;
SGA 1, Exp. VIII, Théorème 1.1, for faithfully flat quasi-compact morphisms). Route: Tau Ceti's
`RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.faithfullyFlat`, applied as in 2.1, makes `A` faithfully flat over `A^R`,
and Mathlib's `comonadicExtendScalars` with `RingHom.faithfullyFlat_algebraMap_iff` gives
comonadicity; equivalently 4.2, since faithfully flat maps are pure. *Needs:* 1.1, 2.1, 4.1, 4.2,
Tau Ceti `RingHom.IsSpecEquivRel.faithfullyFlat`, Mathlib `Algebra.IsEffective`,
`Algebra.codRestrictEqLocusPushoutCocone.surjective_of_isEffective`, `comonadicExtendScalars`,
`RingHom.faithfullyFlat_algebraMap_iff`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

**Checks.**

- `I = ker μ`: `A^R = A`, `A ⊗[A] A = A`, and both statements are trivial
  (`Algebra.IsEffective.of_section` for the inverse of `A^R ≅ A`). `I = ⊥` with `k` a field and
  `A ≠ 0`: `A^R = k`, and `k → A` is faithfully flat, so modules descend
  (`Algebra.IsEffective.of_faithfullyFlat`).
- The Amitsur condition for `A^R` alone does not give purity. For `C = k[x, y] ⧸ (xy)` and its
  normalisation `A = k[s] × k[t]`, `x ↦ (s, 0)`, `y ↦ (0, t)`, take for `I` the ideal of
  `A ⊗[C] A` in `A ⊗[k] A`: `k[s] ⊗[C] k[t] = k`, so the equalizer of `A ⇉ A ⊗[C] A` is
  `{(f, g) | f(0) = g(0)} = C` and `A^R = C`. But `C → A` is not pure:
  `C ⧸ (x + y) = k[x] ⧸ (x²)`, of dimension `2`, maps to `A ⧸ (x + y)A = k × k` with `x ↦ 0`.
- Purity without flatness on a quotient: for `char k ≠ 2`, the relation `x ~ -x` on `𝔸²_k`,
  `I = (x₁ - y₁, x₂ - y₂) ∩ (x₁ + y₁, x₂ + y₂)`, has `A^R = k[x₁², x₁x₂, x₂²]` (`a ⊗ 1 - 1 ⊗ a`
  lies in the second ideal iff `a(x) = a(-x)`). The Reynolds operator `a ↦ (a(x) + a(-x)) ⁄ 2`
  is an `A^R`-linear retraction, so `A^R → A` is pure (Stacks, Example 35.4.6, Tag 08WM) and 4.2
  applies; `A` is not flat over `A^R`, since `A^R` is not regular at the origin while `k[x₁, x₂]`
  is (Stacks, Lemma 10.110.9, Tag 00OF).

### 4.4 Universal effective epimorphisms of affine schemes

Prove `universally_effectiveEpi_iff`: for a map of commutative rings `A → B`,
`Spec B → Spec A` is a universal effective epimorphism in the category
`AlgebraicGeometry.AffineScheme` (every base change along a morphism of affine schemes is a
`CategoryTheory.EffectiveEpi`, Mathlib's `MorphismProperty.universally`) iff `A → B` is pure.
So, by 4.2, along a universal effective epimorphism of affine schemes base change of modules is
fully faithful and descent of modules is effective, and conversely. Route: by Stacks,
Lemma 35.13.2 (Tag 0EUA), for the family with one member, `Spec B → Spec A` is a universal
effective epimorphism of affine schemes iff, for every `A`-module `M`, `M` is the equalizer of
`B ⊗[A] M ⇉ (B ⊗[A] B) ⊗[A] M`, that is `IsAmitsurEqualizer A B M`; then 4.2. Effectivity of
the base change along `Spec C → Spec A` alone is `Algebra.IsEffective C (C ⊗[A] B)` with
`C → C ⊗[A] B` injective, the case `M = C` of the Amitsur condition over the new base, so
universality is what brings in every module. *Needs:* 4.1, 4.2, Mathlib `Algebra.IsEffective`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.AffineScheme.Spec`, `CategoryTheory.EffectiveEpi`,
`CategoryTheory.MorphismProperty.universally`, `CommRingCat.ofHom`.

**Checks.**

- `ℤ → ℚ` is an epimorphism of rings, so `Spec ℚ → Spec ℤ` is a monomorphism of affine schemes;
  its kernel pair has equal projections, so it would be an isomorphism if it were an effective
  epimorphism. It is not one, in accordance with `ℤ → ℚ` not being pure (4.2). A definition by
  flatness and injectivity of `A → B` would accept it.
- `ℤ → ℤ × ℤ/2` is split, hence pure, and not flat (4.2): `Spec (ℤ × ℤ/2) → Spec ℤ` is a
  universal effective epimorphism of affine schemes without being an fpqc covering. A definition
  by faithful flatness would reject it.
- Universality is needed: for `C = k[x, y] ⧸ (xy) → A = k[s] × k[t]` of 4.3, `C` is the
  equalizer of `A ⇉ A ⊗[C] A`, so `Spec A → Spec C` is an effective epimorphism of affine
  schemes; its base change along `C → C ⧸ (x + y) = k[x] ⧸ (x²)` is `k[x] ⧸ (x²) → k × k`,
  `x ↦ 0`, which is not injective, so the base change is not even an epimorphism of affine
  schemes.

### Examples

- Finite products: for `f₁, …, fₙ` generating the unit ideal, `A → A_{f₁} × ⋯ × A_{fₙ}` is pure
  (Stacks, Example 35.4.7, Tag 08WN), and 4.2 is Zariski descent of modules.
- Kernel pairs of split subalgebras `C ⊆ A`, such as invariants of a finite group of invertible
  order: `C → A` is pure and modules descend along `Spec A → Spec C`; when the retraction is a
  map of `C`-algebras, `Algebra.IsEffective C A` is `Algebra.IsEffective.of_section`.
  Conversely, for `C` noetherian and `A` a finite `C`-module, `A ⧸ C` is finitely presented, so
  `C → A` is pure only if it is split as a map of `C`-modules (Stacks, Lemma 10.82.4, Tag 058L).

## Research context

Grothendieck asked the question in the third of his Bourbaki talks on the construction of schemes:
without flatness he did not know whether equivalence relations in the category of finite schemes
over `S` are effective, reduced it to `S` the spectrum of a local artinian ring with algebraically
closed residue field, and said that it was open even when `S` is the spectrum of a field (FGA III,
§2, p. 212-05). Kollár asked the existence of geometric quotients by finite equivalence relations in
characteristic zero (Kollár 2012, Question 8) and proved it in positive characteristic for schemes
essentially of finite type (Theorem 6); Raicu's appendix answers the effectivity question on `𝔸²`
and Raicu 2010 proves the toric case. Grothendieck also asked whether descent data on flat
quasi-coherent sheaves along a finite strict epimorphism are effective, even over a local artinian
ring (FGA I, §A, n° 2, c); descent of flat modules along finite morphisms of local artinian rings
need not be effective (Venken 1971), and Philippe 1973 studies descent along unramified
morphisms. Without flatness, descent of all modules along a ring map is effective exactly when the
map is pure: announced by Olivier, proved by Joyal and Tierney in unpublished work, with a first
published proof by Mesablishvili (Mesablishvili 2000; Stacks, Section 35.4). Being finitely
generated, flat or finite projective descends along pure maps (Mesablishvili 2002, Theorem 3.2;
Stacks, Theorem 35.4.25, Tag 08XD), so pure maps are effective descent morphisms for these
modules too (Mesablishvili 2002, Theorem 1.1), and a ring map along which descent of flat modules
is not effective is not pure. For quasi-compact morphisms of schemes, stable effective descent of
quasi-coherent modules is equivalent to purity (Mesablishvili 2004a), and purity to being a
stable regular epimorphism (Mesablishvili 2004b).

Whether a non-effective closed equivalence relation exists on a finite scheme of length less
than `15`, and whether every non-effective example comes, as in 3.2, from restricting one on an
affine space, is not known.
