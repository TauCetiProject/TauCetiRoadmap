# Roadmap: stable, periodic, and curved homological algebra

**Summary.** Three constructions turn exact or differential data into triangulated categories. The
*stable category* of a Frobenius exact category, one with enough projectives and enough injectives
and the two classes equal, is its quotient by the maps factoring through a projective-injective
object; Happel's theorem makes it triangulated. A *periodic complex* is a complex indexed by `ℤ/n`
with `n ≥ 1`; its homotopy category `K_n` and derived category `D_n` are triangulated. A *curved DG
module* over a curved DG algebra `(A, d, w)` satisfies `d²(m) = m·w` instead of `d² = 0`; it has no
homology, and its *derived categories of the second kind* are Verdier quotients of the curved
homotopy category by classes generated by totalizations of short exact sequences. The three meet in
matrix factorizations of an element `w` of a commutative ring `S`: pairs of maps `P₀ → P₁ → P₀` of
finitely generated projective modules whose two composites are multiplication by `w`. The roadmap
keeps these quotients apart and compares them only by explicit functors under named hypotheses.

If `R` is left and right noetherian with finite self-injective dimension on both sides, placing a
module in degree zero is a triangle equivalence from the stable category of finitely generated
Gorenstein-projective right `R`-modules to the singularity category `D^b(mod-R)/K^b(proj-R)`
(Buchweitz). If `Λ` is a finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension and `n ≥ 1`, folding
complexes modulo `n` gives a fully faithful functor from the orbit category `D^b(mod-Λ)/[n]` to
`D_n(mod-Λ)` whose image generates `D_n(mod-Λ)` under shifts and cones (Stai). If the graded ring
underlying a curved DG algebra has finite right global dimension, its absolutely acyclic, coacyclic
and contraacyclic right modules coincide (Positselski). If `S` is regular local and `w ≠ 0` lies in
its maximal ideal, taking cokernels is an equivalence from the homotopy category of matrix
factorizations of `w` to the stable category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay `S/(w)`-modules (Eisenbud).
The flagship calculation: over any field `k`, for `A = k[x]/(x^n)` the stable morphisms from
`A/(x^i)` to `A/(x^j)` form a space of dimension `min(i, j, n - i, n - j)`, and the maps `x^i`,
`x^(n-i)` are at once the periodic resolution of `A/(x^i)` and a matrix factorization of `x^n`
over `k[x]`.

Exact structures come from GrothendieckEulerForms, and the grading, sign and DG conventions from
DGAInfinity, whose objects are uncurved. HopfologicalAlgebra builds its relative hopfological
categories on the stable quotients and Happel's triangulation here; McKaySkewGroup and FukayaTheory
use the morphism-ideal quotients and the curved module categories.

Layer 0 fixes morphism ideals, Frobenius exact categories and Frobenius algebras, and Layer 1 the
triangulated stable category. Layer 2 treats Gorenstein-projective modules and singularity
categories, with Buchweitz's and Keller–Vossieck's comparisons. Layers 3 and 4 build periodic
complexes, compression, the orbit category, Stai's hull and periodic resolutions; Layers 5 and 6
curved DG algebras and modules and the derived categories of the second kind; Layer 7 matrix
factorizations with Eisenbud's and Orlov's comparisons; Layer 8 the comparison functors and the
worked examples.

for module categories. Layer 3 is independent of Layer 2 except through Layer 1's triangulated

## Conventions

These choices are part of the specification.

1. **Modules.** Right modules are primary. A right `A`-module is a Mathlib `Module Aᵐᵒᵖ M`, so the
   category of finitely generated right `A`-modules is `FGModuleCat Aᵐᵒᵖ`; a left `A`-module is
   treated as a right `Aᵐᵒᵖ`-module. Every module theorem says whether it concerns all modules,
   finitely generated modules or finite-dimensional modules. Bounded derived and singularity
   categories in the comparison theorems use finitely generated right modules; infinite
   coproducts in coderived categories and infinite products in contraderived categories use the
   category of all modules.
2. **Exact categories.** An exact category is an additive category with a Quillen exact structure,
   `TauCeti.ExactStructure`, whose conflations are short complexes; inflations, deflations and
   conflations are those of GrothendieckEulerForms §0.2, and conflation-exact functors those of
   GrothendieckEulerForms §0.4. `TauCeti.ExactStructure.abelian C` is the exact structure of all
   short exact sequences of an abelian category and `TauCeti.ExactStructure.split C` that of the
   split ones.
3. **Grading and signs.** Gradings are cohomological and by `ℤ`; differentials have degree `+1`;
   parity is reduction modulo two. Moving degree-`q` data past a homogeneous element of degree `p`
   contributes `(-1)^(p*q)`. The shift is `X[1]^p = X^(p+1)` with `d_{X[1]} = -d_X`; for a duplex
   the shift swaps the even and odd pieces and negates both differentials. Composition is
   Mathlib's: `f ≫ g` is `f` followed by `g`.
4. **Homotopies.** For an odd map `h`, its boundary is `d_N h + h d_M`; for a homogeneous map `f`
   of degree `r`, `δ(f) = d_N f - (-1)^r f d_M`.
5. **Curvature.** The stored element of a curved DG algebra is the *right-module curvature* `w`:
   `d_A²(a) = a*w - w*a`, `d_A(w) = 0`, and a right curved module satisfies `d_M²(m) = m*w`.
   Positselski's curvature `h` satisfies `d_A²(a) = h*a - a*h`, `d²(m) = h*m` on left modules and
   `d²(m) = -m*h` on right modules, so `w = -h`. The sign is load-bearing: storing `h` while using
   the right-module equation `d_M² = m*w` is inconsistent. A change of connection by a degree-one
   `a`, `d^a = d + [a, -]`, has curvature `w^a = w - d(a) - a²`. Strict morphisms preserve `d` and
   `w`; change-of-connection morphisms are a separate bundled notion with these equations and are
   not coerced to strict morphisms.
6. **Graded opposite.** For homogeneous `a, b`, `op(a) * op(b) = (-1)^(|a||b|) op(b*a)` and
   `d_op(op(a)) = op(d_A(a))`. With the right-curvature convention, the graded opposite has
   curvature `-op(w)`.
7. **Central potentials.** If `w` is graded-central of degree `2`, then `d_A² = 0`; if moreover
   `d_A = 0`, right curved modules satisfy `d_M²(m) = m*w`. In parity grading `w` is even.
8. **Matrix factorizations.** For a commutative ring `S` and `w ∈ S`, a matrix factorization is a
   pair of maps `P₀ → P₁ → P₀` whose two composites are multiplication by `w`.
9. **Universes and size.** A generic exact category has objects in `Type u` and morphisms in
   `Type v`; its stable quotients keep the same objects and hom universes. No smallness is imposed
   on module categories. A construction that needs a small localization, a set of morphisms or a
   Grothendieck group takes an essentially small full subcategory and uses Mathlib's small-model
   (`Shrink`) machinery; it never installs a `SmallCategory` instance on all modules. Module
   categories live in the universe of their ring: for `R : Type u`, finitely generated right
   modules, complete resolutions, matrix-factorization components and global-dimension
   quantifiers range over `ModuleCat.{u} Rᵐᵒᵖ`, `FGModuleCat.{u} Rᵐᵒᵖ` or `ModuleCat.{u} S`, so that
   the regular module and its finite free powers are objects of the category quantified over; a
   fixed small module universe under an unrestricted ring universe tests the wrong category. The
   small coproducts and products of Layer 6 are indexed by types in the hom universe of the
   category being closed.
10. **Idempotent completion is visible.** A functor is stated as an equivalence before idempotent
    completion only when the cited theorem and its hypotheses give that equivalence; otherwise the
    target is an equivalence after Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi`, together with
    the comparison functor before completion.
11. **Quotients that stay distinct.** The following constructions are different, and no two of
    them are made definitionally equal; they are reconciled only by the functors of Layer 8.

    | Construction | Objects | Maps killed or inverted | When triangulated |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Stable additive quotient `E/[P]` | objects of `E` | maps factoring through projective-injectives | `E` Frobenius exact, with Happel's triangles (§1.1) |
    | Homotopy category `K(E)` or `K_n(E)` | complexes or periodic complexes | null-homotopic maps | by shifts and cones; also the stable category of the degreewise-split structure (§3.3) |
    | Periodic derived category `D_n(A)` | `n`-periodic complexes | quasi-isomorphisms, equivalently the Verdier quotient of `K_n` by the acyclics | under the hypotheses of §3.4 |
    | Singularity category `D_sg(R)` | bounded complexes of finitely generated modules | Verdier quotient by the perfect complexes | as a Verdier quotient (§2.4) |
    | Absolute, co- and contraderived curved categories | curved DG modules | Verdier quotient by the corresponding second-kind acyclic class | from the curved homotopy category (§6.3) |
    | Hopfological quotient | modules over a module algebra | the hopfological null-system of HopfologicalAlgebra §4.1 | only by HopfologicalAlgebra §4.2 |

    A stable ideal quotient is never called a Verdier quotient, and a hopfological quotient is not
    identified with any quotient here (a projective-injective ideal, an acyclic Verdier kernel or
    a curved acyclic class) unless a comparison theorem proves it under named hypotheses.
12. **Acyclic and contractible.** "Acyclic" means zero homology, and is used only where `d² = 0`
    and kernels and images exist. "Contractible" means that the identity is null-homotopic. Every
    contractible square-zero complex is acyclic, and the converse fails (§3.3). A curved module
    with `w ≠ 0` has no homology in general, so a curved module is never called acyclic by
    homology, and quasi-isomorphism is not its primitive weak equivalence.

## Layer 0: additive ideals and Frobenius exact categories

### 0.1 Morphism ideals and the additive quotient

A two-sided ideal of a preadditive category `C` is a `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal C`: an additive
subgroup `I(X, Y)` of each hom group, stable under composition with arbitrary maps on both sides.
Its congruence relates `f` and `g` when `f - g ∈ I(X, Y)`, and the quotient `I.Quotient` is
Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Quotient` by that congruence, with the objects of `C`. The facts used
later:

- the quotient is preadditive, and the quotient functor is additive, full and essentially
  surjective; its hom group from `X` to `Y` is the quotient group `(X ⟶ Y)/I(X, Y)`
  (`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.homAddEquiv`);
- an additive functor `F` factors through the quotient exactly when it kills `I`
  (`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.exists_quotientFunctor_comp_eq_iff`); the factorization
  `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.lift` is additive and unique, and natural transformations descend
  uniquely (Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Quotient.natTransLift` and `CategoryTheory.Quotient.lift_unique`);
- an additive functor carrying `I` into `J` induces `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.map` between the
  quotients, compatibly with identities, composition and natural transformations, and an
  equivalence carrying `I` onto `J` induces an equivalence `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.mapEquivalence`;
- the opposite of the quotient is the quotient of the opposite category by the opposite ideal
  (`TauCeti.MorphismIdeal.opQuotientEquivalence`), and for an `R`-linear `C` the quotient and the
  quotient functor are `R`-linear.

For an arbitrary ideal of an arbitrary additive category the quotient is only additive; no
triangulation is attached to it at this level.
*Needs:* Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Quotient`, `CategoryTheory.Quotient.preadditive`,
`CategoryTheory.Quotient.linear`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MorphismIdeal`.

### 0.2 The ideal of maps factoring through a class of objects

For a class `P` of objects of `C`, `CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.factorIdeal P` is the ideal
additively generated by the maps `X → Q → Y` with `P(Q)`. It is defined as an additive closure,
so no closure of `P` under biproducts is needed to form the quotient. If `P` is nonempty and
closed under binary biproducts, in particular if it contains a zero object and is closed under
finite biproducts and isomorphisms, every element of the generated ideal itself factors through
one `P`-object (`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.factorsThrough_of_mem_factorIdeal`). An ideal
contains `factorIdeal P` exactly when it contains every identity of a `P`-object
(`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.factorIdeal_le_iff`).
*Needs:* §0.1; Tau Ceti `CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.factorIdeal`.

**Checks.**
- In finite-dimensional vector spaces with `P` the one-dimensional spaces, the identity of `k²` is
  the sum of the two coordinate projections, each factoring through `k`, but it factors through no
  single `P`-object, since a map through `k` has rank at most one. So the maps factoring through a
  `P`-object do not form a subgroup when `P` is not closed under biproducts, and the generated
  ideal is strictly larger: here it contains every map.

### 0.3 Relative projectives, Frobenius exact categories and the stable category

For an exact structure `E` on `C`, an object is *relatively projective*,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.isProjective E`, when every map into the target of a deflation lifts along
it, and *relatively injective*, `TauCeti.ExactStructure.isInjective E`, when every map out of the
source of an inflation extends along it. `E` has *enough projectives*
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.EnoughProjectives`) when every object is the target of a deflation from a
relatively projective object, and dually *enough injectives*. A *Frobenius exact category* is one
with enough projectives, enough injectives, and the same relatively projective and relatively
injective objects: `TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius E` (Bühler, §§7, 10–13). The
projective-injective class `TauCeti.ExactStructure.projectiveInjective E` is closed under
isomorphisms, finite biproducts and retracts.

The *stable category* is the additive quotient
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.ProjectiveStableCategory E` by the ideal of maps factoring through a
relatively projective object; for `E` Frobenius this is the ideal of maps factoring through a
projective-injective (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.projectiveStableIdeal_eq_factorIdeal`),
and an object is zero in it exactly when it is projective-injective. This layer attaches no
triangulation to the stable category; that is Layer 1, under the Frobenius hypothesis.
*Needs:* §0.2; GrothendieckEulerForms §0.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ExactStructure`.

**Checks.**
- For the split exact structure on an additive category every object is projective-injective
  (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.split_isFrobenius`), so the stable category has only zero objects.
- For `A = k[x, y]/(x, y)²` over a field, the abelian exact structure on finite-dimensional
  modules has enough projectives (free modules) and enough injectives (sums of the dual `A*`),
  but it is not Frobenius: the socle of `A` is `(x, y)/(x, y)²`, of dimension two, while an
  indecomposable injective module over a local finite-dimensional algebra has a simple socle, so
  the projective `A` is not injective. Having both enough projectives and enough injectives does
  not make an exact category Frobenius.

### 0.4 Frobenius functionals and self-injective algebras

Let `k` be a field and `A` a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra. A *Frobenius functional* is a linear
`λ : A → k` whose pairing `(a, b) ↦ λ(ab)` is nondegenerate, `LinearMap.IsFrobeniusFunctional`;
it is *symmetric* when `λ(ab) = λ(ba)`, `LinearMap.IsSymmetricFrobeniusFunctional`. A Frobenius
functional exists exactly when `A` is isomorphic, as a right module, to its dual `A⁺ = Hom_k(A, k)`
with the right action `(φ·a)(b) = φ(a*b)`, through `a ↦ (b ↦ λ(a*b))`
(`LinearMap.exists_isFrobeniusFunctional_iff`, with the explicit bijection
`TauCeti.frobeniusFunctionalEquivDualEquiv`). The Nakayama automorphism `ν` of a Frobenius
functional is pinned by `λ(a*b) = λ(b*ν(a))` (`LinearMap.IsFrobeniusFunctional.nakayamaAut`); it is
the identity exactly when `λ` is symmetric. Symmetric Frobenius algebras are a special case, not
every Frobenius algebra, and notions called "Frobenius" elsewhere (Frobenius coalgebras, Frobenius
exact categories) are not identified with this one.

A Frobenius algebra is self-injective on both sides
(`LinearMap.IsFrobeniusFunctional.moduleInjective_self`,
`LinearMap.IsFrobeniusFunctional.moduleInjective_op_self`). If `A` is self-injective on both sides,
the abelian exact structure on finitely generated `A`-modules is Frobenius
(`FGModuleCat.abelian_isFrobenius`); applied to `Aᵐᵒᵖ` it gives the statement for finitely
generated right modules, the finite-dimensional ones.
*Needs:* §0.3; Mathlib `Module.Injective`; Tau Ceti
`LinearMap.IsFrobeniusFunctional`, `FGModuleCat.abelian_isFrobenius`.

**Checks.**
- For `A = k[x]/(x^n)` with `n ≥ 2`, the coefficient of `x^(n-1)` is a symmetric Frobenius
  functional over every field: on the basis `1, x, …, x^(n-1)` the pairing is `1` exactly when the
  exponents sum to `n - 1`, an antidiagonal matrix. The constant coefficient is not a Frobenius
  functional: it vanishes on `x·b` for every `b`.
- For the exterior algebra on `x, y` over a field of characteristic other than two, the
  coefficient of `xy` is a Frobenius functional that is not symmetric, since `λ(xy) = 1` and
  `λ(yx) = -1`; its Nakayama automorphism is `ν(x) = -x`, `ν(y) = -y`. For instance
  `λ(x*y) = 1 = λ(y*ν(x))`.

### Examples

- The coefficient of `x^(n-1)` on `k[x]/(x^n)` is a symmetric Frobenius functional
  (`AdjoinRoot.isSymmetricFrobeniusFunctional_lastCoeff_X_pow`), so the finitely generated right
  modules over `k[x]/(x^n)` form a Frobenius exact category; §8.2 computes its stable category.
- The symmetric Frobenius traces on zigzag algebras of ZigzagPreprojective §2.1 give further
  symmetric Frobenius algebras to which this layer applies.

### Dependencies

Mathlib: preadditive and linear quotient categories, injective modules, nondegenerate bilinear
forms. Tau Ceti: morphism ideals and their quotients, the factoring ideal, exact structures and
relative projectives and injectives, Frobenius exact structures and their stable categories,
Frobenius functionals and the module-category Frobenius theorem. GrothendieckEulerForms §§0.2 and
0.4 for the exact-structure vocabulary.

## Layer 1: the triangulated stable category

### 1.1 Suspension, loops and Happel's triangulation

Let `E` be a Frobenius exact category. Choosing for each `X` a conflation `X ↪ I_X ↠ ΣX` with
`I_X` projective-injective, and dually `ΩX ↪ P_X ↠ X`, the pushout and pullback constructions
descend to additive functors `TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableSuspension` and
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.EnoughProjectives.stableLoop` on the stable category, which are
quasi-inverse autoequivalences (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableSuspensionEquivalence`);
other choices give canonically isomorphic functors
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.suspensionToStableOfPresentationsIso`). The suspension generates a
`ℤ`-shift (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableHasShift`, with
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableShiftFunctorOneIso`). A conflation `X ↪ Y ↠ Z` gives the
standard triangle `X → Y → Z → ΣX` (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableConflationTriangle_mem`),
and with these triangles the stable category is pretriangulated and triangulated in Mathlib's sense
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stablePretriangulated`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableIsTriangulated`): this is Happel's theorem (Happel,
Chapter I §2, Theorem I.2.6). The hypothesis is the full Frobenius condition; enough projectives
alone, enough injectives alone, or an additive quotient alone do not suffice.
*Needs:* §0.3; Mathlib `Pretriangulated`, `IsTriangulated`, `HasShift`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableIsTriangulated`.

**Checks.**
- For `A = k[x]/(x^n)` and `1 ≤ i < n`, the conflation `M_(n-i) ↪ A ↠ M_i` with `M_j = A/(x^j)`
  (the first map multiplication by `x^i`) has projective middle term, so its triangle
  `M_(n-i) → 0 → M_i → Σ M_(n-i)` exhibits `M_i ≅ Σ M_(n-i)` in the stable category.
- For finitely generated abelian groups with the abelian exact structure there are enough
  projectives but `Ω` is not an equivalence of the quotient by maps through projectives: the
  conflation `2ℤ ↪ ℤ ↠ ℤ/2` gives `Ω(ℤ/2) ≅ ℤ`, which is zero in the quotient, while `ℤ/2` is not
  (every map from `ℤ/2` to a free group is zero). Without the Frobenius condition there is no
  triangulation with shift `Ω⁻¹`.

### 1.2 Stable functors

An additive functor `F` between Frobenius exact categories that is conflation-exact and sends
projective-injectives to projective-injectives (`TauCeti.StableConflationExact`) descends to an
additive functor of stable categories `TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableFunctor`. Its suspension
comparison, built from the chosen conflations, makes it commute with the shifts
(`TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableFunctorCommShift`), and it is a triangle functor
(`TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableFunctorIsTriangulated`). Natural transformations descend
(`TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableNatTrans`, commuting with the shifts), and an exact
equivalence descends to a triangle equivalence
(`TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.StableConflationExact.stableEquivalenceIsTriangulated`). For an extension-closed full
subcategory `P` of a Frobenius exact category that contains the projective-injectives and the
kernels of deflations and cokernels of inflations between its objects with projective-injective
middle term (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobeniusSubcategory`), the inherited exact structure is
Frobenius and the inclusion descends to a faithful triangle functor of stable categories. More
generally, the inclusion of any extension-closed full subcategory whose inherited exact structure
is Frobenius, and whose projective-injectives are projective-injective in the ambient category, is
conflation-exact and preserves projective-injectives, so it descends to a triangle functor by the
above (Happel, Chapter I §2).
*Needs:* §1.1; GrothendieckEulerForms §0.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.StableConflationExact`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobeniusSubcategory`.

### 1.3 Sums, opposites and idempotent completion

The stable category of a product of Frobenius exact categories is the product of the stable
categories as a triangulated category (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.projectiveStableProdEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.stableProdIsTriangulated`), and direct sums of distinguished triangles are
distinguished (Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Pretriangulated.binaryBiproductTriangle_distinguished`).
The stable category of the opposite exact structure is the opposite triangulated category
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.projectiveStableOpEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.projectiveStableOpEquivalenceIsTriangulated`).

Prove the idempotent-completion comparison. For a Frobenius exact structure `E` on `C`, the
exact structure `TauCeti.ExactStructure.karoubi E` on Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi C`,
whose conflations are the retracts of images of conflations of `E`, is Frobenius, its
projective-injectives being the retracts of projective-injectives of `E`. The inclusion
`toKaroubi`, which is conflation-exact (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.isConflationExact_toKaroubi`) and
preserves projective-injectives, descends by §1.2 to a triangle functor
`Stable(E) → Stable(karoubi E)`; prove that it is fully faithful and that every object of
`Stable(karoubi E)` is a direct summand of an object in its image. The comparison is stated in
this form, not as an equivalence: by Convention 10 the stable category of `E` need not be
idempotent complete.
*Needs:* §§1.1–1.2; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.karoubi`, `TauCeti.ExactStructure.isConflationExact_toKaroubi`;
Bühler, §6, Proposition 6.13, for the exact structure on the idempotent completion.

**Checks.**
- If `C` is idempotent complete, for instance finitely generated modules over a finite-dimensional
  self-injective algebra, `toKaroubi` is an equivalence of exact categories, so the comparison is a
  triangle equivalence.

### Examples

- For a finite-dimensional algebra self-injective on both sides, the stable category of finitely
  generated right modules is triangulated with shift the cosyzygy (§2.1).
- The split exact structures on complexes, periodic complexes, curved duplexes, curved modules and
  matrix factorizations are Frobenius, and their stable categories are the corresponding homotopy
  categories (§§3.3, 6.1, 7.3), triangulated by §1.1.

Layer 0. Mathlib: shifts, pretriangulated and triangulated categories, triangle functors,

stable functors, Frobenius subcategories, products and opposites, the exact structure on the

## Layer 2: self-injective modules, total acyclicity, and singularities

### 2.1 The stable module category

Let `A` be a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra, self-injective on both sides. With the abelian exact
structure, finitely generated right `A`-modules form a Frobenius exact category whose
projective-injectives are exactly the projective modules (§0.4; Tau Ceti
`FGModuleCat.projective_iff_injective_of_moduleInjective_self`). Its stable category is
`stmod-A`, `TauCeti.FGModuleCat.stableModuleCategory A`, triangulated by §1.1. Every
finite-dimensional Frobenius algebra satisfies these hypotheses. `stmod-A` is kept distinct from
the stable category `StMod-A` of all modules (Happel, Chapter I §2, for the stable category of a
Frobenius category).
*Needs:* §§0.4, 1.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.FGModuleCat.stableModuleCategory`,
`FGModuleCat.abelian_isFrobenius`.

**Checks.**
- For `A = k[x]/(x²)` the indecomposable modules are `k` and `A`; `A` is zero in `stmod-A`, while
  the identity of `k` does not factor through a projective, every map `k → A` landing in `(x)` and
  every map `A → k` killing `(x)`. So `stmod-A` has one indecomposable object up to isomorphism,
  with endomorphism ring `k`, and `Ωk ≅ k`.

### 2.2 Totally acyclic complexes and Gorenstein-projective modules

A complex `P` of right `R`-modules is *totally acyclic*, `CochainComplex.IsTotallyAcyclic`, when
every term is finitely generated and projective, `P` is acyclic, and `Hom_R(P, R)` is acyclic.
Finite generation is a condition on every term, not a consequence of finite generation of one
cycle. A module is *Gorenstein-projective*, `TauCeti.IsGorensteinProjective`, when it is the
degree-zero cycle module of a totally acyclic complex, its *complete resolution*; two complete
resolutions of the same module are homotopy equivalent
(`CochainComplex.IsTotallyAcyclic.homotopyEquiv`), so nothing depends on the choice. For finitely
generated modules over a noetherian ring, the description in the abelian category of finitely
generated modules, `CategoryTheory.isGorensteinProjective`, agrees with this one (Tau Ceti
`FGModuleCat.isGorensteinProjective_iff`). These definitions need no noetherian hypothesis; the
theorems below state the noetherian hypotheses they use. Buchweitz's complete resolutions
(Buchweitz, Definition 3.1.1) are acyclic complexes of finitely generated projectives with no
condition on `Hom_R(P, R)`; over an Iwanaga–Gorenstein ring (§2.4) of self-injective dimension
`d` that condition holds automatically, since every cycle module of such a complex is a `d`-th
syzygy and so has `Ext^i(-, R) = 0` for `i ≥ 1`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `HomologicalComplex.Acyclic`, `Module.Projective`, `Module.Finite`; Tau Ceti
`CochainComplex.IsTotallyAcyclic`, `TauCeti.IsGorensteinProjective`.

**Checks.**
- Adding to a complete resolution a contractible two-term complex `F → F` (the identity) on an
  infinitely generated free module, away from degree zero, keeps the complex acyclic, keeps
  `Hom_R(-, R)` acyclic (the dual is again a contractible two-term complex) and leaves the
  degree-zero cycle module unchanged. A definition requiring finite generation only of the cycle
  module would accept the result, which is not a complex of finitely generated modules.
- Over `ℤ`, the module `ℤ/2` is not Gorenstein-projective: a cycle module of a complex of
  projectives is a submodule of a projective module, hence torsion free.

### 2.3 The Frobenius category of Gorenstein-projectives

Finitely generated Gorenstein-projective right modules, with the exact structure inherited from
all modules, form a Frobenius exact category whose projective-injectives are exactly the finitely
generated projective modules (`TauCeti.GorensteinProjectiveModuleCat`,
`TauCeti.GorensteinProjectiveModuleCat.exactStructure_isFrobenius`,
`TauCeti.GorensteinProjectiveModuleCat.exactStructure_isProjective_iff`); its stable category is
`stable(Gproj-R)`. Taking degree-zero cycles is an equivalence from the homotopy category of
totally acyclic complexes to `stable(Gproj-R)`
(`TauCeti.TotallyAcyclicComplex.stableCyclesEquivalence`).

Prove that this equivalence is a triangle equivalence: the homotopy category of totally acyclic
complexes is a triangulated subcategory of the homotopy category of all complexes of modules,
closed under shifts and mapping cones, and the cycle functor commutes with the shifts (the
cosyzygy on the stable side) and sends mapping-cone triangles to distinguished triangles. Give the
quasi-inverse explicitly as the *complete-resolution functor*: a module goes to a chosen complete
resolution, and a stable map to the homotopy class of the chain map it induces between complete
resolutions (Buchweitz, §4; for a general Frobenius category, Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.completeResolutionFunctor`).

Prove that the two descriptions of `stable(Gproj-R)` agree: for a left noetherian ring `A`, the
inclusion of finitely generated modules induces a triangle equivalence between the stable category
of `TauCeti.GorensteinProjectiveModuleCat A` and the stable category of the Frobenius exact
category `CategoryTheory.GorensteinProjective.exactStructure (FGModuleCat A)` of
Gorenstein-projective objects of the abelian category of finitely generated `A`-modules, the two
having the same objects up to isomorphism by `FGModuleCat.isGorensteinProjective_iff`. Right
modules over a right noetherian `R` are the case `A = Rᵐᵒᵖ`, and modules over the commutative
hypersurface `S/(w)` of §7.5 the case `A = S/(w)`. Sections 2.5, 2.6 and 7.5 pass between the two
through this equivalence.
*Needs:* §§1.1–1.2, 2.2; Mathlib `HomotopyCategory`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.TotallyAcyclicComplex.stableCyclesEquivalence`,
`CategoryTheory.GorensteinProjective.exactStructure`, `FGModuleCat.isGorensteinProjective_iff`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.IsFrobenius.stableEquivCompleteResolutionHomotopyCategory`.

**Checks.**
- `R = ℤ/4` is self-injective, so every finitely generated module is Gorenstein-projective. The
  stable category has one indecomposable object `ℤ/2`, with `Ω(ℤ/2) ≅ ℤ/2` from
  `2ℤ/4 ↪ ℤ/4 ↠ ℤ/2`, and stable endomorphism ring `𝔽₂`: the composite `ℤ/2 → ℤ/4 → ℤ/2` of any
  two maps is zero. Its complete resolution is the two-sided complex with every term `ℤ/4` and
  every differential multiplication by `2`.

### 2.4 Iwanaga–Gorenstein rings and the singularity category

A ring `R` is *Iwanaga–Gorenstein*, `TauCeti.IsIwanagaGorenstein R`, when it is left and right
noetherian and its regular module has finite injective dimension both as a left and as a right
module. For an abelian category `C`, the singularity category `TauCeti.SingularityCategory C` is
the Verdier quotient of Mathlib's bounded derived category `DerivedCategory.Bounded C` by the
perfect objects `TauCeti.DerivedCategory.Bounded.perfect C`, the thick closure of the projective
objects placed in degree zero; with enough projectives these are the bounded complexes of
projectives (`DerivedCategory.Bounded.perfect_iff`). For a right noetherian `R`,
`D_sg(R) = D^b(mod-R)/K^b(proj-R)` is `TauCeti.SingularityCategory (FGModuleCat Rᵐᵒᵖ)`, triangulated
as a Verdier quotient (Buchweitz, Definition 1.2.2).
*Needs:* Mathlib `DerivedCategory.Bounded`, `ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope`, `ObjectProperty.trW`,
`HasInjectiveDimensionLE`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsIwanagaGorenstein`, `TauCeti.SingularityCategory`.

**Checks.**
- For `R = ℤ/4`, the module `ℤ/2` has infinite projective dimension (its minimal resolution has
  every term `ℤ/4` and every map multiplication by `2`), so it is not perfect and is nonzero in
  `D_sg(ℤ/4)`. A quotient by all bounded complexes, instead of the perfect ones, would be zero.

### 2.5 Buchweitz's theorem

For an abelian category `C`, placing a Gorenstein-projective object in degree zero induces an
additive, fully faithful functor from the stable category of Gorenstein-projective objects to the
singularity category (`CategoryTheory.GorensteinProjective.toSingularityCategory`,
`CategoryTheory.GorensteinProjective.fullyFaithfulToSingularityCategory`).

Prove `buchweitz_isEquivalence`: if `R` is Iwanaga–Gorenstein, this functor for
`C = FGModuleCat Rᵐᵒᵖ` is an equivalence (Buchweitz, §4 and Theorem 4.4.1). Prove moreover that it
commutes with the shifts and is a triangle functor, so that `stable(Gproj-R) ≃ D_sg(R)` is a
triangle equivalence, and give its quasi-inverse through complete resolutions: an object of
`D_sg(R)` is isomorphic to a shift of a high syzygy `M` of a bounded complex, which is
Gorenstein-projective, and goes to `M`. Every hypothesis (left and right noetherian, finite
self-injective dimension on both sides) is part of the statement, not of an ambient assumption.
*Route.* Every object of the singularity category is a shift of a module `M` with
`Ext^(i+1)(M, Y) = 0` for all `Y` of injective dimension at most a fixed `d` (Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.SingularityCategory.exists_iso_Q_obj_singleFunctor_obj`); over an Iwanaga–Gorenstein ring
these modules are exactly the Gorenstein-projective ones, which gives essential surjectivity.
*Needs:* §§2.2–2.4; Tau Ceti `CategoryTheory.GorensteinProjective.toSingularityCategory`,
`TauCeti.SingularityCategory.exists_iso_Q_obj_singleFunctor_obj`, `TauCeti.IsIwanagaGorenstein`.

**Checks.**
- For `R = ℤ/4`, both sides have one indecomposable object up to isomorphism, `ℤ/2`, with
  endomorphism ring `𝔽₂` (§§2.3, 2.4).
- The Iwanaga–Gorenstein hypothesis is needed. For `R = k[x, y]/(x, y)²` the only finitely
  generated Gorenstein-projectives are the free modules: a nonfree one would have a nonzero
  minimal syzygy inside `𝔪F`, killed by `𝔪`, hence a sum of copies of `k`, making `k`
  Gorenstein-projective; but `0 → 𝔪 → R → k → 0` gives `dim Ext¹(k, R) = 4 - 3 + 2 = 3`, while a
  Gorenstein-projective module has `Ext¹(-, R) = 0`. So `stable(Gproj-R)` is zero, while `k`, of
  infinite projective dimension, is nonzero in `D_sg(R)`.

### 2.6 The self-injective and finite-global-dimension cases

If `R` is a finite-dimensional algebra self-injective on both sides, every finitely generated
right module is Gorenstein-projective (`TauCeti.isGorensteinProjective_of_moduleInjective_self`).
Prove that the stable module category `stmod-R` of §2.1 is equivalent, as a triangulated category,
to the stable category of Gorenstein-projective objects of `FGModuleCat Rᵐᵒᵖ`, and hence by §2.5
to `D_sg(R)`, the composite being induced by placing a module in degree zero.

Prove that if `R` is left and right noetherian and every right `R`-module has projective dimension
at most `d` (`TauCeti.globalDimension Rᵐᵒᵖ ≤ d`), every object of `D_sg(R)` is zero: every bounded
complex of finitely generated modules is perfect. This is not a claim about the quotient of
`mod-R` by the maps factoring through projectives, which need not vanish; the self-injective
specialization and the finite-global-dimension specialization have different hypotheses
(Buchweitz, §1.1 and §8.1).
*Needs:* §§2.1, 2.4, 2.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.isGorensteinProjective_of_moduleInjective_self`,
`TauCeti.globalDimension`, `DerivedCategory.Bounded.perfect_iff`.

**Checks.**
- For `R = ℤ`, of global dimension one, `D_sg(ℤ) = 0`, while the quotient of finitely generated
  abelian groups by the maps factoring through projectives is not zero: the identity of `ℤ/2` does
  not factor through a free group.
- For `R = k[x]/(x²)`, `D_sg(R)` has one indecomposable object, the image of `k`, matching
  `stmod-R` (§2.1).

### 2.7 The Keller–Vossieck comparison

Let `E` be an exact structure on an additive category `C`. A bounded complex over `C` is
*exact-acyclic* when every differential factors as a deflation onto an object `Z^(i+1)` followed by
an inflation, through conflations `Z^i ↪ X^i ↠ Z^(i+1)`. Construct the bounded derived category
`D^b(E)` as the Verdier quotient of Mathlib's bounded homotopy category by the isomorphism closure
of the exact-acyclic complexes, after proving that this closure is a triangulated subcategory
(Keller, *Chain complexes and stable categories*, §§1–4; Bühler, §§7, 10–13). When `C` is abelian and
`E` its abelian structure, it is equivalent to `DerivedCategory.Bounded C` compatibly with the
localization functors.

For `E` Frobenius, the bounded complexes of projective-injectives form a triangulated subcategory
`K^b(ProjInj E)` of `D^b(E)`. Prove the Keller–Vossieck theorem: for `E` essentially small and
Frobenius, the functor `Stable(E) → D^b(E)/K^b(ProjInj E)` induced by placing an object in degree
zero, which kills projective-injectives, is a triangle equivalence (Keller–Vossieck, Example 2.3).
`D^b(E)` is not replaced by the bounded homotopy category: the analogous quotient of `K^b(C)` need
not be equivalent. When `C` is idempotent complete, the exact-acyclic bounded complexes are
already closed under isomorphism, and the comparisons for `E` and for `karoubi E` (§1.3) commute
up to natural isomorphism with the functors induced by `toKaroubi`.
*Needs:* §§1.1–1.3; Mathlib `HomotopyCategory`, `ObjectProperty.trW`, `Localization`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.HomotopyCategory.Bounded`, `TauCeti.ExactStructure`.

**Checks.**
- For the split exact structure on an additive category `C`, an exact-acyclic complex is
  contractible, so `D^b(split C)` is the bounded homotopy category; every object is
  projective-injective, and both sides of the comparison are zero.

### Examples

- `R = ℤ/4` and `R = k[x]/(x^n)` are self-injective, hence Iwanaga–Gorenstein with both
  self-injective dimensions zero; every finitely generated module is Gorenstein-projective, and
  `stable(Gproj-R) = stmod-R ≃ D_sg(R)`.
- For `S` regular local and `0 ≠ w ∈ 𝔪`, the hypersurface `S/(w)` is Iwanaga–Gorenstein; §7.4
  compares its stable category of Gorenstein-projectives with matrix factorizations.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–1. Mathlib: bounded derived and homotopy categories, Verdier localization, triangulated
envelopes, injective and projective dimension. Tau Ceti: the stable module category, totally
acyclic complexes and Gorenstein-projective modules, their Frobenius category and stable
equivalence, Iwanaga–Gorenstein rings, singularity categories, the functor placing
Gorenstein-projectives in degree zero, global dimension.

## Layer 3: differential modules and periodic complexes

### 3.1 Differential modules, periodic complexes and two-periodic complexes

Three related objects are kept apart.

1. A *differential module* in a category `C` with zero morphisms is an object with an endomorphism
   `d` such that `d² = 0`, `TauCeti.DifferentialModule C`; it is one-periodic. A right
   `R`-module version is the case `C = ModuleCat Rᵐᵒᵖ`.
2. For `n ≥ 1`, an *`n`-periodic complex* is a cochain complex indexed by `ℤ/n`, Mathlib's
   `HomologicalComplex C (ComplexShape.up (ZMod n))`: objects `X^i` for `i ∈ ZMod n` with
   differentials of degree one and consecutive composites zero. The period is positive by
   hypothesis: `ZMod 0` is `ℤ`, and `n = 0` would give ordinary complexes, not a finite cyclic
   grading.
3. A *genuine two-periodic complex* is the case `n = 2`: a parity-graded pair `X⁰ ⇄ X¹` with both
   composites zero.

Differential modules are the one-periodic complexes
(`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.onePeriodicComplexEquivalence`), and for a shift on `C` with a
natural identification of the one-step shift with the identity they are Mathlib's differential
objects (`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.differentialObjectEquivalence`), the shifted-square law being
derived from `d² = 0` through the naturality of that identification, not assumed. Differential
modules and two-periodic complexes are not identified: forgetting parity
(`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.forgetParity`) sends a two-periodic complex to the differential module
on `X⁰ ⊕ X¹`, and has no inverse construction. A *duplex* is the parity-graded curved object of
§5.5; its curvature-zero case is exactly a genuine two-periodic complex
(`TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.periodicComplexEquivalence`) (Avramov–Buchweitz–Iyengar, §§1–2, for
differential modules; Stai, §§3.1–3.4, for periodic complexes).
*Needs:* Mathlib `HomologicalComplex`, `ComplexShape.up`, `CategoryTheory.DifferentialObject`; Tau
Ceti `TauCeti.DifferentialModule`, `TauCeti.DifferentialModule.forgetParity`.

**Checks.**
- Over `Λ = k[t]/(t²)` with `k` a field, the differential module `(Λ, d = t)` is not isomorphic to
  the image under forgetting parity of any two-periodic complex of `Λ`-modules: `Λ` is
  indecomposable, so one of the two parity pieces would vanish, which forces `d = 0`. Forgetting
  parity is therefore not essentially surjective, and no inverse construction exists.

### 3.2 Shifts, homotopies, cones and totalizations

The shift of `n`-periodic complexes is `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.shiftFunctor`: `(X[k])^i = X^(i+k)`
with differential multiplied by `(-1)^k`, generating the `ℤ`-shift
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.instHasShift`; for `k` even and divisible by `n` it is isomorphic to the
identity (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.shiftFunctorIsoId`). Homotopies and null-homotopic maps are
Mathlib's, evaluation in a residue class is Mathlib's `HomologicalComplex.eval`, and the mapping
cone of `f : X → Y` is Mathlib's `HomologicalComplex.homotopyCofiber f`, with
`cone(f)^i = X^(i+1) ⊕ Y^i` and differential `[[-d_X, 0], [f, d_Y]]`, with its projection
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.coneProjection` onto `X[1]`. The biproducts the cone needs are hypotheses
on the ambient additive category, not added by a later theorem.

Define, for `n` even, the Hom complex of two `n`-periodic complexes: its degree-`r` piece, for
`r ∈ ZMod n`, consists of the families `X^i → Y^(i+r)`, with `δ(f) = d_Y f - (-1)^r f d_X`; prove
`δ² = 0`, that the degree-zero cycles are the chain maps and the degree-zero boundaries the
null-homotopic maps, and that for `n = 2` the two pieces are the even and the odd morphisms. For
`n` odd the sign `(-1)^r` is not defined on `ZMod n`, and only closed degree-zero maps and
homotopies are used.

Define the *totalization* of a double complex `X^(p,q)` with `p ∈ ℤ` ranging over finitely many
values and `q ∈ ZMod n`, whose rows `X^(p,•)` are `n`-periodic complexes and whose horizontal
maps `d_h : X^(p,q) → X^(p+1,q)` square to zero and commute with the row differentials `d_v`: the
`n`-periodic complex with `Tot^r = ⊕_(p + q = r) X^(p,q)` and differential `d_h + (-1)^p d_v`.
Prove that it is an
`n`-periodic complex, that totalization is an additive functor, and that in an abelian category the
totalization of a double complex with acyclic rows is acyclic (Stai, §§3.1–3.4, for periodic
complexes and their morphisms).
*Needs:* §3.1; Mathlib `HomologicalComplex.homotopyCofiber`, `Homotopy`, finite biproducts; Tau
Ceti `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.shiftFunctor`, `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.coneProjection`.

**Checks.**
- The cone of `𝟙_X` is contractible, with homotopy `h(x', y) = (y, 0)`: the differential is
  `D(x', y) = (-d x', x' + d y)`, and `D h + h D` is the identity. With the sign `[[d_X, 0], [f, d_Y]]`
  instead, `D²(x', y) = (0, 2 d x')`, and the cone would not be a complex.
- For `n` odd there is no function `ε : ZMod n → {±1}` with `ε(i + 1) = -ε(i)`, so the sign
  `(-1)^r` of the Hom differential, and the identification of `X[n]` with `X` by such signs, exist
  only for `n` even.

### 3.3 The split Frobenius structure and the periodic homotopy category

Let `A` be an additive category. The degreewise split exact structure on `n`-periodic complexes,
and on differential modules, is Frobenius
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.homologicalComplex_split_up'_isFrobenius`,
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.differentialModule_split_isFrobenius`); its projective-injectives are the
contractible complexes (`TauCeti.ExactStructure.homologicalComplex_split_isProjective_iff`); and its
stable category is the homotopy category `K_n(A) = HomotopyCategory A (ComplexShape.up (ZMod n))`,
respectively `K₁(A)` for differential modules
(`TauCeti.ExactStructure.homologicalComplexSplitStableHomotopyEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.stableHomotopyEquivalence`). `K_n(A)` carries Mathlib's preadditive
structure and zero object, the `ℤ`-shift `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCategoryHasShift`
generated by the cyclic shift, additive shift functors, and the triangulated structure
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCategoryPretriangulated`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCategoryIsTriangulated`, in which the cone triangle
`X → Y → cone(f) → X[1]` is distinguished (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.mappingCone_mem_distTriang`).
The triangulation by Happel's construction (§1.1) and by mapping cones agree: the comparison from
the stable category is a triangle equivalence (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.stableToHomotopy_isTriangulated`,
`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.stableToHomotopy_isTriangulated`) (Keller, *Chain complexes and stable
categories*, §§1–4; Stai, §§3.1–3.4).

Prove that the projective-injectives are the retracts of finite direct sums of elementary disks.
For an object `B` and `i ∈ ZMod n` with `n ≥ 2`, the disk `D_i(B)` has `B` in degrees `i` and
`i + 1`, joined by the identity, and zero elsewhere; for `n = 1` it is the differential module
`B ⊕ B` with `d(a, b) = (0, a)`. A periodic complex is contractible exactly when it is a retract
of a finite direct sum of disks.
*Needs:* §§1.1, 3.2; Mathlib `HomotopyCategory`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ExactStructure.homologicalComplex_split_isFrobenius`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCategoryIsTriangulated`.

**Checks.**
- The two-periodic complex of abelian groups with both terms `ℤ/4` and both differentials
  multiplication by `2` is acyclic (kernel and image are `2ℤ/4`), but not contractible: the two
  components of a homotopy are multiplications by some `c₀, c₁ ∈ ℤ/4`, and `d h + h d` is then
  multiplication by `2c₀ + 2c₁`, which lies in `2ℤ/4` and is never `1`. It is not projective-injective for the
  split structure, so acyclic and contractible differ.
- Every disk is contractible: for `n = 1`, `h(a, b) = (b, 0)` satisfies `d h + h d = 1`.

### 3.4 Periodic homology, quasi-isomorphisms and the periodic derived category

Let `A` be abelian. The homology of a periodic complex in each residue class is Mathlib's; the
acyclic complexes form a triangulated subcategory `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.subcategoryAcyclic` of
`K_n(A)`, and the periodic derived category `D_n(A)` is the Verdier quotient
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory A n`, with Mathlib's triangulated structure on the
localization. The localization functor from periodic complexes is a localization at the
quasi-isomorphisms, the maps with acyclic cone
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.quasiIso_eq_trW_subcategoryAcyclic`), so the two descriptions, by quasi-isomorphisms and by the Verdier quotient `K_n(A)/Ac_n(A)`,
coincide, with comparison equivalence commuting with the localization functors
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory.quasiIsoLocalizationEquivalence`). Later theorems about
`D_n` (compression, the orbit functor, the triangulated hull) are stated against this triangulated
structure. The abelian hypothesis is needed for homology: an additive base supports the
homotopy category of §3.3 but not homology objects.

Prove the exact-category version. For an additive category `A` with an exact structure `E`, a
periodic complex is *exact-acyclic* when in every residue class its differential factors as a
deflation `X^i ↠ Z^(i+1)` followed by an inflation `Z^(i+1) ↪ X^(i+1)`, through conflations
`Z^i ↪ X^i ↠ Z^(i+1)`, and a map is a *quasi-isomorphism* when its cone is exact-acyclic. Prove
that the isomorphism closure of the exact-acyclic complexes in `K_n(A)` is a triangulated
subcategory; define `D_n(E)` as the Verdier quotient of `K_n(A)` by it; prove that its localization
functor from periodic complexes is a localization at the quasi-isomorphisms, so that the two
descriptions of `D_n(E)` agree through a comparison equivalence commuting with the localization
functors; and prove that for `A` abelian with its abelian structure, `D_n(E)` agrees with `D_n(A)`
by a triangle equivalence commuting with the localization functors (Keller, *Chain complexes and
stable categories*, §§1–4, for the integer-graded case).
*Needs:* §§3.2–3.3; Mathlib `HomologicalComplex.homology`, `ObjectProperty.trW`, `Localization`;
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory`, `TauCeti.ExactStructure`.

**Checks.**
- The complex of §3.3 with terms `ℤ/4` and differentials `2` is zero in `D₂` of abelian groups and
  nonzero in `K₂`.
- For the split exact structure, an exact-acyclic periodic complex is a direct sum of disks, so
  `D_n(E)` is `K_n(A)`; for the abelian structure, exact-acyclic means acyclic.

### Examples

- The square-zero duplex of §8.4: for an object `B` of an additive category, both parity pieces
  `B ⊕ B` and both differentials `(a, b) ↦ (0, a)`, a contractible two-periodic complex with zero
  homology in both parities.
- The two-periodic complex `ℤ/4 ⇄ ℤ/4` with differentials `2` separates acyclic from contractible
  (§3.3).

### Dependencies

Layers 0–1. Mathlib: homological complexes over `ZMod n`, homotopies, homotopy cofibers, homotopy
categories, homology, Verdier localization, differential objects. Tau Ceti: differential modules
and their equivalences, the shift of periodic complexes, the split Frobenius structure on complexes
and differential modules, the triangulation of `K_n`, acyclic periodic complexes and `D_n` of an
abelian category, the curved duplex comparison at curvature zero.

## Layer 4: compression, periodic resolutions, and relative projectives

### 4.1 Compression

For an additive category with finite coproducts, compression
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.compressionFunctor n` folds a bounded complex into an `n`-periodic one:
`(Δ_n X)^r = ⊕_(i ≡ r mod n) X^i`, a finite sum because `X` is bounded, with differential the sum
of the differentials of `X`; the Koszul signs appear in the periodicity isomorphism
`Δ_n(X[k]) ≅ Δ_n(X)` for `k` divisible by `n`, whose component on `X^i` carries `(-1)^(k*i)`.
Compression preserves homotopies and mapping cones (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.compressionMappingConeIso`),
descends to a triangle functor `K^b(A) → K_n(A)`
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCompressionFunctor`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.homotopyCompressionFunctor_isTriangulated`) and, for `A` abelian, to a
triangle functor `D^b(A) → D_n(A)` (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.derivedCompressionFunctor`); a bounded
complex is acyclic exactly when its compression is (Stai, §§3.1–3.4).
*Needs:* §§3.2–3.4; Mathlib `DerivedCategory.Bounded`, `CochainComplex.Bounded`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.compressionFunctor`.

**Checks.**
- The compression `Δ₂` of the complex `B → B` (the identity) in degrees `0` and `1` is the disk
  `D_0(B)`, and `Δ₁` of a complex with zero differential is the differential module on the sum of
  its terms with `d = 0`.

### 4.2 The orbit category

For a preadditive category `D` with an additive `ℤ`-shift and `n ∈ ℤ`, the orbit category
`TauCeti.OrbitCategory D n` has the objects of `D` and hom groups
`Hom(X, Y) = ⊕_(j ∈ ℤ) Hom_D(X, Y[nj])`. Homogeneous pieces `f : X → Y[ni]` and `g : Y → Z[nj]`
compose to `f`, then `g[ni]`, then the coherence isomorphism `Z[nj][ni] ≅ Z[n(i+j)]` of Mathlib's
`shiftFunctorAdd'`, extended bilinearly. It is preadditive; the projection
`TauCeti.OrbitCategory.projection` (identity on objects, degree-zero inclusion on morphisms) is
additive; and the periodicity isomorphism `TauCeti.OrbitCategory.periodicity` from `π ∘ [n]` to
`π` has components the identity placed in degrees `1` and `-1`. Keller's universal property: an
additive `G : D → E` with an isomorphism `G ∘ [n] ≅ G` descends to `TauCeti.OrbitCategory.lift`,
which restricts to `G` along the projection compatibly with the two periodicity isomorphisms
(`TauCeti.OrbitCategory.projectionCompLift`), and every additive functor with these properties is
isomorphic to it (`TauCeti.OrbitCategory.isoLift`). A record storing an unspecified category with
a projection and a descent operation is not an orbit category: it is satisfied by categories with
the wrong hom groups. No orbit category is claimed to be triangulated.
*Needs:* Mathlib `HasShift`, `shiftFunctorAdd'`, `DirectSum`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.OrbitCategory`;
Keller, *On triangulated orbit categories*, §1.

**Checks.**
- For `D = D^b(mod-k)` over a field and `n ≠ 0`, the endomorphisms of `k` in the orbit category
  are `⊕_j Hom(k, k[nj]) = k`; for `n = 0` the formula would give a countable sum of copies of `k`,
  which is why the period is nonzero below.

### 4.3 Relatively projective, projective-flag and homotopically projective objects

Let `A` be an abelian category with enough projectives, for instance `mod-Λ`. An `n`-periodic
complex, a differential module for `n = 1` by §3.1, is *relatively projective* when every term is
projective (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.subcategoryDegreewiseProjective`); it *has a projective flag*
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.HasProjectiveFlag`) when it is obtained from zero by finitely many
extensions, split in each degree, by complexes with projective terms and zero differential, that
is, it has a finite filtration split on underlying objects whose subquotients are projective with
zero differential, the differential being strictly triangular; it is *homotopically projective*
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.IsHomotopicallyProjective`) when every map from it to an acyclic complex
is null-homotopic. If the homology objects of a periodic complex have projective dimension less
than `d`, it is homotopically projective exactly when it is homotopy equivalent to a complex with a
projective flag (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.isHomotopicallyProjective_iff_exists_hasProjectiveFlag`).
If every object of `A` has projective dimension less than `d`, relatively projective complexes are
homotopically projective, and the homotopically projective complexes are exactly those homotopy
equivalent to relatively projective ones
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.isHomotopicallyProjective_of_projective`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.isHomotopicallyProjective_iff_exists_projective`; Stai, Proposition 3.10
for `n = 1` and the remark closing §3.4 for general `n`; Avramov–Buchweitz–Iyengar, §§1–2, for free
flags). Under the same bound, the functor from the
full subcategory of `K_n(A)` on the periodic complexes of projectives to `D_n(A)` is an
equivalence
(`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory.projectiveEquivalence`).

Let `k` be a field and `Λ` a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra of finite global dimension,
`TauCeti.globalDimension Λᵐᵒᵖ ≤ d` in the universe of `Λ`: every right `Λ`-module has projective
dimension at most `d`, and let `n ≥ 1`. Prove `stai_projective_isEquivalence`: the periodic Verdier
localization restricts to an equivalence `K_n(proj-Λ) → D_n(mod-Λ)` from the full subcategory of
`K_n(mod-Λ)` on the periodic complexes of finitely generated projective right `Λ`-modules (Stai,
the remark closing §3.4). Prove moreover that the isomorphism closure of these complexes is a
triangulated subcategory
of `K_n(mod-Λ)` and that the equivalence is a triangle equivalence for its induced triangulation.
*Route.* Every finitely generated right `Λ`-module has projective dimension at most `d` in
`FGModuleCat Λᵐᵒᵖ`, which has enough projectives, so the statements above hold for `A = mod-Λ`.
*Needs:* §§3.3–3.4; Mathlib `Projective`, `HasProjectiveDimensionLT`, `HasProjectiveDimensionLE`;
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.IsHomotopicallyProjective`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.HasProjectiveFlag`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory.projectiveEquivalence`, `TauCeti.globalDimension`.

**Checks.**
- The acceptance test that keeps acyclic, contractible, relatively projective and homotopically
  projective apart: over `Λ = k[t]/(t²)`, the differential module `(Λ, d = t)` is acyclic, since
  `ker d = im d = (t)`; its underlying module is projective; it is not contractible, since a
  `Λ`-linear `h` is multiplication by some `c` and `t h + h t = 2tc ∈ (t)`. It is nonzero in
  `K₁(proj-Λ)` and zero in `D₁(mod-Λ)`, and it is relatively projective but not homotopically
  projective, so Proposition 3.10 fails for this algebra of infinite global dimension.

### 4.4 Stai's triangulated hull

Throughout this section `k` is a field and `Λ` a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra of finite global
dimension, `TauCeti.globalDimension Λᵐᵒᵖ ≤ d` in the universe of `Λ`: every right `Λ`-module has
projective dimension at most `d` (Stai, §§3–4). Let `n ≥ 1` and `mod-Λ = FGModuleCat Λᵐᵒᵖ`. The
orbit functor `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.orbitDerivedCompressionFunctor n` from `D^b(mod-Λ)/[n]` to
`D_n(mod-Λ)` is the descent (§4.2) of compression along its periodicity isomorphism for the shift
by `n`; by definition its restriction along the projection is compression.

Prove `stai_orbitDerivedCompressionFunctor_full_faithful`: the orbit functor is full and faithful
(Stai, Lemma 3.12 for `n = 1` and the remark following it for general `n`).
*Route.* The orbit functor of bounded homotopy categories `K^b/[n] → K_n` is full and faithful
(Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.full_orbitCompressionFunctor`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.faithful_orbitCompressionFunctor`); in finite global dimension bounded
complexes of finitely generated modules are quasi-isomorphic to bounded complexes of finitely
generated projectives, and `D_n(mod-Λ) ≃ K_n(proj-Λ)` (§4.3).

Prove that the essential image of the orbit functor consists of the objects isomorphic to the
image of a *gradable* periodic complex, one isomorphic as a periodic complex to the compression of
a bounded complex of finitely generated right `Λ`-modules (Stai, §3).

Prove `stai_eq_top_of_essImage_le`: every isomorphism-closed triangulated subcategory of
`D_n(mod-Λ)` containing the essential image of the orbit functor is all of `D_n(mod-Λ)` (Stai,
Theorem 4.3 for `n = 1` and the remark following it for general `n`). Consequently the thick
envelope `ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope` of the essential
image is also everything.

"Triangulated hull" means exactly these two statements about the triangulated category
`D_n(mod-Λ)` of §3.4: the orbit category embeds fully faithfully, and the embedding generates the
target under shifts and cones alone, before any idempotent completion. This is the notion Keller
attaches to the DG orbit category of an enhancement, whose triangulated hull is the triangulated
closure of the representables, not their idempotent-complete thick closure. Prove the comparison
with it: for the DG category `𝒟` of bounded complexes of finitely generated projective right
`Λ`-modules, a DG enhancement of `D^b(mod-Λ)` in finite global dimension, the DG orbit category
`𝒟/[n]` has Hom complexes `⊕_(j ∈ ℤ) Hom_𝒟(X, Y[nj])` (Keller, *On triangulated orbit
categories*, §5.1), and `H⁰` of its pretriangulated
envelope (DGAInfinity, Layer 5) is triangle-equivalent to `D_n(mod-Λ)`, compatibly with the orbit
functor, without an idempotent completion. No other notion of hull is used; an orbit category is
not claimed to be triangulated or essentially surjective, and no hull statement is made without
the finite-global-dimension hypothesis.
*Needs:* §§3.4, 4.1–4.3; DGAInfinity, Layer 5; Mathlib `HasDerivedCategory`,
`ObjectProperty.IsTriangulated`, `ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.orbitDerivedCompressionFunctor`,
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory.projectiveEquivalence`, `TauCeti.globalDimension`.

**Checks.**
- For `Λ = k` every `n`-periodic complex of finite-dimensional spaces is quasi-isomorphic to its
  homology with zero differential, the fold of a graded space, so the orbit functor is essentially
  surjective; on homs both sides are `⊕_j Hom(V, W[nj])`, so it is an equivalence.

### 4.5 Periodic projective resolutions and Eisenbud's periodicity

Define an *eventually periodic projective resolution* of a module `M` as a projective resolution
`P → M`, with differentials `d_i : P_i → P_(i-1)`, together with a preperiod `p ≥ 0` and a period
`m ≥ 1` such that `P_(i+m) = P_i` for `i ≥ p` and `d_(i+m) = d_i` for `i > p`. One period
`P_p, …, P_(p+m-1)`, with `P_i` in degree `-i mod m` and the differentials `d_(p+1), …, d_(p+m)`
(the last one `P_p = P_(p+m) → P_(p+m-1)`), is an `m`-periodic complex. Prove that the tail
`⋯ → P_(p+1) → P_p`, with `P_i` in degree `-i`, is the brutal (stupid) truncation in degrees at
most `-p` of the unfolding (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.unfoldFunctor`) of this `m`-periodic
complex, which is itself unbounded in both directions; and that maps of modules lift to chain maps between such
resolutions uniquely up to homotopy, so that an eventually periodic resolution is homotopy
equivalent to every projective resolution of `M`. Prove that over a noetherian local ring `(R, 𝔪)`,
in particular an artinian local ring, every
finitely generated module, of finite or infinite projective dimension, has a *minimal* free
resolution, by finitely generated free modules with every differential landing in `𝔪` times its
target, and that any two minimal free resolutions are isomorphic as complexes over `M`.

Prove Eisenbud's theorem: let `S` be a regular local ring, `w` a nonzero nonunit
non-zero-divisor, `R = S/(w)` and `M` a finitely generated `R`-module; then the minimal free
resolution of `M` over `R` is eventually periodic of period one or two, its periodic part being
the reduction modulo `w` of a pair of maps `P₀ → P₁ → P₀` of finitely generated free `S`-modules
whose two composites are multiplication by `w`, with the bound on the preperiod and the
minimality clauses of Eisenbud's Theorem 6.1.
*Needs:* §3.2; Mathlib `ProjectiveResolution`, `IsRegularLocalRing`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.unfoldFunctor`,
`TauCeti.exists_minimal_finiteProjectiveResolution_of_hasProjectiveDimensionLE`.

**Checks.**
- Over `k[x]/(x²)` the minimal resolution of `k` is periodic of period one (every differential is
  multiplication by `x`); over `k[x]/(x³)` it alternates `x` and `x²`, of period two. For
  `R = k⟦x, y⟧/(xy)`, the module `R/(x)` has the resolution alternating multiplication by `x` and
  `y`, periodic of period two from the start.

### Examples

- The dual-number differential module of §4.3, over any nontrivial commutative ring `k`:
  `TauCeti.dualNumberDifferentialModule` is not contractible
  (`TauCeti.dualNumberDifferentialModule_not_contractible`), its one-periodic complex is nonzero in
  the homotopy category (`TauCeti.not_isZero_quotient_obj_dualNumberPeriodicComplex`) and zero in
  the derived category (`TauCeti.isZero_Q_obj_dualNumberPeriodicComplex`) of all modules, and it is
  not homotopically projective (`TauCeti.not_isHomotopicallyProjective_dualNumberPeriodicComplex`).
- Over `A = k[x]/(x^n)`, the module `A/(x^i)` has the periodic resolution alternating `x^i` and
  `x^(n-i)` (§8.2), with preperiod `0` and period `2`; by §4.5, without `A/(x^i)`, it is the
  brutal truncation in degrees at most zero of the unfolding of the reduction modulo `x^n` of the
  matrix factorization `(x^(n-i), x^i)` of §8.3.

orbit-category shift coherences, projective dimension, projective resolutions, regular local rings.

## Layer 5: curved DG algebras, modules, and duplexes

### 5.1 Curved DG algebras

Let `k` be a commutative ring and `A` a `ℤ`-graded unital associative `k`-algebra, the grading a
Mathlib internal grading `𝒜 : ℤ → Submodule k A` with `[GradedAlgebra 𝒜]`. A right-convention
curved DG algebra structure is `TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra 𝒜 d w`: a `k`-linear derivation `d` of
degree one with `d(ab) = d(a)b + (-1)^|a| a d(b)`, a curvature `w ∈ A²`, `d²(a) = a*w - w*a` and
`d(w) = 0`. The curvature is not assumed central. The square is the graded commutator with `-w`
(`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.sq_eq_neg_lie`). Base change along a commutative `k`-algebra `S` gives
a curved DG algebra over `S` with curvature `1 ⊗ w` (`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.baseChange`); no
flatness is needed. Strict morphisms preserve the grading, `d` and `w` (`TauCeti.CurvedDGAlgHom`).
A change of connection by `a ∈ A¹` gives the curved DG algebra with differential `d + [a, -]` and
curvature `w - d(a) - a*a` (`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.connectionChange`), and
change-of-connection morphisms are a separate bundled notion (`TauCeti.CurvedDGConnectionHom`). At
curvature zero the structure is exactly a DG algebra (`TauCeti.isCurvedDGAlgebra_zero_iff`), and
with central curvature the differential squares to zero
(`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.toIsDGAlgebra_of_mem_center`) (Positselski, §6.2).

The Bianchi identity `d(w) = 0` is part of the definition. Prove what the square equation forces
without it: if `d` is a degree-one derivation with `d²(a) = a*w - w*a` for all `a`, then `d(w)` is
graded-central, `d(w)*a = (-1)^|a| a*d(w)` for homogeneous `a`, by computing `d³(a)` as `d(d²(a))`
and as `d²(d(a))`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `GradedAlgebra`, `DirectSum.Decomposition`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra`;
DGAInfinity, Layer 1, for the DG conventions.

**Checks.**
- For `d = 0`, `w = 0` and `a` of degree one, `d^a(x) = ax - (-1)^|x| xa` satisfies
  `(d^a)²(x) = a²x - xa²`, which is `x*w' - w'*x` for `w' = -a²`, as the formula
  `w^a = w - d(a) - a²` gives; the formula `w + a²` would give the opposite sign.

### 5.2 The graded opposite

The graded opposite `TauCeti.GradedOpposite` of an internally graded algebra carries the
Koszul-twisted multiplication `op(a) * op(b) = (-1)^(|a||b|) op(b*a)` on homogeneous elements
(`TauCeti.GradedOpposite.op_mul`); it is not the ring structure of `MulOpposite`. With
`d_op(op(a)) = op(d_A(a))`, a curved DG algebra with curvature `w` has graded opposite with
curvature `-op(w)`, not `op(w)` (`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.gradedOpposite`) (Positselski, §6.2).
*Needs:* §5.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GradedOpposite`, `TauCeti.IsCurvedDGAlgebra.gradedOpposite`.

**Checks.**
- Since `w` has even degree, `op(a)*op(w) = op(w*a)` and `op(w)*op(a) = op(a*w)`, so
  `d_op²(op a) = op(a*w - w*a) = op(a)*(-op w) - (-op w)*op(a)`. Storing `op(w)` would give the
  negative of `d_op²` whenever `a*w ≠ w*a`.

### 5.3 Curved DG modules

A right curved DG module over `(A, d, w)` is `TauCeti.IsCurvedDGRightModule h ℳ d_M`: a graded right
`A`-module with a degree-one differential satisfying `d_M(m*a) = d_M(m)*a + (-1)^|m| m*d_A(a)` and
`d_M²(m) = m*w`; bundled, these form `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat h`. Left modules are
`TauCeti.IsCurvedDGLeftModule`, with `d²(m) = -w*m`, and they are exactly the right modules over
the graded opposite (`TauCeti.isCurvedDGLeftModule_iff_gradedOppositeRight`); left modules are never
given the right-module square (Positselski, §6.2).
*Needs:* §5.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCurvedDGRightModule`, `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat`.

**Checks.**
- A right module over the graded opposite with curvature `-op(w)` has `d²(m) = m·(-op(w))`, and
  `m·op(w) = (-1)^(|w||m|) w*m = w*m` because `|w| = 2`; so `d²(m) = -w*m`.

### 5.4 The DG category of curved modules and tensor products

For modules over the same curved DG algebra, the Hom differential `δ(f) = d_N f - (-1)^r f d_M`
squares to zero (`TauCeti.curvedDGRightModuleHomComplex`), making `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat h`
a DG category, with closed degree-zero category, shift `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.shift`
(grading moved by `n`, differential multiplied by `(-1)^n`), cone `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.cone`
on `M × N` with differential `(x, y) ↦ (-d_M x, f x + d_N y)`, and homotopy category, triangulated by
shifts and cones (§6.1) although individual curved modules have no homology. A right module and a
left module over the same curved DG algebra have a balanced tensor product whose curvatures cancel,
a complex (`TauCeti.BalancedTensorProduct.curvedDifferential`), functorial on homotopy categories
(`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.tensorHomotopyFunctor`). Tensor products are formed only after the
handedness and curvature are spelled out, and each formula is proved rather than inferred from the
uncurved tensor product.

Prove the combining case. For curved DG algebras `(A, d_A, w_A)` and `(B, d_B, w_B)` over `k`, the
graded tensor product `A ⊗ B`, with multiplication `(a ⊗ b)(a' ⊗ b') = (-1)^(|b||a'|) aa' ⊗ bb'` and
differential `d_A ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ d_B` with the Koszul sign, is a curved DG algebra with curvature
`w_A ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ w_B`; and for right curved modules `M` over `A` and `N` over `B`, `M ⊗_k N` with
differential `d_M ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ d_N` is a right curved module over `A ⊗ B` (Positselski, §6.2, for
curved DG modules and their Hom complexes).
*Needs:* §5.3; DGAInfinity, Layer 1 (tensor products of graded algebras and their sign formulas);
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.curvedDGRightModuleHomComplex`, `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.cone`.

**Checks.**
- For modules of different curvatures the Hom differential is not a differential: for a right
  `A`-linear `f`, `δ²(f)(m) = f(m)*(w_N - w_M)` with `w_N, w_M` the two curvatures, nonzero as soon
  as `f(m)` is not killed by their difference.
- In the combining case the cross terms of `(d_M ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ d_N)²` cancel by the Koszul sign, leaving
  `m*w_A ⊗ n + m ⊗ n*w_B`, which is `(m ⊗ n)·(w_A ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ w_B)` because `w_A` is even.

### 5.5 Central potentials and curved duplexes

When `w` is graded-central of degree two the differential of `A` squares to zero (§5.1). In parity
grading, a *curved duplex* of potential `w`, in an `R`-linear category with `w ∈ R`, is a pair
`M₀ ⇄ M₁` whose two composites are `w` times the identity, `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex`; the two
equations are separate fields and simp lemmas, and `d² = 0` is never derived from `d² = w`
without the hypothesis `w = 0`. At `w = 0` a curved duplex is a genuine two-periodic complex
(§3.1). Curved duplexes have a parity shift `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.parityShift`, a homotopy category
`TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.HomotopyCategory` and tensor products `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.tensorObj` with
added potentials (Frenkel–Khovanov–Schiffmann, §§2–4).

Prove that curved duplexes are curved modules. For a commutative ring `S` and `w ∈ S`, give
`S[u, u⁻¹]` the grading `|u| = 2`, zero differential and the central curvature `w*u`. Prove that the
closed degree-zero category of right curved modules over it is equivalent to the category of
curved duplexes of `S`-modules of potential `w`, a module `M` going to `M⁰ ⇄ M¹` with maps `d` and
`m ↦ d(m)*u⁻¹`, compatibly with shifts, cones and homotopies, hence with an equivalence of
homotopy categories.
*Needs:* §§3.1, 5.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex`, `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.periodicComplexEquivalence`.

**Checks.**
- In this equivalence `d₁(d₀(m)) = d²(m)*u⁻¹ = m*w*u*u⁻¹ = m*w`, and `d₀(d₁(m)) = d²(m)*u⁻¹ = m*w`
  because `u⁻¹` is even and the differential of `S[u, u⁻¹]` is zero.

### Examples

- For an element `b` of degree one of a graded algebra, `d = [b, -]` and `w = -b²` form a curved
  DG algebra: `d²(a) = [b², a] = a*w - w*a`, and `d(w) = -[b, b²] = 0`.
- At curvature zero, ordinary DG modules are curved modules, and square-zero duplexes are
  two-periodic complexes (§3.1).

## Layer 6: curved acyclicity and derived categories of the second kind

### 6.1 The curved homotopy category and graded modules

Throughout, `R` and `A` lie in one universe `u` and curved modules have carriers in `Type u`
(Convention 9). The closed degree-zero morphisms of right curved modules over `h` form a category;
an odd homotopy is a right-linear map of degree `-1`, with no Koszul sign because the algebra acts
on the other side, and its boundary `d_N h + h d_M` is closed
(`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.closedHomotopyBoundary`) because source and target have the same
curvature. The curved homotopy category, `TauCeti.DGHomotopyCategory R (TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat h)`,
is the quotient by boundaries. It is triangulated by Happel's construction for the Frobenius
structure of graded-split sequences (`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.gradedSplitExact_isFrobenius`),
equivalently by shifts and cones (`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.homotopyPretriangulated`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.homotopyIsTriangulated`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.coneTriangle_distinguished`), and it has the small coproducts and
products, indexed by types in `Type u`, inherited from graded modules. The abelian category of
graded right modules over the underlying graded ring, without differential, is
`TauCeti.GradedModuleCat` over the opposite internal grading, with the faithful exact functor
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.forgetDifferential` from the closed category; Positselski's
homological-dimension hypotheses live there (Positselski, §§6.2, 7.6).
*Needs:* §§1.1, 5.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.homotopyIsTriangulated`,
`TauCeti.GradedModuleCat`, `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.forgetDifferential`.

### 6.2 Totalizations and the three acyclic classes

The totalization of a short complex `K → L → M` of closed maps is the iterated cone
`cone(cone(K → L) → M)`, with underlying graded module `K[2] × L[1] × M`
(`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.totalization`); `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.totalizations` is
the class of objects of the curved homotopy category represented by totalizations of short exact
sequences in the abelian closed category. Inside the curved homotopy category:

- the *absolutely acyclic* objects `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.absolutelyAcyclic` form the
  smallest thick triangulated subcategory containing the totalizations, Mathlib's
  `ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope`;
- the *coacyclic* objects `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.coacyclic` form the smallest
  isomorphism-closed triangulated subcategory containing them and closed under small coproducts;
- the *contraacyclic* objects `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.contraacyclic` form the smallest
  isomorphism-closed triangulated subcategory containing them and closed under small products.

These are specific object properties of the constructed homotopy category; none is a parameter.
`Ac_abs ⊆ Ac_co` and `Ac_abs ⊆ Ac_ctr`
(`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.absolutelyAcyclic_le_coacyclic`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.absolutelyAcyclic_le_contraacyclic`), since a triangulated subcategory
closed under countable coproducts or products is thick (Positselski, Definitions 7.4–7.6).

Prove that a short complex of closed maps is short exact in the closed category exactly when its
underlying sequence of modules is short exact, so that the totalizations are those of the short
exact sequences of curved modules in the sense of closed maps whose underlying linear maps are
short exact.
*Needs:* §6.1; Mathlib `ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope`, `ShortComplex.ShortExact`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.totalizations`, `CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.localizingClosure`,
`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.colocalizingClosure`.

### 6.3 Derived categories of the second kind

The absolute, co- and contraderived categories are the Verdier quotients of the curved homotopy
category by the three classes, `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.AbsoluteDerivedCategory`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.CoderivedCategory` and
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.ContraderivedCategory`, Mathlib `trW` localizations with their
triangulated structure. Their universal properties are those of Verdier localization: a triangle
functor out of the curved homotopy category inverts the corresponding weak equivalences exactly when
it kills the class (`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.trW_isInvertedBy_iff`; for the absolute class
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.absolutelyAcyclic_trW_isInvertedBy_iff`). There is no "ordinary
derived category of curved modules" defined by homology. The inclusions of §6.2 induce triangle
functors `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.AbsoluteDerivedCategory.toCoderived` and
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.AbsoluteDerivedCategory.toContraderived` through the universal
property of the absolute localization; each commutes with the localization functors up to the
natural isomorphism supplied by Mathlib's `Localization.fac`
(`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.AbsoluteDerivedCategory.toCoderivedFactors`). A localization never
supplies a strict equality of functors, and demanding one is false already for equivalent
presentations of the same localization. There is no canonical comparison in either direction
between the coderived and the contraderived categories without an additional theorem. The closure,
quotient and comparison machinery is stated once for an arbitrary triangulated category with a
generating class (`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.localizingClosure`,
`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.colocalizingClosure`,
`CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.triangEnvelope_le_localizingClosure`) and instantiated with the curved
homotopy category and its totalizations (Positselski, §7.6).
*Needs:* §6.2; Mathlib `ObjectProperty.trW`, `Localization.fac`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.CoderivedCategory`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.AbsoluteDerivedCategory.toCoderived`.

### 6.4 Positselski's comparison theorems

The theorems are translated to right modules through the graded opposite; the hypotheses below are
stated in the category of graded right modules of §6.1, which is where Positselski's left-module
conditions land after the translation: his finite left homological dimension becomes a bound on
the projective dimensions of graded right modules, and his conditions on countable direct sums of
injectives and countable products of projectives are on graded right modules. Write `Gr-A` for the
category of graded right modules over the graded ring underlying `A`.

- Prove `positselski_7_8a`: if every object of `Gr-A` has projective dimension at most `e`, the
  absolutely acyclic, coacyclic and contraacyclic classes coincide (Theorem 7.8(a), applied to the
  graded opposite).
- Prove `positselski_7_8b`: at curvature zero, if every object of `Gr-A` has projective dimension at
  most `e` and **either** `A^p = 0` for every `p > 0`, **or** all three of `A^p = 0` for every
  `p < 0`, `A⁰` classically semisimple and `A¹ = 0`, then the objects represented by modules with
  exact differential, `acyclic`, are exactly the absolutely acyclic, the coacyclic and the
  contraacyclic objects (Theorem 7.8(b)). Curvature zero is literal: `w = 0`.
- Prove `positselski_7_9a`: under the right-module form of condition `(*)`, one bound `e` on the
  injective dimensions in `Gr-A` of all countable direct sums of injective objects of `Gr-A`, the
  full subcategory `gradedInjective` of the curved homotopy category on the modules whose
  underlying graded module is injective maps by an equivalence onto the coderived category
  (Theorem 7.9(a)).
- Prove `positselski_7_9b`: under the right-module form of condition `(**)`, one bound `e` on the
  projective dimensions in `Gr-A` of all countable products of projective objects of `Gr-A`, the
  full subcategory `gradedProjective` on the modules whose underlying graded module is projective
  maps by an equivalence onto the contraderived category (Theorem 7.9(b)).

In each statement the change of side is recorded: noetherian, product, coproduct and
finite-dimensionality conditions are on graded right modules (Positselski, §7.6, Theorems 7.8–7.9).
*Needs:* §§6.1–6.3; Mathlib `HasProjectiveDimensionLE`, `HasInjectiveDimensionLE`,
`IsSemisimpleRing`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.CoderivedCategory`,
`TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.ContraderivedCategory`, `TauCeti.CurvedDGRightModuleCat.toGradedModuleCat`.

**Checks.**
- For `A = k` a field in degree zero, with `d = 0` and `w = 0`, every graded module is projective
  and injective, so the hypotheses of all four statements hold with `e = 0`. Acyclic complexes of
  vector spaces are contractible, so the totalizations, and hence all three acyclic classes and
  `acyclic`, consist of the zero objects of the homotopy category; all four conclusions hold, and
  the coderived and contraderived categories are the homotopy category itself.

### Examples

- Over `A = k` concentrated in degree zero with `d = 0` and `w = 0`, curved modules are complexes
  of vector spaces, and the absolute, co- and contraderived categories all coincide with the
  homotopy category (§6.4).

## Layer 7: matrix factorizations

### 7.1 Matrix factorizations and their homotopy category

For a commutative ring `S` and `w ∈ S`, a matrix factorization `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization S w`
is a curved duplex of finitely generated `S`-modules with projective components: maps
`P₀ → P₁ → P₀` with `d₁d₀ = w` and `d₀d₁ = w`, the finite-projective curved duplexes. They form
an additive category whose morphisms are the closed even maps, with the `ℤ/2`-graded Hom complex
of curved duplexes (`TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.homComplex`), null-homotopies, the parity shift
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.parityShift` (swapping the components and negating both maps), direct
sums, tensor products `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.tensorObj` with summed potentials, mapping
cones `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.cone`, and the homotopy category
`HMF(S, w) = TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory`, which is triangulated. A rank-one
factorization `w = ab` gives `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.rankOne` (Eisenbud, §5).
*Needs:* §5.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization`, `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory`.

**Checks.**
- The factorization `S → S → S` with `d₀ = 1` and `d₁ = w` is contractible (the homotopy with
  `h₁ = 1`, `h₀ = 0` satisfies `d₁h₀ + h₁d₀ = 1` and `d₀h₁ + h₀d₁ = 1`), and so is the one with
  `d₀ = w` and `d₁ = 1`; both are zero in `HMF(S, w)`. The cokernels of `d₀` reduced modulo `w`, the
  objects the cokernel functor of §7.4 assigns to them, are `0` and the free `S/(w)`-module `S/(w)`,
  both zero in the stable category. A functor taking cokernels of maps of free modules without
  passing to the stable category would send these two zero objects to nonisomorphic modules.

### 7.2 Duality

For `P = (P₀ --d₀--> P₁ --d₁--> P₀)` with finitely generated projective components, the dual
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.dual P` has `(Pᵛ)₀ = P₀ᵛ`, `(Pᵛ)₁ = P₁ᵛ`, `d₀ᵛ = (d₁)ᵗ : P₀ᵛ → P₁ᵛ` and
`d₁ᵛ = -(d₀)ᵗ : P₁ᵛ → P₀ᵛ`; both composites are multiplication by `-w`. Duality is a contravariant
functor from potential `w` to potential `-w` (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.dualFunctor`), and the
double dual is naturally isomorphic to the identity (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.doubleDualIso`).
Any other placement of the single minus sign is related to this one by an explicit natural
isomorphism.
*Needs:* §7.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.dual`.

**Checks.**
- For `S = k[x]`, `w = x^n` and `P = (x^i, x^(n-i))` on rank-one free modules, the dual is
  `(x^(n-i), -x^i)`, of potential `-x^n`: `(-x^i)·x^(n-i) = -x^n`. The unsigned transpose
  `(x^(n-i), x^i)` has potential `x^n`, so the sign is what makes the dual a factorization of `-w`.

### 7.3 The Frobenius description

With componentwise split sequences, matrix factorizations form a Frobenius exact category
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.splitExact`, `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.splitExact_isFrobenius`),
whose projective-injectives are the contractible factorizations
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.splitExact_isProjective_iff`) and whose stable category is `HMF(S, w)`
as a triangulated category (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.stableHomotopyEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.stableToHomotopy_isTriangulated`) (Frenkel–Khovanov–Schiffmann,
§§2–4, for the stable description of curved duplexes).
*Needs:* §§1.1, 7.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.splitExact_isFrobenius`.

### 7.4 Eisenbud's local theorem

For `w` a non-zero-divisor, taking the cokernel of `d₀` reduced modulo `w` is a fully faithful
triangle functor `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory.quotientGorensteinProjectiveStableCokernel`
from `HMF(S, w)` to the stable category of finitely generated Gorenstein-projective
`S/(w)`-modules (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory.fullyFaithfulQuotientGorensteinProjectiveStableCokernel`,
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory.quotientGorensteinProjectiveStableCokernel_isTriangulated`),
whose essential image consists of the Gorenstein-projective `S/(w)`-modules that have, as
`S`-modules, a resolution of length one by finitely generated projective `S`-modules
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.quotientGorensteinProjectiveCokernel_essImage_iff`).

Let `(S, 𝔪)` be a regular local ring and `w ∈ 𝔪` nonzero, hence a non-zero-divisor and a nonunit,
and `R = S/(w)`. Over the local ring `S` finitely generated projective modules are free, so these
are Eisenbud's finite free matrix factorizations. Prove `eisenbud_isEquivalence`: the cokernel
functor is an equivalence (Eisenbud, §5 and Theorem 6.1; Buchweitz, §4). With §7.6 this is the
comparison `HMF(S, w) ≃ stable(MCM R)`, and prove Eisenbud's minimality clause with it: the
reduced matrix factorizations, those whose two maps have entries in `𝔪` in some bases, correspond
through the cokernel bijectively, up to isomorphism, to the maximal Cohen–Macaulay `R`-modules
without nonzero free summands (Eisenbud, Corollary 6.3). Eventual two-periodicity of resolutions is
§4.5.
*Route.* Over `S` regular local, a finitely generated Gorenstein-projective `R`-module has
projective dimension at most one over `S`
(`TauCeti.IsGorensteinProjective.hasProjectiveDimensionLE_restrictScalars_quotient_of_isRegularLocalRing`),
so it lies in the essential image.
*Needs:* §§2.3, 7.1, 7.3; Mathlib `IsRegularLocalRing`, `IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.HomotopyCategory.quotientGorensteinProjectiveStableCokernel`,
`TauCeti.IsGorensteinProjective.hasProjectiveDimensionLE_restrictScalars_quotient_of_isRegularLocalRing`.

**Checks.**
- For `S = k⟦x⟧` and `w = x^n`, the factorizations `(x^i, x^(n-i))` for `1 ≤ i ≤ n - 1` go to the
  modules `A/(x^i)` over `A = k[x]/(x^n)`, the `n - 1` indecomposable objects of `stmod-A` (§8.2).
- If `w ∈ 𝔪 ∖ 𝔪²`, then `S/(w)` is regular, its Gorenstein-projectives are free and its stable
  category is zero; accordingly every matrix factorization of `w` is contractible.

### 7.5 Orlov's theorem

Let `X = Spec S` be a smooth affine variety over a field `k`, or more generally the affine case of
a regular scheme satisfying Orlov's (ELF) conditions: separated, noetherian, of finite Krull
dimension, with enough locally free sheaves of finite rank. Require the morphism `W : X → 𝔸¹`
given by `w` to be flat, that is, `S` flat over `k[t]` through `t ↦ w`, and let `X₀ = Spec S/(w)` be
its zero fibre; use finitely generated projective `S`-modules. Modules over the commutative ring
`S/(w)` are its left modules, as in §7.4, so that here `D_sg(S/(w))` is
`TauCeti.SingularityCategory (FGModuleCat (S ⧸ Ideal.span {w}))`. Prove Orlov's theorem: the
functor from `HMF(S, w)` to `D_sg(S/(w))` given by the cokernel functor of §7.4, the equivalence of
§2.3 for `A = S/(w)` and the functor of §2.5 for `C = FGModuleCat (S ⧸ Ideal.span {w})` is an
exact equivalence (Orlov, Proposition 3.3 and Theorem 3.9). Under these
hypotheses no idempotent completion is inserted. Flatness of `W` is the standing hypothesis of
Orlov's Theorem 3.9 (Orlov, §3.1), and the theorem is not applied at `w = 0`. If another source
gives, in a global or non-affine setting, only density up to direct summands, its statement is made
after idempotent completion, with the comparison functor before completion.
*Needs:* §§2.3, 2.5, 7.4; Mathlib `Module.Flat`, `IsRegularRing`, `ringKrullDim`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.SingularityCategory`.

**Checks.**
- At `w = 0` the zero fibre is `X`, smooth, so `D_sg = 0`, while `HMF(S, 0)` is nonzero: the
  factorization with `P₀ = S`, `P₁ = 0` and zero maps is not null-homotopic, every homotopy being
  zero. The map `k[t] → S`, `t ↦ 0`, is not flat, as the theorem requires.

### 7.6 Gorenstein-projective and maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules over a hypersurface

Let `(S, 𝔪)` be a regular local ring, `0 ≠ w ∈ 𝔪` and `R = S/(w)`. A finitely generated
`R`-module `M` is *maximal Cohen–Macaulay* when `M = 0` or its depth equals the Krull dimension of
`R`. Prove that `R` is Iwanaga–Gorenstein, with both self-injective dimensions equal to
`dim R`, and that a finitely generated `R`-module is Gorenstein-projective exactly when it is
maximal Cohen–Macaulay, so that in this commutative hypersurface setting `stable(Gproj-R)` is the
stable category of maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules and Buchweitz's comparison (§2.5) applies to it
(Buchweitz, §4).
*Needs:* §§2.2, 2.4; Mathlib `IsRegularLocalRing`, `ringKrullDim`, `IsLocalRing.depth`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.IsGorensteinProjective`, `TauCeti.IsIwanagaGorenstein`.

**Checks.**
- For `R = k⟦x, y⟧/(xy)`, the module `R/(x)` has depth one, equal to `dim R`, and is
  Gorenstein-projective (its complete resolution alternates multiplication by `x` and `y`); the
  residue field has depth zero and is neither.

### 7.7 Matrix factorizations, duplexes, curved modules and base change

Matrix factorizations are a full subcategory of curved duplexes
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.inclusion`), fully faithfully on homotopy categories by a triangle
functor (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.homotopyInclusion`). Base change along a ring map is
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.baseChangeFunctor`. The forgetful functor to two-periodic complexes
exists only when `w = 0` or after base change to `S/(w)`, where both composites become zero:
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.toPeriodicComplex` for a ring map killing `w`, and
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.quotientPeriodicComplex` over `S/(w)`. For `w` a non-zero-divisor, the
unfolded reduction of a matrix factorization is a complete resolution of the reduced cokernel
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.isTotallyAcyclic_unfoldFunctor_obj_quotientPeriodicComplex_obj`), which
relates the periodic resolution of the cokernel to the singularity comparison.

Prove the comparison with curved DG modules: through §5.5, matrix factorizations of `w` are the
full subcategory of curved right modules over `S[u, u⁻¹]` with curvature `w*u` whose components
in degrees `0` and `1` are finitely generated projective, compatibly with homotopy categories
(Eisenbud, §5, for matrix factorizations and their cokernels).
*Needs:* §§5.5, 7.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.inclusion`,
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.quotientPeriodicComplex`.

### Examples

- For `S = k[x]` and `w = x^n`, the factorization `(x^i, x^(n-i))` reduces modulo `x^n` to a
  two-periodic complex whose unfolding, brutally truncated in degrees at most one, is the periodic
  resolution of `A/(x^i)` over `A = k[x]/(x^n)` with `A/(x^i)` deleted and `P_k` in degree
  `1 - k` (§§8.2–8.3).
- Rank-one factorizations `w = ab` give cokernels `S/(a)` reduced modulo `w`, which are
  Gorenstein-projective over `S/(w)` for `w` a non-zero-divisor.

### Dependencies

Layers 1, 2 and 5. Mathlib: regular local and regular rings, flatness, Krull dimension and depth.
Tau Ceti: matrix factorizations, their homotopy category, duality, tensor products and Frobenius
description, the cokernel functor to stable Gorenstein-projectives and its essential image, base
change and reduction to two-periodic complexes, the Gorenstein-projective projective-dimension bound
over regular local rings, singularity categories.

## Layer 8: comparison functors and reconciliation

### 8.1 The comparison functors

The constructions are linked by these named functors; for each, the object and morphism formulas,
the additive, DG or triangle structure, the kernel or weak-equivalence behaviour, full faithfulness
and essential image are those stated in the cited section, under exactly the hypotheses stated
there.

- Bounded complexes to periodic complexes by compression (§4.1), with the orbit functor (§4.4).
- Periodic homotopy to periodic derived categories by Verdier localization
  (`TauCeti.PeriodicComplex.DerivedCategory.Qh`, §3.4), a triangle functor killing the acyclics.
- Stable Gorenstein-projectives to singularity categories by placing in degree zero (§2.5).
- Matrix factorizations to curved duplexes by forgetting finite projectivity (§7.7).
- Matrix factorizations over `(S, w)` to two-periodic complexes over `S/(w)` by base change (§7.7).
- Matrix factorizations to stable maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules by cokernel (§§7.4, 7.6).
- Ordinary DG modules to curved DG modules at `w = 0`: the DG functor
  `TauCeti.DGRightModuleCat.dgToCurvedFunctor`, an equivalence of closed degree-zero and of homotopy
  categories (`TauCeti.DGRightModuleCat.closedToCurvedEquivalence`,
  `TauCeti.DGRightModuleCat.homotopyToCurvedEquivalence`).
- Left curved modules to right curved modules over the graded opposite (§5.3).

Prove these comparisons: that the equivalence of homotopy categories from ordinary DG
modules to curved modules at `w = 0` is a triangle equivalence; that left curved modules over
`(A, d, w)` and right curved modules over the graded opposite form equivalent DG categories, with
equivalent closed and homotopy categories, extending the correspondence of objects of §5.3; and
that the comparison diagrams commute up to natural isomorphism: for `w` a non-zero-divisor, the
functor of §7.5 is the composite of the cokernel functor with the functors of §§2.3 and 2.5, and
the unfolded reduction of a matrix factorization is a complete resolution of its reduced cokernel
(§7.7). Naturality under ring maps is proved for each functor only with the centrality,
potential-preservation, flatness, finite-generation and regularity hypotheses that functor needs.
No two categories of Convention 11 become definitionally equal; the reconciliation is by these
explicit functors and equivalences.
*Needs:* §§2.3, 2.5, 3.4, 4.1, 4.4, 5.3, 7.4–7.7; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.DGRightModuleCat.homotopyToCurvedEquivalence`,
`TauCeti.isCurvedDGLeftModule_iff_gradedOppositeRight`.

### 8.2 The stable category of `k[x]/(x^n)`

Let `k` be any field, `n ≥ 2`, `A = k[x]/(x^n)` and `M_i = A/(x^i)` for `1 ≤ i ≤ n`. The coefficient
of `x^(n-1)` is a symmetric Frobenius functional
(`AdjoinRoot.isSymmetricFrobeniusFunctional_lastCoeff_X_pow`), with no restriction on the
characteristic. `M_1, …, M_n` are the indecomposable finitely generated right `A`-modules up to
isomorphism, pairwise non-isomorphic
(`TauCeti.isIndecomposableModule_iff_exists_linearEquiv_quotient_span_op_root_X_pow_pow`,
`TauCeti.nonempty_linearEquiv_quotient_span_op_root_X_pow_pow_iff`); `M_n = A` is projective and
zero in `stmod-A` (`TauCeti.FGModuleCat.isZero_stableModuleFunctor_obj_cyclicModule_root_pow_self`);
for `1 ≤ i < n`, `ΩM_i ≅ M_(n-i)` and `Ω²M_i ≅ M_i` in the stable category
(`TauCeti.FGModuleCat.stableModuleLoopCyclicModuleRootPowIso`,
`TauCeti.FGModuleCat.stableModuleLoopLoopCyclicModuleRootPowIso`); and for `1 ≤ i, j < n` the stable
morphisms form a space of dimension `min(i, j, n - i, n - j)`
(`TauCeti.FGModuleCat.finrank_stableModuleHom_cyclicModule_root_pow`).

Prove that for `1 ≤ i < n` the minimal free resolution of `M_i` is the complex alternating
multiplication by `x^i` and by `x^(n-i)`, augmented by `A → M_i`: it is exact
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXPeriodicComplex_acyclic`), every differential lands in `(x)`,
and, with `M_i` deleted and `P_k` in degree `-k`, it is the brutal truncation in degrees at most
zero of the unfolding of the two-periodic complex
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXPeriodicComplex` taken at the exponent `n - i`, whose differential is `x^(n-i)` from parity zero to parity one and
`x^i` from parity one to parity zero. Prove that `min(i, j, n - i, n - j)` equals
`min(i, j) - max(0, i + j - n)`.
*Needs:* §§0.4, 2.1, 4.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.FGModuleCat.finrank_stableModuleHom_cyclicModule_root_pow`,
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXPeriodicComplex`.

**Checks.**
- `n = 2`: the only nonzero stable dimension is `i = j = 1`, with value `1`. `n = 3`: all four
  values for `i, j ∈ {1, 2}` are `1`. For `n = 3` and `i = j = 2` the ordinary Hom space has
  dimension `2`, the stable one `1`: a computation of ordinary instead of stable morphisms is told
  apart already here.
- For `i ≤ j`, `min(i, j, n - i, n - j) = min(i, n - j) = i - max(0, i + j - n)`.

### 8.3 A matrix factorization of `x^n`

Let `S = k[x]`, `w = x^n` and `1 ≤ i < n`. The factorization `S --x^i--> S --x^(n-i)--> S`,
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXOfLE`, has both composites multiplication by `x^n`; its base
change to `A = S/(x^n)` is the two-periodic complex
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXPeriodicComplex`
of §8.2 at the exponent `i` (`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXOfLEBaseChangePeriodicIso`), and its
cokernel is `M_i`
(`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXOfLECokernelIso`).

Prove that `k[x]` is a free `k[t]`-module through `t ↦ x^n`, with basis `1, x, …, x^(n-1)`, hence
flat, so that Orlov's hypothesis holds. Prove that the classes correspond under the three separate
comparisons, each with its own hypotheses: under Orlov's theorem (§7.5) for `S = k[x]`; under
Eisenbud's theorem (§7.4) for the regular local ring `S_(x)`, which has the same quotient
`S_(x)/(x^n) ≅ A`; and under Buchweitz's theorem (§2.5) for the self-injective `A`. Through
`HMF(S, w)`, `stable(MCM A) = stmod-A` and `D_sg(A)` the factorization goes to `M_i`. Prove that the
parity shift corresponds to the syzygy: the shift of `(x^i, x^(n-i))` is isomorphic to
`(x^(n-i), x^i)`, whose cokernel is `M_(n-i) ≅ ΩM_i`, and two shifts return `M_i`.
*Needs:* §§2.5, 7.4–7.6, 8.2; Mathlib `Polynomial`, `Module.Free`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXOfLE`, `TauCeti.MatrixFactorization.powerXOfLECokernelIso`.

**Checks.**
- The parity shift of `(x^i, x^(n-i))` is `(-x^(n-i), -x^i)`, and the maps `1` on the first
  component and `-1` on the second form an isomorphism to `(x^(n-i), x^i)`.
- The factorizations `(1, x^n)` and `(x^n, 1)` are contractible; their cokernels are `0` and `A`,
  both zero in `stmod-A`.

### 8.4 A square-zero duplex and the dual numbers

For an object `B` of an additive category, the square-zero duplex `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.squareZero B`
of curvature zero has both parity pieces `B ⊕ B` and both differentials `(a, b) ↦ (0, a)`. Its two
composites are zero, so by §3.1 it is a genuine two-periodic complex; its homology vanishes in both
parities (`TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.isZero_squareZero_periodicHomology`), and it is contractible, with
homotopy `(a, b) ↦ (b, 0)` in both parities (`TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.nullHomotopicMap_squareZero`).
Prove that its shift is the duplex with both differentials `(a, b) ↦ (0, -a)`, isomorphic to it by the
identity in even and minus the identity in odd parity; that the cone of its identity is
contractible; and that forgetting parity (§3.1) sends it to the differential module on
`(B ⊕ B) ⊕ (B ⊕ B)` with `d((a, b), (a', b')) = ((0, a'), (0, a))`. Forgetting parity has no
inverse construction: it is not essentially surjective (the Check of §3.1).

Over `Λ = k[t]/(t²)`, the differential module `(Λ, d = t)` of §4.3 is acyclic and not
contractible. The two examples test different things: the square-zero duplex tests the object and
sign conventions of Layers 3 and 5, and the dual-number module separates the homotopy and derived
quotients (Frenkel–Khovanov–Schiffmann, §§2–4, for duplexes; Avramov–Buchweitz–Iyengar, §§1–2,
for differential modules).
*Needs:* §§3.1–3.3, 4.3, 5.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.CurvedDuplex.squareZero`,
`TauCeti.DifferentialModule.forgetParity`, `TauCeti.dualNumberDifferentialModule`.

**Checks.**
- Both differentials are needed. The pair `B ⊕ B ⇄ B ⊕ B` with `d₀(a, b) = (0, a)` and `d₁ = 0` is
  also a two-periodic complex, but its even homology is `ker d₀ / im d₁ = (0 ⊕ B)/0 ≅ B` and its odd
  homology is `ker d₁ / im d₀ = (B ⊕ B)/(0 ⊕ B) ≅ B`; for `B ≠ 0` it is neither acyclic nor
  contractible, while the square-zero duplex is both.
