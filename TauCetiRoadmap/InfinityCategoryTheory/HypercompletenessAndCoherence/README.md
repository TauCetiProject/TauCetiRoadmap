# Roadmap: hypercompleteness, sheaves of spectra and coherent ∞-topoi

**Summary.** This Part develops three strands of the theory of ∞-topoi beyond Part InfinityTopoi: hypercompleteness,
stable coefficients, and finiteness.

It builds on the ∞-topoi of Part InfinityTopoi (Giraud's theorem, effective epimorphisms, sheaves on sites,
geometric morphisms, homotopy sheaves, n-connective morphisms and Eilenberg–MacLane objects), the stable theory of
Part StableInfinityCategories, and Mathlib's order theory and spectral spaces.

- **Hypercompleteness (Layers 1–3).** Hypercomplete objects and ∞-topoi (defined in §1.1), the hypercompletion,
  hypercoverings and hyperdescent, and homotopy dimension.
- **Sheaves of spectra (Layers 4–6).** Sheaves of spectra, their t-structure, and their hypercompleteness.
- **Finiteness (Layers 7–12).** Stone duality, coherent ∞-topoi, finitary sites, points and Deligne completeness,
  semisimplicial hypercoverings, and Postnikov complete ∞-topoi.

**Headline theorems.**
- The ∞-connective morphisms of an ∞-topos form a strongly saturated class of small generation, so the
  hypercomplete objects form a left exact localization X^∧ with a universal property among geometric morphisms from
  hypercomplete ∞-topoi (HTT, Props. 6.5.2.8, 6.5.2.13, pp. 686–687); every accessible left exact localization is a
  topological localization followed by a cotopological one (HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.19, p. 690).
- **Hyperdescent**: an ∞-topos is hypercomplete exactly when every hypercovering of every slice is effective, and
  X^∧ is the localization of X at the realizations of hypercoverings (HTT, Thm. 6.5.3.12, Cor. 6.5.3.13, p. 698).
- An ∞-topos locally of finite homotopy dimension has convergent Postnikov towers and is hypercomplete (HTT,
  Prop. 7.2.1.10, p. 738; Cor. 7.2.1.12, p. 740).
- Sheaves of spectra on an ∞-topos, which are its spectrum objects by Part StableInfinityCategories §10.1 (SAG,
  Rem. 1.3.2.2, p. 118), agree with spectrum-valued sheaves on any site presenting it (SAG, Prop. 1.3.1.7, p. 117).
- Sheaves of spectra carry a right complete t-structure compatible with filtered colimits, whose heart is the
  abelian group objects of the underlying 1-topos, with t-exact pullback along geometric morphisms (SAG,
  Prop. 1.3.2.7, Rem. 1.3.2.8, pp. 119–120).
- A sheaf of spectra is hypercomplete exactly when its underlying object is, exactly when it receives no nonzero
  map from an ∞-connective sheaf, and hypercompleteness of objects is local (SAG, Props. 1.3.3.3, 1.3.3.6,
  pp. 121–122).
- Stone duality for distributive lattices and Boolean algebras (SAG, Prop. A.1.5.10, p. 1749; Thm. A.1.6.11,
  p. 1751).
- A locally coherent hypercomplete ∞-topos is the hypercomplete sheaf ∞-topos of a finitary site (SAG,
  Thm. A.3.4.1, p. 1770), and the topology generated by covering morphisms has a Čech sheaf criterion (SAG,
  Props. A.3.2.1, A.3.3.1, pp. 1764–1766).
- The **∞-categorical Deligne completeness theorem**: a locally coherent hypercomplete ∞-topos has enough points
  (SAG, Thm. A.4.0.5, p. 1774).
- The Rourke–Sanderson theorem for semisimplicial Kan complexes (SAG, Cor. A.5.3.5, p. 1792); a trivial Kan
  fibration of semisimplicial objects of an ∞-topos realizes to an ∞-connective map when it promotes to a map of
  simplicial objects or its target satisfies the Kan condition (SAG, Thm. A.5.3.1, p. 1791); the hypercomplete
  sheaf criterion (SAG, Prop. A.5.7.2, p. 1806).
- Postnikov complete ∞-topoi are hypercomplete, and ∞-topoi locally of finite homotopy dimension are Postnikov
  complete (SAG, Def. A.7.2.1, p. 1828; Rem. A.7.2.3, p. 1829; HTT, Prop. 7.2.1.10, p. 738).

- The description of 𝒞-valued sheaves as a tensor product of presentable ∞-categories (SAG,
  Rem. 1.3.1.6, p. 117) needs the symmetric monoidal structure on presentable ∞-categories and is

  AdicEtaleGeometry §A1.15 (*coherent-topos-enough-points*); this Part proves the ∞-categorical
  theorem and recovers the classical one only as a remark.

## Conventions

Locators such as (HTT, Prop. 5.2.7.8, p. 385) refer to the editions listed under [References](#references).

Every statement to prove, define or construct carries a label `X.Y.Z` — its section, then its position within that
section — and a single labeled statement, cited as `§2.1.2` here and `Part HypercompletenessAndCoherence §2.1.2`
elsewhere, is one target.

- **∞-categories.** An ∞-category is a quasicategory, Mathlib's `SSet.Quasicategory`. Functor ∞-categories are
  internal Homs, written Fun(C, D). A full subcategory is a full simplicial subset spanned by a set of vertices.
  Witness: the nerve of an ordinary category is an ∞-category (Mathlib instance
  `CategoryTheory.Nerve.quasicategory`), and the horn Λ[2,1] is not one: its identity map is an inner horn with
  no filler, since Λ[2,1] has no 2-simplex with the required boundary.
- **Size.** Small means small for the fixed universe of Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §1.1. ∞-topoi and presentable
  ∞-categories are large and locally small; `RTop` and `LTop` are subcategories of the ∞-category Ĉat∞ of large
  ∞-categories, Tau Ceti `SSet.catInfty` one universe up (Part InfinityTopoi §10.3). Witness: the ∞-category S
  of spaces is not small, but each mapping space of S is a small Kan complex.
- **∞-topoi and presentations.** An ∞-topos is an ∞-category satisfying Giraud's axioms (Part InfinityTopoi
  §3.1; HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6 (3), p. 547). Letters X, Y and script letters 𝒳, 𝒴 alike denote ∞-topoi. By Giraud's
  theorem (Part InfinityTopoi §4.4; HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6), every ∞-topos X admits a left exact presentation: an
  accessible left exact localization L : P(C) → X with a fully faithful right adjoint, for a small ∞-category C.
  Several routes below reduce through such a presentation to presheaves and then, objectwise, to spaces.
  Witness: for X = S one may take C = Δ[0] and L the identity.
- **Geometric morphisms.** A geometric morphism from Y to X is a functor f_* : Y → X with a left exact left adjoint
  f^* : X → Y (Part InfinityTopoi §10.1; HTT, Def. 6.3.1.1, p. 617). This roadmap keeps that direction throughout, as
  Part InfinityTopoi §10.1.1 fixes it: a geometric morphism goes from Y to X, and where SAG writes its pullback the
  text says "the pullback `f^* : X → Y` of a geometric morphism from Y to X". A point of X is a geometric morphism
  from S to X (Part InfinityTopoi §10.6; HTT, Rem. 6.5.4.7, p. 703), named there p_* : S → X; so SAG's "geometric
  morphism f^* : X → S" is a point of X in that sense. SAG's `∞Top` is `RTop` of Part InfinityTopoi §10.3. Witnesses:
  the constant presheaf functor S → P(C) is the pullback of the global sections geometric morphism from P(C) to S; for
  a space T and t ∈ T, the point of Shv(T) at t is the stalk functor F ↦ F_t from Shv(T) to S, and the pushforward
  along {t} → T goes the other way. Swapping the two would make a point a functor S → Shv(T) that preserves limits but
  not colimits.
- **Truncation indices.** Truncation runs from −2: an object is (−2)-truncated when it is final, (−1)-truncated
  objects are the subterminal objects, and a morphism is (−1)-truncated exactly when it is a monomorphism. τ≤k
  is the left adjoint of the inclusion of k-truncated objects. Witness: τ≤0 S is the nerve of the category of
  small sets, and τ≤−1 S is Δ[1].
- **Connectivity indices.** A morphism is n-connective when it is an effective epimorphism and its homotopy
  sheaves vanish in degrees below n (Part InfinityTopoi §12.1; HTT, Def. 6.5.1.10, p. 679); every morphism is
  (−1)-connective and the 0-connective morphisms are the effective epimorphisms. This is one more than the
  classical index: an n-connective space is a classically (n − 1)-connected one. Witness: in S, ∂Δ[1] → Δ[0] is
  0-connective and not 1-connective, since ∂Δ[1] is not connected; the circle ∂Δ[2] → Δ[0] is 1-connective and
  not 2-connective, since π₁ of the circle is ℤ.
- **Slices, discrete objects and the hypercompletion.** X/U is the slice of X over U, written `X_{/U}` in code
  spans. Disc(X/U) is the full subcategory of discrete (0-truncated) objects of X/U, equivalent to the nerve of
  an ordinary category (Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §10.6; HTT, Prop. 2.3.4.18). Homotopy sheaves πₙ(f) of a morphism f
  : U → V are objects of Disc(X/U) (Part InfinityTopoi §12.1; HTT, Def. 6.5.1.1). 𝒳♥ = τ≤0 𝒳 is the underlying
  1-topos, equivalent to the nerve of Disc(𝒳), and Ab(Disc(𝒳)) is its category of commutative group objects,
  Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.CommGrp` for the Cartesian monoidal structure
  `CategoryTheory.CartesianMonoidalCategory.ofHasFiniteProducts` with its braiding
  `CategoryTheory.BraidedCategory.ofCartesianMonoidalCategory`, the only one. X^∧ ⊆ X is the full subcategory of
  hypercomplete objects (§1.1), written X^hyp in SAG, with left adjoint L : X → X^∧ of the inclusion. Witness:
  for X = S, Disc(S) is the category of small sets, Ab(Disc(S)) is the category of abelian groups, S^∧ = S, and
  π₀ of U → Δ[0] is the constant family of sets π₀U over U.
- **Spectra, shifts and levels.** Sp is the ∞-category of spectra (Part StableInfinityCategories §7.6; HA, Def.
  1.4.3.1), defined there as Sp(S∗); this Part uses the equivalent model Sp(S), through the forgetful
  equivalence Sp(S∗) ≃ Sp(S) (Part StableInfinityCategories §7.3; HA, Rem. 1.4.2.18). Sp(𝒞) = Exc∗(S^fin_∗, 𝒞)
  is the ∞-category of spectrum objects of an ∞-category 𝒞 with finite limits (Part StableInfinityCategories
  §7.2; HA, Def. 1.4.2.8). F[1] = ΣF, F[−1] = ΩF, and Ω^(∞−n) = Ω^∞ ∘ [n] for n ∈ ℤ, so that Ω^(∞+n) is Ω^∞ ∘
  [−n]. Witness: Ω^(∞−1) F ≃ Ω^∞(ΣF), and its loop object is Ω^∞ F.
- **t-structures.** t-structures are homological (Part StableInfinityCategories §3.1; HA, Def. 1.2.1.4): C≥n is
  closed under [1], C♥ = C≥0 ∩ C≤0 and πₙ X = π₀(X[−n]). Witness: in Sp, Σ𝕊 lies in Sp≥1 and π₁(Σ𝕊) ≅ ℤ.
- **Lattices.** SAG's lattices have all finite meets and joins, including the empty ones. A lattice is a Mathlib
  `Lattice` with a `BoundedOrder`, a distributive lattice is a `DistribLattice` with a `BoundedOrder`, a lattice
  homomorphism is a Mathlib `BoundedLatticeHom`, SAG's category Lat is Mathlib's `BddDistLat` and SAG's BAlg is
  `BoolAlg`. An upper semilattice is a `SemilatticeSup` with an `OrderBot`. Witness: the map {0} → {0 < 1}
  sending 0 to 1 preserves binary meets and joins (it is a Mathlib `LatticeHom`) but not ⊥; the inverse image of
  the prime ideal {0} under it is empty, which is not an ideal, so Spec would not be functorial for unbounded
  homomorphisms.
- **Ideals and filters.** SAG's ideals of an upper semilattice are Mathlib's `Order.Ideal`, its filters are
  `Order.PFilter`, and its prime ideals are `Order.Ideal.IsPrime`. Witness: an SAG ideal contains ⊥ (the empty
  join) and is closed under binary joins, a Mathlib ideal is a nonempty directed lower set, and in an upper
  semilattice these agree; in {0 < 1} the ideals are {0} and {0, 1}, and only {0} is prime, because a filter is
  nonempty.
- **Coherent spaces.** A coherent topological space (SAG, §A.1, p. 1738) is sober, has a basis of quasi-compact
  open sets, and is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. This is Mathlib's `SpectralSpace`: SAG's "sober" includes
  uniqueness of generic points, which is `QuasiSober` together with `T0Space`. A quasi-compact continuous map of
  coherent spaces (SAG, Def. A.1.5.9, p. 1748) is a Mathlib `IsSpectralMap`. Witness: the Sierpiński space is
  spectral; an infinite set with the cofinite topology is quasi-compact and T1 but not sober, since the whole
  space is irreducible with no generic point.
- **Semisimplicial objects.** These are the semisimplicial objects of Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §9.5 (HTT,
  Not. 6.5.3.6, p. 694; SAG, Not. A.5.1.1, p. 1785): maps from the nerve of `SemiSimplexCategoryᵒᵖ` to C, with
  Δ_s Mathlib's `SemiSimplexCategory` and `SemiSimplexCategory.toSimplexCategory` its inclusion into
  `SimplexCategory`. Witness: there is no morphism from [1] to [0] in Δ_s, so a semisimplicial set has no
  degeneracies; the semisimplicial set with one vertex and no simplices of positive dimension is not the
  underlying semisimplicial set of any simplicial set, because a simplicial set with a vertex also has the
  degenerate edge on it.
- **n-coherence indexing.** "0-coherent" means quasi-compact, and coherence conditions are indexed by n ≥ 0.
  Witness: an object of S is 0-coherent exactly when it has finitely many components, and the final object Δ[0]
  is n-coherent for every n.

## How to read the build

The Part has three strands, each in dependency order. Layers 1–3 are unstable and work in a single
∞-topos: Layer 1 constructs the hypercompletion as the localization at the ∞-connective morphisms,
with its universal property and cotopological localizations; Layer 2 characterizes it by
hyperdescent along simplicial hypercoverings; Layer 3 introduces homotopy dimension and proves the
convergence of Postnikov towers and Jardine's hypercompleteness theorem. Layers 4–6 put stable
coefficients on an ∞-topos: Layer 4 defines 𝒞-valued sheaves and identifies sheaves of spectra with
spectrum objects, Layer 5 builds their t-structure, whose heart is computed by the classification of
Eilenberg–MacLane objects, and Layer 6 compares sheaves of spectra on X and on X^∧. Layers 7–12 are
finiteness theory: Layer 7 is classical Stone duality and uses no ∞-categories; Layer 8 defines
coherence and shows it survives hypercompletion; Layer 9 relates locally coherent hypercomplete
∞-topoi to finitary sites; Layer 10 proves Deligne completeness, using the Boolean part of Layer 7
and the hypercompleteness theorem of Layer 3; Layer 11 studies semisimplicial hypercoverings and
uses Layer 10 to reduce descent statements to spaces; Layer 12 records Postnikov complete ∞-topoi.

## Layer 1: The hypercompletion

### 1.1 Hypercomplete objects and ∞-topoi

**1.1.1.** Define an object U of an ∞-topos X to be **hypercomplete** when it is local (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §1.2) for the
class of ∞-connective morphisms (Part InfinityTopoi §12.1), and write X^∧ ⊆ X for the full subcategory of
hypercomplete objects (HTT, §6.5.2, p. 687, after Prop. 6.5.2.8). Define X to be **hypercomplete** when
X^∧ = X, equivalently when every ∞-connective morphism of X is an equivalence (HTT, §6.5.2, p. 687, after Lemma
6.5.2.10). That X^∧ is a left exact localization of X is §1.2. Prove:

- If f : U → V is ∞-connective and U and V are hypercomplete, then f is an equivalence: locality of U gives
  g : V → U with g ∘ f ≃ id_U, and locality of V, applied to f ∘ g and id_V, gives f ∘ g ≃ id_V. This is the
  equivalence of the two descriptions of a hypercomplete ∞-topos above.

**Checks.**
- Δ[0] is hypercomplete: its only morphism is an identity.
- The class must be the ∞-connective morphisms, not the n-connective ones for some finite n. In `S`, S¹ is
  hypercomplete, since `S` is (next Check), but S¹ is not local for the 1-connective morphism S¹ → ∗:
  Map(∗, S¹) → Map(S¹, S¹) is not an equivalence, because π₀ of the target is ℤ and of the source a point. So
  the variant "local for the 1-connective morphisms" would exclude S¹.
- `S` is hypercomplete, by Whitehead's theorem, while the ∞-topos of sheaves on the Hilbert cube Q is not: the sheaf
  of HTT, Counterexample 6.5.4.2, p. 700, on `Q × [0, 1]` is ∞-connective along `Q × {0}` and has no section near it,
  so its restriction to `Q × {0} ≅ Q` is an ∞-connective sheaf that is not final — a deduction from that example,
  which the source states for proper base change. So the condition is not automatic.

*Needs:* Part PresentableInfinityCategories §1.2.1 (local objects); Part InfinityTopoi §12.1.2 (∞-connective morphisms);
ModelCategoryTheory, Part KanQuillen §1.3.3 (Whitehead's theorem, for the Checks).

### 1.2 ∞-connective morphisms are strongly saturated

**1.2.1.** Prove that, in an ∞-topos X, the ∞-connective morphisms form a strongly saturated class of small
generation (HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.8, p. 686).

The route is Lurie's. The class is closed under colimits in Fun(Δ[1], X) by the accessibility step
below and under pushouts by Part InfinityTopoi §14.5 (HTT, Cor. 6.5.1.17). For two-out-of-three on a
2-simplex exhibiting h ≃ g ∘ f: if f is ∞-connective, Part InfinityTopoi §14.5 (HTT,
Prop. 6.5.1.16 (5)) applies. If g and h are ∞-connective, the long exact sequence of homotopy sheaves
(Part InfinityTopoi §12.3) gives πₙ(f) ≃ ∗ for all n, and it remains that f is an effective
epimorphism. By Part InfinityTopoi §14.4 for n = 0, the 2-simplex is an effective epimorphism of X/Z
exactly when f is one of X, and by Part InfinityTopoi §14.1 in X/Z this reduces to the 0-truncations
of h and g in X/Z being final, which holds by Part InfinityTopoi §14.2 there. Small generation follows
from accessibility.

The accessibility step is that, for 0 ≤ n ≤ ∞, the full subcategory D(n) of Fun(Δ[1], X) spanned by
the n-connective morphisms is accessible and closed under colimits in Fun(Δ[1], X) (HTT,
Lemma 6.5.2.7, p. 685). For n < ∞, let L be the left adjoint of the inclusion of the
(n − 1)-truncated morphisms, given by the reflection steps below. f is n-connective exactly when L f,
the relative (n − 1)-truncation of f, is an equivalence, because an n-connective, (n − 1)-truncated
morphism is an equivalence (Part InfinityTopoi §14.2). The equivalences are closed under colimits
among (n − 1)-truncated morphisms and L preserves colimits, so D(n) is closed under colimits, and it
is the categorical pullback of the equivalences along L, hence accessible. For n = ∞, D(∞) is the
intersection of the D(m) for m < ∞. The reflection steps, each with its locator:

- **Relative localization** (HTT, Lemmas 6.5.2.3, 6.5.2.4, pp. 684–685). Let p : C → D be a
  Cartesian fibration of ∞-categories and C⁰ ⊆ C a full subcategory such that the source of every
  p-Cartesian morphism with target in C⁰ lies in C⁰. A morphism of a fiber C_D exhibiting its
  target as a C⁰_D-localization of its source also exhibits it as a C⁰-localization in C: p induces
  a Cartesian fibration of slices under the source, the given morphism is initial in the fiber over
  the identity of D, and initial objects of the fiber over an initial object of the base are initial
  in the total space. Hence, if every fiber C⁰_D is reflective in C_D, then C⁰ is reflective in C.
- **Truncated components** (HTT, Lemma 6.5.2.5, p. 685). For a presentable ∞-category X, an
  accessible ∞-category C and a natural transformation α : F → G between accessible functors
  C → X, the full subcategory of C on the objects at which α is n-truncated is accessible. By
  induction on n: for n = −2 it is the categorical pullback of the equivalences in Fun(Δ[1], X)
  along α, the inclusion of the equivalences being equivalent to the accessible diagonal
  X → Fun(Δ[1], X); for n ≥ −1, apply the inductive hypothesis to the diagonal F → F ×_G F.
- **Truncated morphisms are strongly reflective** (HTT, Lemma 6.5.2.6, p. 685). For a presentable
  ∞-category X and −2 ≤ n < ∞, the n-truncated morphisms span a strongly reflective subcategory of
  Fun(Δ[1], X). Evaluation at the target is a Cartesian fibration with the pullback squares as
  Cartesian morphisms, n-truncated morphisms are stable under pullback, and each fiber X/V has its
  n-truncated objects as a reflective subcategory. So relative localization gives reflectivity and
  the truncated-components step gives accessibility.

Consequently, the hypercomplete objects of §1.1, the local objects for this class,
form an accessible localization X^∧ of X (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §4.4; HTT, Prop. 5.5.4.15). It is left
exact because the class is stable under pullback (Part InfinityTopoi §14.5, HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.16 (6);
Part PresentableInfinityCategories §4.5, HTT, Prop. 6.2.1.1), and so X^∧ is an ∞-topos (HTT, §6.5.2, the discussion
after Prop. 6.5.2.8, p. 687).

### 1.3 The universal property of the hypercompletion

**1.3.1.** Prove that, for ∞-topoi X and Y with Y hypercomplete, composition with the inclusion X^∧ ⊆ X
induces an isomorphism of simplicial sets Fun_*(Y, X^∧) → Fun_*(Y, X) (HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.13,
p. 687).

The route is Lurie's. Since X^∧ ⊆ X is a full subcategory, it suffices that every geometric morphism
f_* : Y → X factors through X^∧. That is, f^* carries each ∞-connective morphism u of X to an
equivalence. f^*(u) is ∞-connective by Part InfinityTopoi §14.5 (HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.16 (4)), and Y is
hypercomplete.

### 1.4 Cotopological localizations

**1.4.1.** Prove that, for ∞-topoi X and Y and a left exact colimit-preserving functor f^* : X → Y, the
following are equivalent (HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.16, p. 689):

1. for every monomorphism u of X, if f^* u is an equivalence, then u is an equivalence;
2. for every morphism u of X, if f^* u is an equivalence, then u is ∞-connective.

The route is Lurie's. (2) ⇒ (1): an ∞-connective monomorphism is a monomorphism and an effective
epimorphism, hence an equivalence. (1) ⇒ (2) is an induction on the connectivity of u. For degree 0,
factor u as an effective epimorphism followed by a monomorphism u″ (Part InfinityTopoi §7.3). f^* u″ is
an effective epimorphism, by the behavior of effective epimorphisms under composition (Part
InfinityTopoi §7.4), and a monomorphism, because f^* is left exact. So it is an equivalence, hence so
is u″, and u is an effective epimorphism. For the inductive step, apply the hypothesis to the diagonal
of u, which f^* also carries to an equivalence, and use Part InfinityTopoi §14.4.

**1.4.2.** Define an accessible left exact localization Y ⊆ X of an ∞-topos to be cotopological when the left
adjoint L : X → Y of the inclusion satisfies these equivalent conditions (HTT, Def. 6.5.2.17,
p. 690).

- A cotopological localization inverts only ∞-connective morphisms.
- The hypercompletion X^∧ ⊆ X is cotopological, because its localization inverts exactly the
  ∞-connective morphisms (§1.2).
- For f^* satisfying the conditions, a morphism u is an effective epimorphism exactly when f^*(u) is
  one, and X^∧ is the maximal cotopological localization of X: every cotopological localization of X
  contains X^∧ (HTT, Remark 6.5.2.18 and the paragraph after it, p. 690). For the first claim, factor
  u as an effective epimorphism followed by a monomorphism u″, which is an equivalence exactly when
  f^*(u″) is one. For the second, the class S_L of morphisms inverted by a cotopological L consists of
  ∞-connective morphisms, and the L-local objects are the S_L-local objects. A hypercomplete object
  is local for every ∞-connective morphism, hence S_L-local.

**Checks.**

- The identity localization X ⊆ X is cotopological, and also topological.
- The localization of a nontrivial ∞-topos X onto its final object, Δ[0] ⊆ X, is topological,
  generated by the monomorphism ∅ → 1. It is not cotopological, because it inverts that monomorphism,
  which is not an equivalence.
- S is hypercomplete by Whitehead's theorem for Kan complexes, so its only cotopological
  localization is S itself. A definition requiring L to reflect all equivalences would admit only
  identity localizations, and so would exclude the hypercompletion of every ∞-topos that is not
  hypercomplete.

### 1.5 Factoring left exact localizations

**1.5.1.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos X and an accessible left exact localization X″ ⊆ X, there is a
topological localization X′ ⊆ X such that X″ ⊆ X′ is a cotopological localization of X′ (HTT,
Prop. 6.5.2.19, p. 690).

The route is Lurie's. Let S be the monomorphisms of X inverted by the localization L onto X″, and X′
the S-local objects. S is stable under pullback because L is left exact. The strongly saturated class
generated by S is then topological, and of small generation, a point the source leaves implicit: X
has universal colimits, so the members of S with target in a small generating set of objects form a
small set, because the subobjects of an object of a presentable ∞-category form a small set (Part
InfinityTopoi §6.1; HTT, Prop. 6.2.1.3), and that set generates the same class (Part InfinityTopoi
§6.2; HTT, Prop. 6.2.1.5). Hence X′ is a
topological localization, and it contains X″. A monomorphism of X′ inverted by L lies in S and is
between S-local objects, hence an equivalence. So X″ ⊆ X′ is cotopological.

## Layer 2: Hypercoverings and hyperdescent

### 2.1 Coskeleta

**2.1.1.** Define, for n ≥ −1 and a presentable ∞-category X, the n-coskeleton functor coskₙ as the composite
of restriction from simplicial objects of X to functors N(Δ≤n)^op → X with its right adjoint, right
Kan extension along N(Δ≤n)^op ⊆ N(Δ)^op. Here Δ≤n is the full subcategory of Δ on [0], …, [n], and
Δ≤−1 is empty (HTT, Notation 6.5.3.1, p. 693; Lurie writes n ≥ 0, and the case n = −1 gives the
hypercovering condition in degree 0).

**2.1.2.** Define a simplicial object U of an ∞-category X to be n-coskeletal when it is a right Kan extension
of its restriction to N(Δ≤n)^op. A semisimplicial object is n-coskeletal when it is a right Kan
extension of its restriction to the opposite of the nerve of Δ_s ×_Δ Δ≤n, where Δ_s is the
subcategory of injective maps. By cofinality of N(Δ_s)^op ⊆ N(Δ)^op, colimits of a simplicial object
and of its underlying semisimplicial object agree (HTT, the definitions after Lemma 6.5.3.7, p. 694).

- There is a unit map U → coskₙ U, and (coskₙ U)_k ≃ U_k for k ≤ n.
- coskₙ preserves small limits, being a composite of right adjoints.
- When X is presentable, U is n-coskeletal exactly when U → coskₙ U is an equivalence, and an
  n-coskeletal object is m-coskeletal for every m ≥ n.

**Checks.**

- cosk₋₁ U is the constant simplicial object on a final object, since a right Kan extension from the
  empty category takes final values.
- (cosk₀ U)ₘ is the (m + 1)-fold power of U₀, so cosk₀ U is the Čech nerve of U₀ → 1, which is
  0-coskeletal. Using left Kan extension (skeleta) instead would give the constant object on U₀.
- The constant simplicial object on an object V is 0-coskeletal exactly when V is (−1)-truncated: its
  first term V must be V × V, the diagonal being an equivalence. In S, the constant object on ∂Δ[1]
  is not 0-coskeletal.
- The nerve of the monoid (ℕ, +), regarded as a simplicial object of the nerve of the category of
  sets, is 2-coskeletal but not 1-coskeletal. (cosk₁ U)₂ consists of all triples of edges, while the
  2-simplices are the composable pairs with their composite. This separates the indices 1 and 2.

### 2.2 Hypercoverings

**2.2.1.** Define a hypercovering of an ∞-topos X to be a simplicial object U of X such that, for every n ≥ 0,
the unit map Uₙ → (coskₙ₋₁ U)ₙ is an effective epimorphism. U is an effective hypercovering when its
colimit is a final object of X (HTT, Def. 6.5.3.2, p. 693).

- For n = 0 the condition says that U₀ → 1 is an effective epimorphism, and for n = 1 that
  U₁ → U₀ × U₀ is.
- Hypercoverings are stable under equivalence of simplicial objects and under left exact
  colimit-preserving functors between ∞-topoi, since these commute with coskeleta and preserve
  effective epimorphisms.
- For an object V, a hypercovering of the ∞-topos X/V (Part InfinityTopoi §10.4) is a simplicial
  object of X over V whose matching maps are effective epimorphisms of X/V, equivalently of X (Part
  InfinityTopoi §14.4). Its colimit, computed in X, comes with a map to V, written |U| → V.

**Checks.**

- The Čech nerve of an effective epimorphism U → 1 is a hypercovering: for n ≥ 1 the maps
  Uₙ → (coskₙ₋₁ U)ₙ are equivalences. It is effective.
- In a nontrivial ∞-topos, the constant simplicial object on ∅ is not a hypercovering, because
  ∅ → 1 is not an effective epimorphism.
- In S, the constant simplicial object on the circle K = ∂Δ[2] satisfies the conditions for n = 0
  and n = 1, since K and K × K are connected. It fails for n = 2. (cosk₁ K)₂ is the cotensor of K by
  ∂Δ[2], the free loop space, with π₀ ≅ ℤ, and K hits only one component. So asking only that each
  Uₙ → 1 be an effective epimorphism would be the wrong definition.

### 2.4 Realizations of hypercoverings are ∞-connective

**2.4.1.** Prove that, for a hypercovering U of an ∞-topos X, the colimit of U is an ∞-connective object (HTT,
Lemma 6.5.3.11, p. 698).

The route is Lurie's. For each n ≥ 0, the map from U to coskₙ₊₁ U is an equivalence in degrees
≤ n + 1, so the induced map of colimits is n-connective by the connectivity step below. coskₙ₊₁ U is
an (n + 1)-coskeletal hypercovering, so its colimit is final by the effectivity step. Hence the
colimit of U is n-connective for every n, and ∞-connective by Part InfinityTopoi §14.5 (HTT,
Prop. 6.5.1.16 (1)). The steps, each with its locator:

- **Coskeletality is semisimplicial** (HTT, Lemma 6.5.3.8, p. 695). A simplicial object of an
  ∞-category is n-coskeletal exactly when its underlying semisimplicial object is. For each [m], the
  inclusion of the category of injections into [m] from objects [m″] with m″ ≤ n into the category of
  all such maps is cofinal. By Theorem A it suffices that, for each map θ : [m′] → [m], the category
  of factorizations of θ through an injection from some [m″] with m″ ≤ n has weakly contractible
  nerve; it has an initial object, the factorization through the image.
- **Coskeletal hypercoverings are effective** (HTT, Lemma 6.5.3.9, p. 695, after
  Dugger–Hollander–Isaksen). In an ∞-topos every n-coskeletal hypercovering U is effective. For
  n = 0, U is the Čech nerve of the effective epimorphism U₀ → 1, whose colimit is final. For n > 0,
  let V = coskₙ₋₁ U, an (n − 1)-coskeletal hypercovering, effective by induction, and let W⁺ be a
  Čech nerve of U → V in simplicial objects, with diagonal D of the bisimplicial object W = W⁺
  restricted to N(Δ × Δ)^op. Then two statements finish the induction, since together with the
  agreement of simplicial and semisimplicial colimits (§2.1) they show that the colimit of U is a
  retract of a final object, hence final.
  - Every colimit of D is final (HTT, Lemma 6.5.3.9, first half of the inductive step,
    pp. 695–696). Each Uₘ → Vₘ is a finite composite of pullbacks of Uₙ → Vₙ, hence an effective
    epimorphism, so the columns of W⁺ are colimit diagrams. W⁺ is therefore a left Kan extension of
    W, and colimits of W are colimits of the augmentation column V, which are final. The diagonal
    N(Δ)^op → N(Δ × Δ)^op is cofinal because N(Δ)^op is sifted.
  - The semisimplicial object underlying U is a retract of the one underlying D (HTT,
    Lemma 6.5.3.9, second half of the inductive step, pp. 696–697). Both are n-coskeletal as
    semisimplicial objects, by the previous step, so it suffices to work in degrees ≤ n. In degrees
    below n the maps Uₘ → Vₘ are equivalences, so both restrictions are determined, over the
    restriction of V to degrees below n, by their degree-n terms. There the degree-n term of D is
    the (n + 1)-fold fiber power of Uₙ over Vₙ, the map from U is the diagonal, and any of the n + 1
    projections is a left inverse.
- **Connectivity of realizations** (HTT, Lemma 6.5.3.10, p. 697). For a map f : U → V of simplicial
  objects in X such that f_k is an equivalence for every k ≤ n, the induced map of colimits is
  n-connective. Choose a left exact presentation L : P(C) → X and lift f to a map of simplicial
  presheaves that is an equivalence in degrees ≤ n. L preserves colimits and n-connectivity (Part
  InfinityTopoi §14.5 (4)), and in P(C) connectivity is tested objectwise (Part InfinityTopoi §14.2
  and the commutation of truncation with evaluation), which reduces to X = S. There, represent f by
  a map of projectively cofibrant simplicial diagrams of Kan complexes and compute colimits as
  homotopy colimits. The homotopy colimit of a simplicial diagram of Kan complexes is the diagonal of
  a bisimplicial set, and a map of bisimplicial sets whose k-th column is (n − k)-connective has
  n-connective diagonal. Here the columns in degrees ≤ n are weak equivalences, and every map is
  (n − k)-connective for k > n.

### 2.5 Hyperdescent

**2.5.1.** Prove that an ∞-topos X is hypercomplete exactly when, for every object V of X, every hypercovering
of X/V is effective (HTT, Thm. 6.5.3.12, p. 698).

The route is Lurie's. If every such hypercovering is effective, let f : U → V be ∞-connective. The
constant simplicial object of X/V on f is a hypercovering by §2.3, so f, its colimit, is final in
X/V, that is, an equivalence. Conversely, if X is hypercomplete, the colimit of a hypercovering of
X/V is ∞-connective in X/V by §2.4, X/V being an ∞-topos (Part InfinityTopoi §10.4), and hence final.

## Layer 3: Homotopy dimension

### 3.1 Homotopy dimension

**3.1.1.** Define, for an integer n ≥ −1, an ∞-topos X to have homotopy dimension ≤ n when every n-connective
object U of X admits a global section 1 → U. X has finite homotopy dimension when it has homotopy
dimension ≤ n for some n ≥ 0 (HTT, Def. 7.2.1.1, p. 735).

- Homotopy dimension ≤ n implies homotopy dimension ≤ m for every m ≥ n, since m-connective objects
  are n-connective.
- The property is invariant under equivalence of ∞-topoi.
- If X is a coproduct in `RTop` of ∞-topoi X_α, then X has homotopy dimension ≤ n exactly when every
  X_α does (HTT, Remark 7.2.1.5, p. 736). The product ∏ X_α of Part InfinityTopoi §11.1 is a
  coproduct in `RTop`: passing to left adjoints identifies Fun_*(∏ X_α, Y) with ∏ Fun_*(X_α, Y),
  because Fun^*(Y, ∏ X_α) is ∏ Fun^*(Y, X_α). In the product, connectivity, final objects and global
  sections are computed componentwise.

**Checks.**

- S has homotopy dimension ≤ 0, since a nonempty Kan complex has a vertex. So does S × S,
  componentwise.
- Homotopy dimension ≤ −1 means that every object, including ∅, has a global section. So only Δ[0],
  up to equivalence, has homotopy dimension ≤ −1. A definition with "n-connective" read classically
  would shift this.
- The ∞-topos Fun(Bℤ, S) of spaces with an action of ℤ does not have homotopy dimension ≤ 0. The
  free ℤ-set ℤ is 0-connective, being nonempty, but its global sections are its homotopy fixed
  points, which are empty. A definition through points instead of global sections would accept it,
  since evaluation at the base point is a point.

### 3.2 Relative homotopy dimension

**3.2.1.** Define a geometric morphism from X to Y, with direct image `f_* : X → Y`, to have homotopy dimension ≤ n
when, for every k ≥ n and every k-connective morphism U → U′ of X, the morphism f_* U → f_* U′ is (k − n)-connective
in Y. The condition does not depend on the choice of f_* within its equivalence class (HTT, Def. 7.2.1.6, p. 737).

- Homotopy dimension ≤ n implies ≤ m for every m ≥ n.
- Equivalences of ∞-topoi have homotopy dimension ≤ 0.

**Checks.**

- The identity of S has homotopy dimension ≤ 0, since it carries k-connective morphisms to
  k-connective ones.
- The geometric morphism Δ[0] → X picking a final object has homotopy dimension ≤ 0 for every X.
  Every k-connective morphism of Δ[0] is an identity, and it is sent to an identity, which is
  ∞-connective.
- The global sections geometric morphism Fun(Bℤ, S) → S, homotopy fixed points, does not have
  homotopy dimension ≤ 0. It sends the 0-connective morphism ℤ → ∗ to ∅ → ∗, which is not an
  effective epimorphism of S. This agrees with the comparison of §3.2.2 and the third Check of §3.1.

**3.2.2.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos X with geometric morphism F_* : X → S, unique up to equivalence by Part
InfinityTopoi §10.5, X has homotopy dimension ≤ n exactly when F_* does (HTT, Lemma 7.2.1.7, p. 737).

The route is Lurie's. If F_* has homotopy dimension ≤ n, then for an n-connective object U, F_* U is
a 0-connective, that is nonempty, Kan complex. A vertex of it gives, by adjunction, a global section
of U, since F^* preserves the final object. Conversely, assume homotopy dimension ≤ n and let s be
k-connective, with k ≥ n, and argue by induction on k. For k = n one must show that F_* s is
surjective on π₀, that is, that every global section of the target lifts after passing to π₀. The
pullback of s along a global section is n-connective in X, so it has a global section. For k > n,
apply the inductive hypothesis to the diagonal of s (Part InfinityTopoi §14.4).

### 3.3 Local homotopy dimension

**3.3.1.** Define an ∞-topos X to be locally of homotopy dimension ≤ n when there is a collection of objects
{U_α} of X that generates X under colimits and such that every slice X/U_α has homotopy
dimension ≤ n (HTT, Def. 7.2.1.8, p. 738).

- Local homotopy dimension ≤ n implies local homotopy dimension ≤ m for every m ≥ n, with the same
  generating family (§3.1).
- If the final object generates X under colimits, as it does for S, then homotopy dimension ≤ n
  implies local homotopy dimension ≤ n. In general the final object does not generate: in P(N([1]))
  the colimits of the final object are the constant presheaves.
- X/U_α is an ∞-topos (Part InfinityTopoi §10.4), so the condition makes sense.

- S is locally of homotopy dimension ≤ 0, generated by the point.

  dimension ≤ 0, although by §3.1 it does not have homotopy dimension ≤ 0. So local and global
  homotopy dimension differ.

### 3.4 Convergence of Postnikov towers

**3.4.1.** Prove that, in an ∞-topos X locally of homotopy dimension ≤ n for some integer n, Postnikov towers
converge (HTT, Prop. 7.2.1.10, p. 738).

The route is Lurie's. Check the convergence criterion: a tower is a Postnikov tower exactly when it
is a limit diagram whose underlying pretower is a Postnikov pretower. The substantive direction asks
that, for a limit tower whose underlying pretower is highly connected, the map from the limit to each
stage is highly connected. Reindex so that the map from stage p to stage q is (m + q)-connective, and
argue by induction on m. The inductive step passes to the tower of diagonals (Part InfinityTopoi
§14.4). For m = 0, cover the bottom stage by an effective epimorphism from a coproduct of generators.
Lift step by step, using homotopy dimension ≤ n of the slices X/U_α through §3.1 and §3.2. The
resulting map from the coproduct to the bottom stage factors through the limit, so the limit maps by
an effective epimorphism. For the other direction, a Postnikov tower is a limit diagram: the map from
its top object to the limit of its pretower is ∞-connective by the first direction, both towers
having the same truncations. It suffices to test it on maps out of each U_α, that is, after the
global sections functor of X/U_α, which carries it to an ∞-connective map of Kan complexes by §3.2.
That map is an equivalence by Whitehead's theorem.

### 3.5 Local finite homotopy dimension implies hypercompleteness

**3.5.1.** Prove that an ∞-topos X locally of homotopy dimension ≤ n for some integer n is hypercomplete (HTT,
Cor. 7.2.1.12, p. 740, attributed there to Jardine [Jar87]).

The route is Lurie's. Extend an object to a Postnikov tower by the step below. Each stage is
truncated, hence hypercomplete by the second consequence of Part InfinityTopoi §14.2: maps out of an
∞-connective morphism into a truncated object are equivalences. The tower converges (§3.4), so the
object is a limit of hypercomplete objects. X^∧ is closed under limits, being the local objects for a
class of morphisms (§1.2).

- **Postnikov towers exist** (HTT, Lemma 7.2.1.11, p. 739). For a presentable ∞-category X,
  evaluation at ∞, restricted to the full subcategory of Postnikov towers in the ∞-category of towers
  Fun(N(ℤ≥0 ∪ {∞})^op, X), is a trivial fibration onto X; in particular every object extends to a
  Postnikov tower. Let C be the full subcategory of X × N(ℤ≥0 ∪ {∞})^op on the pairs (U, n) with U
  n-truncated. Its projection to N(ℤ≥0 ∪ {∞})^op is a Cartesian fibration, since m-truncated objects
  are n-truncated for m ≤ n, and a coCartesian fibration, because the truncation inclusions have left
  adjoints. Postnikov towers are the coCartesian sections. The inclusion of the cone point is marked
  anodyne, so restriction to it is a trivial fibration of the corresponding mapping spaces of marked
  simplicial sets.

## Layer 4: Sheaves of spectra

### 4.1 Valued sheaves on an ∞-topos

**4.1.1.** Define, for an ∞-topos 𝒳 and an ∞-category 𝒞, a 𝒞-valued sheaf on 𝒳 to be a functor 𝒳^op → 𝒞 that
preserves small limits, that is, carries small colimit diagrams of 𝒳 to limit diagrams of 𝒞, and
Shv_𝒞(𝒳) to be their full subcategory of Fun(𝒳^op, 𝒞) (SAG, Def. 1.3.1.4, p. 116). A sheaf of
spectra on 𝒳 is an Sp-valued sheaf, and Shv_Sp(𝒳) is their ∞-category (SAG, Def. 1.3.2.1, p. 118).

- A functor 𝒞 → 𝒟 that preserves small limits carries 𝒞-valued sheaves to 𝒟-valued sheaves by
  composition.
- If 𝒞 has small limits, Shv_𝒞(𝒳) is closed under small limits in Fun(𝒳^op, 𝒞), which are computed
  pointwise (Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §8.2; HTT, Prop. 5.1.2.2), since limits commute with
  limits.
- For 𝒞 = S, the Yoneda embedding is an equivalence from 𝒳 onto Shv_S(𝒳): a functor 𝒳^op → S on a
  presentable 𝒳 is representable exactly when it preserves small limits (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §2.1;
  HTT, Prop. 5.5.2.2), and the Yoneda embedding is fully faithful (Part PresheavesAndCompletions
  §1.3; HTT, Prop. 5.1.3.1).
- The definition tests all small colimits of 𝒳 and is not the 𝒞-valued sheaf condition of Part
  InfinityTopoi §8.2 for a Grothendieck topology on 𝒳, such as the canonical one; no clash arises,
  since an ∞-topos is essentially small only when it is equivalent to Δ[0] (SAG, Warning 1.3.1.5,
  pp. 116–117).

**Checks.**

- For 𝒳 = Δ[0], the ∞-topos P(∅), the unique object is the colimit of the empty diagram, so a sheaf
  takes a final value: Shv_𝒞(Δ[0]) is the full subcategory of final objects of 𝒞, contractible when
  𝒞 has one (Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §5.5; HTT, Prop. 1.2.12.9) and empty otherwise. Dropping the limit
  condition would give all of 𝒞.
- For 𝒳 = S and 𝒞 with small limits, a sheaf F satisfies F(K) ≃ F(Δ[0])^K, the cotensor, since K is
  the colimit of the constant K-diagram at Δ[0]; so evaluation at Δ[0] identifies Shv_𝒞(S) with 𝒞,
  and F(∂Δ[1]) ≃ F(Δ[0]) × F(Δ[0]). A covariant definition, by limit-preserving functors S → 𝒞,
  forces no such formula, since ∂Δ[1] = Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0] is not a limit of copies of Δ[0] in S: for
  𝒞 = S, the limit-preserving functor Map(∂Δ[1], −) takes the value of four points at ∂Δ[1] and a
  point at Δ[0].
- For 𝒳 = S × S, the presheaf ∞-topos on a two-element set, a sheaf of spectra is determined by its
  values on (Δ[0], ∅) and (∅, Δ[0]), and its value on the initial object (∅, ∅) is the zero spectrum,
  so Shv_Sp(S × S) ≃ Sp × Sp. A definition sending only finite colimits to limits would not reduce
  to these two values, since an infinite coproduct of copies of (Δ[0], ∅) is not a finite colimit of
  them, and it would lose the products F(⊔ᵢ Uᵢ) ≃ ∏ᵢ F(Uᵢ) over infinite families.

**4.1.2.** For 𝒞 = Sp, Shv_Sp(𝒳) ≃ Sp(𝒳) compatibly with Ω^∞, and Shv_Sp(𝒳) is presentable and stable: since
𝒳 is presentable, this is Part StableInfinityCategories §10.1 at 𝒫 = 𝒳 (SAG, Rem. 1.3.2.2, p. 118),
with the left adjoint Σ^∞₊ of Ω^∞ and the description of a sheaf of spectra F by its levels
Ω^(∞−n) F as there. For 𝒳 = S × S both sides are Sp × Sp, matching the third Check.

### 4.2 Valued sheaves on a site

**4.2.1.** Prove that, for a small ∞-category T with a Grothendieck topology, sheafification
L : P(T) → Shv(T), the Yoneda embedding j : T → P(T) and an ∞-category 𝒞 with small limits,
composition with L ∘ j gives an equivalence Shv_𝒞(Shv(T)) → Shv_𝒞(T) (SAG, Prop. 1.3.1.7, p. 117).
The target is the ∞-category of 𝒞-valued sheaves on the site T of Part InfinityTopoi §8.2, the source
that of §4.1 on the ∞-topos Shv(T).

The route is Lurie's. Limit-preserving functors P(T)^op → 𝒞 are the colimit-preserving functors
P(T) → 𝒞^op, and composition with j identifies them with Fun(T^op, 𝒞) (Part PresheavesAndCompletions
§1.4; HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6). Since Shv(T) is the localization of P(T) at the monomorphisms into
representables that correspond to covering sieves (Part InfinityTopoi §8.2, §8.3), composition with L
is fully faithful on colimit-preserving functors out of Shv(T), with essential image those inverting
these monomorphisms (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §4.4; HTT, Prop. 5.5.4.20). Under the correspondence between
sieves and subobjects of representables (Part InfinityTopoi §8.1; HTT, Prop. 6.2.2.5), the subobject
of j(U) attached to a sieve is the colimit of j over the sieve, so inverting it is exactly the sheaf
condition of Part InfinityTopoi §8.2 at that sieve.

- For 𝒞 = Sp this identifies sheaves of spectra on Shv(T) with Sp-valued sheaves on the site T. For
  the trivial topology, Shv(T) = P(T) and Shv_𝒞(P(T)) ≃ Fun(T^op, 𝒞).

**Checks.**

- The hypothesis on 𝒞 is needed: for 𝒞 = Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0], with no final object, and T = Δ[0] with the
  trivial topology, Shv_𝒞(T) = Fun(Δ[0], 𝒞) has two objects, while Shv_𝒞(Shv(T)) = Shv_𝒞(S) is
  empty, because a sheaf on S must send ∅ to a final object.
- For T = Δ[0] with the empty sieve covering, Shv(T) ≃ Δ[0] and both sides are the final objects of
  𝒞 (§4.1 and Part InfinityTopoi §8.2).
- For T discrete on two objects with the trivial topology, both sides are 𝒞 × 𝒞.

### 4.3 Pushforward and pullback of sheaves of spectra

**4.3.1.** Prove that, for a geometric morphism g_* : 𝒴 → 𝒳 with left adjoint g^*, the functor
g^* : Shv_Sp(𝒳) → Shv_Sp(𝒴) obtained from Sp(g^*), composition of spectrum objects with the left
exact g^*, through §4.1, is left adjoint to the pushforward g_* : Shv_Sp(𝒴) → Shv_Sp(𝒳),
F ↦ F ∘ (g^*)^op (SAG, Rem. 1.3.2.8, first part, p. 120).

The pushforward lands in sheaves because g^* preserves small colimits. Under §4.1 it is Sp(g_*),
since Map(g^*U, V) ≃ Map(U, g_*V) sends the representable sheaf of V on 𝒴 to that of g_*V on 𝒳.
Both g^* and g_* are left exact, so composition with them preserves reduced excisive functors. The
unit of g^* ⊣ g_*, composed pointwise, is a unit transformation for the induced functors of spectrum
objects, which therefore form an adjunction (Part AdjunctionsAndLocalizations §1.3; HTT,
Prop. 5.2.2.8).

- Both functors are exact: g_* preserves limits, and g^* is a left adjoint between stable
  ∞-categories (Part StableInfinityCategories §2.3).
- If g_* is fully faithful, so is the pushforward of sheaves of spectra, by composition with a fully
  faithful functor.

**Checks.**

- For the geometric morphism from P(C) to S, with g^* the constant presheaf functor, the pushforward
  sends a sheaf of spectra F, a functor C^op → Sp by §4.2, to its value F(1) at the final presheaf,
  the limit of F over C^op. Composing a sheaf on 𝒳 with g_* does not give a sheaf on 𝒴, because g_*
  need not preserve colimits: for 𝒴 = S × S over S, g_*(U, V) = U × V sends the coproduct
  (Δ[0], ∅) ⊔ (∅, Δ[0]) to Δ[0], not to ∅ (Part InfinityTopoi §10.1).
- For 𝒳 = 𝒴 and g the identity, both functors are the identity.
- For 𝒴 = Δ[0] and g_* the geometric morphism picking a final object of 𝒳, Shv_Sp(Δ[0]) ≃ Δ[0], the
  pushforward picks the zero sheaf of spectra on 𝒳, and g^* is the unique functor to Δ[0]; the
  adjunction holds because the zero sheaf is final. A pushforward that kept a nonzero value would
  contradict Map(g^*F, 0) ≃ ∗.

### 5.1 Homotopy sheaves of sheaves of spectra

### 5.2 Truncated and connective sheaves of spectra

**5.2.1.** Define a sheaf of spectra F on an ∞-topos 𝒳 to be n-truncated when Ω^(∞+n) F, that is Ω^∞(F[−n]),
is a discrete object of 𝒳, and n-connective when πₘ F ≃ 0 for all m < n; connective means
0-connective, equivalently Ω^(∞−m) F is an m-connective object of 𝒳 for every m ≥ 0. Shv_Sp(𝒳)≥n and
Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤n are the full subcategories of n-connective and n-truncated objects (SAG, Def. 1.3.2.5,
pp. 118–119).

- F is n-truncated exactly when the spectrum F(U) is n-truncated for every object U of 𝒳 (SAG,
  Rem. 1.3.2.6, p. 119): Ω^∞(F[−n]) is discrete exactly when each mapping space
  Map(U, Ω^∞(F[−n])) ≃ Ω^∞(F(U)[−n]) is, and a spectrum has discrete Ω^∞ exactly when it lies in
  Sp≤0 (Part StableInfinityCategories §11.3).
- Truncatedness is pointwise; connectivity is not (second Check).

**Checks.**

- For 𝒳 = S, these are Sp≥n and Sp≤n of Part StableInfinityCategories §11.3.
- Connectivity is not tested on sections. Let 𝒳 = P(Bℤ), presheaves on the nerve of the one-object
  category ℤ, so Shv_Sp(𝒳) is Fun(Bℤ^op, Sp), spectra with a ℤ-action (§4.2). The sheaf Hℤ with
  trivial action is connective, in the heart. Its value at the final object, the limit over Bℤ^op,
  is the fiber of the zero map Hℤ → Hℤ, that is Hℤ ⊕ Hℤ[−1], with π₋₁ ≅ ℤ. A definition of
  connectivity by connectivity of every F(U) would reject it.
- The index runs the right way: Hℤ[1] on S is 1-connective and 1-truncated, and not 0-truncated,
  since Ω^∞(Hℤ[1]) is the circle, which is not discrete.

### 5.3 The t-structure

**5.3.1.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos 𝒳 (SAG, Prop. 1.3.2.7, pp. 119–120):

1. (Shv_Sp(𝒳)≥0, Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0) is a t-structure on Shv_Sp(𝒳);
2. it is compatible with filtered colimits: Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0 is closed under filtered colimits;
3. it is right complete;
4. π₀ restricts to an equivalence from the heart Shv_Sp(𝒳)♥ onto the nerve of Ab(Disc(𝒳)).

The route is Lurie's, through §4.1.

- *(1).* The t-structure on spectrum objects of the presentable 𝒳 (Part StableInfinityCategories
  §11.3; HA, Prop. 1.4.3.4) has Sp(𝒳)≤−1 the F with Ω^∞ F final, so after a shift its coconnective
  part is the F with Ω^∞(F[−1]) ≃ ΩΩ^∞ F final. This is Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0: Ω^∞ F ≃ ΩΩ^(∞−1) F is a loop
  object, so its diagonal is a base change of the unit 1 → Ω^∞ F, and Ω^∞ F is discrete exactly when
  that unit is (−1)-truncated (Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §10.7; HTT, Ex. 5.5.6.13). The unit is
  (−1)-truncated exactly when its own diagonal, `1 → 1 ×_{Ω^∞ F} 1 = ΩΩ^∞ F`, is an equivalence, that
  is, when ΩΩ^∞ F is final. Its connective part is the class C of F whose mapping spaces into G[−1]
  are contractible for every G in Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0. Writing F as the levels F(n) = Ω^(∞−n) F with F(n) ≃
  ΩF(n + 1) (§4.1), the truncations F′(n) = τ≤n−1 F(n) again form a spectrum object F′, because
  truncation of pointed objects commutes with loops with a shift of one (Part InfinityTopoi §12.3,
  through the diagonal). Maps out of F into such targets factor through F → F′, so F lies in C
  exactly when F′ ≃ 0, that is, when every F(n) is n-connective (Part InfinityTopoi §14.2). This is
  Shv_Sp(𝒳)≥0.
- *(2).* Filtered colimits in 𝒳 are left exact (Part InfinityTopoi §10.7; HTT, Ex. 7.3.4.7), and
  filtered colimits of pointed objects are computed in 𝒳 (Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §8.3, with Part
  PresheavesAndCompletions §4.3; HTT, Lemma 5.3.1.20). So Ω on 𝒳_* preserves filtered colimits, and
  so does each Ω^(∞+1) : Shv_Sp(𝒳) → 𝒳_*. Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0 is the fiber of Ω^(∞+1) over the zero object,
  hence closed under filtered colimits.
- *(3).* Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤0 is closed under countable coproducts, which are filtered colimits of finite
  coproducts, and finite coproducts are finite products in the stable Shv_Sp(𝒳) (Part
  StableInfinityCategories §1.5). An object of every Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤−n has all levels final, so it is
  zero. The dual of the completeness criterion (Part StableInfinityCategories §8.1; HA,
  Rem. 1.2.1.20) applies.
- *(4).* Shv_Sp(𝒳) is the limit of the tower ⋯ → 𝒳_* → 𝒳_* of loop functors (Part
  StableInfinityCategories §7.5; HA, Rem. 1.4.2.25). F lies in the heart exactly when each level F(n)
  is n-truncated and n-connective, so the heart is the limit of the tower
  ⋯ → EM₂(𝒳) → EM₁(𝒳) → EM₀(𝒳) of loop functors, EMₙ(𝒳) being the Eilenberg–MacLane objects of
  Part InfinityTopoi §13.6. By the classification of Part InfinityTopoi §15.2 this tower is constant,
  up to equivalence, at the nerve of Ab(Disc(𝒳)) from degree 2 on, and its limit is that nerve (Part
  Straightening §3.2; HTT, Prop. 3.3.3.1); the projection is πₙ of the n-th level, which is π₀ F.

### 5.4 t-exactness of pullback

**5.4.1.** Prove that, for a geometric morphism g_* : 𝒴 → 𝒳, the pullback g^* : Shv_Sp(𝒳) → Shv_Sp(𝒴) of §4.3
is t-exact, carrying Shv_Sp(𝒳)≥n into Shv_Sp(𝒴)≥n and Shv_Sp(𝒳)≤n into Shv_Sp(𝒴)≤n, and that the

### 6.1 ∞-connective sheaves of spectra and the hypercompletion

**6.1.1.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos 𝒳 and n < ∞, every n-truncated object of 𝒳 is hypercomplete (HTT,
Lemma 6.5.2.9, p. 687): it is local for (n + 1)-connective morphisms by Part InfinityTopoi §14.2
(HTT, Cor. 6.5.1.14), and ∞-connective morphisms are (n + 1)-connective.

*Needs:*

- §1.1.1 (HTT, §6.5.2)

**6.1.2.** Define a sheaf of spectra F on 𝒳 to be ∞-connective when it is n-connective for every n, that is,
πₙ F ≃ 0 for every n ∈ ℤ (SAG, Def. 1.3.3.1, p. 120).

**6.1.3.** Prove that, with f_* : 𝒳^∧ → 𝒳 the inclusion, a geometric morphism whose left adjoint f^* is the
hypercompletion (§1.2), the adjunction f^* ⊣ f_* of §4.3 on sheaves of spectra has f_* fully
faithful, and that F is ∞-connective exactly when f^*F ≃ 0 (SAG, Rem. 1.3.3.2, p. 121). The route:
full faithfulness by §4.3. If F is ∞-connective, each level Ω^(∞−n) F is an ∞-connective object of
𝒳, so the hypercompletion, which inverts ∞-connective morphisms, sends it to a final object, and
f^*F, computed levelwise, is zero. Conversely, f^* commutes with τ≤0, being left exact (Part
PresentableInfinityCategories §6.2; HTT, Prop. 5.5.6.28), and is the identity on discrete objects by §6.1.1, so
πₙ F ≃ πₙ(f^*F).

**Checks.**

- For hypercomplete 𝒳, such as S or P(C), f^* is the identity, so the only ∞-connective sheaf of
  spectra is 0; for 𝒳 = S this is the statement that homotopy groups detect equivalences of spectra
  (Part StableInfinityCategories §11.3).
- Requiring πₙ F ≃ 0 only for n ≥ 0 is weaker: Hℤ[−1] on S satisfies it and is not zero.
- 0 is ∞-connective, and ∞-connective sheaves are closed under shifts in both directions, unlike
  n-connective ones for finite n.

### 6.2 Hypercomplete sheaves of spectra

**6.2.1.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos 𝒳 and a sheaf of spectra F, the following are equivalent (SAG,
Prop. 1.3.3.3, pp. 121–122):

1. Ω^∞ F is a hypercomplete object of 𝒳;
2. F lies in the essential image of the fully faithful f_* : Shv_Sp(𝒳^∧) → Shv_Sp(𝒳);
3. Map(G, F) is contractible for every ∞-connective G;
4. every map from an ∞-connective G to F is nullhomotopic.

Define F to be hypercomplete when these hold (SAG, Def. 1.3.3.4, p. 122).

The route is Lurie's.
- *(1) ⇒ (2).* It suffices that every level Ω^(∞−n) F, n ≥ 0, is hypercomplete; by induction,
  applied to shifts of F, it suffices that Ω^(∞−1) F is. Rotating the truncation sequence of F[1]
  gives a fiber sequence Ω^(∞−1) F → π₋₁ F → U with U = Ω^∞((τ≥0 F)[2]). π₋₁ F is discrete, hence
  hypercomplete (§6.1), and hypercomplete objects are closed under limits, so it suffices that U is.
  U is a 2-connective pointed object, and so is its hypercompletion U′, since the hypercompletion
  commutes with τ≤1. By the delooping statement of Part InfinityTopoi §15.1 (HTT, Lemma 7.2.2.11
  (1)), applied twice, a map of 2-connective pointed objects is an equivalence when its double loop
  map is. The hypercompletion is left exact, so Ω²U′ is the hypercompletion of
  Ω²U ≃ Ω^∞(τ≥0 F) ≃ Ω^∞ F, which is hypercomplete by (1).
- *(2) ⇒ (1)* is immediate; *(2) ⇒ (3)* holds because Map(G, f_*H) ≃ Map(f^*G, H) and f^*G ≃ 0
  (§6.1); *(3) ⇒ (4)* is immediate.
- *(4) ⇒ (2).* Let u : F → f_*f^*F be the unit. f^*u is an equivalence since f_* is fully faithful,
  so fib(u) is ∞-connective (§6.1) and fib(u) → F is null by (4). Hence cofib(u) ≃ fib(u)[1] is a
  retract of f_*f^*F, and each Ω^(∞−n)(cofib u) is a retract of a hypercomplete object, hence
  hypercomplete. It is also ∞-connective, so it is final (§1.1), and cofib(u) ≃ 0.

- The ∞-connective and the hypercomplete sheaves of spectra form a t-structure with trivial heart:
  every F sits in a fiber sequence F′ → F → F″ with F′ ∞-connective and F″ hypercomplete, namely
  fib(u) → F → f_*f^*F (SAG, Rem. 1.3.3.5, p. 122).
- The full subcategory of hypercomplete sheaves of spectra is equivalent to Shv_Sp(𝒳^∧).

**Checks.**

- If 𝒳 is hypercomplete, every sheaf of spectra is hypercomplete, by (2).
- An object that is both ∞-connective and hypercomplete is zero, by (3) applied to its identity; so
  a definition through homotopy sheaves, which are discrete and hence always hypercomplete (§6.1),
  would make every sheaf of spectra hypercomplete, including any nonzero ∞-connective one.
- Hypercomplete sheaves of spectra are closed under shifts and small limits, by (3), since
  ∞-connective sheaves are closed under shifts; for 𝒳 = Δ[0] the only sheaf of spectra is 0, which is
  both ∞-connective and hypercomplete, consistent with the second Check.

*Needs:*
- §1.2.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.8), §4.1.1, §4.1.2, §5.2.1, §5.3.1, §6.1.1, §6.1.2, §6.1.3
- §1.1.1 (HTT, §6.5.2); Part PresentableInfinityCategories §1.2.1 (HTT, Def. 5.5.4.1)

### 6.3 Hypercompleteness is local

**6.3.1.** Prove that, for an ∞-topos 𝒳, an object X and a family of objects Uα such that ⊔α Uα → 1 is an
effective epimorphism, if each X × Uα → Uα is a hypercomplete object of the ∞-topos 𝒳/Uα, then X is
hypercomplete (SAG, Prop. 1.3.3.6, p. 122).

The route is Lurie's. Let U = ⊔α Uα. By descent, 𝒳/U is the product of the 𝒳/Uα (Part InfinityTopoi
§2.4; HTT, Thm. 6.1.3.9), and X × U is hypercomplete there, since its components are and
∞-connective morphisms of a product of ∞-topoi are computed componentwise (Part InfinityTopoi §11.1;
HTT, Prop. 6.3.2.1). Let U• be the Čech nerve of U → 1. For an
∞-connective f : Y → Z, Z is the colimit of Z × U• by universality of colimits, so
Map(Z, X) → Map(Y, X) is the totalization of the maps Map(Z × Uₙ, X) → Map(Y × Uₙ, X). Each is the
map, between mapping spaces in 𝒳/U, from maps Z × Uₙ → X × U to maps Y × Uₙ → X × U, where
Y × Uₙ → Z × Uₙ is ∞-connective as a pullback of f (Part InfinityTopoi §14.5; HTT,
Prop. 6.5.1.16 (6)), also in 𝒳/U (Part InfinityTopoi §14.4; HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.19), so each is an
equivalence.

- For a sheaf of spectra F and étale pullbacks uα^* : Shv_Sp(𝒳) → Shv_Sp(𝒳/Uα), if each uα^*F is
  hypercomplete, so is F (SAG, Cor. 1.3.3.7, p. 123), by §6.2 (1), since uα^* Ω^∞ F ≃ Ω^∞ uα^*F.

**Checks.**

- The effective epimorphism hypothesis is needed: for the empty family, ⊔α Uα = ∅ and the
  hypothesis on X is vacuous, but ∅ → 1 is not an effective epimorphism in a nontrivial 𝒳, and not
  every object need be hypercomplete.
- For the one-element family U = 1, the statement is a tautology, since 𝒳/1 ≃ 𝒳.
- In S × S, with U₁ = (Δ[0], ∅) and U₂ = (∅, Δ[0]), the coproduct is final and 𝒳/Uᵢ is the i-th
  factor; the statement reduces to hypercompleteness factor by factor.

*Needs:* for Prop. 1.3.3.6,
- §1.1.1 (HTT, §6.5.2); Part InfinityTopoi §1.1.1 (HTT, Def. 6.1.1.2) and §7.1.1 (HTT, §6.2.3)
- Part InfinityTopoi §2.4.2 (HTT, Thm. 6.1.3.9), §7.5.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.2.3.15), §10.4.1 (HTT,
  Prop. 6.3.5.1), §11.1.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.3.2.1), §14.4.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.19), §14.5.6 (HTT,
  Prop. 6.5.1.16 (6))
- Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §9.4.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.1.2.11)

*Needs:* for Cor. 1.3.3.7, Prop. 1.3.3.6 above, §4.3.1, §6.2.1

### Examples

- For a hypercomplete ∞-topos, Shv_Sp(𝒳^∧) = Shv_Sp(𝒳) and every sheaf of spectra is hypercomplete.
- For any ∞-topos, the hypercomplete part of Shv_Sp(𝒳) is the stable analogue of 𝒳^∧, and §6.3
  shows that hypercompleteness of a sheaf of spectra can be checked on a cover.
- For an ∞-topos locally of finite homotopy dimension, such as P(C), every sheaf of spectra is
  hypercomplete, since the ∞-topos is hypercomplete by §3.5.

## Layer 7: Stone duality

### 7.1 Semilattices, lattices and ideals

Use Mathlib's order structures for SAG's order-theoretic vocabulary.

- An **upper semilattice** is a poset in which every finite subset, the empty one included, has a
  supremum (SAG, Def. A.1.1.1, p. 1738). It suffices to have a least element `⊥` and binary joins,
  so this is Mathlib's `SemilatticeSup` with `OrderBot`, a commutative idempotent monoid under `∨`
  with unit `⊥`.
- A **lattice** is a poset `Λ` such that `Λ` and `Λᵒᵖ` are upper semilattices (SAG, Def. A.1.4.1,
  p. 1746), that is, a Mathlib `Lattice` with `BoundedOrder`. Write `⋀S` for the meet of a finite
  subset, `⊤` for the greatest element and `x ∧ y` for binary meets (SAG, Not. A.1.4.2, p. 1746).
- A **lattice homomorphism** preserves all finite joins and meets, empty ones included: a Mathlib
  `BoundedLatticeHom` (SAG, Def. A.1.5.3, p. 1748).

**Checks.**

- The empty poset is not an upper semilattice, because the empty subset has no supremum. A
  definition with only binary joins would accept it. `ℤ` is not one either, since it has no least
  element, though every pair in it has a join.
- The opens of a topological space form an upper semilattice under union (SAG, Ex. A.1.1.6,
  p. 1740), and so do its quasi-compact opens: a finite union of quasi-compact sets is
  quasi-compact, and `∅` is quasi-compact.
- The empty meet is `⊤`, so `ℕ` is not a lattice in this sense, although it is a Mathlib `Lattice`.
  This rules out `Lattice` without `BoundedOrder`.
- For a space with a basis of quasi-compact opens, the quasi-compact opens form a lattice with meet
  `∩` exactly when the whole space is quasi-compact and the intersection of two quasi-compact opens
  is quasi-compact; a definition of lattice without the empty meet would drop the first condition.
- The constant map `{⊥ < ⊤} → {⊥ < ⊤}` with value `⊤` preserves binary joins and meets but not
  `⊥`, so it is not a lattice homomorphism. There is exactly one lattice homomorphism from
  `{⊥ < ⊤}` to any lattice, so `{⊥ < ⊤}` is initial.

*Needs:* Mathlib `SemilatticeSup`, `OrderBot`, `Lattice`, `BoundedOrder`, `BoundedLatticeHom`.

Use Mathlib's `Order.Ideal`, `Order.PFilter` and `Order.Ideal.IsPrime` for SAG's ideals, filters
and prime ideals of an upper semilattice (SAG, Def. A.1.2.1, p. 1740). An ideal is a downward
closed subset closed under finite joins. A filter is an upward closed subset in which every finite
subset has a lower bound. An ideal `I` is prime when `Λ − I` is a filter.

- Every ideal contains `⊥`, and a prime ideal is proper, since the empty subset of a filter needs
  a lower bound.
- In a lattice, an ideal `I` is prime exactly when it is proper and `x ∧ y ∈ I` implies `x ∈ I` or
  `y ∈ I` (Mathlib `Order.Ideal.isPrime_iff_mem_or_mem`).

**Checks.**

- In `{0 < 1}`, the only prime ideal is `{0}`. The ideal `{0, 1}` is not prime, because its
  complement is empty and so not a filter. A definition dropping nonemptiness of filters would
  accept it.
- In `ℕ`, the ideals are the initial segments `{0, …, n}` and `ℕ` itself, and every proper ideal is
  prime, since in a total order the complement of a proper ideal is a filter.
- In the four-element Boolean algebra `{⊥, a, b, ⊤}`, the ideal `{⊥}` is not prime, because
  `a ∧ b = ⊥` with neither `a` nor `b` in it. The prime ideals are `{⊥, a}` and `{⊥, b}`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Order.Ideal`, `Order.PFilter`, `Order.Ideal.IsPrime`; §7.1.

### 7.2 Distributivity and distributors

**7.2.1.** Define a **distributor** from an upper semilattice `Λ` to an upper semilattice `Λ'` (SAG,
Def. A.1.1.3, p. 1739). It is a relation `D ⊆ Λ × Λ'` with three properties:

- (i) it is downward closed in the first variable and upward closed in the second;
- (ii) for a finite family `yᵢ` in `Λ'` and `x ∈ Λ`, we have `(x, ⋁yᵢ) ∈ D` exactly when
  `x = ⋁xᵢ` for some `xᵢ` with `(xᵢ, yᵢ) ∈ D`;
- (iii) if `(x, yᵢ) ∈ D` for each member of a finite family, there is `y ≤` every `yᵢ` with
  `(x, y) ∈ D`.

Call `Λ` **distributive** when the order relation `{(x, y) : x ≤ y}` is a distributor from `Λ` to
itself. For the order relation, (i) holds by transitivity and (iii) with `y = x` (compare SAG,
Rem. A.1.1.4, p. 1739), so distributivity says: whenever `x ≤ y ∨ z`, we can write `x = y' ∨ z'`
with `y' ≤ y` and `z' ≤ z`.
The binary case gives the finite case by induction, and the empty case says that `x ≤ ⊥` forces
`x = ⊥`.

**Checks.**

- The diamond `M₃` with atoms `a, b, c`, viewed as an upper semilattice, has `a ≤ b ∨ c = ⊤`, but
  no join of an element below `b` and one below `c` equals `a`. So it is not distributive.
- The finite subsets of a set, and the quasi-compact opens of a space with a basis of
  quasi-compact opens, form distributive upper semilattices (SAG, proof of Prop. A.1.3.3,
  p. 1744). The meet-free formulation matters, because quasi-compact opens need not be closed
  under intersection.
- From `{⊥}` to any `Λ'` there is exactly one distributor, `{⊥} × Λ'`, matching the fact that `{⊥}`
  has no prime ideal. From `Λ` to `{⊥}` a distributor exists only when `Λ = {⊥}`: (iii) for the
  empty family needs some `(x, ⊥) ∈ D` for each `x`, and (ii) forces `x = ⊥`.
- For `Λ = Λ' = {⊥ < ⊤}`, the only distributor is the order relation: (ii) for the empty family
  forces `(⊥, ⊥) ∈ D` and `(⊤, ⊥) ∉ D`, and (iii) for the empty family forces `(⊤, ⊤) ∈ D`. Without
  (iii), `{(⊥, ⊥), (⊥, ⊤)}` would be a second one.

*Needs:* §7.1.

**7.2.2.** Prove that for a lattice `Λ` the following are equivalent, and call `Λ` a **distributive lattice**
when they hold, that is, a Mathlib `DistribLattice` with `BoundedOrder` (SAG, Prop. A.1.4.4 and
Def. A.1.4.5, p. 1746):

1. `Λ`, regarded as an upper semilattice, is distributive;
2. `x ∧ (y ∨ z) = (x ∧ y) ∨ (x ∧ z)` for all `x, y, z`;
3. `Λᵒᵖ`, regarded as an upper semilattice, is distributive;
4. `x ∨ (y ∧ z) = (x ∨ y) ∧ (x ∨ z)` for all `x, y, z`.

Proof route: (2) ⇔ (4) is Mathlib's (`DistribLattice.ofInfSupLe`, `inf_sup_left`). For (2) ⇒ (1),
if `x ≤ y ∨ z` then `x = (x ∧ y) ∨ (x ∧ z)`. For (1) ⇒ (2), write `x ∧ (y ∨ z) = y' ∨ z'` with
`y' ≤ y`, `z' ≤ z`. (3) ⇔ (4) is the dual statement.

**Checks.**

- The pentagon `N₅` and the diamond `M₃` are bounded lattices that are not distributive. In `M₃`,
  `a ∧ (b ∨ c) = a` while `(a ∧ b) ∨ (a ∧ c) = ⊥`.
- Every total order with `⊥` and `⊤` is distributive.
- The open sets of a space form a distributive lattice, while the lattice of subspaces of `𝔽₂²` is
  `M₃`.

*Needs:* §7.1; §7.2.1; Mathlib `DistribLattice`.

Use Mathlib's `BooleanAlgebra` for SAG's Boolean algebras: distributive lattices in which every
element has a complement. The full subcategory `BAlg` of the category `Lat` of distributive
lattices is Mathlib's `BoolAlg` inside `BddDistLat` (SAG, Def. A.1.6.4, p. 1750; Def. A.1.5.3,
p. 1748). Morphisms of Mathlib's `BoolAlg` are bounded lattice homomorphisms, so it is a full
subcategory of `BddDistLat`, as SAG's definition requires. This matches the classical facts that in
a distributive lattice complements are unique when they exist and lattice homomorphisms preserve
them (compare SAG, Rems. A.1.6.1–A.1.6.3, p. 1750); nothing below uses those remarks.

**Checks.**

- The chain `{⊥ < a < ⊤}` is distributive, but `a` has no complement.
- Power sets and the clopen subsets of any space (Mathlib `TopologicalSpace.Clopens`) are Boolean
  algebras.
- The one-element lattice, with `⊥ = ⊤`, is a Boolean algebra. It has no prime ideal, since its only
  ideal is not proper; a definition requiring `⊥ ≠ ⊤` would exclude it.

*Needs:* §7.2.2; Mathlib `BooleanAlgebra`, `BoolAlg`, `BddDistLat`.

**7.2.3.** Construct the **category `SLat`** of distributive upper semilattices and distributors (SAG,
Constr. A.1.1.5, p. 1739), and the **faithful functor `Latᵒᵖ → SLat`** sending `λ : Λ' → Λ` to
`D_λ = {(x, y) : x ≤ λ(y)}` (SAG, Constr. A.1.5.5 and Rem. A.1.5.6, p. 1748).

- Composition is relational: `D' ∘ D = {(x, z) : ∃ y, (x, y) ∈ D, (y, z) ∈ D'}`. Composites of
  distributors are distributors, and composition is associative.
- The identity of `Λ` is its order relation. It is a two-sided unit exactly because `Λ` is
  distributive, which is why objects are restricted to distributive semilattices.
- `D_λ` is a distributor, and the functor is faithful because `λ(y)` is the largest `x` with
  `(x, y) ∈ D_λ`.

**Checks.**

- Equality is not a distributor when `Λ ≠ {⊥}`. For `Λ = {⊥ < ⊤}` it contains `(⊥, ⊥)` but not
  `(⊥, ⊤)`, so (i) fails. So the identity cannot be equality.
- For `λ` an identity, `D_λ` is the order relation, and composites of homomorphisms go to
  composites of distributors in reverse order.
- (ii) for the empty family says `(x, ⊥) ∈ D_λ` exactly when `x = ⊥`. This uses `λ(⊥) = ⊥` and
  fails for the constant map of §7.1. (iii) uses meets: `x ≤ λ(y₁)` and `x ≤ λ(y₂)` give
  `x ≤ λ(y₁ ∧ y₂)`, which fails for a join-preserving map that does not preserve meets.

*Needs:* §7.2.1, §7.2.2; §7.1.

### 7.3 The spectrum of a lattice

The classes of spaces are SAG's (SAG, §A.1, p. 1738; Def. A.1.5.1, p. 1747; Def. A.1.5.9, p. 1748; Def. A.1.6.8, p. 1751).

- A space with a basis of quasi-compact opens is **quasi-separated** when the intersection of two
  quasi-compact opens is quasi-compact. For such spaces this is Mathlib's `QuasiSeparatedSpace`.
- A **coherent** space is sober, quasi-compact and quasi-separated, with a basis of quasi-compact
  opens. This is Mathlib's `SpectralSpace`: SAG's "sober" includes uniqueness of generic points,
  which is `QuasiSober` with `T0Space`. `Top_coh` has as morphisms the quasi-compact continuous
  maps, Mathlib's `IsSpectralMap`.
- A **Stone space** is a compact Hausdorff space with a basis of clopen sets, equivalently a Mathlib `Profinite`
  space; `Top_St ⊆ Top` is the full subcategory.

**7.3.1.** Prove that a space is a Stone space exactly when it is Hausdorff and coherent (SAG, Rem. A.1.6.10, p. 1751,
whose intermediate claim that clopen sets of a Hausdorff space are quasi-compact needs compactness). A Hausdorff space
is sober, and its quasi-compact opens are clopen; in a compact Hausdorff space they are exactly the clopen sets.

**Checks.**

- Let `X = ℕ ∪ {a, b}`, with points of `ℕ` open and the neighborhoods of `a` (and of `b`) the sets
  containing it and all but finitely many elements of `ℕ`. Then `X` is quasi-compact with a basis of
  quasi-compact opens, but `({a} ∪ ℕ) ∩ ({b} ∪ ℕ) = ℕ` is not quasi-compact. A definition testing
  only quasi-compactness of `X` would accept it as coherent.
- An infinite set with the cofinite topology is quasi-compact and `T1` but not sober: the whole
  space is irreducible with no generic point.
- Let `X = ℕ ∪ {∞}` be the one-point compactification and `Y` the Sierpiński space with open point
  `1`. Sending `ℕ` to `1` and `∞` to `0` is continuous between spectral spaces, but the inverse
  image of the quasi-compact open `{1}` is `ℕ`, not compact. So continuity does not imply
  quasi-compactness.
- `ℕ` discrete is Hausdorff and `ℕ` is clopen in it but not quasi-compact, which is why the
  Stone criterion needs coherence. The Sierpiński space is coherent but not Hausdorff, and `[0, 1]`
  is compact Hausdorff with no clopen basis.

*Needs:* Mathlib `QuasiSeparatedSpace`, `PrespectralSpace`, `SpectralSpace`, `IsSpectralMap`,
`CompactSpace`, `T2Space`, `IsTopologicalBasis`, `IsClopen`, `Profinite`.

**7.3.2.** Define the **spectrum** `Spec(Λ)` of a distributive upper semilattice `Λ` as its set of prime ideals (SAG,
Constr. A.1.2.3, p. 1740). For an ideal `I`, `Spec(Λ)_I` is the set of primes `p` with `I ⊄ p`, and for `x ∈ Λ`,
`Spec(Λ)_x` is the set of primes not containing `x`, which is `Spec(Λ)_I` for the principal ideal of `x` (SAG, Not.
A.1.2.4, p. 1740). Prove (SAG, Prop. A.1.2.5, pp. 1740–1741): The `Suggested.lean` declaration is
`exists_orderIso_opens`.

1. the sets `Spec(Λ)_I` are the open sets of a topology on `Spec(Λ)`;
2. `I ↦ Spec(Λ)_I` is an order isomorphism from ideals of `Λ` to open subsets of `Spec(Λ)`
   (for lattices, with (1), the `Suggested.lean` declaration `LatticeSpectrum.exists_orderIso_opens`);
3. the `Spec(Λ)_x` form a basis;
4. for a finite subset `S` with join `x`, `Spec(Λ)_x` is the union of the `Spec(Λ)_y`, `y ∈ S`;
5. each `Spec(Λ)_x` is quasi-compact, and every quasi-compact open is `Spec(Λ)_x` for a unique `x`;
6. `Spec(Λ)` is sober.

Proof route: the key input is the **separation lemma**: for `x ∉ I` there is a prime containing `I`
and not `x` (SAG, Lemma A.1.2.6, p. 1741), by Zorn's lemma on ideals containing `I` and avoiding
`x`, with distributivity making the complement of a maximal one a filter. For lattices it is
Mathlib `DistribLattice.prime_ideal_of_disjoint_filter_ideal` applied to the principal filter of
`x`. Then (1), (3), (4) are direct; injectivity in (2) and uniqueness in (5) use the separation
lemma; quasi-compactness in (5) holds because a cover of `Spec(Λ)_x` by `Spec(Λ)_{Iⱼ}` puts `x` in
a finite join; for (6), the generic point of an irreducible closed set is the union of the ideals
whose opens miss it.

**Checks.**

- `Spec({⊥}) = ∅`, since its only ideal is not proper. `Spec({⊥ < ⊤})` is one point, `{⊥}`, and
  `Spec({⊥ < a < ⊤})` is the Sierpiński space on `{⊥}` and `{⊥, a}`.
- For the four-element Boolean algebra, `Spec` is the two points `{⊥, a}` and `{⊥, b}`. Using all
  proper ideals would add `{⊥}` as a third point.
- For the finite subsets of `ℕ`, the primes are the ideals of finite subsets of `ℕ − {n}`, so
  `Spec` is `ℕ` discrete: quasi-separated, not quasi-compact, matching Prop. A.1.5.2, since the
  finite subsets of `ℕ` have no greatest element.

*Needs:* §7.1, §7.2.1 (distributive upper semilattices); Mathlib
`DistribLattice.prime_ideal_of_disjoint_filter_ideal` for the lattice case, `QuasiSober`,

**7.3.3.** Prove that the spectrum detects lattices and Boolean algebras. For a distributive upper semilattice `Λ`:
The `Suggested.lean` declaration is `t2Space_iff_complementedLattice`.

- `Λ` is a distributive lattice exactly when `Spec(Λ)` is quasi-compact and quasi-separated (SAG,
  Prop. A.1.5.2, p. 1747). Route: the whole space is `Spec(Λ)_⊤` exactly when `Λ` has a greatest
  element, and `Spec(Λ)_x ∩ Spec(Λ)_y` is quasi-compact exactly when it is `Spec(Λ)_z`, and then

- `Λ` is a Boolean algebra exactly when `Spec(Λ)` is compact and Hausdorff (SAG, Prop. A.1.6.5,

  `Spec(Λ)_{xᶜ}`, so basic opens are clopen and separate points. Conversely, in a Hausdorff
  spectrum a quasi-compact open `Spec(Λ)_x` is closed, its complement is some `Spec(Λ)_y`, and `y`
  is a complement of `x`.

### 7.4 The duality theorems

**7.4.1.** Prove that `Spec` is a duality at three levels. The `Suggested.lean` declarations are `spectralSpace`,
`isOpen_and_isCompact_iff`, `basicOpen_subset_basicOpen_iff`, `isSpectralMap_iff_existsUnique_boundedLatticeHom` and
`exists_homeomorph`.

1. **Semilattices.** `Spec` is a fully faithful functor from `SLat` to topological spaces whose
   essential image is the sober spaces with a basis of quasi-compact opens (SAG, Prop. A.1.3.3,
   p. 1743). A distributor `D` from `Λ` to `Λ'` sends a prime `p` to the set of `y ∈ Λ'` such that
   every `x` with `(x, y) ∈ D` lies in `p`; this is a prime, and `Spec(D)` is continuous (SAG,
   Constr. A.1.3.1 and Rem. A.1.3.2, pp. 1742–1743).
2. **Lattices.** `Spec` induces an equivalence `Latᵒᵖ ≃ Top_coh` (SAG, Prop. A.1.5.10, p. 1749).
3. **Boolean algebras.** A distributor from a distributive lattice to a Boolean algebra is `D_λ`
   for a unique lattice homomorphism `λ` (SAG, Cor. A.1.6.6, p. 1750), and `Spec` is a fully
   faithful functor `BAlgᵒᵖ → Top` with essential image `Top_St` (SAG, Thm. A.1.6.11, p. 1751).

Here `Spec(Λ)` of a bounded distributive lattice is built from Mathlib's order ideals with the
prime predicate, with the topology generated by the basic opens `Spec(Λ)_x`. Level 2 consists of
the following parts:

- the spectrum is a spectral space (Prop. A.1.2.5(5),(6) and Prop. A.1.5.2; `Suggested.lean`
  declaration `LatticeSpectrum.spectralSpace`);
- its compact opens are exactly the basic opens (Prop. A.1.2.5(3),(5);
  `LatticeSpectrum.isOpen_and_isCompact_iff`);
- `Spec(Λ)_x ⊆ Spec(Λ)_y` exactly when `x ≤ y` (Prop. A.1.2.5(5), with Lemma A.1.2.6;
  `LatticeSpectrum.basicOpen_subset_basicOpen_iff`);
- every spectral space is homeomorphic to a spectrum (Prop. A.1.5.10(1);
  `LatticeSpectrum.exists_homeomorph`);
- a map of spectra is spectral exactly when it is induced, by pulling back primes, by a unique
  bounded lattice homomorphism in the other direction (Prop. A.1.5.10(2);
  `LatticeSpectrum.isSpectralMap_iff_existsUnique_boundedLatticeHom`).

The spectrum is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `LatticeSpectrum`, with basic opens
`LatticeSpectrum.basicOpen`. Level 3 consists of two parts: every Stone space is homeomorphic to
the spectrum of a Boolean algebra (`Suggested.lean` declaration
`exists_homeomorph_latticeSpectrum_of_isTopologicalBasis_isClopen`), and continuous maps of such
spectra are exactly those induced by unique Boolean algebra homomorphisms the other way
(`continuous_iff_existsUnique_boundedLatticeHom`).

Proof route:

- (1): `(x, y) ∈ D` exactly when `Spec(Λ)_x ⊆ f⁻¹ Spec(Λ')_y`, by the separation lemma, which
  recovers `D` from `f = Spec(D)`. A sober space with a basis of quasi-compact opens is `Spec` of
  its semilattice of quasi-compact opens: points are primes by sobriety, opens are ideals by
  Prop. A.1.2.5(2).
- (2): combine (1) with `Latᵒᵖ → SLat`. Prop. A.1.5.2 identifies the image, and a distributor
  between lattices is `D_λ` exactly when its map of spectra is quasi-compact, `λ(y)` being the
  element whose basic open is the inverse image of `Spec(Λ')_y`.
- (3): quasi-compact opens of the Hausdorff `Spec(Λ')` are closed, so their inverse images are
  closed in the quasi-compact `Spec(Λ)`, hence quasi-compact; then (2), Prop. A.1.6.5 and
  Rem. A.1.6.10 give the theorem.

*Needs:* for (1), §7.2.1, §7.2.3 (distributors, `SLat`), §7.3.2 (Prop. A.1.2.5, Lemma A.1.2.6), Mathlib
`CategoryTheory.Functor.FullyFaithful` and `CategoryTheory.Equivalence`; for (2), (1), §7.2.3
(`Latᵒᵖ → SLat`), §7.3.3 (Prop. A.1.5.2), §7.3 (coherent spaces), Mathlib `SpectralSpace`, `IsSpectralMap`,
`BoundedLatticeHom`; for (3), (2), §7.3.3 (Prop. A.1.6.5), §7.3 (Stone spaces).

## Layer 8: Coherent ∞-topoi

### 8.1 Quasi-compactness

**8.1.1.** Define an ∞-topos 𝒳 to be **quasi-compact** when every covering has a finite subcovering: if
`⊔_{i ∈ I} Uᵢ → 1` is an effective epimorphism onto the final object, then so is
`⊔_{i ∈ I₀} Uᵢ → 1` for some finite `I₀ ⊆ I`. Call an object `X` quasi-compact when the slice
∞-topos `𝒳_{/X}` is quasi-compact (SAG, Def. A.2.0.12, first part, p. 1751).

- Equivalently, `X` is quasi-compact when every effective epimorphism `⊔ Uᵢ → X` admits a finite
  subfamily that is still an effective epimorphism.
- The trivial ∞-topos, whose final object is initial, is quasi-compact: the empty family covers.
- Maps out of a quasi-compact object `C` see coproducts finitely: for a family `{Xᵢ}_{i ∈ I}` with
  `X_J = ⊔_{i ∈ J} Xᵢ`, the map from the colimit over finite `J ⊆ I` of `Map(C, X_J)` to
  `Map(C, X_I)` is a homotopy equivalence (SAG, Lemma A.3.4.4, p. 1771). Route: coproducts in an
  ∞-topos are disjoint and universal, so each summand inclusion `X_J → X_I` is a monomorphism (HTT,
  Lemma 6.1.5.1), and `Map(C, X_J) → Map(C, X_I)` is then the inclusion of a union of components
  (HTT, Def. 5.5.6.8). A filtered colimit of such inclusions in `S` is their union (HTT,
  Prop. 3.3.4.5), so the colimit over the filtered poset of finite `J` is the union of these
  components, and it remains that every
  `φ : C → X_I` factors through some `X_J`. Pulling back the decomposition of `X_I` along `φ` covers
  `C` by the `C ×_{X_I} Xᵢ` (universality of coproducts), a finite subfamily still covers, so
  `C ×_{X_I} X_J → C` is an effective epimorphism and a monomorphism, hence an equivalence, which
  gives the factorization.

**Checks.**

- In `S`, an object is quasi-compact exactly when it has finitely many components. A countably
  infinite discrete space is not quasi-compact, since its points form a cover with no finite
  subcover.
- For a topological space `T`, `Shv(T)` is quasi-compact when `T` is. The representable of a
  non-quasi-compact open `U` is a non-quasi-compact object. This rules out a definition through
  global sections, asking that `Map(1, X)` be quasi-compact in `S`: for `U ≠ T`, the representable
  of `U` has no global section, and the empty space is quasi-compact.
- Quasi-compactness is not compactness in the sense of HTT, Def. 5.3.4.5. In `S`, `BZ/2` is
  quasi-compact, being connected, but it is not compact: it is not a retract of a finite space, as
  its mod 2 homology is nonzero in every degree.

*Needs:* for the definition, Part InfinityTopoi §3.1.2 (∞-topoi, HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6 (3)); Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §1.2 (final
objects, HTT, §1.2.12), Part InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective epimorphisms, HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5) and
§10.4.1 (slices, HTT, Prop. 6.3.5.1); for Lemma A.3.4.4, the definition, Part InfinityTopoi §1.1.1, §1.1.2 (universal colimits and disjoint coproducts, HTT, §6.1.1) and §3.1.2 (HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6 (3)), Part
InfinityCategoryFoundations §10.7.1 (truncated morphisms, HTT, Def. 5.5.6.8), Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §8.3.1
(colimits in spaces, HTT, Prop. 3.3.4.5), and Part InfinityTopoi §1.6.2 (summand inclusions are

### 8.2 n-coherent ∞-topoi, objects and morphisms

**8.2.1.** Define by induction on `n ≥ 0` (SAG, Def. A.2.0.12, inductive part, pp. 1751–1752):

- an ∞-topos is **0-coherent** when it is quasi-compact;
- an object `U` is **n-coherent** when `𝒳_{/U}` is n-coherent;
- `X` is **locally n-coherent** when every object admits an effective epimorphism from a coproduct
  of n-coherent objects;
- `X` is **(n+1)-coherent** when it is locally n-coherent and the n-coherent objects are closed
  under finite products.

The basic closure properties come with the definition (SAG, Rems. A.2.0.14–A.2.0.17, p. 1752).
Each is proved by induction on `n`, using `𝒳_{/1} ≃ X`, `(𝒳_{/X})_{/U} ≃ 𝒳_{/U}` and
`Y_{/⊔Uᵢ} ≃ ∏ Y_{/Uᵢ}`.

- An n-coherent ∞-topos is m-coherent for `m ≤ n`, and for `n > 0` its final object is
  (n−1)-coherent (Rem. A.2.0.14).
- In a locally n-coherent `X`, every slice is locally n-coherent, and an object is (n+1)-coherent
  exactly when it is n-coherent and its fiber products of n-coherent objects are n-coherent
  (Rem. A.2.0.15).
- A finite product of ∞-topoi, a coproduct in `∞Top`, is n-coherent exactly when each factor is;
  so finite coproducts of objects are n-coherent exactly when their summands are (Rem. A.2.0.16).
- In a locally n-coherent ∞-topos, a quasi-compact object admits an effective epimorphism from a
  single n-coherent object (Rem. A.2.0.17): take a finite subcover and its coproduct.

**Checks.**

- In `S`, an object `X` is 0-coherent exactly when `π₀(X)` is finite (§8.1). For `n = 1` and
  connected `X`, it is 1-coherent exactly when `π₁(X)` is finite. By Rem. A.2.0.15 the condition is
  that `A ×_X B` has finitely many components whenever `A` and `B` over `X` do. For connected `A`
  and `B`, with images `H` and `K` of their fundamental groups in `π₁(X)`, `π₀(A ×_X B)` is the
  double-coset set `H\π₁(X)/K`; in general it is a finite union of such sets. All are finite when
  `π₁(X)` is finite, and `A = B = Δ[0]` shows that finiteness of `π₁(X)` is needed.
- `S¹` is 0-coherent but not 1-coherent, because `Δ[0] ×_{S¹} Δ[0] ≃ ℤ` is not quasi-compact. A
  definition asking only for quasi-compactness at every level would call `S¹` coherent.
- In `∏_ℕ S`, the final object is covered by the objects `eₙ` that are `Δ[0]` in coordinate `n` and
  empty elsewhere, with no finite subcover, so it is not 0-coherent. Each `eₙ` has slice `S`, so
  `∏_ℕ S` is locally 0-coherent.

*Needs:* §8.1.1; Part InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective epimorphisms, HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5), §10.3.1
(geometric morphisms and `∞Top`, HTT, Def. 6.3.1.5) and §10.4.1 (slices, HTT, Prop. 6.3.5.1).

**8.2.2.** Define a morphism `f : X' → X` of a locally n-coherent ∞-topos to be **relatively n-coherent**
when `U ×_X X'` is n-coherent for every n-coherent object `U` and every map `U → X` (SAG,
Def. A.2.1.1, p. 1753).

- Relatively n-coherent morphisms are stable under pullback.
- If `X'` is n-coherent and `X` is (n+1)-coherent, then every `f : X' → X` is relatively n-coherent
  (SAG, Ex. A.2.1.2, p. 1753), by the fiber product criterion of Rem. A.2.0.15.

**Checks.**

- In `S` with `n = 0`, `ℤ → Δ[0]` is not relatively 0-coherent, since its fiber over the point is
  `ℤ`. The map `{0, 1} → Δ[0]` is.
- An equivalence is relatively n-coherent for every n. The map `Δ[0] → S¹` has coherent source but
  is not relatively 0-coherent, since its pullback along `Δ[0] → S¹` is `ℤ`, which is not
  quasi-compact. A definition testing only `X'` would accept it.
- For `n = 0` this is relative quasi-compactness. In `S`, a map `X' → X` with `X` connected is
  relatively 0-coherent exactly when its fiber `F` over a point has finitely many components: for a
  connected `U` over `X`, the components of `U ×_X X'` are a quotient of `π₀(F)`. So the point
  `Δ[0] → BZ/2`, with fiber `F = ℤ/2`, is relatively 0-coherent.

*Needs:* §8.2.1; Tau Ceti `SSet.slice.IsLimit` (fiber products).

**8.2.3.** Define an ∞-topos to be **coherent** when it is n-coherent for every `n`, an object `U` to be
coherent when `𝒳_{/U}` is, and `X` to be **locally coherent** when every object admits an effective
epimorphism from a coproduct of coherent objects (SAG, Def. A.2.1.6, p. 1755).

- A coherent ∞-topos is locally n-coherent for every `n`.
- In any ∞-topos the coherent objects are closed under finite coproducts and under fiber products
  (`U ×_W V` is coherent whenever `U`, `V` and `W` are), and in a coherent ∞-topos under all finite
  limits (SAG, Rem. A.2.1.8, p. 1755). Finite coproducts are handled by Rem. A.2.0.16. For coherent
  `U, V` over a coherent `W`, the slice `𝒳_{/W}` is (n+1)-coherent for every `n`, and `U → W`,
  `V → W` are n-coherent objects of it because `(𝒳_{/W})_{/U} ≃ 𝒳_{/U}`; so their product there,
  `U ×_W V`, is n-coherent for every `n`, by the definition of (n+1)-coherence in `𝒳_{/W}`. In a
  coherent `X` the final object is coherent, so fiber products over it give finite products. The
  base must be coherent: in `S`, `Δ[0] ×_{S¹} Δ[0] ≃ ℤ` is not coherent, although `Δ[0]` is.
- The guiding question is how far a coherent, locally coherent ∞-topos is determined by its
  coherent objects (SAG, Question A.0.6.7, p. 1735).

**Checks.**

- `S` is coherent and locally coherent. The discrete space `ℤ` is not 0-coherent, since
  `S_{/ℤ} ≃ ∏_ℤ S` is not quasi-compact. `S¹` is not 1-coherent: `Δ[0] → S¹` is a quasi-compact object
  of `S_{/S¹}`, but its self-product there, `Δ[0] ×_{S¹} Δ[0] ≃ ℤ`, is not. The description of all
  coherent spaces is the Check of §8.3.
- `BG` is coherent exactly when the group `G` is finite, because `Δ[0] ×_{BG} Δ[0] ≃ G` must be a
  coherent discrete space. So `BZ = S¹` is quasi-compact but not coherent.
- `∏_ℕ S`, a coproduct of countably many copies of `S` in `∞Top`, is locally coherent but not
  coherent, since its final object is not quasi-compact. A definition of local coherence through the
  final object alone would reject it.

*Needs:* §8.2.1, §8.2.2; Part InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective epimorphisms, HTT,
Cor. 6.2.3.5); for Rem. A.2.1.8, §8.2.1 (Rems. A.2.0.14 and A.2.0.16) and Part InfinityTopoi §10.4.1
(slices, HTT, Prop. 6.3.5.1).

### 8.3 Detecting coherence on covers

**8.3.1.** Prove that coherence can be tested on a cover.

1. For `f : X₀ → X`, assuming, if `n > 0`, that `X` is locally (n−1)-coherent and `f` relatively
   (n−1)-coherent: `f` is relatively m-coherent for every `m < n`, and if `f` is an effective
   epimorphism with `X₀` n-coherent, then `X` is n-coherent (SAG, Prop. A.2.1.3, p. 1753).
2. If a full subcategory `X⁰ ⊆ X` consists of n-coherent objects and covers every object by
   coproducts of its objects, then `f : X' → X` is relatively n-coherent exactly when `U ×_X X'` is
   n-coherent for every `U ∈ X⁰` over `X`, and an object `X` is (n+1)-coherent exactly when it is
   quasi-compact and `U ×_X V` is n-coherent for all `U, V ∈ X⁰` over `X` (SAG, Cor. A.2.1.4,
   p. 1754).

Proof route: (1) by induction on `n`; quasi-compactness descends along effective epimorphisms,
which are stable under pullback, and the product condition is checked on the cover by
Rems. A.2.0.14, A.2.0.15, A.2.0.17 and Ex. A.2.1.2. For (2), reduce n-coherent test objects to finite
coproducts of objects of `X⁰` (Rem. A.2.0.16) and apply (1).

**Checks.**

- In `S`, an object `X` is n-coherent exactly when `π₀(X)` is finite and `πₖ(X, x)` is finite for
  `1 ≤ k ≤ n` and every base point `x`. Apply (2) with `X⁰` the point `Δ[0]`, which is coherent and
  covers every space by its points: `X` is (n+1)-coherent exactly when `π₀(X)` is finite and every
  path space `Δ[0] ×_X Δ[0]` is n-coherent. A path space is empty or equivalent to `ΩX` at a base
  point, whose components and homotopy groups are those of `X` shifted down by one, and induction
  on n concludes.

*Needs:* for (1), §8.2.1 (Rems. A.2.0.14, A.2.0.15, A.2.0.17), §8.2.2 (Def. A.2.1.1, Ex. A.2.1.2), Part InfinityTopoi §3.1.2 (Giraud's axioms, HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6 (3)), Part InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective
epimorphisms, HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5) and §7.5.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.2.3.15); for (2), (1) and §8.2.1
(Rems. A.2.0.15, A.2.0.16), §8.2.2 (Ex. A.2.1.2).

### 8.4 Coherence under hypercompletion

**8.4.1.** Prove that for an ∞-topos 𝒳 that is locally n-coherent for every `n`, with `L : 𝒳 → 𝒳^∧` (SAG,
Prop. A.2.2.2, p. 1756):

1. `X^∧` is locally n-coherent for every `n`;
2. an object of `X^∧` is coherent there exactly when it is coherent in `X`;
3. `X ∈ 𝒳` is coherent exactly when `LX` is coherent.

In particular, if `X` is locally coherent, so is `X^∧`. Proof route: `L` is a cotopological
localization (§1.4; HTT, Def. 6.5.2.17 and the paragraph after Rem. 6.5.2.18, p. 690), and coherence
transfers along any cotopological localization
`f^* : X → Y` (SAG, Lemma A.2.2.1, p. 1755): if `n = 0` or `X` is locally (n−1)-coherent, then
`X ∈ 𝒳` is n-coherent exactly when `f^* X` is, `Y ∈ Y` is n-coherent exactly when `f_* Y` is, and
for `n > 0` `Y` is locally (n−1)-coherent. The lemma is proved by induction on `n`: `f^*` preserves
and detects effective epimorphisms and preserves fiber products, so Rem. A.2.0.15 transfers both
ways.

## Layer 9: Finitary sites

### 9.1 Locally left exact functors

**9.1.1.** Define a functor `f : C → X` from a small ∞-category to an ∞-topos to be **locally left exact**
when it is homotopic to a composite `C → P(C) → X`. Here the first map is the Yoneda embedding and
the second preserves small colimits and finite limits. `Fun^llex(C, X) ⊆ Fun(C, X)` is the full
subcategory they span (SAG, Def. 20.4.2.1, p. 1546).

- Every functor `C → X` extends essentially uniquely to a colimit-preserving `P(C) → X`
  (HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6). Local left exactness asks that this extension be left exact. So
  `Fun^llex(C, X)` is equivalent to the ∞-category of left exact colimit-preserving functors
  `P(C) → X`, that is, the pullbacks of geometric morphisms from `X` to `P(C)`.

**Checks.**

- If `C` has finite limits, a functor `C → X` that preserves finite limits is locally left exact,
  a case of SAG, Prop. 20.4.3.1, since each `C_{/q}` then has a final object, the limit of `q`. The
  functor constant at the initial object is not locally left exact unless `X` is trivial, because
  its colimit extension sends the final presheaf to the initial object.
- For `C` the discrete two-object category, a functor `C → S` is a pair `(A, B)`, with colimit
  extension `(X, Y) ↦ (X × A) ⊔ (Y × B)`. This preserves the final object exactly when
  `A ⊔ B ≃ Δ[0]`, and binary products only if `A × B` is empty. So the locally left exact ones are
  `(Δ[0], ∅)` and `(∅, Δ[0])`, the two points of `P(C) = S × S`.
- For `C = (a → c ← b)`, the functor constant at `Δ[0]` is not locally left exact, although `C` is
  weakly contractible. Its colimit extension takes a presheaf to the pushout of
  `F(a) ← F(c) → F(b)`. It sends the product of two copies of `(Δ[0], S⁰, Δ[0])` to the pushout
  of four points over two, a wedge of three circles, and not to `S¹ × S¹`.

*Needs:* Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §8.1.1 (presheaves, HTT, Def. 5.1.0.1); Part PresheavesAndCompletions §1.1.1 (the Yoneda embedding, HTT, §5.1.3); Part InfinityTopoi §3.1.2 (∞-topoi, HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6 (3)); Part PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3 (HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6).

**9.1.2.** Prove that for an ∞-topos 𝒳, a small ∞-category `C` and `f : C → X`, the following are
equivalent (SAG, Prop. 20.4.3.1, p. 1548):

1. `f` is locally left exact;
2. for every finite simplicial set `K` and `q : K → C`, the canonical map from the colimit of `f`
   restricted to `C_{/q}` to the limit of `f ∘ q` is an equivalence in `X`.

Proof route: (1) ⇒ (2) because the limit of `j ∘ q` in `P(C)` is the colimit of the representables
over `C_{/q}` (HTT, Lemma 5.1.5.3). For the converse, write `f = F ∘ j` with `F`
colimit-preserving. `K = ∅` gives that `F` preserves the final object.
Call an object `Z ∈ P(C)` good when `F` carries every pullback square over `Z` to a pullback
square. Since colimits are universal in `P(C)` and in `X`, `F` preserves colimits, and every
presheaf is a colimit of representables (HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6), it suffices to test pullbacks
`j(a) ×_Z j(b)` of representables. Representables are good by (2), since the limit of `j ∘ q` for a
cospan `q` is the colimit of the representables over `C_{/q}`. A full subcategory of `P(C)` closed
under coequalizers and small coproducts is closed under small colimits (HTT, Prop. 4.4.3.3), and
the only such subcategory containing the representables is `P(C)` itself (HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6). So it
remains to close good objects under both.
- *Coproducts.* Let `Z = ⊔ Zᵢ` with each `Zᵢ` good. Coproducts in `P(C)` are computed objectwise
  (HTT, Prop. 5.1.2.2), so by Yoneda a map `j(c) → Z` is a point of `⊔ Zᵢ(c)` and factors through
  exactly one summand `Zᵢ`. For maps into two different summands, the pullback over `Z` is initial,
  and so is its image in `X`, since `F` preserves coproducts and coproducts are disjoint in both
  ∞-topoi (HTT, Lemma 6.1.5.1). For maps into the same summand, the summand inclusion is a
  monomorphism by the same lemma, in `P(C)` and, after `F`, in `X`; so the pullback over `Z` is the
  pullback over `Zᵢ`, which `F` preserves because `Zᵢ` is good.
- *Coequalizers.* Let `s : Q → Z` exhibit `Z` as a coequalizer of `Q₁ ⇉ Q` with `Q` good.
  Coequalizers in `P(C)` are computed objectwise, and the coequalizer of spaces is surjective on π₀
  from `Q(c)` (HTT, Prop. 3.3.4.5), so every map `j(c) → Z` factors through s. Choose such lifts
  for `j(a)` and `j(b)` and paste: `j(a) ×_Z j(b) ≃ j(a) ×_Q (Q ×_Z Q) ×_Q j(b)`. `F` carries the
  two outer squares, which lie over Q, to pullbacks because Q is good. It carries the kernel pair
  square `Q ×_Z Q` to a pullback by HTT, Prop. 6.1.4.2, applied to F with source `P(C)` and target
  `X`: both are presentable, groupoid objects are effective in both by Giraud's axioms, and F is
  left exact at Q because Q is good. A pasting of pullback squares is a pullback, so Z is good.

under coequalizers and coproducts gives closure under colimits), §8.2.2 (pointwise colimits,

**9.1.3.** Prove that a dense and fully faithful functor `f : C → X` from a small ∞-category to an ∞-topos is

### 9.2 Dense subcategories of hypercomplete ∞-topoi

**9.2.1.** Prove that for a hypercomplete ∞-topos 𝒳 and an essentially small full subcategory `C ⊆ X`, the
following are equivalent (SAG, Prop. 20.4.5.1, p. 1552):

- (a) every object of `X` admits an effective epimorphism from a coproduct of objects of `C`;
- (b) `X` is generated under small colimits by `C`;
- (c) `C` is dense in `X`;

and that, when they hold, the inclusion `C → X` is locally left exact, so that `X` is a left exact
localization of `P(C)` through the colimit extension of the inclusion (SAG, Rem. 20.4.5.2, p. 1552).

The route goes through a lemma, which needs no hypercompleteness. Prove that if `X` is any ∞-topos and
the essentially small full subcategory `C ⊆ X` satisfies (a), then the colimit of the inclusion
`C → X` is an ∞-connective object of `X` (SAG, Lemma 20.4.5.4, p. 1553). Proof route for the lemma:
write `L` for the colimit and show by induction on `n ≥ 0`, for all ∞-topoi at once, that `L` is
n-connective. For `n = 0`, the effective epimorphism `⊔_{U ∈ C} U → 1` factors through `L → 1`, so
`L → 1` is an effective epimorphism (HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.12). For the inductive step it suffices that
the diagonal `L → L × L` is (n−1)-connective (HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.18). Colimits are universal, so they
commute with finite products, `L × L` is the colimit of `V × W` over pairs `V, W ∈ C`, and the diagonal is
the colimit of the maps `colim_{U ∈ C ×_𝒳 𝒳_{/V×W}} U → V × W`. This rewriting is a cofinality step:
let E be the ∞-category of triples `(V, W, U → V × W)` with `U, V, W ∈ C`. For each `c ∈ C`, the fiber
`E ×_C C_{c/}` of the projection `(V, W, U → V × W) ↦ U` has the initial object
`(c, c, c → c × c, id_c)`, so the projection is cofinal (Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §5.4, Theorem A)
and `colim_E U ≃ L`. Each of these maps is (n−1)-connective
by the inductive hypothesis in the slice `𝒳_{/V×W}`, where the objects of `C` over `V × W` again
satisfy (a), and a colimit of (n−1)-connective morphisms is (n−1)-connective.

Proof route for the proposition: (c) ⇒ (b) ⇒ (a) are immediate. For (a) ⇒ (c), show that the
canonical map from the colimit of `C ×_𝒳 𝒳_{/X} → X` to `X` is ∞-connective, which is the lemma
applied in the slice `𝒳_{/X}`; hypercompleteness makes it an equivalence. The last assertion is
Cor. 20.4.3.3: the colimit extension `P(C) → X` is then left exact, and its right adjoint
`X ↦ Map(−, X)` is fully faithful by density.

InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective epimorphisms, HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5), §7.4.2 (HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.12), §10.4.1

### 9.3 Finitary topologies

**9.3.1.** Define a Grothendieck topology on an ∞-category `C` with pullbacks to be **finitary** when every
covering sieve on an object `C` contains a finite family `{Cᵢ → C}` that already generates a
covering sieve (SAG, Def. A.3.1.1, p. 1763).

- For any Grothendieck topology on `C`, the sieves containing a finite family that generates a
  covering sieve form a Grothendieck topology, the finest finitary topology coarser than the given
  one (SAG, Rem. A.3.1.2, p. 1763).

**Checks.**

- The trivial topology, where only the maximal sieves cover, is finitary. The topology on the
  opens of a space `T` is finitary when every open of `T` is quasi-compact, and fails to be when
  `T = ℝ`, covered by the bounded open intervals with no finite subcover.
- The finitary coarsening of a finitary topology is itself. For the open-cover topology on the
  opens of `ℝ`, it removes the cover of `ℝ` by bounded intervals and keeps every finite open cover.
- The finitary coarsening of the canonical topology on `S` keeps covers `⊔ᵢ Uᵢ → X` only when a
  finite subfamily is still surjective on `π₀`. For `X = ℤ` discrete, the cover by its points is
  dropped.

*Needs:* Part InfinityTopoi §8.1.1 (Grothendieck topologies, HTT, Def. 6.2.2.1); Tau Ceti
`SSet.slice.IsLimit` (pullbacks).

**9.3.2.** Prove that a class `S` of morphisms in an ∞-category `C` generates a finitary Grothendieck
topology, in which a sieve on `C` covers exactly when it contains a finite family `{Cᵢ → C}` with
`⊔ᵢ Cᵢ → C` in `S`, under four hypotheses (SAG, Prop. A.3.2.1, pp. 1764–1765; Rem. A.3.2.2,
p. 1765):

- (a) `S` contains the equivalences and is closed under composition, hence under homotopy;
- (b) `C` has pullbacks and `S` is stable under pullback;
- (c) `C` has finite coproducts and `S` is stable under finite coproducts;

### 9.4 The Čech sheaf criterion

**9.4.1.** Prove the **Čech sheaf criterion**. Assume `C` and `S` satisfy the hypotheses of Prop. A.3.2.1
together with (e): coproducts in `C` are disjoint, so `C ×_{C ⊔ C'} C'` is initial. Then for any
∞-category `D`, a functor `F : Cᵒᵖ → D` is a D-valued sheaf for the topology of Prop. A.3.2.1
exactly when two conditions hold (SAG, Prop. A.3.3.1, p. 1766):

1. `F` preserves finite products;
2. for each `f : U₀ → X` in `S`, with Čech nerve `U•`, the composite of `U•` with `F` is a limit
   diagram, so `F(X)` is the totalization of `[n] ↦ F(Uₙ)`.

Proof route: reduce to `D = S` by composing with the functors corepresented by objects of `D`
(HTT, Prop. 5.1.3.2). A sheaf preserves finite products: a finite coproduct is covered by its
summands, and disjointness identifies the Čech nerve. A sheaf also satisfies Čech descent for a
covering morphism, since the sieve generated by `f` has its colimit computed by the Čech nerve
(HTT, Thm. 4.1.3.1, Prop. 3.3.4.5, Lemma 4.3.2.7). Conversely, (1) reduces descent for a finite
covering family `{Cᵢ → C}` to the single morphism `⊔ Cᵢ → C`, and (2) handles that.

### 9.5 Hypersheaves on finitary sites

**9.5.1.** Prove that for an ∞-topos 𝒳 the following are equivalent (SAG, Thm. A.3.4.1, p. 1770):

1. `X` is locally coherent and hypercomplete;
2. there are a small ∞-category `C` with fiber products, a finitary topology on `C`, and an
   equivalence `X ≃ Shv(C)^∧`.

Moreover, `C` can be taken with finite coproducts and a subcanonical topology, and with finite
limits when `X` is coherent. For hypercomplete ∞-topoi this answers the question of §8.2 (SAG,
Question A.0.6.7): a locally coherent hypercomplete ∞-topos is determined by its coherent objects
with the effective epimorphisms among them. The presentation is explicit (SAG, Prop. A.3.4.2, p. 1770): if `X` is
hypercomplete and `C ⊆ X` is an essentially small full subcategory of coherent objects, closed
under finite coproducts and fiber products, that covers every object by effective epimorphisms from
coproducts of its objects, then `{Uα → X}` covers in `C` exactly when `⊔ Uα → X` is an effective
epimorphism in `X`, this topology is finitary, and `X ↦ h_X = Map_X(−, X)|_C` is an equivalence
`X → Shv(C)^∧`.

Proof route:

- Prop. A.3.4.2: the topology comes from Prop. A.3.2.1 applied to the effective epimorphisms
  between objects of `C`. `h` is fully faithful with a left exact left adjoint by Prop. 20.4.5.1
  and Rem. 20.4.5.2, lands in sheaves by Prop. A.3.3.1 and in hypersheaves because `X` is
  hypercomplete, so `h = f_*` for a geometric morphism `f^* : Shv(C)^∧ → X`. `f_*` is
  conservative and preserves n-connective morphisms, by induction using effective epimorphisms
  from objects of `C`. The unit is an equivalence on coproducts of representables, by
  subcanonicality, disjointness and Lemma A.3.4.4 for the quasi-compact objects of `C`, and in
  general by passing to effective epimorphisms and hypercompleteness.
- (2) ⇒ (1): `Shv(C)` is locally coherent by Prop. A.3.1.3, and so is its hypercompletion by
  Prop. A.2.2.2.
- (1) ⇒ (2): since `X` is presentable, a small set of coherent generators exists. Close it under
  finite coproducts (Rem. A.2.0.16) and fiber products (Rem. A.2.1.8) and apply Prop. A.3.4.2. For coherent `X`,
  the final object is coherent (Rem. A.2.0.14) and can be added to `C`.

*Needs:* for Prop. A.3.4.2, §1.1.1 (hypercompleteness, HTT, §6.5.2, p. 687, after Prop. 6.5.2.8),
§8.1.1 (Lemma A.3.4.4), §8.2.3 (coherent objects), §9.2.1 (Prop. 20.4.5.1,
Rem. 20.4.5.2), §9.3.2 (Prop. A.3.2.1), §9.4.1 (Prop. A.3.3.1), Part InfinityTopoi §4.4.1 (HTT,
Thm. 6.1.0.6), §7.3.2 (HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5), §8.2.1 (HTT, Def. 6.2.2.6) and §12.1.2 (∞-connective morphisms, HTT, Def. 6.5.1.10); for Thm. A.3.4.1, Prop. A.3.4.2, §8.2.1 (Rems. A.2.0.14, A.2.0.16), §8.2.3 (Rem. A.2.1.8), §8.4.1
(Prop. A.2.2.2), §9.3.3 (Prop. A.3.1.3), Part PresentableInfinityCategories §1.1.1 (presentable ∞-categories, HTT,

### Examples

- For a coherent topological space `T`, the quasi-compact opens with the open-cover topology form
  a finitary site. `Shv(T)^∧` is the locally coherent hypercomplete ∞-topos it presents
  (Thm. A.3.4.1).
- For a small category with finite limits and a finitary topology, `Shv(C)` is coherent
  (Prop. A.3.1.3(3)). This covers the Zariski and étale sites of a
  quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme.
- `S` itself is `Shv(C)^∧` for `C` the ∞-category of finite sets with jointly surjective finite
  covers. This is a case of Prop. A.3.4.2 with `C` the finite discrete spaces.

### 10.1 Jointly surjective geometric morphisms

**10.1.1.** Define a family of geometric morphisms from ∞-topoi Xα to X, with pullbacks `fα^* : X → Xα`, to be
**jointly surjective** when it satisfies the following conditions, and a single `f^*` to be **surjective** when the
one-element family is (SAG, Def. A.4.2.2, p. 1779). Prove that the conditions are equivalent (SAG, Prop. A.4.2.1, p.
1778):

1. a monomorphism `u` of `X` is an equivalence exactly when every `fα^*(u)` is;
2. a morphism `u` is an effective epimorphism exactly when every `fα^*(u)` is;
3. for each `n ≥ 0`, `u` is n-connective exactly when every `fα^*(u)` is;
4. `u` is ∞-connective exactly when every `fα^*(u)` is.

Proof route: (1) ⇒ (2) applies (1) to the image of `u`, the monomorphism part of its
(effective epimorphism, monomorphism) factorization (HTT, Ex. 5.2.8.16, case n = −1), which every `fα^*`
preserves (HTT, Prop. 6.5.1.16 (4)). (2) ⇒ (3) is induction with the diagonal criterion (HTT,
Prop. 6.5.1.18). (3) ⇒ (4) is immediate. (4) ⇒ (1): an ∞-connective monomorphism is an
equivalence, since its diagonal is one.

- If `X` has enough points in the sense of Part InfinityTopoi §10.6 (HTT, Rem. 6.5.4.7), its points
  form a jointly surjective family by (1), and for hypercomplete `X` the converse holds by (4).

**Checks.**

- The identity of `X` is surjective. The empty family is jointly surjective only for the trivial
  ∞-topos, because every map `∅ → 1` would have to be an effective epimorphism.
- The hypercompletion `L : X → X^∧` is surjective (SAG, Ex. A.4.2.4, p. 1779), although it is not
  an equivalence when `X` is not hypercomplete. So surjectivity detects ∞-connective maps, not
  equivalences, which rules out the reading "jointly conservative".
- For the one-point space `T`, `Shv(T)` is `S` and its stalk is the identity, a jointly surjective
  family by the first Check above. That the stalk functors are jointly surjective for every topological
  space is the stalk lemma of §10.5.

### 10.2 Sheaves on complete Boolean algebras

Use Mathlib's `CompleteBooleanAlgebra` for complete Boolean algebras. Such a `Λ` is a locale, so
it has a 0-localic ∞-topos `Shv(Λ)` (SAG, §A.4.1, p. 1775; Part InfinityTopoi §18.1 (HTT,
Def. 6.4.2.3), §20.2 (HTT, Prop. 6.4.5.7)). Prove that for a complete Boolean algebra `Λ`:

1. every 0-connective object of `Shv(Λ)` has a global section, so `Shv(Λ)` has homotopy
   dimension ≤ 0 (SAG, Prop. A.4.1.3, p. 1775);
2. `Shv(Λ)` is locally of homotopy dimension ≤ 0 (SAG, Cor. A.4.1.4, p. 1776);
3. `Shv(Λ)` is hypercomplete (SAG, Cor. A.4.1.5, p. 1776).

Proof route:

- (1): if `X` is 0-connective with no global section, build by transfinite induction a strictly
  increasing chain of `U_β ∈ Λ` with sections of `X` over them. At a successor stage the complement
  of the last `U` contains a nonzero `U'` over which `X` has a section, and since `U ∧ U' = ⊥` the
  sections glue over `U ∨ U'`; at limit stages, amalgamate. A chain longer than `|Λ|` is impossible.
- (2): the `χ_U`, `U ∈ Λ`, generate under colimits, and `Shv(Λ)_{/χ_U} ≃ Shv({V : V ≤ U})`, a
  complete Boolean algebra to which (1) applies.
- (3): an ∞-topos locally of finite homotopy dimension is hypercomplete (§3.5; HTT, Cor. 7.2.1.12).

*Needs:* for (1), §3.1.1 (homotopy dimension, HTT, Def. 7.2.1.1), Part InfinityTopoi §14.3.1
(0-connective objects, HTT, Def. 6.5.1.11), §18.1.1 (HTT, Def. 6.4.2.3) and §20.2.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.4.5.7),

### 10.4 Stone covers

**10.4.1.** Prove that for a small ∞-category `C` with finite limits and a finitary topology, there is a
surjective geometric morphism `h^* : Shv(C) → Shv(T)` with `T` a Stone space (SAG, Lemma A.4.4.3,
p. 1784).

Proof route:

- The comparison with the Stone space: for a complete Boolean algebra `Λ`, `ι : Λ → U(Spec(Λ))`,
  `λ ↦ Spec(Λ)_λ`, has image the clopen sets (§7.4) and a left adjoint
  `φ(U) = ⋁{λ : Spec(Λ)_λ ⊆ U}` preserving finite meets and arbitrary joins. This frame map gives
  `f^* : Shv(Spec(Λ)) → Shv(Λ)` (SAG, §A.4.4, p. 1783). Then `f_*` is fully faithful, so
  `f^* f_* ≃ id`, and for a finite family with an effective epimorphism `⊔ Xᵢ → Y` in `Shv(Λ)`,
  the map `⊔ f_* Xᵢ → f_* Y` is an effective epimorphism (SAG, Lemma A.4.4.1, p. 1783). Full
  faithfulness holds because `f_*` is right Kan extension along the fully faithful `ι` (HTT,
  Prop. 4.3.2.15). For the second claim reduce to `Y = χ_U`; each `Xᵢ → χ_{Uᵢ}` has a section by
  §10.2 (1), and finitely many clopens `Uᵢ` cover `U` in the compact `Spec(Λ)`.
- Factorization: every `g^* : Shv(C) → Shv(Λ)` factors as `f^* ∘ h^*` with
  `h^* : Shv(C) → Shv(Spec(Λ))` (SAG, Lemma A.4.4.2, p. 1784). The functor `f_* ∘ g^* ∘ j` is left
  exact (HTT, Prop. 5.2.3.5) and sends finite covering families to effective epimorphisms by
  Lemma A.4.4.1 and finitarity, so it induces `h^*` (HTT, Prop. 6.2.3.20), and `f^* h^* ≃ g^*`.
- Choose a surjection `g^*` by Cor. A.4.3.2, factor it, and take `T = Spec(Λ)`, a Stone space by
  §7.4. Surjectivity of `f^* h^* ≃ g^*` gives surjectivity of `h^*`.

### 10.5 The Deligne completeness theorem

**10.5.1.** Prove the stalk lemma used in the proof of §10.5.2: for a topological space `T`, the stalk functors
`t^* : Shv(T) → S`, `t ∈ T`, form a jointly surjective family, and composing a surjective geometric
morphism `h^* : Y → Shv(T)` with them gives a jointly surjective family on `Y` (the step "easy to
see" in SAG, proof of Thm. A.4.0.5, p. 1785). The route stays inside the ∞-topos `Shv(T)` and uses
condition 2 of §10.1.

- The stalk `t^*` is the pullback along the geometric morphism of the frame map `U ↦ U ∩ {t}` from
  the opens of `T` to those of `{t}` (Part InfinityTopoi §20.2; HTT, Prop. 6.4.5.7). It preserves
  colimits and sends the sheaf represented by an open U to `Δ[0]` if `t ∈ U` and to `∅` otherwise.
  Writing F as the colimit of the representables over its elements (U, s), this gives
  `t^* F ≃ colim_{U ∋ t} F(U)`: `t^*χ_U` is `Δ[0]` exactly on the cosieve of elements (U, s) with
  `t ∈ U` and `∅` elsewhere, so the colimit over the elements of F is the colimit over that cosieve,
  which is the colimit over the filtered poset of open neighborhoods of t (HTT, Thm. 5.1.5.6,
  Prop. 3.3.4.5). Since
  π₀ = τ≤0 is a left adjoint (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §6.2), `π₀ t^* F = colim_{U ∋ t} π₀ F(U)`, a
  filtered colimit of sets, whose elements are germs of sections.
- Let `u : F → G` be a morphism of `Shv(T)` with every `π₀ t^*(u)` surjective. By the local-lifting
  step of Part InfinityTopoi §9.2 (HTT, Lemma 6.2.4.5), u is an effective epimorphism as soon as every
  section of G over an open U lifts along u locally, on an open cover of U. Given such a section s and t ∈ U, the germ of s at t
  lifts to a germ of F by surjectivity on π₀, so there are an open `V ∋ t` in U and a section of F
  over V whose image agrees with `s|_V`. These V cover U, so u is an effective epimorphism.
- A morphism of `S` is an effective epimorphism exactly when it is surjective on π₀ (Part InfinityTopoi
  §7.7), so the stalks satisfy condition 2 of §10.1, and the family is jointly surjective. For the composite,
  condition 2 for `h^*` and for the stalks chain.

(HTT, Lemma 6.2.4.5), §10.6.1 (points, HTT, Rem. 6.5.4.7) and §20.2.1 (HTT, Prop. 6.4.5.7); Part

**10.5.2.** Prove the **∞-categorical Deligne completeness theorem**: a locally coherent hypercomplete
∞-topos `X` has enough points. If `α : X → Y` in `X` is not an equivalence, some point
`f^* : X → S` has `f^*(α)` not an equivalence (SAG, Thm. A.4.0.5, p. 1774).

Proof route:

- Points of `X^∧` are the points of `X`, by composing with `L`, because `S` is hypercomplete. So
  the theorem is equivalent to: every coherent ∞-topos has a jointly surjective family of points
  (SAG, Rem. A.4.2.6, p. 1779). This uses the universal property of hypercompletion (§1.3; HTT,
  Prop. 6.5.2.13) and Prop. A.2.2.2 (§8.4). One may assume `X` coherent, testing on a cover by coherent
  objects.
- By Thm. A.3.4.1 (§9.5), `X ≃ Shv(C)^∧` for a small `C` with finite limits and a finitary topology.
- Lemma A.4.4.3 (§10.4) gives a surjection `h^* : Shv(C) → Shv(T)` with `T` a Stone space. The
  stalks at points `t ∈ T` give points of `Shv(C)`, and these are jointly surjective (the stalk
  lemma, §10.5.1).
- In a hypercomplete ∞-topos, a jointly surjective family detects ∞-connective maps, which are
  exactly the equivalences (Prop. A.4.2.1).

This recovers the classical theorem of Deligne for coherent 1-topoi by restricting to discrete
objects (SAG, Cor. A.4.0.6, p. 1774).

(§10.5.1), §8.2.3 (coherent ∞-topoi), §9.3.1, §9.5.1 (Thm. A.3.4.1), §10.1.1 (Prop. A.4.2.1), §10.4.1

Prop. 6.5.2.8).

### Examples

- `Shv(T)^∧` for a coherent topological space `T` has enough points, the points of `T`. This is
  the ∞-categorical form of the statement that a spectral space has enough points.
- For a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme `X`, the hypercomplete étale ∞-topos has enough
  points, by Thm. A.4.0.5 applied to the finitary étale site.
- `Shv(Λ)` for an atomless complete Boolean algebra `Λ`, such as the regular open sets of `[0, 1]`,
  is hypercomplete with no points at all. So the coherence hypothesis cannot be dropped, and the
  Stone cover of Lemma A.4.4.1 is not an equivalence, since `Spec(Λ)` has points.

## Layer 11: Semisimplicial hypercoverings

### 11.1 Local ∞-pretopoi

**11.1.1.** Define a **local ∞-pretopos** to be an ∞-category `C` satisfying the following axioms (SAG,
Def. A.6.1.1, p. 1807):

- (a) `C` has fiber products;
- (b) `C` has finite coproducts, and (b′) they are universal;
- (c) coproducts are disjoint;
- (d) every groupoid object of `C` is effective: it has a geometric realization `|X•|`, and
  `X₁ → X₀ ⇉ |X•|` is a pullback square;
- (d′) geometric realizations of groupoid objects are universal.

`C` is an **∞-pretopos** when it is a local ∞-pretopos with a final object.

- For each object `X` of a local ∞-pretopos `C`, the slice `C_{/X}` is an ∞-pretopos. Conversely,
  an ∞-category with finite coproducts all of whose slices are ∞-pretopoi is a local ∞-pretopos
  (SAG, Rem. A.6.1.3, p. 1808).

**Checks.**

- Every ∞-topos is an ∞-pretopos, by Giraud's axioms (HTT, Thm. 6.1.0.6).
- The finite sets do not form an ∞-pretopos, though they form a 1-categorical pretopos. The
  action of `ℤ/2` on a point is a groupoid object of finite sets. Its colimit there is a point,
  and `ℤ/2 → pt ×_{pt} pt` is not an equivalence, so (d) fails. In `S` its realization is
  `BZ/2`. The finite CW spaces fail already at (a), since `pt ×_{S²} pt = ΩS²` is not finite.
- The nonempty spaces do not form a local ∞-pretopos. There is no initial object, the empty
  coproduct, and fiber products such as `pt ×_{S⁰} pt` over distinct points are missing.

*Needs:* Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §1.2 (final objects, HTT, §1.2.12); Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §9.1.2 (groupoid objects, HTT,
Def. 6.1.2.7); Part InfinityTopoi §1.1.1, §1.1.2 (universal colimits and disjoint coproducts, HTT, §6.1.1); Part
LimitsAndKanExtensions §9.4.2 (geometric realizations) and §9.7.1 (realizations of semisimplicial
objects); Tau Ceti `SSet.slice.IsLimit`.

**11.1.2.** Prove that a local ∞-pretopos `C` has a factorization system `(S_L, S_R)` (SAG, Prop. A.6.2.1, p.
1809). Here `S_L` is the effective epimorphisms and `S_R` the (−1)-truncated morphisms. A morphism
`f : X → Y` is an **effective epimorphism** when its Čech nerve, a groupoid object, has geometric
realization `Y` through `f` (SAG, §A.6.2, p. 1809). Proof route: factor `f` through the realization
of its Čech nerve `Č(f)`, which exists by (d). The first map `X → |Č(f)|` is an effective
epimorphism: by (d), `X ×_{|Č(f)|} X ≃ Č(f)₁ = X ×_Y X`, so its Čech nerve is `Č(f)` itself, whose
realization is `|Č(f)|`. Universality (d′) shows the second map is (−1)-truncated. For
orthogonality, let `f : A → B` be an effective epimorphism with Čech nerve `A•` and `g : X → Y` a
(−1)-truncated morphism, with a square from `f` to `g`. Composition with `g` makes
`Map(B, X) → Map(B, Y)` the inclusion of a union of components, so the space of lifts is empty or
contractible, and it is nonempty when the map `B → Y` lifts. `B` is the realization of `A•`, so
`Map(B, X)` is the limit of the `Map(Aₙ, X)`; the maps `Aₙ → A₀ → X` given by the square lift the
compatible maps `Aₙ → Y`, uniquely up to contractible choice because `g` is (−1)-truncated, and
their limit is the required lift `B → X`. In an ∞-topos these effective epimorphisms are those of
Part InfinityTopoi §7.1, whose condition is the same colimit condition on the Čech nerve.

**Checks.**

- In `S` the effective epimorphisms are the π₀-surjections (Part InfinityTopoi §7.7); `∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]`
  is one.
- `∅ → Δ[0]` is not one: its Čech nerve is empty in every degree and realizes to `∅`. So a
  definition asking only that the Čech nerve have a realization would accept it.
- A monomorphism `X → Y` is an effective epimorphism only if it is an equivalence: `X ×_Y X ≃ X`, so
  its Čech nerve is constant at `X`, with realization `X`. This excludes `∅ → Δ[0]` and the
  inclusion of one point of `∂Δ[1]`.

### 11.2 Semisimplicial Kan fibrations and hypercoverings

**11.2.1.** Define a morphism `f : X• → Y•` of semisimplicial objects of an ∞-topos `X` to be a **Kan
fibration** when each map `X•[Δ[n]] → Y•[Δ[n]] ×_{Y•[Λ[n,i]]} X•[Λ[n,i]]`, for `n > 0` and
`0 ≤ i ≤ n`, is an effective epimorphism. It is a **trivial Kan fibration** when each map
`X•[Δ[n]] → Y•[Δ[n]] ×_{Y•[∂Δ[n]]} X•[∂Δ[n]]`, for `n ≥ 0`, is an effective epimorphism. A
morphism of simplicial objects has either property when its underlying semisimplicial morphism
does (SAG, Def. A.5.2.1, p. 1788).

**Checks.**

- For `n = 0`, the trivial Kan fibration condition says `X₀ → Y₀` is an effective epimorphism,
  since `∂Δ[0] = ∅`. A definition starting at `n = 1` would accept `∅ → Y•` for any `Y•`.
- In `S` with `Y• = Δ[0]`, a semisimplicial set `X•` is a trivial Kan fibration over the point
  exactly when every map `∂Δ[n] → X•` of semisimplicial sets extends to `Δ[n]`. For `n = 1`, this
  asks for an edge between any two vertices.
- Identities are trivial Kan fibrations. The inclusion of `Δ[0]` as the vertex `0` of `Δ[1]`, both
  regarded as semisimplicial sets, is not a Kan fibration. For `n = 1` and `i = 0`, the map
  `X₁ → Y₁ ×_{Y₀} X₀` goes from the one edge of `Δ[0]` to the two edges `00` and `01` of `Δ[1]`
  starting at `0`, so it is not surjective.

*Needs:* Part LimitsAndKanExtensions §9.7.1 (Rem. A.5.1.2), §9.7.3 (`X•[K]`, Ex. A.5.1.8, Prop. A.5.1.9); Part
InfinityTopoi §7.3.2 (effective epimorphisms, HTT, Cor. 6.2.3.5); Part InfinityTopoi §3.1.2 (∞-topoi).

**11.2.2.** Prove that a morphism of simplicial sets is a Kan fibration (trivial Kan fibration) in the sense
of Def. A.5.2.1, regarded as a morphism of levelwise discrete simplicial objects of `S`, exactly
when it is one in the usual sense (SAG, Ex. A.5.2.2, p. 1788). For Kan fibrations, the
`Suggested.lean` declaration `fibration_iff_semisimplicialKanFibration` states the combinatorial
core: a map of simplicial sets is a Mathlib Kan fibration exactly when its underlying map of
semisimplicial sets satisfies the relative horn-filling condition `SemisimplicialKanFibration`.
Proof route: for levelwise discrete
objects, `X•[K]` is the set of semisimplicial maps from the nondegenerate part of `K` to `X•`,
which for a simplicial set is the set of simplicial maps `K → X`. Effective epimorphisms between
discrete spaces are surjections.

*Needs:* §11.2.1 (Def. A.5.2.1); Mathlib `SSet.KanComplex`, `SSet.horn`, `SSet.boundary` and the Kan

**11.2.3.** Define, for a local ∞-pretopos `C` (SAG, Def. A.6.5.1, p. 1817):

- a semisimplicial object `X•` satisfies the **Kan condition** when `Xₙ = X•[Δ[n]] → X•[Λ[n,i]]` is
  an effective epimorphism for `n > 0` and `0 ≤ i ≤ n`. The target exists by Prop. A.5.1.9;
- when `C` is an ∞-pretopos, `X•` is a **hypercovering** when `Xₙ → X•[∂Δ[n]]` is an effective
  epimorphism for every `n ≥ 0`;
- for an object `X`, a semisimplicial object of `C_{/X}` is a hypercovering of `X` when it is a
  hypercovering of the ∞-pretopos `C_{/X}` (Rem. A.6.1.3).

For an ∞-topos `X` the same definitions apply (SAG, Def. A.5.2.3, p. 1788). The unique map
`X• → 1•` to the constant semisimplicial final object is a Kan fibration exactly when `X•`
satisfies the Kan condition, and a trivial Kan fibration exactly when `X•` is a hypercovering
(SAG, Rem. A.5.2.4, pp. 1788–1789).

**Checks.**

- In degree `0`, a hypercovering of `X` has `X₀ → X` an effective epimorphism, since `X•[∂Δ[0]]` is
  the final object `X` of `C_{/X}`. In degree `1`, `X₁ → X₀ ×_X X₀` is an effective epimorphism.
- The Čech nerve of an effective epimorphism `U → X`, as a semisimplicial object of `C_{/X}`, is a
  hypercovering of `X`. Each map `Xₙ → X•[∂Δ[n]]` is an equivalence for `n ≥ 1`, since the nerve
  is 0-coskeletal, and is the effective epimorphism `U → X` for `n = 0`. For `U → X` not an
  effective epimorphism, the Čech nerve is not a hypercovering.
- The two conditions differ. The semisimplicial set underlying the Kan complex `Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0]`
  satisfies the Kan condition but is not a hypercovering of the point, since `X₁ → X₀ × X₀` misses
  the pairs of distinct vertices. The semisimplicial set with two vertices and no edges fails the
  Kan condition, since the horn `Λ[1, 0]` at a vertex needs an edge out of it.
- For a simplicial object `U` of an ∞-topos, `U•[∂Δ[n]]` is `(coskₙ₋₁ U)ₙ`, because the
  nondegenerate simplices of `∂Δ[n]` are cofinal among all its simplices (HTT, Lemma 6.5.3.8). So
  `U` is a hypercovering in the sense of §2.2 (HTT, Def. 6.5.3.2) exactly when its underlying
  semisimplicial object is one here.

### 11.3 Semisimplicial Kan complexes

**11.3.1.** Prove that if `f : X• → Y•` is a Kan fibration of semisimplicial sets and `Y•` underlies a
simplicial set `Y`, then `f` lifts to a Kan fibration of simplicial sets `X → Y`: there is a
simplicial set `X` with underlying semisimplicial set `X•` and a Kan fibration `X → Y` whose
underlying semisimplicial map is `f` (SAG, Prop. A.5.3.4, p. 1791; `Suggested.lean` declaration
`exists_fibration_of_semisimplicialKanFibration`). Here a Kan fibration of semisimplicial sets is a
map with the relative horn-filling condition (`Suggested.lean` declaration
`SemisimplicialKanFibration`), and Kan fibrations of simplicial sets are Mathlib's.
Proof route: by Zorn's lemma, take a maximal partial system of degeneracies on `X•` compatible
with `f` and with those of `Y•`. A missing degeneracy of a simplex is supplied by a lifting problem
against `f` for the inclusion `K• ⊆ Δ[n]`, where `Δ[n]` is regarded as a semisimplicial set,
degenerate simplices included, and `K•` consists of `∂Δ[n]` and the nondegenerate n-simplex: a
k-simplex `[k] → [n]` lies in `K•` exactly when it is an isomorphism or is not surjective. This
inclusion has the left lifting property against Kan fibrations of semisimplicial sets (SAG,
Lemma A.5.3.6, p. 1792). Its proof is by induction on `n`. By the small object argument, the maps
with this lifting property are the weakly saturated class generated by the semisimplicial horn
inclusions. Adjoining a cone point turns a monomorphism of semisimplicial sets into a map of that
class, and turns a map of that class into another after a pushout. From this the nondegenerate
part of `Δ[m]`, glued to any simplicial subset of `∂Δ[n]` along its nondegenerate part, is shown
to include into its cone by such a map, and a retraction of the cone on `Δ[n]` onto `Δ[n]`
exhibits `K• ⊆ Δ[n]` as a retract. The resulting map of simplicial sets is a Kan fibration
because its underlying semisimplicial map is (Ex. A.5.2.2).

### 11.5 The hypercomplete sheaf criterion

**11.5.1.** Let `Δ_{s,+}` be the category of the ordered sets `[n]` for `n ≥ −1`, with `[−1] = ∅`, and strictly
increasing maps. An **augmented semisimplicial object** of an ∞-category `C` is a functor from the
nerve of `Δ_{s,+}ᵒᵖ` to `C`. When `C` has fiber products, the n-th **matching object** `Mₙ(X)`,
for `n ≥ 0`, is the limit of the `Xₘ` over the injections `[m] → [n]` with `m < n`, a finite limit
in the slice over `X₋₁` (second Check). For a class `S` of morphisms, `X•` is an
**S-hypercovering** when every `Xₙ → Mₙ(X)`, `n ≥ 0`, lies in `S` (SAG, Def. A.5.7.1, p. 1805,
where `C` is assumed to have finite limits; only these limits in slices are used).

**Checks.**

- `M₀(X) = X₋₁`, since `[−1]` is the only proper subset of `[0]`. So `X₀ → X₋₁` lies in `S`. And
  `M₁(X) = X₀ ×_{X₋₁} X₀`.
- Every matching object is a limit in the slice over `X₋₁`, where finite limits exist when `C` has
  fiber products. So the hypotheses of Prop. A.3.2.1 suffice to form S-hypercoverings.
- For `S` the effective epimorphisms of an ∞-topos, an S-hypercovering with `X₋₁ = X` is a
  hypercovering of `X` in the sense of §11.2. The augmented Čech nerve of a morphism in `S` is an
  S-hypercovering when `S` contains equivalences.

*Needs:* Mathlib `SemiSimplexCategory`; Tau Ceti `SSet.slice.IsLimit`; Part InfinityCategoryFoundations §1.2.4 (limits, HTT, Def. 1.2.13.4).

**11.5.2.** Call a functor `F : Cᵒᵖ → D` a **hypercomplete D-valued sheaf** when, for every `D ∈ D`, the
composite of `F` with the functor corepresented by `D` is a hypercomplete object of `Shv(C)`. This
is the notion used in SAG, Prop. A.5.7.2, whose proof reduces to `D = S` in this way.

**Checks.**

- For `D = S`, the functor corepresented by Δ[0] is the identity, so the condition contains
  hypercompleteness of `F` in `Shv(C)`; conversely each `Map(D, F(−))` is a limit of copies of `F`,
  and hypercomplete objects, being local (§1.1), are closed under limits, so the notion is hypercompleteness of
  `F`.
- For `D = S × S` and `F = (F₁, F₂)`, the objects `(Δ[0], ∅)` and `(∅, Δ[0])` corepresent `F₁` and `F₂`, so `F`
  qualifies exactly when both are hypercomplete. A definition testing one object of `D` would accept `(1, F₂)` with
  `F₂` the sheaf of the next Check.
- For `C` the opens of the Hilbert cube with open covers and `D = S`, some sheaf is not hypercomplete
  (HTT, Counterexample 6.5.4.2, p. 700), so the condition is not implied by the sheaf condition.

**11.5.3.** Prove the **hypercomplete sheaf criterion**. Let `C` and `S` satisfy the hypotheses of
Prop. A.3.2.1 and condition (e) of Prop. A.3.3.1, and give `C` the topology of Prop. A.3.2.1. Then
a functor `F : Cᵒᵖ → D` to any ∞-category is a hypercomplete D-valued sheaf exactly when two
conditions hold (SAG, Prop. A.5.7.2, p. 1806):

1. `F` preserves finite products;
2. for every S-hypercovering `X• : Δ_{s,+}ᵒᵖ → C`, the composite of `X•ᵒᵖ` with `F` is a limit
   diagram.

Proof route: reduce to `D = S`. By definition `F` is a hypercomplete D-valued sheaf when each
composite of `F` with a functor corepresented by an object of `D` is one, and (1) and (2) hold for
`F` exactly when they hold for all these composites, since corepresented functors preserve limits
and jointly detect them (HTT, Prop. 5.1.3.2).

- "Only if": condition (1) holds by Prop. A.3.3.1 (§9.4). For (2), show that the sheafified Yoneda image
  of `X•`, followed by hypercompletion, is a colimit diagram. Apply Thm. A.5.3.1 (§11.4) in
  `Shv(C)_{/j(X₋₁)}`. The image is a hypercovering, a trivial Kan fibration to the final object
  (Rem. A.5.2.4).
- "If": `F` is a sheaf by Prop. A.3.3.1, since a Čech nerve is an S-hypercovering. For an
  ∞-connective `α : F → G` with `G` hypercomplete, show `α_C` is n-connective for all `n`, by
  induction using diagonals. The case `n = 0` reduces to an ∞-connective sheaf satisfying (2)
  having a section over a final object.
- That section comes from building an S-hypercovering of `C` and a compatible lift to the right
  fibration classified by `F`, degree by degree. At each degree, use ∞-connectivity to kill the
  sphere `∂Δ[m] → F(Mₘ(X))` on a finite cover in `S`, and use (1) to merge the finite cover. Then
  extend the lift to the augmentation by (2) and HTT, Cor. 3.3.3.3.

*Needs:* §1.2.1 (hypercompletion, HTT, Prop. 6.5.2.8), §9.3.2 (Prop. A.3.2.1), §9.4.1 (Prop. A.3.3.1),
§11.2.3 (Rem. A.5.2.4), §11.4.2 (Thm. A.5.3.1), §11.5.1 (S-hypercoverings); Part InfinityTopoi §8.1.1
(Grothendieck topologies, HTT, Def. 6.2.2.1), §8.2.1 (sheaves, HTT, Def. 6.2.2.6), §8.2.2 (D-valued sheaves,
SAG, Def. 1.3.1.1) and §8.3.1 (left exactness of sheafification, HTT, Prop. 6.2.2.7); Part
PresheavesAndCompletions §1.3.2 (the Yoneda embedding preserves limits, HTT, Prop. 5.1.3.2), so that

### Examples

- For `C` the finite sets and `S` the surjections, conditions (a)–(e) hold. The resulting sheaf
  ∞-topos is `S`, every sheaf is determined by its value on the point, and every sheaf is
  hypercomplete (compare the Examples of Layer 9).
- Take `C` the affine schemes étale over a fixed affine base and `S` the étale surjections. Then
  Prop. A.5.7.2 says that a functor on `C` is a hypercomplete étale sheaf exactly when it takes
  finite disjoint unions to products and satisfies descent along étale hypercoverings.
- The semisimplicial set underlying any Kan complex is a semisimplicial Kan complex. Conversely,
  Cor. A.5.3.5 gives every semisimplicial Kan complex degeneracies, though not uniquely.

## Layer 12: Postnikov complete ∞-topoi

### 12.1 Postnikov complete ∞-topoi

**12.1.1.** Define `∞Top^c ⊆ ∞Top` to be the full subcategory of ∞-topoi that are Postnikov complete in the
sense of Part PresentableInfinityCategories §6.4 (SAG, Def. A.7.2.1, p. 1828): the functor from X to the limit of
its truncations τ≤n X, sending an object to its tower of truncations, is an equivalence; by Part
PresentableInfinityCategories §6.4 this is the convergence of Postnikov towers in X.

- A Postnikov complete ∞-topos is hypercomplete, since every object is the limit of its Postnikov
  tower and an ∞-connective morphism is an equivalence on every truncation (SAG, Rem. A.7.2.3,
  p. 1829). The same remark asserts that the converse fails; no target here uses or proves that.
- An ∞-topos locally of finite homotopy dimension has convergent Postnikov towers (§3.4), so it lies
  in `∞Top^c`.
- A geometric morphism between Postnikov complete ∞-topoi is a morphism of `∞Top^c`; there is no
  further condition, since the subcategory is full.

**Checks.**

- `S` lies in `∞Top^c`, since it is Postnikov complete (Part PresentableInfinityCategories §6.4, Checks).
- `Shv(T)` for the Hilbert cube `T` is an ∞-topos that is not hypercomplete (HTT, Counterexample
  6.5.4.2, p. 700, recorded in §1.1), so by the first bullet it does not lie in
  `∞Top^c`; the subcategory is proper.
- The trivial ∞-topos Δ[0] lies in `∞Top^c`, every tower in it being constant at the final object.

  Def. 6.5.1.10)

### Examples

- `S` and every presheaf ∞-topos P(C) are Postnikov complete: Postnikov towers converge in them
  (§3.4, P(C) being locally of homotopy dimension ≤ 0 by §3.3) and limits of truncation towers are
  computed objectwise.
- `Shv(T)` for the Hilbert cube `T` is not hypercomplete, so it is not Postnikov complete
  (Rem. A.7.2.3).
