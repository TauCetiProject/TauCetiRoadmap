### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Nodal families**: being at worst nodal is syntomic of relative dimension one with unramified singular locus. It is local on the source, stable under base change, and holds for smooth curves ([`AtWorstNodalOfRelativeDimensionOne`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/Node/Relative.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.AtWorstNodalOfRelativeDimensionOne)). Prestable curves and maps are built on it.
- **Relative Proj**: Proj of a graded quasi-coherent algebra over any scheme, glued from affine charts and proper when the algebra is of finite type ([`isProper_relativeProjToBase`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/RelativeProj/Proper.html#CategoryTheory.CommMon.isProper_relativeProjToBase)). Blowups of schemes and the later contractions need it.
- **Dual graphs and stabilization**: abstract dual graphs with genus, log-canonical degree, clutching and tail-and-bridge contraction. Stabilization terminates in the stable range ([`DualGraph.exists_reflTransGen_stabilizationStep_isStable`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Curves/DualGraph/Contraction.html#TauCeti.DualGraph.exists_reflTransGen_stabilizationStep_isStable)), with a degree-decorated version for maps. Nothing yet attaches a dual graph to an actual curve.

## The frontier

- **Genus-zero nodal reduction in general (Layer 7)**: produce a degree-one divisor after a separable extension of degree at most two.
- **Nodal families, fibrewise (Layer 1)**: the equivalence with "pure one-dimensional fibres with at worst nodes", and the étale local normal form uv = πⁿ.
- **Stability of curves and maps (Layers 3 and 10)**: `StableFamily` and `IsStableMap` as fibrewise positivity of log-canonical degree. This needs dual graphs of actual geometric fibres, which in turn need scheme normalization.
- **Blowups of schemes (Layer 4)**: the blowup of a quasi-coherent ideal as relative Proj, then exceptional divisors and strict transforms.
- **Numerical type of a regular model (Layers 5–6)**: intersection numbers of vertical components, so that the finished combinatorics can be applied to geometry.
