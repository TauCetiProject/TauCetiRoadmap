# Roadmap: Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models and Hanf numbers for Lω₁ω

A first-order theory in a countable language with an infinite model has models of every infinite
cardinality. An Lω₁ω sentence need not, even in a countable language: the Scott sentence of a
countable structure can have only countable models, and for every countable ordinal α there are
sentences whose largest models have cardinality exactly ℶ_(α+1). The **Morley–Hanf theorem** says
that this is the whole story: if an Lω₁ω sentence has models of cardinality at least ℶ_α for every α
< ω₁, it has models of every infinite cardinality. Together with those bounded examples it computes
the **Hanf number** of the logic,

```math
\mathrm{Hanf}(L_{\omega_1\omega})=\beth_{\omega_1}.
```

The proof is the prototype of a method used throughout model theory: partition calculus turns large
models into **indiscernible** configurations, those configurations are recorded as an
**Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski template**, and the template is stretched, over any linear order, into a
model generated by indiscernibles. Over a highly order-transitive order the stretched model has many
automorphisms and therefore realizes only countably many complete types. That gives the
**small-model theorem**: under the same hypothesis the sentence has, in every infinite cardinality,
a model realizing only countably many Lω₁ω-types. In a countable relational language the small
structures are exactly those satisfying a complete sentence, which gives **complete, κ-categorical
strengthenings** of κ-categorical sentences.

This roadmap specifies that development as a library: the finite-exponent Erdős–Rado theorem
with its end-homogeneity engine, indiscernible sequences and templates for Lω₁ω, Skolem
expansions of a countable set of formulas, the Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski term model with its truth
lemma and functoriality, the Morley–Hanf theorem with exact cardinalities, the sharpness
sentences, and the small-model and completion theory.

It continues the [InfinitaryLogic roadmap](../InfinitaryLogic/README.md), whose "Out of scope"
list names Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski stretching, partition calculus and Morley–Hanf, and it consumes
the [InfinitaryModelExistence](../InfinitaryModelExistence/README.md) ([#1315](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/pull/1315)) roadmap for the specific interfaces listed under
[Existing library and ownership](#existing-library-and-ownership).

## Conventions

1. **Languages and universes.** Languages are `L : FirstOrder.Language.{u, v}` of arbitrary
   cardinality. Every statement about models fixes one carrier universe `w` and quantifies over `M :
   Type w`; cardinals are `Cardinal.{w}` and ordinals `Ordinal.{w}`. A sentence mentions only
   countably many symbols, so the constructions of Layers 2–3 run over a countable language in
   `Language.{0, 0}`: the small presentation `smallPresentation` (IME 0.3) of the sublanguage of
   symbols occurring in the sentence with dummy symbols added, extended by Skolem functions.
   Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models over `J : Type w` then live in `Type w`. Results are stated over the
   original `L` and carriers in `Type w`.
2. **Formulas.** Lω₁ω is the parent's `BoundedFormulaω L`, with `Sentenceω L` its sentences and
   `BoundedFormulaInf.Realize`, `SentenceInf.Realize` its semantics. Formulas used as properties of
   tuples are `BoundedFormulaω L Empty n`, with the tuple in the `n` bound positions, as in the
   parent's tuple-indexed constructions. A set of such formulas of varying arity is a
   `Set (Σ n, BoundedFormulaω L Empty n)`.
3. **Tuples from a linear order.** An increasing `n`-tuple from a linear order `J` is an order
   embedding `Fin n ↪o J`. In a linear order these are the same as `n`-element subsets, and
   `Finset.orderEmbOfFin` converts between them. Homogeneity, indiscernibility and templates are
   stated with `Fin n ↪o J` throughout.
4. **`ω₁` and beth numbers.** Use `Ordinal.omega 1` with the scoped notation `ω₁`, and
   `Cardinal.beth` with the scoped notation `ℶ_`. Mathlib's `ℶ_ 0 = ℵ₀` and `ℶ_ (succ o) = 2 ^ ℶ_
   o`. The iterated exponential `ℶₙ(κ)` is a new definition `iteratedTwoPow κ n` (`ℶ₀(κ) = κ`,
   `ℶₙ₊₁(κ) = 2 ^ ℶₙ(κ)`), with `iteratedTwoPow (ℶ_ α) n = ℶ_ (α + n)`.
5. **The Morley–Hanf hypothesis.** "φ has models of cardinality at least ℶ_α for every α < ω₁" is
   written out as `∀ α < ω₁, ∃ M, M ⊨ φ ∧ ℶ_ α ≤ #M`. It is weaker than having one model of
   cardinality at least `ℶ_ω₁`, and every Layer 3 and Layer 5 headline is stated under it. This
   cofinal formulation is the convention by which Keisler and Knight define the Hanf number (§1.6
   of "Barwise: infinitary logic and admissible sets"): the least `κ` such that every sentence
   having, for each `μ < κ`, a model of cardinality at least `μ` has models of arbitrarily large
   cardinality. At `κ = ℶ_ω₁` the two forms agree, since the `ℶ_α` with `α < ω₁` are cofinal below
   `ℶ_ω₁`. `IsHanfBound` (Convention 6) is the single-model form, implied by the cofinal one.
6. **Arbitrarily large models.** `HasArbLargeModels φ` means a model of cardinality at least `κ`
   for every `κ : Cardinal.{w}` (these models are nonempty from `κ = 1` on); `IsHanfBound φ κ`
   means that a **nonempty** model of cardinality at least `κ` implies `HasArbLargeModels φ`;
   `hanfNumber φ` is the least Hanf bound. The Hanf predicates count nonempty models only, as
   IME's satisfiability conventions do. With empty models counted, the Hanf number would not be
   invariant under language expansion: over the empty language `∀x ⊥` has only the empty model
   and Hanf number `1`, while its image in a language with one constant has no model and Hanf
   number `0`. The Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski construction of Layer 2 still allows empty index orders
   and empty structures.
7. **Complete sentences** are semantic: `σ` is complete when, for every Lω₁ω sentence `ψ` of the
   same language, all models of `σ` satisfy `ψ` or all satisfy `¬ψ`. `κ`-categoricity of `φ` means
   any two models of `φ` of cardinality `κ` are isomorphic (`M ≃[L] N`).
8. **No bundled hypotheses.** Countability of a language is carried as
   `[Countable (Σ n, L.Functions n)]` and `[Countable (Σ n, L.Relations n)]`, relationality as
   `[L.IsRelational]`, each only where used, as in the parent roadmap.
9. **Indiscernibles live in constructed models.** No theorem here extracts a fully indiscernible
   `ω`-sequence for a countable set of formulas from the given large model. Such an extraction for
   all definable colourings would give the partition relation `ℶ_ω₁ → (ω)^{<ω}_2`, which is false:
   the least cardinal with that property is the Erdős cardinal `κ(ω)`, which is inaccessible and so
   larger than every `ℶ_α` with `α` below it. The
   given models supply only homogeneous sets for finitely many formulas at a time (Layer 3). (For
   the comparison, code a colouring of the increasing `n`-tuples of a well-ordered carrier, for
   each `n`, by an `n`-ary relation symbol of a countable language with an order symbol.)

## Layer 0 — The partition calculus of the Morley–Hanf proof

Suggested home: `TauCeti/SetTheory/Cardinal/Partition/{Basic, EndHomogeneous, ErdosRado}.lean`.

This layer is pure combinatorics with no logic in it. It owns exactly the partition results that
the Morley–Hanf construction uses: the pigeonhole principle for successor cardinals, end-homogeneous
sets, the finite-exponent Erdős–Rado theorem with the colour bound `κ`, and the `ω₁`-pigeonhole
principle. Infinite Ramsey and the rest of the partition calculus are
[out of scope](#out-of-scope).

### 0.1 Vocabulary

For a linear order `I` and a colour type `C`:

- `IsHomogeneousOn c H` for `c : (Fin n ↪o I) → C` and `H : Set I`: any two increasing `n`-tuples
  with entries in `H` have the same colour. For `n = 0` every set is homogeneous.
- `IsEndHomogeneousOn c H` for `c : (Fin (n + 2) ↪o I) → C`: two increasing tuples from `H` that
  agree on their first `n + 1` entries have the same colour.
- `PartitionRel κ λ n μ`, written `κ → (λ)ⁿ_μ`: for every linear order `J : Type u` with `#J = κ`
  and every colouring of `Fin n ↪o J` by a type of cardinality at most `μ`, some `H` with
  `λ ≤ #H` is homogeneous.

API: homogeneity is inherited by subsets and transported along order embeddings and order
isomorphisms; the restriction of a colouring along an order embedding has the pulled-back
homogeneous sets; `IsHomogeneousOn` implies `IsEndHomogeneousOn`; `PartitionRel.mono` (larger
source, smaller target, fewer colours); and `partitionRel_iff_finset`, the order-free form with
colourings of `n`-element subsets `{s : Finset X // s.card = n}`. The order-free form shows that
`PartitionRel` does not depend on the linear order chosen on a set of cardinality `κ`, so a
statement proved for well-orders (`κ.ord.ToType`) holds for every linear order of that cardinality.

### 0.2 Pigeonhole

- `partitionRel_succ_one`: for infinite `κ`, `κ⁺ → (κ⁺)¹_κ`. A map from a set of cardinality `κ⁺`
  to a set of cardinality at most `κ` has a fibre of cardinality `κ⁺`, by
  `Cardinal.infinite_pigeonhole_card` and `Cardinal.isRegular_succ`.
- `exists_unbounded_fiber`: a map from the countable ordinals `Set.Iio ω₁` to a countable type takes
  some value at unboundedly many ordinals below `ω₁`. A bounded subset of `ω₁` is countable
  (`Cardinal.countable_toType_of_lt_omega_one`), so a countable union of bounded sets is bounded by
  `Cardinal.isRegular_aleph_one`. The finite-colour case is the one the Morley–Hanf decisions use;
  the countable case is the one its witness choices use.
- A downward-closed predicate on `Set.Iio ω₁` that holds at unboundedly many ordinals holds at
  all of them. Layer 3 applies this to "approximable at level `α`".

### 0.3 End-homogeneous sets

**`exists_isEndHomogeneousOn`.** Let `λ` be infinite, `I` well-ordered with `(2^λ)⁺ ≤ #I`, and
`c : (Fin (n + 2) ↪o I) → C` with `#C ≤ λ`. Then some `H ⊆ I` with `λ⁺ ≤ #H` is end-homogeneous
for `c`.

The proof is the Erdős–Rado tree argument. Build, by transfinite recursion along the well-order of
`I`, a tree whose nodes at level `β` record, for a branch of length `β`, the colour of a new point
against every increasing `(n + 1)`-tuple from the branch below it. A node at level `β < λ⁺` is a
function from `(n + 1)`-tuples of a set of cardinality at most `λ` to `C`, so each level has at
most `2^λ` nodes. Every point of `I` determines a path through the tree, so a source of cardinality
`(2^λ)⁺` forces a live branch of length `λ⁺`, and the representatives along that branch form an
end-homogeneous set. The level bound needs only `#C ≤ λ`; the argument compares colours only
against tuples from the branch prefix, never against all lower tuples of `I`, whose colour
functions would range over a set too large to bound.

Also export the `n = 0` instance as the pair form: from `(2^λ)⁺ ≤ #I`, a set of cardinality `λ⁺`
on which the colour of a pair depends only on its smaller point. With pigeonhole this gives
`(2^λ)⁺ → (λ⁺)²_λ`, the `n = 1` case of the Erdős–Rado theorem.

### 0.4 The Erdős–Rado theorem

**`erdos_rado`.** For infinite `κ` and every `n : ℕ`,

```math
\beth_n(\kappa)^+\longrightarrow(\kappa^+)^{n+1}_\kappa .
```

Induction on `n`. The case `n = 0` is `partitionRel_succ_one`. For the step, a colouring of
increasing `(n + 2)`-tuples of a set of cardinality `ℶₙ₊₁(κ)⁺ = (2^{ℶₙ(κ)})⁺` (well-ordered, by
`exists_wellFoundedLT` and the order-free form) has, by 0.3 with `λ = ℶₙ(κ) ≥ κ`, an
end-homogeneous set `H` of cardinality `ℶₙ(κ)⁺`. Remove the largest element of `H`, if there is
one. On what remains, the colour of an `(n + 2)`-tuple is a function of its first `n + 1` entries,
and every increasing `(n + 1)`-tuple has a point of `H` above it, so this defines an induced
colouring of `(n + 1)`-tuples with at most `κ` colours. The induction hypothesis gives a subset of
cardinality `κ⁺` homogeneous for the induced colouring, and hence for the original one.

Consumer forms, each a stated theorem:

- **`erdos_rado_beth`**: `ℶ_(α+n)⁺ → (ℶ_α⁺)ⁿ⁺¹_{ℶ_α}` for every ordinal `α`, from
  `iteratedTwoPow_beth`. Since `ℶ_(α+n)⁺ ≤ ℶ_(α+n+1)`, a set of cardinality at least `ℶ_(α+n+1)`
  suffices.

**Acceptance check:** `erdos_rado` at `n = 1`, i.e. `(2^κ)⁺ → (κ⁺)²_κ`, and at `κ = ℵ₀`, i.e.
`ℶ₁⁺ → (ℵ₁)²_{ℵ₀}`.

⚠ **API warning.** The partition relation must quantify over all colour types of cardinality at
most `κ`, not over `Bool`. The Morley–Hanf witness choices colour tuples by the index of a true
disjunct, a countable colour set. In the induction step the induced colouring has as many colours
as the original. A two-colour statement cannot serve either.

⚠ **API warning.** Do not try to obtain one `ω₁`-sized set homogeneous for all finite arities at
once from a source of cardinality `ℶ_ω₁`. That is the relation `ℶ_ω₁ → (ω₁)^{<ω}_2`, which already
fails with output `ω` (Convention 9). Each application of Erdős–Rado here concerns finitely many
arities.

## Layer 1 — Indiscernible sequences and templates

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/EM/{Indiscernible, Template}.lean`.

### 1.1 Indiscernibility

For a linear order `J`, a structure `M`, a sequence `a : J → M` and a set
`Δ : Set (Σ n, BoundedFormulaω L Empty n)`:

- `IsIndiscernibleOn a Δ`: for every `⟨n, ψ⟩ ∈ Δ` and all `s t : Fin n ↪o J`,
  `ψ.Realize Empty.elim (a ∘ s) ↔ ψ.Realize Empty.elim (a ∘ t)`.
- `IsIndiscernible a`: indiscernibility for every formula of every arity.

API, each a target: monotonicity in `Δ`; restriction along order embeddings `K ↪o J`
(`IsIndiscernibleOn.comp`) and transport along order isomorphisms; invariance of indiscernibility
under isomorphisms of the structure; closure of `Δ` under the operations that preserve it: negation,
implication, the countable connectives `iSup`/`iInf`, and reindexing of bound positions along an
order embedding `Fin n ↪o Fin m` (the parent's `mapBound`). Quantifiers do not preserve
indiscernibility, and the docstring says so.

Indiscernibility of an injective sequence for the equality formulas is automatic, and the
sequence's increasing tuples realize the same equality pattern.

### 1.2 Templates

- `EMTemplate L`: for each arity `n`, a set of formulas `BoundedFormulaω L Empty n` declared true
  of increasing tuples.
- `templateOf a`: the formulas true at every increasing tuple of `a`.
- `EMTemplate.RealizesOn Φ a Δ`: for each `⟨n, ψ⟩ ∈ Δ` and each `t : Fin n ↪o J`, `ψ` holds at
  `a ∘ t` exactly when `Φ` declares it true.
- **Coherence**: `Φ` is coherent on a set `Δ` closed under reindexing when `ψ ∈ Φ` iff
  `mapBound r ψ ∈ Φ` for every order embedding `r : Fin n ↪o Fin m` with `ψ ∈ Δ`.

Theorems: on an infinite index order, `IsIndiscernibleOn a Δ ↔ (templateOf a).RealizesOn a Δ`
(`isIndiscernibleOn_iff_realizesOn_templateOf`); the template of an indiscernible sequence is
coherent on any reindexing-closed `Δ`; a subsequence along `K ↪o J` with `K` infinite has the
same template on `Δ`; and if `b : K → N` realizes the template of `a` on `Δ` then `b` is
indiscernible on `Δ`.

### 1.3 The Morley seed

`disEq : BoundedFormulaω L Empty 2` is `¬(x₀ = x₁)`, and `morleySeed φ = {⟨0, φ⟩, ⟨2, disEq⟩}`.
`isIndiscernibleOn_morleySeed`: every injective sequence in a model of `φ` is indiscernible for
the Morley seed, since the sentence ignores the tuple and `x₀ ≠ x₁` holds at every increasing pair.
If `b : J → N` realizes the template of such a sequence on the seed, then `N ⊨ φ` and `b` is
injective, so `#J ≤ #N`.

**Acceptance check:** `isIndiscernibleOn_morleySeed`, compiled on the parent's Layer 0 alone.

⚠ **API warning.** Realizability of a template is not a finite-character property in Lω₁ω. Over
unary predicates `Pᵢ`, let `M` have elements of every finite height with `Pᵢ x ↔ i ≤ height x`,
and `a n` an element of height `n`. Every `Pᵢ` is eventually true along `a`, and `⋀ᵢ Pᵢ x` is never
true, so the eventual template contains every `Pᵢ(x₀)` and `¬⋀ᵢ Pᵢ(x₀)`: each finite part is
realized and the whole is not. Templates used for stretching therefore carry the witness
conditions of Layer 2, and no statement here asserts that a finitely realized template is
realizable.

## Layer 2 — Skolem towers and Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/EM/{SkolemTower, SkolemTemplate, TermModel,
Stretch}.lean`.

### 2.1 The Skolem tower of a countable set of formulas

Fix a countable language `L₀ : Language.{0, 0}` and a countable set `Γ₀` of `L₀`-formulas in bound
positions containing `x₀ = x₁` and the atomic formula `R(x₀, …, x_{l-1})` for every relation symbol
`R` of `L₀`. Build, by recursion on `k : ℕ`, languages `L_k` and countable formula sets `Γ_k`:

- `L_{k+1} = L_k.sum (skolemSymbols Γ_k)`, where `skolemSymbols Γ_k` has one `n`-ary function
  symbol `f_ψ` for each `ψ ∈ Γ_k` of arity `n + 1` (the `n + 1`-st position is the witnessed
  variable) and no relation symbols. This is the shape of Mathlib's `Language.skolem₁`, restricted
  to `Γ_k`.
- `Γ_{k+1}` is the closure of the image of `Γ_k` in `L_{k+1}` (under `onBoundedFormulaInf` of the
  inclusion, which commutes with term substitution by `onBoundedFormulaInf_substBounds`), together
  with the Skolem formulas `∀-closure of (∃y ψ(x̄, y) → ψ(x̄, f_ψ(x̄)))` for `ψ ∈ Γ_k`, under
  subformulas (including every component of `iSup`/`iInf`), reindexing of bound positions along any
  map `Fin n → Fin m` (`mapBound`), substitution of `L_{k+1}`-terms for bound positions (IME's
  `substBounds`), and negation.

The Skolem language `L*` is the directed union of the `L_k` (a directed colimit of languages along
the inclusions `LHom.sumInl`), and `Γ*` the union of the images of the `Γ_k`. Targets:
`L*` has countably many function and relation symbols; `Γ*` is countable; `Γ*` is closed under the
operations above, and is in particular a `Fragment L*` in IME's sense; every `ψ ∈ Γ*` of arity
`n + 1` has its Skolem symbol in `L*`; the inclusion `L₀ →ᴸ L*` is an `LHom` along which
`onBoundedFormulaInf` transports formulas with `realize_onBoundedFormulaInf`.

The **Skolem axiom sentence** `sk(Γ₀)` is the countable conjunction, over `ψ ∈ Γ*`, of the
universal closures of `∃y ψ(x̄, y) → ψ(x̄, f_ψ(x̄))`, coded into the carrier `ℕ` with
`iInfAlong`. Every nonempty `L₀`-structure has an expansion to `L*` satisfying `sk(Γ₀)`: interpret
`f_ψ(x̄)` as a witness for `∃y ψ` when there is one and arbitrarily otherwise (choice). Every
`L*`-substructure of a model of `sk(Γ₀)` is `Γ*`-elementary (`AElementary Γ*`), by IME's
`aElementary_of_tarskiVaught` with the values of the Skolem functions as the required witnesses,
and the substructure generated by a set `X` has cardinality at most `max ℵ₀ #X` (Mathlib's
`Substructure.closure` bounds, `L*` being countable).

### 2.2 Skolem templates

Fix a language `L'`, a set `Γ` of `L'`-formulas in bound positions, closed under constructor
components and containing `x₀ = x₁` and every atom `R(x̄)`, and a **witness assignment** `w`
giving, for each member `∀y ψ` of `Γ` of arity `n`, an `L'`-term `w_{∀yψ}` in `n` variables. An
`L'`-structure `N` **obeys `w`** when, for every such member and every `b̄`, if `ψ(b̄, y)` fails for
some `y` then it fails at `y = w_{∀yψ}(b̄)`. An **instance** `ψ(τ̄)` is a member `ψ` of `Γ` of
arity `n` with an `n`-tuple `τ̄` of `L'`-terms in `m` variables. A **Skolem template** for
`(Γ, w)` is a predicate `Φ` on instances such that:

1. **(coherence)** `Φ ψ(τ̄[r]) ↔ Φ ψ(τ̄)` for every order embedding `r : Fin m ↪o Fin k`, where
   `τ̄[r]` relabels the variables along `r`;
2. **(finite satisfiability)** for every `m` and every finite set `Δ` of instances in `m` variables
   there are an `L'`-structure `N : Type w` obeying `w` and `b : Fin m → N` with
   `N ⊨ ψ(τ̄(b)) ↔ Φ ψ(τ̄)` for every `ψ(τ̄) ∈ Δ` (the universe `w` of the witness structures is a
   parameter; the term model of 2.3 uses them only propositionally);
3. **(ω-completeness)** if `Φ (iSup ψs)(τ̄)` then `Φ (ψs i)(τ̄)` for some `i`, and if
   `¬Φ (iInf ψs)(τ̄)` then `¬Φ (ψs i)(τ̄)` for some `i`.

By coherence, finitely many instances in different numbers of variables can be lifted to one, so
(2) is the finite satisfiability of the whole assignment. There are two instances, and they share
the one term model of 2.3:

- **`isSkolemTemplate_templateOf`**: for an Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski source `(N, a)` on `Γ` (2.4),
  with `w` the Skolem terms of its condition (3), the template of `a` on instances,
  `Φ ψ(τ̄) :↔ N ⊨ ψ(τ̄(a ∘ s))` for any increasing `s`, is a Skolem template for `(Γ, w)`. The
  choice of `s` and coherence are term indiscernibility, `N` itself witnesses (2) at `b = a ∘ s`,
  and ω-completeness holds because each disjunct of a member is a member, itself constant along
  increasing tuples.
- the template of Layer 3 on `(Γ*, w)` over `L*`, with `w_{∀yψ} = f_{¬ψ}`: every model of
  `sk(Γ₀)` obeys this `w`, and since `Γ*` is closed under substitution of terms, the template is
  read on instances through its values on members (`isSkolemTemplate_of_approximated`, 3.4).

### 2.3 The Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski term model

For a Skolem template `Φ` for `(Γ, w)` and any linear order `J : Type w'`, define `EM(J, Φ)`:

- **elements**: pairs `(τ, t)` of an `L'`-term `τ` in `m` bound variables and `t : Fin m ↪o J`,
  modulo `(τ, t) ~ (τ', t')` when, for the increasing enumeration `u : Fin k ↪o J` of
  `range t ∪ range t'` and the factorizations `t = u ∘ r`, `t' = u ∘ r'`, `Φ` declares the
  equation `τ[r] = τ'[r']` true. The relation is independent of the enumeration by coherence, and
  is an equivalence relation and a congruence by finite satisfiability;
- **functions and relations** of `L'` act on representatives through a common enumeration in the
  same way;
- the **skeleton** `c : J → EM(J, Φ)`, `c j = (x₀, j)`.

Targets:

- **`realize_em_iff` (truth lemma).** For an instance `ψ(τ̄)` in `m` variables and
  `t : Fin m ↪o J`, `EM(J, Φ)` satisfies `ψ` at the values of `τ̄` at `t` exactly when `Φ ψ(τ̄)`.
  Proof by induction on `ψ`, for all `m`, `τ̄`, `t`: atoms by definition (every atom is an instance
  of `x₀ = x₁` or of some `R(x̄)`); `falsum` and implication by finite satisfiability;
  `iSup`/`iInf` by ω-completeness and finite satisfiability. For `∀y χ`: if `Φ (∀y χ)(τ̄)` and
  `(σ, t')` is an element, enumerate `range t ∪ range t'` by `u` with `t = u ∘ r`, `t' = u ∘ r'`;
  coherence gives `Φ (∀y χ)(τ̄[r])`, and finite satisfiability on
  `{(∀y χ)(τ̄[r]), χ(τ̄[r], σ[r'])}` gives `Φ χ(τ̄[r], σ[r'])`. If `¬Φ (∀y χ)(τ̄)`, finite
  satisfiability on `{(∀y χ)(τ̄), χ(τ̄, w_{∀yχ}(τ̄))}` in a structure obeying `w` gives
  `¬Φ χ(τ̄, w_{∀yχ}(τ̄))`, refuting `∀y χ` at the element `(w_{∀yχ}(τ̄), t)`. No reduction to
  quantifier-free formulas is needed: `Φ` is total on instances, and the witness structures enter
  only through finite satisfiability.
- **Model of the template's sentences.** Every sentence `σ ∈ Γ` with `Φ σ` holds in `EM(J, Φ)`
  (the truth lemma at `m = 0`). For the template of Layer 3 this gives `EM(J, Φ) ⊨ φ`, and
  `EM(J, Φ) ⊨ sk(Γ₀)` because each Skolem formula lies in `Γ*` and is declared true by finite
  satisfiability.
- **Indiscernibility.** The skeleton is indiscernible on `Γ` and realizes `Φ` there.
- **Generation and cardinality.** `EM(J, Φ)` is generated by the skeleton, and its elements are
  values of terms at finite increasing index tuples, so for a template over any language `L'`,

  ```math
  |\mathrm{EM}(J,\Phi)|\le\max\bigl(\aleph_0,\ |J|,\ |\textstyle\sum_n L'_{\mathrm{fun},n}|\bigr),
  ```

  stated with `Cardinal.lift` since `J` and the symbols live in different universes. If `Φ disEq`,
  the skeleton is injective and `|J| ≤ |EM(J, Φ)|`. When `L'` has countably many function
  symbols, as the Skolem language `L*` does, the language term is absorbed: for infinite `J` with `Φ disEq`,
  `#EM(J, Φ) = #J`, and for finite `J`, `EM(J, Φ)` is countable.
- **Functoriality.** An order embedding `e : J ↪o K` induces a `Γ`-elementary embedding
  `EM(J, Φ) → EM(K, Φ)` commuting with the skeletons, functorially in `e`. An order isomorphism
  induces an isomorphism `EM(J, Φ) ≃[L'] EM(K, Φ)`, and so `J ≃o J` acts on `EM(J, Φ)` by
  automorphisms.

### 2.4 Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski sources and exact cardinality

The interface between the construction of a template (Layer 3) and its stretching is a
semantically stated source. For a language `L'`, a set `Γ` of formulas in bound positions, a
structure `N` and `a : ℕ → N`, **`IsEMSource Γ N a`** means:

1. `Γ` is closed under constructor components (both sides of `imp`, the body of `all`, every
   component of `iSup` and `iInf`); that is, `Γ` is the underlying set of an IME `Fragment`
   (IME 1.2), and the prototype states the four closure conditions directly only because it does
   not import IME;
2. `Γ` contains the equality atom `x₀ = x₁` and, for every relation symbol `R` of `L'`, the atom
   `R(x₀, …, x_{l-1})`;
3. **Skolem terms**: for every member `∀y ψ(x̄, y)` of `Γ` of arity `n` there is an `L'`-term
   `t(x̄)` in `n` variables such that, in `N`, whenever `ψ(b̄, y)` fails for some `y` it fails at
   `y = t(b̄)`;
4. **term indiscernibility**: for every member `ψ` of `Γ` of arity `n`, every tuple `τ̄` of `n`
   terms in `m` variables and all increasing `s, t : Fin m ↪o ℕ`, `ψ` holds at `τ̄(a ∘ s)` exactly
   when it holds at `τ̄(a ∘ t)`.

By `isSkolemTemplate_templateOf` (2.2) the template of `a` is a Skolem template for `(Γ, w)`,
with `w` the Skolem terms of (3). Conversely, for a Skolem template `Φ` for `(Γ, w)`, the skeleton
of `EM(ℕ, Φ)` is an Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski source on `Γ` (**`isEMSource_em`**: (3) with the terms
`w` and (4) by the truth lemma of 2.3, and the skeleton is injective when `Φ disEq`). Two
milestones, each with its own statement:

- **`exists_emModel`** (stretching with the cardinality bound). Let `L' : Language.{0, 0}` and
  `IsEMSource Γ N a`. For every linear order `J : Type w` there are `E : Type w`, an
  `L'`-structure on `E` and `c : J → E` such that `E` and `N` satisfy the same sentences of `Γ`,
  `c` realizes the template of `a` on `Γ`, `c` is injective when `a` is, and

  ```math
  |E|\le\max\bigl(\aleph_0,\ |J|,\ |\textstyle\sum_n L'_{\mathrm{fun},n}|\bigr).
  ```

  Construction: `E := EM(J, Φ)` of 2.3 for the template `Φ` of `a` (`isSkolemTemplate_templateOf`),
  with its skeleton `c`. Agreement with `N` on the sentences of `Γ` and realization of the template
  are the truth lemma of 2.3; `c` is injective when `a` is, because `Φ` then declares `x₀ = x₁`
  false; and the cardinality bound is that of 2.3, which counts pairs `(τ, t)`: at most
  `max ℵ₀ |L'_fun|` terms in each finite number of variables and at most `max ℵ₀ |J|` finite
  increasing tuples. There is one term model in this roadmap, used here and in Layers 3 and 5.
- **`exists_emModel_card_eq`** (exact cardinality). If moreover `L'` has countably many function
  symbols, `a` is injective and `J` is infinite, the structure of `exists_emModel` has cardinality
  exactly `#J`: the language term is at most `ℵ₀`, `max ℵ₀ #J = #J`, and `c` injects `J` into `E`.

**Acceptance check:** take `ℚ` in the language of one binary relation symbol interpreted as `<`,
`Γ` the two atoms `x₀ = x₁` and `x₀ < x₁`, and `a : ℕ → ℚ` the inclusion. Then
`IsEMSource Γ ℚ a`: `Γ` has no compound or universal members, so (1) and (3) hold vacuously, and
since the language has no function symbols every term is a variable, so (4) says only that
increasing tuples of `a` have the same order type. The EM model over any linear order `J` is `J`
itself: the skeleton is an order isomorphism onto `EM(J, Φ)`. This checks the construction, not
density. A dense linear order without endpoints over `J` needs Skolem functions for density and
unboundedness, together with a sequence indiscernible for every term built from them, and such a
source is what the extraction of Layer 3 produces.

⚠ **API warning.** Do not Skolemize all Lω₁ω formulas of a language at once. There are `2^ℵ₀`
formulas even over a finite language, and the resulting language is uncountable, so its term model
has the wrong cardinality and the truth lemma's family no longer fits in a countable set. The
tower above adds Skolem symbols only for the countable family that the construction needs.

## Layer 3 — The Morley–Hanf theorem

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Hanf/{Defs, Approximation, MorleyHanf}.lean`.

### 3.1 Hanf bounds

`HasArbLargeModels φ`, `IsHanfBound φ κ`, `hanfNumber φ` (Convention 6), with:
`IsHanfBound.mono`; every sentence has a Hanf bound (if `φ` lacks arbitrarily large models, some
cardinal bounds all its models); `hanfNumber_isHanfBound`, `hanfNumber_le_of_isHanfBound`,
`hanfNumber_le_iff_isHanfBound` and `lt_hanfNumber_iff_not_isHanfBound`; invariance of all three
notions under sentence equivalence and under `onBoundedFormulaInf` along an injective language map
(IME 0.1). The invariance uses that the Hanf predicates count only nonempty models (Convention 6):
a nonempty model of `φ` expands to a model of its image, interpreting the new symbols arbitrarily,
and a model of the image restricts to a model of `φ` on the same carrier.

### 3.2 Reduction to a countable Skolemized setting

Given `φ : Sentenceω L` satisfying the Morley–Hanf hypothesis:

- let `L_φ = (symbSublang (functionsIn φ) (relationsIn φ)).sum D`, where `D` has one dummy
  function symbol and one dummy relation symbol of each arity; it has countably many symbols of
  each sort (IME 0.2–0.3). Put `L₀ = smallPresentation L_φ : Language.{0, 0}` and let
  `g : L →ᴸ L₀` be the composite of the map `L →ᴸ L_φ` sending the symbols of `φ` to themselves
  and every other symbol to the dummy of its arity with `(smallPresentationEquiv L_φ).toLHom`
  (IME 0.3); `restrictSymbols φ` and `realize_smallPresentation` relate `φ` to its image;
- build the Skolem tower of `Γ₀ = {φ', disEq}` (2.1), where `φ' = onBoundedFormulaInf g φ` and `Γ₀`
  also contains the atoms required in 2.1, and put `σ* = φ' ∧ sk(Γ₀)`; below, `φ` in a template
  means `φ'`;
- each model `M` of `φ` with `ℶ_α ≤ #M` is infinite; interpret the dummies as the first projection
  (positive arity), a fixed element (arity `0`) and the empty relation, so that by symbol locality
  `M ⊨ onBoundedFormulaInf g φ`, and expand to a model of `σ*`; fix a linear
  order on it (any linear order; `exists_wellFoundedLT` supplies one).

### 3.3 Approximation by homogeneous sets

A **condition** is a finite partial truth assignment `p` on `Γ*`, given by the sets `pos` and
`neg` of members assigned `true` and `false`. For `α < ω₁`, `p` is **`ℶ_α`-approximated**
(`IsApproximatedAt σ* pos neg (ℶ_ α)`) when there are an `L*`-structure `N ⊨ σ*` with an extra
linear order on its carrier and `H ⊆ N` with `ℶ_α ≤ #H` such that every `ψ` in the domain of `p`,
of arity `n`, has truth value `p ψ` at every increasing `n`-tuple from `H`. It is **cofinally
approximated** (`IsCofinallyApproximated σ* pos neg`) when it is `ℶ_α`-approximated for every
`α < ω₁`. Both definitions and the four lemmas below are stated for any language, any sentence and
any sets `pos`, `neg`: they need neither finiteness of the conditions nor countability of the
language, and countability of `Γ*` matters only for the enumeration of 3.4. Approximation at `α` implies approximation at every `β ≤ α` (pass to a subset of
`H`), so by 0.2 cofinal approximation is approximation at unboundedly many `α`.

Targets:

- **`isCofinallyApproximated_start`.** `{φ ↦ true, disEq ↦ true}` is cofinally approximated: take
  `H = M` for a model of size at least `ℶ_α`.
- **`IsCofinallyApproximated.decide`.** If `p` is cofinally approximated and `ψ ∈ Γ*` has arity `n`, then `p ∪ {ψ ↦ true}`
  or `p ∪ {ψ ↦ false}` is cofinally approximated. For `n = 0` the truth value is constant on each
  approximating structure. For `n ≥ 1` and each `α`, approximate `p` at `α + n`, colour the
  increasing `n`-tuples of `H` by the truth value of `ψ`, and apply `erdos_rado_beth` at exponent
  `n` (two colours, `ℶ_(α+n) ≥ ℶ_(α+n-1)⁺`) to get a homogeneous `H' ⊆ H` of cardinality `ℶ_α⁺` and
  a truth value `d(α)`. By `exists_unbounded_fiber` one value is taken unboundedly often.
- **`IsCofinallyApproximated.exists_iSup_witness`.** If `p` is cofinally approximated with
  `p (iSup ψs) = true`, then `p ∪ {ψs i ↦ true}` is cofinally approximated for some `i`. For
  `n ≥ 1` and each `α`, approximate `p` at `α + n`, colour each increasing `n`-tuple of `H` by the
  least `i` with `ψs i` true there (countably many colours, at most `ℶ_α`), apply `erdos_rado_beth`
  at exponent `n`, and choose `i` by `exists_unbounded_fiber` on the countable colour set. For
  `n = 0` there is no exponent to use: the colour is the least true disjunct in the approximating
  structure itself.
- **`IsCofinallyApproximated.exists_iInf_witness`.** Dually, `p (iInf ψs) = false` gives some `i` with
  `p ∪ {ψs i ↦ false}` cofinally approximated.

### 3.4 The template and the theorem

Fix an enumeration `θ₀, θ₁, …` of `Γ*` and build cofinally approximated conditions
`p₀ ⊆ p₁ ⊆ …`, `p₀` the start condition. At stage `k`, if `θ_k` is not in the domain of `p_k`,
extend by the decision lemma; then, if the condition assigns `true` to `θ_k = iSup ψs` or `false`
to `θ_k = iInf ψs`, extend once more by the witness lemma, giving `p_{k+1}`. A member that received
its value earlier, as a witness, receives its own witness at its own stage, from `p_k`, which
contains it. The union `Φ` is total on `Γ*`, ω-complete, and each finite part lies in some `p_k`.
Targets:

- **`exists_total_of_cofinallyApproximated`** (the construction): there is a total, ω-complete
  truth assignment `Φ` on `Γ*` with `Φ φ` and `Φ disEq`, every finite part of which is cofinally
  approximated.
- **`isSkolemTemplate_of_approximated`**: a total, ω-complete truth assignment on `Γ*` whose finite
  parts are all approximated with `H` infinite is, read on instances through substitution, a Skolem
  template for `(Γ*, w)` with `w_{∀yψ} = f_{¬ψ}` (2.2). Coherence: a formula with constant value `d`
  on the increasing tuples of `H` keeps the value `d` after reindexing along an order embedding,
  which gives both directions; finite satisfiability: an approximated condition is realized at an
  increasing tuple of `H` in a model of `σ*`, which obeys `w`; ω-completeness is assumed.
  Approximation with `H` infinite at a single level already suffices here: the cofinal levels are
  needed only to keep the construction of 3.4 going.
- **`exists_emSource_of_forall_beth`** (extraction, its own milestone). Under the Morley–Hanf
  hypothesis there are a countable language `L' : Language.{0, 0}` (countably many function and
  relation symbols), a language map `g : L →ᴸ L'`, a countable `Γ` and an injective `a : ℕ → N` with
  `IsEMSource Γ N a`, `onBoundedFormulaInf g φ ∈ Γ` and `N ⊨ onBoundedFormulaInf g φ`. Here `L'` is
  the Skolem language `L*` of the tower over `L₀ = smallPresentation L_φ` (3.2), and `g` is the map
  `L →ᴸ L₀` of 3.2 followed by the inclusion into `L*`; `Γ` is `Γ*`; and `(N, a)` is the skeleton of
  `EM(ℕ, Φ)` for the template `Φ` above, a source by `exists_total_of_cofinallyApproximated`,
  `isSkolemTemplate_of_approximated` and `isEMSource_em`. The dummies are interpreted in the approximating models as the first projection
  (arity `≥ 1`), a fixed element (arity `0`) and the empty relation, so they change none of the
  arguments of 3.3.
- **`exists_model_card_eq_of_forall_beth`** (cardinality control and transfer, the headline):
  under the Morley–Hanf hypothesis, for every infinite `κ : Cardinal.{w}` there is `M : Type w` with
  `M ⊨ φ` and `#M = κ`. Apply `exists_emModel_card_eq` to the Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski source of
  `exists_emSource_of_forall_beth` along `J = κ.ord.ToType` (an infinite order of cardinality `κ`),
  obtaining an `L'`-structure `E` of cardinality exactly `κ` with `E ⊨ onBoundedFormulaInf g φ`,
  and give `E` the `L`-structure `g.reduct`; `realize_onBoundedFormulaInf` (IME 0.1, with
  `LHom.isExpansionOn_reduct`) gives `E ⊨ φ`. The cardinality is exactly `κ` with no term for the
  size of `L`, even when `κ < #(Σ n, L.Functions n)`: the stretching runs over the countable `L'`,
  where the language term of `exists_emModel` is absorbed, and the reduct keeps the carrier.
- **`hasArbLargeModels_of_forall_beth`** and **`morley_hanf`**: `ℶ_ω₁` is a Hanf bound for every
  Lω₁ω sentence, over any language.
- **`hanfNumber_le_beth_omega_one`**.
- **`morley_hanf_theory`**: a countable `T : Theoryω L` (IME 1.1), through `Theoryω.conjunction`,
  satisfying the Morley–Hanf hypothesis
  has models of every infinite cardinality.
- **`morley_omitting_types`** (Morley 1965): over any language, let `T` be a first-order theory and
  `Σ : Set (Σ n, Set (L.BoundedFormula Empty n))` a set of partial first-order types, each tagged
  with its arity, with `T.Countable`, `Σ.Countable` and `∀ p ∈ Σ, p.2.Countable`. If `T` has models
  omitting every type of `Σ` of cardinality at least `ℶ_α` for every `α < ω₁`, it has such models
  of every infinite cardinality. Apply the headline to the Lω₁ω sentence
  `⋀T ∧ ⋀_{p ∈ Σ} ∀x̄ ⋁_{θ ∈ p} ¬θ(x̄)`, built with `BoundedFormula.toOmega` and
  `iInfAlong`/`iSupAlong`; the three countability hypotheses are exactly what makes its
  conjunctions and disjunctions countable. Countability of each type is needed separately from
  countability of the family, and the theorem fails without it: over constants `cᵢ` for `i` in a set
  `I` of cardinality `ℶ_ω₁`, the empty theory and the single type `{x ≠ cᵢ | i ∈ I}`, the models
  omitting the type are those in which every element is some `cᵢ`; they exist in every nonzero
  cardinality up to `ℶ_ω₁` and in none above it. This follows the separate hypotheses `hP` and
  `hPc` of IME's `model_existence_omitting`.
- **`morley_omitting_types_of_countable`**: for a language with countably many function and relation
  symbols, the three countability hypotheses reduce to `Σ.Countable`, since every set of
  first-order formulas is then countable.

**Acceptance check:** `morley_hanf` for the empty language, where the EM model of the template of
an injective sequence is a pure set of the prescribed cardinality.

⚠ **API warning.** The source of the template is the family of approximating models, not an
indiscernible sequence in one of them (Convention 9). The template is decided one formula at a
time, so each use of Erdős–Rado concerns one arity and finitely or countably many colours, and the
`ω₁`-pigeonhole chooses among countably many outcomes. Deciding a whole arity at once would colour
tuples by a complete type, with `2^ℵ₀` colours, where the pigeonhole over `ω₁` fails.

⚠ **API warning.** Witnesses are chosen during the construction, by the witness lemma applied to a
stage condition. They cannot be recovered from the finished assignment: a total `Φ` all of whose
finite parts are cofinally approximated, built by the decision lemma alone, need not be
ω-complete (compare the height example of Layer 1).

## Layer 4 — The Hanf number of Lω₁ω

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Hanf/{Global, Ladder, VonNeumannModel}.lean`.

### 4.1 Global Hanf bounds

`IsLω₁ωHanfBound κ`: `κ : Cardinal.{w}` is a Hanf bound for every sentence of every language in
`Language.{u, v}`. `lomega1omegaHanfNumber` is the least such cardinal. Targets:
`beth_omega_one_isLω₁ωHanfBound` (from `morley_hanf`), the attained infimum, the universal property
`lomega1omegaHanfNumber_le_iff`, and the bounded-spectrum criterion
`lt_lomega1omegaHanfNumber_of_maximal_model`: a sentence with a nonempty model of cardinality at
least `κ` and no model of cardinality greater than `κ` shows `κ < lomega1omegaHanfNumber`.

### 4.2 The beth ladder

For `α < ω₁`, the **ladder language** has constants `cₙ` (`n ∈ ℕ`), unary predicates `U_i` for `i`
in the well-order of type `α + 2` (the levels `0, …, α + 1`), and one binary relation `E`. Symbol
types live in `Language.{u, v}` through a countable encoding. The **ladder sentence** is the
conjunction of:

- `⋀ₙ U_⊥(cₙ)` and `∀x (U_⊥(x) → ⋁ₙ x = cₙ)`;
- `∀x U_⊤(x)`;
- for `i < j`, `∀x (U_i(x) → U_j(x))`;
- for limit `j`, `∀x (U_j(x) → ⋁_{i<j} U_i(x))`;
- for `j` the successor of `i`, `∀x ∀y (U_j(x) ∧ E(y, x) → U_i(y))`;
- extensionality: `∀y ∀z ((∀x (E(x, y) ↔ E(x, z))) → y = z)`.

Index sets are countable because `α + 2 < ω₁`, so the conjunctions and disjunctions are coded into
`ℕ` with `iInfAlong`/`iSupAlong`. Targets:

- **`realize_ladderSentence_iff`**: satisfaction is the conjunction of the six semantic clauses,
  as a structure `IsLadderModel`.
- **Upper bound**, `card_le_beth_of_isLadderModel`: in a model, `#U_i ≤ ℶ_i` by well-founded
  induction on `i`. At `⊥`, `U_⊥` is covered by the countably many constants. At a successor `i +
  1`, sending `x` to its set of `E`-predecessors is injective (extensionality) into the powerset of
  `U_i`, giving `#U_{i+1} ≤ 2^{ℶ_i} = ℶ_{i+1}`. At a limit, `U_j` is a countable union of smaller
  levels, each of cardinality at most `ℶ_j`. Then `#M = #U_⊤ ≤ ℶ_(α+1)`.
- **Maximal model**: interpret `U_i` as the von Neumann stage `V_{ω+i}`, `E` as membership and the
  constants as an enumeration of `V_ω`, on a carrier in `Type w` equivalent to `V_{ω+α+1}`
  (`Shrink`). The clauses follow from `ZFSet.vonNeumann_add_one`, `ZFSet.mem_vonNeumann`,
  `ZFSet.isTransitive_vonNeumann` and ZFSet extensionality, and
  `#V_{ω+α+1} = preBeth (ω + α + 1) = ℶ_(α+1)` by `ZFSet.card_vonNeumann` and
  `Cardinal.beth_eq_preBeth`.
- **`exists_sentence_maximal_model`**: the ladder sentence has a model of cardinality exactly
  `ℶ_(α+1)` and none larger.

### 4.3 Sharpness

- **`beth_succ_lt_lomega1omegaHanfNumber`**: `ℶ_(α+1) < lomega1omegaHanfNumber` for every `α < ω₁`.
- **`iSup_beth_succ_omega_one`**: `⨆_{α < ω₁} ℶ_(α+1) = ℶ_ω₁`, from `Cardinal.beth_limit` and
  cofinality of the successors in the limit `ω₁`.
- **`lomega1omegaHanfNumber_eq_beth_omega_one`** (headline): `Hanf(Lω₁ω) = ℶ_ω₁`.

The cardinal arithmetic used here follows from Mathlib, with the consumer forms derived as small
lemmas where convenient: a countable sum of cardinals at most an infinite `κ` is at most `κ`
(`Cardinal.sum_le_sum`, `Cardinal.sum_const` and `Cardinal.mul_le_of_le`); a countable union of
sets of cardinality at most an infinite `κ` has cardinality at most `κ`
(`Cardinal.mk_iUnion_le_sum_mk`, or `Cardinal.mk_iUnion_le_lift` across universes, then the sum
bound); an injection into `Set Y` bounds by `2 ^ #Y` (`Cardinal.mk_le_of_injective` and
`Cardinal.mk_set`).

**Acceptance check:** the ladder at `α = 0`, with `Set ℕ` as maximal model (`cₙ = {n}`, `U_⊥` the
singletons, `E(x, y)` iff `x = {n}` with `n ∈ y`) and every model of cardinality at most `2^ℵ₀`.

## Layer 5 — Small models, complete sentences and categoricity

Suggested home: `TauCeti/Order/HighlyTransitive.lean` and
`TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Small/{Defs, SmallModels, Companion, Complete}.lean`.

### 5.1 Highly order-transitive orders

- `HighlyOrderTransitive J`: for all `n` and `s t : Fin n ↪o J`, some `e : J ≃o J` has
  `e ∘ s = t`. Basic API: invariance under order isomorphism; such an order is dense without
  endpoints when it has two points; `ℚ` is highly order-transitive.
- **`HighlyOrderTransitive.of_field`**: every linearly ordered field is highly order-transitive.
  Move the points of an increasing tuple one at a time, from left to right, by a translation and
  then by maps that are the identity below a point `a` and affine with positive slope above it.
- **`exists_highlyOrderTransitive`**: every infinite cardinal `κ : Cardinal.{w}` is the cardinality
  of a highly order-transitive order in `Type w`. Take a set `X` of cardinality `κ`, the value group
  `Γ = Lex (X →₀ ℤ)`, the ordered field `Lex ℚ⟦Γ⟧` of Hahn series, and the subfield generated by
  the monomials `t^{e_x}` (`x ∈ X`). It has cardinality `κ` by `Subfield.cardinalMk_closure_le_max`
  and injectivity of `x ↦ t^{e_x}`, and it is highly order-transitive by `of_field`.

### 5.2 Small structures

Using IME's `infinitaryType` and `realizedTypes` (IME 4.1):

- `IsLω₁ωSmall M`: for every `n`, `(realizedTypes M n).Countable`, i.e. `M` realizes countably many
  complete Lω₁ω-types in `n` variables.
- API: invariance under isomorphism; every countable structure is small
  (`isLω₁ωSmall_of_countable`); smallness descends to reducts along a language map (types of the
  reduct are determined by types in the expansion); potentially isomorphic structures realize the
  same complete types, so smallness transfers along `PotentialIso`; if every tuple's complete
  type is determined by its orbit under a group of automorphisms with countably many orbits on
  `n`-tuples for each `n`, the structure is small.

### 5.3 Small models of every infinite cardinality

**`exists_small_model_card_eq`.** Under the Morley–Hanf hypothesis, for every infinite
`κ : Cardinal.{w}`, `φ` has a small model of cardinality `κ`.

Take the template `Φ` of 3.4 and a highly order-transitive `J` of cardinality `κ` (5.1). In
`EM(J, Φ)` every element is the value of a term at an increasing tuple from the skeleton. A tuple of
elements has a **code**: a number `k` and a tuple of `L*`-terms in `k` variables whose values at
some increasing `k`-tuple of indices are the given elements. There are countably many codes for
each arity, since `L*` is countable. If two tuples have the same code, at index tuples `u` and `u'`,
an order automorphism of `J` carries `u` to `u'`, and the automorphism of `EM(J, Φ)` it induces
(2.3, functoriality) carries one tuple to the other, so they have the same complete type. Each
realized type is therefore the type of some code, and there are countably many. The `L`-structure
is the reduct along `g` of 3.4, so automorphisms of the `L'`-structure are automorphisms of the
reduct, and 5.2 gives smallness. The cardinality is `κ` by the cardinality clause of 2.3:
`#EM(J, Φ) = #J` for infinite `J` when `Φ disEq`.

### 5.4 Countable companions and complete sentences

For the rest of this layer `L` is relational with countably many relation symbols, as the parent's
Scott sentences require.

- **`exists_countable_potentialIso_of_isLω₁ωSmall`** (countable companion): a small structure `M` is
  potentially isomorphic to a countable structure. Let `F` be the fragment generated by isolating
  formulas `χ_p` of `exists_isolatingFormula` for all realized types `p` of all arities, together
  with their one-variable existential closures (the isolator seed of IME 4.1); it is countable by
  `Fragment.generated_countable`. Take `M₀ ⊆ M` countable with `AElementary F M₀.subtype` by
  `exists_countable_aElementary_substructure` with `X = ∅` (IME 2.3; a relational language has no
  function symbols). The pairs of tuples of `M₀` and `M` with the same complete type in `M` form a
  tuple back-and-forth system. Going back, let `ā` from `M₀` and `b̄` from `M` have the same type
  and `d ∈ M`, and put `q = tp(b̄ d)`. Then `M ⊨ ∃y χ_q(b̄, y)`, so `M ⊨ ∃y χ_q(ā, y)`, so `M₀ ⊨ ∃y
  χ_q(ā, y)` by `F`-elementarity, and a witness `c ∈ M₀` has `tp(ā c) = q` in `M`. Going forth, the
  witness is found in `M` directly. Conclude with the parent's
  `potentialIso_iff_exists_isTupleBFSystem`.
- **`isCompleteSentence_scottSentence`**: the Scott sentence of a countable structure `M₀` is
  complete. A model `N` of it, of any cardinality, satisfies `BFEquiv β` with `M₀` at every level
  (`TauCeti.bfEquiv_of_realize_scottSentence`), hence is potentially isomorphic to `M₀`
  (`TauCeti.potentialIso_iff_bfEquiv_all`), hence agrees with `M₀` on every Lω₁ω sentence
  (`TauCeti.PotentialIso.infEquivAt` at the carrier `ℕ`).
- **`isLω₁ωSmall_iff_exists_isCompleteSentence`** (headline): `M` is small exactly when it satisfies
  a complete sentence. Forward: the Scott sentence of a countable companion. Backward: a complete
  `σ` has a countable model `M₀ ⊆ M`, a countable substructure that is `AElementary` for
  `Fragment.generated {⟨0, σ⟩}` (IME 1.2, 2.3); `σ` decides `scottSentence M₀`, which `M₀`
  satisfies, so every model of `σ`, `M` included, is potentially isomorphic to `M₀` and realizes
  only its countably many types.
- **`exists_isCompleteSentence_entails`**: if a small `M` satisfies `φ`, a complete `σ` with
  `M ⊨ σ` entails `φ` on all models.

### 5.5 Categorical completions

**`exists_isCompleteSentence_isCategorical`** (headline). Let `L` be countable relational, `φ`
satisfy the Morley–Hanf hypothesis, `κ` be infinite and `φ` be `κ`-categorical. Then there is a
complete `σ` entailing `φ`, with a model of cardinality `κ`, and `σ` is `κ`-categorical. Take a
small model of cardinality `κ` (5.3) and the complete sentence it satisfies (5.4). Models of `σ` are
models of `φ`, so `κ`-categoricity passes to `σ`.

**Acceptance check:** `isLω₁ωSmall_of_countable`.

⚠ **API warning.** Smallness concerns the complete Lω₁ω-type, all of whose formulas are allowed,
not the type relative to a fragment. A structure realizing countably many types relative to every
countable fragment need not be small.

⚠ **API warning.** Do not prove completeness of a Scott sentence by comparing it only with
countable models. The completeness quantifies over models of every cardinality in `Type w`, and the
argument must go through a back-and-forth system with an arbitrary model, which
`bfEquiv_of_realize_scottSentence` supplies.

## Worked examples

Discharge these alongside the layers; they check that the API describes real structures.

1. **Erdős–Rado at small parameters.** `ℶ₁⁺ → (ℵ₁)²_{ℵ₀}` and `ℶ₂⁺ → (ℵ₁)³_{ℵ₀}` as instances of
   `erdos_rado`; the pigeonhole instance `ℵ₁ → (ℵ₁)¹_{ℵ₀}`.
2. **Morley seed.** `isIndiscernibleOn_morleySeed` for an injective sequence in any model of `φ`.
3. **The height example.** The template-realizability counterexample of the Layer 1 warning, with
   its finitely realized and unrealizable template proved.
4. **EM models of the atomic order fragment.** The Layer 2 acceptance check, with the skeleton an
   order isomorphism from `J` onto the EM model.
5. **Pure sets.** For the empty language and `φ = "there are infinitely many elements"` (a countable
   conjunction of first-order sentences), the Layer 3 construction produces pure sets of every
   infinite cardinality; every pure set is small and the sentence is complete and
   `κ`-categorical for every infinite `κ`.
6. **Dense linear orders.** The Scott sentence of `(ℚ, <)` is complete, has models of every infinite
   cardinality by `exists_model_card_eq_of_forall_beth`, and is `ℵ₀`-categorical; the complete
   strengthening produced by 5.5 at `κ = ℵ₀` is equivalent to it.
7. **A countable spectrum.** Over constants `cₙ`, the sentence "every element is some `cₙ`, and the
   `cₙ` are pairwise distinct" has exactly one model up to isomorphism, of cardinality `ℵ₀`, so
   `ℵ₀ < lomega1omegaHanfNumber`.
8. **The ladder at `α = 0`.** The Layer 4 acceptance check with `Set ℕ`.
9. **Omitting types.** The Hanf number for omitting a countable set of countable types in a
   countable first-order theory is at most `ℶ_ω₁`, via `morley_omitting_types`; the example of 3.4
   with uncountably many constants shows that countability of each type cannot be dropped.

## Completion criteria

Each definition comes with the API listed in its layer, and the headline theorems are assembled
from those APIs without hypotheses asserting homogeneity, realizability of a template,
compactness or the existence of indiscernibles. The following chains are visible in the exported
results:

- end-homogeneous sets → Erdős–Rado → beth form → decision and witness lemmas →
  `exists_total_of_cofinallyApproximated` → `isSkolemTemplate_of_approximated` → `realize_em_iff`
  → `isEMSource_em` → `exists_emSource_of_forall_beth` → `exists_emModel_card_eq` →
  `exists_model_card_eq_of_forall_beth` → `morley_hanf`;
- ladder sentence → upper bound and von Neumann model → bounded spectra → with `morley_hanf`,
  `lomega1omegaHanfNumber_eq_beth_omega_one`;
- highly order-transitive orders → equivariant EM model → countably many codes →
  `exists_small_model_card_eq`;
- isolation → countable companion → complete Scott sentence →
  `isLω₁ωSmall_iff_exists_isCompleteSentence` → `exists_isCompleteSentence_isCategorical`.
