# Roadmap: Igusa varieties and torsion concentration, Part II: compact unitary Shimura varieties

**Summary.** This roadmap proves the theorem of Caraiani and Scholze on the generic part of the cohomology of compact unitary Shimura varieties ([CS17], Theorem 1.1 and its precise form Theorem 6.3.1). The setting is a compact unitary PEL Shimura variety `S_K` of type (A), attached to a CM field `F = F⁺𝒦` that contains an imaginary quadratic field `𝒦`. It is of one of two kinds: Kottwitz's simple Shimura varieties (a central division algebra over `F`), or a hermitian space over `F` that is quasi-split at every finite place, with `F⁺ ≠ ℚ`. Let `𝔪` be a maximal ideal of the abstract spherical Hecke algebra `𝕋^S` in the support of `H^*(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)`. Then `𝔪` has a semisimple Galois representation `ρ_𝔪 : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)`. If `ρ_𝔪` is decomposed generic at a suitable auxiliary prime split in `𝒦`, the localized cohomology `H^*(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` is concentrated in the middle degree `dim S_K`. The ingredients are the geometry of the Hodge–Tate period map and of Igusa varieties, supplied by the parent roadmap, together with a comparison of the cohomology of Igusa varieties with automorphic representations through the trace formula. Layer 8 fixes the data, Hecke algebras, duality and auxiliary primes. Layer 9 shows that generic Igusa cohomology lives on the `μ`-ordinary stratum for the quasi-split datum ([CS17] Theorem 5.5.7). Layer 10 does the same for Kottwitz's simple Shimura varieties ([CS17] Corollary 5.6.3). Layer 11 assembles Theorem 6.3.1, Theorem 1.1 and their integral and twisted-coefficient forms.

This roadmap is Part II of IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration and begins where that roadmap stops. The parent constructs central leaves, perfect and Mantovan Igusa varieties for unramified PEL data of types (A) and (C) (its Layers 0–1). For compact PEL data whose Shimura datum is of Hodge type it identifies the stalks of the pushforward along the Hodge–Tate period map with Igusa cohomology (its §3.17) and proves perversity and concentration at a minimal Newton stratum (its §§4.4–4.5). It owns the splitting of generic local lifts (its §5.5). Its trace-formula, duality and end-theorem layers (Layers 5–7) are written for the quasi-split unitary datum of signature `(n, n)` at a totally split prime, the non-compact setting of Caraiani–Scholze's sequel. This Part II supplies compact unitary data of every rank and signature, including the wholly definite data of rank at least two, which violate the Shimura axiom SV3 and have finite moduli problems (§§8.1, 8.9), auxiliary primes that are only split in `𝒦`, the trace comparisons for these data, and the end theorems of the compact paper.

## Conventions

`F = F⁺𝒦` is a CM field with `F⁺` totally real and `𝒦 ⊂ F` imaginary quadratic, and `c` is complex conjugation. `Spl_{F/F⁺}` is the set of rational primes all of whose places in `F⁺` split in `F`. `Spl_{𝒦/ℚ}` is the set of rational primes split in `𝒦`. `G` is the unitary similitude group of the datum. Its signature at `τ : F ↪ ℂ` is `(p_τ, q_τ)`, with `p_τ + q_τ = n` and `(p_{τc}, q_{τc}) = (q_τ, p_τ)`. The Hodge cocharacter is `μ`, with `μ(t) = diag(t^{(p_τ)}, 1^{(q_τ)})` in the `τ`-factor, and `E` is the reflex field. The dimension is `d = Σ_{τ∈Φ} p_τq_τ = ⟨2ρ, μ⟩ = dim S_K` for any CM type `Φ` of `F`. `S_K` always denotes the PEL moduli problem. Following the literature it is called a (simple, compact unitary) Shimura variety; it is a union of canonical models of Shimura varieties of Shimura data exactly when the axiom SV3 holds, that is unless the datum is wholly definite of rank `n ≥ 2` (§8.1).

Newton classes lie in `B(G, μ⁻¹)` with the covariant normalization of the parent roadmap (slopes in `[−1, 0]`). We write `d_b = ⟨2ρ, ν_b⟩` for the dominant Newton point `ν_b`. The `μ`-ordinary class is the class whose Newton point is the Hodge average `(μ⁻¹)◇` of BunGAndNewtonStrata §1.26, that is, the average of the dominant representative of `μ⁻¹` over its orbit under the Frobenius action on the based root datum of `G_{ℚ_p}`. At the primes `p ∉ S` used here `G_{ℚ_p}` is unramified, hence quasi-split, so this class exists and is the maximal element of `B(G, μ⁻¹)` (BunGAndNewtonStrata §§1.25–1.26). Frobenius preserves the based root datum and fixes `2ρ`, so `⟨2ρ, (μ⁻¹)◇⟩ = ⟨2ρ, (μ⁻¹)_dom⟩ = ⟨2ρ, μ⟩ = d`, and `d_b = d` exactly for the `μ`-ordinary class. The average equals `(μ⁻¹)_dom` exactly when the conjugacy class of `μ` is defined over `ℚ_p`, for instance when `p` splits completely in `F`; at a prime split only in `𝒦` the two differ in general (§8.3, Checks).

Relative Lie algebra cohomology of a representation `π_∞` of `G(ℝ)` is taken for the reduced pair `(𝔪_G, K_∞)`. Here `K_∞ ≅ ∏_{τ∈Φ} U(p_τ) × U(q_τ)` is the maximal compact subgroup of the centralizer of `h` in `G(ℝ)`, `A_G` is the maximal `ℚ`-split torus in the centre of `G`, `A_{G,∞}^0 = A_G(ℝ)^0`, `𝔪_G = 𝔤/𝔞_G` with `𝔞_G = Lie A_G(ℝ)`, and `π_∞` has trivial central character on `A_{G,∞}^0`. The centralizer of `h` is `K_∞A_{G,∞}^0`, so `X = G(ℝ)/K_∞A_{G,∞}^0`, and the pair is `(𝔤, K_∞A_{G,∞}^0)`. For the unitary similitude groups here `A_G` is the scalar `𝔾_m`, so the full pair `(𝔤, K_∞)` would multiply every cohomology space by `Λ^*(𝔞_G^*)`, of Euler characteristic `1 − 1 = 0`. "Cohomological for the trivial representation" means `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞) ≠ 0`.

`K_∞` is the identity component of a maximal compact subgroup `K_max ⊃ K_∞` of `G(ℝ)`, and `e = [K_max : K_∞] = #π_0(G(ℝ))`. An element of similitude `ν < 0` carries a hermitian form of signature `(p_τ, q_τ)` on `V_τ` to one of signature `(q_τ, p_τ)`, so `e = 1` when some `p_τ ≠ q_τ`. When `p_τ = q_τ` for every `τ`, the element exchanging orthonormal bases of a positive and a negative definite half of each `V_τ` has similitude `−1` and order 2, and `e = 2`. The suppliers work with `K_max`: ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.0.4 builds `X^G = G(ℝ)/K_maxA_{G,∞}^0`, of which `X` is the connected-compact variant `X̃^G`; ALS.5b.4 is the automorphic comparison on the quotients built from `X^G`; and AutomorphicSpectralTheory §§6.19–6.20 are stated for the pair `(𝔪_G, K_max)`. They are carried to `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` as follows. For a `(𝔤, K_max)`-module `M`, `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; M) = ⊕_χ H^*(𝔪_G, K_max; M ⊗ χ)` over the `e` characters `χ` of `G(ℝ)/G(ℝ)^0 = K_max/K_∞` (Frobenius reciprocity for the normal subgroup `K_∞` of finite index). So the Euler–Poincaré function of `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` is `Σ_χ χ·f_max`, for the function `f_max` of AutomorphicSpectralTheory §6.19 for `(𝔪_G, K_max)`. When `e = 2` the nontrivial `χ` is `sgn ∘ ν`. It is the archimedean component of the automorphic character `ω ∘ ν` of `G(𝔸)`, where `ω = ω_∞ω_f` is the quadratic Hecke character of `𝒦/ℚ` and `ω_∞ = sgn` because `𝒦` is imaginary. For `K` on which `ω_f ∘ ν` is trivial, the function `[x, g] ↦ sgn ν(x)·ω_f(ν(g))` on `G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)` is well defined and locally constant, because `ω` is trivial on `ℚ^×`. Each of its two fibres maps isomorphically onto `G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X^G × G(𝔸_f)/K)`, and right translation by `g ∈ G(𝔸_f)` exchanges them when `ω_f(ν(g)) = −1`. So the cohomology of the pieces of `S_K(ℂ)` is that of the quotients built from `X^G` plus its twist by `ω_f ∘ ν`, and ALS.5b.4, reindexed by `π ↦ π ⊗ (ω ∘ ν)`, gives Matsushima's formula for the pair `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` (§10.3).

`ι_ℓ : ℚ̄_ℓ ≅ ℂ` is fixed. At an auxiliary prime `p` split in `𝒦`, `u` is a chosen place of `𝒦` above `p` (`p = uu^c`), and `ι_p : ℚ̄_p ≅ ℂ` is compatible with a chosen prime `𝔭 | p` of `E`. Every statement below that uses the sets `S_i` names the place `u` it uses; the auxiliary-pair condition of §8.7 asks for one `u`. The primes of `F` above `u` are `𝔭_1, …, 𝔭_m`, and `S_i = {τ : F ↪ ℂ | ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ induces 𝔭_i}`. ([CS17] fixes `ι_p : ℚ̄_p ≅ ℂ`, CS17 line 3976, and prints `ι_p ∘ τ` in Theorem 5.5.7 and Corollary 5.6.3, CS17 lines 3979 and 4103. With that `ι_p` the composite is not defined, since `τ` lands in `ℂ` and `ι_p` has source `ℚ̄_p`.) PELModuli §M0.27 fixes the inverse isomorphism `ℂ ≅ ℚ̄_p` and calls it `ι_p`. Its `ι_p` is `ι_p⁻¹` here, and its composite `ι_p ∘ τ` is `ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ` here, so its `τ`-parts at `p` are indexed by the same sets `S_i` (§8.3, Checks).

Hecke polynomials are `P_v(X) = Σ_{i=0}^n (−1)^i q_v^{i(i−1)/2} T_{i,v} X^{n−i}`, matched with geometric Frobenius as in AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.0. In this convention the mod `ℓ` cyclotomic character `ε̄` takes the value `q_v⁻¹` on a geometric Frobenius at `v`. Decomposed genericity (`λ_a/λ_b ∉ {1, q_v}` for `a ≠ b`) does not depend on the choice of Frobenius, since inversion permutes the ordered pairs. The involution `ι` of `𝕋^S` sends `[KgK]` to `[Kg⁻¹K]`, and `𝔪^∨ = ι(𝔪)`.

`Red^b_n⃗` is the normalized Newton reduction of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §14.2, including the modulus twist `δ̄^{1/2}_{P(ν_b)}`. In the trace layers of Case 2 we put `S′ = S ∪ {∞, p} ∪ Ram(ϖ)`, where `ϖ` is a Hecke character of `𝒦` extending the quadratic character of `𝒦/ℚ` and ramified only at primes of `Spl_{F/F⁺}` (since every prime ramified in `F` lies in `Spl_{F/F⁺}` in Case 2, such a `ϖ` exists by gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance with `D = ∅`; EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.3 itself gives only finiteness of `Ram_ℚ(ϖ)`), and `S′_fin = S′ ∖ {∞}`. `BC^{S′}` is the base change outside `S′`, and one base change is used throughout. ([CS17] Theorem 5.5.7 prints `BC^p` at CS17 line 3987, but the proof at line 3990 uses `BC^S`.) At infinite level at `p`, localization at `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` is replaced by localization at its contraction to `𝕋^{S∪{p}}` (§11.1). All cohomology is étale cohomology after base change to an algebraically closed field; for adic spaces this field is `C = ℂ_p`. Coefficient rings are `Λ ∈ {ℤ/ℓ^r, 𝔽_ℓ, ℤ_ℓ, ℚ̄_ℓ}` with `ℓ ≠ p`.

Localization with torsion coefficients is ordinary localization of modules; a finite module over a finite quotient of `𝕋^S` is the direct sum of its localizations (§11.1), and at a maximal ideal not containing `ℓ` the localization of an `ℓ`-power torsion module is zero. Localization with integral coefficients is taken only at maximal ideals `𝔪` containing `ℓ`, and means the following. Let `O` be `ℤ_ℓ` or the ring of integers `O_λ` of a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, and `C` a perfect complex of `O`-modules with a Hecke action `𝕋^S → End_{D(O)}(C)` (for instance `RΓ(S_{K,Ē}, ℤ_ℓ)`, `RΓ(S_{K,Ē}, L_ξ)` or a finite-level Igusa complex). The `O`-subalgebra `𝕋(C) ⊂ End_{D(O)}(C)` generated by the image of `𝕋^S` is finite over `O`, hence the product of its localizations at its finitely many maximal ideals, all of which contain `ℓ` (IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§2.1–2.2). `C_𝔪` is the direct summand of `C` cut out by the idempotent of the factors whose maximal ideals contract to `𝔪`, zero if there are none, and `H^i(C)_𝔪 = H^i(C_𝔪)`; equivalently `C_𝔪 = R lim_r (C ⊗^L ℤ/ℓ^r)_𝔪`, since `(C ⊗^L ℤ/ℓ^r)_𝔪 = C_𝔪 ⊗^L ℤ/ℓ^r` (ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.4.9). So `H^i(C)_𝔪` is a direct summand of `H^i(C)`, finite over `O`, free when `H^i(C)` is, with `C_𝔪 ⊗^L 𝔽_ℓ = (C ⊗^L 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪`. It is not the ordinary localization of the `𝕋^S`-module `H^i(C)` at `𝔪`, which inverts elements of `𝕋^S ∖ 𝔪` that act through non-units of `O` on other factors and need not be finitely generated; at a maximal ideal not containing `ℓ` that localization is a `ℚ_ℓ`-vector space, possibly nonzero (§8.9, Checks).

## How to read the build

Layer 8 is used by every other layer. Layers 9 (Case 2) and 10 (Case 1) run in parallel, except that 10 reuses the two local lemmas of 9: Newton reduction of generic principal series (§9.2) and non-`μ`-ordinary classes (§9.5). Layer 11 combines both cases with the parent's geometry. For data satisfying SV3, Hochschild–Serre and the Leray sequence for `π_HT` reduce the cohomology of `S_K` to that of Igusa varieties. Wholly definite data of rank at least two are not Shimura data; their moduli problems are finite, and §8.9 makes the same reduction at finite level, with no perfectoid tower. The parent's minimal-stratum concentration concentrates the minimal Newton class in one degree. 9 or 10 then forces that class to be `μ`-ordinary, so the cohomology sits in degree `d`. The Galois representations of Theorem 6.3.1(1) come out of the same argument, with no genericity hypothesis.

## Layer 8: Compact unitary PEL data, Hecke algebras and duality

### 8.1 Compact unitary PEL data (Cases 1 and 2)

Define a compact unitary PEL datum of Caraiani–Scholze type as follows. Take a totally real field `F⁺`, an imaginary quadratic field `𝒦` and their compositum `F = F⁺𝒦`, with the field `𝒦` part of the datum. Take a rational PEL datum `(B, *, V, ⟨·,·⟩, h)` of type (A) whose similitude group `G` over `ℚ` falls in one of two cases ([CS17] §6.3, pp. 756–757, CS17 lines 4363–4379; §5.1, pp. 730–731; §5.6, p. 749).

- Case 1, Kottwitz's simple Shimura varieties. `B` is a central division algebra over `F` of degree `n`, `*` is an involution of the second kind (restricting to `c` on `F`), and `V ≅ B` is the simple left `B`-module.
- Case 2. `B = F`, `* = c`, and `V` is an `n`-dimensional `F`-vector space with an alternating form that is hermitian for `c`. Moreover `F⁺ ≠ ℚ`, `G` is quasi-split at every finite place, and every rational prime ramified in `F` lies in `Spl_{F/F⁺}`.

In both cases `G^ad` is anisotropic over `ℚ`; this is automatic in Case 1.

Call the datum *wholly definite* if `n ≥ 2` and `p_τq_τ = 0` for every `τ`. For `n ≥ 2` the group `G^ad` is the restriction of scalars from `F⁺` to `ℚ` of an absolutely simple adjoint group of type `A_{n−1}`, hence `ℚ`-simple, and the projection of `h` to its real factor at the place of `F⁺` below `τ` is trivial exactly when `p_τq_τ = 0`; for `n = 1`, `G^ad` is trivial. So the axiom SV3, that `h` projects nontrivially to every `ℚ`-simple factor of `G^ad` (PELModuli Conventions and §M0.12), holds exactly when the datum is not wholly definite, that is when `n = 1` or `d > 0`. Then `G`, being of type (A), is connected, and each `(G^{(a)}, X)` below is a Shimura datum of Hodge type (PELModuli §M0.12). A wholly definite datum has central `h`, compact `G^ad(ℝ)` and a one-point class `X`; it is not a Shimura datum, and §8.9 treats it. The definition below admits both kinds. ([CS17] §5.1, p. 730, CS17 line 3281, asserts that `(G, {h})` is a Shimura datum in the sense of its §2.1, whose third condition, p. 660, CS17 line 447, excludes `ℚ`-factors of `G^ad` with compact real points; that assertion fails for wholly definite data.)

The signatures `(p_τ, q_τ)`, the Hodge cocharacter `μ` and the reflex field `E` are functions of the rational PEL datum, not further choices. The moduli problem `S_K` is defined for every neat compact open `K ⊂ G(𝔸_f)` (PELModuli §M1.9), and the Hecke algebra `𝕋^S` for every finite set `S` of primes. These are parameters, which the datum does not store. The theorems below take them with the following hypotheses, stated where they are used:

- a finite set `S` of rational primes outside which `F` and `G` are unramified, with `S ⊂ Spl_{F/F⁺}` in Case 2;
- a neat compact open `K = K_SK^S ⊂ G(𝔸_f)` with `K^S` a product of hyperspecial maximal compact subgroups `K_q`.

Thus `𝕋^S` (§8.4) depends on the datum and `S`, and `S_K` on the datum and `K`. Layers 9–11 also use other sets and levels of the same datum (`S ∪ {p}`, `S′ ⊇ S`, `K^pK′_p`, `K′ ⊂ K`).

`S_K` is the PEL moduli problem over `E`. It is the disjoint union over `a ∈ ker¹(ℚ, G)` of open and closed pieces `S_K^{(a)}` with `S_K^{(a)}(ℂ) = G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)`, where `X` is the `G(ℝ)`-conjugacy class of `h`, the `G^{(a)}` are locally isomorphic to `G` and the levels are transported through fixed local identifications (PELModuli §M3.4, which does not use SV3). When SV3 holds, `S_K^{(a)}` is the canonical model of the Shimura variety `Sh_{K^{(a)}}(G^{(a)}, X)` (PELModuli §M4.3). Put `d = dim S_K = Σ_{τ∈Φ} p_τq_τ`. Record the two cases as two constructors of one structure, so that every statement of Layers 9–11 names the case it uses. The rational PEL datum, its similitude group, signatures and reflex field come from PELModuli §§M0.6, M0.7, M0.13 and M0.16. Type (A) is the second-kind case of PELModuli §M0.2. The index set `ker¹(ℚ, G)` is the finite set of PELModuli §M0.9. The `ker¹` decomposition is PELModuli §§M3.1 and M3.4, with the canonical-model identification of §M4.3 under SV3, where `E` is the reflex field of `(G, X)` (PELModuli §M0.17), and the dimension formula is PELModuli §M2.10.

The API:

- A Case 1 datum is built from a rational PEL datum with positivity and Shimura homomorphism `h`, a central division algebra over `F` with an involution of the second kind, and the condition that `V` is free of rank one as a left `B`-module.
- A Case 2 datum is built from a rational PEL datum with `B = F`, the condition `F⁺ ≠ ℚ`, quasi-splitness at finite places, the ramification condition, and a proof that `G^ad` is anisotropic over `ℚ`.
- The signature is a function of `τ` with `p_τ + q_τ = n` whose value at `τ ∘ c` is the swap of its value at `τ`.
- The dimension `Σ_{τ∈Φ} p_τq_τ` does not depend on the CM type `Φ`, and it equals `⟨2ρ, μ⟩`.
- The underlying rational PEL datum has type (A) in the classification of PELModuli §M0.2.
- SV3 holds if and only if the datum is not wholly definite, that is if and only if `n = 1` or `d > 0`; then each `(G^{(a)}, X)` is a Shimura datum of Hodge type. For a wholly definite datum `h` is central and `X` is a point.
- The moduli problem `S_K` is the disjoint union over `ker¹(ℚ, G)` of open and closed pieces with complex points `G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)` (PELModuli §M3.4). When SV3 holds, these pieces are the canonical models of the Shimura varieties of the inner forms `G^{(a)}` (PELModuli §M4.3).
- Two data are equal when their underlying rational PEL data (with the identification of the centre of `B` with `F`), subfields `𝒦 ⊂ F` and case tags agree, since every other field is a proposition: in Case 1, that `B` is a division algebra of degree `n` with an involution of the second kind and `V` is free of rank one over `B`; in Case 2, `B = F`, `* = c`, `F⁺ ≠ ℚ`, quasi-splitness, the ramification condition and anisotropy. The set `S` and the level `K` are parameters, not fields, and the rule says nothing about them.

*Needs:* PELModuli §§M0.2, M0.6, M0.7, M0.9, M0.12, M0.13, M0.16, M0.17, M1.9, M2.10, M3.1, M3.4, M4.3, M5.5; Mathlib `NumberField.IsCMField`, `NumberField.maximalRealSubfield`.

**Checks.**

- For `n = 1` in Case 2 every signature is `(1, 0)` or `(0, 1)`, so `d = 0` and `G` is a torus. A dimension formula summing `p_τq_τ` over all embeddings rather than over a CM type would still give `0` here, so the next check separates the two.
- For `[F⁺:ℚ] = 2`, `n = 3`, signature `(1, 2)` at `τ₀` and `(0, 3)` at `τ₁` (`τ₀, τ₁ ∈ Φ`), `d = 1·2 + 0·3 = 2`. Summing over all four embeddings gives `4`, which is twice the dimension. For the Harris–Taylor signature (`(1, n−1)` at one `τ₀ ∈ Φ` and `(0, n)` elsewhere) `d = n − 1`.
- The signature at `τ ∘ c` is `(q_τ, p_τ)`. A structure storing an independent signature at `τc` would admit `(1, 2)` at both `τ` and `τc`, which corresponds to no hermitian form.
- `F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)` (`F⁺ = ℚ`) with `B = F` is not a Case 2 datum: `2` ramifies in `F` and does not split in `F/F⁺ = 𝒦/ℚ`, so the ramification condition fails. Every imaginary quadratic field has a ramified prime, so the ramification condition alone excludes `F⁺ = ℚ` ([CS17] §5.1, footnote 27, p. 730, CS17 line 3293). The condition `F⁺ ≠ ℚ` is kept in the form in which Layer 9 uses it (`[F⁺:ℚ] ≥ 2`, EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.15). The converse fails: `F⁺ = ℚ(√5)` and `𝒦 = ℚ(i)` give `F⁺ ≠ ℚ`, but `2` is inert in `F⁺` and ramifies in `F`, so `2 ∉ Spl_{F/F⁺}`. A definition that kept `F⁺ ≠ ℚ` and dropped the ramification condition would admit this field, for which no auxiliary character `ϖ` ramified only at primes of `Spl_{F/F⁺}` exists (gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance).
- Case 2 with `n = 3` and signature `(1, 2)` at every `τ ∈ Φ` is not compact. A hermitian space of dimension at least 3 is isotropic at every finite place, so by the Hasse principle for hermitian forms it is isotropic once it is indefinite at every real place of `F⁺`, and then `G^ad` is isotropic. A definition that omits the anisotropy hypothesis in Case 2 would admit this non-proper example.
- Keep a Case 1 datum `D` and take `S` and `S ∪ {q}` for a prime `q ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` split in `𝒦`. Both sets satisfy the hypotheses on `S`, the datum is the same, and the Hecke algebras differ: `𝕋^S` contains the spherical factor at `q` and `𝕋^{S∪{q}}` does not. A structure that stored `S` but left it out of the extensionality rule would identify `(D, S)` with `(D, S ∪ {q})`, hence `𝕋^S` with `𝕋^{S∪{q}}`. Likewise two distinct neat levels `K ≠ K′` give different moduli problems `S_K` and `S_{K′}` of the same datum.
- For `n = 1` the adjoint group is trivial, so SV3 holds vacuously although `d = 0`. A criterion "SV3 if and only if `d > 0`" would put the rank-one tori, for instance Case 1 with `B = F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)`, on the wrong side.
- For `n = 2` with signature `(1, 1)` at `τ₀` (hence at `τ₀c`), `h` at the real place below `τ₀` is `diag(z, z̄)` up to scalars, and its image in the adjoint group acts on the root spaces through `z/z̄` and `z̄/z`, which are nontrivial. So SV3 holds, and `d = 1`.
- For `n = 2` with signature `(2, 0)` or `(0, 2)` at every `τ`, `h(z)` is the scalar `z` or `z̄` on each `V_τ`. Its image in `G^ad`, of dimension `3[F⁺:ℚ]` with compact real points, is trivial, so SV3 fails and `d = 0`. Such data occur in Case 1. Let `D` be the quaternion algebra over `ℚ` ramified exactly at `5` and `∞`, and `F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)`. Since `5 = (2 + i)(2 − i)` splits in `F`, `B = D ⊗_ℚ F` keeps local index `2` at both places above `5`, so it is a division algebra of degree `2` over `F`. Quaternion conjugation tensored with `c` is an involution of the second kind, and on `B ⊗_ℚ ℝ ≅ M_2(ℂ)` it is the conjugate transpose, so it is positive. Taking `V = B` with an alternating form whose hermitian form is definite gives a Case 1 datum with signatures `(2, 0)` and `(0, 2)`. In Case 2 with `n` odd every hermitian space is quasi-split at every finite place, so a hermitian space definite at every real place of `F⁺` is a wholly definite Case 2 datum whenever `F⁺ ≠ ℚ` and `F` satisfies the ramification condition. A definition that demanded SV3 would exclude these data, and a route through the Hodge-type suppliers would be applied outside their range.

### 8.2 Smooth proper integral models at good primes

Let a compact unitary datum (Case 1 or 2) and a set `S` as in §8.1 be given, and let `p ∉ S`, so that `F` and `G` are unramified at `p` (in Case 1, `B` splits at every place of `F` above `p`). Let `K = K_pK^p` with `K^p` neat and `K_p` the stabilizer in `G(ℚ_p)` of a lattice of `V ⊗ ℚ_p` that is self-dual for `⟨·,·⟩` and stable under a maximal `*`-stable order of `B ⊗ ℚ_p`; such a `K_p` is hyperspecial. When `p` splits in `𝒦`, as every auxiliary prime of Layers 9–11 does, every hyperspecial `K_p` is of this form. Indeed `G(ℚ_p) ≅ ∏_i GL_n(F_{𝔭_i}) × ℚ_p^×` (§8.3(1), in Case 1 after the Morita equivalence), a hyperspecial subgroup is `∏_i GL(L_i) × ℤ_p^×` for lattices `L_i ⊂ V_{𝔭_i}`, and it is the stabilizer of the self-dual lattice `⊕_i (L_i ⊕ L_i^⊥)`, where `L_i^⊥ ⊂ V_{𝔭_i^c}` is the lattice dual to `L_i`. At a prime with a place of `F⁺` inert in `F` a hyperspecial `K_p` need not stabilize a self-dual lattice (Layer 11 Examples), and PELModuli §M2.8 does not apply at such a level. Let `𝔭 | p` be a prime of `E`. Prove that the PEL moduli problem is represented by a scheme `𝒮_K` over `O_{E,𝔭}` that is smooth and proper of relative dimension `d`, with generic fibre `S_K ⊗_E E_𝔭`. Prove also that the prime-to-`p` Hecke correspondences extend to finite étale correspondences of the `𝒮_K` ([CS17] §5.1, pp. 730–731; §4.4, Theorem 4.4.4, p. 729, CS17 line 3236; §6.3, p. 756). In particular every compact unitary datum satisfies the properness hypothesis of the compact fibre theorem (IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §3.17).

Representability and smoothness at a good prime are PELModuli §M2.8, with relative dimension from §M2.10. Representability gives a smooth algebraic space, which is a quasi-projective scheme by ShimuraCompactifications §7.18. For properness, choose a projective admissible fan, which is a complete collection, for ShimuraCompactifications §7.3. Its toroidal compactification is proper (§7.5), and its boundary is indexed by cusp labels (§1.4), which come from rational parabolic subgroups of `G`. Anisotropy of `G^ad` leaves no proper rational parabolic, hence no boundary, so the toroidal compactification equals the moduli space. In Case 1 PELModuli §M2.11 gives properness directly, since `End_B(V) = B^op` is a division algebra. The prime-to-`p` Hecke correspondences are PELModuli §§M1.13 and M6.1. Only the toroidal compactification of the moduli problem is used, not its comparison with Shimura varieties, so this argument does not need SV3. The compact fibre theorem itself is applied only to data satisfying SV3 (§8.1). For a wholly definite datum `d = 0`, and `𝒮_K` is finite étale over `O_{E,𝔭}` (§8.9).

*Needs:* PELModuli §§M1.13, M2.8, M2.10, M2.11, M6.1; ShimuraCompactifications §§1.4, 7.3, 7.5, 7.18; this roadmap §8.1.

### 8.3 The local datum at an auxiliary prime split in 𝒦

Let `p ∉ S` be split in `𝒦`, `p = uu^c`, and let `𝔭_1, …, 𝔭_m` be the primes of `F` above `u`. Then `pO_F = ∏_i 𝔭_i𝔭_i^c`, and the `𝔭_i` correspond to the primes of `F⁺` above `p`. Fix `𝔭 | p` in `E` and a compatible `ι_p`. Construct the following data and prove their properties ([CS17] §5.4, p. 740; Theorem 5.5.7 and its proof, pp. 747–748, CS17 lines 3978–4036, with footnote 30).

1. An isomorphism `G_{ℚ_p} ≅ ∏_{i=1}^m Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p} GL_n × 𝔾_m`, the last factor being the similitude factor, through the projection `V ⊗ ℚ_p → ⊕_i V ⊗_F F_{𝔭_i}`. In Case 1 it depends on a choice of splitting of `B ⊗_F F_{𝔭_i}`. Hence `G(ℚ_p) ≅ ∏_i GL_n(F_{𝔭_i}) × ℚ_p^×`, and every irreducible `π_p` is `⊗_i π_{𝔭_i} ⊗ π_0`.
2. The sets `S_i = {τ | ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ induces 𝔭_i}`. They are pairwise disjoint, and `τ` lies in their union exactly when `τc` does not.
3. The decomposition `μ = ((μ_i)_i, μ_0)` with `μ_i = (μ_{i,τ})_{τ∈S_i}` and `μ_{i,τ}(t) = diag(t^{(p_τ)}, 1^{(q_τ)})`.
4. A bijection `B(G, μ⁻¹) = ∏_i B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` with `G_i = Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p} GL_n`. For `b = ((b_i), b_0)` it gives `J_b ≅ ∏_i J_{b_i} × 𝔾_m`.
5. Suppose `S_i` contains exactly one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, and let `c_i` be the number of `τ′ ∈ S_i` of signature `(n, 0)`. Shapiro's bijection followed by `b ↦ p^{c_i}b` identifies `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` with `B(GL_n/F_{𝔭_i}, μ_{i,τ}⁻¹)`. This preserves `J_b` up to restriction of scalars and preserves the `μ`-ordinary class. If `S_i` contains no such `τ`, then `μ_i` is central and `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is a single point.

The decomposition of `G_{ℚ_p}` comes from `F ⊗ ℚ_p = ∏_i (F_{𝔭_i} × F_{𝔭_i^c})`, with the hermitian pairing identifying the `𝔭_i^c`-part with the dual of the `𝔭_i`-part (PELModuli §M0.27; IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §0.11). Products and unramified restriction of scalars in `B(G)` are BunGAndNewtonStrata §1.30. The bound there is the sum of the component cocharacters `Σ_{τ′∈S_i} μ_{i,τ′} = μ_{i,τ} + c_i·z` with `z(t) = t·I_n`. Translation by the central, Frobenius-fixed element `p^{c_i}` subtracts `c_i z` from the bound, preserving `J_b` and the maximal element. [CS17] identifies the two Kottwitz sets without this translation.

The API:

- The places `𝔭_1, …, 𝔭_m` are in bijection with the primes of `F⁺` above `p`.
- The sets `S_i` are pairwise disjoint, and `τ ∈ ⋃_i S_i` if and only if `τc ∉ ⋃_i S_i`.
- `G_{ℚ_p} ≅ ∏_i Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p} GL_n × 𝔾_m`, and the induced decomposition of irreducible representations.
- `B(G, μ⁻¹) ≅ ∏_i B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)`, compatibly with `J_b`, with `ν_b` and with the `μ`-ordinary class.
- For `S_i` with a single nontrivial `τ`, `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹) ≅ B(GL_n/F_{𝔭_i}, μ_{i,τ}⁻¹)` through Shapiro and the translation by `p^{c_i}`.
- If `S_i` has no `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0` then `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is a singleton.

*Needs:* PELModuli §M0.27; IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §0.11; BunGAndNewtonStrata §§1.13, 1.23, 1.25, 1.26, 1.30; this roadmap §8.1.

**Checks.**

- If `p` splits completely in `F`, then `m = [F⁺:ℚ]` and every `S_i` is a singleton. A definition of `S_i` through the primes of `F⁺` instead of `F` would put `τ` and `τc` in the same set.
- Take `S_i = {τ, τ′}` with `(p_τ, q_τ) = (1, 1)` and `(p_{τ′}, q_{τ′}) = (2, 0)` (`n = 2`, `c_i = 1`). The Shapiro bound is `diag(t², t) = μ_{i,τ} + z`. Its Kottwitz invariant is `−3`, while that of `μ_{i,τ}⁻¹` is `−1`. Translation by `p` (determinant valuation `2`) carries one set onto the other. Without the translation the two sets would have different `κ`, so they could not be identified.
- In the same example `F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p` is unramified quadratic, `G_i ⊗ ℚ̄_p ≅ GL_2 × GL_2` with factors indexed by `τ, τ′`, and Frobenius exchanges the factors. The dominant representative of `μ_i⁻¹` is `((0, −1), (−1, −1))`, which is not Frobenius-invariant and so is the Newton point of no class. The `μ`-ordinary Newton point is its average `((−1/2, −1), (−1/2, −1))`. With `2ρ = (1, −1)` in each factor both vectors pair to `1 + 0 = 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 = p_τq_τ + p_{τ′}q_{τ′}`, so `d_b = d` survives the averaging. A convention setting `ν_b = (μ⁻¹)_dom` would leave this factor with no `μ`-ordinary class.
- For the Harris–Taylor signature with `n ≥ 2`, exactly one `S_i` contains an embedding with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, namely the one containing `τ₀` or `τ₀c`, whichever lies over `u`. For `n = 1` every `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is a point.
- The printed set `{τ | ι_p ∘ τ induces 𝔭_i}` is not defined. A definition through `ι_p ∘ τ` cannot be formed, and through `τ ∘ ι_p` it would need an embedding `F ↪ ℚ̄_p` as input instead of `τ`.
- Direction of `ι_p`. Take `F = ℚ(i)`, `p = 5` and `τ` the inclusion `F ⊂ ℂ`. Then `ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ` sends `i` to a square root of `−1` in `ℤ_5`, congruent to `2` or to `3` modulo `5`. In the first case `2 − i ↦ 0` modulo `5`, and `τ` lies in the set `S_i` of the prime `(2 − i)`; in the second `2 + i ↦ 5 ≡ 0`, and it lies in that of `(2 + i)`. PELModuli §M0.27 obtains the same embedding `F ↪ ℚ̄_5` as its `ι_p ∘ τ`, since its `ι_p` is the inverse of the one here.

### 8.4 The Hecke algebra `𝕋^S`, the operators `T_{i,v}` and the Hecke polynomial

Fix a prime `ℓ`. Define the abstract Hecke algebra `𝕋^S = ⊗′_{q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})} ℤ[G(ℚ_q)//K_q]`, the restricted tensor product of the spherical Hecke rings ([CS17] §6.3, p. 757, CS17 lines 4380–4383). For `q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})` and a place `v` of `F` above `q` we have `G(ℚ_q) = ∏_w GL_n(F_w) × ℚ_q^×`, with `w` over the place of `𝒦` below `v`. Let `T_{i,v} ∈ ℤ[G(ℚ_q)//K_q]` (`0 ≤ i ≤ n`) be the characteristic function of `GL_n(O_{F_v}) diag(ϖ_v^{(i)}, 1^{(n−i)}) GL_n(O_{F_v}) × ∏_{w≠v} GL_n(O_{F_w}) × ℤ_q^×`. Define `P_v(X) = Σ_{i=0}^n (−1)^i q_v^{i(i−1)/2} T_{i,v} X^{n−i} ∈ 𝕋^S[X]` with `q_v = #(O_F/𝔭_v)` ([CS17] Theorem 6.3.1(1), CS17 lines 4387–4408). [CS17] prints `ℤ_p^×` and `ℚ_p^×` for the similitude factor at `q`; the factor at `q` is `ℤ_q^× ⊂ ℚ_q^×`. `𝕋^S` acts on `H^i(S_{K,Ē}, Λ)` for every coefficient ring `Λ` through the Hecke correspondences of PELModuli §M1.13. For `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}`, its subalgebra `𝕋^{S∪{p}}` acts on the cohomology of Igusa varieties through `G(𝔸_f^p)`. The `p`-factor of `𝕋^S` has no such action.

The spherical Hecke ring at `q` is the double-coset ring of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.1.2 (Mathlib's `HeckeRing` with Tau Ceti's `HeckeCosetModule.instRingHeckeRing`). `T_{i,v}` is the `GL_n(F_v)` generator of SR.4.3 placed in the `v`-factor. `T_{n,v}` is the class of the central element `ϖ_v`, hence a unit. Galois-type maximal ideals follow IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §3b.1, with this polynomial and geometric Frobenius.

The API:

- `𝕋^S` is commutative, since each spherical Hecke ring of an unramified group is commutative.
- `T_{0,v} = 1`, and `T_{n,v}` is a unit with inverse the class of `ϖ_v⁻¹`.
- `P_v` is monic of degree `n` with constant term `(−1)^n q_v^{n(n−1)/2} T_{n,v}`.
- For `S ⊂ S′` there is an inclusion `𝕋^{S′} → 𝕋^S`, and localization at `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` factors through localization at `𝔪 ∩ 𝕋^{S′}` (§11.1).
- Under the Satake isomorphism of SR.4.1 the image of `P_v` is `∏_j (X − q_v^{(n−1)/2} x_j)` in the Satake variables of `GL_n(F_v)`.
- The prime-to-`p` action on Igusa cohomology is a `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-action compatible with restriction of the finite-level action on `S_K`.

*Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.1.2, SR.4.1, SR.4.3; Mathlib `HeckeRing`; Tau Ceti `HeckeCosetModule.instRingHeckeRing`; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §3b.1; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.0 «Automorphic specialization of the integral Hecke polynomial»; PELModuli §M1.13; this roadmap §8.1.

**Checks.**

- For `n = 1`, `T_{1,v}` is the class of `ϖ_v ∈ F_v^×` and `P_v(X) = X − T_{1,v}`. For `n = 2`, `P_v(X) = X² − T_{1,v}X + q_vT_{2,v}`. Omitting the factor `q_v^{i(i−1)/2}` would give constant term `T_{2,v}`, and the roots would then not be the Satake parameters scaled by `q_v^{1/2}`.
- For `n = 2` the Satake image of `P_v` is `(X − q_v^{1/2}x_1)(X − q_v^{1/2}x_2)`. This agrees with the generators of SR.4.3, `S(T_1) = q_v^{1/2}(x_1 + x_2)` and `S(T_2) = x_1x_2`, since `e_2 = q_v x_1x_2 = q_v S(T_2)`.
- The operator defined with the factor `ℤ_p^×` at a prime `q ≠ p` is not an element of `ℤ[G(ℚ_q)//K_q]`, since `ℤ_p^×` is not a subgroup of `ℚ_q^×`. The definition uses `ℤ_q^×`.
- For `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` the spherical `p`-factor belongs to `𝕋^S`, but `G(𝔸_f^p)` supplies no action of it on Igusa cohomology. Forgetting this factor gives the `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-action.

### 8.5 The dual ideal 𝔪^∨ and its Galois representation

Let `ι` be the involution of `𝕋^S` induced by `[K_qgK_q] ↦ [K_qg⁻¹K_q]`. It is an anti-involution of a commutative ring, hence an involution. Put `𝔪^∨ = ι(𝔪)` for a maximal ideal `𝔪` with finite residue field `κ(𝔪) = 𝕋^S/𝔪`, of any residue characteristic. Then `𝔪^∨` is maximal and `ῑ : κ(𝔪) → κ(𝔪^∨)`, `t mod 𝔪 ↦ ι(t) mod 𝔪^∨`, is an isomorphism of fields. Polynomials modulo `𝔪^∨` are compared with polynomials modulo `𝔪` through `ῑ⁻¹`, which sends `t mod 𝔪^∨` to `ι(t) mod 𝔪`. Prove the following ([CS17] §6.3, proof of Theorem 6.3.1, p. 759).

1. `ι(T_{i,v}) = T_{n,v}⁻¹ T_{n−i,v}`.
2. Let `v` lie above `q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})`, and suppose `q ∉ 𝔪`, so that `q_v` is a unit modulo `𝔪`. Let `λ_1, …, λ_n` be the roots of `P_v` modulo `𝔪` in an algebraic closure `k̄` of `κ(𝔪)`. Then every `λ_j` is nonzero, and the roots of `P_v` modulo `𝔪^∨`, carried into `k̄` by `ῑ⁻¹`, are `q_v^{n−1}λ_1⁻¹, …, q_v^{n−1}λ_n⁻¹`. If `ℓ ∈ 𝔪`, the hypothesis holds because `q ≠ ℓ`; for an embedding `θ : κ(𝔪) ↪ 𝔽̄_ℓ` one takes `k̄ = 𝔽̄_ℓ` through `θ` and reads `𝔪^∨` through `θ ∘ ῑ⁻¹`. For `n = 1` the hypothesis `q ∉ 𝔪` is not needed. For `n ≥ 2` and `q ∈ 𝔪` the constant term of `P_v` vanishes modulo `𝔪`, `0` is a root, and no such rule holds (Checks).
3. Let `𝔪` be of Galois type with semisimple `ρ_𝔪 : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)` for an embedding `θ : κ(𝔪) ↪ 𝔽̄_ℓ`, unramified outside the places above `S ∪ {ℓ}`. Then `𝔪^∨` is of Galois type for the embedding `θ ∘ ῑ⁻¹`, with `ρ_{𝔪^∨} ≅ ρ_𝔪^∨ ⊗ ε̄^{1−n}`.
4. At a place `𝔭_i` of `F` not above `S ∪ {ℓ}`, `ρ_𝔪` is unramified and decomposed generic if and only if `ρ_{𝔪^∨}` is. Hence a pair `(p, 𝔭)` satisfies the auxiliary-pair condition of §8.7 for `ρ_𝔪` through a place `u` of `𝒦` if and only if it does for `ρ_{𝔪^∨}` through the same `u`.

This is the rank-`n` form of IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §5.1, with the same involution formalism (Tau Ceti `HeckeAntiInvolution.ofAmbient`). For (1), the double coset of `diag(ϖ_v^{(i)}, 1^{(n−i)})⁻¹` is `ϖ_v⁻¹ · GL_n(O) diag(1^{(i)}, ϖ_v^{(n−i)}) GL_n(O)`, and multiplying by the central double coset `T_{n,v}⁻¹` stays inside a single double coset. For (2), `q_v` is invertible modulo `𝔪` by hypothesis, so `T_{i,v} ≡ q_v^{−i(i−1)/2} e_i(λ)` modulo `𝔪`. In particular `λ_1⋯λ_n = e_n(λ) ≡ q_v^{n(n−1)/2} T_{n,v}` is a product of two units (§8.4), so no `λ_j` is `0`. Carrying `P_v` modulo `𝔪^∨` to `κ(𝔪)` by `ῑ⁻¹` replaces `T_{i,v}` by `ι(T_{i,v})`. Substituting (1), the coefficient of `X^{n−i}` becomes `(−1)^i q_v^{i(n−1)} e_i(λ⁻¹)`; the exponent is `i(i−1)/2 + n(n−1)/2 − (n−i)(n−i−1)/2 = i(n−1)`. Part (3) is AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.7 «Dual and character-twist Hecke comparison» at rank `n`, with uniqueness from Brauer–Nesbitt and Chebotarev. For (4), the Frobenius eigenvalues of `ρ_{𝔪^∨}` at `𝔭_i` are the `q_v^{n−1}λ_j⁻¹` by (3), where `q_v` is a unit of `𝔽̄_ℓ` because `𝔭_i ∤ ℓ`; the ordered-ratio condition is invariant under `λ ↦ q_v^{n−1}λ⁻¹`, which sends `λ_a/λ_b` to `λ_b/λ_a`, and duals and cyclotomic twists preserve unramifiedness at `𝔭_i ∤ ℓ`.

The API:

- `ι` is a ring automorphism of `𝕋^S` with `ι ∘ ι = id`. On each spherical factor it is the anti-automorphism `[K_qgK_q] ↦ [K_qg⁻¹K_q]`, which is an automorphism because the factor is commutative.
- For a maximal ideal `𝔪` with finite residue field, `𝔪^∨` is maximal, `ι` induces an isomorphism `𝕋^S/𝔪 ≅ 𝕋^S/𝔪^∨` of residue fields, and `(𝔪^∨)^∨ = 𝔪`.
- `ι(T_{i,v}) = T_{n,v}⁻¹ T_{n−i,v}` for `0 ≤ i ≤ n`; in particular `ι(T_{n,v}) = T_{n,v}⁻¹` and `ι(T_{0,v}) = 1`.
- Let `v` lie above `q ∉ 𝔪` (automatic when `ℓ ∈ 𝔪`, since `q ≠ ℓ`). The roots of `P_v` modulo `𝔪` in an algebraic closure of `κ(𝔪)` are nonzero, and the roots of `P_v` modulo `𝔪^∨`, carried there by `ῑ⁻¹`, are their images under `λ ↦ q_v^{n−1}λ⁻¹`. For `n ≥ 2` and `q ∈ 𝔪` the root `0` occurs and no such rule holds.
- When `𝔪` is of Galois type for an embedding `θ : κ(𝔪) ↪ 𝔽̄_ℓ`, the residual representation of `𝔪^∨` for `θ ∘ ῑ⁻¹` is `ρ_𝔪^∨ ⊗ ε̄^{1−n}`.
- For `S ⊂ S′`, `ι` preserves the subalgebra `𝕋^{S′}`, and `(𝔪 ∩ 𝕋^{S′})^∨ = 𝔪^∨ ∩ 𝕋^{S′}`.

**Checks.**

- For `n = 1`, (1) reads `ι(T_{1,v}) = T_{1,v}⁻¹`, the exponent `i(n−1)` is `0`, and the root `λ` of `P_v = X − T_{1,v}` modulo `𝔪` goes to `λ⁻¹` modulo `𝔪^∨`. A formula `ι(T_{i,v}) = T_{n−i,v}` without the central factor would give `ι(T_{1,v}) = T_{0,v} = 1`, which no ring automorphism does.
- For `n = 2`, `q_v = 7`, `ℓ = 11` and roots `{1, 2}` modulo `𝔪`: `T_{1,v} ≡ 3` and `T_{2,v} ≡ 7⁻¹·2 = 8·2 ≡ 5`. By (1), modulo `𝔪^∨` one has `T_{1,v} ≡ 5⁻¹·3 = 9·3 ≡ 5` and `T_{2,v} ≡ 5⁻¹ ≡ 9`, so `P_v ≡ X² − 5X + 7·9 = X² − 5X + 8`, whose roots `{7, 9}` are `{7·1⁻¹, 7·2⁻¹}` as (2) predicts. The root multisets at `v` differ, so `𝔪^∨ ≠ 𝔪`.
- In the same example the formula `ι(T_{i,v}) = T_{n−i,v}` would give `T_{1,v} ≡ 3` and `T_{2,v} ≡ T_{0,v} = 1` modulo the dual ideal, hence `X² − 3X + 7` with roots `{4, 10}` instead of `{7, 9}`, and a constant term `(−1)^n q_v^{n(n−1)/2}` independent of `𝔪`.
- Roots `{3, 6}` at `q_v = 7`, `ℓ = 11` go to `{7·3⁻¹, 7·6⁻¹} = {7·4, 7·2} = {6, 3}`, the same multiset, while their ordered ratios `2` and `6` lie outside `{1, 7}`. So a strongly generic eigensystem can be self-dual at `v`, and (2) does not force `𝔪^∨ ≠ 𝔪`.
- Negative control for the hypothesis `q ∉ 𝔪`. The degree character `deg : 𝕋^S → ℤ` sends the class of `K_qgK_q` to the number of left cosets `hK_q` it contains, factor by factor; it is the character by which `𝕋^S` acts on the invariants of the trivial representation, hence a ring homomorphism. Since `G(ℚ_q)` is unimodular, `KgK` and `Kg⁻¹K` contain equally many left cosets, so `deg ∘ ι = deg`. Take a datum with `n = 2`, `𝒦 = ℚ(i)`, `ℓ = 3` and `5 ∉ S`, so that `5 ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})`, and `v` of residue degree one over `q = 5`: `deg(T_{1,v}) = q_v + 1 = 6` and `deg(T_{2,v}) = 1`, so `deg(P_v) = X² − 6X + 5 = (X − 1)(X − 5)`. For `𝔪_5 = deg⁻¹(5ℤ)`, `𝔪_5^∨ = 𝔪_5` and `P_v ≡ X² − X` modulo both, with roots `{0, 1}`. The rule `λ ↦ 5λ⁻¹` is undefined at `0` and sends `1` to `0`, so it fails; here `q = 5 ∈ 𝔪_5`.
- For `𝔪_3 = deg⁻¹(3ℤ)` in the same setting, `ℓ = 3 ∈ 𝔪_3`, `𝔪_3^∨ = 𝔪_3` and `P_v ≡ X² + 2` with roots `{1, 2}` in `𝔽_3`. The rule `λ ↦ 5λ⁻¹ = 2λ⁻¹` sends them to `{2, 1}`, the same multiset, as (2) predicts. Over `ℚ` the roots `{1, 5}` of `deg(P_v)` are exchanged by `λ ↦ 5λ⁻¹` as well.
- The transport through `ῑ` matters even when `𝔪^∨ = 𝔪`. Take `n = 1`, `ℓ = 3`, and a character `χ : 𝕋^S → 𝔽_9` with values of order dividing `4` on the double cosets and `χ(T_{1,v}) = a`, where `a² = −1`. Since `χ ∘ ι` inverts these values and `x⁻¹ = x³` for `x⁴ = 1`, `χ ∘ ι` is `χ` followed by the Frobenius of `𝔽_9`, so `𝔪 = ker χ` satisfies `𝔪^∨ = 𝔪`, and `ῑ` is that Frobenius. Through `ῑ⁻¹` the root of `P_v` modulo `𝔪^∨` is `a⁻¹ = a³ = −a`, as (2) predicts; read through the identity of `κ(𝔪)` it would be `a ≠ −a`.

### 8.6 Hecke-equivariant Poincaré duality on S_K and on Mantovan's Igusa varieties

Prove the following two duality statements ([CS17] §6.3, proof of Theorem 6.3.1, p. 759; §5, introduction, p. 729).

1. Let `Λ = ℤ/ℓ^r`. The Poincaré pairing `H^i(S_{K,Ē}, Λ) × H^{2d−i}(S_{K,Ē}, Λ(d)) → Λ` of the smooth proper `E`-variety `S_K` is perfect, and the transpose of `t ∈ 𝕋^S` is `ι(t)`. Hence, for every maximal ideal `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S`, `RΓ(S_{K,Ē}, Λ)_𝔪 ≅ RHom_Λ(RΓ(S_{K,Ē}, Λ)_{𝔪^∨}, Λ)[−2d](−d)`. In particular, if `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪^∨} = 0` for all `i < d`, then `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `i > d`. The same holds for the local system `L_V` of a finite `𝔽_ℓ[K/K′]`-module `V`, where `K′ ⊂ K` is a normal compact open subgroup with `K′_q = K_q` for every `q` outside a finite set `S′ ⊇ S`, for the operators and the maximal ideals of `𝕋^{S′}`, with `L_{V^∨}(d)` on the dual side. The operators of `𝕋^S` at the primes of `S′ ∖ S` need not act on `H^*(S_K, L_V)`.
2. Let `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` be split in `𝒦` and `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)`. For the operators of `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`, `RΓ_c(I^b_{Mant,m}, Λ)_{𝔪^∨} ≅ RHom_Λ(RΓ(I^b_{Mant,m}, Λ)_𝔪, Λ)[−2d_b](−d_b)`. This is compatible with pullback along the finite étale transition maps on `RΓ` and trace on `RΓ_c`.

Perfectness of the pairing for smooth varieties with torsion coefficients is EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves §0.5, with `H_c = H` because `S_K` is proper (§8.2, at any prime `p ∉ S` split in `𝒦`). A double coset `[KgK]` acts by the correspondence `S_{K∩gKg⁻¹} ⇉ S_K` (pullback, then trace). Its transpose under cup product is the correspondence with the two maps exchanged, which is `[Kg⁻¹K]`. This is the étale form of ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.5a.4. For `L_V` and `g ∈ G(ℚ_q)` with `q ∉ S′`, the correspondence lifts to the level `K′`, since `g` lies in the factor at `q`, where `K′_q = K_q`; so the two pullbacks of `L_V` are identified and `[KgK]` acts on `H^*(S_K, L_V)`. Since the pairing is `𝕋^S`-equivariant (`𝕋^{S′}`-equivariant for `L_V`) for `t` on the left and `ι(t)` on the right, the `𝔪`-part pairs perfectly with the `𝔪^∨`-part. Part (2) is the argument of IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §5.2 for the data of this roadmap. It uses only that `I^b_{Mant,m}` is smooth of dimension `d_b` (its §1.3) and the action away from `p`. For a maximal ideal `𝔪` containing `ℓ`, both statements pass to `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, with the integral localization of the Conventions, by taking `R lim_r` over `Λ = ℤ/ℓ^r`: the complexes are perfect, and `(C ⊗^L ℤ/ℓ^r)_𝔪 = C_𝔪 ⊗^L ℤ/ℓ^r` (ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.4.9). Since `ι` fixes `ℓ`, `𝔪^∨` contains `ℓ` as well.

### 8.7 Strong decomposed genericity and Caraiani–Scholze auxiliary pairs

Let `F` be a number field, `L/F` a finite Galois extension, `k` a finite field of characteristic `ℓ`, and `r : Gal(L/F) → GL_n(k)` a homomorphism. Define `IsStrongGenericAt r v` for a prime `v` of `𝓞 F` to mean the following. The prime `v` is maximal and does not contain `ℓ`. For every prime `Q` of `𝓞 L` above `v`, every element of the inertia group of `Q` maps to `1`. For every arithmetic Frobenius `σ` at `Q` (Mathlib `IsArithFrobAt`), the characteristic polynomial of `r(σ)` over `k̄` is `∏_i (X − α_i)` with `α_i/α_j ∉ {1, q_v}` for all `i ≠ j`, where `q_v = #(𝓞 F/v)` ([CS17] Definition 1.9, p. 652, CS17 line 149; Definition 6.2.1, p. 755, CS17 line 4326). Every continuous `ρ̄ : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)` has finite image in some `GL_n(k)` and factors through such an `r`. `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic at `v` in the sense of [CS17] exactly when its finite realization satisfies `IsStrongGenericAt` at `v`. Define `IsCompletelySplitStrongGenericPrime r p` to mean the following: `p` is prime, there are `[F:ℚ]` primes of `𝓞 F` above `p` (so `p` splits completely in `F`), and `IsStrongGenericAt r v` holds for every prime `v` above `p`.

Let now `ρ̄ : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)` be continuous, semisimple and unramified outside the places above `S ∪ {ℓ}`, for the field `F` of a compact unitary datum. A pair `(p, 𝔭)` is a CS-auxiliary pair for `ρ̄` if `p ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})`, `𝔭 | p` is a prime of `E`, and there is a place `u` of `𝒦` above `p` (`p = uu^c`) such that, for an `ι_p` compatible with `𝔭`, the following holds at every prime `𝔭_i` of `F` above `u`. The set `S_i` of §8.3 contains at most one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, and if it contains one, `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic at `𝔭_i` ([CS17] Theorem 6.3.1(2), p. 757, CS17 lines 4409–4413, whose hypothesis includes the decomposition `p = uu^c`, a choice by §5.4, p. 740, CS17 line 3679). We then say that `(p, 𝔭)` is auxiliary through `u`. The condition does not depend on the `ι_p` compatible with `𝔭`. Two choices inducing the same embedding of `E` differ by an automorphism `g` of `ℂ` fixing `E`; this replaces `S_i` by `g⁻¹S_i` at the same `𝔭_i`, and `p_{gτ} = p_τ` because `E` is the reflex field. Two embeddings of `E` inducing `𝔭` differ by a continuous automorphism of `ℚ̄_p`, which induces the same primes of `F`. The condition does depend on `u`. Passing to `u^c` replaces `𝔭_i` by `𝔭_i^c` and `S_i` by `{τ ∘ c | τ ∈ S_i}`, which has as many embeddings with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`; only the genericity part changes (Checks).

The local predicate is AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.7 «Strong local decomposed genericity», applied to `ρ̄|_{Gal(F̄_v/F_v)}`. The auxiliary-pair condition depends only on `ρ̄^{ss}`, and arithmetic and geometric Frobenius give the same condition.

The API:

- `IsStrongGenericAt r v` does not depend on the choice of `Q` above `v` or of the Frobenius at `Q`. Two choices of `Q` are conjugate, and two Frobenius elements at `Q` differ by inertia, which `r` kills.
- `IsStrongGenericAt r v` implies the weaker condition `α_i ≠ q_vα_j` for `i ≠ j`. The converse holds when the `α_i` are pairwise distinct.
- `IsStrongGenericAt` is preserved by `r ↦ r^∨ ⊗ χ` for every character `χ` of `Gal(L/F)` unramified at `v`. Applied to `χ = ε̄^{1−n}` (after enlarging `L` by `ζ_ℓ`), this gives the invariance under `𝔪 ↦ 𝔪^∨` of §8.5.
- If `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` splits completely in `F` and `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic at all places above `p`, then every `𝔭 | p` in `E` gives an auxiliary pair, through either place `u` above `p`, because `p` splits in `𝒦` and every `S_i` is a singleton. The hypothesis `p ∉ S` is not implied by the others, since `S` may contain good primes.
- If `p_τq_τ = 0` for every `τ` (definite `G_ℝ`), every `(p, 𝔭)` with `p ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})` is an auxiliary pair, through either place `u` above `p`.
- The auxiliary-pair condition for `ρ̄` through a place `u` holds if and only if it holds through the same `u` for `ρ̄^∨ ⊗ ε̄^{1−n}` and for `ρ̄^{ss}`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `IsArithFrobAt`, `Ideal.inertia`, `Ideal.primesOver`, `Ideal.LiesOver`; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.7 «Strong local decomposed genericity»; this roadmap §8.3.

**Checks.**

- For `n = 1` there are no pairs `i ≠ j`. `IsStrongGenericAt` then reduces to `v ∤ ℓ` and unramifiedness. Every signature is `(1, 0)` or `(0, 1)`, so no `S_i` contains a `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, and the auxiliary-pair condition reduces to `p ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})`. A definition that also asked `α_1 ≠ q_v` would wrongly exclude characters.
- For `n = 2`, `q_v = 7` and `ℓ = 11`, repeated eigenvalues `{3, 3}` fail strong genericity (ratio `1`). They satisfy the weaker condition, since `3 ≠ 7·3 = 21 ≡ 10`. A definition with only the weaker condition accepts this example. The weaker condition is satisfied here because `q_v ≢ 1` modulo `ℓ`.
- For `q_v = 7`, `ℓ = 11` and eigenvalues `{1, 2}`, the ordered ratios are `2` and `2⁻¹ = 6`, neither in `{1, 7}`, so the condition holds. Eigenvalues `{1, 7}` fail it.
- If some `S_i` contains two embeddings with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, the pair is not auxiliary through `u`, whatever `ρ̄` is. Nor is it through `u^c`: the set attached to `𝔭_i^c` is `{τ ∘ c | τ ∈ S_i}`, and `p_{τc}q_{τc} = q_τp_τ`.
- Dependence on `u`. Take signatures as in the Worked examples (`F⁺` real quadratic, `n = 3`, `(1, 2)` at `τ₀` and `(0, 3)` at `τ₁`), `ℓ = 11`, a prime `p ∉ S` split completely in `F` with `p ≡ 7` modulo `11`, and `ι_p` such that `ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ₀` induces a prime `𝔭_1` above the place `u`. Let `χ : Gal(F̄/F) → 𝔽_11^×` be a character unramified above `p` with `χ(Frob_{𝔭_1}) = 2` and `χ(Frob_{𝔭_1^c}) = 1` (Grunwald–Wang, order `10`), `S` containing the primes below its ramification, and `ρ̄ = 1 ⊕ χ ⊕ χ²`. Through `u` the only set with a nontrivial embedding is `S_1 = {τ₀}`; the eigenvalues at `𝔭_1` are `{1, 2, 4}`, with ordered ratios `{2, 4, 6, 3}`, none in `{1, 7}`, so the pair is auxiliary through `u`. Through `u^c` that set is `{τ₀c}`, at `𝔭_1^c`, where the eigenvalues are `{1, 1, 1}` with ratio `1`, so it is not auxiliary through `u^c`. A definition that leaves `u` unnamed gives this pair two truth values; with the existential form it is auxiliary.
- Case 1 with `n = 1`, `B = F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)`, `ℓ = 3`, trivial `ρ̄` and `S = {2, 5}`. The prime `5 = (2 + i)(2 − i)` splits completely in `F` and `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic at both places above it (rank one, `5 ≠ 3`), but `5 ∈ S`, so `(5, 𝔭)` is not an auxiliary pair; the complete-splitting implication of the API does not apply because its hypothesis `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` fails. For `p = 13 = (3 + 2i)(3 − 2i) ∉ S ∪ {3}` both the hypothesis and the conclusion hold.
- For `v` above `ℓ` the predicate fails by definition. Without the condition `ℓ ∉ v`, [CS17] Definition 1.9 (which assumes `ℓ ≠ p`) would be applied outside its range.

### 8.8 Existence of auxiliary primes

Prove the following two statements ([CS17] Remarks 6.3.2–6.3.3, p. 758, CS17 lines 4422–4434, with footnote 33; Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.5, pp. 651–652, CS17 lines 89–112).

(a) `infinite_setOf_isCompletelySplitStrongGenericPrime`: for every finite Galois realization `r`, if some rational prime satisfies `IsCompletelySplitStrongGenericPrime r`, infinitely many do. In particular such primes can be chosen outside any finite set, for example outside `S ∪ {ℓ}`. For each such `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` and every `𝔭 | p`, `(p, 𝔭)` is a CS-auxiliary pair for `ρ̄`, through either place of `𝒦` above `p` (§8.7).

(b) `infinite_setOf_isStrongGenericAt_degree_one`: if `IsStrongGenericAt r v₀` holds at one prime `v₀` of `𝓞 F` of any residue degree, it holds at infinitely many primes `v` with `#(𝓞 F/v)` prime. Consequently, suppose the signature of `G` is definite at all infinite places of `F⁺` but one, and `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic at some finite place. Then there are infinitely many degree-one places `v` over a rational prime `p ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` at which `ρ̄` is unramified and decomposed generic. Such a `p` is split in `𝒦`. Let `u` be the place of `𝒦` below `v`. Choose `ι_p`, hence `𝔭`, so that `ι_p⁻¹ ∘ τ₀` induces `v` for the embedding `τ₀` with `p_{τ₀}q_{τ₀} ≠ 0` lying over `u`. Then `(p, 𝔭)` is a CS-auxiliary pair through `u`.

For (a), let `L_r` be the field cut out by `r` and `L̃` the Galois closure over `ℚ` of `L_r(ζ_ℓ)`. The prime `p₀` is unramified in `L̃`, since it is unramified in `F`, in `L_r` (because `r` is unramified above it) and in `ℚ(ζ_ℓ)` (because `p₀ ≠ ℓ`). Its Frobenius class in `Gal(L̃/ℚ)` lies in the normal subgroup fixing the Galois closure of `F`. For every place above `p₀` this class determines the Frobenius class in `Gal(L_r/F)`, and it determines `p₀` modulo `ℓ`. Chebotarev's theorem for `L̃/ℚ` (Tau Ceti `NumberField.Chebotarev.infinite_frobeniusPrimeSet`, with the class read through `IsArithFrobAt` by `NumberField.Chebotarev.mem_frobeniusPrimeSet_mk_iff_exists_isArithFrobAt`) gives infinitely many rational primes unramified in `L̃` with the same class, and each of them other than `ℓ` satisfies the predicate. This is the argument of AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.7 «Infinitely many decomposed generic primes», run with the strong condition. For (b), `v₀` is unramified in `L_r(ζ_ℓ)` because `r` kills its inertia and `v₀ ∤ ℓ`; let `C` be its Frobenius class in `Gal(L_r(ζ_ℓ)/F)`. The primes of `𝓞 F` with Frobenius class `C` have Dirichlet density `#C/[L_r(ζ_ℓ) : F] > 0` (Tau Ceti `NumberField.Chebotarev.hasDirichletDensity_frobeniusPrimeSet`). Only places of degree one contribute to the Dirichlet density ([CS17] footnote 33; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.hasDirichletDensity_inter_compl_higherDegreePrimes_iff`), so the degree-one primes of class `C` have the same positive density and are infinite in number (`NumberField.Set.HasDirichletDensity.infinite`). The class `C` fixes `r(Frob_v)` up to conjugacy, and it fixes `q_v` modulo `ℓ` through the action on `ζ_ℓ`, so each of these primes not above `ℓ` satisfies the predicate. [CS17] Remark 6.3.3 says there is just one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`. In fact, when the non-definite place is indefinite, there are exactly two such embeddings, `τ₀` and `τ₀c`, one over `u` and one over `u^c`. Only one of them lies in `⋃_i S_i`, and the choice of `ι_p` puts it in the `S_i` with `𝔭_i = v`. If that place is also definite there is none, and the condition holds trivially.

### 8.9 Data of dimension zero: finite moduli and their Igusa varieties

Let a compact unitary datum with `d = 0` be given, that is `n = 1` or a wholly definite datum (§8.1), with `S` and a neat `K = K_SK^S` as in §8.1. This is the setting in which SV3 may fail, and nothing below uses it. Prove the following (for the one-point set `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` when `μ_i` is central, [CS17] proof of Theorem 5.5.7, p. 748, CS17 lines 4030–4032; for Mantovan's Igusa varieties on a PEL datum that asks for the positivity of `⟨·, h(√−1)·⟩` and not for SV3, [Shin09] Definitions 5.2 and 5.3, author pp. 13 and 16, Shin09 lines 570–575 and 703; the finiteness, cohomology and Igusa-pullback statements are deduced here from the supplier sections of the *Needs* line and are not stated in [CS17]).

1. `h` is central in `G_ℝ` and `X = {h}` is a point. `S_K` is finite étale over `E`, and `S_K(ℂ) = ⊔_a G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)` is a finite set, compatibly with level change and the Hecke correspondences.
2. For every étale sheaf `L` on `S_K` (the constant sheaves `Λ`, the local systems `L_V` and the lattices `L_ξ` of §11.8), `H^i(S_{K,Ē}, L) = 0` for `i > 0` and `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, L) = ∏_{x ∈ S_K(Ē)} L_x`. In particular `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, ℤ_ℓ)` is free of rank `#S_K(Ē)` with reduction `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)` modulo `ℓ`, and `H^*(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` is concentrated in degree `0 = d` for every maximal ideal `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S`. For every maximal ideal `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` containing `ℓ`, the localization `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪` of the Conventions is free over `ℤ_ℓ` with reduction `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` modulo `ℓ`, and likewise `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, L_ξ)_𝔪` is free over `O_λ` with reduction `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, L_ξ/λ)_𝔪`.
3. Let `p ∉ S` be split in `𝒦`, with `K_p` hyperspecial, and let `𝔭 | p` be a prime of `E`. Then `𝒮_K` (§8.2) is finite étale over `O_{E,𝔭}`, and specialization gives a bijection `S_K(Ē_𝔭) ≅ 𝒮_K(𝔽̄_p)` compatible with the prime-to-`p` Hecke correspondences. `B(G, μ⁻¹)` consists of one class `b₀`, which is `μ`-ordinary, with `d_{b₀} = 0` and `J_{b₀} ≅ G_{ℚ_p}`. Every finite-level Igusa variety `I^{b₀}_{Mant,m}` is a finite set of points, so `H^i(Ig^{b₀}, Λ) = 0` for `i > 0`.
4. In the situation of (3), let `𝔪′ ⊂ 𝕋^{S∪{p}}` be a maximal ideal with `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0`. Then `H^0(Ig^{b₀}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0`, and `H^0(I^{b₀}_{Mant,m}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0` for every large enough `m`.

For (1): by the signature, `h(z)` acts on each `V_τ` as the scalar `z` or `z̄` when the datum is wholly definite, and `G` is a torus when `n = 1`, so `h` is central and its `G(ℝ)`-class is a point. By §8.2 at a prime `p ∉ S`, `S_K ⊗_E E_𝔭` is smooth and proper of dimension `0`, hence finite étale, and finite étaleness descends to `E`. The complex points are PELModuli §M3.4, and the quotient is finite by PELModuli §M3.3, since the adjoint real group is compact. For (2): `S_{K,Ē}` is a finite disjoint union of copies of `Spec Ē`, on which an étale sheaf is the family of its stalks and has no higher cohomology. A finite free `ℤ_ℓ`-module (or `O_λ`-module) `M` with commuting Hecke operators is the direct sum of its localizations `M_𝔪` in the sense of the Conventions, over the finitely many maximal ideals `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` containing `ℓ` that are contractions of maximal ideals of the finite `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra (or `O_λ`-algebra) generated by the operators. Each `M_𝔪` is a direct summand, hence projective over the local ring `ℤ_ℓ` (or `O_λ`) and free, and `M_𝔪/ℓM_𝔪 = (M/ℓM)_𝔪` is the ordinary localization of the finite module `M/ℓM`. For (3): a finite étale scheme over the henselization of `O_{E,𝔭}` has the same geometric points over its generic and its special fibre, and the Hecke correspondences are finite étale over `O_{E,𝔭}` (§8.2). No `S_i` contains a `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, so every `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is a point (§8.3(5)). Under Shapiro's bijection and the translation by `p^{c_i}` of §8.3, this point is the trivial class of `B(GL_n/F_{𝔭_i})`, whose group `J` is `GL_n`; so `J_{b_i} ≅ G_i`, and `J_{b₀} ≅ ∏_i G_i × 𝔾_m ≅ G_{ℚ_p}` by §8.3(4). The Newton point of `b₀` is central, so `d_{b₀} = 0 = d`, and `b₀` is `μ`-ordinary (Conventions). The finite-level Igusa varieties of `b₀` are finite étale over a central leaf of the finite special fibre and have dimension `d_{b₀} = 0` (IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §§1.3, 1.4a), and `H^i(Ig^{b₀}) = colim_m H^i(I^{b₀}_{Mant,m})` (its §1.4). For (4): the Newton map sends every geometric point of `𝒮_{K,𝔽̄_p}` to `b₀` (its §0.12). So the special fibre is the disjoint union of the central leaves `C^X` (its §0.24) of the finitely many `p`-divisible groups `X` with `G`-structure, in the class `b₀`, that occur in it. Each leaf is open and closed in the finite special fibre and stable under the prime-to-`p` Hecke correspondences. The perfect Igusa variety `Ig^X → C^X` is a pro-étale torsor over the perfection of `C^X`, hence surjective, and `Ig^X ≅ Ig^{b₀}` compatibly with `G(𝔸_f^p)`, which acts through the Hecke correspondences (its §§1.1, 1.2, 1.6). Pullback therefore gives a `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-equivariant injection `H^0(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ) ≅ H^0(𝒮_{K,𝔽̄_p}, 𝔽_ℓ) = ⊕_X H^0(C^X, 𝔽_ℓ) ↪ ⊕_X H^0(Ig^{b₀}, 𝔽_ℓ)`, and localization is exact. The transition maps of Mantovan's tower are finite étale and surjective, hence injective on `H^0`, which gives the finite-level statement. No perfectoid tower or period map is used: for central `μ` the flag variety `G/P_μ` is a point.

### 9.1 The stable Igusa trace identity for the compact quasi-split datum

Fix `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)` and the following test functions:

- an unramified `(f_n)^{S′} ∈ H^ur(𝒢_n(𝔸^{S′}))`;
- `f_{n,S′_fin∖{p}} ∈ C_c^∞(𝒢_n(𝔸_{S′_fin∖{p}}))`, with base-change transfers `φ^{S′}` and `φ_{S′_fin∖{p}}` to `G`;
- an acceptable `φ_p ∈ C_c^∞(J_b(ℚ_p))`.

Put `φ = φ^{S′}φ_{S′_fin∖{p}}φ_p`. For each elliptic endoscopic group `G_n⃗` of `G`, take `n⃗ = (n)` or `n⃗ = (n₁, n₂)` with `n₁ ≥ n₂ > 0` and `n₁ + n₂ = n`, omitting the pairs excluded by Kottwitz's condition. Let `φ^n⃗` be Shin's endoscopic test function, whose components away from `p` and `∞` are Langlands–Shelstad transfers, chosen at the places of `S′_fin ∖ {p}` as in the local matching statement below, and let `f^n⃗` be a base-change matching function on `𝒢_n⃗ = Res_{𝒦/ℚ}(G_n⃗ ×_ℚ 𝒦)`. Its archimedean component `f^n⃗_∞` is the combination of twisted Lefschetz functions attached to the signature of `G_ℝ`. Prove `tr(φ | ι_ℓ[H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]) = |ker¹(ℚ, G)| τ(G) Σ_n⃗ ε_n⃗ I^{𝒢_n⃗θ}_spec(f^n⃗θ)`, with `ε_n⃗ = 1/2` if `n⃗ = (n/2, n/2)` and `ε_n⃗ = 1` otherwise ([CS17] §5.2, Theorem 5.2.3, Corollary 5.2.5 and Remark 5.2.6, pp. 733–735, CS17 lines 3419–3491; §5.3, Proposition 5.3.1 and Theorem 5.3.2, pp. 737–738, CS17 lines 3551–3603).

Prove also the following local matching statement, used at the places of `S′_fin ∖ {p}`. Let `v ∈ Spl_{F/F⁺}`, split in `𝒦` or not, let `f_v ∈ C_c^∞(𝒢_n(ℚ_v))` have a base-change transfer `φ_v ∈ C_c^∞(G(ℚ_v))`, and let `G_n⃗` be an elliptic endoscopic group of `G`. Then `φ_v` has a Langlands–Shelstad transfer to `G_n⃗(ℚ_v)` that is a base-change transfer of some `f^n⃗_v ∈ C_c^∞(𝒢_n⃗(ℚ_v))` ([CS17] §5.3, p. 737, CS17 lines 3563–3582, which takes `f^n⃗_v = ζ̃*(f_v)` and cites [Shin11] §4.4, (4.19)–(4.20), p. 1683, Shin11 lines 1685–1716; [Shin11] §4.2, Case 2, pp. 1678–1679, Shin11 lines 1474–1527).

A route is the following. Start from Shin's stable trace formula for Igusa varieties of PEL type (A) (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §8.21; [CS17] Theorem 5.2.3), applied to the alternating Igusa cohomology of IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §1.8, identified with EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §7.10. Since `G` is quasi-split at all finite places, the elliptic endoscopic data of `G` are those of `G_n` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §1.8), and `ι(G, G_n⃗) = τ(G)τ(G_n⃗)⁻¹|Out|⁻¹` with `|Out| = 2` exactly for `n₁ = n₂` ([CS17] Remark 5.2.6). The stable–twisted identity `ST^{G_n⃗}_e(φ^n⃗) = τ(G_n⃗) I^{𝒢_n⃗θ}_spec(f^n⃗θ)` holds for every rank and both parities and for `G_ℝ` of arbitrary signature (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.25). That identity uses `[F⁺:ℚ] ≥ 2` (§6.15) and the archimedean matching of §6.10. Matching at the finite places is as follows.

- Away from `S′`, put `φ^n⃗_v = η̃*(φ_v)` and `f^n⃗_v = ζ̃*(f_{n,v})`. They match by the unramified transfers and their compatibility square (§6.8(a)–(b)). §6.8 is stated outside a finite set containing `ℓ`, and `ℓ ∉ S′` when `ℓ ∉ S ∪ Ram(ϖ)`. At `v = ℓ` the same maps, defined through the Satake isomorphism (SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.4.1) with the square of §6.6(c), are transfers by the spherical fundamental lemmas: §4.9(b) for base change when `ℓ` is inert in `𝒦`, §6.9(a) when `ℓ` splits in `𝒦`, and §4.10a, which has no restriction on the residue characteristic, for endoscopic transfer along `η̃`. §6.8(c) gives `ζ̃*` at every place.
- At `p`, take `f^n⃗_p` with `BC*(f^n⃗_p) = φ^n⃗_p`, by surjectivity of `BC*` at the split prime `p` (§6.9(a)).
- At `v ∈ S′_fin ∖ {p} ⊂ Spl_{F/F⁺}`, use the local matching statement, proved as follows. At such `v`, `G(ℚ_v) ≅ ℚ_v^× × ∏_x GL_n(F_x)` and `G_n⃗(ℚ_v) ≅ ℚ_v^× × ∏_x GL_n⃗(F_x)`, the products over one place `x` of `F` above each place of `F⁺` above `v`, the first factor being the similitude ([Shin11] §4.2, (4.6), p. 1678, Shin11 line 1476). `G_n⃗` is a Levi subgroup through the block-diagonal embedding, which preserves the similitude, and transfer matches a regular semisimple class with its image ([Shin11] §3.4, Cases 2–3, pp. 1668–1669, Shin11 lines 1046–1108). In both groups conjugacy and stable conjugacy agree. The norm map from `𝒢_n⃗(ℚ_v) = G_n⃗(𝒦 ⊗ ℚ_v)` multiplies similitudes by their conjugates and is onto in the determinants of the general linear factors. So by the norm criterion (§6.9(d)) a semisimple element of `G(ℚ_v)` or of `G_n⃗(ℚ_v)` is a norm exactly when its similitude lies in `N(𝒦_v^×)`, an open subgroup of `ℚ_v^×` of index at most `2`, equal to `ℚ_v^×` when `v` splits in `𝒦`. Let `Y ⊂ G_n⃗(ℚ_v)` be the open, closed and conjugation-stable set of elements whose similitude lies in `N(𝒦_v^×)`. By §6.9(b) the stable, hence ordinary, orbital integrals of `φ_v` vanish at elements whose similitude is not in `N(𝒦_v^×)`. So for any transfer `φ′` of `φ_v` (§8.20), `φ′·1_Y` has the same regular stable orbital integrals as `φ′`, both vanishing outside `Y`, and is again a transfer of `φ_v`. It is supported on norms, so it is a base-change transfer of some `f^n⃗_v` (§6.9(c)). The stable distribution `ST^{G_n⃗}_e(φ^n⃗)` depends only on the stable orbital integrals of the local transfers (§8.20), so this choice does not change the identity. Only existence of `f^n⃗_v` is used: §9.3 keeps the functions at `S′_fin ∖ {p}` fixed.

`θ` is the automorphism of `𝒢_n⃗` of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.6(d), `θ(λ, g) = (λ^c, λ^c g^♯)` with `g^♯ = Φ_n⃗ ᵗ(g^c)⁻¹ Φ_n⃗⁻¹`.

### 9.2 Newton reduction kills generic principal series at a non-quasi-split factor

Work in the split-prime decomposition of §8.3, and let `π^n⃗_p = ⊗_{i=1}^m π^n⃗_{𝔭_i} ⊗ π^n⃗_0 ∈ Irr(G_n⃗(ℚ_p))`, where `G_n⃗(ℚ_p) = ∏_i GL_n⃗(F_{𝔭_i}) × ℚ_p^×`. Suppose that for some `i` the transfer of `π^n⃗_{𝔭_i}` to `GL_n(F_{𝔭_i})` along `η̃_n⃗` is a generic principal series, and `J_{b_i}` is a non-quasi-split inner form of `M_{b_i}`. The transfer is normalized induction twisted by the `ϖ`-character. Prove that `Red^b_n⃗(π^n⃗_p) = 0` ([CS17] §5.4, Lemma 5.4.3 and proof, p. 741, CS17 line 3714; definition of `Red^b_n⃗`, p. 739). [CS17] prints the conclusion as `Red^b_n⃗(π_p) = 0`; the representation reduced is `π^n⃗_p`.

`Red^b_n⃗` is the composite of the normalized Jacquet functors to the effective endoscopic Levi subgroups, transfer along `η̃_n⃗` to `M_b`, and the Langlands–Jacquet map `Groth(M_b(ℚ_p)) → Groth(J_b(ℚ_p))` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §14.2). All three factor over the `𝔭_i`. A generic principal series has Langlands parameter a sum of characters with no ratio `|·|^{±1}`. Since the `L`-morphism `^LM_{G_n⃗} → ^LM_b` is the restriction of `η̃_{n₁,n₂}`, every constituent of the image in `Groth(M_{b_i}(F_{𝔭_i}))` is an irreducible generic principal series (SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.2.2). The Langlands–Jacquet map of a non-quasi-split inner form kills these (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §10.32), so the `J_{b_i}`-factor and hence the tensor product vanish. The twist `δ̄^{1/2}_{P(ν_b)}` does not affect vanishing.

### 9.3 Spectral expansion of Igusa cohomology for the compact quasi-split datum

Fix `b`, `(f_n)^{S′}`, `f_{n,S′_fin∖{p}}` and their base-change transfers, and let `φ_p ∈ C_c^∞(J_b(ℚ_p))` be arbitrary (not necessarily acceptable). Prove the following ([CS17] §5.5, Lemma 5.5.1 and proof, pp. 741–743, CS17 line 3740; Corollary 5.5.2 and Remark 5.5.3, p. 743, CS17 lines 3804–3829).

1. `tr(φ | ι_ℓ[H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)])` is a finite linear combination of products of three traces: `tr((Π^n⃗)^{S′}((f^n⃗)^{S′}) ∘ A^{S′})`, `tr((Π^n⃗)_{S′_fin∖{p}}(f^n⃗_{S′_fin∖{p}}) ∘ A)` and `tr(Red^b_n⃗(π^n⃗_p)(φ_p))`. Here `Π^n⃗` runs over `θ`-stable irreducible isobaric automorphic representations of `𝒢_n⃗` of the form `n-Ind(Π_M)_ξ` with `Π_M` discrete on a Levi `M`. The component `Π^n⃗_∞` is cohomological for one of the coefficient systems `Ξ(φ_n⃗)` entering `f^n⃗_∞`; it is trivial exactly when `n⃗ = (n)`. Finally `π^n⃗_p ∈ Irr(G_n⃗(ℚ_p))` is the unique representation base-changing to `Π^n⃗_p`.
2. Fix `φ_{S′_fin∖{p}}` and `φ_p` and put `Π^{S′} = ζ̃_{n⃗,*}(Π^n⃗)^{S′}`. Sum the terms of (1) over all `n⃗′` and `Π^{n⃗′}` with `ζ̃_{n⃗′,*}(Π^{n⃗′})^{S′} ≅ Π^{S′}`. If this sum is a nonzero function of `(f_n)^{S′}`, then `Π^{S′}` occurs in `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur})`. Conversely, every irreducible `𝒢_n(𝔸^{S′})`-subrepresentation of `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur})` is of the form `ζ̃_{n⃗,*}(Π^n⃗)^{S′}`.

Finiteness is for fixed test functions, not uniform in the level. For acceptable `φ_p`, combine §9.1 with the spectral side of the simple twisted trace formula (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.22). Each `n-Ind(Π_M)_ξ` is irreducible and isobaric (§6.1 there, and the residual-spectrum classification of AutomorphicSpectralTheory used in §5.1 there). Each is determined by its component outside `S′` (AutomorphicLFunctionsAndLocalFactors AL.3.7.2 «Strong multiplicity one for isobaric sums»). When `n⃗` is proper, `Π^n⃗_∞` is cohomological for `Ξ(φ_n⃗)` and not for the trivial representation (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.10). At `p`, base change at the split prime is injective. So `tr(Π^n⃗_p(f^n⃗_p) ∘ A′_p) = ± tr π^n⃗_p(φ^n⃗_p)`, which §14.3 there rewrites as `tr Red^b_n⃗(π^n⃗_p)(φ_p)`. All `φ_p` are reached by the Frobenius-twist argument (§8.11 there). For (2), rewrite the `(f^n⃗)^{S′}` traces through `ζ̃^*` and `BC^*` (§6.8 there, and at `v = ℓ` §§4.9(b), 4.10a and 6.9(a) there, as in §9.1). Once the test functions at the bad places are fixed, only finitely many characters of the commutative unramified Hecke algebra occur, and they are linearly independent. Only the total coefficient of an eigensystem is detected, so the hypothesis of (2) is on that total.

### 9.4 The r-part of the base-changed Igusa class

Let `r : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)` be continuous and semisimple, unramified outside the places above `S′ ∪ {ℓ}`. Write `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur}) = Σ_j n_j [Π_j^{S′} ⊗ σ_j]`, with `Π_j^{S′}` irreducible unramified representations of `𝒢_n(𝔸^{S′})` and `σ_j` irreducible representations of `G(𝔸_{S′_fin∖{p}}) × J_b(ℚ_p)`. Define the `r`-part `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur})_r` as the sub-sum over the `j` with `r_{Π_j^{S′},ℓ} ≅ r` ([CS17] §5.5, paragraph after Remark 5.5.6, p. 746, CS17 lines 3950–3977). By the converse part of §9.3(2), every `Π_j^{S′}` that occurs is `ζ̃_{n⃗,*}(Π^n⃗)^{S′}`. So `r_{Π_j^{S′},ℓ}` is defined by AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.2 «The Caraiani–Scholze discrete normalization»: `r_{Π^{S′},ℓ} = ⊕_i r_i ⊗ ε_i`, with Hecke polynomials at the places above `q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S′ ∪ {ℓ})` ([CS17] Corollary 5.5.5, CS17 line 3899). It depends only on `Π^{S′}`, by Chebotarev and Brauer–Nesbitt.

The API:

- The class `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur})` is the locally finite sum of its `r`-parts over isomorphism classes of `r`.
- The `r`-part is zero if and only if no constituent `Π^{S′}` has `r_{Π^{S′},ℓ} ≅ r`.
- The `r`-part depends only on the isomorphism class of `r`.
- For a constituent of the `r`-part and `v | q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S′ ∪ {ℓ})`, the eigenvalues of `r(Frob_v)` (geometric Frobenius) are, via `ι_ℓ`, the numbers `q_v^{(n−1)/2}x_j`, where `x_1, …, x_n` are the Satake parameters of the `GL_n(F_v)`-component `Π⁰_v` of `Π^{S′}`. Equivalently they are the Satake parameters of `Π⁰_v|det|^{(1−n)/2}`, and the characteristic polynomial of `r(Frob_v)` is the Satake image `∏_j (X − q_v^{(n−1)/2}x_j)` of `P_v` from §8.4.
- The index `r` is determined by the normalized `GL_n(𝔸_F)`-component. Two constituents that differ only in the `GL_1(𝔸_𝒦)` similitude character have the same index.

*Needs:* this roadmap §9.3; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.2 «The Caraiani–Scholze discrete normalization»; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §§1.8, 5.9; IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §1.8.

**Checks.**

- For the zero class every `r`-part is zero, and for every class the sum of the `r`-parts is the whole class. A definition that selected a single constituent instead of the sub-sum over the index would fail the second test as soon as two constituents share `r`.
- Replacing `BC^{S′}` by base change at the single place `p` (the printed `BC^p`) does not give a sub-sum indexed by Galois representations, since `r_{Π,ℓ}` depends on the component outside `S′`.
- For `n = 1`, `Π = ψ ⊗ Π⁰` has a similitude factor `ψ` on `GL_1(𝔸_𝒦)` and a factor `Π⁰` on `GL_1(𝔸_F)`. Distinct `Π⁰`-components outside `S′` give distinct Galois characters. Changing only `ψ` leaves the index unchanged, so `r`-parts need not separate automorphic characters of `𝒢_1`. A definition indexing by the full character would split this `r`-part in two.
- For `n = 2`, `q_v = 7` and `Π⁰_v` the trivial representation of `GL_2(F_v)`, with Satake parameters `(√7, 1/√7)`, the eigenvalues of `r(Frob_v)` are `√7·√7 = 7` and `√7·(1/√7) = 1`. The characteristic polynomial `(X − 7)(X − 1) = X² − 8X + 7` is the image of `P_v = X² − T_{1,v}X + 7T_{2,v}` under `S(T_1) = √7(√7 + 1/√7) = 8` and `S(T_2) = 1`. Reading the Satake parameters themselves as Frobenius eigenvalues would give `X² − (8/√7)X + 1`. For `n = 1` the scaling factor is `q_v^0 = 1`, so rank one does not detect the twist.

### 9.5 A non-μ-ordinary class has a non-quasi-split factor at a nontrivial 𝔭_i

In the split-prime decomposition of §8.3, assume each `S_i` contains at most one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`. Prove the following. If `b = ((b_i), b_0) ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)` is not `μ`-ordinary, there is an index `i` such that `S_i` contains a `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0` and `J_{b_i}` is not quasi-split. Conversely, for the `μ`-ordinary class every `J_{b_i}` is quasi-split, and `J_{b_i} ≅ Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p}(GL_{p_τ} × GL_{q_τ})` at the nontrivial indices ([CS17] proof of Theorem 5.5.7, pp. 747–748; Lemma 5.5.8, p. 748, CS17 line 4037).

The maximal element of the product `B(G, μ⁻¹) = ∏_i B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is the product of the maximal elements (BunGAndNewtonStrata §§1.25, 1.26, 1.30). Each factor `G_i = Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p} GL_n` is quasi-split, so its maximum is the ordinary class. If `S_i` contains no `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`, `B(G_i, μ_i⁻¹)` is a point. If it contains exactly one, the identification of §8.3(5) preserves `J_b` up to `Res_{F_{𝔭_i}/ℚ_p}` and preserves the `μ`-ordinary class. By BunGAndNewtonStrata §1.32 only the `μ`-ordinary class then has quasi-split `J_b`, and restriction of scalars preserves quasi-splitness and its failure.

### 9.6 Genericity forces the μ-ordinary Newton stratum

Fix `ι_p` compatible with `𝔭 | p` and let `r : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)` be continuous and semisimple, unramified outside the places above `S′ ∪ {ℓ}`. Assume (i) each `S_i` contains at most one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`. Assume (ii) for each `i` such that `S_i` contains such a `τ`, `r|_{Gal(F̄_{𝔭_i}/F_{𝔭_i})} ≅ χ_{i,1} ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ χ_{i,n}` with `χ_{i,a}χ_{i,b}⁻¹` not the cyclotomic character for all `a ≠ b`. Prove that `BC^{S′}([H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]^{S′-ur})_r = 0` for every `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)` that is not `μ`-ordinary ([CS17] §5.5, Theorem 5.5.7 and proof, pp. 747–748, CS17 lines 3978–4036).

Suppose the `r`-part is nonzero for a non-`μ`-ordinary `b`. By §9.3(2) there is a `θ`-stable isobaric `Π^n⃗` with nonzero total contribution and `r_{ζ̃_*(Π^n⃗)^{S′},ℓ} ≅ r`, and `π^n⃗_p` is its base-change preimage. Local–global compatibility at `𝔭_i` ([CS17] Remark 5.5.6; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII gap:unitary-discrete-local-comparison) and hypothesis (ii) make the transfer `Π⁰_{𝔭_i}` of `π^n⃗_{𝔭_i}` to `GL_n(F_{𝔭_i})` a generic principal series at every nontrivial `i`. Only the semisimplification of the comparison is used, as follows. Put `π = Π⁰_{𝔭_i}|det|^{(1−n)/2}`. Through `ι_ℓ`, forgetting the monodromy of `rec(π)` and semisimplifying gives `⊕_a χ_{i,a}`, and this is the semisimple parameter `σ_ss(π)` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §10.30, which identifies the classical correspondence with the trace-defined parameter of §10.26 there, whose semisimple Weil part is `σ_ss`). Let `(ρ_1, …, ρ_k)` be the supercuspidal support of `π`, so that `π` is a constituent of the normalized induction `ρ_1 × ⋯ × ρ_k` (SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.3.1 «Cuspidal support»). Then `σ_ss(π) = ⊕_j σ_ss(ρ_j)` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §10.20, the constituent sum), and `σ_ss` of a supercuspidal of `GL_m(F_{𝔭_i})` is irreducible of dimension `m` (§10.21 there). Since `σ_ss(π)` is a sum of characters, every `ρ_j` is a character `ψ_j` of `F_{𝔭_i}^×`, and after renumbering `ψ_a` corresponds to `χ_{i,a}` under rank-one geometric reciprocity (§10.20 there). That reciprocity matches the cyclotomic character with `|·|`, so by (ii) no two `ψ_a` differ by `|·|^{±1}`. The product `ψ_1 × ⋯ × ψ_n` is therefore irreducible and generic (SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.5.2 «Modular generic segments», in characteristic zero), and `π` equals it. So `Π⁰_{𝔭_i}` is the generic principal series induced from the characters `ψ_a|·|^{(n−1)/2}`. By §9.5 some nontrivial `i` has `J_{b_i}` not quasi-split. By §9.2, `Red^b_n⃗(π^n⃗_p) = 0`. Every term of the expansion attached to `Π^n⃗` then vanishes for every `φ_p`, which is a contradiction.

*Needs:* this roadmap §§8.3, 9.2, 9.3, 9.4, 9.5; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.7 (gap:unitary-discrete-local-comparison); EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §§10.20, 10.21, 10.26, 10.30; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.3.1 «Cuspidal support», SR.5.2 «Modular generic segments».

### Examples

At a split `GL_2`-factor with minuscule signature `(1, 1)`, `B(GL_2/F_{𝔭_i}, μ⁻¹)` has two classes. The ordinary class has slopes `(−1, 0)` and `J_b = GL_1 × GL_1`. The basic class has slope `−1/2` and `J_b = D^×`, which is not quasi-split. Let `π_{𝔭_i}` be an unramified generic principal series, for example with Satake ratio not in `{1, q^{±1}}`. Its Jacquet–Langlands image on `D^×` is zero, so it contributes nothing to the basic Igusa class. The trivial representation of `GL_2` is not generic, and its image is `−1_{D^×} ≠ 0`, since `1 = Ind − St` in the Grothendieck group and `St ↦ 1_{D^×}`. A generic `r` therefore excludes the basic factor. This is a local computation and asserts nothing about global data with arbitrarily prescribed signatures. For `n⃗ = (1, 1)` the weight is `ε = 1/2`, not `1`.

Degenerate cases: with `φ_p = 0` both sides of §9.1 vanish, and so does every term of §9.3(1). In §9.3(2), two terms with the same unramified eigensystem and coefficients `1` and `−1` cancel, and (2) then asserts nothing for that eigensystem. If no `S_i` contains a `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0` (`G_ℝ` definite), `B(G, μ⁻¹)` is a point, and §9.6 is empty.

The local matching statement of §9.1 is tested as follows.

- Every Case 2 datum has places of `S′_fin ∖ {p}` that do not split in `𝒦`: a prime ramified in `𝒦` ramifies in `F`, so it lies in `S ⊂ Spl_{F/F⁺}`. For `𝒦 = ℚ(√−3)` and `v = 3`, `N(𝒦_3^×)` is the set of nonzero `a² + 3b²`; it contains `3 = N(√−3)` and not `−1`, since the Hilbert symbol `(−1, −3)_3 = (−1/3) = −1`. So the elements of `G(ℚ_3)` of similitude in `−N(𝒦_3^×)`, for instance `−1` and `−3`, are not norms, and every base-change transfer `φ_3` has orbital integral `0` there; Shin's explicit transfer vanishes there identically ([Shin11] §4.2, p. 1679, Shin11 lines 1509–1515). Cutting a transfer of `φ_3` off by `1_Y` removes only these elements.
- At `v` split in `𝒦`, `N(𝒦_v^×) = ℚ_v^×`, `Y` is the whole group, the cut-off changes nothing, and §6.9(a) of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison gives the matching directly. A cut-off at an index-two subgroup of `ℚ_v^×` there would discard elements whose orbital integrals need not vanish.
- For `n⃗ = (n)` the transfer of `φ_v` may be taken to be `φ_v` itself, which is a base-change transfer of `f_v` by hypothesis. Its orbital integrals already vanish outside `Y`, so the cut-off does not change them.

## Layer 10: Kottwitz's simple Shimura varieties

Throughout this layer we work in the Case 1 setting. That is, a Case 1 datum of §8.1 (`B` a central division algebra of degree `n` over `F` with an involution of the second kind, `V ≅ B`) and a prime `p ∉ S` split in `𝒦`. Then `B` splits at every place of `F` above `p` and `G(ℚ_p) ≅ ∏_i GL_n(F_{𝔭_i}) × ℚ_p^×`. Also `K_p` is hyperspecial, `G_n` is the quasi-split inner form of `G` over `ℚ` with a fixed isomorphism `G_n ≅ G` over `ℚ_p`, and `ι_ℓ` is fixed.

### 10.1 Only the stable term in the Igusa trace formula

Let `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)` and `φ = φ^pφ_p ∈ C_c^∞(G(𝔸_f^p) × J_b(ℚ_p))` with `φ_p` acceptable. Prove `tr(φ | ι_ℓ[H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]) = |ker¹(ℚ, G)| ι(G, G_n) ST^{G_n}_e(φ^{G_n})`, where `φ^{G_n}` is Shin's test function for the trivial endoscopic datum ([CS17] §5.6, Proposition 5.6.1 and proof, p. 749, CS17 lines 4068–4072).

Start from Shin's stable trace formula (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §8.21), a sum over the elliptic endoscopic data of `G_n` (§1.8 there). The proper terms vanish as follows. For a proper datum `G_{n₁,n₂}`, let `d_v` be the local index of `B` at a place `v` of `F`. Since `B` is a division algebra of degree `n`, `lcm_v d_v = n`, so some `v` has `d_v ∤ n₁`. At that place the local factor of `G` is `GL_{n/d_v}(D_v)`, and that of the endoscopic group is `GL_{n₁} × GL_{n₂}`. A semisimple element of `GL_n(F_v)` coming from `GL_{n/d_v}(D_v)` has reduced characteristic polynomial `∏_i f_i^{m_i}`, with `f_i` irreducible of degree `e_i`, in which every isotypic degree `m_i e_i` is divisible by `d_v`: its `f_i`-isotypic part of `D_v^{n/d_v}` is a module over the central simple algebra `F_v[X]/(f_i) ⊗_{F_v} D_v`, whose index is `d_v / gcd(d_v, e_i)`, so `m_i` is a multiple of that index and `m_i e_i` is a multiple of `lcm(d_v, e_i)`. The irreducible factors themselves need not have degree divisible by `d_v`: a central `γ ∈ F_v^×` has reduced characteristic polynomial `(X − γ)^n`. For the image of a `(G, H)`-regular `γ_H`, the factors `P_1` of degree `n₁` and `P_2` of degree `n₂` of the characteristic polynomial have no common root, so `P_1` is a product of whole isotypic factors `f_i^{m_i}` and `d_v | n₁`. So no such `γ_H` has an image (§1.16 there), the transfer of `φ_v` vanishes on all `(G, H)`-regular classes, and `ST^H_e(φ^H) = 0`. The surviving term is the trivial datum with `ι(G, G_n) = τ(G)τ(G_n)⁻¹`. [CS17] follows [Shin12] §6 (CS17 line 4065), whose standing condition (B2) (author p. 20, Shin12 line 981) takes `p` inert in `F⁺`. That assumption is not used.

### 10.2 The stable Lefschetz identity for the simple Shimura variety

Put `[H(Sh, ℚ̄_ℓ)] = Σ_i (−1)^i colim_K H^i(S_{K,Ē}, ℚ̄_ℓ) ∈ Groth(G(𝔸_f))`. Let `Red^b_n : Groth(G(ℚ_p)) → Groth(J_b(ℚ_p))` be the Newton reduction for the trivial datum: the normalized Jacquet functor, then the Langlands–Jacquet map, with the modulus twist. It is applied to the `p`-component through `G(ℚ_p) ≅ G_n(ℚ_p)`. Prove `Red^b_n([H(Sh, ℚ̄_ℓ)]) = ε_G · d(G_ℝ) · [H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]` in `Groth(G(𝔸_f^p) × J_b(ℚ_p))`, where `ε_G` and `d(G_ℝ)` are nonzero constants depending only on `G`. They are produced by comparing the Euler–Poincaré function of the pair `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` of the Conventions with Shin's archimedean test function for the trivial datum ([CS17] §5.6, p. 749, CS17 lines 4073–4078).

A route is the following. Since `S_K` is proper, `H^*(S_{K,Ē}, ℚ̄_ℓ) ≅ H^*(S_K(ℂ), ℂ)` through `ι_ℓ` and Artin's comparison (ComplexComparisonPartII §§C6.3–C6.4), Hecke-equivariantly. Matsushima's formula for the pair `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` (Conventions; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.5b.4) and the trace formula for compact quotients (AutomorphicSpectralTheory §6.17) give the following for `f ∈ C_c^∞(G(𝔸_f))`; when `e = 1` this is Arthur's `L²`-Lefschetz formula (§§6.19, 6.20 there). We have `tr(f | [H(Sh)]) = Σ_{a∈ker¹(ℚ,G)} Σ_{π on G^{(a)}} m_a(π) tr π_f(f) ep(π_∞) = Σ_a I^{G^{(a)}}(f^{(a)} f_EP^{(a)})`. Here `π` runs over the automorphic representations of `G^{(a)}(𝔸)` with trivial central character on `A_{G,∞}^0`, and `ep` and the Euler–Poincaré function `f_EP = Σ_χ χ·f_max` are those of the reduced pair `(𝔪_G, K_∞)` on `G(ℝ)/A_{G,∞}^0` (Conventions; AutomorphicSpectralTheory §6.19). An elliptic maximal torus of `G_ℝ` preserves the lines of an orthogonal basis of each `V_τ` and scales each line by some `λ` with `|λ|² = ν`, so `ν > 0` and `χ = 1` on elliptic elements. Hence `f_EP` has `e` times the elliptic orbital integrals of `f_max`, and it is stable cuspidal as `f_max` is, so the geometric side has only elliptic terms. All terms use the fixed local identifications with `G`. Stabilize the elliptic terms (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.18). The proper endoscopic terms vanish by the local-index argument of §10.1, leaving `ι(G, G_n) ST^{G_n}_e(f^{G_n})`. Take `f = φ^p f_p` with `f_p` the transport of `φ^{G_n}_p` to `G(ℚ_p)`. Then `tr π_p(f_p) = tr Red^b_n(π_p)(φ_p)` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §14.3). The archimedean test functions differ by the nonzero factor `ε_G d(G_ℝ)` (§6.10 there). Comparing with §10.1 gives the identity for acceptable `φ_p`. It extends to all `φ_p` by the Frobenius-twist argument (§8.11 there; [CS17] prints the reference as Lemma 6.4 of [Shin11], CS17 lines 4077–4078, and the lemma is [Shin09] Lemma 6.4, author p. 20, Shin09 line 879). Nondegeneracy of the trace pairing gives the identity in the Grothendieck group.

### 10.3 Igusa cohomology of simple Shimura varieties in terms of automorphic representations

Prove that in `Groth(G(𝔸_f^p) × J_b(ℚ_p))`, `[ι_ℓH_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)] = (−1)^{q(G)} Σ_{π_f} c(π_f) [π_f^p] [Red^b_n(π_p)]` ([CS17] §5.6, Corollary 5.6.2, p. 749, CS17 lines 4082–4090). The sum runs over the irreducible admissible `π_f` of `G(𝔸_f)` such that `π_f ⊗ π_∞` is automorphic on some `G^{(a)}` for some `π_∞` cohomological for the trivial representation. The coefficient is `c(π_f) = (−1)^{q(G)}(ε_G d(G_ℝ))⁻¹ Σ_{a∈ker¹(ℚ,G)} Σ_{π_∞ on G^{(a)}} m_a(π_f ⊗ π_∞) ep(π_∞)`, with the following ingredients:

- `m_a` is the automorphic multiplicity on the component `G^{(a)}`, with finite parts transported to `G` by the fixed local identifications;
- `ep(π_∞) = Σ_i (−1)^i dim H^i(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞)`, for the reduced pair of the Conventions, `𝔪_G = 𝔤/𝔞_G` and `K_∞` the maximal compact subgroup of the centralizer of `h`, which is the identity component of a maximal compact subgroup `K_max` of `G(ℝ)`, of index `e`;
- `q(G) = ½ dim G(ℝ)/K_∞A_{G,∞}^0`, which equals `d`, since `G(ℝ)/K_∞A_{G,∞}^0` has the real dimension `2d` of the hermitian symmetric domain `X`.

The sum is locally finite: for each compact open subgroup only finitely many terms have invariants.

Apply Matsushima's formula separately to each piece of `S_K`, whose complex points are `G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)` (PELModuli §M3.4; for a wholly definite datum `X` is a point and the formula is the decomposition of the functions on a finite set). It gives `H^*(S_K(ℂ), ℂ) = ⊕_a ⊕_{π on G^{(a)}} m_a(π) π_f^K ⊗ H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞)`, with `π` trivial on `A_{G,∞}^0`. This is ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.5b.4 (with AutomorphicSpectralTheory §4.7) on the quotients built from `X^G` of ALS.0.4 when `e = 1`. When `e = 2` the pieces are built from the connected-compact variant `X̃^G = X`, and the splitting of the Conventions carries ALS.5b.4 to them. So the coefficient of `[π_f]` in `[H(Sh)]` is `Σ_a Σ_{π_∞} m_a(π_fπ_∞) ep(π_∞)`. Apply §10.2 and divide by `ε_G d(G_ℝ)`; `Red^b_n` acts only on the `p`-component. Only `π_∞` with `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞) ≠ 0` contribute.

*Needs:* ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.0.4, ALS.5b.4; AutomorphicSpectralTheory §§4.7, 5.10, 5.12, 5.14, 6.19; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C6.3–C6.4; PELModuli §M3.4; this roadmap §10.2.

**Checks.**

- Case 1 with `n = 1` and `B = F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)`: `G(ℝ) = ℂ^×`, `K_∞ = U(1)`, `A_{G,∞}^0 = ℝ_{>0}`, so `𝔪_G = Lie U(1)` and `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; ℂ)` is `ℂ` in degree `0` and zero above. Hence `ep(1) = 1`, `q(G) = 0 = d`, and the trivial `π_f` has coefficient `(ε_G d(G_ℝ))⁻¹ Σ_a m_a ≠ 0`, recovering the nonzero `H^0` of the finite set `S_K` and of the zero-dimensional Igusa variety. With the full pair `(𝔤, K_∞)` the quotient `𝔤/𝔨 = 𝔞_G` is a line, the cohomology is `ℂ` in degrees `0` and `1`, `ep = 0`, and the right side of §10.3 would vanish.
- A discrete series `π_∞` that is cohomological for the trivial representation has `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞)` concentrated in degree `q(G)`, of dimension `e`. It is one-dimensional when some `p_τ ≠ q_τ`. When `p_τ = q_τ` for every `τ`, the restriction of `π_∞` to `G(ℝ)^0` is a sum of two discrete series: an element of similitude `−1` exchanges the positive and negative halves of each `V_τ`, so it sends a Harish-Chandra parameter, determined by which `p_τ` of the `n` entries of `ρ = ((n−1)/2, …, −(n−1)/2)` sit on the positive half, to the complementary choice, which is never the same. So `ep(π_∞) = e(−1)^{q(G)}`, and the two signs in `c(π_f)` cancel (Examples of this layer). The full pair would tensor this with `Λ^*(𝔞_G^*)` and give `ep = 1 − 1 = 0` for every `π_∞`.
- Negative control for the choice of `K_∞`. Take a Case 1 datum with `n = 2` and `p_τ = q_τ = 1` at every `τ`; for `F = 𝒦`, `G(ℝ) = GU(1, 1)`, `X = ℍ^±` and `S_K` is a compact Shimura curve. Here `e = 2` and `K_∞ = U(1) × U(1)`, so `H^*(𝔪_G, K_∞; ℂ) = H^*(ℙ¹)` and `ep(1) = 2`, which is the coefficient of the trivial `π_f` in the alternating cohomology of each piece `G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∖ (X × G(𝔸_f)/K)` (one copy in `H^0`, one in `H^2`). With `K_max` in place of `K_∞`, the element of similitude `−1` acts by `−1` on `H^2(ℙ¹)`, so `ep(1) = 1`. An automorphic form invariant under `G(𝔸_f)` is constant, because `G(ℚ)` is dense in `G(ℝ)` (real approximation), so `π_∞ = 1` is the only partner of the trivial `π_f`, and the formula would give `1` for each piece. Likewise a weight-two discrete series has two-dimensional `H^1(𝔪_G, K_∞; π_∞)` (holomorphic and antiholomorphic forms), which gives `dim H^1(S_K(ℂ), ℂ) = 2g` for the total genus `g`; `K_max` would give `g`.
- For the Harris–Taylor signature, `dim G(ℝ)/K_∞ = 2(n − 1) + 1`, the extra `1` being `A_{G,∞}^0 = ℝ_{>0}`. Half of it is not an integer, while `q(G) = ½ dim G(ℝ)/K_∞A_{G,∞}^0 = n − 1 = d`, the degree in which a cohomological discrete series contributes.

### 10.4 Galois representations for the division-algebra unitary group

Let `π = π_f ⊗ π_∞` be automorphic on `G(𝔸)` with `π_∞` cohomological for the trivial representation, and let `S_π` be the finite set of primes where `G` or `π` ramifies. Prove the following ([CS17] §5.6, p. 749, CS17 lines 4091–4093; [Shin-BCU] Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2, Shin-BCU lines 41–52, for B = F).

- There is an isobaric automorphic representation `Π = ψ ⊗ Π⁰` of `GL_1(𝔸_𝒦) × GL_n(𝔸_F)`, with `Π⁰ = Π_1 ⊞ ⋯ ⊞ Π_r` and each `Π_j` discrete and conjugate self-dual. It satisfies `Π_v ≅ BC_v(π_v)` at every finite place `v ∉ S_π`, and at every `v ∈ S_π` split in `𝒦` above which `B` splits. Its component `Π⁰_∞` has the infinitesimal character of the base change of the trivial representation.
- There is a continuous semisimple `r_{π,ℓ} : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)`, unramified at every finite place `v` of `F` above `q ∉ S_π ∪ {ℓ}`, including `q` not split in `𝒦`. At every such `v` the characteristic polynomial of `r_{π,ℓ}(Frob_v)` (geometric Frobenius) is that of `ι_ℓ⁻¹ rec(Π⁰_v |det|^{(1−n)/2})(Frob_v)`, that is `∏_j (X − ι_ℓ⁻¹(q_v^{(n−1)/2} x_j))` for the Satake parameters `x_1, …, x_n` of the unramified `Π⁰_v`. At `v` above `q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S_π ∪ {ℓ})`, where §8.4 defines `T_{i,v}` and `P_v`, this polynomial is the image of `P_v` under `ι_ℓ⁻¹` of the eigencharacter of `ℤ[G(ℚ_q)//K_q]` on `π_q^{K_q}`.
- `WD(r_{π,ℓ}|_{Gal(F̄_w/F_w)})^{F-ss} ≅ ι_ℓ⁻¹ rec(Π⁰_w |det|^{(1−n)/2})` at every place `w ∤ ℓ` of `F` above a prime split in `𝒦` at which `B` splits.

The route has two steps. The first is base change, through the point-counting formula for the proper `S_K`, its stabilization against the twisted trace formula of `Res_{𝒦/ℚ} G_𝒦`, and purity to rule out cancellation. For B = F this is [Shin-BCU] Theorem 1.1. For `B` a division algebra, the group `Res_{𝒦/ℚ}(G ×_ℚ 𝒦)` has factors `(B ⊗_F F_w)^×` where `B` ramifies, so the transfer to `GL_n(𝔸_F)` also needs the global Jacquet–Langlands correspondence. The second step is Galois representations of the conjugate self-dual discrete pieces `Π_j` with their algebraic twists (AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.3 «Arbitrary regular conjugate-self-dual existence»). Local compatibility comes from AG2.5 «Caraiani’s full away-prime compatibility». The base change of every locally isomorphic inner form, the assembly at every rank `n` and unramifiedness at all good places are the contract gap:division-algebra-unitary-base-change. The Hecke polynomial matching at `v | q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S_π ∪ {ℓ})` uses the unramified base-change identity (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.8; at `q = p`, which §6.8 excludes, the base change at a place split in `𝒦` of §6.9(a) there) and the Satake normalization of §8.4. EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.23 (signature `(1, n−1)` at one place and `(0, n)` elsewhere, `[F⁺:ℚ]` even) and §6.25 (`[F⁺:ℚ] ≥ 2`, and `G(𝔸_f) ≅ G_n(𝔸_f)`, which for a Case 1 datum holds only when `n = 1`) are special cases, not steps of this route; neither covers a general Case 1 datum, which is why the base change is a gap.

### 10.5 The r-part of the Igusa class of a simple Shimura variety

Let `r : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)` be continuous and semisimple. Define the `r`-part `[H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]_r = (−1)^{q(G)} Σ_{π_f : r_{π,ℓ} ≅ r} c(π_f)[π_f^p][Red^b_n(π_p)]`. It is the sub-sum of the formula of §10.3 over the `π_f` whose Galois representation of §10.4 is isomorphic to `r` ([CS17] §5.6, pp. 749–750, CS17 lines 4093–4099). It is well defined, because `r_{π,ℓ}` depends only on the components of `π_f` at almost all places split in `𝒦` (Chebotarev and Brauer–Nesbitt).

The API:

- The Igusa class is the locally finite sum of its `r`-parts over isomorphism classes of `r`.
- The `r`-part depends only on the isomorphism class of `r`.
- The `r`-part vanishes if and only if the terms `c(π_f)[π_f^p][Red^b_n(π_p)]` with `r_{π,ℓ} ≅ r` sum to zero.
- For each `π_f` in the `r`-part and `v | q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S_π ∪ {ℓ})`, the eigenvalues of `r(Frob_v)` are, via `ι_ℓ`, the numbers `q_v^{(n−1)/2}x_j` for the Satake parameters `x_1, …, x_n` of the `GL_n(F_v)`-factor of `π_q` (§8.4). Equivalently, the characteristic polynomial of `r(Frob_v)` is the image of `P_v` under the eigencharacter of `ℤ[G(ℚ_q)//K_q]` on `π_q^{K_q}`.

*Needs:* this roadmap §§10.3, 10.4; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §§1.8, 5.9.

**Checks.**

- The `r`-part of the zero class is zero. If `Red^b_n(π_p) = 0` for every `π_f` with `r_{π,ℓ} ≅ r`, the `r`-part is zero.
- For `n = 1` the `r`-part is the sum over the automorphic characters `π` of `G(𝔸)` whose `ℓ`-adic character is `r`. An index by the full automorphic character would separate characters that differ only on the similitude factor, which have the same `r`.
- A single `π_f` with `c(π_f) ≠ 0` and `r_{π,ℓ} ≅ r` need not give a nonzero `r`-part. Two `π_f` with equal `π_f^p`, equal `Red^b_n(π_p)` and opposite coefficients cancel. A "pointwise" definition, asserting nonvanishing for each contributing `π_f`, fails this test.
- For `n = 2`, `q_v = 7` and a `π_f` whose `GL_2(F_v)`-factor is trivial, with Satake parameters `(√7, 1/√7)`, the eigenvalues of `r(Frob_v)` are `7` and `1`, matching the image `X² − 8X + 7` of `P_v`. Indexing by the unscaled parameters would ask for Frobenius eigenvalues `√7` and `1/√7`, which no `r` with that Hecke polynomial has.

### 10.6 Genericity forces μ-ordinarity for simple Shimura varieties

With `ι_p` compatible with `𝔭 | p`, let `r : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)` be continuous and semisimple. Assume each `S_i` contains at most one `τ` with `p_τq_τ ≠ 0`. Assume also that for each `i` such that `S_i` contains such a `τ`, `r|_{Gal(F̄_{𝔭_i}/F_{𝔭_i})}` is a sum of characters `χ_{i,1} ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ χ_{i,n}` with `χ_{i,a}χ_{i,b}⁻¹` not the cyclotomic character for `a ≠ b`. Prove that `[H_c(I^b_Mant, ℚ̄_ℓ)]_r = 0` for every non-`μ`-ordinary `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)` ([CS17] §5.6, Corollary 5.6.3, p. 750, CS17 lines 4102–4111).

For `π_f` in the `r`-part, local–global compatibility at `𝔭_i` (§10.4; `B` splits above `p`) and the hypothesis make `π_{𝔭_i}` a generic principal series at every nontrivial `i`, by the argument of §9.6 applied to the component `Π⁰_{𝔭_i} ≅ π_{𝔭_i}` of the base change of §10.4 (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §§10.20, 10.21, 10.26, 10.30; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.3.1 «Cuspidal support», SR.5.2 «Modular generic segments»). For non-`μ`-ordinary `b`, §9.5 gives a nontrivial `i` with `J_{b_i}` not quasi-split. §9.2 for the trivial datum `n⃗ = (n)` then gives `Red^b_n(π_p) = 0`, so every term of the `r`-part vanishes.

*Needs:* this roadmap §§8.3, 9.2, 9.5, 10.4, 10.5; EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §§10.20, 10.21, 10.26, 10.30; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.3.1 «Cuspidal support», SR.5.2 «Modular generic segments».

### Examples

For `n = 1`, `B = F`. There is no proper endoscopy, the identity of §10.1 is the trivial-datum case of Shin's formula, and §10.4 is class field theory for the torus `G`. In the Harris–Taylor case with `b` basic, §10.2 expresses the Igusa class of the basic stratum through the Jacquet–Langlands transfer of the global cohomology. For the `μ`-ordinary `b`, `Red^b_n` is the normalized Jacquet functor to `M_b = J_b` followed by the modulus twist `δ̄^{1/2}_{P(ν_b)}`. The twist is kept in the ordinary case too.

Suppose `ker¹(ℚ, G)` has two elements and the transported multiplicities are `m₁` and `m₂`. Then the coefficient in §10.3 uses `m₁ + m₂`. Keeping only one component fails, unless all component multiplicities coincide, in which case the coefficient is `|ker¹|` times the single-component one. When every contributing `π_∞` is a discrete series, `ep(π_∞) = e(−1)^{q(G)}` with `e = [K_max : K_∞]` of the Conventions, and `c(π_f) = e·(ε_G d(G_ℝ))⁻¹ Σ_a Σ_{π_∞} m_a(π_f ⊗ π_∞)`, a nonzero constant times the summed automorphic multiplicity. Its sign is that of `ε_G d(G_ℝ)`, which is not fixed here: [CS17] records only that the two constants are nonzero (CS17 line 4077).

With `φ_p = 0` both sides of §10.1 vanish. One-dimensional automorphic representations (`π_∞` trivial on `G(ℝ)^0`) contribute to §10.3 with `ep(π_∞) = ∏_{τ∈Φ} binom(n, p_τ)`, the Euler characteristic of the compact dual `∏_{τ∈Φ} Gr(p_τ, n)` of `X`: `n` for the Harris–Taylor signature, `1` for `n = 1` and for wholly definite data, and `2` for the Shimura curve of the Checks of §10.3. In the Harris–Taylor setting of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §6.23 (with `[F⁺:ℚ]` even), the transfer to `GL_n(𝔸_F)` in the first assertion of §10.4 is the classical result recorded there, through Clozel's base change and the global Jacquet–Langlands correspondence. For definite `G_ℝ`, `B(G, μ⁻¹)` is a point, and §10.6 is empty.

## Layer 11: Torsion concentration for compact unitary Shimura varieties

Throughout this layer we fix a compact unitary datum (Case 1 or 2) of §8.1, with `S`, `K = K_SK^S`, a prime `ℓ` and the Hecke algebra `𝕋^S`. Where an auxiliary prime is used, it is `p ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})` with `K = K_pK^p` and `K_p` hyperspecial. §§11.2–11.3 and the minimal-stratum concentration in §11.4(1) rest on suppliers stated for Shimura data of Hodge type, and are applied only to data satisfying SV3 (§8.1); for wholly definite data §8.9 replaces them. These suppliers also take `K^p` small enough for the perfectoid tower (PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.2), which a neat level need not be; §11.2(c) reduces every neat level to such a level, and §§11.5–11.6 begin with that reduction.

### 11.1 Contraction of a maximal ideal to the Hecke operators away from p

Let `S′ ⊇ S` be finite with `ℓ ∉ S′ ∖ S`, let `𝕋^{S′} ⊂ 𝕋^S` be the subalgebra generated by the spherical algebras at the primes of `Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S′ ∪ {ℓ})`, and let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` be maximal with finite residue field. Prove the following ([CS17] §6.3, proof of Theorem 6.3.1, p. 759). The contraction `𝔪′ = 𝔪 ∩ 𝕋^{S′}` is maximal. For every `𝕋^S`-module `M`, `M_𝔪` is a localization of `M_{𝔪′}`; hence `M_{𝔪′} = 0` implies `M_𝔪 = 0`. If `M` is finite over a finite ring, `M_{𝔪′} = ⊕_{𝔐 ∩ 𝕋^{S′} = 𝔪′} M_𝔐`.

The image of `𝕋^{S′}` in the finite field `𝕋^S/𝔪` is a finite domain, hence a field, so `𝔪′` is maximal. Elements of `𝕋^{S′} ∖ 𝔪′` lie outside `𝔪`, so localizing at `𝔪` factors through localizing at `𝔪′`. A finite module over a finite ring is a module over an Artinian quotient of `𝕋^S` and splits as the sum of its localizations at maximal ideals.

### 11.2 Hochschild–Serre between finite level and the perfectoid Shimura variety

Assume that the datum satisfies SV3 (§8.1). Let `𝒮_{K^p} ∼ lim_{K′_p} 𝒮_{K^pK′_p}` be the perfectoid Shimura variety over `C` at infinite level at `p`, and `H^b(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ) = colim_{K′_p} H^b(S_{K^pK′_p}, 𝔽_ℓ)`. Let `K^p` be small enough for the perfectoid tower. Prove that there is a convergent `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-equivariant spectral sequence `E_2^{a,b} = H^a_cts(K_p, H^b(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)) ⇒ H^{a+b}(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)`. Consequently, for a maximal ideal `𝔪` of `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`:

- (a) if `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`, then `H^j(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0` for some `j ≤ i`;
- (b) if `H^b(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `b < d`, then `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `i < d`

([CS17] §6.3, proof of Theorem 6.3.1, pp. 758–759, CS17 lines 4436–4440, with footnote 34).

Prove also the following, for every datum, with or without SV3; it uses no tower.

- (c) Reduction to small level. Let `K = K_SK^S` be neat and `p ∉ S`. There is an open normal subgroup `K_N ⊂ K` with `(K_N)_q = K_q` for every `q ∉ S` such that `K_N^p` lies in the principal congruence subgroup of level `N`, for some `N ≥ 3` prime to `p`, of a Siegel embedding given by a self-dual lattice `Λ`, as PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.2 asks. The lattice lies in `V ⊕ V`: a hyperspecial `K_q` need not stabilize a self-dual lattice of `V` (Examples). For every maximal ideal `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S`, if `H^*(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0` then `H^*(S_{K_N}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`, and if `H^b(S_{K_N}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `b < d` then `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `i < d`. ([CS17] takes `K` sufficiently small from the start, §6.3, p. 757, CS17 line 4377.)

The perfectoid representative of the tower is PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.2: under SV3 each `(G^{(a)}, X)` is a Shimura datum of Hodge type (PELModuli §M0.12), and the tower of `S_K` is the disjoint union of their towers. Continuity of étale cohomology along the tower is DiamondEtaleCohomology C0.18. The comparison of algebraic and analytic étale cohomology of the proper `S_{K^pK′_p}` is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.2. The map `𝒮_{K^p} → 𝒮_K` is a pro-finite-étale `K_p`-torsor, and the Cartan–Leray spectral sequence with continuous-cohomology `E_2` is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3. Equivalently, it is the colimit of the finite Hochschild–Serre sequences of SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15. Hecke operators at `q ∉ S ∪ {p, ℓ}` commute with `K_p`, and localization is exact. For (a), let `j₀` be the least degree with `H^{j₀}(𝒮_{K^p})_𝔪 ≠ 0`. Then `E_2^{a,b}` localized at `𝔪` vanishes for `b < j₀`, so `H^i(S_K)_𝔪 = 0` for `i < j₀`. [CS17] asserts `H^i(𝒮_{K^p})_𝔪 ≠ 0` for the least `i` with `H^i(S_K)_𝔪 ≠ 0`. That needs `H^a_cts(K_p, −)` to vanish in positive degrees, which holds for pro-`p` `K_p` but not for hyperspecial `K_p`. Only (a) is used. Part (b) is the same vanishing argument.

For (c): `S` is not empty, since a prime ramified in `𝒦` (one exists, `𝒦` being imaginary quadratic) ramifies in `F` and so lies in `S`; hence there is an integer `N ≥ 3` divisible exactly by the primes of `S`, and it is prime to `p`. Embed `G` into `GSp(V ⊕ V, ⟨·,·⟩ ⊕ ⟨·,·⟩)` by `g ↦ (g, g)`. It sends `h` to `h ⊕ h`, and `⟨·, (h ⊕ h)(i)·⟩` is positive definite, so this is a Siegel embedding. Write `V ⊕ V = V ⊗_F F²`. On `x ⊗ α` and `y ⊗ β` the form is `⟨x, H(α, β)y⟩` with `H(α, β) = ᾱ₁β₁ + ᾱ₂β₂`, and `G` acts on the first factor. Let `L ⊂ V` be a lattice whose closure in `V ⊗ 𝔸_f` is `K`-stable, for instance the sum of the translates of any lattice by representatives of the finitely many cosets of its stabilizer in `K`. Then `L ⊗ ℤ_q` is self-dual at almost every `q`. At `q ∉ S` the algebra `F ⊗ ℚ_q` is a product of fields unramified over the completions of `F⁺` below them and of split factors, so `−1 = t̄t` for some `t ∈ F ⊗ ℚ_q`. Then `e = (1, t)` and `f = (1, −t)/2` satisfy `H(e, e) = H(f, f) = 0` and `H(e, f) = 1`. For a `K_q`-stable lattice `M ⊂ V ⊗ ℚ_q`, its dual `M^∨` is `K_q`-stable, because the similitudes of the compact group `K_q` are units. The lattice `M ⊗ e + M^∨ ⊗ f` is `K_q`-stable and self-dual: both summands are isotropic, and the pairing between them is that of `M` with `M^∨`. Take `Λ ⊗ ℤ_q` to be `(L ⊕ L) ⊗ ℤ_q` at the `q ∉ S` where `L ⊗ ℤ_q` is self-dual, `(L ⊗ ℤ_q) ⊗ e + (L ⊗ ℤ_q)^∨ ⊗ f` at the finitely many other `q ∉ S`, and a self-dual lattice of the symplectic space `(V ⊕ V) ⊗ ℚ_q` at `q ∈ S`. These are the completions of a self-dual `ℤ`-lattice `Λ ⊂ V ⊕ V`, which gives a Siegel embedding `G ⊂ GSp(Λ ⊗ ℚ) ≅ GSp_{4g}`, with `2g = dim_ℚ V`, such that `K_q ⊂ GSp(Λ ⊗ ℤ_q)` for `q ∉ S`. Only the compactness of `K_q` is used here, not that it is hyperspecial. Let `Γ(N)` be the principal congruence subgroup of level `N` of `GSp(Λ ⊗ Ẑ)`; its components at `q ∉ S` are `GSp(Λ ⊗ ℤ_q) ⊇ K_q`. Let `K_N` be the intersection of the `K`-conjugates of `K ∩ Γ(N)`. There are finitely many, since `K ∩ Γ(N)` is open in the compact `K`, so `K_N` is open and normal, `(K_N)_q = K_q` for `q ∉ S`, and `K_N ⊂ Γ(N)`. Since `K` is neat and `K_N` is normal in `K`, `S_{K_N} → S_K` is a finite étale Galois covering with group `K/K_N` (PELModuli §M6.1). Its Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence `E_2^{a,b} = H^a(K/K_N, H^b(S_{K_N,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)) ⇒ H^{a+b}(S_{K,Ē}, 𝔽_ℓ)` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15) is `𝕋^S`-equivariant, because `(K_N)_q = K_q` at the primes of the Hecke operators, which therefore commute with `K/K_N`. Localization at `𝔪` is exact and commutes with the cohomology of the finite group `K/K_N`, so the localized `E_2` page vanishes in the rows where `H^b(S_{K_N}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` does, which gives both assertions. The integer `N` may be replaced by any multiple supported on `S`, so any further smallness of the same kind can be met.

### 11.3 Reduction of perfectoid-level cohomology to Igusa cohomology through π_HT

Assume that the datum satisfies SV3 (§8.1), and let `K^p` be small enough for the perfectoid tower, as in §11.2. Let `π_HT : 𝒮_{K^p} → Fℓ_{G,μ}` be the Hodge–Tate period map and `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^{S∪{p}}` maximal. Prove the following ([CS17] §6.3, proof of Theorem 6.3.1, pp. 758–760; Theorem 4.4.4, p. 729, CS17 line 3236; proof of Corollary 6.1.4, p. 754). `Rπ_HT*𝔽_ℓ` is a complex of sheaves of `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-modules. For every geometric point `x̄` of `Fℓ^b_{G,μ}`, `((Rπ_HT*𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪)_x̄ ≅ RΓ(Ig^b, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪`. Consequently:

- (a) if `RΓ(Ig^b, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for every `b ∈ B(G, μ⁻¹)`, then `RΓ(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0`;
- (b) if `H^i(Ig^b, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `i < d` and all `b`, then `H^i(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for all `i < d`.

The period map is PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.5. Hecke operators away from `p` act trivially on `Fℓ_{G,μ}`, so the pushforward is a complex of sheaves of `𝕋^{S∪{p}}`-modules. Localization commutes with stalks and with cohomology on the qcqs locally noetherian flag variety (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4), with the pushforward bounded below and of finite torsion cohomological dimension. The stalks are given by the compact fibre theorem (IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §3.17), Hecke-equivariantly, for the proper integral model of §8.2. Stalks at higher-rank points are controlled by rank-one points (its §3.14), and the strata `Fℓ^b` cover `Fℓ_{G,μ}`. By the Leray spectral sequence on the étale sites (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.2 on DiamondEtaleCohomology C0.1), `RΓ(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = RΓ(Fℓ_{G,μ}, (Rπ_HT*𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪)`. If all stalks vanish, the complex is zero. If all stalks lie in degrees `≥ d`, so do the complex and its hypercohomology.

### 11.5 Galois representations for torsion Hecke eigensystems

Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` be maximal with `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0` for some `i`. Prove that there is a unique continuous semisimple `ρ_𝔪 : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)`, unramified outside the places above `S ∪ {ℓ}`, with the following property. For every finite place `v` of `F` above `q ∈ Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S ∪ {ℓ})`, the characteristic polynomial of `ρ_𝔪(Frob_v)` is the image of `P_v` under a fixed embedding `𝕋^S/𝔪 ↪ 𝔽̄_ℓ` ([CS17] §6.3, Theorem 6.3.1(1), p. 757, CS17 lines 4387–4408, and proof, pp. 758–759; Theorem 1.1, first assertion, and Remark 1.2, p. 651, CS17 lines 89–100). In Case 2, the elimination of `Ram(ϖ)` from the bad set depends on gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance.

2. Under SV3, §11.2(a) gives `RΓ(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0`, and by §11.3(a) some `RΓ(Ig^b, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′}` is nonzero; take `b` with `d_b` minimal. For wholly definite data, `H^0(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0` (degree `0` is the only nonzero degree by §8.9(2), and §11.1 passes to `𝔪′`), and §8.9(4) gives `H^0(Ig^{b₀}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0` directly; take `b = b₀`.
3. In Case 2, choose `ϖ` as in the Conventions (gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance with `D = ∅`) and put `S′ = S ∪ {p, ∞} ∪ Ram(ϖ)`. §11.4 gives a constituent `Π^{S′}` lifting `(𝔪′)^∨ ∩ 𝕋^{S′_fin}`. AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.2 «The Caraiani–Scholze discrete normalization» (through §9.4) and AG2.7 «Residual representation of `π`» then give a residual representation unramified outside `S′_fin ∪ {ℓ}`, with the required polynomials at `v` above `Spl_{𝒦/ℚ} ∖ (S′_fin ∪ {ℓ})`. Dualize and twist by §8.5. In Case 1, use §10.4 with bad set `S ∪ {p}`.
4. Uniqueness: two semisimple representations with the same Frobenius characteristic polynomials on a density-one set of places are isomorphic (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §§1.8, 5.9; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §1.2).
5. To eliminate every extra bad prime, fix a rational `q ∉ S ∪ {ℓ}` and choose `p ≠ q`. In Case 2, also choose `ϖ` unramified above `q` (gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance). This gives a construction unramified above `q`, with the required polynomial there when `q` splits in `𝒦`. All constructions agree by uniqueness on a common density-one set. Varying `q` gives unramifiedness outside `S ∪ {ℓ}` and all the stated polynomials. Choosing two auxiliary primes eliminates those primes but not `Ram(ϖ)`.

### 11.6 Middle-degree concentration of the generic part

Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` be maximal with `H^*(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`, and let `ρ_𝔪` be as in §11.5. Suppose there is a CS-auxiliary pair `(p, 𝔭)` for `ρ_𝔪` (§8.7). Prove that `H^j(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0` only for `j = d = dim S_K` ([CS17] §6.3, Theorem 6.3.1(2), p. 757, CS17 lines 4409–4416, and proof, pp. 759–760; §6.2, Lemma 6.2.2, p. 756, CS17 line 4339).

For wholly definite data `d = 0` and `H^j(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ) = 0` for `j > 0` (§8.9(2)), so the conclusion holds for every `𝔪`, with or without an auxiliary pair. The route below is for data satisfying SV3. In Case 2 it passes through §9.6, which depends on gap:unitary-discrete-local-comparison and, with all of Layer 9, on gap:auxiliary-character-finite-avoidance.

The route runs as follows.

1. Fix a place `u` of `𝒦` through which `(p, 𝔭)` is auxiliary for `ρ_𝔪`, and use the decomposition of §8.3 for this `u`. By §8.6(1), and since `(p, 𝔭)` is then auxiliary for `ρ_{𝔪^∨}` through the same `u` (§8.5(4)), it suffices to prove `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for `i < d` for every such `𝔪`. By §11.2(c) it suffices to prove this at the level `K_N`, where there is nothing to prove unless `H^*(S_{K_N}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`; then `ρ_𝔪` is the same representation and the auxiliary pair, a condition on `ρ_𝔪`, `p ∉ S` and the datum, is unchanged. Replace `K` by `K_N` and contract to `𝔪′ ⊂ 𝕋^{S∪{p}}`. By §11.2(b) it suffices to show `H^i(𝒮_{K^p}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} = 0` for `i < d`.
2. Let `b` have `d_b` minimal with `RΓ(Ig^b)_{𝔪′} ≠ 0`. §11.4 gives a Galois representation `r` with nonzero `r`-part of the Igusa class of `b`. By §11.5 and Chebotarev with Brauer–Nesbitt, the semisimplified reduction of `r` is `ρ_{𝔪^∨} = ρ_𝔪^∨ ⊗ ε̄^{1−n}`, globally and hence at every `𝔭_i`.
3. At each nontrivial `𝔭_i`, `ρ_{𝔪^∨}` is decomposed generic. By the splitting of generic lifts over `F_{𝔭_i}` (IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §5.5; [CS17] Lemma 6.2.2), `r|_{Gal(F̄_{𝔭_i}/F_{𝔭_i})}` is a sum of characters with non-cyclotomic ratios. §9.6 (Case 2) or §10.6 (Case 1) forces `b` to be `μ`-ordinary, so `d_b = d`.
4. Since `d_{b′} ≤ d` with equality only for the `μ`-ordinary class, minimality forces every `b′` with `RΓ(Ig^{b′})_{𝔪′} ≠ 0` to be `μ`-ordinary. Its cohomology is concentrated in degree `d` by §11.4(1). So `H^i(Ig^{b′}, 𝔽_ℓ)_{𝔪′} = 0` for `i < d` and all `b′`, and §11.3(b) concludes.

### 11.7 Caraiani–Scholze's theorem for compact unitary Shimura varieties

Let a compact unitary datum (Case 1 or 2) be given, with `K = K_SK^S` neat and `K^S` hyperspecial; in Case 2, `S ⊂ Spl_{F/F⁺}`. Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` be maximal with `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`. Prove the following ([CS17] §1, Theorem 1.1 with footnote 2, p. 651, CS17 lines 84–95 and 113–115; Remarks 1.2–1.5, pp. 651–652, CS17 lines 96–112; §6.3, Remarks 6.3.2–6.3.3, p. 758).

1. There is an associated `ρ_𝔪 : Gal(F̄/F) → GL_n(𝔽̄_ℓ)` as in §11.5.
2. If some rational prime `p ≠ ℓ` splits completely in `F` and `ρ_𝔪` is unramified and decomposed generic at every place of `F` above `p`, then `i = d`.
3. If `G_ℝ` is definite at all infinite places of `F⁺` but one, and `ρ_𝔪` is unramified and decomposed generic at some finite place of `F`, then `i = d`.

In both (2) and (3), `H^*(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` is concentrated in degree `d`. [CS17] states Theorem 1.1 for the locally symmetric spaces `X_Γ = Γ\X`, with Theorem 6.3.1 as its precise form for `S_K(ℂ) = ⊔_j Γ_j\X`. The statement here is that precise form, and nothing is asserted about the localized cohomology of a single component.

Part (1) is §11.5. For (2), §8.8(a) provides such a `p` outside `S ∪ {ℓ}`, and every `(p, 𝔭)` is a CS-auxiliary pair; apply §11.6. For (3), §8.8(b) produces a CS-auxiliary pair; apply §11.6. The comparison with the locally symmetric spaces of the introduction is `S_K(ℂ) = ⊔_j Γ_j\X` with `Γ_j = G^{(a)}(ℚ) ∩ g_jKg_j⁻¹` (PELModuli §M3.4).

*Needs:* this roadmap §§8.1, 8.7, 8.8, 11.5, 11.6; PELModuli §M3.4.

### 11.8 Integral coefficients and twisted coefficient systems

Localizations with integral coefficients are those of the Conventions, at maximal ideals containing `ℓ`. Prove the following ([CS17] Remark 1.3, p. 651, CS17 line 101; Remark 1.7.1, p. 652, CS17 line 131).

1. Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^S` be a maximal ideal containing `ℓ` with `H^i(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for `i ≠ d`. Then `H^i(S_K, ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪 = 0` for `i ≠ d`, and `H^d(S_K, ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪` is a free `ℤ_ℓ`-module.
2. Let `S′ ⊇ S` be finite and `K′ ⊂ K` a normal compact open subgroup with `K′_q = K_q` for `q ∉ S′`. Assume the datum and `K′` satisfy all hypotheses of §11.6 with `S′` in place of `S`; in Case 2 this includes `S′ ⊂ Spl_{F/F⁺}`. Let `V` be a finite `𝔽_ℓ[K/K′]`-module and `L_V` the associated local system on `S_K`. Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^{S′}` be maximal. Suppose that, if `H^*(S_{K′}, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 ≠ 0`, the representation `ρ_𝔪` attached at level `K′` (§11.5 with `S′` in place of `S`) admits a CS-auxiliary pair. Then `H^i(S_K, L_V)_𝔪 ≠ 0` only for `i = d`.
3. Let `ξ` be an irreducible algebraic representation of `G` over a finite extension `E_λ/ℚ_ℓ` with a `K`-stable `O_λ`-lattice. Let `𝔪 ⊂ 𝕋^{S′}` be a maximal ideal containing `ℓ`, and suppose the hypotheses of (2) hold for it at a normal compact open `K′ ⊂ K` on which `L_ξ ⊗ 𝔽_ℓ` becomes constant. Then `H^i(S_K, L_ξ)_𝔪 = 0` for `i ≠ d` and `H^d(S_K, L_ξ)_𝔪` is free over `O_λ`.

In (2) and (3), only Hecke operators at primes where `K′ = K` are used. The existence of a trivializing level satisfying the hypotheses is part of the hypothesis of (3).

For (1), `RΓ(S_K, ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪` is a direct summand of the perfect complex `RΓ(S_{K,Ē}, ℤ_ℓ)`, so its cohomology groups are finite `ℤ_ℓ`-modules, and `RΓ(S_K, ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪 ⊗^L 𝔽_ℓ = RΓ(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪` is concentrated in degree `d` (Conventions; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.4.9). The universal coefficient sequences `0 → H^i(ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪/ℓ → H^i(𝔽_ℓ)_𝔪 → H^{i+1}(ℤ_ℓ)_𝔪[ℓ] → 0` and Nakayama give vanishing for `i ≠ d` and torsion-freeness in degree `d`. With the ordinary localization of the `𝕋^S`-modules `H^i(S_K, ℤ_ℓ)`, or at a maximal ideal not containing `ℓ`, the finiteness that Nakayama needs fails (§8.9, Checks). For (2), the Galois cover `S_{K′} → S_K` with group `K/K′` trivializes `L_V`. Its Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence `E_2^{a,b} = H^a(K/K′, H^b(S_{K′}, 𝔽_ℓ) ⊗ V) ⇒ H^{a+b}(S_K, L_V)` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15) is `𝕋^{S′}`-equivariant. Localized at `𝔪`, the `E_2` page vanishes outside the row `b = d` by §11.6 at level `K′`, so `H^i(S_K, L_V)_𝔪 = 0` for `i < d`. For the upper bound, Poincaré duality with locally constant coefficients (EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves §0.5; §8.6(1)) identifies `H^i(S_K, L_V)_𝔪` with the dual of `H^{2d−i}(S_K, L_{V^∨}(d))_{𝔪^∨}`. That group vanishes for `2d − i < d`, by the lower bound for `V^∨` and `𝔪^∨`; §8.5(4) gives the auxiliary pair for `𝔪^∨` at the same level, through the same place `u`. For (3), apply (1)–(2) to `L_ξ/λ` and pass to `O_λ` by the argument of (1). For wholly definite data (1)–(3) also follow directly from §8.9(2).

*Needs:* this roadmap §§8.5, 8.6, 8.9, 11.1, 11.5, 11.6; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15; EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves §0.5; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§2.1–2.2; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.4.9; Mathlib `Module.Free`.

### Examples

For `n = 1`, `d = 0`, every `b` is `μ`-ordinary, all statements are about functions on a finite set, and `ρ_𝔪` is the reduction of the Galois character attached by class field theory to the Hecke character of the torus `G`. For wholly definite data of rank `n ≥ 2` only the degree-zero statements carry over, through §8.9: `d = 0`, `B(G, μ⁻¹)` is a point and the cohomology is `H^0` of a finite set. There `G` is not a torus (`G^ad` has dimension `(n² − 1)[F⁺:ℚ]`, for instance `3` for the quaternion datum of §8.1), and `ρ_𝔪` has rank `n` and comes from §11.5, not from class field theory. Concentration fails without localization: all even-degree groups `H^{2i}(S_K, 𝔽_ℓ)` are nonzero ([CS17] Remark 1.7, CS17 lines 124–130), so `H^0` and `H^d` are both nonzero when `d > 0`. Without localization, `H^0(S_K, L_V) = (H^0(S_{K′}, 𝔽_ℓ) ⊗ V)^{K/K′}`, and the action of `K/K′` on components cannot be ignored. For `V = 𝔽_ℓ` and `K′ = K`, §11.8(2) is §11.6 itself.

Suppose `q` lies in `Ram(ϖ)` but not in `S ∪ {p, ℓ}`. Then one construction in §11.5 gives no unramifiedness at `q`. A second construction must avoid `q` in both its auxiliary prime and its character ramification before uniqueness eliminates `q`.

Further boundary cases for the layer:

- For `S′ = S`, §11.1 is trivial; it is used with `S′ = S ∪ {p}`.
- Take a datum of rank `n ≥ 2`, a prime `q₁ ∈ S` split in `𝒦` at which `F` and `G` are unramified (`S` may contain good primes), and a neat `K` whose component at `q₁` is the pro-`q₁` Iwahori subgroup of `G(ℚ_{q₁}) ≅ ∏_i GL_n(F_{𝔭_i}) × ℚ_{q₁}^×` (as in §8.3(1)) and whose other components are maximal compact. For no `N ≥ 3` and no self-dual lattice `Λ` of `V` or of `V ⊕ V` is `K` contained in the principal congruence subgroup `Γ(N)` of `GSp(Λ ⊗ Ẑ)`. Suppose first `q₁ | N`. In the factor `GL_n(F_{𝔭_1})`, `K_{q₁}` contains `u_j = 1 + E_{j,j+1}` for `j < n` and `u_n = 1 + q₁E_{n,1}`, all of similitude `1`. The product `(u_1 − 1)⋯(u_n − 1)` is `q₁E_{11}` on the `𝔭_1`-part of `V ⊗ ℚ_{q₁}` (on each copy of the standard representation in Case 1). On the `𝔭_1^c`-part, where the `u_j` act through the contragredient, it is `q₁E_{22}` if `n = 2` and `0` if `n ≥ 3`; on the other parts it is `0`. So it acts as `q₁e` for a nonzero idempotent `e`, on `V ⊕ V` as well. If every `u_j − 1` mapped `Λ ⊗ ℤ_{q₁}` into `q₁Λ ⊗ ℤ_{q₁}`, then `eΛ ⊂ q₁^{n−1}Λ ⊂ q₁Λ`, hence `eΛ = e(eΛ) ⊂ q₁eΛ`, and `eΛ = 0` by Nakayama, although `eΛ` spans the nonzero space `e(V ⊗ ℚ_{q₁})`. Suppose next `q₁ ∤ N`. Some prime `q ≠ q₁` has `q^a` exactly dividing `N` with `q^a ≥ 3`, and the maximal compact `K_q` contains the central scalars `ℤ_q^×`, among them `−1 ≢ 1` modulo `q^a`. So PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.2 does not apply at this `K`, and §§11.5–11.7 reach it only through §11.2(c). A route applying §§11.2–11.4 directly at every neat level would use that supplier outside its hypothesis.
- For `n = 1` the previous example fails, and the restriction `n ≥ 2` is needed. Then `G` is a torus and its pro-`q₁` Iwahori subgroup is its principal congruence subgroup of level `q₁`. For `B = F = 𝒦 = ℚ(i)`, `S = {2, 5}`, `q₁ = 5`, `Λ = ℤ[i]` with `⟨x, y⟩ = Tr(xȳ·i/2)` (self-dual: `⟨1, i⟩ = 1` and `⟨1, 1⟩ = ⟨i, i⟩ = 0`) and the other components maximal compact, `K` is neat (the only unit of `ℤ[i]` congruent to `1` modulo both primes above `5` is `1`) and `K ⊂ Γ(5)`, with `N = 5 ≥ 3`. In the argument above `n − 1 = 0`, and the inclusion `eΛ ⊂ q₁^{n−1}Λ` gives nothing.
- At a prime `q ∉ S` with a place of `F⁺` inert in `F`, a hyperspecial `K_q` need not stabilize a self-dual lattice of `V ⊗ ℚ_q`; this is why §11.2(c) works in `V ⊕ V`. Take the hermitian plane over the unramified quadratic extension `F_w` of `F⁺_u = ℚ_q` with isotropic basis `e₀, f₀` pairing to `1`, so that `M₀ = O_{F_w}e₀ ⊕ O_{F_w}f₀` is self-dual, for instance at `q = 3` for the quaternion datum of §8.1 after Morita. The element `g` with `ge₀ = qe₀`, `gf₀ = f₀` has similitude `q`, and `gK₀g⁻¹` is hyperspecial, the stabilizer of `gM₀ = O_{F_w}qe₀ ⊕ O_{F_w}f₀`. Its stable lattices are the `q^k gM₀`, since `K₀` acts irreducibly on `M₀/qM₀`, and `(q^k gM₀)^∨ = q^{−1−k}gM₀`, so none is self-dual. In §11.2(c), `M = gM₀` gives `gM₀ ⊗ e + q⁻¹gM₀ ⊗ f`, which is self-dual. Rescaling `⟨·,·⟩` by `q⁻¹` repairs one place, but not two places `u₁, u₂` above `q` inert in `F` carrying lattices with `M_{u₁}^∨ = M_{u₁}` and `M_{u₂}^∨ = q⁻¹M_{u₂}`: a rational scalar shifts both exponents equally, and replacing `M_u` by `q^kM_u` shifts its exponent by `−2k`.
- If `K_p` were pro-`p`, the edge map `H^i(S_K)_𝔪 → H^i(𝒮_{K^p})_𝔪^{K_p}` would be injective in the least degree. For hyperspecial `K_p` this fails in general, which is why §11.2(a) only gives some `j ≤ i`. For `d = 0`, §11.2(b) is empty.
- For the split local `GL_2` datum with `μ = (1, 0)`, the `μ`-ordinary part of the flag variety `ℙ¹` is `ℙ¹(ℚ_p)`, and its stalks in §11.3 are ordinary Igusa cohomology. For restriction-of-scalars factors the flags use the corresponding local field, so ordinary strata are not all `ℚ_p`-rational flags.
- If `b` is `μ`-ordinary then `d_b = d` in §11.4.
- `ρ_{𝔪^∨} ≅ ρ_𝔪^∨ ⊗ ε̄^{1−n}` (§§8.5, 11.5).
- For the Harris–Taylor signature, §11.7(3) needs only one decomposed generic place ([CS17] Remark 1.5).

## Downstream consumers

Middle-degree concentration and the Galois representations of torsion eigensystems for compact unitary Shimura varieties are inputs to local–global compatibility and modularity arguments that use the cohomology of compact unitary Shimura varieties with torsion coefficients. The auxiliary-prime existence theorems of §8.8 apply to any residual representation of a number field.

## Worked examples

Let `F⁺` be real quadratic and `n = 3`, in Case 2 with signature `(1, 2)` at `τ₀ ∈ Φ` and `(0, 3)` at `τ₁ ∈ Φ`, so `d = 2`. Let `p = uu^c` be split in `𝒦`. The union `⋃_i S_i` consists of the two embeddings whose restriction to `𝒦` corresponds to `u` under `ι_p`, so it contains exactly one of `τ₀, τ₀c` and exactly one of `τ₁, τ₁c`. Both `τ₀` and `τ₀c` have `p_τq_τ = 2 ≠ 0`, while `τ₁` and `τ₁c` are definite.

- If `p` splits in `F⁺`, `u` has two primes `𝔭_1, 𝔭_2` in `F` of residue degree one and each `S_i` is a singleton. Exactly one of them, say `S_1`, contains the nontrivial embedding, and the auxiliary-pair condition through `u` asks for decomposed genericity of `ρ_𝔪` at `𝔭_1` alone, with `q_{𝔭_1} = p`.
- If `p` is inert in `F⁺`, then `m = 1`, and `S_1` contains one embedding from each conjugate pair, exactly one of them nontrivial. The condition through `u` asks for decomposed genericity at the single prime `𝔭_1` of residue degree two, with `q_{𝔭_1} = p²`. The definite embedding in `S_1` has signature `(3, 0)` or `(0, 3)`, so the central correction of §8.3 is `c_1 = 1` or `c_1 = 0` accordingly.

In the first case `F ⊗ ℚ_p = (ℚ_p × ℚ_p) ⊗_{ℚ_p} (ℚ_p × ℚ_p) = ℚ_p⁴`, so `p` splits completely in `F`, with `(e, f, g) = (1, 1, 4)`. The hypothesis of §11.7(2), which is that of the second assertion of [CS17] Theorem 1.1 (CS17 lines 92–95), then asks for decomposed genericity at all four primes of `F` above `p`, whereas the auxiliary-pair condition through `u` asks for it at `𝔭_1` alone: complete splitting is a condition on `p` in `F` and genericity a condition on `ρ_𝔪`, and for this `p` (outside `S ∪ {ℓ}`) the auxiliary-pair condition is strictly weaker than the hypothesis of §11.7(2). In the second case `F ⊗ ℚ_p = ℚ_{p²} × ℚ_{p²}`, with `(e, f, g) = (1, 2, 2)`, so `p` does not split completely in `F` and this auxiliary prime is not covered by the hypothesis of §11.7(2) at all.

For `n = 2`, `q_v = 7`, `ℓ = 11` and residual Frobenius roots `{1, 2}`, §8.5 gives dual roots `{7, 9}`. §8.7 shows both systems are strongly generic, and the `r` of §11.6 is a sum of characters at `v` by IgusaVarietiesAndTorsionConcentration §5.5.
