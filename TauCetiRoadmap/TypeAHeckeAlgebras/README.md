# Roadmap: type-A Hecke algebras, braid traces and positive quotients

**Summary.** Fix a commutative ring R and a unit q of R. The type-A Hecke algebra Hₙ(q) is the
R-algebra generated by T₁, …, Tₙ₋₁ subject to the quadratic relation (Tᵢ − q)(Tᵢ + q⁻¹) = 0, the
braid relation TᵢTᵢ₊₁Tᵢ = Tᵢ₊₁TᵢTᵢ₊₁ and the commutation of distant generators. Adding an uncrossed
strand embeds Hₙ(q) in Hₙ₊₁(q), and the union of this tower is the infinite Hecke algebra H∞(q).
Throughout, Q = q² and gᵢ = qTᵢ, so that (gᵢ + 1)(gᵢ − Q) = 0. The roadmap develops the module
theory of these algebras, their compatible traces, the positive quotients of those traces, and the
von Neumann algebras and subfactors that the positive traces produce. It starts from the Tau Ceti
library, which has the presentation, the basis (T_w) indexed by permutations, the embeddings into
H∞(q), the Ocneanu trace and its braid invariant, the two involutions, and the comparisons with the
Temperley–Lieb and balanced projection algebras.

Its headline theorems are these.

- *Semisimplicity and branching.* Over a field, Hₙ(q) is semisimple exactly when
  `∏_(r=1)^n (1 + Q + ⋯ + Q^(r−1)) ≠ 0`. It is then split semisimple, its simple modules are
  Specht modules indexed by the partitions of n, and restriction to Hₙ₋₁(q) removes one box, with
  multiplicity one.
- *Positive Markov traces (Wenzl).* Let η be the value of a Markov trace on the eigenprojection of
  Tₙ for the eigenvalue q. For q real and nonzero, with the generators self-adjoint, the trace is positive exactly when η lies in the closed interval between 1/(1 + Q)
  and Q/(1 + Q) or equals `a_d(Q) = (1 − Q^(d+1))/((1 + Q)(1 − Q^d))` for a nonzero integer d.
  For |q| = 1 and Q ≠ ±1, with the generators unitary, it is positive exactly when η is 0 or 1, or
  `Q = e^(±2πi/l)` with l ≥ 3 and η = a_k(Q) for some 1 ≤ k ≤ l − 1.
- *Wenzl's subfactors.* For `Q = e^(±2πi/l)`, l ≥ 4 and 2 ≤ k ≤ l − 2, the weak closure B of the
  trace-GNS tower and the weak closure A_m of its tail from the (m+1)-st generator on are
  hyperfinite II₁ factors with `[B : A_m] = d^(2m)`, where d = sin(kπ/l)/sin(π/l), and
  `A_m′ ∩ B = B_m`.
- *Atomic models.* For Q > 0, Q ≠ 1, every positive Markov trace is the restriction of a product
  state on an infinite tensor product of ℓ²(J), J a countable alphabet of positive and negative
  letters with weights w_j. The Hecke von Neumann algebra and its tail are the fixed points of the
  diagonal torus action, and `[B : A] = Σ_j 1/w_j` and `H(B|A) = 2 Σ_j −w_j log w_j`.

Besides the library, the roadmap rests on SchurWeyl, whose symmetric-group Specht modules, symmetric
functions and Pieri rule it deforms; on TemperleyLiebOperatorAlgebras and Subfactors, whose
Temperley–Lieb positivity theorem of Jones it transports through the Hecke-to-Temperley–Lieb
quotient map; and on VonNeumannAlgebras and Subfactors for GNS constructions, incomplete tensor
products, conditional expectations, Jones index and relative entropy. GeometricTopology consumes the
algebraic braid invariant and compares it with the geometric HOMFLY polynomial.

Layer 1 builds the module theory of Hₙ(q); Layer 2 completes the algebraic theory of the Ocneanu
trace and its braid invariant; Layer 3 specifies the involutions and positive states, relates the
Hecke, Temperley–Lieb and balanced projection carriers, classifies the positive Markov traces and
realizes them on atomic tensor models; Layer 4 computes the tail inclusions of these traces, with
their relative commutants, index and relative entropy.

Layers 1 and 2 open with a section (§§1.1, 2.1) that fixes, by their Tau Ceti names, the carriers
and theorems the library supplies, and records the Checks the rest of the layer relies on; these two
sections plan nothing. Elsewhere a supplied declaration is named in the section that uses it, and
the remaining sections are the targets. Layer 4 has three parts: §§4.1–4.5
treat the root-of-unity and `Q = 1` traces through Wenzl's path models and Schur–Weyl duality;
§§4.6–4.16 treat the atomic models at real `Q ≠ 1` through the full phase fixed algebras, whose
key input is that the literal first-site colour projections lie in the Hecke von Neumann algebra;
§§4.17–4.18 collect the scalar endpoints and the invariants of every positive family. Each
*Needs:* line lists what its statement and its route use, so that a target proved by another route
is not blocked behind an unrelated section. Other roadmaps cite the former numbering: Layers 1–3
were H1–H3, §§4.1–4.5 and 4.18 were H4, and §§4.6–4.17 were its sub-layer H4a.

## Conventions

1. **Coefficients and generators.** R is a commutative ring and q a unit of R. Hₙ(q) is Tau
   Ceti's `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra R q n`, with n − 1 generators and n strands; H∞(q) is
   `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra R q`. The quadratic relation is `T * T = (q − q⁻¹) • T + 1`,
   that is `(T − q)(T + q⁻¹) = 0`, and the inverse of a generator is `T − (q − q⁻¹)`. This is the
   convention of Tau Ceti's Jimbo R-matrix `TauCeti.KnotTheory.jimboGenerator`; every other
   convention (Wenzl's `(g − Q)(g + 1) = 0`, the skein form `g − g⁻¹ = z`) is reached by an explicit
   change of generators stated where it is used. Q = q² and gᵢ = qTᵢ.
2. **Indexing.** Prose numbers generators from one: T₁, …, Tₙ₋₁ in Hₙ(q), and the new Markov
   generator of Hₙ₊₁(q) over Hₙ(q) is Tₙ. Lean numbers them from zero: the prose Tᵢ is
   `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.T q ⟨i − 1, _⟩`, crossing the strands i − 1 and i counted from zero.
   The stage `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.stage R q n` is the subalgebra of H∞(q) generated by
   the first n − 1 generators, and Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.stageEquiv` identifies it
   with Hₙ(q). On at most one strand there are no generators and the algebra is R. The tower map
   Hₙ(q) → Hₙ₊₁(q) sends each generator to the identically indexed generator.
3. **Markov traces.** A Markov trace with parameter z ∈ R is a linear functional τ on H∞(q) with
   τ(1) = 1, τ(xy) = τ(yx) and τ(xTₙ) = zτ(x) for x ∈ Hₙ(q) (Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace`). The Ocneanu trace
   `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.ocneanuTrace R q z` is the unique one. Block multiplicativity
   means τ(x·sh_a(y)) = τ(x)τ(y) for x ∈ H_a(q), where sh_a, Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.shift R q a`, shifts every generator index by a.
4. **Projections and parameters.** When q + q⁻¹ is a unit, the native projection is
   `eᵢ = (Tᵢ + q⁻¹)/(q + q⁻¹) = (gᵢ + 1)/(Q + 1)`, the eigenprojection of Tᵢ for the eigenvalue q,
   so that `Tᵢ = q eᵢ − q⁻¹(1 − eᵢ)`. The native projection parameter of a Markov trace is
   `η = τ(eₙ) = (z + q⁻¹)/(q + q⁻¹)`. Wenzl's projection is `eᵢ^W = (Q − gᵢ)/(1 + Q) = 1 − eᵢ`, with
   parameter `η_W = 1 − η`. Every statement says which of the two it uses.
5. **Wenzl's parameters.** For d ∈ ℤ ∖ {0}, `a_d(Q) = (1 − Q^(d+1))/((1 + Q)(1 − Q^d))` for
   Q ≠ −1 with `Q^d ≠ 1`, and `a_d(1) = (d + 1)/(2d)`; this is `wenzlParameter d Q` (§3.5).
6. **Involutions.** For q fixed by the involution of a commutative star ring (a real parameter),
   the real-parameter involution fixes every Tᵢ and conjugates scalars. For q unitary (|q| = 1),
   the unit-circle involution sends Tᵢ to Tᵢ⁻¹ and conjugates scalars. Both are Tau Ceti's; they
   live on different parameter fibres and agree only where q = q⁻¹.
7. **States and inner products.** A positive trace is a trace τ with τ(x*x) ≥ 0 for every x; on
   ℂ the order is Mathlib's `ComplexOrder`. The inner product of a state μ is `⟨x, y⟩ = μ(x* y)`,
   conjugate-linear in the first variable and linear in the second, as in Mathlib.
8. **Partitions and tableaux.** Partitions and Young diagrams are Mathlib's `Nat.Partition` and
   `YoungDiagram`, related by `YoungDiagram.ofPartition`. Cells (i, j) are numbered from one in
   prose, i the row and j the column; the content of a cell is j − i; λ′ is the transpose;
   `f^λ` is the number of standard λ-tableaux, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.standardCount`. A horizontal
   k-strip Λ/ν is a skew diagram of k cells, no two in one column. Symmetric functions, Schur
   functions `s_λ`, power sums `p_m` and the Hall form are those of SchurWeyl Layer 7.
9. **Thoma parameters.** A Thoma pair is two nonincreasing sequences α₁ ≥ α₂ ≥ ⋯ ≥ 0 and
   β₁ ≥ β₂ ≥ ⋯ ≥ 0 with `γ = 1 − Σ_i α_i − Σ_j β_j ≥ 0`; ties are allowed. In an atomic tensor
   model the alphabet carries its own linear order, the R-order, which is part of the data and is
   in general not the order of the Thoma lists.
10. **Subfactor labels.** In Layer 4 the generator labels of the subfactor statements are Wenzl's
   one-based labels, and the trace there is named by its Wenzl parameter.
11. **Names.** The acceptance file `Suggested.lean` states the headline theorems in the namespace
   `TauCetiRoadmap.TypeAHeckeAlgebras`. A backticked name of this roadmap's own is declared there;
   targets without a declaration are named in prose.

## Layer 1: The finite Hecke tower and its module theory

### 1.1 The finite and infinite Hecke algebras

This section fixes the carriers of the roadmap; they are Tau Ceti's, and the section has no target.
Hₙ(q) is `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra R q n`, the quotient of `FreeAlgebra R (Fin (n - 1))` by the
quadratic, braid and distant-commutation relations, with generators
`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.T`, universal property `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.lift` and
`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.hom_ext`. H∞(q) is `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra R q`, the
quotient of `FreeAlgebra R ℕ`, with stages `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.stage R q n`
exhausting it (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.iSup_stage`). The library supplies, among the
facts listed under the supplier contracts:

- the tower maps `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.strandIncl` and the braid homomorphisms
  `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.braidHom`, compatible with each other, and the injectivity of the
  tower maps (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.strandIncl_injective`);
- the map `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.toInfinite` to H∞(q), injective
  (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.toInfinite_injective`) with image the stage
  (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.range_toInfinite`), and the resulting `stageEquiv`;
- functoriality in the coefficients (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.map`, `map_id`, `map_map`), the
  base-change isomorphism `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.algebraTensorAlgEquiv`, and the sign twist
  `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.negEquiv : Hₙ(q) ≃ Hₙ(−q)`, Tᵢ ↦ −Tᵢ;
- for a Coxeter system of type `A_(n−1)`, in particular `TauCeti.permCoxeterSystem n` on
  `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` (lengths are inversion numbers), the elements
  `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.Tw`, well defined by Matsumoto's theorem `TauCeti.matsumoto`, the
  length-increase and length-decrease rules for multiplication by a generator, with the
  coefficient q − q⁻¹ in the descent case, and the basis `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.basisTw`;
  Hₙ(q) is free of rank n!;
- for q = q⁻¹ (in particular q = ±1, where Tᵢ² = 1), the identification
  `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.groupAlgebraEquiv` with the group algebra, Tᵢ ↦ sᵢ;
- the factorization `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.jimbo` of Tau Ceti's Jimbo braid representation
  through Hₙ(q) (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.jimbo_braidHom`). This is one representation of Hₙ(q)
  and is not identified with its regular module;
- the decomposition `H_(n+2) = H_(n+1) + H_(n+1) T_(n+1) H_(n+1)` of the stages
  (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.toSubmodule_stage_add_two`) and the left and right bases of
  `H_(n+1)` over `H_n` of rank n + 1 by the minimal coset representatives
  (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cosetEquiv`, `cosetEquivRight`, `cosetRep`);
- the index character Tᵢ ↦ q and the sign character Tᵢ ↦ −q⁻¹
  (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.indexCharacter`, `signCharacter`), and the complement
  automorphism `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.complementEquiv`, Tᵢ ↦ −Tᵢ⁻¹, that is
  gᵢ ↦ Q − 1 − gᵢ.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra`, `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra` and the
declarations above; Mathlib `FreeAlgebra`, `RingQuot`, `CoxeterSystem`, `CoxeterMatrix.A`,
`MonoidAlgebra`.

**Checks.**
- In H₃(q), s₁ is a left descent of w = s₁s₂, and the descent rule gives
  `T₁ T_(s₁s₂) = (q − q⁻¹) T_(s₁s₂) + T_(s₂)`, which is `T₁T₁T₂ = ((q − q⁻¹)T₁ + 1)T₂`. In Wenzl's
  normalization `(g − Q)(g + 1) = 0` the same rule has coefficient Q − 1 on `g_w`; mixing the two
  conventions gives wrong structure constants.
- At q = −1 two isomorphisms Hₙ(−1) ≅ R[Sₙ] are available: `groupAlgebraEquiv`, Tᵢ ↦ sᵢ, and the
  sign twist to Hₙ(1) followed by it, Tᵢ ↦ −sᵢ. They differ by the sign character, so a comparison
  with the group algebra must name its generator map.
- The sign twist sends T to −T and q to −q: `(−T)² = T² = (q − q⁻¹)T + 1 = ((−q) − (−q)⁻¹)(−T) + 1`.
  The map T ↦ −T at the same parameter q is not a homomorphism unless 2(q − q⁻¹) = 0 (in
  characteristic 2 it is the identity).

### 1.2 Split semisimplicity and the Specht modules

Over a field K put `[r]_Q = 1 + Q + ⋯ + Q^(r−1)`. Prove `isSemisimpleRing_typeAHeckeAlgebra_iff`:
Hₙ(q) is semisimple exactly when `∏_(r=1)^n [r]_Q ≠ 0` (Andersen–Stroppel–Tubbenhauer, Theorem
5.1, p. 21, in this normalization, for every q except q = −1 in characteristic ≠ 2, which the sign
twist of §1.1 carries to q = 1), and prove
`nonempty_algEquiv_pi_matrix_of_prod_ne_zero`: it is then split semisimple, isomorphic as a
K-algebra to `∏_(λ⊢n) M_(f^λ)(K)`.

Over every commutative ring R and unit q, construct one cellular basis of Hₙ(q) in the sense of
Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.CellularBasis`: its cells are the partitions λ ⊢ n, the cells strictly below λ
being the partitions that strictly dominate λ; its index set at λ is the set of standard
λ-tableaux; its anti-involution * fixes every Tᵢ, so that `T_w* = T_(w⁻¹)`; and its basis element
for standard λ-tableaux s, t is `T_(d(s))* x_λ T_(d(t))`, where x_λ is the row symmetrizer of the
Young parabolic subgroup of λ, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.parabolicEigenSum` at the
eigenvalue q, and d(t) is the permutation carrying the row-reading λ-tableau to t, the
minimal-length element of its coset of that subgroup. This is Murphy's basis, cellular over every
commutative ring and unit parameter (Mathas, Example 2.15, p. 6, in the normalization
`(gᵢ − Q)(gᵢ + 1) = 0`, gᵢ = qTᵢ, where its elements are unit multiples of these). The Specht
module S^λ is the cell module of λ, with its basis indexed by the standard λ-tableaux; every later
statement about Specht modules refers to this fixed module and basis. The general theory of
cellular algebras over a field (the
simple modules are the nonzero heads of the cell modules, and the algebra is semisimple exactly
when every cell form is nondegenerate) is TemperleyLieb Layer 5's and is consumed here. Compute
the Gram determinants of the cell forms of Hₙ(q) and deduce the criterion from them, not by an
appeal to q being generic. Under the criterion prove that the S^λ are simple, pairwise
non-isomorphic and absolutely simple, that every simple module is one of them, that
`dim S^λ = f^λ`, and the Wedderburn decomposition
`Hₙ(q) ≅ ∏_λ End_K(S^λ)`, which realizes the isomorphism above on the fixed Specht modules. Over ℂ,
when Q is not an l-th root of unity for any 2 ≤ l ≤ n (so Q ≠ 1), the irreducibility and pairwise
non-isomorphism of the tableau representations and the faithfulness of their sum are Wenzl, Theorem
2.2 (p. 365).

- Over a field of characteristic zero, at q = 1 the module S^λ is carried by `groupAlgebraEquiv` to
  the Specht module S^λ of SchurWeyl Layer 3; at q = −1 the index character becomes the sign character, and S^λ goes to
  `S^λ ⊗ sgn ≅ S^(λ′)`. The ordinary symmetric-group theory is consumed there and not developed
  again.
- The rank identity `Σ_(λ⊢n) (f^λ)² = n!` agrees with the basis theorem.

*Needs:* §1.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.basisTw`,
`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.parabolicEigenSum`, `TauCeti.CellularBasis`,
`TauCeti.CellularBasis.cellRep`, `TauCeti.CellularBasis.gramMatrix`, `TauCeti.standardCount`,
`TauCeti.StandardYoungTableau`, `TauCeti.permCoxeterSystem`; Mathlib `IsSemisimpleRing`,
`Nat.Partition`, `YoungDiagram.ofPartition`; TemperleyLieb Layer 5; SchurWeyl Layers 3–5.

**Checks.**
- n = 2: `H₂(q) = K[T]/((T − q)(T + q⁻¹))` is semisimple exactly when q ≠ −q⁻¹, that is
  `[2]_Q = 1 + Q ≠ 0`. At q = i one has `(T − i)² = 0`. At `q = e^(iπ/3)`, a root of unity,
  `1 + Q = 1 + e^(2πi/3) ≠ 0` and H₂(q) is semisimple: "q is not a root of unity" is not the
  criterion.
- At Q = 1 the product is n!, so the criterion is Maschke's condition that the characteristic of
  K does not divide n!; for n = 3 it fails in characteristics 2 and 3.
- n = 3: the partitions (3), (2,1), (1,1,1) have `f^λ = 1, 2, 1` and `1 + 4 + 1 = 6 = 3!`.
- n = 2: `x_(2) = 1 + qT₁` and `x_(1,1) = 1`. Then `T₁ · 1 = q⁻¹ x_(2) − q⁻¹`, which is `−q⁻¹ · 1`
  modulo the cell (2), so (2) lies below (1,1); with the cells ordered by dominance itself the
  cellular axiom fails at (1,1). The cell forms are given by `x_(2)² = (1 + Q) x_(2)` and `1 · 1 = 1`,
  with Gram determinants `[2]_Q` and 1, both nonzero exactly when the criterion holds.

### 1.3 Branching and induction

Under the criterion of §1.2 for n + 1, prove that the restriction of S^Λ along the tower map
`Hₙ(q) → Hₙ₊₁(q)` is `⊕_(λ = Λ − □) S^λ`, over the partitions λ obtained by removing one box, each
with multiplicity one, and that induction of S^λ is `⊕_(Λ = λ + □) S^Λ`. Give the multiplicity-one
maps explicitly and prove their compatibility with the chosen tableau bases: the basis vectors
indexed by tableaux with n + 1 in a fixed removable box span the summand of that box modulo the
summands of the boxes below it. The induction functor `Hₙ₊₁(q) ⊗_(Hₙ(q)) −` is computed with the
supplied right coset basis `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cosetEquivRight`, and the recursive
trace computations of Layer 2 with the left basis `cosetEquiv`; these bimodule decompositions, and
the left and right bases of rank n + 1, are the induction and recursive-trace suppliers.

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cosetEquiv`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cosetEquivRight`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.toSubmodule_stage_add_two`,
`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.strandIncl`; SchurWeyl Layer 4.

**Checks.**
- S^(2,1) restricts to `S^(2) ⊕ S^(1,1)`, of dimensions 1 + 1 = 2; S^(2) induces to
  `S^(3) ⊕ S^(2,1)`, of dimension 3, the rank of H₃(q) over H₂(q).

### 1.4 Roots of unity: cellular forms and the semisimple quotient

When `∏_(r=1)^n [r]_Q = 0`, keep the cellular basis of §1.2, its cell modules S^λ and their
bilinear forms. TemperleyLieb Layer 5 supplies, for every cellular algebra over a field, the
classification of the simple modules: the quotients `D^λ = S^λ / rad S^λ` of the cell modules by
the radicals of their forms, for the λ whose form is nonzero, are simple, pairwise non-isomorphic
and exhaust the simple modules. Apply it to Hₙ(q), and construct the semisimple quotient of Hₙ(q)
by its Jacobson radical with its simple factors indexed by the λ with `D^λ ≠ 0`. Never extend the generic matrix units through a vanishing denominator. The canonical
symmetrizing trace `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.canonicalTrace` is nondegenerate at every parameter
(`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.forall_canonicalTrace_mul_eq_zero_iff`), so Hₙ(q) is a symmetric
algebra also where it is not semisimple.

*Needs:* §§1.2–1.3; TemperleyLieb Layer 5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.canonicalTrace`;
Mathlib `Ring.jacobson`, `IsSemisimpleRing`.

**Checks.**
- n = 2, Q = −1: q = −q⁻¹, so S^(2) (T ↦ q) and S^(1,1) (T ↦ −q⁻¹) are the same one-dimensional
  module, H₂(q) is a single block, and its semisimple quotient is K. A definition that read off
  the simple modules from the generic matrix units would divide by `1 + Q = 0`.

### 1.5 The cocenter and the cycle-block elements

For the complex specialization q = √Q, Q > 0, define the cocenter of Hₙ(q) as the quotient complex
vector space of Hₙ(q) by the linear span of the commutators xy − yx; this span is not an ideal.
The cycle word on m strands is `ζₘ = gₘ₋₁ ⋯ g₁` with ζ₁ = 1, Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cycleWord q m`, which lies in the stage of the first m strands.
For a partition λ of n define ζ_λ by placing the cycle words ζ_(λ₁), ζ_(λ₂), … on the consecutive
disjoint strand blocks of sizes λ₁, λ₂, …, through the shifts `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.shift`
and the block embeddings `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.blockIncl`, made explicit, and the
tower maps.

- Prove the cycle-block cocenter basis theorem: the classes of the ζ_λ, λ ⊢ n, form a basis of the
  cocenter of Hₙ(q) (Neretin, §1.4, p. 5, which cites Geck–Pfeiffer, §8.2).
- Hence two complex-linear trace functionals on Hₙ(q) that agree on every ζ_λ are equal.
- Prove that the cycle-block elements are compatible with the tower maps and the block
  embeddings.
- The library proves that two tracial, block-multiplicative functionals on H∞(q) agreeing on every
  ζₘ are equal (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.towerTrace_eq_of_cycleChain_values`), and that a
  Markov trace with parameter z takes the value `(qz)^(m−1)` on ζₘ
  (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.map_cycleWord`). The block-multiplicativity
  hypothesis is stated explicitly wherever the comparison is used: the values on the cycle words
  alone do not determine a trace that is not block multiplicative.

These cocenter and comparison interfaces belong to this layer; Layers 2 and 3 consume them.

*Needs:* §1.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.cycleWord`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.shift`, `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.blockIncl`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.towerTrace_eq_of_cycleChain_values`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.map_cycleWord`; Mathlib `Submodule.span`,
`Nat.Partition`.

**Checks.**
- The cocenter of H₃(q) has dimension 3, with basis the classes of ζ_(1,1,1) = 1, ζ_(2,1) = g₁ and
  ζ_(3) = g₂g₁. The class of g₂ equals that of g₁, since `g₂ = (g₁g₂) g₁ (g₁g₂)⁻¹` by the braid
  relation.
- At Q = 1 the normalized character τ = χ^(3,1)/3 of S₄ is a trace with τ(s₁) = 1/3 and
  τ(s₁s₃) = −1/3 ≠ τ(s₁)τ(s₃) = 1/9: a single irreducible character is not block multiplicative, so
  the hypothesis of the cycle-chain comparison is not vacuous.

### 1.6 Jucys–Murphy elements

The library defines the normalized Jucys–Murphy elements `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.jucysMurphy`,

rescaled elements `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.additiveJucysMurphy`, L₁ = 0 and
`L_(i+1) = Q⁻¹ gᵢ Lᵢ gᵢ + Q⁻¹ gᵢ`, over every commutative ring and every unit q. It proves
`Jᵢ = 1 + (Q − 1) Lᵢ` (`jucysMurphy_eq_one_add_smul_additiveJucysMurphy`), so that
`Lᵢ = (Jᵢ − 1)/(Q − 1)` when Q − 1 is a unit, the pairwise commutation of the Jᵢ and of the Lᵢ, and
that at Q = 1 the recursion of the Lᵢ is the additive Jucys–Murphy recursion of the symmetric group.
On a generic semisimple complex fibre prove:

- for Q > 0 the Jᵢ are self-adjoint for the real-parameter involution;
- on the fixed tableau basis of S^λ (§1.2), Jᵢ acts triangularly for the dominance order of
  standard λ-tableaux, with diagonal entry `Q^(c)` at the tableau t, c the content of the box of i
  in t, and Lᵢ likewise with diagonal entry `[c]_Q = (Q^c − 1)/(Q − 1)`, `[c]_1 = c` (for the Lᵢ
  over every commutative ring, Mathas, Example 2.15, p. 7); where these eigenvalues separate the
  standard λ-tableaux, a unitriangular change of the fixed basis gives the seminormal basis, on
  which Jᵢ and Lᵢ act diagonally with these eigenvalues;
- the joint spectral projections of J₁, …, Jₙ are the branching projections onto the summands of
  §1.3, path by path.

Use the contents of the Lᵢ to state a continuous comparison as Q → 1; the path projections at
Q = 1 are not obtained by dividing by Q − 1. The multiplicative eigenvalues separate paths only
when Q ≠ 1 and the eigenvalues `Q^c` of the addable boxes of each preceding partition are distinct.
The library proves that two distinct addable cells of a partition of m − 1 have contents differing
by at least 2 and at most m (`YoungDiagram.IsAddable.two_le_cellContent_sub`, `YoungDiagram.IsAddable.cellContent_sub_le`),
that separation at every step m ≤ n holds exactly when `Q^d ≠ 1` for 2 ≤ d ≤ n

Q ≠ 1 implies it (`YoungDiagram.injOn_zpow_cellContent_of_prod_geom_sum_ne_zero`). At Q = 1 use the
group-algebra and Schur–Weyl route with the additive elements; at the other roots of unity use the
cellular quotient of §1.4.

### 1.7 Ram's character formula

Let `χ^λ` be the character of S^λ. Define `q_m ∈ Λ_(ℚ(Q))` for m ≥ 1 by the generating function
`1 + (Q − 1) Σ_(m≥1) q_m t^m = ∏_i (1 − x_i t)/(1 − Q x_i t)`. Since
`log ∏_i (1 − x_i t)/(1 − Q x_i t) = Σ_(m≥1) (Q^m − 1) p_m t^m / m`, each `q_m` is a polynomial in
the power sums with coefficients in ℚ(Q). Put `q_μ = ∏_i q_(μ_i)`. Prove
Ram's formula on the cycle-block elements: for λ, μ ⊢ n on a generic semisimple fibre,
`χ^λ(ζ_μ) = ⟨q_μ, s_λ⟩`, the Hall form of SchurWeyl Layer 7, with these exact Q-dependent
coefficients (Ram, Theorem 4.14, p. 475, with the generating function in the proof of Theorem
4.13(b), p. 474; Ram's q is our Q); at Q = 1, `q_m = p_m` and this is the Frobenius formula.
Layers 3 and 4 consume it: the cocenter basis of §1.5 alone does not determine the Schur
character weights. The power sums over ℂ are SchurWeyl §7.5's rational power-sum presentation of
`Λ_ℂ` (Newton's identities), which this section uses for the change of basis between the `q_μ`,
the `p_μ` and the `s_λ`.

*Needs:* §§1.2, 1.5; SchurWeyl §§7.5–7.6.

**Checks.**

  acts by Q on S^(2) and by −1 on S^(1,1).
- In one variable x₁ = 1 the generating function is `(1 − t)/(1 − Qt)`, so `q_m(1, 0, …) = Q^(m−1)`,
  which is `χ^(m)(ζₘ)`: on the index character gᵢ acts by Q.

### 1.8 The shuffle carrier

The library defines, for a module U of `H_l(q)` given by `ρ : H_l(q) → End U`, the shuffle module
`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.shuffleRep k ρ` of `H_(k+l)(q)` on the functions from the masks, the
k-subsets of the k + l positions (`TauCeti.TypeAHeckeAlgebra.ShuffleMask k l`), to U. In the
normalization gᵢ = qTᵢ, two selected positions give the scalar Q, two rest positions act by the
corresponding consecutive generator of U, and a mixed pair in the order (selected/rest,
rest/selected) acts by the block `[[Q − 1, −q], [−q, 0]]`. The library proves the Hecke relations,
that the initial mask {1, …, k} carries `triv_k ⊗ U` for the parabolic subalgebra generated by the
first k − 1 and the last l − 1 generators, that the module is generated by it, and that along a
shuffle path the generators act triangularly with leading coefficient `(−q)^length`.

For Q > 0, q = √Q, and U a unitary `H_l(q)`-module (a Hilbert space on which the generators act
self-adjointly), call `Sh_k(U)` the Hilbert space `ℓ²(masks) ⊗ U` with the orthonormal-mask
norm, and prove:

- the generators of `H_(k+l)(q)` act self-adjointly on `Sh_k(U)`;
- the parabolic subalgebra `H_k(q) ⊗ H_l(q)` has a coset basis in `H_(k+l)(q)` indexed by the
  minimal shuffle coset words, and the map from the induced module
  `Ind_(H_k ⊗ H_l)^(H_(k+l))(triv_k ⊗ U)` to `Sh_k(U)` sending h ⊗ u to h applied to u at the initial
  mask is triangular on these words with nonzero leading coefficient `(−√Q)^length`, hence an
  isomorphism. The norm is the specified orthonormal-mask norm, not an unspecified norm transported
  through the algebraic isomorphism;
- a unitary intertwiner of rest modules extends by the identity on masks to a unitary intertwiner
  of shuffle modules, and preserves the projection onto the masks containing the first position.

### 1.9 The regular carrier and continuous Young projectors

The library gives the regular module, for a real nonzero parameter, the inner product of the
canonical trace, under which the elements `g_w = q^(ℓ(w)) T_w` are orthogonal with

- the additive Jucys–Murphy elements act with eigenvalues `[c]_Q`, and for consecutive contents

  defined by separating coincident contents from unrelated paths;

### 1.10 The positive-real parabolic Pieri rule

For Q > 0 prove the parabolic induction rule
`Ind_(H_k ⊗ H_l)^(H_(k+l))(triv_k ⊗ S^ν) ≅ ⊕_(Λ/ν horizontal k-strip) S^Λ`, with multiplicity one
and with gᵢ acting by Q on `triv_k`, together with its orthogonal decomposition in `Sh_k(S^ν)`;
include k = 0 and l = 0. This is broader than one-box restriction. The route: by §1.9 the rank of
`z_Λ B_ν` is constant in Q, and at Q = 1 it is computed from SchurWeyl §7.6. There the block
embedding `S_k × S_l → S_(k+l)`, the external product of class functions and
`ch(Ind(f ⊠ g)) = ch(f) ch(g)`, with the row Specht module trivial and `s_(k) = h_k`, give
`ch(Ind(triv_k ⊠ S^ν)) = h_k s_ν = Σ_(Λ/ν horizontal k-strip) s_Λ` by the finite-variable Pieri rule
(Tau Ceti `TauCeti.hsymm_mul_schurPoly`, with interlacing `YoungDiagram.InterlacedBy`). Hence
`rank(z_Λ B_ν) = f^Λ` on these strips and 0 otherwise, and `dim S^Λ = f^Λ` gives the rule. The
construction stays in finite-dimensional positive-real Hecke algebras: it asserts no
root-of-unity deformation, no higher-rank quantum-group theorem and no signed supergroup
semisimplicity. The ordinary representation-theoretic inputs stay with SchurWeyl; the transport in
the Hecke parameter belongs here.

*Needs:* §§1.2, 1.8, 1.9; SchurWeyl §7.6; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.hsymm_mul_schurPoly`,
`YoungDiagram.InterlacedBy`, `TauCeti.standardCount`.

**Checks.**
- k = 2, l = 1, ν = (1): the horizontal 2-strips over (1) give (3) and (2,1), but not (1,1,1),
  whose two new cells share a column. The dimensions `1 + 2 = 3` equal the number of 2-subsets
  of 3 positions, the dimension of `Sh_2(triv_1)`.

## Layer 2: Algebraic Ocneanu trace and braid closure

### 2.1 The Ocneanu trace

This section fixes the algebraic trace, which is Tau Ceti's; it has no target. For every commutative
ring R, unit q and z ∈ R, `TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.ocneanuTrace R q z` is a Markov trace
with parameter z (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.isMarkovTrace_ocneanuTrace`) and is the only
one (`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.existsUnique_isMarkovTrace`; Jones, Theorem 5.1). It is
computed recursively by `τ(a Tₙ b) = z τ(ba)` for a, b ∈ Hₙ(q)
(`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.map_mul_T_mul`), it is block multiplicative
(`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.map_mul_shift`), which the cycle-chain comparison
of §1.5 consumes, and at z = q and z = −q⁻¹ it is the index and the sign character
(`ocneanuTrace_eq_indexCharacter`, `ocneanuTrace_eq_signCharacter`). Precomposition with the
complement automorphism sends a Markov trace with parameter z to one with parameter q − q⁻¹ − z
(`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.comp_complementEquiv`). No positivity is asserted
at this level, and a one-dimensional representation of a relation quotient is kept distinct from a
compatible, nontrivial positive trace on a tower.

`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.existsUnique_isMarkovTrace`,
`TauCeti.InfiniteTypeAHeckeAlgebra.IsMarkovTrace.map_mul_shift`.

**Checks.**

  generic z it is not τ(T₁)².
- `τ(T₁T₂T₁) = τ(T₁²T₂) = z((q − q⁻¹)z + 1)` and `τ(T₂T₁T₂) = τ(T₁T₂²) = (q − q⁻¹)z² + z`: the two

### 2.2 Finite trace radicals

The library defines, for a tracial functional τ on H∞(q), the radical of the multiplication pairing

### 2.3 The normalized braid-closure invariant

### 3.2 Projection characters and the Jones quotient

With `Tᵢ = q eᵢ − q⁻¹(1 − eᵢ)`, the projection characters eᵢ ↦ 0 and eᵢ ↦ 1 are the Hecke

representations exist for every parameter, and they establish neither a nontrivial Markov tower
nor the interior range 0 < η < 1.

The ordinary Jones (Temperley–Lieb) quotient Aₙ of Jones §11 is generated by self-adjoint
projections eᵢ with distant commutation and both adjacent relations `eᵢ eᵢ₊₁ eᵢ = τ eᵢ` and
`eᵢ₊₁ eᵢ eᵢ₊₁ = τ eᵢ₊₁`. The balanced relation together with either adjacent relation gives the
other, and either relation alone is insufficient. With `τ⁻¹ = 2 + t + t⁻¹`, `gᵢ = t eᵢ − (1 − eᵢ)`
and `Tᵢ = q⁻¹ gᵢ` at t = q², this quotient adds to the Hecke relations Jones's relation

```text

```

### 4.3 The finite-colour inclusions at Q = 1

- tail minimality, directly by site permutations: the algebra `A = E_tail^(PU(k))` contains every

  tail alone the same argument gives `A′ ∩ E_tail = ℂ`;

### 4.6 Full phase fixed algebras: index and entropy

  fixed algebra of the tail.

### 4.11 The finite joint edge law in the tensor model

### 4.12 The countable hook product and branch masses

For the continuous atomic family at Q ≠ 1, prove the colour-projection theorem directly in its
countable product state. Write `Q_* = max(Q, Q⁻¹)` and `t = Q_*⁻¹ ∈ (0, 1)`, and
`α_i = A t^(i−1)`, `β_j = B t^(j−1)` with A, B ≥ 0 and A + B = 1 − t: these are the Thoma lists of
§3.14, in its R-order, a vanished sector being omitted. The finite positive and negative discrete
models keep their realization of §4.7. This route proves the full signed countable conclusion
without replacing the actual state by normalized retained-colour states; its estimates are
countable-state estimates, and no uniform finite-alphabet truncation estimate is needed or asserted.
First take Q > 1, A > 0, and let P be the literal first-site projection of the first positive
colour, of weight A, the least in R-order. The actual normalized overlap, its all-shape weighted
estimate, the branch estimate and literal membership are proved in that order (§§4.12–4.13); every
other colour is recovered afterwards (§§4.14–4.16). An edge projection, a Schur shape frequency and
a first-site colour projection are distinct objects throughout.

*Complete countable hook product.* For arbitrary nonnegative A, B, not necessarily normalized, prove

```text
s_λ(A, At, …; B, Bt, …)
 = t^(n(λ)) ∏_((i,j)∈λ) (A + B t^(j−i)) / (1 − t^(h_λ(i,j))),       (H)
```

where `n(λ) = Σ_i (i−1) λ_i` and `h_λ(i,j) = λ_i − j + λ′_j − i + 1` (Wenzl, (3.5), p. 376, with his
a = A, b = −B and q = t, after Macdonald, Ch. I, §3, Ex. 3). A finite polynomial argument gives
the identity, countable convergence included. The degree-d Jacobi–Trudi specialization
`p_m = (1 − u^m)/(1 − t^m)` is polynomial in u. At `u = t^M`, M ≥ ℓ(λ), the finite geometric
bialternant (Vandermonde) calculation gives

```text
t^(n(λ)) ∏_(i<j≤M) (1 − t^(λ_i − λ_j + j − i))/(1 − t^(j−i))
 = t^(n(λ)) ∏_((i,j)∈λ) (1 − t^(M + j − i))/(1 − t^(h_λ(i,j))).
```

For the finite cancellation: in each row i the hook lengths together with `λ_i − λ_j + j − i`,
i < j ≤ M, partition {1, …, λ_i + M − i}; scanning the columns from right to left, each drop in
column height skips exactly one of the listed row differences. With
`(t;t)_r = ∏_(d=1)^r (1 − t^d)` this gives the hook denominator
`∏_i (t;t)_(λ_i+M−i) / ∏_(i<j) (1 − t^(λ_i − λ_j + j − i))`; the numerator is
`∏_i (t;t)_(λ_i+M−i)/(t;t)_(M−i)`, and `∏_i (t;t)_(M−i) = ∏_(i<j) (1 − t^(j−i))`. So the identity holds
at infinitely many distinct u = t^M, hence as polynomials in u. Put u = −B/A, multiply by `A^d`,
and use polynomial continuity at A = 0. The power sums are then exactly
`(A^m + (−1)^(m−1) B^m)/(1 − t^m)`, the signed specialization of §4.8. All positive-mass edges have
positive denominator; the one-sector endpoint identities use ordinary and transposed support and
omit zero-mass blocks.

*Branch masses.* For an addable row a set `d_j = λ_j + 1 − j` for every j, zero parts retained,
`c = d_a` and `Δ_j = |d_a − d_j|`. Dividing (H) gives

```text
P_a(λ) = ∏_(j<a) (1 − t^(Δ_j − 1))/(1 − t^(Δ_j)) · ∏_(j>a) (1 − t^(Δ_j + 1))/(1 − t^(Δ_j)),
r_a = s_(λ+e_a)/s_λ = (A + B t^c) t^(a−1) P_a = α_a P_a + B t^(λ_a) P_a.          (B)
```

For L ≥ max(a, ℓ(λ)) the zero-row tail of the product is exactly `1/(1 − t^(c+L))`, with c + L > 0,
so the infinite product and the ratio are specified with no bound on the number of rows or on the
hook lengths. Transposition interchanges A and B and proves the corresponding ratio for a negative
column.

*The all-shadow event.* For a supported positive colour a choose 0 < u < α_a < v such that [u, v]
contains no other positive frequency. For λ ⊢ n − 1 let `DirectGood(n, a, G)` mean: row a is
addable; `d_a ∈ [nu + G, nv − G]`; and every `d_j`, j ≠ a, lies outside `(nu − G, nv + G)`. Blocked
rows and all zero rows are included, and endpoints may be assigned consistently by closed
inequalities. All addable boxes are among these same-sector shadows, so the window selects exactly
one edge; no test is made on the infinitely many opposite-sector zero-column ghosts, since the
signed hook factor already incorporates that sector. On this event `Δ_j ≥ 2G`, `Δ_j ≥ |j − a|` and
`λ_a ≥ G`. For G ≥ 1 define `C_P = 2/[√t (1 − √t)(1 − t)]`; then

```text
0 < P_a ≤ 1/(1 − t),
|P_a − 1| ≤ C_P t^G,
|r_a − α_a| ≤ [α_a C_P + B/(1 − t)] t^G.                                         (B1)
```

Each factor with j < a is `1 − u_j` with `u_j ≤ t^(Δ_j − 1)`, and each with j > a is `1 + v_j` with
`v_j ≤ t^(Δ_j)`; the two gap bounds give `t^(Δ_j) ≤ t^G t^(|j−a|/2)`, so the total of the absolute
perturbations is at most `2t^G/[√t(1 − √t)]`. The factors before a are at most one; those after a
decrease with Δ, and their product is bounded by `∏_(k≥1) (1 − t^(k+1))/(1 − t^k) = 1/(1 − t)`. The
telescoping difference of finite products, followed by the convergent tail limit, proves (B1).

Prove `Prob(DirectGood(n, a, nδ)) → 1` for a fixed small δ > 0 from the signed Schur law. Choose δ
with α_a inside [u + 3δ, v − 3δ], every other positive limit outside [u − 3δ, v + 3δ], and δ < u/4.
Choose a fixed K ≥ a with 1/(K + 1) < u/2. For all j > K, deterministically,
`d_j/n ≤ λ_j/n ≤ (n − 1)/(nj) ≤ 1/j < u/2 < u − δ`. For j ≤ K the finitely many fixed-row laws of
large numbers of §4.9 give every margin with probability tending to one. For a > 1 the strict
geometric gap gives `λ_(a−1) > λ_a` with high probability, hence addability; row 1 is always
addable. This controls all the infinitely many row shadows without a growing finite-colour cutoff;
transposition supplies the column statement.

*Needs:* §§3.14, 4.8, 4.9; SchurWeyl §7.5; Mathlib `Polynomial`, `tprod`.

**Checks.**
- λ = (1): (H) gives `s_(1) = (A + B)/(1 − t)`, the first power sum; λ = (2) gives
  `(A + B)(A + Bt)/((1 − t)(1 − t²))`, which is `(p₁² + p₂)/2` with `p₂ = (A² − B²)/(1 − t²)`; and
  λ = (1,1) gives `(A + B)(At + B)/((1 − t)(1 − t²))`.

  `r₁ = (A + Bt)/(1 − t²)` and `r₂ = (At + B)/(1 − t²)`, summing to 1.

### 4.13 The first positive colour: overlap, tail bound and literal membership

*Ordinary-Hilbert overlap in the signed carrier.* Fix exactly k occurrences of the selected first
positive colour among n sites, and put l = n − k. An orthonormal word is uniquely a k-subset mask
together with its ordered rest subword; this is a Hilbert unitary onto
`ℓ²({S ⊆ {1, …, n} : |S| = k}) ⊗ K_rest^(⊗l)`. The generator matrices are exactly those of
`Sh_k(K_rest^(⊗l))` of §1.8: the selected block is the scalar Q, the mixed blocks are
`[[Q − 1, −√Q], [−√Q, 0]]`, and rest/rest pairs act by the consecutive rest generator. The literal P is
`1_(1∈S) ⊗ 1`. A unitary decomposition of the rest module extends by the identity on masks and
preserves P, so arbitrary rest signs are spectator multiplicities of ordinary finite Hecke simples,
with no signed-supergroup decomposition assumed.

Use the first-site Jucys–Murphy recursion `X₁ = 1`, `Xₙ = Q⁻¹ R₁ X_(n−1)^tail R₁`. Reversing the
generators of the Jucys–Murphy elements of §1.6 preserves the central Young labels (prove it by
conjugation with the longest braid); `Xₙ` centralizes the shifted tail and has eigenvalue
`Q^(content(Λ/λ))` on its full/tail edge. Writing `E_rest = 1 − P`, prove the operator identity

```text
E_rest X_n E_rest = I_(tail masks of size k) ⊗ X_l(rest).                       (JM)
```

When l = 0 the compressed space is zero; for k = 0 it is the defining rest identity. Induct on n on
every first-rest mask. If physical site 2 is selected, the rightmost R₁ has zero diagonal on
rest/selected and must swap with coefficient −√Q; the tail operator keeps site 1 selected; to
survive the final `E_rest`, site 2 must return to rest and the left R₁ must swap back; the first-rest
compression of the tail is the inductive identity, the mask is restored, and the two swaps cancel
Q⁻¹. If site 2 is rest, the right R₁ is the first rest generator; a tail output with site 2 selected
is killed by the final `E_rest`, again by the zero diagonal; on the surviving rest output induction
gives the remaining rest recursion, so the output is `Q⁻¹ g₁^rest X_(l−1)^(rest tail) g₁^rest = X_l(rest)`.
The two cases have no cross terms and preserve every later mask coordinate. That the selected
colour is least in R-order is essential to this proof.

For a rest simple S^ν fix a tail shape λ and write its isotypic space unitarily as `S^λ ⊗ M_λ`; P
and X act on `M_λ`. If ν interlaces λ (`λ_j ≥ ν_j ≥ λ_(j+1)`), the first-selected subspace is
`Sh_(k−1)(S^ν)`, so the parabolic Pieri rule of §1.10 makes P on `M_λ` a rank-one orthogonal
projection vv*, ‖v‖ = 1. The full Pieri rule and one-box restriction give X the simple active
eigenvalues `x_a = Q^(λ_a + 1 − a)` for `Λ = λ + e_a` interlacing ν; (JM) and rest restriction give its
compression to the orthogonal complement the active eigenvalues `y_j = Q^(ν_j − j)` when
`μ = ν − e_j` and λ/μ is a horizontal strip. These are spectra of the actual operators, not a count
of formal dimensions. Pad λ to length R and ν to length R − 1; then
`J = {j : ν_j > λ_(j+1)}` and `I = {1} ∪ {j + 1 : j ∈ J}` are the complement and full spectra, the
inequalities give actual removability and addability (`ν_j > λ_(j+1) ≥ ν_(j+1)` and
`λ_j ≥ ν_j > λ_(j+1)`), and for j outside J, `y_j = x_(j+1)` is an inactive matched pair. Thus
|I| = |J| + 1, and padded zeros introduce only cancelling pairs. The rank-one resolvent cofactor
identity gives

```text
⟨v, (z − X)⁻¹ v⟩ = det(z − E_rest X E_rest |_(v⊥)) / det(z − X)
               = ∏_(j∈J) (z − y_j) / ∏_(i∈I) (z − x_i),
```

whose residues at `x_a` are the actual probabilities `‖E_(x_a) v‖²`. Inserting the inactive matched
pairs and dividing each factor by `x_a` gives

```text
D_a(λ, ν) = ∏_(j=1)^(R−1) (1 − t^(λ_a − ν_j + j − a + 1)) / ∏_(i=1, i≠a)^R (1 − t^(λ_a − λ_i + i − a)).   (D)
```

No denominator exponent is zero for a partition λ: it is negative for i < a and positive for i > a.
On interlacing ν the inactive branches have a zero numerator and the active branches give the
orthogonal compression scalar, so `0 ≤ D_a ≤ 1`. If ν interlaces `Λ = λ + e₁` but not λ, then exactly
`ν₁ = Λ₁ = λ₁ + 1`, the first-selected tail range is absent and `D₁ = 0`, as its first numerator
factor also gives; this case is treated separately rather than asserting rank one there. For k = 0,
P = 0; for l = 0 there is the single all-selected row edge with scalar 1, agreeing with the cancelled
formula. This establishes the Hilbert normalization without an unproved isometry of a Clebsch map.

*The signed countable density.* On each rest isotypic component choose a unitary finite-Hecke
decomposition

```text
K_rest^(⊗l) = ⊕_ν S^ν ⊗ M_ν,
D_rest^(⊗l) = ⊕_ν I_(S^ν) ⊗ d_ν,   Tr(d_ν) = s_ν(rest).
```

The last equality follows from the primitive-path dictionary of §4.8 after normalizing the
remaining mass 1 − A > 0: its geometric parameters are `At/(1 − A)` and `B/(1 − A)`, again summing to
1 − t, so the normalized rest model is another model of §3.14; multiply its degree-l trace by
`(1 − A)^l` and use Schur homogeneity. For this unnormalized rest alphabet p₁ = 1 − A, which is not
reset to 1, and the equality is not inferred from (D). Under the mask unitary the density is
`A^k I_masks ⊗ D_rest^(⊗l)`. Each full/tail/rest edge contributes `A^k f^λ s_ν(rest)` and its
P-compression contributes that factor times `D₁`; cancelling `f^λ` against the full edge trace
`f^λ s_Λ(A;B)` proves the literal coefficient identity

```text
c_1(λ, Λ) = Σ_(ν interlaces Λ) π_Λ(ν) D_1(λ, ν),
π_Λ(ν) = A^(n−|ν|) s_ν(At; B) / s_Λ(A; B),   Λ = λ + e_1.                       (W)
```

Even-letter Schur branching makes π a probability law. At fixed n there are finitely many k and rest
shapes, while the multiplicity spaces may be infinite-dimensional; normal nonnegative weighted
traces pass to the countable carrier. Opposite signs with equal numerical weights stay distinct
letters. So (W) is the actual first-site coefficient, not only a Schur marginal or a matched rank.

*Uniform weighted tail over all shapes.* In π fix ν₂, ν₃, …; then x = ν₁ ranges over the whole
integer interval [Λ₂, Λ₁]. The homogeneous hook ratio (H), (B), now with the remaining parameters At
and B, gives

```text
weight(x+1)/weight(x) = [t + (B/A) t^x] P_1(ν),     P_1(ν) ≤ 1/(1 − t^(x − ν_2 + 1)),
```

the inequality by comparing the factors with the telescoping product in which every lower row
equals ν₂. So for x ≥ Λ₂ + L the ratio is at most `[t + (B/A)t^L]/[1 − t^(L+1)]`. Put ρ = (1 + t)/2 and
choose a finite integer L₀ ≥ 0 with `[(B/A) + ρt] t^(L₀) ≤ (1 − t)/2`; then the ratio is at most ρ
above Λ₂ + L₀. Its starting weight is a term of the conditional normalizer, so a geometric sum, then
averaging over all lower rows, proves for every integer M ≥ 0

```text
π_Λ(ν_1 ≥ Λ_2 + L_0 + M) ≤ ρ^M / (1 − ρ).                                         (T)
```

If the starting point exceeds Λ₁ the event is empty. Zero weights are omitted; for A > 0 the positive
geometric remainder makes the supported weights positive. The bound is uniform in n, in the whole
partition, in all interlacing coordinates and blocked shadows, B = 0 included.

For the top edge write `H = Λ₁ − Λ₂ = λ₁ − λ₂ + 1`. Every numerator factor j ≥ 2 of D₁ is at least its
paired denominator factor i = j, since ν_j ≤ Λ_j = λ_j, and the final unpaired denominator is less
than one; so the actual overlap satisfies `1 − D₁ ≤ t^(Λ₁ − ν₁)`. Splitting (W) at
ν₁ ≥ Λ₂ + ⌊H/2⌋ and using (T) on the upper part gives

```text
1 − c_1 ≤ t^(H/2) + min(1, ρ^(max(0, ⌊H/2⌋ − L_0)) / (1 − ρ)).                    (M)
```

This is a weighted trace bound; it does not assert that every vector in the edge range has small
leakage, nor that `(1 − P) q_edge` has small operator norm.

*Constants, loss and literal membership.* For row 1 on DirectGood the shadow identity is
`H = d₁ − d₂ = λ₁ − λ₂ + 1 ≥ 2G`, also when row 2 is blocked or zero. Set

```text
C_P = 2/[√t (1 − √t)(1 − t)],
C_B = max(A C_P + B/(1 − t), t⁻¹),
C_M = 1 + ρ^(−L_0 − 1)/(1 − ρ),
κ = log(ρ)/log(t) > 0,    C_loss = C_B + 2C_M.
```

On DirectGood, for all G ≥ 0,

```text
|r_* − A| ≤ C_B t^G,
r_* (1 − c_*) ≤ C_M ρ^G = C_M t^(κG),
ell_λ := Σ_Λ r_(λ,Λ) (c_1(λ,Λ) − f(λ,Λ))² ≤ C_loss ρ^G.                          (L)
```

For G ≥ 1 the branch bound is (B1); for G < 1, |r_* − A| ≤ 1 and the t⁻¹ term covers it. For
G ≥ L₀ + 1, ⌊H/2⌋ ≥ G − 1 in (M), and t ≤ ρ proves the mixed bound; for G ≤ L₀ + 1 the trivial
`r_*(1 − c_*) ≤ 1` is dominated by the displayed C_M. No further unspecified constant or shape
restriction is hidden; the transition is the actual normalized Schur probability, so r_* ≤ 1. Here
f is the indicator of the positive A-window, and on DirectGood exactly one edge has f = 1.
Positivity and the actual conditional colour identity `Σ_Λ r_(λ,Λ) c₁(λ,Λ) = A` give
`ell_λ ≤ A − r_* + 2r_*(1 − c_*) ≤ |r_* − A| + 2r_*(1 − c_*)`, and on the complement ell_λ ≤ 1.
Averaging with the actual tail Schur law and putting G = nδ gives
`Loss_n ≤ C_loss ρ^(nδ) + Prob(not DirectGood(n, 1, nδ)) → 0`. This proves both estimates and the
full loss in the complete countable state, with no retained mass, finite-colour typicality or
normalization by an omitted tail.

Use the finite tracial centralizer C and the exact edge expansion of §4.10. For the window projection
`Fₙ ∈ Bₙ`, `‖E_(Bₙ)(P) − Fₙ‖₂² = Loss_n`. From VonNeumannAlgebras Layer 11's trace-specialized
Takesaki expectation and its composition laws and Layer 6's tracial standard form and bounded L²

```text
E_B((P − E_B(P))*(P − E_B(P))) = E_B(P) − E_B(P)² = 0,
```

hence the literal `P = E_B(P) ∈ B`. No membership of P was assumed in its coefficient, gap or
expectation calculation. In the standard representation bounded L² convergence gives strong
convergence Fₙ → P.

### 4.14 Deleting a recovered colour

Let P be the first positive colour projection, of weight p = A ∈ (0, 1), and suppose P ∈ B. The
tensor shift is normal and preserves the Hecke algebra, so every site projection `Pᵢ` lies in B. Put
`Eᵢ = 1 − Pᵢ` and q = √Q. The actual block gives the partial isometry

```text
V_i = −q⁻¹ (E_i P_(i+1)) R_i (P_i E_(i+1)) ∈ B.
```

On a pair (deleted colour, remaining colour b) it is the literal swap (1, b) ↦ (b, 1), with
coefficient +1, for every remaining b, and its adjoint reverses this swap; so
`Vᵢ*Vᵢ = Pᵢ Eᵢ₊₁` and `Vᵢ Vᵢ* = Eᵢ Pᵢ₊₁`. This uses only P and R, not any remaining-colour projection.
Both projections, Rᵢ and Vᵢ preserve colour multiplicities, hence lie in the state centralizer.

Fix m ≥ 1. For every increasing `S = (j₁ < ⋯ < j_m)` with L = j_m let

```text
C_S = ∏_(i∈S) E_i ∏_(1≤i≤L, i∉S) P_i,
```

which fixes the first m remaining-colour positions. These projections are pairwise orthogonal, and
their sum is 1 strongly: in the product state, fewer than m remaining positions has probability 0
because 1 − p > 0, and faithfulness turns zero complementary state mass into the zero projection
(this also follows from the negative-binomial sum below). Use adjacent Vᵢ and Vᵢ* to move the
remaining letters at j₁, …, j_m stably to the positions 1, …, m, each step crossing one deleted and
one remaining letter. The result `W_S ∈ B` is a partial isometry with initial projection C_S and
final projection

```text
D_(m,L) = E_1 ⋯ E_m P_(m+1) ⋯ P_L;
```

it preserves the order of all remaining letters, is the literal basis-word packing permutation on
that range, lies in the state centralizer, and the same stable packing is used for every m. For
x ∈ B(K_rest^(⊗m)), extended by zero off the range of E₁⋯E_m, define

```text
ι_m(x) = strong-Σ_S W_S* [x ⊗ P^(⊗(L−m))] W_S.
```

Every summand is supported on C_S, so the sum exists strongly, is bounded by ‖x‖, and defines a
unital *-homomorphism of the virtual m-site algebra. Refining the partition by the (m+1)-st
remaining position gives exactly `ι_(m+1)(x ⊗ 1) = ι_m(x)`, a basis-word identity since stable
packing preserves the order of the remaining letters; this constructs the whole virtual local tensor
algebra, not only its diagonal functions. Let φ_rest be the normalized product state on the
remaining colours, with weights `w_b/(1 − p)`. By centralizer cyclicity for `W_S`,

```text
φ(W_S* [x ⊗ P^(⊗(L−m))] W_S) = (1−p)^m p^(L−m) φ_rest^m(x),
```

there are binomial(L − 1, m − 1) masks with last position L, and
`Σ_(L≥m) binomial(L−1, m−1) (1−p)^m p^(L−m) = 1`, so `φ(ι_m(x)) = φ_rest^m(x)`; the map preserves *
and both state L² norms. It extends normally to the full remaining product von Neumann algebra: for
a bounded x take its bounded local product-expectation approximants xₙ, which converge in state L²
together with their adjoints; their images are uniformly bounded and state-L² Cauchy. On the
ambient standard-form Hilbert space the product vector Ω is separating, hence cyclic for the
commutant, and for y′ in that commutant `‖[ι(xₙ) − ι(x_m)]y′Ω‖ ≤ ‖y′‖ ‖[ι(xₙ) − ι(x_m)]Ω‖ → 0`.
Uniform boundedness then gives strong convergence on the whole space, and likewise for adjoints;
the strong-* limit is independent of the approximants, respects products and preserves the faithful
state, and monotone convergence of the state with faithfulness gives normality. This is an explicit
normal injective *-homomorphism; no ambient cyclicity of the smaller algebra is assumed.

The virtual adjacent checked-R generator is `ι_(r+1)(R_r^rest)`; its packed block is
`[E₁ ⋯ E_(r+1) P_(r+2) ⋯ P_L] R_r`, which lies in B and preserves the packed range, and conjugating by
W_S and summing proves `ι(R_r^rest) ∈ B`; by normality `ι(B_rest) ⊆ B`. The first-positive theorem of
§4.13, applied to the normalized remaining model, gives its literal first-site first-positive
projection `P_b^rest ∈ B_rest`, whose image is

```text
ι(P_b^rest) = strong-Σ_(j≥1) P_1 ⋯ P_(j−1) (P_b)_j.
```

Multiplication by E₁ kills every term with j > 1 and leaves `(P_b)₁`, so the next literal first-site
positive colour projection lies in B; the virtual first site is not assumed to be the original
first site. After deleting the first positive weight A the normalized remaining geometric
parameters are

```text
A_new = At/(1 − A),   B_new = B/(1 − A),   A_new + B_new = 1 − t,
```

and the R-order is still positive ω followed by negative ω*, so §4.13 applies again. Finite
iteration recovers every fixed positive colour, keeping the whole countable remaining alphabet at
each step, with no n-dependent finite truncation and no vanishing retained-word mass.

*Needs:* §§4.6, 4.13; VonNeumannAlgebras Layers 2 and 6.

**Checks.**

  `Eᵢ Pᵢ₊₁` keeps only `−√Q b_b ⊗ b₁`, and `−q⁻¹` turns it into `b_b ⊗ b₁` with coefficient +1.
- `Σ_(L≥m) binomial(L−1, m−1)(1−p)^m p^(L−m) = (1−p)^m (1 − p)^(−m) = 1`, the negative binomial
  series.

### 4.15 The inversion gauge on the phase fixed algebra

Let F be the full phase fixed algebra, defined independently of B. On an n-letter word put
`Jₙ = (−1)^(inv(word))`. For a local balanced matrix unit |u⟩⟨v| with equal colour multiplicities,
`inv(u·tail) − inv(v·tail) = inv(u) − inv(v)`, because every inversion between the prefix and the
tail depends only on those common multiplicities; so conjugation by Jₙ is compatible with
x ↦ x ⊗ 1 on every local balanced matrix unit. These units span the finite phase fixed algebras,
and Haar averaging with local approximation gives their weak density in F. Each Jₙ commutes with
the product density, so the compatible conjugations extend, by the bounded L² and adjoint
approximation of §4.14, to a state-preserving normal *-automorphism of F that fixes every site
colour projection. This does not assume F = B, and does not claim that Jₙ converges to a unitary on
the whole ambient tensor algebra; no such global unitary is needed.

*Needs:* §§4.6, 4.14.

**Checks.**
- Words u = ab and v = ba with a < b, tail c: inv(abc) − inv(bac) = −1 = inv(ab) − inv(ba) whatever
  c is, because c contributes the same inversions against the multiset {a, b} in both.

### 4.16 The negative sector at Q > 1 and the reduction of Q < 1

*The negative sector at Q > 1.* Replace each generator by `Q − 1 − Rᵢ`; this generates the same unital
algebra B. Reverse the total order of the colours and swap their parity. The new positive sector is
the old negative ω* sector reversed into ω, with first weight B, and the new negative sector is the
old positive ω sector reversed into ω*, with first Thoma weight A. Under the order-reversing
relabelling the complement generator has the block `[[Q − 1, +√Q], [+√Q, 0]]` on the new ascending
pair; apply the inversion gauge of §4.15 to change the off-diagonal sign to negative. Equal old
negative letters now have eigenvalue Q and equal old positive letters eigenvalue −1. These are
exactly the signed checked-R matrices at the same Q with geometric parameters (B, A), and the
phase-fixed, state-preserving isomorphism sends the old B to the new B and the literal old negative
colour projections to the corresponding new positive ones. If B > 0, apply §§4.13–4.14 in the new
model to recover every old negative projection; if B = 0 there is no supported negative colour.
This argument does not use the recovery of any positive projection of the original model; it uses
only the generator complement, order reversal, parity swap and the compatible gauge.

*Reduction of Q < 1 to Q′ > 1.* For Q < 1 put Q′ = 1/Q. Keep the total order of the colours, swap
the parity, and consider `−Rᵢ/Q`. On an ascending pair its block is `[[Q′ − 1, +√Q′], [+√Q′, 0]]`, and
the same gauge changes the off-diagonal sign to negative; equal old positive colours acquire
eigenvalue −1 and equal old negative colours eigenvalue Q′. This is the signed checked-R model with
parameter Q′ > 1. The old order for Q < 1 was negative ω followed by positive ω*, so after the parity
swap it is the new positive ω and negative ω* order. The geometric t is unchanged (t = Q = Q′⁻¹),
and the two Thoma lists exchange roles. The phase-fixed, state-preserving isomorphism takes B_old to
B_new, each transformed generator being a nonzero scalar multiple of its new generator, and fixes
the literal colour projections up to their explicit relabelling. So recovery of every projection
for Q′ > 1 gives recovery for Q < 1. The case Q = 1 uses the separate routes of §§4.3–4.4.

*Conclusion.* The construction recovers every supported signed colour at the literal first site, for
the whole continuous family and all Q > 0, Q ≠ 1. It proves the hypotheses of the stationary-shift
and balanced-matrix-unit argument of §4.6, hence B = F and its generator-labelled tail equality.
The finite discrete and scalar families keep their realization of §4.7. All the inclusion,
relative-commutant, corner, index and entropy calculations then apply on their stated carriers. No
conclusion about Q = 1 or a root-of-unity fibre is inferred by deforming this infinite countable
proof.

*Needs:* §§4.6, 4.13–4.15.

**Checks.**
- Complement on an old ascending pair a < b: `Q − 1 − [[Q − 1, −√Q], [−√Q, 0]] = [[0, √Q], [√Q, Q − 1]]`;
  in the reversed order the ascending pair is (b, a) and the block reads `[[Q − 1, √Q], [√Q, 0]]`;
  equal positive letters go to Q − 1 − Q = −1 and equal negative letters to Q − 1 + 1 = Q.
- `−R/Q` on an ascending pair: `[[(1 − Q)/Q, √Q/Q], [√Q/Q, 0]] = [[Q′ − 1, √Q′], [√Q′, 0]]` with
  Q′ = 1/Q; equal positive letters go to −1 and equal negative letters to `1/Q = Q′`.

### 4.17 The Q = 1 families and the scalar endpoints

At Q = 1 use the finite-colour Schur–Weyl models of §4.3 for the two discrete families and the
regular `S_∞` carrier of §4.4 for the collapsed point; the latter uses the specialization
α = β = 0, γ = 1 and the ordinary Plancherel coherent law. Prove for each fixed i, j that
`λ_i(n)/n → 0` and `λ′_j(n)/n → 0` almost surely, by permutation RSK and the Plancherel path law; this
is not obtained from the quantum-group weak-closure theorem at Q ≠ 1. For the projection parameter
η = 0 prove that every eᵢ is zero in the trace-GNS quotient, and for η = 1 that every eᵢ is one; in
both cases identify the ambient algebra and its shifted tail with ℂ, and compute index 1 and
relative entropy 0.

weak closure written there `R_(α,θ)`, in the hyperbolic parameters of §2.5: this is the tail
inclusion A₁ ⊂ B of §4.1, whose index and relative entropy are those of §4.18.

*Needs:* §§4.3, 4.4, 4.8, 4.9; SchurWeyl §7.2 (RSK); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.rowInsert`,

**Checks.**
