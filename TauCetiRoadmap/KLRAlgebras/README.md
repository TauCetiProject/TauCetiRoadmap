# Roadmap: KLR algebras and the categorification of quantum groups

**Summary.** Fix a symmetrizable Cartan datum on an index set `I`, not necessarily finite: a
generalized Cartan matrix `(aᵢⱼ)` with positive integers `dᵢ` such that `dᵢaᵢⱼ = dⱼaⱼᵢ`, and write
`(αᵢ, αⱼ) = dᵢaᵢⱼ`. Over a commutative ring `k`, choose, following Rouquier and Brundan, units
`tᵢⱼ ∈ kˣ` for `i ≠ j` with `tᵢⱼ = tⱼᵢ` when `aᵢⱼ = 0`, and scalars `sᵢⱼ^(pq) ∈ k` for
`0 ≤ p < −aᵢⱼ`, `0 ≤ q < −aⱼᵢ` with `sᵢⱼ^(pq) = sⱼᵢ^(qp)`, and put `Qᵢᵢ = 0` and

    Qᵢⱼ(u, v) = tᵢⱼ u^(−aᵢⱼ) + Σ_(p,q) sᵢⱼ^(pq) u^p v^q + tⱼᵢ v^(−aⱼᵢ)      (i ≠ j),

read as the constant `tᵢⱼ` when `aᵢⱼ = 0`. Then `Qᵢⱼ(u, v) = Qⱼᵢ(v, u)`. The parameters are
*homogeneous* when `sᵢⱼ^(pq) = 0` unless `pdᵢ + qdⱼ = −dᵢaᵢⱼ`, and *filtered* when
`sᵢⱼ^(pq) = 0` unless `pdᵢ + qdⱼ ≤ −dᵢaᵢⱼ` (the Newton polygon of `Qᵢⱼ` lies in the triangle with
vertices `(0,0)`, `(−aᵢⱼ,0)`, `(0,−aⱼᵢ)`). In finite type every choice is filtered.

For each `ν = Σ νᵢαᵢ` in `Q⁺` of height `n`, the **KLR algebra** (Khovanov–Lauda–Rouquier algebra, or quiver Hecke algebra) `R(ν)` is the
`k`-algebra generated by idempotents `e(𝐢)`, one for each sequence `𝐢 ∈ Iⁿ` of content `ν`,
together with `y₁, …, yₙ` and `ψ₁, …, ψₙ₋₁`, subject to the relations of §1.1. It is always filtered, and for homogeneous
parameters it is graded, with `y_r e(𝐢)` of degree `2d_(i_r)` and `ψ_r e(𝐢)` of degree
`−(α_(i_r), α_(i_(r+1)))`. When `I` is a
single point, `R(nα)` is the nilHecke algebra `NHₙ`, generated by multiplication by the variables
and the Demazure operators `∂_r f = (f − s_r f)/(x_r − x_(r+1))` on `k[x₁, …, xₙ]`.

The roadmap builds the theory of these algebras for every symmetrizable Cartan datum and every
choice of parameters. Everything that holds over `ℤ` is stated over an arbitrary commutative ring,
usually by proving it over Rouquier's universal parameter ring
`ℤ[tᵢⱼ^(±1), sᵢⱼ^(pq)]` and base changing; only statements that genuinely need a field (Krull–Schmidt,
simple modules, surjectivity in the categorification theorem) are stated over one. Its headline
theorems are these.

- *The basis theorem* (Khovanov–Lauda, Rouquier). `R(ν)` acts faithfully on
  `⊕_𝐢 k[y₁, …, yₙ]e(𝐢)`, and for any choice of a reduced expression `ŵ` for each permutation `w`,
  the elements `ψ_ŵ y₁^(a₁) ⋯ yₙ^(aₙ) e(𝐢)` form a `k`-basis of `R(ν)`. Its centre is the
  invariants of the symmetric group in `⊕_𝐢 k[y]e(𝐢)`, and `R(ν)` is free of finite rank over it.
- *The nilHecke algebra is a matrix algebra.* `k[x₁, …, xₙ]` is free of rank `n!` over the
  symmetric polynomials `Λₙ`, and the action is an isomorphism `NHₙ ≅ End_(Λₙ)(k[x₁, …, xₙ])`.
- *Categorification of `f`* (Khovanov–Lauda, Rouquier). Over a field, the split Grothendieck group
  of finitely generated graded projective modules over `R = ⊕_ν R(ν)`, with product given by
  induction and `[P⟨1⟩] = q[P]`, is isomorphic as a `ℤ[q, q⁻¹]`-algebra to Lusztig's integral form
  `_𝒜f` of the positive half of the quantum group, by a map sending the divided power `θᵢ^(m)` to
  the class of the projective `R(mαᵢ)e(i^(m))`. Under it Lusztig's bilinear form is the graded
  dimension of `P^ω ⊗_R P′`.
- *Simple modules.* The graded simple modules of `R(ν)` are finite-dimensional, absolutely
  irreducible and self-dual up to shift, their number is `dim f_ν`, and their formal characters
  are linearly independent.
- *Cyclotomic quotients* (Kang–Kashiwara). For a dominant integral weight `Λ`, the quotient
  `R^Λ(ν)` of `R(ν)` by the two-sided ideal generated by the `y₁^(⟨hᵢ₁, Λ⟩) e(𝐢)` is
  finite-dimensional, and the induction and restriction functors along `R^Λ(ν) → R^Λ(ν + αᵢ)` are
  exact and make the Grothendieck group of `⊕_ν R^Λ(ν)`-projectives into the integrable module
  `V(Λ)` over the quantum group, integrally.

The roadmap rests on three suppliers. **KacMoody** supplies the generalized Cartan matrix and
its symmetrization. **QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras** supplies Lusztig's algebra `f`, its bilinear form
and the quantum Serre relations, which are the target of the categorification theorem, and the
Verma modules and character formula used for `V(Λ)`. **GrothendieckEulerForms**, with the graded
module categories and Laurent Grothendieck groups already in Tau Ceti, supplies `K₀` and `G₀` of
graded modules. Mathlib supplies symmetric polynomials and the fundamental theorem of symmetric
polynomials, and Tau Ceti the type-`A` Coxeter system and Matsumoto's theorem. **Foams** works
over the same `Λ_N = ℤ[e₁, …, e_N]`, with `deg Xⱼ = 2`, and builds the partially symmetric rings
`A_(a,N)` free over `Λ_N` (the Grassmannian case); Layer 0 here builds the complete flag case,
with Demazure operators, which Foams does not.

The layers, in build order:

- **Layer 0** builds Demazure operators on polynomial rings, the nilHecke algebra, and its
  structure as a matrix algebra over symmetric polynomials. It has no colours and no Cartan datum.
- **Layer 1** defines the KLR algebra by generators and relations, its grading, its
  anti-involution and symmetries, and its behaviour under base change and under change of `Q`.
- **Layer 2** proves the polynomial representation, faithfulness, the basis theorem and the centre.
- **Layer 3** builds induction and restriction, the Mackey filtration, and divided-power
  idempotents.
- **Layer 4** builds Lusztig's form by counting diagrams, and the simple modules and characters
  of `R(ν)`, with the operators `εᵢ`, `ẽᵢ`.
- **Layer 5** proves the categorification `_𝒜f ≅ K₀(R)`: injectivity over any ring, surjectivity
  over a field, and the dual statement for finite-dimensional modules.
- **Layer 6** treats cyclotomic quotients.

## Conventions

- `I` is a type with `DecidableEq`, not assumed finite: `R(ν)` only sees the finite support of `ν`.
  Layers 4–6 consume suppliers stated for finite `I` and prove their results for finite `I`
  first, then for arbitrary `I` as a filtered colimit over finite subsets (both sides commute with
  restriction to a full subdatum). The Cartan datum is the one of KacMoody Layer 0, read
  with the row convention `aᵢⱼ = ⟨αⱼ, hᵢ⟩` of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras. The symmetric form on
  `Q = I →₀ ℤ` is `(αᵢ, αⱼ) = dᵢaᵢⱼ`; it is never normalized so that short roots have length
  `2` unless the datum is so normalized.
- `Q⁺ = I →₀ ℕ`, as in QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras. For `ν ∈ Q⁺` of height `n`, `Seq(ν)` is the
  set of `𝐢 : Fin n → I` with content `ν`; `Sₙ` acts on it by `(w𝐢)_r = 𝐢_(w⁻¹ r)`, and `s_r`
  is the transposition of `r` and `r + 1`.
- **The relations and parameters** are those of Rouquier, *Quiver Hecke algebras and 2-Lie
  algebras* §3.2.2 (any commutative ring, `Q` not necessarily homogeneous), with Rouquier's and
  Brundan's (*On the definition of Kac–Moody 2-category*, §1) parametrization of `Q` by `tᵢⱼ`,
  `sᵢⱼ^(pq)`: `ψ_r y_(r+1) e(𝐢) = (y_r ψ_r + δ_(i_r i_(r+1))) e(𝐢)`, and the braid defect is the
  divided difference of `Q` in §1.1. These are the conventions references; Khovanov–Lauda's
  presentation, with its orientation signs, is an instance (§1.3).
- **Composition** is written algebraically; when diagrams are used in a docstring, `ab` is `a`
  stacked above `b` (as in Khovanov–Lauda), and strands are numbered left to right.
- **Grading and shift.** `M⟨1⟩` is `M` with degrees raised by one, `M⟨1⟩_d = M_(d−1)`, and
  `[M⟨1⟩] = q[M]` in Grothendieck groups, as in GrothendieckEulerForms and Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.laurentK0_projective_of_shiftObj`.
- **Quantum parameter and graded dimension.** `𝒜 = ℤ[q, q⁻¹]`. The graded dimension of a
  graded space with finite-dimensional pieces, zero in sufficiently negative degree, is
  `gdim M = Σ_d (dim M_d) q^d ∈ ℤ((q))`; so `gdim k[y] = 1/(1 − q^(2dᵢ))` for `deg y = 2dᵢ`. This is
  the convention forced by GrothendieckEulerForms, whose `qdim Hom(M, N) = Σⱼ q⁻ʲ dim Hom(M, N{j})`
  gives `qdim Hom(R(ν)e(𝐢), N) = gdim e(𝐢)N`. The form on `f` used here is the one with
  `(θᵢ, θᵢ) = (1 − qᵢ²)⁻¹`, as in Khovanov–Lauda; it is the form of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §1.0
  (normalization `(1 − qᵢ⁻²)⁻¹`) transported along the ring automorphism `q ↦ q⁻¹` of `ℚ(q)`. Both
  have the same radical (QEA §1.0, the forms for `χ` and `χ⁻¹`), so `f` itself is unchanged; the
  comparison is a single stated lemma (§4.1), and nothing else in the roadmap mixes the two forms.
- Modules are graded left modules; "projective" means finitely generated graded projective.
  The anti-involution `ω` (§1.2) turns right modules into left modules.
- **Coefficients.** Layers 0–3, §4.1, the well-definedness and injectivity of `γ` in §5.1, and
  the definition and freeness of the cyclotomic quotients in §6.2 are over an arbitrary commutative
  ring, the last for filtered parameters. Grading-dependent statements assume homogeneous
  parameters. The rest of Layers 4–6 is over a field.

## Layer 0: Demazure operators and the nilHecke algebra (KLR0)

### 0.1 Demazure operators

For a commutative ring `k` and `n : ℕ`, let `Polₙ = MvPolynomial (Fin n) k`, with `Sₙ` acting
by permuting variables. Define `∂_r : Polₙ → Polₙ` for `1 ≤ r < n` by
`∂_r f = (f − s_r f)/(x_r − x_(r+1))`, the quotient being exact.

- `∂_r` is `Polₙ^(s_r)`-linear, satisfies the twisted Leibniz rule
  `∂_r(fg) = ∂_r(f) g + (s_r f) ∂_r(g)`, kills `Polₙ^(s_r)`, and has image in it.
- `∂_r² = 0`; `∂_r∂_s = ∂_s∂_r` for `|r − s| > 1`; `∂_r∂_(r+1)∂_r = ∂_(r+1)∂_r∂_(r+1)`.
- `x_r ∂_r − ∂_r x_(r+1) = 1 = ∂_r x_r − x_(r+1) ∂_r` as operators.
- For `w ∈ Sₙ` and any reduced word `ŵ`, `∂_ŵ` depends only on `w` (`∂_w`); `∂_ŵ = 0` for a
  non-reduced word. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.permCoxeterSystem` (the type-`A` Coxeter system on
  `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, `TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/Coxeter.lean`) and `TauCeti.matsumoto`
  (`TauCeti/GroupTheory/Coxeter/Matsumoto.lean`), whose monoid form gives `∂_w` directly from the
  braid relations; Mathlib's `CoxeterSystem` lists Matsumoto only as a TODO.
- `∂_(w₀)` is the symmetrizer `f ↦ Σ_w (−1)^ℓ(w) w(f) / Π_(r<s)(x_r − x_s)`.

**Checks.** `∂₁(x₁) = 1`, `∂₁(x₁²) = x₁ + x₂`, `∂₁(x₁x₂) = 0`; in `n = 3`,
`∂_(w₀)(x₁²x₂) = 1`.

### 0.2 Polynomials over symmetric polynomials

Let `Λₙ = Polₙ^(Sₙ)` (Mathlib `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra`).

- `Polₙ` is a free `Λₙ`-module of rank `n!`, with basis the monomials `x^a`,
  `0 ≤ a_r ≤ n − r`, and also with basis the Schubert polynomials `𝔖_w`.
- The pairing `(f, g) ↦ ∂_(w₀)(fg)` is a perfect `Λₙ`-bilinear pairing on `Polₙ`.
- With `deg x_r = 2`, the graded rank of `Polₙ` over `Λₙ` is
  `Σ_(w ∈ Sₙ) q^(2ℓ(w)) = Π_(r=1)^n (1 − q^(2r))/(1 − q²) = q^(n(n−1)/2) [n]!`, where `[n]!` is the
  symmetric quantum factorial of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §0.2 (`[r] = (q^r − q^(−r))/(q − q⁻¹)`).
  Check: `n = 2` gives `1 + q² = q[2]`.

### 0.3 The nilHecke algebra

Define `NHₙ` as the `k`-algebra presented by `x₁, …, xₙ`, `∂₁, …, ∂ₙ₋₁` and the relations of 0.1,
and its action on `Polₙ`.

- The action is faithful; `{x^a ∂_w}` is a `k`-basis; `NHₙ ≅ End_(Λₙ)(Polₙ)`, so `NHₙ` is a
  matrix algebra of size `n!` over `Λₙ`. The centre of `NHₙ` is `Λₙ`.
- `eₙ = x₁^(n−1)x₂^(n−2) ⋯ xₙ₋₁ ∂_(w₀)` is an idempotent (since `∂_(w₀)(x₁^(n−1) ⋯ xₙ₋₁) = 1`),
  `a ↦ a(x₁^(n−1) ⋯ xₙ₋₁)` is an isomorphism `NHₙeₙ ≅ Polₙ⟨−n(n−1)⟩`, and `NHₙ ≅ (NHₙeₙ)^(⊕ n!)`
  as ungraded left modules. With gradings (`deg x_r = 2`, `deg ∂_r = −2`), let
  `Pₙ = NHₙeₙ⟨n(n−1)/2⟩ ≅ Polₙ⟨−n(n−1)/2⟩`; then `NHₙ ≅ Pₙ^(⊕[n]!)`, meaning `⊕_d Pₙ⟨d⟩^(⊕c_d)`
  with `Σ c_d q^d = [n]!` (Khovanov–Lauda I §2.2; Rouquier, *2-Kac–Moody algebras*, §4.1.1). Proof
  of the count: `gdim NHₙ = gdim Polₙ · Σ_w q^(−2ℓ(w)) = q^(−n(n−1)/2)[n]! gdim Polₙ`.

**Checks.** `NH₁ = k[x]`. `gdim NH₂ = (1 + q⁻²)/(1 − q²)²` and `gdim P₂ = q⁻¹/(1 − q²)²`, so that
`gdim P₂⟨1⟩ + gdim P₂⟨−1⟩ = gdim NH₂`; the opposite shift `P₂ = NH₂e₂⟨−1⟩` would give
`gdim P₂ = q⁻³/(1 − q²)²` and fail this. `NH₂` has rank 4 over `Λ₂ = k[x₁ + x₂, x₁x₂]`. `e₂ = x₁∂₁` is an
idempotent with complement `1 − x₁∂₁ = −∂₁x₂`; `x₂∂₁` is not an idempotent (`(x₂∂₁)² = −x₂∂₁`).

### Dependencies

Mathlib `MvPolynomial`, `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra`, the fundamental theorem of
symmetric polynomials (`MvPolynomial.esymmAlgEquiv`), `CoxeterSystem`, `Module.Free`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.permCoxeterSystem`, `TauCeti.matsumoto`.

## Layer 1: the KLR algebra (KLR1)

### 1.1 Data and presentation

Define a **KLR datum** over `k`: a Cartan datum as in Conventions and parameters `tᵢⱼ`, `sᵢⱼ^(pq)`
as in the summary, with `Q` built from them. The universal datum lives over
`k_C = ℤ[tᵢⱼ^(±1), sᵢⱼ^(pq)]` (Rouquier §3.3.3), and every KLR datum is a base change of it. For `ν ∈ Q⁺` of height `n`, define
`R(ν)` as the quotient (`RingQuot`) of the free `k`-algebra on `e(𝐢)` (`𝐢 ∈ Seq(ν)`, a finite set
even when `I` is infinite), `y_r` (`1 ≤ r ≤ n`) and `ψ_r` (`1 ≤ r < n`) by:

    e(𝐢)e(𝐣) = δ_(𝐢𝐣) e(𝐢),   Σ_𝐢 e(𝐢) = 1,
    y_r e(𝐢) = e(𝐢) y_r,    ψ_r e(𝐢) = e(s_r𝐢) ψ_r,
    y_r y_s = y_s y_r,
    ψ_r y_s = y_s ψ_r                      (s ≠ r, r+1),
    ψ_r ψ_s = ψ_s ψ_r                      (|r − s| > 1),
    ψ_r y_(r+1) e(𝐢) = (y_r ψ_r + δ_(i_r i_(r+1))) e(𝐢),
    y_(r+1) ψ_r e(𝐢) = (ψ_r y_r + δ_(i_r i_(r+1))) e(𝐢),
    ψ_r² e(𝐢) = Q_(i_r i_(r+1))(y_r, y_(r+1)) e(𝐢),
    (ψ_(r+1)ψ_rψ_(r+1) − ψ_rψ_(r+1)ψ_r) e(𝐢)
        = δ_(i_r i_(r+2)) · (Q_(i_r i_(r+1))(y_(r+2), y_(r+1)) − Q_(i_r i_(r+1))(y_r, y_(r+1)))/(y_(r+2) − y_r) · e(𝐢).

The quotient in the last line is a polynomial; define it as such (a divided difference of 0.1
applied in the first variable), not as a fraction. For finite `I`, the algebra `Rₙ` presented in
the same way with all `𝐢 ∈ Iⁿ` is `Π_(|ν| = n) R(ν)`; nothing in the roadmap needs `Rₙ` for
infinite `I`.

**Checks.**

- `I` a point: `R(nα) ≅ NHₙ`, by `y_r ↦ x_r`, `ψ_r ↦ −∂_r` (so that `ψ_r y_(r+1) − y_r ψ_r = 1`
  matches `x_r ∂_r − ∂_r x_(r+1) = 1`). `ψ_r ↦ ∂_r` is not a homomorphism; it gets this sign wrong.
- `aᵢⱼ = 0`, `Qᵢⱼ = 1`: `R(αᵢ + αⱼ)` is a matrix algebra `Mat₂(k[y₁, y₂])`.
- `aᵢⱼ = −1`, `tᵢⱼ = 1`, `tⱼᵢ = −1`, so `Qᵢⱼ(u, v) = u − v` and `Qⱼᵢ(u, v) = v − u` (type `A₂`):
  the braid defect is `1` on `e(iji)` and `−1` on `e(jij)`.
- A non-example for the unit condition: `aᵢⱼ = 0` with `tᵢⱼ` replaced by `0`, so `Qᵢⱼ = 0`. Then
  `ψ₁e(ij)` and `ψ₁e(ji)` have product `0` both ways, `R(αᵢ + αⱼ)e(ij)` and `R(αᵢ + αⱼ)e(ji)` are
  non-isomorphic (the only degree-zero maps between them are multiples of `ψ₁`, modulo `y`'s), and
  `θᵢθⱼ = θⱼθᵢ` fails in `K₀`. With `tᵢⱼ` a unit, `ψ₁e(ij)` is an isomorphism.

### 1.2 Grading, anti-involution and symmetries

- `Rₙ` is filtered with `e(𝐢)`, `y_r` in degree 0 and `ψ_r` in degree 1, and its associated graded
  is the nil-wreath product `k[y]^I ≀ NH⁰ₙ`, in which `ψ_r² = 0`, `ψ_r y = s_r(y) ψ_r` and the
  braid relations hold (Rouquier Prop. 3.7, the PBW property).
- For filtered parameters, `Rₙ` is filtered by the degree of the summary (the extra terms of `Q`
  have at most the top degree), and its associated graded is `R(ν)` for the homogeneous parameters
  obtained by deleting the `sᵢⱼ^(pq)` with `pdᵢ + qdⱼ < −dᵢaᵢⱼ`.
- For homogeneous parameters, `Rₙ` is a `ℤ`-graded algebra (Mathlib `GradedAlgebra`) with the
  degrees of the summary; all relations are homogeneous.
- The anti-automorphism `ω` fixing every generator; the automorphism reversing strands
  (`e(𝐢) ↦ e(𝐢^rev)`, `y_r ↦ y_(n+1−r)`, `ψ_r ↦ −ψ_(n−r)`), for the same `Q`. The sign is needed:
  it exchanges the two dot–crossing relations, and with `ψ_r ↦ +ψ_(n−r)` they fail.
- An automorphism of the Cartan datum compatible with `Q` acts on `R`.
- Base change: `k′ ⊗_k R(ν; Q) ≅ R(ν; k′ ⊗ Q)`.

### 1.3 Dependence on Q

- Rescaling: if `Q′ᵢⱼ(u, v) = cᵢⱼ Qᵢⱼ(u, v)` with `cᵢⱼ = cⱼᵢ ∈ kˣ`, and the `cᵢⱼ` factor as
  `cᵢⱼ = λᵢⱼλⱼᵢ`, then `ψ_r e(𝐢) ↦ λ_(i_r i_(r+1)) ψ_r e(𝐢)` is an isomorphism `R(Q) ≅ R(Q′)`.
- Khovanov–Lauda's algebras are instances with `sᵢⱼ^(pq) = 0`. Khovanov–Lauda I (simply laced) has
  `Qᵢⱼ(u, v) = u + v` for `aᵢⱼ = −1`. Khovanov–Lauda II, final section, has, for an orientation of
  the Dynkin graph and units `τᵢⱼ`, `τⱼᵢ` per oriented edge `i → j`, the instance
  `tᵢⱼ = τᵢⱼ`, `tⱼᵢ = −τⱼᵢ`, i.e. `Qᵢⱼ(u, v) = τᵢⱼ u^(−aᵢⱼ) − τⱼᵢ v^(−aⱼᵢ)` (read off from their
  polynomial representation: `ψ e(ij)` acts by `(τᵢⱼ x_(k+1) − τⱼᵢ x_k) s_k` and `ψ e(ji)` by `s_k`).
  Reversing an edge and negating both `τ`s is the identity on generators; rescaling the crossings
  shows that with `sᵢⱼ^(pq) = 0` the algebra depends only on the products of `tᵢⱼ/tⱼᵢ` around the
  cycles of the Dynkin graph, so when the graph is a forest all such choices give isomorphic
  algebras.
- Brundan's renormalization (*On the definition of Kac–Moody 2-category*, Theorem 2.3) of the
  parameters `tᵢⱼ`, `sᵢⱼ^(pq)`, as an isomorphism of KLR algebras.

## Layer 2: the polynomial representation and the basis theorem (KLR2)

- Define the action of `Rₙ` on `Polₙ^I = ⊕_𝐢 k[y₁, …, yₙ]e(𝐢)`: `y_r` by multiplication, and
  `ψ_r e(𝐢)` by the Demazure operator when `i_r = i_(r+1)` and by `s_r` followed by multiplication
  by a factor of `Q` otherwise (Rouquier §3.2.3: order `I`, put `Pᵢⱼ = Qᵢⱼ` and `Pⱼᵢ = 1` for `i < j`, and let `ψ_r e(𝐢)` act by
  `P_(i_r i_(r+1))(y_(r+1), y_r) s_r` when `i_r ≠ i_(r+1)`, and by `−∂_r` when they are equal;
  Khovanov–Lauda §2.3).
  Prove the relations hold.
- Faithfulness, and the basis theorem of the summary, for every choice of reduced expressions.
- `R(ν)` is free of finite rank over `Z(ν) = (Polₙ^I)^(Sₙ)` restricted to `Seq(ν)`, which is its
  centre; the graded rank is an explicit Laurent polynomial. `R(ν)` is Noetherian, and its graded
  pieces are finite free `k`-modules, zero in sufficiently negative degree.

**Checks.** For `aᵢⱼ = −1`, `gdim R(αᵢ + αⱼ) = 2(1 + q)/(1 − q²)²` (simply laced, `dᵢ = 1`).
`gdim R(2αᵢ) = (1 + q^(−2dᵢ))/(1 − q^(2dᵢ))²`, agreeing with `NH₂` under Layer 0.

## Layer 3: induction, restriction and divided powers (KLR3)

- The non-unital embedding `R(ν) ⊗ R(ν′) → R(ν + ν′)` (concatenation), the induction functor
  `Ind_(ν,ν′) = R(ν + ν′)e(ν, ν′) ⊗_(R(ν)⊗R(ν′)) −`, its associativity, and restriction.
  Induction and restriction are exact and preserve finitely generated projectives.
- `R(ν + ν′)e(ν, ν′)` is free as a right `R(ν) ⊗ R(ν′)`-module, with basis indexed by minimal
  coset representatives.
- The Mackey theorem: `Res_(λ,λ′) ∘ Ind_(ν,ν′)` has a filtration with subquotients
  `Ind ∘ twist ∘ Res`, indexed by double cosets, with explicit grading shifts (Khovanov–Lauda I
  §2.6).
- Divided powers: `e(i^(m)) ∈ R(mαᵢ)`, the image of `eₘ` under Layer 0; `R(mαᵢ)e(iᵐ)` is a sum of
  shifted copies of `P(i^(m)) = R(mαᵢ)e(i^(m))` with multiplicity `[m]ᵢ!`.
- The Serre decomposition: for `i ≠ j` and `m = 1 − aᵢⱼ`,
  `⊕_(r even) P(i^(r) j i^(m−r)) ≅ ⊕_(r odd) P(i^(r) j i^(m−r))`, with grading shifts.
  Over any commutative ring and for all parameters, this is Rouquier, *2-Kac–Moody algebras*,
  Prop. 4.2: a complex of the `P(i^(m−r)ji^(r))` with explicit maps, built from crossings and
  `tⱼᵢ⁻¹`, that is split exact (homotopy equivalent to `0`); the graded version follows it. Special
  cases: Khovanov–Lauda I Prop. 2.13 (simply laced), Khovanov–Lauda II Prop. 6 and Cor. 7.

## Layer 4: the form, simple modules and characters (KLR4)

This layer builds everything the categorification theorem of Layer 5 consumes, in the order of
Khovanov–Lauda I §§2.5 and 3.2. Homogeneous parameters throughout; §4.1 is combinatorial (values in `ℤ((q))`), the
rest over a field `k`.

### 4.1 The form by counting diagrams

On the free algebra `′f` over `𝒜` on the `θᵢ`, with words `θ_𝐢`, define the form combinatorially:
`(θ_𝐢, θ_𝐣) = Σ_(w ∈ Sₙ, w𝐢 = 𝐣) q^(deg(ψ_w e(𝐢))) · Π_r (1 − q^(2d_(i_r)))⁻¹`, the generating
function of the basis of Layer 2 restricted to `e(𝐣)R(ν)e(𝐢)`, so that
`(θ_𝐢, θ_𝐣) = gdim e(𝐣)R(ν)e(𝐢)`. The degree of `ψ_w e(𝐢)` is `−Σ (α_(i_a), α_(i_b))` over the
inversions `(a, b)` of `w`, so this sum is defined directly, independent of Layer 2, and Layer 2's
basis theorem is what identifies it with a graded dimension.

- The form is symmetric and satisfies `(xy, z) = (x ⊗ y, r(z))` for Lusztig's twisted coproduct
  on `′f ⊗ ′f` with the multiplication `(x₁ ⊗ x₂)(x₁′ ⊗ x₂′) = q^(−(|x₂|,|x₁′|)) x₁x₁′ ⊗ x₂x₂′`
  (Khovanov–Lauda II, p. 1; QEA's `χ(|y|, |x′|) = q^((|y|,|x′|))` under `q ↦ q⁻¹`); hence it is the transported QEA form
  (Conventions), by the uniqueness clause of QEA §1.0 (`freePairing_characterisation`).
- Define `_𝒜f` as the `𝒜`-subalgebra of `f` over `ℚ(q)` generated by the divided powers
  `θᵢ^(m) = θᵢᵐ/[m]ᵢ!`. Freeness of `_𝒜f` over `𝒜` with `ℚ(q) ⊗ _𝒜f = f` is a consequence of 5.2,
  not an input to it.

**Checks.** `(θᵢ, θᵢ) = 1/(1 − qᵢ²)`; `(θᵢθⱼ, θⱼθᵢ) = q^(−(αᵢ, αⱼ))/((1 − qᵢ²)(1 − qⱼ²))`;
`(θᵢ², θᵢ²) = (1 + qᵢ⁻²)/(1 − qᵢ²)²`, the graded dimension of `NH₂` (Layer 2 Checks).

### 4.2 Simple modules

- `R(ν)` is a finitely generated module over its centre `Z(ν)` (Layer 2), and `Z(ν)` is
  non-negatively graded with `Z(ν)₀ = k`. Hence every graded simple `R(ν)`-module is killed by
  `Z(ν)₊`, is finite-dimensional, and there are finitely many up to isomorphism and shift.
- Every graded simple is absolutely irreducible, so the set of simples is unchanged by field
  extension. Each simple `L` has exactly one shift with `L^⊛ ≅ L`, where `M^⊛ = Hom_k(M, k)` with
  the action twisted by `ω` (§1.2).
- Every finitely generated graded projective is a finite sum of shifts of projective covers `P_L`
  of simples; `K₀(R(ν))` is free over `𝒜` on the `[P_L]` with `L` self-dual, `G₀(R(ν))` is free on
  the `[L]`, and `⟨[P], [M]⟩ = gdim Hom(P, M)` is a perfect pairing with `⟨[P_L], [L′]⟩ = δ_(LL′)`.
  *Needs:* §4.2 first item; graded Krull–Schmidt for algebras with finite-dimensional graded pieces
  bounded below; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.LaurentK0` of `TauCeti.gradedFiniteProjectiveModulesExactStructure`
  and of `TauCeti.gradedFiniteModulesExactStructure` for `K₀` and `G₀`, and `TauCeti.gradedCartanMap`.

### 4.3 Characters

- The character `ch M = Σ_(𝐢 ∈ Seq(ν)) gdim(e(𝐢)M) · 𝐢` in the free `𝒜`-module on `Seq(ν)`. It is
  additive on short exact sequences, so defined on `G₀`, and turns induction into the quantum
  shuffle product (*Needs:* the Mackey filtration of Layer 3).
- For a simple `L`, `εᵢ(L)` is the largest `m` with `e(ν − mαᵢ, mαᵢ)L ≠ 0`. With `m = εᵢ(L)`,
  `e(ν − mαᵢ, mαᵢ)L ≅ L′ ⊠ L(iᵐ)` up to shift as an `R(ν − mαᵢ) ⊗ R(mαᵢ)`-module, for a simple `L′`
  with `εᵢ(L′) = 0`; here `L(iᵐ)` is the unique simple `NHₘ`-module. Write `ẽᵢ^m L = L′`; the head of
  `Ind(L′ ⊠ L(iᵐ))` is `L`, and all its other composition factors `L″` have `εᵢ(L″) < m`.
  *Needs:* Layer 0 (§0.3: `NHₘ` has a unique graded simple module up to shift), Layer 3 (Mackey).
- `ch` is injective on `G₀(R(ν))`. Proof by induction on `ν`, using the previous item: a minimal
  counterexample is separated by `εᵢ` for some `i`.
- The `ẽᵢ`, `f̃ᵢL = hd Ind(L ⊠ L(i))` on simples, with `ẽᵢf̃ᵢL ≅ L`, are part of this item; their
  crystal-theoretic identification is outside this roadmap (Scope).

**Checks.** `R(αᵢ) = k[y]` has one simple `L(i) = k`, `ch L(i) = i`. For `aᵢⱼ = −1`, `R(αᵢ + αⱼ)` has
two simples, with characters `ij` and `ji`; for `aᵢⱼ = 0` it has one, with character `ij + ji`.

## Layer 5: categorification of the positive half (KLR5)

`K₀(R_k)` is the split graded Grothendieck group of finitely generated graded projective
`R(ν)`-modules over `k`, summed over `ν`: for each `ν`, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.LaurentK0` of
`TauCeti.gradedFiniteProjectiveModulesExactStructure` for the grading of §1.2 (GrothendieckEulerForms
§§2.1, 2.6), an `𝒜`-module by grading shift, and an `𝒜`-algebra by induction (Layer 3). Homogeneous parameters throughout.

### 5.1 The map γ over any ring

Over an arbitrary commutative ring `k` with `Spec k` connected (so that graded ranks of finitely
generated projective `k`-modules are defined):

- The form `([P], [P′]) = grank_k(P^ω ⊗_R P′)` on `K₀(R_k)`, with values in `ℤ((q))`; it satisfies
  Lusztig's adjunction for induction and restriction.
- The algebra map `γ : _𝒜f → K₀(R_k)`, `θᵢ^(m) ↦ [P(i^(m))]`, well-defined by the Serre
  decomposition of Layer 3, which holds over `ℤ`. `γ` is an isometry for the form of §4.1, hence
  injective, since that form is non-degenerate on `_𝒜f`. Base change `K₀(R_k) → K₀(R_(k′))` commutes
  with `γ`. Under `γ`, restriction corresponds to Lusztig's twisted coproduct `r`.
  *Needs:* §4.1, Layer 3; QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §1.0 (`f` and the Serre elements, over `ℚ(q)`).

### 5.2 The isomorphism over a field

- `γ` is surjective, hence an isomorphism `_𝒜f ≅ K₀(R)`. Proof: show `[P_L] ∈ im γ` for every
  self-dual simple `L`, by induction on `ν` and, for fixed `ν`, downward induction on
  `εᵢ(L)` for an `i` with `εᵢ(L) > 0`. With `m = εᵢ(L)` and `L′ = ẽᵢ^m L`,
  `Ind(P_(L′) ⊠ P(i^(m)))` is `P_L` plus projective covers of simples `L″` with `εᵢ(L″) > m`
  (dual to §4.3's head statement), all of whose classes are in `im γ` by induction.
  *Needs:* §4.2, §4.3, 5.1.
- Consequences: the number of self-dual graded simples of `R(ν)` is `dim f_ν`; `_𝒜f` is free over `𝒜`
  with `ℚ(q) ⊗ _𝒜f = f`; `G₀(R)` is identified with the graded dual of `_𝒜f`, and `ch` with the
  embedding of that dual into the quantum shuffle algebra.

## Layer 6: cyclotomic quotients (KLR6)

### 6.1 The integrable module and its integral form

Fix a Cartan datum `(X, Y)` of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §0.1 with the given symmetrized Cartan
matrix. Over `ℚ(q)`, for dominant integral `Λ ∈ X`, let `V(Λ) = M(Λ)/Σᵢ U Fᵢ^(⟨Λ,hᵢ⟩+1) v_Λ`, the quotient of
the Verma module of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §2.3 (every symmetrizable type).

- `V(Λ)` is integrable and lies in `O(U)`; every integrable highest-weight module of highest weight
  `Λ` is a quotient of it. Since QEA §2.3 gives all of them the same character, each such quotient
  is an isomorphism; in particular `V(Λ)` is simple.
- Define `V_𝒜(Λ) = _𝒜f · v_Λ`, with `_𝒜f` from §4.1 acting through `θᵢ ↦ Fᵢ`. It is stable under
  every `Fᵢ^(m)` and every toral generator of `U_𝒜` (QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §3.1, which holds in
  every symmetrizable type).
- Prove in `U`, in every symmetrizable type, the rank-one commutation formula
  `Eᵢ^(a)Fᵢ^(b) = Σ_t Fᵢ^(b−t) [Kᵢ; 2t − a − b choose t] Eᵢ^(a−t)` (Lusztig 1993, Cor. 3.1.9), with
  coefficients the toral binomials of QEA §3.1, and `Eᵢ^(a)Fⱼ^(b) = Fⱼ^(b)Eᵢ^(a)` for `i ≠ j`. Since
  `Eᵢ v_Λ = 0`, moving `Eᵢ^(a)` to the right through a monomial in divided powers `Fⱼ^(b)` shows
  `V_𝒜(Λ)` is stable under every `Eᵢ^(a)`, hence a `U_𝒜`-submodule of `V(Λ)`. This does not use
  the integral triangular decomposition of `U_𝒜`, which QEA proves only in finite type (§3.2).
- In finite type, `V_𝒜(Λ)` is the lattice `V_A(λ)` of QuantumEnvelopingAlgebras §4.1 (both are
  `U_𝒜⁻ v_Λ`; mind the `q ↦ q⁻¹` of Conventions if the comparison passes through the form).

**Checks.** `A₁`, `Λ = 2ω`: `V_𝒜(Λ)` has basis `v, Fv, F^(2)v`, and `Fv` is not divisible by
`[2]` in it. `Λ = 0`: `V_𝒜(0) = 𝒜`.

### 6.2 Cyclotomic quotients

- `R^Λ(ν) = R(ν)/⟨ y₁^(⟨hᵢ₁, Λ⟩) e(𝐢) ⟩` over any commutative ring; over a field it is
  finite-dimensional (Kang–Kashiwara).
- Kang–Kashiwara: `R^Λ(ν + αᵢ)e(ν, i)` is projective over `R^Λ(ν)`; the functors `Eᵢ`, `Fᵢ` on graded
  `R^Λ`-modules satisfy the `sl₂` commutation relations up to explicit graded multiplicities.
- Divided-power functors `Eᵢ^(m)`, `Fᵢ^(m)`, cut out by the idempotent `e(i^(m))` of Layer 3, with
  `Eᵢ^m ≅ Eᵢ^(m)^(⊕[m]ᵢ!)` and likewise for `Fᵢ`.
- `ℚ(q) ⊗ K₀(R^Λ)` is a `U`-module through `[Eᵢ]`, `[Fᵢ]` and the weight grading, and the map
  sending `[R^Λ(0)]` to `v_Λ` is an isomorphism onto `V(Λ)` (by 6.1). It restricts to an isomorphism
  `K₀(R^Λ) ≅ V_𝒜(Λ)`: the image is `_𝒜f · v_Λ` because `K₀(R) → K₀(R^Λ)` is surjective and Layer 5
  identifies `K₀(R)` with `_𝒜f`. Surjectivity: graded indecomposable projectives of a graded algebra with
  finite-dimensional graded pieces are `Ae⟨d⟩` for primitive idempotents `e` of the degree-zero part,
  since degree-zero endomorphisms of `Ae` form `eA₀e`; and `R(ν)₀ → R^Λ(ν)₀` is a surjection of
  finite-dimensional algebras, along which idempotents lift. Hence `V_𝒜(Λ)` is free over `𝒜`, and the isomorphism intertwines the
  divided-power functors with the action of 6.1.
- For homogeneous parameters, `R^Λ(ν)` is free of finite rank over the universal ring of
  homogeneous parameters, hence over every commutative ring by base change (Webster, *Knot
  invariants and higher representation theory*). Proof: over every field `k`,
  `gdim e(𝐣)R^Λ_k(ν)e(𝐢)` is the value on `F_𝐣v_Λ`, `F_𝐢v_Λ` of the Shapovalov form of `V(Λ)`, by
  the previous item and the adjunction between `Eᵢ` and `Fᵢ`, so it is independent of `k`. Each
  graded piece `M_d` of the universal `R^Λ(ν)` is a finitely generated module (a quotient of a graded
  piece of the universal `R(ν)`) over a reduced Noetherian ring whose fibres at all points have the
  same dimension, hence projective, hence free (Quillen–Suslin and Swan, the ring being a Laurent
  polynomial ring over `ℤ`); and `M_d = 0` outside finitely many `d`. Over `ℤ` with fixed integer
  parameters this is the comparison of dimensions over `ℚ` and every `𝔽_p`.
- For filtered parameters, the same freeness holds. Prove the Kang–Kashiwara statements above
  (projectivity, the `sl₂` relations among `Eᵢ`, `Fᵢ`, and the adjunctions) without the grading,
  following Kang–Kashiwara or Webster's proof in *Knot invariants and higher representation
  theory*, which go through for filtered parameters. They give, over every field,
  `dim e(𝐣)R^Λ(ν)e(𝐢) = ⟨F_𝐣v_Λ, F_𝐢v_Λ⟩` at `q = 1`, the value for the homogeneous parameters, and
  the argument of the previous item applies to the ungraded ranks. The filtration of §1.2 alone
  gives only `dim R^Λ_P(ν) ≤ dim R^Λ_hom(ν)`, since `gr R^Λ_P(ν)` is a quotient of the homogeneous
  `R^Λ(ν)`.
