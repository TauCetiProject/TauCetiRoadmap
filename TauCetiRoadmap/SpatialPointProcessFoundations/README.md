# Roadmap: spatial point process foundations

**Summary.** A point process on a locally compact Polish space is a random locally finite counting
measure. This roadmap builds the general theory that spatial statistics rests on. It defines the
space of locally finite measures with its vague topology and the closed subspace of point
measures, whose Borel σ-algebras are generated by the counts on bounded windows. It describes the
law of a random measure through its intensity, its Laplace functional, its joint count laws and,
for simple point processes, its void probabilities. It disintegrates the Campbell measure into
Palm kernels. It builds factorial powers of a counting measure, factorial moment measures,
product densities and Janossy measures, with the series relating the last two and moment
determinacy. For the Poisson random measure it proves existence and uniqueness of the law, the
Laplace functional, the Mecke equation and its multivariate form, factorial moments, simplicity
for diffuse intensities, the binomial form given the count in a window, and the theorems of Rényi
and Slivnyak–Mecke. Mapping, restriction, superposition, marking and thinning act on Poisson
random measures, and thinning acts on factorial moment measures. On `ℝ^d` it treats the
translation action, stationarity, the stationary intensity, the reduced second factorial moment
measure, mixing and ergodicity of the stationary Poisson process, the stationary Palm
probability, the refined Campbell theorem and Slivnyak's characterisation.

The roadmap is a synthesis of several sources: the lectures of Last and Penrose for the Poisson
theory, Kallenberg and Daley–Vere-Jones for random measures and Palm theory, the manuscript of
Baccelli, Błaszczyszyn and Karray for Janossy measures and stationary frameworks, and
Chiu–Stoyan–Kendall–Mecke and Schneider–Weil for the stochastic-geometry forms. Its Poisson
definitions are those of Tau Ceti's library of random measures, which proves a large part of
Layer 5. [ClassicalErgodicTheory](../ClassicalErgodicTheory/README.md) supplies the implication
from mixing to ergodicity used in Layer 7, and
[GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic](../GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic/README.md)
§4.2 the point measure of a finite configuration. It is the foundation for spatial-statistics
roadmaps on determinantal, finite-volume and infinite-volume Gibbs, Cox and cluster processes,
point-pattern statistics, random closed sets and Boolean models, random tessellations, and
point-process likelihood; each takes the parts listed under Downstream consumers.

## How to read the build

Each layer has numbered subsections. Each subsection lists its targets as bold headlines with their
Lean names, a source locator for a headline that restates a source result and a proof route for a
corollary or adaptation, and Checks, which are sanity checks of the key definitions and theorems.
Layer 1 (carriers) and Layer 2 (laws) are the foundation. Layer 3 uses Layer 2. Layer 4 uses
Layer 1 and the determination theorems of §2.2. Layer 5 uses Layers 2–4; §5.5 uses the Palm
kernels of §3.2. Layer 6 uses Layer 5 and, for thinning, the factorial measures of §4.1. Layer 7
uses §2.2, §4.2 and Layer 5; Layer 8 uses Layer 7 and the Mecke equation of §5.3.

## Conventions

- `S` is a metric space that is Polish and locally compact, with its Borel σ-algebra; the same
  standing assumptions hold for a group `G` acting by translation. Results that need no topology
  are stated on a measurable space `X`.
- A locally finite measure is finite on compact sets (`LocallyFiniteMeasure`); a point measure is
  a locally finite measure that is integer-valued on measurable sets of finite mass
  (`PointMeasure`). Multiplicities are allowed; `PointMeasure.IsSimple` excludes them.
- Counts take values in `ℕ∞`: `PointMeasure.count` is the mass on measurable sets, infinite
  counts included. Masses, intensities and all integrals of nonnegative functions are in `ℝ≥0∞`
  and use the lower Lebesgue integral, so no statement needs integrability.
- A random measure is a map `N : Ω → Measure X` (or into one of the carriers) on a probability
  space `(Ω, P)`; its law is `P.map N`. The intensity measure is the mixture `B ↦ 𝔼 N(B)`.
- `ENNReal.expNeg x` is `e^{-x}` with `expNeg ∞ = 0`, as in Tau Ceti; the Laplace functional
  `𝔼 e^{-∫ f dN}` is `MeasureTheory.Measure.laplaceFunctional`.
- A Poisson random measure with intensity `Λ` is `TauCeti.Probability.IsPoissonRandomMeasure P N Λ`:
  measurability, independent Poisson counts on disjoint sets of finite intensity, and almost
  surely infinite counts on measurable sets of infinite intensity. The last clause makes void
  probabilities and Laplace functionals correct without finiteness assumptions. No topology,
  local finiteness or simplicity is part of the definition; `IsPoissonPointProcess` is the same
  property for a process on the point-measure carrier.
- Factorial powers follow Last and Penrose: `μ⁽ⁿ⁾` counts ordered `n`-tuples of distinct points of
  a counting measure. Janossy measures carry the factor `1/n!`, so the `n`th one has total mass
  `P(N(W) = n)`; Daley and Vere-Jones's `J_n` is `n!` times ours.
- Translation moves a point at `x` to the origin: `(x +ᵥ ξ)(B) = ξ(B + x)`, the shift `θ_x` of
  Last and Penrose (8.1) [LP, p. 69]. `E d` is `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin d)` with Lebesgue measure,
  and `unitCube d = [0,1)^d`.

## Layer 1: carriers

### 1.1 Locally finite measures, point measures and counts

Sources: [K, Lemma 1.6, p. 22]; [LP, Definition 6.6, p. 49; Corollary 6.5, p. 48]; [DVJ2, §9.1,
p. 6].

- **Carriers** (`LocallyFiniteMeasure`, `PointMeasure`, `PointMeasure.toLocallyFinite`,
  `MarkedLocallyFiniteMeasure`, `MarkedPointMeasure`, `PointMeasure.count`,
  `PointMeasure.IsSimple`). Marked measures live on `S × E` and are finite on `K × E` for compact
  `K`; their marks need no topology. `MeasureTheory.Measure.IsIntegerValued` is Tau Ceti's.
- **Counts are masses** (`PointMeasure.coe_count`). On a measurable set the count, cast to
  `ℝ≥0∞`, is the mass. Route: integer values on sets of finite mass, and `⊤ ↦ ∞`.
- **Atomic decomposition** (`PointMeasure.exists_eq_sum_dirac`). Every point measure is
  `∑_{n < κ} δ_{x(n)}` for some `κ : ℕ∞` and points `x(n)` [K, Lemma 1.6, p. 22]; for processes this
  is [LP, Corollary 6.5, p. 48]. Route: a locally finite measure on a σ-compact space is σ-finite,
  and an integer-valued σ-finite measure on a countably separated space is a sum of its atoms.

**Checks.**

- `δ_a + δ_a` is a point measure with count `2` on `{a}` and is not simple.
- Lebesgue measure on `ℝ` is locally finite but not a point measure: its mass on `[0, 1/2]` is
  `1/2`.
- Negative control: counting measure on `ℚ ⊂ ℝ` is integer-valued but not locally finite, since
  `[0, 1]` has infinite mass; it is not a `PointMeasure`.
- The count of `∑_{k ∈ ℤ} δ_k` on `ℝ` is `⊤`, and on `[0, 2)` it is `2`.
- `∑_{k ∈ ℤ} δ_{(k, k)}` on `ℝ × ℝ` is a marked point measure: its mass on `[0, 1] × ℝ` is `2`.
- Negative control: `∑_{k ∈ ℤ} δ_{(0, k)}` is a point measure on `ℝ × ℝ`, finite on compact sets,
  but has infinite mass on `{0} × ℝ`, so it is not a marked point measure.

### 1.2 The vague topology and the Borel σ-algebra

Sources: [K, Lemma 4.5, p. 115; Lemma 4.7, p. 117]; [DVJ2, Proposition 9.1.IV, p. 6;
Lemma 9.1.V, p. 6]; [BBK, Proposition 1.5.3, p. 15].

- **The vague topology** (the `TopologicalSpace` instances). The coarsest topology on
  `LocallyFiniteMeasure S` making `μ ↦ ∫ f dμ` continuous for every compactly supported
  continuous `f`; point measures carry the subspace topology, and both carry their Borel
  σ-algebras.
- **Polish** (`polishSpace_locallyFiniteMeasure`). [K, Lemma 4.5, p. 115]; [DVJ2,
  Proposition 9.1.IV(i), p. 6], whose `w#`-topology agrees with the vague topology on a locally
  compact space.
- **Point measures form a closed subspace** (`isClosedEmbedding_toLocallyFinite`). [DVJ2,
  Lemma 9.1.V and Proposition 9.1.IV(iii), p. 6].
- **The σ-algebra is generated by counts** (`measurableSpace_eq_iSup_comap`). The Borel
  σ-algebra of the vague topology is generated by the masses `μ ↦ μ(A)` of relatively compact
  Borel sets, and on point measures by their counts [K, Lemma 4.7, p. 117]; [BBK,
  Proposition 1.5.3, p. 15]; [DVJ2, Proposition 9.1.IV(ii), p. 6].

**Checks.**

- `δ_{1/n} → δ_0` vaguely on `ℝ`, and the counts on `[0, 1]` converge, while those on `(0, 1]`
  stay `1` against the limit `0`: counts are measurable, not continuous.
- `δ_n → 0` vaguely on `ℝ` as `n → ∞`, although every `δ_n` has total mass `1`; in total
  variation `δ_n` stays at distance `2` from `0`, so the vague topology is strictly coarser.
- Negative control: `n δ_0` has no vague limit, so the space is not compact.
- `δ_{1/n} + δ_{-1/n} → 2δ_0`, a limit of simple point measures that is not simple: simple point
  measures do not form a closed set.

### 1.3 Restriction and finite configurations

Sources: [LP, (4.4), p. 28]; [K, Lemma 1.12, p. 27].

- **Restriction and tuples** (`PointMeasure.restrict`, `PointMeasure.ofTuple`). The restriction
  of a point measure to a measurable set, and the point measure of a finite tuple: the
  `configurationMeasure` `∑ᵢ δ_{xᵢ}` of GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.2, with
  multiplicities, bundled with its local finiteness and integer values.
- **Tuples and simplicity** (`PointMeasure.isSimple_ofTuple_iff`). The point measure of a tuple
  is simple exactly when the tuple is injective. Route: the mass of `{x}` is the number of
  indices `i` with `xᵢ = x`.

**Checks.**

- For `a ≠ b`, `ofTuple ![a, b, a]` has mass `2` on `{a}` and total mass `3`.
- Permuting a tuple does not change its point measure.
- Negative control: `ofTuple ![a, a]` is not simple and `![a, a]` is not injective.
- The restriction of `δ_0 + δ_2` to `[0, 1]` is `δ_0`.

## Layer 2: laws of random measures

### 2.1 Intensity and Campbell's formula

Sources: [LP, Proposition 2.7, p. 13]; [K, Lemma 2.4, p. 53]; [SW, Theorem 3.1.2, p. 54]; [CSKM,
Theorem 4.1, p. 114].

- **Intensity measure** (`intensity`). The mixture `P.bind N`, `B ↦ 𝔼 N(B)`.
- **Campbell's formula** (`lintegral_intensity`). `∫ f d(intensity) = 𝔼 ∫ f dN` for measurable
  `f ≥ 0` [LP, Proposition 2.7, p. 13].

**Checks.**

- For `N = δ_U` with `U` uniform on `[0, 1]`, the intensity is Lebesgue measure on `[0, 1]`.
- For a Poisson count `N = K δ_0` with `K ~ Po(λ)`, the intensity is `λ δ_0`.
- Negative control: `N = Kδ_0` with `P(K = ∞) = 1/2` has infinite intensity on `{0}` although `K`
  is finite with probability `1/2`; Campbell's formula is an identity in `ℝ≥0∞`, with no
  integrability assumption.

### 2.2 Determination of the law

Sources: [LP, Proposition 2.10, p. 14]; [K, Theorem 2.2, p. 52]; [DVJ2, Proposition 9.2.III,
p. 26]; [BBK, Corollary 1.3.4, p. 10; Proposition 1.3.11, p. 12].

- **Laplace functional** (`MeasureTheory.Measure.laplaceFunctional`, Tau Ceti's).
- **Joint count laws determine the law** (`map_eq_of_forall_map_count_eq`). Two point processes
  with the same joint count laws on all finite families of disjoint relatively compact Borel
  windows have the same law [DVJ2, Proposition 9.2.III, p. 26]; [BBK, Corollary 1.3.4, p. 10].
  Route: §1.2 and the π-λ theorem, with disjoint windows refining any finite family.
- **The Laplace functional determines the law** (`map_eq_of_laplaceFunctional_eq`). For a finite
  measure `P`, random measures with equal Laplace functionals have equal laws [LP,
  Proposition 2.10, p. 14]; [K, Theorem 2.2, p. 52]; [BBK, Proposition 1.3.11, p. 12].

**Checks.**

- The Laplace functional at `f = 0` is `P(Ω) = 1`, and at `f = ∞ · 1_B` it is the void
  probability `P(N(B) = 0)`.
- `δ_U` with `U` uniform on `[0, 1]` and `δ_{1-U}` have the same count laws and the same law.
- Negative control: `δ_0 + δ_1` and `2δ_{1/2}` have the same total count, but their counts on
  `[0, 1/2)` differ; one window does not determine the law.

### 2.3 Void probabilities

Sources: [DVJ2, Theorem 9.2.XII, p. 35]; [K, Theorem 2.2, p. 52]; [BBK, Theorem 2.1.10, p. 32].

- **Rényi–Mönch** (`map_eq_of_forall_measure_count_eq_zero`). The law of an almost surely
  simple point process is determined by its void probabilities `P(N(A) = 0)` on relatively compact
  Borel sets [DVJ2, Theorem 9.2.XII, p. 35].

**Checks.**

- `δ_U` has void probability `1 - |A ∩ [0, 1]|` on Borel `A`.
- Negative control: `δ_U` and `2δ_U` have the same void probabilities and different laws; the
  simplicity hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- For a simple process, the void probabilities determine the count laws on disjoint unions by
  inclusion–exclusion: `P(N(A) = 0, N(B) ≥ 1) = P(N(A) = 0) - P(N(A ∪ B) = 0)` for disjoint
  `A`, `B`.

## Layer 3: Campbell measure and Palm kernels

### 3.1 The Campbell measure

Sources: [BBK, Lemma 3.1.1, p. 119]; [K, §6.1, p. 215]; [CSKM, §4.3.4, p. 123].

- **Campbell measure** (`campbellMeasure`). The measure on `X × Measure X` with
  `C(B × L) = 𝔼[N(B) 1{N ∈ L}]`.
- **Rectangles and marginal** (`campbellMeasure_prod`). For measurable `N` with σ-finite
  intensity, the rectangle formula holds and the first marginal is the intensity [BBK,
  Lemma 3.1.1, p. 119]. The σ-finiteness makes almost every `N ω` finite on a fixed countable
  partition, where `ω ↦ (N ω).map (x ↦ (x, N ω))` is measurable, so the `bind` defining the
  Campbell measure is not the junk value `0`.

**Checks.**

- For `N = δ_U`, `C = law of (U, δ_U)`, of total mass `1`.
- For `N = 2δ_U`, `C(B × L) = 2 P(U ∈ B, 2δ_U ∈ L)`: multiplicities weight the points.
- Negative control: `C(B × univ) = 𝔼 N(B)`, not `P(N(B) > 0)`; the two differ for `N = 2δ_0`.

### 3.2 Palm kernels

Sources: [DVJ2, Proposition 13.1.IV, p. 273]; [K, Theorem 1.23, p. 37; Lemma 6.10, p. 223];
[BBK, Theorem 3.1.9, p. 121; Definition 3.1.13, p. 124].

- **Palm kernels** (`IsPalmKernel`, `IsReducedPalmKernel`). A Palm kernel is a Markov kernel `κ`
  with `C = intensity ⊗ κ`. A reduced Palm kernel satisfies the Campbell–Little–Mecke formula for
  `N - δ_x` in place of `N`.
- **Existence** (`exists_isPalmKernel`). A random measure on a Polish space with σ-finite
  intensity has a Palm kernel [DVJ2, Proposition 13.1.IV, p. 273]. Route: disintegration
  [K, Theorem 1.23, p. 37] of the Campbell measure, which is σ-finite when the intensity is.
  That theorem needs a Borel target and `Measure X` with the Giry σ-algebra is not known to be
  one, so disintegrate on the subspace of measures finite on a fixed countable measurable
  partition `(Bₖ)` with `intensity(Bₖ) < ∞`; it carries almost every `N ω`, and it is standard
  Borel, being a measurable subset of `∏ₖ` (finite measures on `Bₖ`) with the product of the
  weak topologies.
- **Existence of reduced Palm kernels** (`exists_isReducedPalmKernel`). Under the same
  hypotheses there is a reduced Palm kernel. Route: map the Palm kernel by `μ ↦ μ - δ_x`
  [BBK, Definition 3.1.13, p. 124].
- **Uniqueness.** Two Palm kernels of `N` agree for intensity-almost every `x`, and so do two
  reduced Palm kernels. Route: both sides of `C = intensity ⊗ κ` determine `κ` up to null sets
  when the Campbell measure is σ-finite.
- **Window form of reduced Palm kernels**
  (`IsReducedPalmKernel.lintegral_factorialCountMoment`). For a point process, a kernel `Q` into
  point measures whose image in measures is a reduced Palm kernel satisfies
  `𝔼 ∫_A ∏ⱼ (N(Bⱼ) - 1_{Bⱼ}(x))^{(nⱼ)} N(dx) = ∫_A ∫ ∏ⱼ ξ(Bⱼ)^{(nⱼ)} Q(x, dξ) intensity(dx)` on
  disjoint relatively compact windows. Route: the definition at
  `g(x, μ) = 1_A(x) ∏ⱼ μ(Bⱼ)^{(nⱼ)}`, and the counts of `N - δ_x` at a point `x` of `N`. The
  converse needs moment determinacy (§4.2).
- **Campbell–Little–Mecke** (`IsPalmKernel.lintegral`).
  `𝔼 ∫ g(x, N) N(dx) = ∫∫ g(x, μ) κ(x, dμ) intensity(dx)` for measurable `g ≥ 0` [BBK,
  Theorem 3.1.9, p. 121].

**Checks.**

- For `N = δ_U`, `U` uniform on `[0, 1]`, the Palm kernel is `κ(x) = δ_{δ_x}` and the reduced one
  is `δ_0`.
- For `N = δ_U + δ_V` with `U`, `V` independent uniform, the reduced Palm kernel is the law of
  `δ_V`: one remaining point, uniform.
- Negative control: the Palm kernel of `δ_U + δ_V` is not the law of `N` itself; under it the
  process has a point at `x` with probability one.

## Layer 4: factorial measures, moment measures and Janossy measures

### 4.1 Factorial powers

Sources: [LP, (4.4)–(4.7), p. 28; Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 4.3, p. 29]; [K, Lemma 1.12, p. 27].

- **Factorial powers** (`factorialMeasure`). `μ⁽⁰⁾` is the point mass on the empty tuple and
  `μ⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ = (μ⁽ⁿ⁾ ⊗ μ) ∘ snoc⁻¹ - ∑_{j < n} μ⁽ⁿ⁾ ∘ (x ↦ (x, x_j))⁻¹`, the recursion of
  [LP, Lemma 4.2, p. 29], which characterises the factorial measures of counting measures
  [LP, Proposition 4.3, p. 29].
- **Tuples** (`factorialMeasure_configurationMeasure`). For `μ = ∑ᵢ δ_{xᵢ}` the `n`th factorial
  power is the sum of `δ_{x ∘ σ}` over injections `σ` [LP, (4.4), p. 28; Lemma 4.2, p. 29].
- **Count moments** (`factorialCountMoment`, `factorialMeasure_pi_eq_factorialCountMoment`). On a
  product of windows `B_{c(1)} × ⋯ × B_{c(n)}` with disjoint `Bⱼ` of finite mass, `μ⁽ⁿ⁾` is
  `∏ⱼ μ(Bⱼ)^{(nⱼ)}`, `nⱼ` the number of `i` with `c(i) = j` and `^{(k)}` the falling factorial
  [LP, (4.6)–(4.7), p. 28].
- **Measurability** (`measurable_factorialMeasure`). [LP, Proposition 4.3, p. 29].
- **Measurability along a random measure** (`aemeasurable_factorialMeasure`). On a countably
  generated space, if `N` is measurable and almost every `N ω` is integer-valued and σ-finite,
  then `ω ↦ (N ω)⁽ⁿ⁾` is almost everywhere measurable, so the mixtures over `P` in §4.2 and §4.3
  are not the junk value `0` of `bind`. Route: almost every `N ω` is a countable sum of point
  masses on atoms of a countable generating family, and on such measures `μ⁽ⁿ⁾` of a set in the
  generating algebra of rectangles is a measurable function of finitely many counts
  [LP, Proposition 4.3, p. 29].

**Checks.**

- For `2δ_a + δ_b` the second factorial power is `2δ_{(a,a)} + 2δ_{(a,b)} + 2δ_{(b,a)}`, of total
  mass `6 = 3 · 2`.
- Negative control: the product measure `μ ⊗ μ` of `2δ_a + δ_b` has total mass `9`; it counts
  the pairs `(i, i)`.
- `(3δ_a)⁽³⁾ = 6δ_{(a,a,a)} = 3^{(3)} δ_{(a,a,a)}`.
- For `ξ = 3δ_a + δ_b`, `B = ({a}, {b})` and `c = (1, 1, 2)`, both sides of the count-moment
  identity are `3 · 2 · 1 = 6`.
- Negative control: for the overlapping windows `{a, b}` and `{a}` and `ξ = 3δ_a + δ_b`,
  `ξ⁽²⁾({a, b} × {a}) = 9` while `ξ({a, b}) ξ({a}) = 12`; disjointness is needed.

### 4.2 Factorial moment measures and product densities

Sources: [LP, Definition 4.9, p. 34]; [CSKM, §4.3.2, p. 121; §4.3.3, p. 122]; [BBK,
Corollary 1.3.12, p. 12]; [DVJ1, Theorem 5.4.II, p. 135].

- **Factorial moment measures and product densities** (`factorialMomentMeasure`,
  `IsProductDensity`). `αₙ(C) = 𝔼 N⁽ⁿ⁾(C)` [LP, Definition 4.9, p. 34], and `ρ` is an `n`th
  product density with respect to `μ` when `αₙ = ρ · μⁿ` [CSKM, §4.3.3, p. 122].
- **Window form** (`isProductDensity_iff_factorialCountMoment`). For measurable `ρ` with finite
  integrals over compact boxes, `ρ` is a product density exactly when
  `𝔼 ∏ⱼ N(Bⱼ)^{(nⱼ)} = ∫_{∏ᵢ B_{c(i)}} ρ dμⁿ` on disjoint relatively compact windows. Route: §4.1,
  and products of disjoint relatively compact windows form a generating semiring on which both
  measures are finite.
- **Moment determinacy** (`map_eq_of_factorialMomentMeasure_eq`). If counts on compact sets have
  exponential moments, the factorial moment measures determine the law [BBK, Corollary 1.3.12,
  p. 12]; for finite processes [DVJ1, Theorem 5.4.II, p. 135]. Route: factorial moments give
  moments, exponential moments make the joint count laws moment-determinate, then §2.2.

**Checks.**

- `α₁` is the intensity measure on `Fin 1 → X`.
- For a binomial process of `m` independent points with law `Q`, `αₙ = m^{(n)} Qⁿ`, which vanishes
  for `n > m`.
- Negative control: for `N = Kδ_U` with `K ~ Po(λ)` and `U` uniform on `[0, 1]`,
  `N⁽²⁾ = K(K - 1) δ_{(U,U)}`, so `α₂` has mass `λ²` on the diagonal, a Lebesgue null set; `N` has
  intensity `λ · volume` on `[0, 1]` but no second product density.
- Under the exponential-moment hypothesis Poisson counts qualify:
  `𝔼 e^{t N(K)} = e^{Λ(K)(e^t - 1)}`.

### 4.3 Janossy measures

Sources: [DVJ1, (5.3.2), p. 124; Theorem 5.4.II, p. 135]; [BBK, Definition 4.3.6, p. 174;
Corollary 4.3.9, p. 175; Proposition 4.3.10, p. 176].

- **Janossy measures** (`janossyMeasure`). `J_n = (1/n!) 𝔼[N|_W⁽ⁿ⁾ ; N(W) = n]` on `Wⁿ`.
- **Mass** (`janossyMeasure_univ`). `J_n(Xⁿ) = P(N(W) = n)`. Route: `ν⁽ⁿ⁾` has mass `n!` for a
  counting measure `ν` of mass `n` (§4.1). This is [DVJ1, (5.3.2), p. 124] in the normalization of
  the Conventions.
- **Reconstruction** (`map_restrict_eq_sum_janossyMeasure`). On a relatively compact window, the law
  of `N|_W` is `∑ₙ J_n ∘ configurationMeasure⁻¹` [BBK, Corollary 4.3.9, p. 175]. Route: each of the
  `n!` orderings of `n` points maps to the same configuration.
- **Moment series** (`factorialMomentMeasure_restrict_eq_tsum_janossyMeasure`). On a relatively
  compact window `W`, so that `N(W) < ∞`, the factorial moment measures of `N|_W` are
  `αₖ(C) = ∑ₙ (k + n)!/n! · J_{k+n}(C × Wⁿ)` [DVJ1, (5.4.7) in Theorem 5.4.II, p. 135]; [BBK,
  Proposition 4.3.10, p. 176]. With Daley–Vere-Jones's normalization the factor is `1/n!`.

**Checks.**

- For `N = δ_U`, `U` uniform on `W`, `J₁` is the uniform law on `W` and `Jₙ = 0` for `n ≠ 1`.
- For the Poisson process (§5.3), `∑ₙ (k + n)!/n! · e^{-λ} Λᵏ(C) λⁿ/(k + n)! = Λᵏ(C)` for
  `k = 0, 1, 2, 3`, with `λ = Λ(W)` (symbolic check).
- Negative control: without the factor `1/n!`, the Janossy masses of `2δ_U` would sum to
  `P(N(W) = 2) · 2! = 2`, not `1`.

## Layer 5: Poisson random measures

### 5.1 Definition, existence and uniqueness

Sources: [LP, Proposition 3.2, p. 20; Theorem 3.6, p. 21]; [BBK, Definition 2.1.1, p. 29;
Proposition 2.5.5, p. 76]; [SW, Theorem 3.2.1, p. 60].

- **Poisson random measures** (`TauCeti.Probability.IsPoissonRandomMeasure`,
  `IsPoissonPointProcess`). Tau Ceti's definition, and the same property on the point-measure
  carrier.
- **Existence** (`MeasureTheory.Measure.poissonLaw`, `isPoissonRandomMeasure_poissonLaw`). For
  σ-finite `Λ`, the identity map under Tau Ceti's sampling law `poissonLaw Λ` is a Poisson random
  measure [LP, Theorem 3.6, p. 21]; [BBK, Proposition 2.5.5, p. 76]. The law superposes the finite
  samples `MeasureTheory.Measure.finitePoissonLawOf` of the pieces
  `MeasureTheory.Measure.poissonPiece`, which are the compound Poisson laws
  `TauCeti.Probability.finitePoissonLaw`.
- **Uniqueness** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.map_eq_poissonLaw`). Every Poisson random measure with
  σ-finite intensity has law `poissonLaw Λ` [LP, Proposition 3.2, p. 20].
- **Point-measure carrier** (`exists_isPoissonPointProcess`). A locally finite intensity on `S`
  has a Poisson law on `PointMeasure S`, with atoms allowed [LP, Theorem 3.6, p. 21]; [SW,
  Theorem 3.2.1, p. 60] is the atomless case. Route:
  `poissonLaw Λ` is carried by locally finite integer-valued measures, then §1.2.
- **Intensity** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.intensity_eq`). Route: the mean of `Po(Λ(B))` is `Λ(B)`,
  and the infinite clause covers `Λ(B) = ∞`.

**Checks.**

- For measurable `N`, `IsPoissonRandomMeasure P N 0` holds exactly when `P` is a probability
  measure and `N = 0` almost surely.
- For `Λ = c δ_a` with `c ∈ (0, ∞)`, `N = K δ_a` with `K ~ Po(c)`: an atom of the intensity gives
  a multiple point.
- Negative control: for `Λ = ∞ · δ_a`, the only sets of finite intensity are null, so `N = 0`
  satisfies every finite-intensity clause; the infinite clause excludes it and forces
  `N({a}) = ∞` almost surely. For σ-finite `Λ` the clause follows from the others.
- Any Poisson random measure forces `P` to be a probability measure: the law of the count on `∅`
  is `δ_0`, of mass `1`.

### 5.2 Laplace functional and void probabilities

Sources: [LP, Theorem 3.9, p. 23; (6.6) in Theorem 6.10, p. 50]; [BBK, Proposition 2.1.4, p. 30].

- **Laplace functional** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.laplaceFunctional_eq_expNeg`).
  `𝔼 e^{-∫ f dN} = exp(-∫ (1 - e^{-f}) dΛ)` for measurable `f ≥ 0`, with `exp(-∞) = 0` and no
  finiteness assumption [LP, Theorem 3.9, p. 23]; [BBK, Proposition 2.1.4, p. 30]. The converse,
  that this formula characterises Poisson random measures, is [LP, Theorem 3.9, p. 23] and is
  proved in Tau Ceti.
- **Void probabilities** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.measure_setOf_eq_zero`).
  `P(N(B) = 0) = e^{-Λ(B)}` for every measurable `B`. Route: Poisson zero mass, and the infinite
  clause when `Λ(B) = ∞`.

**Checks.**

- `ENNReal.expNeg 0 = 1`, `ENNReal.expNeg ∞ = 0` and `ENNReal.expNeg (log 2) = 1/2`.
- At `f = t 1_B`, `𝔼 e^{-tN(B)} = e^{-Λ(B)(1 - e^{-t})}`; at `λ = 2`, `t = 0.7` both sides are
  `0.365376`.
- Negative control: without the infinite clause, `N = 0` with `Λ = ∞ · δ_a` would give
  `P(N({a}) = 0) = 1 ≠ 0 = e^{-∞}`.

### 5.3 The Mecke equation and factorial moments

Sources: [LP, Theorem 4.1, p. 27; Theorem 4.4, p. 30; Corollary 4.10, p. 34]; [K, Lemma 6.15,
p. 229]; [SW, Theorem 3.2.5, p. 67; Theorem 3.2.3, p. 65]; [BBK, Proposition 2.3.25, p. 63].

- **Mecke equation** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.lintegral_mecke`).
  `𝔼 ∫ f(x, N) N(dx) = ∫ 𝔼 f(x, N + δ_x) Λ(dx)` for s-finite `Λ` [LP, Theorem 4.1, p. 27].
- **Multivariate Mecke equation** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.lintegral_factorialMeasure_mecke`).
  `𝔼 ∫ f(x, N) N⁽ⁿ⁾(dx) = ∫ 𝔼 f(x, N + ∑ᵢ δ_{xᵢ}) Λⁿ(dx)` [LP, Theorem 4.4, p. 30], stated for
  σ-finite `Λ` on a countably generated space, where Poisson random measures are almost surely
  integer-valued (Tau Ceti).
- **Mecke characterisation** (`isPoissonRandomMeasure_iff_mecke`). For σ-finite `Λ`, the Mecke
  equation characterises Poisson random measures [LP, Theorem 4.1, p. 27]; for random measures
  [K, Lemma 6.15, p. 229].
- **Factorial moments** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.factorialMomentMeasure_eq`). For σ-finite `Λ`
  on a countably generated space, `ω ↦ (N ω)⁽ⁿ⁾` is almost everywhere measurable and `αₙ = Λⁿ`
  [LP, Corollary 4.10, p. 34]; [BBK, Proposition 2.3.25, p. 63]. Route: §4.1 for the
  measurability, then the multivariate Mecke equation at `f = 1_C`.
- **Janossy measures** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.janossyMeasure_eq`). For σ-finite `Λ` on a countably
  generated space and a window of finite intensity, `ω ↦ (N ω|_W)⁽ⁿ⁾` is almost everywhere
  measurable and `J_n = e^{-Λ(W)}/n! · (Λ|_W)ⁿ`. Route: §5.4 and §4.3; [SW, Theorem 3.2.3, p. 65]
  gives the corresponding expansion of `𝔼 f(N|_W)`.

**Checks.**

- `f(x, μ) = 1_B(x) μ(B)` gives `𝔼 N(B)² = Λ(B)(Λ(B) + 1)`; at `Λ(B) = 1.7` both sides are `4.59`.
- `α₂(B × B) = 𝔼 N(B)(N(B) - 1) = Λ(B)²`.
- The Janossy masses `e^{-λ}λⁿ/n!` sum to `1` (at `λ = 2.5`).
- Negative control: for the binomial process of `m` uniform points, `α₂ = m(m - 1) Q²`, not
  `(mQ)²`, so the Mecke equation fails: the binomial process is not Poisson.

### 5.4 Simplicity and the binomial form in a window

Sources: [LP, Proposition 6.9, p. 49; Proposition 3.8, p. 23]; [BBK, Proposition 2.1.9, p. 32].

- **Simplicity** (`IsPoissonPointProcess.ae_isSimple_iff`). A Poisson process with locally finite
  intensity is almost surely simple exactly when the intensity has no atoms [LP,
  Proposition 6.9, p. 49]; [BBK, Proposition 2.1.9, p. 32].
- **Binomial form** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.map_restrict_cond_eq`). Given `N(W) = m`, with
  `Λ(W) < ∞`, the restriction `N|_W` is the configuration of `m` independent points with law
  `Λ|_W / Λ(W)` [LP, Proposition 3.8, p. 23].

**Checks.**

- For `Λ(W) = 3`, `Λ(A) = 1.2`, `A ⊂ W`, `m = 5`, the conditional law of `N(A)` given `N(W) = 5`
  is binomial `(5, 0.4)` (all six probabilities agree to `10⁻¹²`).
- At `m = 0` the conditional law is `δ_0`.
- Negative control: for `Λ = δ_a`, `P(N({a}) ≥ 2) = 1 - 2/e > 0`, so the process is not simple.

### 5.5 Characterisations

Sources: [LP, Theorem 6.10, p. 50]; [K, Corollary 3.9, p. 80; Lemma 6.15, p. 229]; [BBK,
Theorem 2.1.10, p. 32; Theorem 3.2.4, p. 130]; [DVJ2, Proposition 13.1.VII, p. 281]; [CSKM, (2.43),
p. 50].

- **Rényi's theorem** (`isPoissonPointProcess_of_measure_count_eq_zero`). A simple point process
  with void probabilities `e^{-Λ(B)}` on relatively compact windows, for diffuse locally finite
  `Λ`, is Poisson [LP, Theorem 6.10, p. 50]; [K, Corollary 3.9, p. 80]. Route: §2.3 compares it
  with the Poisson process of §5.1, simple by §5.4.
- **Slivnyak–Mecke** (`isPoissonRandomMeasure_iff_isReducedPalmKernel`). A point process with
  σ-finite intensity is Poisson exactly when the constant kernel `x ↦ law(N)` is a reduced Palm
  kernel [K, Lemma 6.15, p. 229]; [BBK, Theorem 3.2.4, p. 130]; [DVJ2, Proposition 13.1.VII,
  p. 281]; [CSKM, (2.43), p. 50].

**Checks.**

- Negative control for Rényi: `2N` for a Poisson `N` with diffuse `Λ` has the void probabilities
  `e^{-Λ(B)}` of `N` and is not Poisson; it is not simple.
- For `N = δ_U`, `U` uniform, the reduced Palm kernel is `δ_0`, not the law of `δ_U`; the process
  is not Poisson.
- Negative control: the integer-valued hypothesis matters. For the deterministic diffuse
  `N = Λ ≠ 0`, Measure subtraction gives `N - δ_x = N`, so the constant kernel `δ_Λ` is a reduced
  Palm kernel; but `N` is not Poisson, since `Var N(B) = 0 ≠ Λ(B)`.

## Layer 6: operations

### 6.1 Mapping, restriction and superposition

Sources: [LP, Theorem 5.1, p. 38; Theorem 5.2, p. 39; Theorem 3.3, p. 20]; [BBK, Corollary 2.2.3,
p. 36]; [DVJ2, Proposition 9.4.IX, p. 63].

- **Mapping theorem** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.map`). The image under a measurable map is
  Poisson with the image intensity [LP, Theorem 5.1, p. 38].
- **Images of point processes.** On the point-measure carrier the image intensity must be locally
  finite for the image to be a point process; the first Check below has infinite counts.
- **Restriction theorem** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.iIndepFun_restrict`). Restrictions to disjoint
  measurable sets are independent [LP, Theorem 5.2, p. 39]. That each restriction `N|_C` is
  Poisson with intensity `Λ|_C` is proved in Tau Ceti (Prior formalization).
- **Superposition** (`isPoissonRandomMeasure_sum`). A countable independent family of Poisson
  random measures sums to a Poisson random measure with the summed intensity [LP, Theorem 3.3,
  p. 20]; [BBK, Corollary 2.2.3, p. 36].

**Checks.**

- The image of a Poisson process on `ℝ²` with Lebesgue intensity under the projection to the
  first coordinate has intensity `∞` on every nonempty open set: it is Poisson in the sense of
  `IsPoissonRandomMeasure`, with infinite counts, and not a point process.
- Superposing Poisson processes of intensities `λ₁ δ_0` and `λ₂ δ_0` gives `Po(λ₁ + λ₂)` at `0`.
- Negative control: superposing a Poisson process with a copy of itself gives `2N`, which is not
  Poisson; independence is needed.

### 6.2 Marking

Sources: [LP, Definition 5.3, p. 40; Proposition 5.5, p. 42; Theorem 5.6, p. 42]; [K, Theorem 3.2,
p. 75]; [BBK, Theorem 2.2.21, p. 45].

- **Markings** (`markExponent`, `IsMarking`). `M` is a `K`-marking of `N` when, on every event
  `{N ∈ C}`, `𝔼 e^{-∫ g dM}` equals `𝔼 ∏_{x ∈ N} ∫ e^{-g(x, y)} K(x, dy)`, written as
  `exp(-∫ markExponent K g dN)`. This is the conditional independence of the marks given `N` of
  [LP, Definition 5.3, p. 40], in a form that needs no enumeration of the points.
- **Enumerated marks** (`isMarking_of_condDistrib`). If `N = ∑_{n < κ} δ_{Xₙ}` and the marks are,
  given `(κ, X)`, independent with laws `K(Xₙ, ·)`, then `∑_{n < κ} δ_{(Xₙ, Yₙ)}` is a
  `K`-marking. Route: conditional Laplace functional of independent marks.
- **Intensity** (`IsMarking.intensity_eq`). The intensity of a marking of an almost surely
  integer-valued random measure with s-finite intensity is `intensity ⊗ K` [LP, Proposition 5.5,
  p. 42]. Without s-finiteness `⊗ₘ` is the junk value `0`.
- **Marking theorem** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.isPoissonRandomMeasure_of_isMarking`). A
  `K`-marking of a Poisson random measure with s-finite intensity `Λ` is Poisson with intensity
  `Λ ⊗ K` [LP, Theorem 5.6, p. 42]; [BBK, Theorem 2.2.21, p. 45]; [K, Theorem 3.2, p. 75]. Route:
  the defining identity at `C = univ` and the Poisson Laplace functional (§5.2) at
  `markExponent K g` give `exp(-∫ (1 - e^{-g}) d(Λ ⊗ K))`, and Laplace functionals of this form
  characterise Poisson random measures (§5.2).
- **Tau Ceti's marking is a marking.** The marked realization that Tau Ceti main constructs for a
  Poisson random measure with σ-finite intensity on a standard Borel space, by a Markov kernel
  from `Measure X` to `Measure (X × E)` (Prior formalization), is a `K`-marking of `N` in the
  sense of `IsMarking`. Route: the kernel acts on `N` alone, and it is an independent marking of
  a measurable enumeration of the points, so `isMarking_of_condDistrib` applies.

**Checks.**

- For `N = δ_a` and `K(a) = Bernoulli(1/2)` on `Bool`, with `g = ∞ · 1{mark = true}`, both sides
  of the defining identity are `1/2`: `markExponent K g a = log 2`.
- For a deterministic kernel `K(x) = δ_{h(x)}`, the marking is the image of `N` under
  `x ↦ (x, h(x))`.
- Negative control: marking every point of `N` with one common random mark `Y` is not a
  `K`-marking when `N` has two or more points with positive probability; the product formula
  fails for `g` depending on the mark alone.

### 6.3 Thinning

Sources: [LP, Definition 5.7, p. 43; Corollary 5.9, p. 44]; [BBK, Corollary 2.2.7, p. 38;
Proposition 2.3.24, p. 62].

- **Thinning theorem** (`IsPoissonRandomMeasure.thinning`). For a `Bool`-marking of a Poisson
  random measure with s-finite intensity `Λ` and retention probability `p(x) = K(x, {true})`, the
  retained and the deleted points are independent Poisson random measures with intensities `pΛ`
  and `(1 - p)Λ` [LP, Corollary 5.9, p. 44]; [BBK, Corollary 2.2.7, p. 38]. Route: the marking
  theorem, the restriction theorem on `X × {true}` and `X × {false}`, and the mapping theorem.
- **Factorial moments of a thinning** (`IsMarking.factorialMomentMeasure_thinning`). For an
  almost surely integer-valued and σ-finite random measure on a countably generated space, the
  factorial powers of `N` and of the retained points are almost everywhere measurable (§4.1), and
  `αₙ` of the retained points is `∏ᵢ p(xᵢ) · αₙ(dx)` [BBK, Proposition 2.3.24, p. 62].
- **Product densities of a thinning.** Product densities therefore multiply by `∏ᵢ p(xᵢ)`.

**Checks.**

- For `N(W) ~ Po(2)` and `p = 0.3`, the joint law of retained and deleted counts factorises into
  `Po(0.6) ⊗ Po(1.4)` (all `100` probabilities with counts below `10` agree to `10⁻¹⁴`).
- For a binomial `(5, 0.4)` count thinned with `p = 0.3`,
  `𝔼 (retained)^{(2)} = 0.288 = p² 𝔼 N^{(2)}`.
- Negative control: for a binomial `(5, 0.4)` count, the retained and deleted counts have
  covariance `p(1 - p)(Var N - 𝔼 N) = 0.21 · (1.2 - 2) = -0.168`; independence needs the Poisson
  hypothesis.

## Layer 7: stationarity on `ℝ^d`

### 7.1 Translation and stationarity

Sources: [LP, (8.1), p. 69; Definition 8.1, p. 70]; [DVJ2, Lemma 12.1.I, p. 178]; [BBK,
Lemma 6.1.17, p. 302].

- **Translation** (`E`, `unitCube`, the `AddAction` instances on `PointMeasure G` and
  `LocallyFiniteMeasure G`). `x +ᵥ ξ = ξ ∘ (· - x)⁻¹`, so `(x +ᵥ ξ)(B) = ξ(B + x)`; translating
  by `0` is the identity and by `x + y` is translating by `y`, then by `x`.
- **Joint measurability** (`measurable_vadd_pointMeasure`). Each shift is continuous [DVJ2,
  Lemma 12.1.I, p. 178]; route for joint measurability: `(x, ξ) ↦ ξ(B + x) = ∫ 1_B(y - x) ξ(dy)`
  is measurable, then §1.2.
- **Stationarity** (`IsStationary`). The law is invariant under every translation, Mathlib's
  `VAddInvariantMeasure` [LP, Definition 8.1, p. 70].
- **Window form** (`isStationary_iff_forall_map_count`). A measurable point process is stationary
  exactly when the joint count laws on relatively compact windows are translation invariant.
  Route: §2.2.

**Checks.**

- `x +ᵥ δ_x = δ_0`, and `x +ᵥ (y +ᵥ δ_z) = δ_{z - y - x} = (x + y) +ᵥ δ_z`.
- `(x +ᵥ ξ).count A = ξ.count (A + x)` for measurable `A`.
- Negative control: `δ_U` with `U` uniform on `[0, 1]` has translation-invariant total count but
  is not stationary: its count on `[0, 1]` is `1` and on `[5, 6]` it is `0`.
- `unitCube 2` has Lebesgue measure `1`, and the cubes `unitCube d + k`, `k ∈ ℤ^d`, tile `ℝ^d`;
  the closed cube `[0,1]^d` would count lattice points of `ℤ^d` up to `2^d` times.

### 7.2 Intensity of a stationary process

Sources: [LP, Proposition 8.2, p. 70; Proposition 8.4, p. 71]; [BBK, Lemma 6.1.17, p. 302;
Proposition 6.1.18, p. 303]; [DVJ2, Proposition 12.1.VI, p. 187]; [K, Lemma 5.2, p. 158].

- **Intensity density** (`intensityDensity`). `γ = 𝔼 N(U)` for a chosen unit set `U`, here
  `unitCube d`.
- **Stationary intensity** (`IsStationary.intensity_eq`). A stationary point process with finite
  `γ` has intensity `γ · volume` [LP, Proposition 8.2, p. 70]; [BBK, Lemma 6.1.17, p. 302].
- **Stationary Poisson processes** (`IsPoissonPointProcess.isStationary_iff`). A Poisson process
  with locally finite intensity is stationary exactly when its intensity is `γ · volume` for some
  `γ : ℝ≥0` [BBK, Proposition 6.1.18, p. 303].
- Zero–infinity law (prose): a stationary point process has `P(0 < N(ℝ^d) < ∞) = 0` [LP,
  Proposition 8.4, p. 71]; [DVJ2, Proposition 12.1.VI, p. 187]; [K, Lemma 5.2, p. 158].

**Checks.**

- For the stationary Poisson process of intensity `γ`, `intensityDensity = γ`.
- At `d = 0`, `E 0` is a point, Lebesgue measure is `δ_0`, and every point process is stationary.
- Negative control: `δ_U` with `U` uniform on `unitCube d` has `γ = 1` but intensity
  `volume` restricted to the cube, not `1 · volume`; stationarity is needed.

### 7.3 The reduced second factorial moment measure

Sources: [LP, Definition 8.6, p. 71; Proposition 8.7, p. 72]; [DVJ2, Proposition 12.6.III,
p. 238]; [CSKM, §4.5, p. 139].

- **Reduced second factorial moment measure** (`reducedSecondFactorialMomentMeasure`).
  `α₂^!(B) = ∫ 1{x ∈ [0,1)^d, y - x ∈ B} α₂(d(x, y))` [LP, Definition 8.6, p. 71], with the
  half-open cube of the Conventions, which has the same Lebesgue measure as the closed one.
- **Disintegration** (`IsStationary.lintegral_factorialMomentMeasure_two`). For a stationary,
  locally square-integrable point process, `α₂^!` is locally finite and
  `∫ f(x, y) α₂(d(x, y)) = ∫∫ f(x, x + y) α₂^!(dy) dx` [LP, Proposition 8.7, p. 72]; [DVJ2,
  Proposition 12.6.III, p. 238].
- **Poisson** (`IsPoissonPointProcess.reducedSecondFactorialMomentMeasure_eq`). For the stationary
  Poisson process, `α₂^! = γ² · volume`. Route: §5.3 gives `α₂ = γ² volume²`.

**Checks.**

- For the stationary Poisson process on `ℝ` with `γ = 3`, `A = [0, 1]`, `B = [0.5, 2]`:
  `𝔼 N(A)N(B) - 𝔼 N(A ∩ B) = γ²|A||B| = 13.5`.
- `α₂^!` of a stationary process vanishes on `{0}` when the process is simple.
- Negative control: the reduced second moment measure without the factorial correction adds
  `γ δ_0`; for the Poisson process it is `γ² volume + γ δ_0`, not `γ² volume`.

### 7.4 Mixing and ergodicity of the stationary Poisson process

Sources: [LP, (8.13)–(8.14), p. 75; Proposition 8.12, p. 75; Proposition 8.13, p. 76]; [CSKM,
(4.13), p. 115].

- **Mixing** (`IsPoissonPointProcess.tendsto_measureReal_inter_vadd_preimage`). For measurable
  sets `A`, `B` of point measures, `P(N ∈ A, x +ᵥ N ∈ B) → P(N ∈ A) P(N ∈ B)` as `x` leaves
  compact sets [LP, Proposition 8.13, p. 76]. Route: [LP, Proposition 8.12, p. 75] reduces to void
  events, where independence of counts on disjoint windows gives the limit. This is the mixing
  class of group actions of ClassicalErgodicTheory §1.3, written out.
- **Ergodicity** (`IsPoissonPointProcess.ergodicVAdd`). For `d ≥ 1`, Mathlib's `ErgodicVAdd`
  [LP, (8.13), p. 75; Proposition 8.13, p. 76]. Route: mixing along `cocompact (E d)`, a
  nontrivial filter for `d ≥ 1`, implies weak mixing and ergodicity (ClassicalErgodicTheory
  §1.3).

**Checks.**

- For `A = B = {N(unitCube d) = 0}`, the limit is `e^{-2γ}` (`0.002479` at `γ = 3`), the value at
  every `x` whose cube translate is disjoint from the cube.
- Negative control: at `d = 0` the translation group is trivial, `N({0}) ~ Po(γ)` with `γ > 0`, and
  the invariant event `{N = 0}` has probability `e^{-γ} ∈ (0, 1)`; the process is not ergodic, and
  the mixing statement holds only because `cocompact (E 0)` is the trivial filter.
- A mixture of stationary Poisson processes with two intensities is stationary and not ergodic.

## Layer 8: the stationary Palm probability

### 8.1 The Palm probability

Sources: [LP, (9.4) in Theorem 9.1, pp. 82–83]; [SW, Theorem 3.3.1, p. 73]; [BBK, Theorem 6.1.29,
p. 307].

- **Palm probability** (`palmProbability`).
  `P⁰(A) = γ⁻¹ 𝔼 ∫_{[0,1)^d} 1{x +ᵥ N ∈ A} N(dx)`, the Campbell ratio of [LP, (9.4), p. 83] with
  `B = [0,1)^d`.
- **Normalization** (`IsStationary.isProbabilityMeasure_palmProbability`). For `0 < γ < ∞`, `P⁰`
  is a probability measure. Route: its mass is `γ⁻¹ 𝔼 N(unitCube d) = 1`.
- **Window independence.** Any window `B` with `0 < |B| < ∞` in place of `[0,1)^d` gives the same
  `P⁰` [LP, (9.4), p. 83]. Route: the refined Campbell theorem (§8.2) at `f = 1_B ⊗ 1_A`.

**Checks.**

- `P⁰` is carried by point measures with a point at the origin.
- For the lattice process `ℤ^d + U`, `U` uniform on `unitCube d`, `γ = 1` and `P⁰ = δ_{ℤ^d}`, of
  mass `1`.
- Negative control: for `γ = 0`, `γ⁻¹ = ∞` multiplies the zero measure and `P⁰ = 0`, not a
  probability; `0 < γ` is needed.

### 8.2 The refined Campbell theorem

Sources: [LP, Theorem 9.1, pp. 82–83]; [SW, Theorem 3.3.3, p. 74]; [BBK, Theorem 6.1.29, p. 307];
[DVJ2, Theorem 13.2.III, p. 288].

- **Refined Campbell theorem** (`IsStationary.lintegral_vadd_eq_lintegral_palmProbability`).
  `𝔼 ∫ f(x, x +ᵥ N) N(dx) = γ ∫∫ f(x, ξ) P⁰(dξ) dx` for measurable `f ≥ 0` [LP, Theorem 9.1,
  pp. 82–83]; [SW, Theorem 3.3.3, p. 74].

**Checks.**

- `f(x, ξ) = 1_B(x)` gives Campbell's formula `𝔼 N(B) = γ |B|`.
- `f(x, ξ) = 1_B(x) 1{ξ ∈ A}` with `B` the unit cube recovers the definition of `P⁰`.
- Negative control: for `f(x, ξ) = 1_B(x) 1{ξ({0}) ≥ 1}` both sides are `γ|B|`, since `x +ᵥ N`
  has a point at the origin for every point `x` of `N`; without the shift the left side is
  `𝔼 ∫ 1_B(x) 1{N({0}) ≥ 1} N(dx) = 0` for a stationary process of finite intensity.

### 8.3 Slivnyak's theorem

Sources: [LP, Theorem 9.4, p. 85]; [SW, Theorem 3.3.5, p. 77]; [CSKM, (2.43), p. 50].

- **Slivnyak's theorem** (`isPoissonPointProcess_iff_palmProbability`). A stationary point process
  with `0 < γ < ∞` is the Poisson process of intensity `γ` exactly when `P⁰` is the law of
  `N + δ_0` [LP, Theorem 9.4, p. 85]; for simple processes [SW, Theorem 3.3.5, p. 77].

**Checks.**

- For the planar Poisson process of intensity `50`, Slivnyak's theorem gives the Palm probability
  that no other point lies within `r = 0.08` of the origin as
  `P(N(B_r) = 0) = e^{-γπr²} = 0.36593`.
- For a stationary lattice process `ℤ^d + U`, `U` uniform on the cube, `P⁰` is `δ_{ℤ^d}`, which is
  not the law of `N + δ_0`; the process is not Poisson.
- Negative control: the law of `N` itself is not `P⁰`: `P(N({0}) ≥ 1) = 0` for a stationary
  process of finite intensity, while `P⁰(ξ({0}) ≥ 1) = 1`.

## Worked examples

- The binomial process of `m` independent uniform points in a window `W` has
  `αₙ = m^{(n)} (Λ|_W/Λ(W))ⁿ`, and its Janossy measures vanish except the `m`th, which is the
  `m`th power of `Λ|_W/Λ(W)` (§§4.2–4.3); mixing its count with `Po(Λ(W))` gives the Poisson
  process (§5.4).
- The stationary Poisson process of intensity `γ` on `ℝ^d`: `αₙ = γⁿ volumeⁿ`, `α₂^! = γ² volume`,
  `P⁰ = law(N + δ_0)`, and the nearest-neighbour distance under `P⁰` has
  `P⁰(no point in B(0, r) \ {0}) = e^{-γ κ_d r^d}`, `κ_d` the volume of the unit ball.

## Scope and ownership

The roadmap is complete when Tau Ceti proves every statement of `Suggested.lean` and the prose
targets of the layers above.

Inputs:
- Mathlib's Giry monad (`Measure.bind`), kernels and their composition products,
  disintegration, conditional distributions, infinite products of probability
  measures, Measure subtraction, compactly supported continuous functions, Polish spaces,
  `VAddInvariantMeasure`, `ErgodicVAdd`, `cocompact` and `poissonMeasure`.
- Tau Ceti's compound Poisson laws and convolution powers; and its library of random measures
  and Poisson random measures, whose definitions are the Poisson definitions here and whose
  proved theorems are listed under Prior formalization.
- [GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic](../GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic/README.md)
  §4.2, for `configurationMeasure`, the point measure of a finite configuration, with its
  evaluation, permutation invariance and measurability (Exact supplier contracts).
- [ClassicalErgodicTheory](../ClassicalErgodicTheory/README.md) §1.3, for mixing along
  `cocompact` implying ergodicity.

Owned here:
- the carriers of locally finite measures, point measures and marked point measures, the vague
  topology, counts, simplicity, restriction and the point measure of a tuple as an element of
  `PointMeasure S`;
- intensity, Campbell's formula, determination of laws by counts, Laplace functionals and void
  probabilities;
- the Campbell measure and Palm kernels;
- factorial powers, the factorial count moments, factorial moment measures, product densities,
  Janossy measures and moment determinacy;
- the Poisson theory of Layer 5 on the point-measure carrier, including the statements Tau Ceti
  proves in its own form, the operations of Layer 6 and markings;
- translation, stationarity and the second-order theory of stationary point processes on `ℝ^d`,
  mixing and ergodicity of the stationary Poisson process;
- the stationary Palm probability, the refined Campbell theorem and Slivnyak's theorem.

Boundaries with roadmaps on main:
- [RandomMatrices](../RandomMatrices/README.md) §8.1 defines eigenvalue counts, the correlation
  functions of eigenvalue point processes by the factorial-moment identity, and their
  determinacy of the joint count law under exponential moments, through counts only and without
  a configuration space. These are the special case, for the finite point process of the
  eigenvalues on `ℝ` or `ℂ`, of the product densities and moment determinacy of §4.2; they stay
  in RandomMatrices, and this roadmap does not restate them.
- GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.2 owns `configurationMeasure` and the finite Poisson
  configurations on the sigma-type carrier used for its covering construction; this roadmap
  consumes the former and builds its own Poisson theory on measures.
- The rate-`α` Poisson counting process on the line with its filtration is
  [StochasticCalculus](../StochasticCalculus/README.md) §0.6 and
  [MarkovChainsAndRenewal](../MarkovChainsAndRenewal/README.md) Layer 7.
- General mixing theory of group actions is ClassicalErgodicTheory.

Outside this roadmap:
- finite point processes given by a density against the Poisson process, Papangelou intensities,
  the finite-volume Georgii–Nguyen–Zessin formula and hard-core densities;
- Cox processes, and the Poisson cluster processes built from marking, mapping and
  superposition;
- temporal point processes: histories, compensators, stochastic intensities, Watanabe's
  characterisation, the Poisson embedding and Hawkes processes;
- the inversion formula over the Voronoi cell [BBK, Theorem 6.2.8, p. 320] and the mean-volume
  identities of tessellation cells.

Boundary in one sentence: the general theory of random measures and point processes on a locally
compact Polish space, the Poisson random measure and its operations, and the stationary and Palm
theory on `ℝ^d` are here; specific models and their statistics are downstream.
