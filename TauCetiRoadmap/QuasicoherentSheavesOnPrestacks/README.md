# Roadmap: quasi-coherent sheaves on prestacks

**Summary.** A prestack is a functor from test rings to spaces, and a quasi-coherent sheaf on it is a compatible family of modules over all its affine points: the ∞-category `QCoh(X)` is the limit of the module ∞-categories `Mod_A` over the points `Spec A → X`, the right Kan extension of `A ↦ Mod_A` along the Yoneda embedding (SAG Definition 6.2.2.1; GR-I Chapter I.3, §1.1). This roadmap builds that theory once, for three ∞-categories of test rings linked by comparison functors — ordinary commutative rings, animated commutative rings and connective commutative algebra objects of `D(ℤ)` — and proves that the classical and animated theories are restrictions of the last. From EnhancedDerivedSheaves it takes the enhanced derived categories `D∞(R)`, presentable and symmetric monoidal ∞-categories, modules over algebras, animated rings and descendable descent; from SchemeAndStackFoundations it takes schemes, stacks in groupoids, quotient stacks, abelian quasi-coherent modules on stacks and the triangulated category `DQCoh` of a scheme; and it imports, then extends, three local theories: the classical `QCohPrestack` of PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0, faithfully flat descent for animated modules and the quasi-coherent categories of derived parameter stacks from LanglandsParameterStacks §§1.7–1.8, and the lattice quotients of HabiroCohomologyFoundations §§1.27–1.28. It supplies the coherent module-category functor and the module theory of connective algebras (t-structure, flatness, perfect and almost perfect modules), flat hyperdescent for module categories over arbitrary connective test rings with no finiteness or descendability hypothesis, invariance of `QCoh` under fpqc (hyper)sheafification and Čech descent, pushforward along affine morphisms with base change and the projection formula, the identification of `QCoh` of the functor of points of every Mathlib scheme with its quasi-coherent derived sheaves, the t-structure and the perfect, dualizable, invertible and almost perfect objects, quotients by group prestacks over a base and by discrete groups, classical quotient stacks with their hearts, and compact generation: qcqs schemes, perfect stacks with their permanence properties, quotient stacks in characteristic zero and the failure of compactness of the structure sheaf in positive characteristic. The main sources are Lurie's *Spectral Algebraic Geometry* (§§6.2–6.3, 9.1 and Appendix D.6) and *Higher Algebra* (§§4.5–4.8, 7.1–7.2), Gaitsgory–Rozenblyum's *A study in derived algebraic geometry* I, Chapter I.3, Ben-Zvi–Francis–Nadler §3, and the Stacks Project.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the functor-of-points theory of quasi-coherent sheaves in the animated and `E∞` contexts of test rings fixed in Conventions, the comparison of these with the classical context, and the classical results that no other roadmap states. Three roadmaps state parts of the theory; this roadmap imports what they state and extends it, and each extended statement says where it agrees with the import:

- PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0 owns the classical theory on prestacks over ordinary rings. This covers `QCohPrestack(X) = lim D(R)` over the points of X, with evaluation, pullback, affine values, the colimit-to-limit property and pointwise detection of equivalences. It also covers perfect = dualizable and pointwise invertibility, Čech descent along faithfully flat ring maps, invariance under fpqc sheafification, and pushforward, base change and the projection formula along flat affine maps. In the classical context `QCoh` is `QCohPrestack`, and the classical cases of 2.2–2.3, 3.2–3.3, 4.2 and 6.3 are these imports, except the presentability of 2.3, which §4.0 asserts only in a larger universe. The statements here extend them to the animated and `E∞` contexts and to flat hypercovers, and 2.4 identifies the classical theory with the restriction of the `E∞` one.
- LanglandsParameterStacks §1.7 owns Čech descent of module categories, and of perfect modules, along faithfully flat maps of animated rings. 3.2 extends it to flat hypercovers and to connective commutative algebras of `D(ℤ)`, and agrees with it on Čech nerves.
- LanglandsParameterStacks §1.8 owns the enhanced `QCoh` and `Perf` of its derived parameter stacks, the quotients `[Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ]` of derived cocycle spaces by the dual group and the classifying stack `BĤ` over animated rings: `QCoh` is the totalization of module categories over the action-groupoid nerve, and `Perf` its chartwise dualizable objects. 7.2, in the animated context, extends this to every action of a group prestack on a prestack and states the agreement there: both sides are the limit over the same bar construction, and the perfect objects of that limit (6.3) are those whose term on the chart is dualizable (1.5).
- HabiroCohomologyFoundations §1.27 owns the lattice quotient `QCoh([Spec C/ℤ^d]) ≃ D(C)^{hℤ^d}`, and §1.28 its heart, vector bundles and perfect complexes. 7.3 extends the first to every discrete group acting on every prestack. 6.2(a) and 6.3 give the t-structure and the perfect and locally free objects on every prestack in their scope, and 7.3 states the agreement with both imports on the lattice quotient.

## Conventions

**Higher categories.** "∞-category" means a quasicategory in Mathlib's sense (`SSet.Quasicategory`), "space" a Kan complex, and all limits, colimits, Kan extensions and adjunctions are those of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0 and E3, characterized by mapping spaces. An equivalence of ∞-categories is a categorical equivalence, never only an equivalence of homotopy categories. Every functor between stable ∞-categories named here is exact, and every tensor product, pullback, pushforward, limit and colimit is the ∞-categorical one.

**Universes.** Fix a universe u. Test rings are u-small. `S` is the ∞-category of u-small spaces and `Ŝ` that of spaces in the universe u+1; `Ĉat∞` is the ∞-category of (u+1)-small ∞-categories, EnhancedDerivedSheaves' `Cat∞` (E0.5) formed in the universe u+1; it has all (u+1)-small limits. Prestacks take values in `Ŝ`, so that every (u+1)-small colimit of corepresentable functors is a prestack. Each of the three ∞-categories of test rings is (u+1)-small, and so is the ∞-category of points `Pts(X)` of every prestack X (2.1). Every `Mod_A` is (u+1)-small, and so is every `QCoh(X)`, a limit indexed by `Pts(X)`; so `QCoh(X)` is an object of `CAlg(Ĉat∞)`. `PreStk_T` is locally (u+1)-small but not (u+1)-small, its objects forming a set of the universe u+2 and not of u+1 (already the constant prestacks do); it is not an object of `Ĉat∞`, and no statement here needs it to be. Witness: `Mod_ℤ`, and `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k)` for a u-small group Γ and a u-small field k, are objects of `Ĉat∞`. "Presentable" means u-presentable in the sense of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.8 (`IsPresentable`): accessible for some regular cardinal, with all u-small colimits. Universe changes are the explicit comparison functors of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.5 and E0.8. Unqualified "small" means u-small and "large" means not u-small; "(u+1)-small" is always written out. So `Mod_A` and every `QCoh(X)` admit small colimits, while limits and colimits of prestacks and of fpqc sheaves, and limits in `Ĉat∞`, are (u+1)-small. Every supplier statement about small ∞-categories, presheaf ∞-categories or ∞-topoi that is applied to T, `T^op`, the nerve of affine schemes, `Pts(X)` or another (u+1)-small indexing ∞-category, to `PreStk_T` or its slices, or to fpqc sheaves on `T^op` and fppf sheaves on `CRing^op`, is applied in the universe u+1, with `Ŝ` as its ∞-category of spaces and `Ĉat∞` as its `Cat∞`. These are straightening, `derivedFamily` and the Yoneda embedding (1.2, 2.1, 2.5), limits as coCartesian sections (1.5, 2.2), the free cocompletion (2.3, 3.1), accessible localization and its universal property (3.1), the topologies generated by the faithfully flat maps and by the faithfully flat maps of finite presentation of ordinary rings, the Čech and hypercomplete sheaf criteria and the comparison of valued sheaves on a site and on its ∞-topos (2.5, 3.1, 3.2), sheaves and (hyper)sheafification (2.5, 3.1–3.4, 4.1), hypercompleteness of truncated objects and hypercompletion (3.1, 3.2, 3.4), and the ∞-topos statements: universal colimits, effective groupoid objects and effective epimorphisms (2.5, 3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 4.1, 6.2, 7.1, 7.4). In that universe `PreStk_T`, the presheaf ∞-category of `T^op`, is presentable and an ∞-topos. Statements about presentable ∞-categories applied to `Mod_A`, to `QCoh(X)` and to u-small diagrams of them (`IsPresentable`, `presentable_limit`, `presentable_limit_right`, `adjointFunctorTheorem`, StableInfinityCategories §11.1.1) take presentability and smallness in the universe u, their limits being formed in `Ĉat∞`: in the universe u+1, `D∞(ℤ)` is not presentable, as the witness below shows. Witness: PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3 is stated for a u-small simplicial set, which `T^op` is not; applied in the universe u+1 to `T^op`, its presheaf ∞-category is `Fun(T, Ŝ) = PreStk_T`, and it gives the colimit-to-limit property of 2.3 for (u+1)-small colimits of prestacks. Witness: for a set K of the universe u+1 that is not u-small, `QCoh(⨿_K Spec ℤ) ≃ ∏_K D∞(ℤ)` is a (u+1)-small limit in `Ĉat∞` (2.3), and each factor `D∞(ℤ)` has small colimits but no coproduct of K copies of ℤ, since such a coproduct C would have `π₀ Map(C, ℚ/ℤ) = ∏_K ℚ/ℤ`, which is not u-small, whereas the mapping spaces of `D∞(ℤ)` are.

**Test rings.** The three ∞-categories of test rings are:

- `CRing`, the nerve of Mathlib's `CommRingCat` of u-small commutative rings (the classical context);
- `AnRing`, the ∞-category `AnimAlg_ℤ` of animated commutative rings of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:animation.1 (the animated context);
- `CAlg^cn`, the ∞-category `CAlg(D(ℤ)_{≥0})` of commutative algebra objects of the connective part of `D∞(ℤ)`, the target of θ in EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:animation.4 (the `E∞` context over ℤ).

They are linked by `ι : CRing → AnRing`, the inclusion of discrete animated rings (EnhancedDerivedSheaves `animatedDiscrete`, with left adjoint `animatedPiZero`), and `θ : AnRing → CAlg^cn` (EnhancedDerivedSheaves `animatedToCAlg` for `R = ℤ`). Over a base ring containing ℚ the analogue of θ is an equivalence (`animatedToCAlg_equiv_of_rational` of that section); over ℤ it is not an equivalence (Check of 1.1). `T` denotes any of the three; a test ring is an object of `T`, and `T^op` is the ∞-category of affines.

**Indexing.** Modules over test rings use homological indexing: for a module M with underlying object of `D∞(ℤ)`, `π_n M = H^{−n}(M)`, and `M[1]` is the suspension, Mathlib's cochain shift by one (cohomological degree −1). M is connective if `π_n M = 0` for `n < 0` and n-truncated if `π_m M = 0` for `m > n`. Witness: `M = 𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p` has `π₀ M = π₁ M = 𝔽_p` and no other homotopy, that is cohomology `𝔽_p` in Mathlib degrees 0 and −1; it is connective and not 0-truncated. A module M is bounded below if `π_m M = 0` for `m ≪ 0`. `QCoh(X)^+` is the union over n of the subcategories `QCoh(X)_{≤n}` of n-truncated objects for the t-structure of 6.2, on `Spec A` the modules with `π_m = 0` for `m > n`; for an abelian category 𝒜, `D⁺(𝒜)` and `D⁻(𝒜)` are the derived ∞-categories of complexes whose cohomology vanishes in sufficiently negative, respectively sufficiently positive, Mathlib degrees. So the `+` of `QCoh(X)^+` and `D⁺` is a cohomological bound, opposite to "bounded below". Witness: `∏_{i≥0} ℤ[−i]` is 0-truncated and lies in `QCoh(Spec ℤ)^+`, but has `π_{−i} = ℤ` for every `i ≥ 0` and is not bounded below; `⊕_{i≥0} ℤ[i]` is connective, hence bounded below, and lies in no `QCoh(Spec ℤ)_{≤n}`.

**Flatness.** A module M over a test ring A is flat if `π₀M` is flat over `π₀A` (Mathlib `Module.Flat`) and `π_n A ⊗_{π₀A} π₀M → π_n M` is an isomorphism for every n; it is faithfully flat if moreover `π₀M` is faithfully flat over `π₀A` (Mathlib `Module.FaithfullyFlat`). A map of test rings is (faithfully) flat if its target is a (faithfully) flat module (HA Definition 7.2.2.10, p. 1240). Witness: `ℤ → ℤ[x]` is faithfully flat; `ℤ → ℚ` is flat and not faithfully flat; `ℤ → 𝔽_p` is not flat; and `𝔽_p → 𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, along either factor, is not flat, since `π₁` of the source is 0 while `π₁` of the target is `𝔽_p`. For discrete A and M the definition is Mathlib's (HA Remark 7.2.2.12).

**Covers.** A finite family `{A → B_i}` of maps of test rings is an fpqc cover if `A → ∏_i B_i` is faithfully flat (SAG Remark D.6.1.1, p. 2174). Witness: the empty family covers exactly the zero ring, and `{ℤ → ℤ[1/2], ℤ → ℤ[1/3]}` covers ℤ.

**Variance and base change.** Prestacks are covariant functors `T → Ŝ`; `Spec A` is the functor corepresented by A, and a point of X is a pair (A, η) with `η ∈ X(A)`, equivalently `η : Spec A → X`. Pullback `f^*` is contravariant in f and pushforward `f_*` is its right adjoint. For a pullback square with `f : Y → X`, `g : X′ → X`, `f′ : Y′ → X′` and `g′ : Y′ → Y`, the Beck–Chevalley map is the mate `g^* f_* → f′_* g′^*` of the identification `f′^* g^* ≃ g′^* f^*` (EnhancedDerivedSheaves `coherentMate`). Witness: in the classical context the square of `Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ ← Spec 𝔽_p` has fibre product `Spec 𝔽_p`; for `F = 𝔽_p` the source of the Beck–Chevalley map is `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, with `π₁ = 𝔽_p`, and its target is `𝔽_p`, so the map is not an equivalence. In `AnRing` the fibre product is `Spec(𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p)` and both sides are `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`.

## Layer 1: Module categories of test rings

This layer assembles the higher algebra of module categories that every later layer uses. Throughout, `D(ℤ)` is EnhancedDerivedSheaves' `D∞(ℤ)` with its symmetric monoidal structure `derivedTensor_symmetricMonoidal`, a presentably symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category whose tensor product preserves colimits in each variable, with the standard t-structure (cohomology in Mathlib's degrees ≤ 0 is the connective part).

### 1.1 Test rings and their comparison functors

Take the three ∞-categories of test rings `CRing`, `AnRing`, `CAlg^cn` and the functors `ι : CRing → AnRing` and `θ : AnRing → CAlg^cn` of the Conventions. For a test ring A write `π_n A` for `π_n` of its underlying object of `D(ℤ)` (for an animated ring, of the underlying object of θA); `π₀A` is an ordinary commutative ring, `π_*A` a graded-commutative ring and each `π_n A` a `π₀A`-module. A test ring is discrete when `π_n A = 0` for `n ≠ 0` (SAG Definition 6.2.2.1 and Remark 6.2.2.2, pp. 490–491, for the role of connective test rings; HA Proposition 7.1.3.18, p. 1219, for discrete commutative algebras as ordinary rings).

The API consists of the following facts.

- θ and θι preserve homotopy groups: `π_*(θB) = π_*(B)` and `θι(R)` is discrete with `π₀ = R`.
- θ preserves small colimits, in particular pushouts. It is the sifted-colimit-preserving extension of the inclusion of finitely generated polynomial rings into `CAlg^cn`, and that inclusion preserves finite coproducts. The empty coproduct is ℤ, initial in both. A binary coproduct in `CAlg^cn` is the tensor product `A ⊗ᴸ_ℤ B` (HA Proposition 3.2.4.7, p. 371), and for polynomial rings `ℤ[x₁, …, x_m] ⊗ᴸ_ℤ ℤ[y₁, …, y_n]` is the discrete polynomial ring `ℤ[x₁, …, x_m, y₁, …, y_n]`, a polynomial ring being free over ℤ, so that its higher Tor groups over ℤ vanish. So the extension preserves all small colimits (`animationExtension`). It also preserves finite products, which are limits and do not follow from this: θ commutes with the forgetful functors of `AnRing` and `CAlg^cn` to `D(ℤ)_{≥0}`, which are conservative and preserve limits, and finite products of test rings are computed on underlying objects.
- ι is fully faithful with left adjoint `π₀` (`animatedDiscrete`, `animatedPiZero`), so it preserves limits; it does not preserve pushouts.
- For discrete A, B, C the pushout `B ⊗_A C` in `AnRing` or `CAlg^cn` has `π_n = Tor_n^A(B, C)` (`animatedPushout`); the pushout in `CRing` is the ordinary tensor product, and the two agree exactly when the positive Tor groups vanish, for instance when B or C is flat over A.
- In each of the three ∞-categories the zero ring is a final object and ℤ (respectively ιℤ, θιℤ) an initial object, and a finite product of test rings is computed by the product of underlying objects.

**Checks.**

- `𝔽_p ⊗_ℤ 𝔽_p` computed in `AnRing` has `π₀ = π₁ = 𝔽_p` and no other homotopy, and θ carries it to the pushout in `CAlg^cn`, with the same homotopy; the pushout in `CRing` is `𝔽_p`, with no `π₁`, so ι does not preserve this pushout.
- Degenerate case: the zero ring is final in all three categories, `θι(0) = 0` and `π_*(0) = 0`; a definition of "test ring" excluding the zero ring would make the empty family of 3.1 cover nothing.
- Products are limits, not colimits: `θι(ℤ × ℤ)` is discrete with `π₀ = ℤ²`, the product of θιℤ with itself in `CAlg^cn`, whereas the coproduct of θιℤ with itself is `ℤ ⊗ᴸ_ℤ ℤ = ℤ`; colimit preservation alone says nothing about the first.
- θ is not an equivalence: it sends the animated polynomial ring `ℤ[x]`, which corepresents the underlying space, to a discrete object, whereas the free object of `CAlg^cn` on `ℤ[0]` has the summand `(ℤ ⊗ᴸ ℤ)_{hΣ₂}`, whose `π₁ = H₁(BΣ₂; ℤ) = ℤ/2` is nonzero.

### 1.2 The module-category functor

Construct the functor `Mod : CAlg^cn → CAlg(PrL_st)` sending a connective commutative algebra A of `D(ℤ)` to the presentably symmetric monoidal stable ∞-category `Mod_A = Mod_A(D(ℤ))` of `moduleObjects`, with unit A, and a map `f : A → B` to extension of scalars `f^* = B ⊗_A − : Mod_A → Mod_B` (`extensionScalars`), as a functor of ∞-categories with all higher coherences. Restricting along θ and θι gives the module functors of `AnRing` and `CRing`; their values are `animatedModules` and `D∞(R)` (HA Theorem 4.5.3.1 and Remark 4.5.3.2, p. 601; HA Corollary 4.8.5.21, p. 754; SAG Definition 6.2.2.1, pp. 490–491).

The API consists of the following facts.

- For `f : A → B`, `f^*` is symmetric monoidal and preserves small colimits; its right adjoint `f_*`, restriction of scalars, preserves small limits and colimits and is conservative, and the projection formula `f_*(f^*M ⊗_B N) ≃ M ⊗_A f_*N` holds for `M ∈ Mod_A`, `N ∈ Mod_B`.
- Base change: for a pushout square of `CAlg^cn` with `B′ = B ⊗_A A′`, the square of extension functors is right adjointable, that is `A′ ⊗_A f_*N ≃ f′_*(B′ ⊗_B N)` for `N ∈ Mod_B`; and `Mod_{B′} ≃ Mod_B ⊗_{Mod_A} Mod_{A′}` in `PrL` (`categoricalModuleBaseChange`; HA Theorem 4.8.4.6, p. 737).
- Products: `Mod_{A × B} ≃ Mod_A × Mod_B`, symmetric monoidally, and `Mod_0` is the zero category.
- Classical values: for `R ∈ CRing`, `Mod_{θιR} ≃ D∞(R)` symmetric monoidally and naturally in R, with extension of scalars along `R → S` corresponding to the derived base change `S ⊗ᴸ_R −` (`animatedModules_discrete`, `animatedModules_baseChange`, `enhancement_compatibility`); for `B ∈ AnRing`, `Mod_{θB}` is `animatedModules B`.

*Proof route.* HA Theorem 4.5.3.1 applies to `D(ℤ)` because it admits geometric realizations and its tensor product preserves them in each variable: the fibration of pairs (A, M) over commutative algebras is coCartesian with symmetric monoidal fibres. Classify it by the supplied straightening, or by `derivedFamily`, applied in the universe u+1 over the (u+1)-small `CAlg^cn`, to obtain a functor to symmetric monoidal ∞-categories; its values are presentable and stable and its transitions preserve colimits (`moduleObjects`, `extensionScalars`), so it lands in `CAlg(PrL_st)`. The projection formula holds for `M = A` and both sides preserve colimits in M, and A generates `Mod_A` under colimits and shifts. Base change is checked on underlying objects of `D(ℤ)`, where both sides are `A′ ⊗_A N`. Products follow from the idempotents of `A × B`.

**Checks.**

- `Mod_ℤ = D∞(ℤ)` with unit `ℤ[0]`, and `Mod_0 = 0`, the zero ring having only the zero module.
- For the pushout square of `ℤ → 𝔽_p` with itself in `CAlg^cn`, so `B′ = 𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, both sides of the base-change map at `N = 𝔽_p` are `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, with `π₁ = 𝔽_p`. Replacing `B′` by the ordinary tensor product `𝔽_p` gives `𝔽_p` on the right, so the square of ordinary rings is not right adjointable.
- Projection formula for `f : R → R[t]`, `M = R[1]`, `N = R[t]`: both sides are the R-module `R[t][1]`, free of infinite rank in degree one; an underived or unshifted formula fails the degree.
- The transition of the classical functor along `ℤ → ℤ/p` sends `ℤ/p` to `ℤ/p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ ℤ/p`, with `π₀ = π₁ = ℤ/p`; the underived extension of scalars would give only `ℤ/p`.

### 1.3 Connective modules: the t-structure and flatness

For `A ∈ CAlg^cn`, define `Mod_A^cn` (respectively `Mod_{A,≤0}`) as the full subcategory of modules whose underlying object of `D(ℤ)` is connective (respectively has `π_n = 0` for `n > 0`), and `π_n M` as `π_n` of the underlying object. Define flat and faithfully flat modules and maps as in the Conventions (HA Definition 7.2.2.10, p. 1240). Prove that `(Mod_A^cn, Mod_{A,≤0})` is an accessible t-structure on `Mod_A`, left and right complete, with both halves closed under small products and filtered colimits, that `Mod_A^cn` is the smallest full subcategory containing A and closed under small colimits, and that `π₀` identifies the heart with the nerve of `ModuleCat (π₀A)`; and that for a flat A-module N and every M, `π_n(M ⊗_A N) ≅ π_n M ⊗_{π₀A} π₀N` (HA Proposition 7.1.1.13, p. 1202; HA Proposition 7.2.2.13, p. 1240; HA Theorem 7.2.2.15, pp. 1240–1241).

*Proof route.* HA states these results for connective `E₁`-rings in `Sp`. For a commutative algebra A of `D(ℤ)_{≥0}` the same arguments apply to `Mod_A(D(ℤ))`: they use that `Mod_A` is presentable and stable with compact generator A, that the forgetful functor to `D(ℤ)` is conservative, t-exact by definition and preserves limits and colimits, the t-structure generated by a set of objects (HA Proposition 1.4.4.11, p. 160, a statement about any presentable stable ∞-category; StableInfinityCategories §11.1.1), and the Künneth spectral sequence of the bar construction computing `π_*(M ⊗_A N)` from `Tor^{π_*A}` (HA Proposition 7.2.1.19, p. 1236). Left completeness of `D(ℤ)`, in which countable products are exact, transfers along the conservative limit-preserving forgetful functor.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Extension of scalars is right t-exact and restriction of scalars is t-exact.
- A flat module is connective, and extension of scalars along a flat map is t-exact; along a faithfully flat map it is moreover conservative on all of `Mod_A`, since `π_n(B ⊗_A M) = π₀B ⊗_{π₀A} π_n M` and `π₀B` is faithfully flat over `π₀A`.
- Flat and faithfully flat maps are stable under composition and under base change along arbitrary maps of `CAlg^cn`; ι and θ preserve and reflect them.
- Lazard: a connective A-module is flat exactly when it is a filtered colimit of finite free A-modules. For discrete A and M this is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.flatModuleFilteredFree`, filtered colimits of discrete modules being discrete.
- For discrete `A = θιR` and discrete M, flatness is Mathlib's `Module.Flat` (HA Remark 7.2.2.12).

**Checks.**

- The heart of `Mod_{𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p}` is `ModuleCat 𝔽_p`, since `π₀ = 𝔽_p`, although `Mod_{𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p}` is not `D∞(𝔽_p)`: its unit has `π₁ = 𝔽_p`.
- `ℤ → ℤ[1/p] × ℤ_p` is faithfully flat: both factors are flat (a localization, and the torsion-free ℤ-module `ℤ_p`), and every prime of ℤ lies under a prime of a factor (`(p)` under `pℤ_p`, the others under primes of `ℤ[1/p]`).
- `ℤ → ℚ` is flat but not faithfully flat: `ℚ ⊗ᴸ_ℤ ℤ/p = 0`, so extension of scalars is not conservative.
- Extension of scalars along the non-flat `ℤ → 𝔽_p` is not t-exact: it carries the discrete `𝔽_p` to `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, which is not discrete.

### 1.4 Perfect, almost perfect and locally free modules

For `A ∈ CAlg^cn`, define a module to be perfect if it lies in the smallest stable full subcategory of `Mod_A` containing A and closed under retracts (HA Definition 7.2.4.1, p. 1260; BZFN Definition 3.1, p. 17); almost perfect if it lies in `Mod_{A,≥k}` for some k and is almost compact there, that is `Map(M, −)` commutes with filtered colimits on `τ_{≤n} Mod_{A,≥k}` for every n (HA Definition 7.2.4.10, p. 1265); and locally free of finite rank if it is a direct summand of `A^n` for some n, equivalently flat and almost perfect (SAG Definition 2.9.1.1 and Remark 2.9.1.2, p. 277).

The API consists of the following facts.

- `Mod_A` is compactly generated by A, and the perfect, compact and dualizable objects of `Mod_A` coincide (HA Propositions 7.2.4.2 and 7.2.4.4, pp. 1261–1263; SAG proof of Proposition 6.2.6.2, p. 497).
- Almost perfect modules form a stable subcategory closed under retracts and containing the perfect ones; a connective almost perfect module is the geometric realization of a simplicial module that is finite free in each degree; if `π₀A` is coherent and each `π_nA` is finitely presented over `π₀A`, a module is almost perfect exactly when it is bounded below (`π_m M = 0` for `m ≪ 0`) and each `π_m M` is finitely presented over `π₀A` (HA Propositions 7.2.4.11 and 7.2.4.17, pp. 1265–1268).
- A connective module is a dualizable object of `Mod_A^cn` exactly when it is locally free of finite rank (SAG Proposition 2.9.1.5, p. 278).
- n-connectivity, flatness, perfectness, almost perfectness and local freeness of finite rank are stable under base change along every map of `CAlg^cn` (SAG Proposition 6.2.5.2, pp. 494–495); their locality for the fpqc topology is 6.1.
- For `A = θιR` discrete, under `Mod_A ≃ D∞(R)` and `enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory`: perfect modules are those satisfying `DerivedDeRham.IsPerfect`, almost perfect modules those satisfying `IsPseudoCoherent`, and Tor amplitude is `HasTorAmplitude` (DerivedModuleAlgebra §1.E.0).

**Checks.**

- `ℤ ⊕ ℤ[3]` is perfect over ℤ and dualizable, with dual `ℤ ⊕ ℤ[−3]`; ℚ and `⊕_ℕ ℤ` are not perfect: the identity of `⊕_ℕ ℤ` does not factor through a finite subsum.
- Over `A = k[ε]/(ε²)` for a field k, `M = k` is almost perfect (A is Noetherian and `π₀M` is finitely presented) but not perfect: `Tor_i^A(k, k) = k` for every `i ≥ 0`, whereas a perfect module has bounded Tor amplitude.
- Over `A = k[x₁, x₂, …]`, the quotient `k = A/(x₁, x₂, …)` is not almost perfect: `π₀` of a connective almost perfect module is finitely presented, and the maximal ideal is not finitely generated.
- `ℤ/p` over ℤ is connective and perfect but not locally free: its dual `ℤ/p[−1]` is not connective, so it is not dualizable in `Mod_ℤ^cn`.
- n-truncatedness is not stable under base change: `𝔽_p` over ℤ is 0-truncated, and its base change to `𝔽_p` is `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, with `π₁ ≠ 0`.

*Needs:* 1.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.1 (`compactObjects`, `IsCompactlyGenerated`), E5:abstract.4 (`idemCompletion`), E1.3 (`enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory`); DerivedModuleAlgebra §1.E.0 (`DerivedDeRham.IsPerfect`, `IsPseudoCoherent`, `HasTorAmplitude`).

### 1.5 Limits of module categories

Prove three statements about limits of ∞-categories, used for diagrams of module categories in Layers 3, 4 and 6.

- **Adjointable limits.** Let `F : χ → χ′` be a natural transformation of diagrams `K → Ĉat∞` indexed by a (u+1)-small ∞-category K, such that every component `F_k` has a right adjoint `G_k` and every naturality square is right adjointable. Then `lim F` has a right adjoint computed componentwise, and every projection square is right adjointable (HA Corollary 4.7.4.18, p. 699, in the form used in the proof of SAG Proposition 6.3.4.1, p. 506).
- **Dualizable and invertible objects.** For a diagram of symmetric monoidal ∞-categories with limit C, an object of C is dualizable exactly when each of its images is dualizable (HA Proposition 4.6.1.11 and Remark 4.6.1.12, p. 620), and invertible exactly when each image is invertible. The invertible half follows from the dualizable half: in a symmetric monoidal ∞-category an object F is invertible exactly when it is dualizable and its evaluation `F^∨ ⊗ F → 1` is an equivalence, since an inverse is a dual and duality data are unique up to equivalence (SAG Remark 2.9.5.5, p. 283, records this criterion for quasi-coherent sheaves on spectral Deligne–Mumford stacks); the projections of C carry duality data to duality data; and a morphism of a limit of ∞-categories is an equivalence exactly when each of its images is (on coCartesian sections, the model of the limit in EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.7, by HTT Proposition 2.4.1.5, PDF p. 134, and Proposition 3.1.2.1, PDF pp. 169–170, in coCartesian form).
- **Descent criterion.** For an augmented cosimplicial ∞-category `C^{−1} → C^•` whose squares satisfy the Beck–Chevalley condition (each coface square is right adjointable), the functor `C^{−1} → lim C^•` is an equivalence exactly when `C^{−1} → C⁰` is comonadic: conservative, and preserving totalizations of cosimplicial objects split after applying it (HA Theorem 4.7.5.2 and Corollary 4.7.5.3, pp. 704–705, in dual form; HA Proposition 5.2.2.36, p. 835).

*Proof route.* The first two are limit arguments on coCartesian sections: adjoints, mates and duality data are defined by finitely many coherent maps and units, and a limit of such data is such data (EnhancedDerivedSheaves `coherentMate` on the coCartesian sections of E0.7, both applied in the universe u+1, as HA Corollary 4.7.4.18 is here; `presentable_limit_right` gives the limit of the right adjoints only for u-small diagrams of presentable ∞-categories). The third reduces, through the comparison with comodules over the comonad of `C^{−1} → C⁰`, to the comonadic form of `barrBeckLurie`, obtained by passing to opposite ∞-categories.

**Checks.**

- The empty diagram has limit the terminal ∞-category, the zero category; its unique object is dualizable and invertible, and the componentwise right adjoint is the identity.
- In `D∞(ℤ) × D∞(ℤ)`, the pair (ℤ, ℚ) is not dualizable, since ℚ is not perfect; the pair (ℤ, ℤ[1]) is invertible, both evaluations `ℤ ⊗ ℤ → ℤ` and `ℤ[−1] ⊗ ℤ[1] → ℤ` being equivalences; the pair (ℤ, ℤ ⊕ ℤ) is dualizable and not invertible, the evaluation `(ℤ²)^∨ ⊗ ℤ² → ℤ` of its second component going from rank 4 to rank 1.
- Adjointability is a hypothesis, not a consequence: the square of extension functors of the ordinary pushout `𝔽_p = 𝔽_p ⊗_ℤ 𝔽_p` (1.2) is not right adjointable, and over a diagram containing it the right adjoint of the limit, which exists by `adjointFunctorTheorem`, is not computed componentwise.
- Compactness is not detected componentwise, unlike dualizability: on the constant prestack `B(ℤ/p) × Spec 𝔽_p` the unit is componentwise compact and dualizable, and not compact (8.5).

*Needs:* 1.2; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.2 (`presentable_limit`, `presentable_limit_right`), E3.2 (`adjointFunctorTheorem`), E3.4 (`coherentMate`, `beckChevalley`), E5:abstract.5 (`barrBeckLurie`), E0.7 (coCartesian sections as limits); CartesianFibrations §1.3.1 (HTT Proposition 2.4.1.5), §5.5.3 (HTT Proposition 3.1.2.1).

### Examples

- Over `A = k[ε]/(ε²)` the residue field is almost perfect, not perfect, and its base change along the flat map `A → A[t]` is `k[t]`, again almost perfect and not perfect.

## Layer 2: Prestacks and quasi-coherent sheaves

This layer defines prestacks and their quasi-coherent sheaves for each of the three ∞-categories of test rings and proves everything that follows from the definition alone: affine values, limits, stability, colimits, the pointwise tensor product, presentability for small colimits of affines, invariance under change of test rings, and the classical prestacks of schemes and stacks in groupoids.

### 2.1 Prestacks and their points

Define `PreStk_T = Fun(T, Ŝ)`, the ∞-category of prestacks on T, and `Spec : T^op → PreStk_T`, `Spec A = Map_T(A, −)`, the Yoneda embedding. For a prestack X, define its ∞-category of points `Pts(X)` as the left fibration over T classified by X under the supplied straightening; an object is a pair (A, η) with `η ∈ X(A)`, equivalently a map `η : Spec A → X` (SAG Notation 6.2.2.3 and Remark 6.2.2.7, pp. 491–492).

The API consists of the following facts.

- Spec is fully faithful, and `Map(Spec A, X) ≃ X(A)` naturally in A and X.
- (u+1)-small limits and colimits in `PreStk_T` are computed pointwise; the empty prestack ∅ is initial and `Spec ℤ` (the image of the initial test ring) is final.
- Every prestack is the colimit of the diagram `Pts(X)^op → PreStk_T`, `(A, η) ↦ Spec A`.
- Fibre products of affines are affines: `Spec B ×_{Spec A} Spec C ≃ Spec(B ⊗_A C)`, with the pushout of T, which is derived in `AnRing` and `CAlg^cn` and ordinary in `CRing` (1.1).
- A map `f : Y → X` induces `Pts(f) : Pts(Y) → Pts(X)` over T, and a space K defines the constant prestack with value K, the colimit over K of the final prestack.

**Checks.**

- `Pts(Spec A)` has the initial object `(A, id)`; a definition of points through ordinary fibred categories would lose the higher homotopy of `X(A)`, which for the constant prestack `BΓ` of a group Γ is `π₁ = Γ`.
- `Spec 0` is not the empty prestack: its value at the zero ring is a point; the two become equivalent after fpqc sheafification (3.3), where the empty family covers 0.
- In `CRing`, `Spec 𝔽_p ×_{Spec ℤ} Spec 𝔽_p = Spec 𝔽_p`, while in `AnRing` the same fibre product is `Spec(𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p)`, a different affine.

*Needs:* 1.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.3–E0.5 (functor ∞-categories, the supplied Yoneda embedding and space-valued straightening, with the universe comparisons of E0.5).

### 2.2 The ∞-category of quasi-coherent sheaves

Define `QCoh : PreStk_T^op → CAlg(Ĉat∞)` as the right Kan extension along `Spec` of `Spec A ↦ Mod_A` (1.2), taken in symmetric monoidal ∞-categories; equivalently `QCoh(X) = lim_{(A,η) ∈ Pts(X)} Mod_A`, the ∞-category of sections of the pullback to `Pts(X)` of the coCartesian fibration of 1.2 that carry every arrow to a coCartesian arrow. Define the evaluation `η^* : QCoh(X) → Mod_A` at a point, the pullback `f^* : QCoh(X) → QCoh(Y)` along `f : Y → X` (restriction along `Pts(f)`), and the structure sheaf `O_X`, the unit of `QCoh(X)` (SAG Definition 6.2.2.1, pp. 490–491; SAG §6.2.6 and Notation 6.2.6.1, pp. 496–497; GR-I Chapter I.3, §§1.1.3–1.1.4, pp. 144–145). In the classical context this is PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `QCohPrestack`: both are the limit over `Pts(X)` of `R ↦ D∞(R)` with transitions `S ⊗ᴸ_R −`, and 1.2's classical values identify the two coherent diagrams naturally in R. In that context the evaluation, pullback, affine value and unit below are `QCohPrestack.eval`, `pullback`, `specEquiv` and `unit_eval`, and the statements here are their extension to the animated and `E∞` contexts.

The API consists of the following facts.

- `QCoh(Spec A) ≃ Mod_A`, symmetric monoidally and naturally in A, by evaluation at the initial point (SAG Remark 6.2.2.5, p. 491).
- Pullback is functorial with coherent identifications `(f g)^* ≃ g^* f^*` and `id^* ≃ id`, and `η^*` is the pullback along `η : Spec A → X` followed by the affine identification.
- An object F amounts to modules `F(η) = η^*F ∈ Mod_A` with equivalences `B ⊗_A F(η) ≃ F(φη)` for `φ : A → B`, coherently; `η^* O_X = A` for every point (SAG Remark 6.2.2.7 and Notation 6.2.6.1).
- Mapping spaces are limits: `Map(F, G) ≃ lim_{(A,η)} Map_{Mod_A}(F(η), G(η))`.
- The forgetful functor `CAlg(Ĉat∞) → Ĉat∞` preserves limits, so the underlying ∞-category of `QCoh(X)` is the limit of the underlying ∞-categories.

**Checks.**

- `QCoh(∅)` is the limit over the empty ∞-category, the terminal ∞-category: the zero category. A colimit formula would give the empty ∞-category, which has no zero object and is not stable.
- `QCoh(Spec ℤ) = D∞(ℤ)` in each of the three contexts, and so is `QCoh` of the final prestack.
- `QCoh(Spec A ⊔ Spec B) ≃ Mod_A × Mod_B ≃ Mod_{A × B}` (1.2, products).
- For the constant prestack `X = BΓ × Spec k` with `Γ = ℤ/2` and `k = 𝔽₂`, `QCoh(X) ≃ Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` and `π₀ Map(O_X, O_X[1]) = H¹(ℤ/2; 𝔽₂) = 𝔽₂` (Mathlib `groupCohomology`), the mapping-space formula giving `Map(O_X, O_X[1])` as `Ω^∞` of the homotopy fixed points `(k[1])^{hΓ}`, while the pullback of this class to every point `(A, η)` lies in `π₀ Map_{Mod_A}(A, A[1]) = π₋₁A = 0`: maps are not determined by their evaluations, and a definition as the limit of the homotopy categories, whose Hom sets are the limits of the pointwise Hom sets (here the Γ-invariants of `Ext¹_k(k, k) = 0`), would lose this class.

*Needs:* 1.2; 2.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E3.1 (right Kan extension along a full inclusion, dual of `leftKanExtension_restrict_trivialFibration`), E0.7 (coCartesian sections as limits), E5:abstract.1–2 (symmetric monoidal ∞-categories and their limits); PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0 (`QCohPrestack`, `QCohPrestack.eval`, `pullback`, `specEquiv`, `unit_eval`, the classical context); Mathlib `groupCohomology`.

### 2.3 Formal properties

Prove: QCoh carries (u+1)-small colimits of prestacks to limits; every `QCoh(X)` is stable and admits small colimits, computed pointwise; every pullback `f^*` preserves small colimits, hence is exact, and is symmetric monoidal; the tensor product is computed pointwise, `(F ⊗ G)(η) ≃ F(η) ⊗_A G(η)`, and preserves small colimits in each variable; a map is an equivalence, and an object is zero, exactly when every evaluation is, whereas the vanishing of a map is not detected pointwise in general (on `Spec A` it is, evaluation at the initial point being an equivalence); and if X is a u-small colimit of affines in `PreStk_T`, then `QCoh(X)` is presentable. In the classical context, the homotopy category of `QCoh(Spec R)` is Mathlib's `DerivedCategory (ModuleCat R)` with its shift and distinguished triangles, and the tensor product descends to the derived tensor product (SAG Proposition 6.2.3.4, pp. 492–493; SAG §6.2.6, pp. 496–497; GR-I Chapter I.3, §§1.1 and 3.2, pp. 143–145 and 159). In the classical context the colimit-to-limit property, stability with small colimits, pointwise detection of equivalences and the homotopy category of an affine are PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `limitOfColimit`, `isEquiv_iff_eval` and `homotopyCategory_spec`, and the statements here extend them to the animated and `E∞` contexts. Presentability is not imported: §4.0 asserts it only in a larger universe, whereas the statement here is u-presentability (Conventions) for a u-small colimit of affines, in every context, the classical one included.

*Proof route.* The right Kan extension along the Yoneda embedding of a functor into an ∞-category with limits carries colimits of prestacks to limits, by the universal property of presheaf ∞-categories as free cocompletions (PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3, applied in the universe u+1 to `T^op`, whose presheaf ∞-category is `PreStk_T`; HTT Theorem 5.1.5.6; SAG Lemma 6.2.1.12, p. 488). Stable ∞-categories with small colimits and colimit-preserving functors are closed under (u+1)-small limits in `Ĉat∞`, and so are presentable ones along u-small diagrams (`presentable_limit`); the values `Mod_A` are presentable and stable and the transitions preserve colimits (1.2). A morphism of a limit of ∞-categories is an equivalence exactly when each of its images is (1.5), and an object F is zero exactly when `F → 0` is an equivalence, the evaluations being exact. The homotopy-category statement is `enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory` with `stableMonoidal_homotopyCategory`, through 1.2's classical values.

**Checks.**

- On `Spec ℤ`, `ℤ/p ⊗ ℤ/p` has `π₀ = π₁ = ℤ/p`, the derived tensor product; the tensor product of `QCoh` is not the ordinary one on hearts.
- Vanishing of maps is not detected pointwise: on `X = BΓ × Spec 𝔽₂` with `Γ = ℤ/2`, the class `O → O[1]` of 2.2 is nonzero, `π₀ Map(O, O[1]) = H¹(ℤ/2; 𝔽₂) = 𝔽₂`, while each of its evaluations lies in `π₀ Map_{Mod_A}(A, A[1]) = π₋₁A = 0`, A being connective, and so is the zero map `A → A[1]`; `π₀` of the limit `lim_η Map(A, A[1])` is not the limit of the `π₀`. No choice of points helps, since every point of X factors up to homotopy through the atlas point `Spec 𝔽₂ → X` (BΓ is connected). Equivalences and zero objects are detected pointwise all the same, here already by the atlas point: the forgetful functor `Fun(BΓ, Mod_𝔽₂) → Mod_𝔽₂` is conservative, and the nonzero class is not an equivalence, its evaluation `𝔽₂ → 𝔽₂[1]` not being one.
- `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k) = Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` is presentable, BΓ being a small colimit of the final prestack; for a large colimit presentability is not asserted.
- In the classical context `Hom(ℤ/p, ℤ/p[1])` in the homotopy category of `QCoh(Spec ℤ)` is `Ext¹_ℤ(ℤ/p, ℤ/p) = ℤ/p`, as in Mathlib's `DerivedCategory (ModuleCat ℤ)`.

*Needs:* 2.2; 1.2; 1.5 (equivalences in a limit); PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3 (free cocompletion); EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.5 (the supplied Yoneda embedding), E5:presentability.2 (`presentable_limit`, `PrL`), E1.3 (`enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory`), E5:abstract.4 (`stableMonoidal_homotopyCategory`), E5 (`enhancement_compatibility`); PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0 (`limitOfColimit`, `isEquiv_iff_eval`, `homotopyCategory_spec`, the classical context); Mathlib `DerivedCategory`.

### 2.4 Change of test rings

Let u be one of `ι : CRing → AnRing`, `θ : AnRing → CAlg^cn` or `θι`, so that the module functor of the source is that of the target restricted along u (1.2). Let `u_! : PreStk_T → PreStk_{T′}` be the left Kan extension along `Spec ∘ u`. Prove that `u_!` preserves (u+1)-small colimits, that `u_! Spec A ≃ Spec u(A)`, and that there is an equivalence `QCoh(u_! X) ≃ QCoh(X)` of symmetric monoidal ∞-categories, natural in X and compatible with pullback and evaluation. For `u = ι` or `θι` the source side is PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `QCohPrestack(X)` (2.2), which this identifies with `QCoh(u_! X)`. Every formal statement about classical prestacks is therefore the restriction of the corresponding statement in a later context (SAG Remark 6.2.2.2, p. 491; GR-I Chapter I.3, §1.2, pp. 145–146).

*Proof route.* Write X as the colimit of its points (2.1); `u_!` preserves colimits and representables, so `u_! X` is the colimit of the `Spec u(A)`, and by 2.3 `QCoh(u_! X) ≃ lim Mod_{u(A)} = lim Mod_A = QCoh(X)`.

The API consists of the following facts.

- `u_!` is left adjoint to restriction `u^*`, `(u^* Y)(A) = Y(u(A))`.
- `u_!` preserves colimits, hence the empty prestack. It preserves the final prestack `Spec ℤ` for a different reason, since a final object is a limit: `u_! Spec ℤ ≃ Spec u(ℤ)` and u carries the initial test ring to the initial test ring (1.1). It does not preserve fibre products: `ι_!` of the classical fibre product `Spec 𝔽_p ×_{Spec ℤ} Spec 𝔽_p = Spec 𝔽_p` is `Spec 𝔽_p`, whereas the fibre product of the images is `Spec(𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p)`.

**Checks.**

- `u_! Spec R ≃ Spec u(R)` by Yoneda, and `u_! ∅ = ∅`.
- Restriction does not preserve `QCoh`: for `Y = Spec(𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p)` in `AnRing`, ι being right adjoint to `π₀` gives `ι^* Y = Spec 𝔽_p`, with `QCoh = D∞(𝔽_p)`, whereas the unit of `QCoh(Y)` has `π₁ = 𝔽_p`.
- For the classical constant prestack `BΓ × Spec 𝔽₂`, the image under `ι_!` is the animated constant prestack, and both have `QCoh = Fun(BΓ, D∞(𝔽₂))`.

*Needs:* 2.3; 1.1; 1.2; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E3.1 (left Kan extensions, the supplied absolute Kan extensions); PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0 (`QCohPrestack`).

### 2.5 Classical prestacks of schemes and stacks in groupoids

For a Mathlib scheme X define its classical prestack `h_X : CRing → Ŝ`, `R ↦ Hom(Spec R, X)`, a discrete space. For a stack in groupoids X of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.18 define `X^♮ : CRing → Ŝ` as follows: restrict the pseudofunctor X to affine schemes, form its Grothendieck construction over the opposite of `CommRingCat` (Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Pseudofunctor.CoGrothendieck`), which is fibred in groupoids (§1.18 `isFiberedInGroupoids`), take the nerve, which is a right fibration over the nerve of affine schemes (HTT Proposition 2.1.1.3, PDF p. 75, in its dual form), and straighten it. Then `X^♮(R)` is equivalent to the nerve of the groupoid `X(Spec R)`, a Kan complex (HTT Proposition 1.2.5.1, PDF p. 53). Strong transformations of stacks give maps of prestacks and modifications give homotopies.

Define the fppf topology on `CRing^op`, the affine form of the topology for which the stacks of §1.18 are stacks. A finite family `{R → S_i}` of ring maps is an fppf cover if `R → ∏_i S_i` is faithfully flat and of finite presentation (Mathlib `Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `RingHom.FinitePresentation`), equivalently if every `R → S_i` is flat and of finite presentation and the family is jointly faithfully flat. Let Φ′ be the class of maps `Spec S → Spec R` of `CRing^op` with `R → S` faithfully flat and of finite presentation. An fppf sheaf on `CRing^op` is a functor `CRing → Ŝ` that carries finite products of rings to products and carries the Čech nerve `S^{⊗_R(•+1)}` of every map in Φ′ to a limit diagram, and `L_fppf` is the left adjoint of the inclusion of fppf sheaves among classical prestacks, the accessible localization of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2.7 (`spaceSheaves`) in the universe u+1. These are the sheaves of a Grothendieck topology. Φ′ contains the isomorphisms and is stable under composition and base change; it is stable under finite products, since for `R → S` and `R′ → S′` in Φ′ the map `R × R′ → S × S′` is flat and surjective on spectra, and finitely presented because finite presentation is Zariski-local on `Spec(R × R′) = Spec R ⊔ Spec R′`; the empty product is the identity of the zero ring. Finite coproducts in `CRing^op` are universal, since for every map `R × R′ → C`, `C ≃ (C ⊗_{R×R′} R) × (C ⊗_{R×R′} R′)`, R and R′ being the localizations of `R × R′` at the idempotents `(1, 0)` and `(0, 1)`, and for the empty coproduct `0 ⊗_R C = 0`; they are disjoint, since `R ⊗_{R×R′} R′ = 0`. So HypercompletenessAndCoherence §9.3.2 gives a topology whose covering sieves are those containing an fppf cover, and its sheaves are the fppf sheaves above (§9.4.1; SAG Propositions A.3.2.1 and A.3.3.1, pp. 1764–1766). On affine schemes this is Mathlib's fppf topology, whose covering sieves are those containing a covering family of `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.fppfPrecoverage` (`CategoryTheory.Precoverage.mem_toGrothendieck_iff_of_isStableUnderComposition`, whose four hypotheses hold for that precoverage: stability under composition and under base change are the two instances derived with `fppfPrecoverage`; it has pullbacks, as every precoverage of schemes does, schemes having all fibre products; and it contains the isomorphisms, since an isomorphism of schemes is surjective, flat and locally of finite presentation, so that its singleton family is a covering family (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.singleton_mem_precoverage_iff`). The last hypothesis is not found by instance search for `fppfPrecoverage` and is supplied by this argument). An fppf cover is a covering family of `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.fppfPrecoverage`, its members being flat and of finite presentation and jointly surjective. Conversely, a covering family `{X_i → Spec R}` of `fppfPrecoverage` is a quasi-compact cover (`fppfPrecoverage_le_fpqcPrecoverage`), so `Spec R` is the union of the images of finitely many quasi-compact opens of the `X_i`, hence of finitely many affine opens `Spec S_j`; each `R → S_j` is flat and of finite presentation, and `{R → S_j}` is an fppf cover through which the family is refined. Hence the topology is, on the nerve, Tau Ceti's affine fppf site `TauCeti.CommAlgCat.fppfTopology ℤ`, transported along the equivalence of `CommAlgCat ℤ` with `CommRingCat`: a sieve on `Spec R` covers there exactly when the sieve it generates among schemes contains a covering family of `fppfPrecoverage` (`TauCeti.CommAlgCat.isCoverDense_algSpec`), that is, by the refinement just made, exactly when it contains an fppf cover.

The API consists of the following facts.

- `h` is a fully faithful functor from Mathlib schemes to classical prestacks, and `h_{Spec R} = Spec R`. A morphism of schemes is determined by, and glued from, its restrictions to an affine open cover of the source, and morphisms between affine schemes are ring maps.
- `(−)^♮` carries the stack in groupoids of a scheme (§1.18 `StackInGroupoids.ofSheaf`) to `h_X`, and 2-fibre products (§1.18 `twoFiberProduct`) to fibre products of prestacks, the nerve of a 2-fibre product of groupoids being the homotopy fibre product of nerves.
- `(−)^♮`, formed by the same construction for every groupoid-valued pseudofunctor F on schemes, carries the fppf stackification (§1.18 `Stack.stackification`) to `L_fppf`: the map `L_fppf(F^♮) → (stackification F)^♮` induced by the unit `F → stackification F` is an equivalence. Its target is an fppf sheaf: the stack condition (§1.18 `Stack.isStack_stackification`) for the Zariski cover of `Spec(R × R′)` by `Spec R` and `Spec R′`, and for the covering family `{Spec S → Spec R}` of a map in Φ′, is, on nerves, the product condition and the limit condition on the Čech nerve, the groupoid of descent data being the limit of the 1-truncated spaces. The map is a monomorphism: base changed along a point (a, b) of `F^♮ × F^♮`, the diagonal of `F^♮ → (stackification F)^♮` is the map from the isomorphism presheaf of a and b in F to that of their images, which is its fppf sheafification (§1.18 `Stack.stackification_isom_sheafify`); `L_fppf` is left exact and preserves colimits, and colimits of prestacks are universal (InfinityTopoi §3.3.2), so `L_fppf` inverts that diagonal. It is an effective epimorphism: every object of the stackification is fppf-locally in the image of F (§1.18 `Stack.stackification_locally_essSurj`), so every point of the target lifts to `F^♮` after an fppf cover, and the local-lifting step of InfinityTopoi §9.2.3 applies to the fppf topology. So it is an equivalence (InfinityTopoi §7.3.4). On an affine scheme the isomorphism sheaves and the local lifts of §1.18 are read on fppf covers of rings, by the comparison of covers above.

## Layer 3: Flat descent

This layer proves that module categories satisfy descent for flat hypercovers of arbitrary connective test rings, with no finiteness, countability or descendability hypothesis, and deduces fpqc descent for `QCoh`. EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:descent proves descent for descendable maps; faithfully flat maps need not be descendable, and the route here does not pass through descendability.

### 3.1 The fpqc topology and flat hypercovers

Define the fpqc topology on `T^op` by the covers of the Conventions, and a flat hypercover of a test ring A as an augmented cosemisimplicial test ring `A → A_•` whose latching maps `L_n(A_•) → A_n` are faithfully flat (SAG Remark D.6.1.1 and Definition D.6.1.4, pp. 2173–2174). The Čech nerve `B^{⊗_A(•+1)}` of a faithfully flat `A → B`, formed with the pushouts of T, is a flat hypercover. Define fpqc sheaves and hypercomplete fpqc sheaves in `PreStk_T` as the functors that carry finite products of test rings to products and satisfy descent for Čech nerves, respectively for all flat hypercovers, and the (hyper)sheafification `L : PreStk_T → Shv_fpqc` as the left adjoint of the inclusion. The sheafification is given by InfinityTopoi §§8.2.1 and 8.3.1 (HTT Definition 6.2.2.6 and Proposition 6.2.2.7, PDF p. 597), applied in the universe u+1 to the (u+1)-small ∞-category `T^op` with the topology of the next paragraph: the fpqc sheaves are the prestacks local for the monomorphisms into corepresentables that correspond to covering sieves, and the inclusion has a left exact accessible left adjoint, so that fpqc sheaves form an ∞-topos. The hypersheafification is the sheafification followed by the hypercompletion (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §1.2.1). In the classical context `CRing^op` is the nerve of an ordinary category, and these are EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2.7's `spaceSheaves` and `hypercompleteSpaceSheaves` for that site, both being the objects local for the same monomorphisms; E2.7, a statement about ordinary sites, does not apply to `AnRing^op` and `(CAlg^cn)^op` (Checks). For an ∞-category 𝒞 with (u+1)-small limits, a functor `T → 𝒞` is a 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaf, respectively a hypercomplete 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaf, under the same two conditions. A map `A → B` of test rings is an open immersion, étale, or flat of finite presentation when it is flat (Conventions) and the ring map `π₀A → π₀B` is, respectively, one whose map of spectra is an open immersion (Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.IsOpenImmersion`), étale (Mathlib `RingHom.Etale`; HA Definition 7.5.0.4, p. 1374, for the same notion on `E_k`-rings), or of finite presentation (Mathlib `RingHom.FinitePresentation`). A flat module M over a discrete test ring A is discrete, its `π_n` being `π_n A ⊗_{π₀A} π₀M = 0` for `n ≠ 0`, so for discrete A these are the ordinary notions for the ring map `A → B`. Zariski, étale and fppf covers are the finite families of open immersions, étale maps, respectively flat maps of finite presentation, that are fpqc covers, that is jointly faithfully flat; in the classical context the fppf covers are those of 2.5.

These are the sheaves of a Grothendieck topology and the hypercomplete ones among them. Let Φ be the class of morphisms `Spec B → Spec A` of `T^op` with `A → B` faithfully flat. It satisfies conditions (a)–(d) of HypercompletenessAndCoherence §9.3.2. (a) Equivalences are faithfully flat, and faithfully flat maps compose (1.3). (b) T has pushouts, and faithful flatness is stable under base change (1.3). (c) The finite coproducts of `T^op` are the finite products of test rings (1.1), and a finite product of faithfully flat maps is faithfully flat. (d) For every map `A × B → C`, `C ≃ (C ⊗_{A×B} A) × (C ⊗_{A×B} B)`, since A and B are the localizations of `A × B` at the complementary idempotents `(1, 0)` and `(0, 1)`; for the empty coproduct, `0 ⊗_A C = 0`. Φ also satisfies condition (e) of §9.4.1: coproducts of `T^op` are disjoint, since `A ⊗_{A×B} B = 0`, so `Spec A ×_{Spec(A × B)} Spec B = Spec 0` is initial. So §9.3.2 gives a Grothendieck topology on `T^op` whose covering sieves are those containing an fpqc cover. Its 𝒞-valued sheaves are the 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaves above (§9.4.1; SAG Proposition A.3.3.1, p. 1766). The flat hypercovers are the Φ-hypercoverings of §11.5.1, the matching objects in `T^op` being the latching objects in T (SAG Definition D.6.1.4). So the hypercomplete 𝒞-valued sheaves of §11.5.2 are the hypercomplete 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaves above (§11.5.3; SAG Proposition A.5.7.2, p. 1806).

The API consists of the following facts.

- fpqc covers are stable under base change and composition, and so are flat hypercovers. ι and θ carry fpqc covers to fpqc covers, Čech nerves of faithfully flat maps to Čech nerves, and flat hypercovers to flat hypercovers. For θ, which preserves colimits and homotopy groups (1.1), this is immediate. For ι, a pushout along a flat map is the same in `CRing` and in `AnRing` (1.1). The latching object `L_n(A_•)` is the colimit of the `A_m` over the proper subsets of `[n]`, the empty subset giving A. It is reached from A by adjoining these subsets one at a time in order of size, each step a pushout along a latching map `L_m(A_•) → A_m` with `m < n`, which is faithfully flat. So by induction on n the latching objects and latching maps formed in `CRing` and in `AnRing` agree, and ι preserves faithful flatness (1.3).
- In `CRing`, a finite family `{R → S_i}` is an fpqc cover exactly when `{Spec S_i → Spec R}` is a covering family of Mathlib's `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.fpqcPrecoverage`; on affine schemes the quasi-compactness condition is automatic.
- `L` is left exact and preserves colimits (InfinityTopoi §8.3.1, with HypercompletenessAndCoherence §1.2.1 for the hypersheafification); a map of prestacks is an fpqc-local equivalence when L inverts it.
- In the classical context every fppf cover (2.5) is an fpqc cover, the class Φ′ of 2.5 being contained in Φ. So every fpqc sheaf on `CRing^op`, hypercomplete or not, is an fppf sheaf, and every map f of classical prestacks inverted by `L_fppf` is inverted by L and by the hypersheafification: for every fpqc sheaf F, `Map(f, F) ≃ Map(L_fppf f, F)` is an equivalence, so `L f` is one by Yoneda among fpqc sheaves, and the hypersheafification of f is the hypercompletion of `L f`. This is the affine form of Mathlib's `fppfTopology_le_fpqcTopology`. The converse fails for covers: `ℤ → ℤ[1/p] × ℤ_p` is in Φ and not in Φ′ (2.5).
- Hypercomplete fpqc sheaves form an ∞-topos, a left exact localization of fpqc sheaves (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §1.2.1), and the hypersheafification is the sheafification followed by this localization.
- Local lifting. A sieve on R covers exactly when it contains an fpqc cover of R, as above (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §9.3.2; SAG Proposition A.3.2.1, pp. 1764–1765). For a map `u : F′ → F` of prestacks, the sheafification `L u` is an effective epimorphism of fpqc sheaves exactly when every point `Spec R → F` lifts to F′, up to homotopy, after some fpqc cover `{R → R_i}`; the same holds for the hypersheafification and hypercomplete fpqc sheaves. In particular every point of `L F` lifts to F after an fpqc cover (u the unit `F → L F`). Proof route. Only if: for a point `η : Spec R → F`, let `P = F′ ×_F Spec R` in `PreStk_T`, formed along η. As L is left exact, `L P → L Spec R` is a pullback of `L u`, so an effective epimorphism (InfinityTopoi §7.5.1). P is the colimit of its points (2.1), so the coproduct of their corepresentables maps onto P by an effective epimorphism (§7.6.1). Hence the image of `P → Spec R` in `PreStk_T` is the monomorphism of the sieve of maps `R → D` along which η lifts (§§7.3.4, 7.4.2 and 9.1.1). L preserves effective epimorphisms (§7.4.1) and monomorphisms, so it carries this image factorization to that of `L P → L Spec R` (§7.3.4). It therefore inverts the monomorphism of the sieve, which covers (§8.4.1). If: by the first part applied to the unit, every point of `L F` lifts to F, hence to F′, after a composite of two covers; then the local-lifting step of §9.2.3 applies to `L u`. Hypercomplete case: the hypercompletion is left exact and colimit-preserving, so it commutes with 0-truncation (PresentableInfinityCategories §6.2.3), and it fixes 0-truncated objects, which are hypercomplete (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §6.1.1). So `L u` and its hypercompletion have the same 0-truncation, and by §14.1.1, applied in both ∞-topoi, one is an effective epimorphism exactly when the other is.
- Universal property: let G be a functor from `PreStk_T^op` to an ∞-category 𝒞 with (u+1)-small limits that preserves (u+1)-small limits. Then G inverts every fpqc-local equivalence of sheafification exactly when its restriction to `T^op` is a 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaf, and every fpqc-local equivalence of hypersheafification exactly when that restriction is a hypercomplete 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaf. For sheaves this is HypercompletenessAndCoherence §4.2.1 (SAG Proposition 1.3.1.7, p. 117), through the identification above and PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3, by which G is determined by its restriction to `T^op`. For hypercomplete sheaves: G inverts a map exactly when `Map(D, G(−))` does for every object D of 𝒞. That functor is `Map(−, F_D)` for the prestack `F_D = Map(D, G(Spec −))` (PresheavesAndCompletions §1.4.3). It inverts the fpqc-local equivalences of hypersheafification exactly when `F_D` is local for them, which for the reflective full subcategory of hypercomplete fpqc sheaves means that `F_D` is one. Asking this for every D is the definition of a hypercomplete 𝒞-valued sheaf (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §11.5.2), which is a hypercomplete 𝒞-valued fpqc sheaf by §11.5.3, as above.

**Checks.**

So `AnRing^op` is not the nerve of a category, and presheaves of spaces on its homotopy category, the ordinary site to which a statement about ordinary sites such as EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2.7 would apply, are not the prestacks of 2.1: the representable presheaf of `ℤ[x]` there has the discrete value `π₀B = 𝔽_p` at B, and is not `Spec ℤ[x]`. ### 3.2 Flat hyperdescent for module categories

Prove: for every `A ∈ CAlg^cn` and every flat hypercover `A → A_•`, the functors `Mod_A^cn → lim_{[n] ∈ Δ_s} Mod^cn_{A_n}` and `Mod_A → lim_{[n] ∈ Δ_s} Mod_{A_n}`, given by extension of scalars, are equivalences, the second one of symmetric monoidal ∞-categories. Equivalently, `Mod : CAlg^cn → CAlg(Ĉat∞)` is a hypercomplete fpqc sheaf. In particular, for every A-module M the map `M → lim_n M ⊗_A A_n` is an equivalence. Through θ and θι, which carry flat hypercovers to flat hypercovers (3.1), the same holds for `AnRing` and `CRing`, and for every faithfully flat map, Čech descent `Mod_A ≃ lim_Δ Mod_{B^{⊗_A(•+1)}}` holds (SAG Theorem D.6.3.1 and Corollaries D.6.3.3–D.6.3.4, pp. 2177–2178; SAG Propositions D.6.1.3 and D.6.2.3, pp. 2174–2177; GR-I Chapter I.3, Theorem 1.3.4 and Corollary 1.3.5, pp. 146–147). On Čech nerves of faithfully flat maps this is LanglandsParameterStacks §1.7 in the animated context, and PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `QCohPrestack.flatDescent` in the classical one. The theorem here extends both to flat hypercovers and to `CAlg^cn`, and its restrictions along θ and θι are the same comparison functors, so they agree with the imports.

*Proof route.* (1) For a faithfully flat `A → B`, extension of scalars is conservative and t-exact (1.3). (2) Fix `n ≥ 0`. The cosimplicial ∞-category `τ_{≤n} Mod^cn_{A_•}` of the Čech nerve satisfies the Beck–Chevalley condition by 1.2's base change. A totalization of uniformly n-truncated modules is computed in each homotopy group by a finite partial totalization (`tot_filteredColimit_of_bounded`), a finite limit, which the exact and t-exact `B ⊗_A −` preserves; with (1) this makes `B ⊗_A −` comonadic, and the descent criterion of 1.5 gives Čech descent for `A ↦ τ_{≤n} Mod^cn_A`. (3) This functor carries finite products to products (1.2), so with Čech descent it is a `Ĉat∞`-valued sheaf for the topology of 3.1 (HypercompletenessAndCoherence §9.4.1). Its values are (n+1)-categories, so for every object D of `Ĉat∞` the space-valued sheaf `Map(D, τ_{≤n} Mod^cn_{(−)})`, whose values are cores of functor ∞-categories into (n+1)-categories, is (n+1)-truncated, hence hypercomplete (HTT Lemma 6.5.2.9, PDF p. 687; HypercompletenessAndCoherence §6.1.1). So the functor is a hypercomplete `Ĉat∞`-valued sheaf (§11.5.2), and it carries flat hypercovers to limit diagrams (§11.5.3). (4) Left completeness (1.3) gives `Mod^cn_A ≃ lim_n τ_{≤n} Mod^cn_A`, and along injective maps of `Δ_s` the transitions of a flat hypercover are flat, hence commute with the truncations, so `A ↦ Mod^cn_A` is a hypercomplete sheaf. (5) Right completeness and stability give `Mod_A ≃ lim(⋯ → Mod^cn_A →Ω Mod^cn_A)`, the stabilization of EnhancedDerivedSheaves `spectra`; the flat transitions are left exact on connective modules and commute with Ω, and limits commute with limits. (6) The comparison is a symmetric monoidal functor that is an equivalence of underlying ∞-categories, hence an equivalence in `CAlg(Ĉat∞)`.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Descendable maps: for a descendable faithfully flat `A → B` in the classical context the equivalence agrees with EnhancedDerivedSheaves `moduleDescent`.
- Hearts: on hearts, Čech descent along a faithfully flat map of ordinary rings is the effectivity of descent data for ordinary modules, the affine case of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.1 `qcoh_fpqc_descent`.
- `Mod` carries finite products of test rings to products (1.2), so with the theorem it is a hypercomplete `CAlg(Ĉat∞)`-valued fpqc sheaf on `T^op` in each of the three contexts.
- Affines are hypercomplete fpqc sheaves: for every test ring B, `Spec B = Map_T(B, −)` is one, in each of the three contexts. It carries finite products of test rings to products. For a flat hypercover `A → A_•`, the case `M = A` of the theorem gives `A ≃ lim_{[n] ∈ Δ_s} A_n` on underlying modules. The forgetful functors of `AnRing` and `CAlg^cn` to `D(ℤ)_{≥0}` (1.1), and that of `CRing` to abelian groups, are conservative and preserve limits, so A is the limit of the `A_n` among test rings, and `Map_T(B, −)` preserves that limit. In `CRing` the values are sets and the limit is the equalizer of the two cofaces `A_0 ⇉ A_1`; on Čech nerves this is the affine form of Mathlib's `Subcanonical` instance for `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.fpqcTopology`.

**Checks.**

- For the identity `A → A` the Čech nerve is constant and the totalization is `Mod_A`.
- For `(f, g) = A`, the Zariski cover `A → A[1/f] × A[1/g]` gives `Mod_A ≃ Mod_{A_f} ×_{Mod_{A_{fg}}} Mod_{A_g}`; a two-term complex on each chart with a gluing equivalence on the overlap descends, differential included.
- `ℤ → ℤ[1/p] × ℤ_p` recovers `D∞(ℤ)`. Its Čech nerve does not stop at the fracture square: `ℤ_p ⊗_ℤ ℤ_p` is not `ℤ_p`, being rationally `ℚ_p ⊗_ℚ ℚ_p`. The exact sequence `0 → ℤ → ℤ[1/p] ⊕ ℤ_p → ℚ_p → 0` puts ℤ in the thick tensor ideal generated by the target, so this cover is also descendable and the equivalence agrees there with `moduleDescent`.
- `ℤ → 𝔽_p` is not a cover and descent fails: the nonzero ℚ is sent to `ℚ ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p = 0`.
- Affines, in the classical context, on the affine line `Spec ℤ[t]`, whose value at a ring is its underlying set. Along the cover `ℤ → ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3]` the equalizer is the set of pairs `(a, b)` with the same image in `ℤ[1/6]`, that is `ℤ[1/2] ∩ ℤ[1/3] = ℤ`, the value at ℤ. Along `ℤ → 𝔽_p × ℤ[1/p]`, surjective on spectra and not flat, the mixed factors of `(𝔽_p × ℤ[1/p]) ⊗_ℤ (𝔽_p × ℤ[1/p])` vanish, so the two cofaces to `𝔽_p × ℤ[1/p]` coincide and the equalizer is all of `𝔽_p × ℤ[1/p]`, which contains `(1, 0)`, outside the image of ℤ. Without flatness, affines are not sheaves.
- `𝔽_p → 𝔽_p[ε]/(ε²)` is faithfully flat (free of rank two), and descent recovers `Mod_{𝔽_p}`; the reduction `𝔽_p[ε]/(ε²) → 𝔽_p` is not flat and is not a cover.
- In the classical context, for an ordinary ring A and `f_1, …, f_n` generating the unit ideal, `A → ∏_i A[1/f_i]` is descendable: this is `descendableCover_zariski` for `X = Spec A` and `U_i = D(f_i)`, read in `D∞(A) ≃ Mod_A` (1.2). It is faithfully flat, and the two descent equivalences coincide on it. For `ℤ → ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3]` its fibre I is `ℤ[1/6][−1]`, by the exact sequence `0 → ℤ → ℤ[1/2] ⊕ ℤ[1/3] → ℤ[1/6] → 0`, and the index is exactly 2: `I^{⊗2} → ℤ` lies in `Ext²_ℤ(ℤ[1/6], ℤ) = 0`, while `I → ℤ` is not null, since ℤ is not a retract of `ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3]` (neither factor maps nonzero to ℤ).

### 3.3 Invariance under sheafification and Čech descent

Prove, in each context: (a) if a map of prestacks `f : Y → X` becomes an equivalence after fpqc hypersheafification, then `f^* : QCoh(X) → QCoh(Y)` is an equivalence; in particular `QCoh(L X) ≃ QCoh(X)` for the sheafification and the hypersheafification; (b) the restriction of `QCoh` to hypercomplete fpqc sheaves carries (u+1)-small colimits of sheaves to limits; (c) for an fpqc surjection `U → X`, that is a map whose base change to every affine point is refined by an fpqc cover, `QCoh(X) ≃ lim_{[n]∈Δ} QCoh(U_n)` for the Čech nerve `U_•` of `U → X`; (d) consequently `QCoh` satisfies descent for fppf, étale and Zariski covers (SAG Proposition 6.2.3.1 and Remarks 6.2.3.2–6.2.3.3, p. 492; GR-I Chapter I.3, Corollaries 1.3.7, 1.3.8 and 1.3.11, p. 147). In the classical context, (a) for sheafification and (c) are PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `sheafificationEquiv` and `cechDescent`; the statements here extend them to hypersheafification and to the animated and `E∞` contexts.

*Proof route.* `QCoh` preserves limits (2.3) and its restriction to representables is `Mod`, a hypercomplete fpqc sheaf (3.2); by the universal property of 3.1 it inverts fpqc-local equivalences of hypersheafification. (c) holds because `|U_•| → X` is an fpqc-local equivalence: in `PreStk_T` it is the image of `U → X`, a monomorphism (InfinityTopoi §7.3.1), and every point of X lifts to U, hence to `|U_•|`, after an fpqc cover, so L carries it to a monomorphism that is an effective epimorphism by the local lifting criterion of 3.1, that is to an equivalence (InfinityTopoi §7.3.4).

**Checks.**

- `∅ → Spec 0` and `Spec A ⊔ Spec B → Spec(A × B)` are fpqc-local equivalences, and indeed `QCoh(Spec 0) = 0 = QCoh(∅)` and `Mod_{A×B} ≃ Mod_A × Mod_B`.
- `Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ` is not an fpqc surjection, and the Čech totalization is not `D∞(ℤ)`: it kills ℚ.
- For a finite group Γ, the constant prestack `BΓ × Spec k` and the classical prestack of the stack of Γ-torsors over k-algebras have the same sheafification, and the same `QCoh`, `Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)`. The map from the first to the second is inverted by `L_fppf`, the second being `L_fppf` of the prestack of trivial torsors (2.5), whose automorphisms `Γ(R)`, the locally constant Γ-valued functions on `Spec R`, are constant on the members of a finite Zariski cover; so L inverts it (3.1). The class of 2.2's Check survives in both.

### 3.4 Zariski gluing and presentability

Prove: (a) for a Mathlib scheme X, `QCoh(h_X) ≃ lim_{U} QCoh(Spec O(U))`, the limit over the poset of affine opens U of X with restrictions, and for a hypercover `U_• → X` of the Zariski topos by disjoint unions of affine opens, `QCoh(h_X) ≃ lim_Δ QCoh(h_{U_•})`; (b) if the fpqc hypersheafification `L X` lies in the smallest full subcategory of hypercomplete fpqc sheaves containing the representables and closed under u-small colimits, then `QCoh(X)` is presentable (GR-I Chapter I.3, Proposition 1.4.4, p. 149; SAG Proposition 6.2.3.4(3), p. 493).

*Proof route.* (a) It suffices that the maps `colim_{U∈B} h_U → h_X`, for the poset B of affine opens, and `|h_{U_•}| → h_X` are inverted by fpqc hypersheafification: 3.3(a) and 2.3 then give the two limit formulas. The first map is already inverted by sheafification. Let `S ⊇ B` be the poset of opens contained in some affine open; then `colim_B h_U ≃ colim_{W∈S} colim_{U∈B, U⊆W} h_U`, the colimit of a left Kan extension along `B ⊆ S`. Each W in S is separated, so the affine opens inside W are closed under finite intersections, and S is closed under them too. Hence, at a test ring R, `colim_{U∈B, U⊆W} h_U` is the disjoint union over `f ∈ h_W(R)` of the nerve of the poset of affine opens of W containing the image of f, contractible when nonempty: it is the subfunctor of `h_W` of the maps landing in an affine open of W. Likewise `colim_{W∈S} h_W` is the subfunctor of `h_X` of the maps landing in a member of S. The affine opens form a basis of the topology of X, so these monomorphisms are surjective Zariski-locally on `Spec R`. L is left exact, preserves colimits and has the Zariski covers among its covers (3.1), and `h_W` and `h_X` are fpqc sheaves (Stacks, Descent, Lemma 35.13.7, tag 023Q). So L carries these monomorphisms to monomorphisms of sheaves that are effective epimorphisms by the local lifting criterion of 3.1, that is to equivalences (InfinityTopoi §7.3.4), and so inverts the first map. For the second map, after L the simplicial object `h_{U_•}` is a hypercover of `h_X`: h preserves fibre products and carries the surjective local isomorphisms `U_n → (cosk_{n−1} U_•)_n` to maps that are surjective Zariski-locally. The realization of a hypercover is ∞-connective and is inverted by hypersheafification (HTT Lemma 6.5.3.11 and Corollary 6.5.3.13, PDF p. 698), whereas sheafification inverts the realizations of all hypercovers only in a hypercomplete ∞-topos (HTT Theorem 6.5.3.12, PDF p. 698); this is where (a) uses hypersheafification and not sheafification. (b) `QCoh^{−1}` of presentable categories is closed under colimits of hypercomplete sheaves by 3.3(b) and `presentable_limit`, and contains the representables; and `QCoh(X) ≃ QCoh(L X)` by 3.3(a).

**Checks.**

- For `ℙ¹_k`, `QCoh(h_{ℙ¹}) ≃ D∞(k[t]) ×_{D∞(k[t,t⁻¹])} D∞(k[t⁻¹])`, and `π₀ Map(O, O(−2)[1])` is the cokernel of `k[t] ⊕ k[t⁻¹] → k[t, t⁻¹]`, `(a, b) ↦ a − t⁻²b` read through the transition of `O(−2)`, which is k, spanned by `t⁻¹`; it restricts to zero on both charts.
- For `X = ⨿_ℕ Spec ℤ`, `QCoh(h_X) ≃ ∏_ℕ D∞(ℤ)`, presentable although X is not quasi-compact.
- For the empty scheme the only affine open is the empty one, `Spec 0`, so the limit is `Mod_0 = 0`; and `h_∅` is the prestack `Spec 0`, not the empty prestack, with `QCoh(h_∅) = 0`.
- `colim_{U∈B} h_U → h_X` is not an equivalence of prestacks. For the plane X with doubled origin over a field k, with charts U and U′, take `R = k[x, y, a, b]/(ax + by − 1)` and the surjection `f : Spec R → 𝔸² ∖ 0 ⊆ X`. The only affine opens containing `𝔸² ∖ 0` are U and U′, which are incomparable, so the fibre over f is two points. The restrictions of f to `D(x)` and `D(y)`, which cover `Spec R`, have images `{x ≠ 0}` and `{y ≠ 0}`, affine opens of `U ∩ U′` that are least among the affine opens containing them, so the fibres there are contractible and the fibre over f becomes contractible after Zariski sheafification, a Čech and not a hypercover statement.

*Needs:* 2.3; 2.5; 3.1 (`L` left exact and colimit-preserving, with the Zariski covers among the fpqc covers; the local lifting criterion; hypercomplete fpqc sheaves and hypersheafification); 3.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.2 (`presentable_limit`); InfinityTopoi §7.3.4 (a monomorphism that is an effective epimorphism is an equivalence); HypercompletenessAndCoherence §2.4.1 (HTT Lemma 6.5.3.11), §2.5.2 (HTT Corollary 6.5.3.13) and §2.5.1 (HTT Theorem 6.5.3.12).

## Layer 4: Pushforward along affine morphisms

### 4.1 Affine morphisms of prestacks

Define a map `f : Y → X` of prestacks on T to be affine if for every point `Spec A → X` the fibre product `Y ×_X Spec A` is affine, `Spec f_η` for a test ring `f_η` under A; it is flat affine if moreover each `A → f_η` is flat (SAG §6.3.2 for relative representability). In the classical context the fibre products are those of `PreStk_CRing`, computed with ordinary tensor products.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Affine morphisms are stable under composition and base change, and contain the maps between affines and the equivalences; affine morphisms to `Spec A` are the maps `Spec B → Spec A`.
- For f affine, `Y ≃ colim_{(A,η)} Spec f_η` over `Pts(X)^op`: X is the colimit of its points (2.1), and pulling back along f preserves colimits, colimits in `PreStk_T` being universal (InfinityTopoi §3.3.2, applied in the universe u+1).
- Affineness is local on the target. Let `Y → Spec R` be a map from an fpqc sheaf (3.1) and `{R → R_i}` an fpqc cover such that every `Y ×_{Spec R} Spec R_i` is affine; then Y is affine (for schemes, Stacks, Descent, Lemma 35.37.1, tag 0245). In the classical context the same holds for an fppf sheaf Y and an fppf cover (2.5). Consequently a map `f : Y → Z` of fpqc sheaves is affine as soon as, for some map `U → Z` along which every point of Z lifts after an fpqc cover (an fpqc surjection, 3.3), the base change of f along every point of U is affine; in the classical context the same holds for fppf sheaves and lifts after fppf covers. Proof route. Put `R′ = ∏_i R_i`, faithfully flat over R, with Čech nerve `R′_•`. Sheaves carry finite products of test rings to products, so among sheaves `Spec R′` is the coproduct of the `Spec R_i`, and as colimits of sheaves are universal (InfinityTopoi §3.3.2), `Y ×_{Spec R} Spec R′` is the affine `Spec B_0`, `B_0` the product of the rings of the `Y ×_{Spec R} Spec R_i`; then each `Y ×_{Spec R} Spec R′_n` is affine, a base change of `Spec B_0 → Spec R′` along `Spec R′_n → Spec R′`. Spec being fully faithful, these form a cosimplicial test ring `B_•` under `R′_•` whose transitions are base changes. Put `B = lim_Δ B_•`, computed on underlying connective modules (1.1). By 3.2 the maps `B ⊗_R R′_n → B_n` are equivalences, B being the R-module that descends the `B_n`; it is connective, and discrete when the `B_n` are, because `R → R′` is faithfully flat (1.3). `Spec R′ → Spec R` is an effective epimorphism of sheaves (3.1, local lifting; for fppf sheaves InfinityTopoi §9.2.3), and so are its base changes to Y and to `Spec B` (InfinityTopoi §7.5.1). So Y and `Spec B`, a sheaf (3.2), are the colimits among sheaves of the Čech nerves of these base changes (InfinityTopoi §7.1.1), the same simplicial object `Spec B_•`, and `Y ≃ Spec B` over `Spec R`. For the consequence, lift a point `Spec R → Z` to U along a cover `{R → R_i}`: each `Y ×_Z Spec R_i` is the base change of f along a point of U, hence affine, and the first statement applies to `Y ×_Z Spec R`.

**Checks.**

- `id_X` and every `Spec B → Spec A` are affine.
- The classical `h_{𝔸² ∖ 0} → h_{𝔸²}` is not affine: its base change along the identity of `𝔸²` is not affine.
- For a nontrivial affine group scheme G over k, `Spec k → BG` is affine with fibre G, whereas `BG → Spec k` is not: its fibre is BG, whose value at the discrete test ring `O(G)` has `π₁ = G(O(G)) ∋ id ≠ 1` at the trivial torsor, while the values of an affine at discrete test rings are discrete. For `G = 1` both maps are the identity of `Spec k`, which is affine.
- The sheaf condition on Y is used. In any context let `Y ⊆ Spec ℤ` be the sieve generated by the fpqc cover `{ℤ → ℤ[1/2], ℤ → ℤ[1/3]}`: `Y(A)` is a point when 2 or 3 is a unit of `π₀A` and empty otherwise. Its base changes along `Spec ℤ[1/2]` and `Spec ℤ[1/3]` are these affines, yet Y is not affine: an affine carries `ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3]` to the product of its values at the factors, here a point, whereas neither 2 nor 3 is a unit of that product, so `Y(ℤ[1/2] × ℤ[1/3])` is empty. Its fpqc sheafification is `Spec ℤ` (3.1, local lifting), affine as the statement predicts.

*Needs:* 1.1 (limits of test rings on underlying modules); 1.3 (faithfully flat extension of scalars); 2.1; 2.5 (fppf sheaves and covers, for the classical fppf form); 3.1 (fpqc sheaves, local lifting); 3.2 (descent of modules; affines are sheaves); 3.3 (fpqc surjections); InfinityTopoi §3.3.2 (colimits in a presheaf ∞-category and in an ∞-topos are universal, HTT Proposition 6.1.3.10), §7.1.1 (Čech nerves of effective epimorphisms), §7.5.1 (pullback stability), §9.2.3 (local lifting, for the fppf topology).

### 4.2 Pushforward, projection formula and base change

Let `f : Y → X` be affine, and in the classical context flat affine. Prove: `f^*` has a right adjoint `f_*` computed pointwise, `(f_* F)(η) = (restriction of scalars along A → f_η)(F(Y_η))`; `f_*` preserves small colimits; the projection formula `f_*(f^*M ⊗ N) ≃ M ⊗ f_*N` holds; and for every pullback square of prestacks in the same context, with `g : X′ → X` arbitrary, the Beck–Chevalley map `g^* f_* → f′_* g′^*` is an equivalence (SAG Proposition 6.3.4.1 and Corollary 6.3.4.3, pp. 505–507; GR-I Chapter I.3, Proposition 2.2.2, p. 153). In the classical context these are PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0's `affinePushforward`, `affineBaseChange` and `affineProjectionFormula`; the statement here extends them to every affine morphism in the animated and `E∞` contexts.

*Proof route.* Write X as the colimit of its points and Y as in 4.1, so `QCoh(X) ≃ lim Mod_A` and `QCoh(Y) ≃ lim Mod_{f_η}` (2.3). For a transition `A → A′` the square of extension functors is a pushout square of test rings (in the classical context because `A′ ⊗_A f_η` is the derived pushout, f being flat), hence right adjointable by 1.2; apply the adjointable-limit statement of 1.5. The projection formula and base change reduce to points, where they are 1.2.

**Checks.**

- For `f = id` the pushforward, the adjunction and the projection formula are identities.
- For `f : Spec R[t] → Spec R`, `f_* O = R[t]` as an R-module, free of infinite rank, not of rank one; its base change to `R′` is `R′[t]`.
- For the classical, non-flat `f = g : Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ`, base change fails (Conventions witness), and the pointwise formula is wrong: it would give `𝔽_p` at the point `Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ`, whereas the pullback of `f_* 𝔽_p` there is `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`. In `AnRing` the same square satisfies base change.
- Projection formula with `M = R[1]` and `N = B` for `Spec B → Spec R`: both sides are the R-module `B[1]`.

*Needs:* 1.2; 1.5; 2.3; 4.1; PrismaticCohomologyPartII §4.0 (`affinePushforward`, `affineBaseChange`, `affineProjectionFormula`, the classical context).

### Examples

- The structure map of `𝔸¹_R` pushes `O` forward to the free R-module `R[t]`, and base change along `R → R′` gives `R′[t]` in every context.
- For a finite locally free `Spec B → Spec A`, `f_*` sends perfect modules to perfect modules, and the projection formula identifies `f_* f^* M` with `M ⊗_A B`.

## Layer 5: Schemes

This layer identifies `QCoh` of the classical prestack of a Mathlib scheme with its quasi-coherent derived sheaves in the enhancement of EnhancedDerivedSheaves, with no quasi-compactness or quasi-separatedness hypothesis, and transports pushforward, the projection formula and base change.

### 5.1 The affine comparison, natural in the ring

For `R ∈ CRing`, let `Φ_R : QCoh(Spec R) ≃ Mod_{θιR} ≃ D∞(R) ≃ Dqc(Spec R)` be the composite of 2.2, 1.2 and `quasicoherentDerived_affine`. Prove that `Φ_R` is a symmetric monoidal equivalence and is natural in R: for `φ : R → S`, `Φ_S ∘ φ^*` is identified with derived pullback `L(Spec φ)^* ∘ Φ_R` (`enhancedPullback`), coherently in composable maps; on homotopy categories `Φ_R` is SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18's `Coherent.DQCoh.affineEquiv`, and it carries the tensor product to `derivedTensor` (`derivedTensor_affine`) (Stacks, Derived Categories of Schemes, Lemma 36.3.5, tag 06Z0; SAG Proposition 2.2.3.3, p. 202, and Remark 6.2.2.5, p. 491).

*Proof route.* The functor `M ↦ M~` from complexes of R-modules to complexes of `O_{Spec R}`-modules is exact, carries K-flat complexes to K-flat complexes compatibly with tensor products, and commutes with base change, `(S ⊗_R M)~ ≅ (Spec φ)^* M~` on K-flat M, by ordinary natural isomorphisms. Localizing this ordinary natural transformation of families of categories at quasi-isomorphisms (`derivedFamily`) gives a coherent natural transformation from `R ↦ D∞(R)` to `R ↦ Dqc(Spec R)`, which is `quasicoherentDerived_affine` objectwise.

**Checks.**

- `O_{Spec R} ↦ R[0]`, and on `Spec ℤ`, `(ℤ/2)[0] ↦ (ℤ/2)~`, supported at `(2)`.
- For `φ : ℤ → 𝔽_p`, the image of `ℤ/p` is the derived pullback, with cohomology `𝔽_p` in degrees 0 and −1; the underived pullback has no degree −1 term.
- `(ℤ/2)~ ⊗ᴸ (ℤ/2)~` has cohomology `(ℤ/2)~` in degrees 0 and −1, matching `ℤ/2 ⊗ᴸ_ℤ ℤ/2` in `Mod_ℤ`.

### 5.2 Zariski descent for quasi-coherent derived sheaves

For any Mathlib scheme X define `Dqc(X)` as the full subcategory (Tau Ceti's `SSet.fullEnhancedSubcategory`, EnhancedDerivedSheaves E0.3) of `D∞(O_X)`, the enhanced derived category of the Zariski ringed site of X, on the objects whose image in Mathlib's `DerivedCategory X.Modules` lies in SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18's `Coherent.DQCoh X`; for qcqs X it is EnhancedDerivedSheaves' `QuasicoherentDerived`. Prove that for every hypercover `U_• → X` of the Zariski topos in which each `U_n` is a disjoint union of affine opens, restriction gives a symmetric monoidal equivalence `Dqc(X) ≃ lim_{[n]∈Δ} Dqc(U_n)`, and that `Dqc` of a disjoint union is the product (Stacks, Derived Categories of Schemes, Lemmas 36.3.2 and 36.3.3, tags 0A0J and 08D3).

*Proof route.* Full faithfulness, for all of `D∞(O_X)`, is `hypercoverPullback_unit`, after identifying Cartesian systems on the simplicial topos `X/U_•` (`cartesianDerivedSystem`) with the coherent limit of the `D∞(O_{U_n})` through `derivedFamily` over `NΔ` and coCartesian sections. For essential surjectivity, truncate a Cartesian system with quasi-coherent cohomology, `τ^{≥−m} K_•`, which stays Cartesian since restriction to opens is t-exact; descend each truncation by `hypercoverDescent_boundedBelow`; form the derived limit `K` of the descended tower (`derivedTowerLimit`). Restriction to an affine open commutes with the derived limit, and on an affine U, `K_U ≃ Rlim τ^{≥−m} K_U` for every complex with quasi-coherent cohomology (Stacks Lemma 36.3.3, tag 08D3), while the cohomology sheaves of K are the quasi-coherent limits of an eventually constant system (Lemma 36.3.2, tag 0A0J); so K lies in `Dqc(X)` and restricts to the given system.

**Checks.**

- For `ℙ¹_k` with its two standard charts, the gluing of `O(−2)[1]` and the class of 3.4's Check, spanned by `t⁻¹`, are recovered from the charts and the overlap.
- For `X = ⨿_ℕ ℙ¹_k`, which has no finite affine cover, the hypercover by all affine opens of the components gives `Dqc(X) ≃ ∏_ℕ Dqc(ℙ¹_k)`.
- The quasi-coherence hypothesis is used: for the generic point `j : U → X` of the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring, `j_! O_U[0]` is not in `Dqc(X)` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18 Check), and the derived-limit step of the route does not apply to it.

### 5.3 Quasi-coherent sheaves on schemes

Prove: for every Mathlib scheme X there is a symmetric monoidal equivalence `Φ_X : QCoh(h_X) ≃ Dqc(X)`, natural in X for pullback, which for `X = Spec R` is `Φ_R` of 5.1, carries the pointwise tensor product to `derivedTensor`, induces on homotopy categories an equivalence with `Coherent.DQCoh X` compatible with shifts and distinguished triangles, and for an open cover of X is compatible with the Zariski gluing of 3.4 and 5.2. ## Layer 6: The t-structure, perfect and almost perfect objects

### 6.1 Pointwise properties

Define, for a property P of pairs (A, M) of a test ring and a module that is stable under base change, an object F of `QCoh(X)` to have P if every `F(η)` has P (SAG Definition 6.2.5.3, p. 495). Apply this to connectivity, flatness, perfectness, almost perfectness and local freeness of finite rank (1.4), obtaining the full subcategories `QCoh(X)^cn`, `Perf(X)`, `APerf(X)` and `Vect(X)`.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Pullback preserves each of these subcategories; on `Spec A` they are the corresponding subcategories of `Mod_A` (SAG Remark 6.2.5.4 and Example 6.2.5.5, p. 495).
- These properties are local for the fpqc topology: if `A → B` is faithfully flat and `B ⊗_A M` has P, so does M (SAG Proposition 2.8.4.2, p. 275; for perfect and almost perfect modules through the descent of 3.2). Hence for an fpqc-local equivalence `f : Y → X`, F has P exactly when `f^* F` does (SAG Remark 6.2.5.6, p. 495).
- `QCoh(X)^cn` contains `O_X`, is closed under small colimits, extensions and tensor products, and is a symmetric monoidal ∞-category (SAG Example 6.2.5.7, p. 495, for colimits and extensions; the paragraph preceding SAG Proposition 6.2.6.3, p. 497, for the unit, tensor products and the symmetric monoidal structure).

**Checks.**

- n-truncatedness is not a pointwise property in this sense: it is not stable under base change (1.4), so it does not define a subcategory of `QCoh(X)` by this recipe.
- On `Spec ℤ`, `⊕_ℕ O` is connective and flat but not perfect, and `ℤ[x]` regarded as a module over `ℤ` is flat, not perfect.
- On `BΓ × Spec k`, an object is perfect exactly when its underlying k-module is perfect, whatever the Γ-action.

*Needs:* 1.4; 2.3; 3.2.

### 6.2 The t-structure

Prove: (a) if X is a u-small colimit of affines in `PreStk_T`, or more generally satisfies the hypothesis of 3.4(b) (its fpqc hypersheafification lies in the smallest full subcategory of hypercomplete fpqc sheaves containing the representables and closed under u-small colimits), then `QCoh(X)^cn` is presentable and is the connective part of an accessible t-structure, `QCoh(X)_{≥0} = QCoh(X)^cn`; pullbacks are right t-exact; on `Spec A` it is the t-structure of 1.3 (SAG Remark 6.2.5.8, pp. 495–496, under condition (3) of SAG Proposition 6.2.3.4, p. 493; GR-I Chapter I.3, §1.5.1, p. 149). Presentability of `QCoh(X)` alone is not the hypothesis: the argument needs the colimit presentation, which also gives presentability of `QCoh(X)^cn`. (b) Let X be a prestack with affine diagonal admitting a faithfully flat map `p : Spec A → X`, that is a flat affine map (4.1; p is affine because the diagonal is) that is an fpqc surjection (3.3). Call X geometric if moreover it is a hypercomplete fpqc sheaf. The sheaf condition belongs to the name only: (a) applies to every such X, and the statements that follow hold for every such X, geometric or not. For such X: F is connective, respectively coconnective, exactly when `p^* F` is; the t-structure is compatible with filtered colimits and is left and right complete; and the heart is the category of descent data of discrete `π₀A`-modules along `p`. In the classical context the same holds for every classical prestack X that has quasi-affine diagonal and admits a flat fpqc surjection `p : Spec A → X`. Here the diagonal is quasi-affine when every base change of `X → X × X` along a point `Spec R → X × X` is the classical prestack `h_Z` (2.5) of a quasi-affine scheme Z. Then every base change `Spec R ×_X Spec A ≃ Spec(R ⊗_ℤ A) ×_{X × X} X` of p along a point `Spec R → X` is the classical prestack of a quasi-affine scheme, and p is flat when each of these schemes is flat over R. Such a p need not be affine, and the descent data of the heart are then taken on the quasi-affine terms of its Čech nerve. For `X = Y^♮` with Y a stack of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.18 with affine diagonal and a flat affine atlas `Spec A → Y` along which every object of Y lifts after an fppf cover, (b) applies to the induced `p : Spec A → Y^♮`: by 2.5 the base changes of the diagonal and of p along points are the classical prestacks of the corresponding affine 2-fibre products, and every point lifts along p after an fppf cover, which is an fpqc cover (3.1). This holds although `Y^♮` is an fppf sheaf that need not be an fpqc sheaf (a Check of 7.4), and the heart is §1.22's `Stacks.QCoh(Y)` (SAG Proposition 9.1.3.1 and Corollary 9.1.3.2, pp. 733–734; GR-I Chapter I.3, Proposition 1.5.4, p. 150).

*Proof route.* (a) Let 𝒞 be the class of prestacks X for which `QCoh(X)^cn` is presentable. Affines lie in 𝒞 (1.3). Let `X = colim_j X_j` be a u-small colimit with every `X_j` in 𝒞, taken in `PreStk_T` or in hypercomplete fpqc sheaves. Then `QCoh(X) ≃ lim_j QCoh(X_j)` (2.3, respectively 3.3(a)–(b)). Moreover `QCoh(X)^cn ≃ lim_j QCoh(X_j)^cn`, because connectivity of an object is detected on the `X_j`. In prestacks every point of X factors through some `X_j`, since `X(A)` is the colimit of the `X_j(A)`. In hypercomplete fpqc sheaves the map `⨿_j X_j → X` is an effective epimorphism (InfinityTopoi §7.6.1); it is the hypersheafification of the map to X from the coproduct in `PreStk_T`, whose value at A is the coproduct of the `X_j(A)`, so by the local lifting criterion of 3.1 every point does so after an fpqc cover; connectivity is stable under base change and fpqc-local (6.1). Extension of scalars preserves colimits and connectivity, so this is a u-small limit of presentable ∞-categories along colimit-preserving functors, which is presentable (`presentable_limit`). Hence 𝒞 is closed under u-small colimits. It contains the X of (a), using `QCoh(X)^cn ≃ QCoh(L X)^cn` (3.3(a), 6.1) in the second case. `QCoh(X)^cn` is closed under colimits and extensions in the stable `QCoh(X)` (6.1), so it is the connective part of a t-structure (HA Proposition 1.4.4.11, p. 160), which is accessible because `QCoh(X)^cn` is presentable. (b) The map to X from the realization of the Čech nerve of the fpqc surjection p is inverted by the hypersheafification (the route of 3.3(c)), so the hypersheafification of X is the colimit, in hypercomplete fpqc sheaves, of that Čech nerve; no sheaf condition on X enters. Its terms are affine, or in the classical quasi-affine case quasi-affine schemes, which are finite colimits of affines in sheaves; so (a) applies. By 3.3, `QCoh(X) ≃ lim_{Δ_s} Mod_{A_n}` for the Čech nerve of p, whose terms are affine by the affine diagonal and whose injective transitions are flat, hence t-exact (1.3); the t-structure of the limit is computed levelwise and inherits completeness and compatibility with filtered colimits from the `Mod_{A_n}`. Its heart is the limit of the hearts, the descent data, which for classical stacks is §1.22's presentation equivalence. In the classical quasi-affine case the terms are quasi-affine schemes, handled by 5.3 and §2.18's `DQCoh.tStructure`.

**Checks.**

- On `Spec A` the heart is `ModuleCat (π₀A)`, and for `A = 𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p` it is `ModuleCat 𝔽_p`.
- Coconnectivity is not pointwise even on an affine (SAG Warning 6.2.5.9, p. 496): `𝔽_p` on `Spec ℤ` lies in `QCoh_{≤0}`, while its pullback along `Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ` is `𝔽_p ⊗ᴸ_ℤ 𝔽_p`, with `π₁ ≠ 0`.
- For `G_m` over ℤ, the heart of `QCoh(BG_m)` is the category of ℤ-graded abelian groups, the comodules over `ℤ[t, t⁻¹]`.
- For `Γ = ℤ/2` and k = `𝔽₂`, the heart of `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k)` is the category of `k[Γ]`-modules, and `Ext¹` of the unit there is `𝔽₂`.
- The plane Z with doubled origin over a field k, with charts `U ≅ U′ ≅ 𝔸²_k`, is covered by the quasi-affine clause and is not geometric. `h_Z` is an fpqc sheaf (Stacks, Descent, Lemma 35.13.7, tag 023Q) with discrete values, hence hypercomplete. The base change of the diagonal of `h_Z` along `U × U′` is `U ∩ U′ = 𝔸² ∖ 0`, quasi-affine and not affine; Z is quasi-separated, so its diagonal is a quasi-compact immersion, and every base change of it along a point `Spec R → h_Z × h_Z` is quasi-affine (Stacks, Morphisms of Schemes, Lemma 29.13.6, tag 02JR). The chart map `p : U ⊔ U′ → h_Z` is flat and an fpqc surjection, and its base change along U is `U ⊔ (𝔸² ∖ 0) → U`, flat and not affine. Accordingly connectivity in `QCoh(h_Z) ≃ Dqc(Z)` (5.3) is tested on the two charts.

## Layer 7: Quotients and equivariant sheaves

### 7.1 Group prestacks, actions and realizations

Fix a prestack S, the base. Define a group prestack over S as a groupoid object `G_•` of the slice `PreStk_T/S` (HTT Definition 6.1.2.7, PDF p. 559) whose object of objects `G_0 → S` is an equivalence. Its underlying prestack is `G = G_1 → S`, with unit `e : S → G` and multiplication on `G ×_S G`. An action of G on a prestack X over S is a groupoid object `(G^{×_S n} ×_S X)_n` of `PreStk_T/S` with object of objects X, together with a map to `G_•` restricting to the projections. Its realization `|G^{×_S •} ×_S X|` is the colimit in `PreStk_T` of the underlying simplicial object; this is also its colimit in the slice, `Δ^op` being weakly contractible. The quotient `[X/G]` is the fpqc hypersheafification of the realization (3.1), and `BG = [S/G]`. The absolute case is the final base `S = Spec ℤ`, where `×_S` is the product. A discrete group Γ gives the constant group prestack Γ over `Spec ℤ`. In the classical context, a group scheme G over a scheme S acting on an S-scheme X gives through h (2.5), which preserves fibre products, a group prestack `h_G` over `h_S` and an action on `h_X`. These are the group spaces and actions of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.13 through `GroupSpace.ofGroupScheme`. Such a G is a group prestack over `h_S` and not an absolute one: its unit has source `h_S`, not the final prestack.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Groupoid objects in `PreStk_T` and in its slices are effective, prestacks forming a presheaf ∞-topos whose slices are ∞-topoi (HTT Theorem 6.1.0.6, PDF pp. 547–548, through Propositions 6.1.3.10 and 6.1.3.19, PDF pp. 566–567 and 573–574). So `X ×_{|G^{×_S •} ×_S X|} X ≃ G ×_S X`, through the action and projection maps.
- For every G the atlas `X → [X/G]` is an fpqc surjection. The map `X → |G^{×_S •} ×_S X|` is an effective epimorphism of `PreStk_T`, its Čech nerve being the effective groupoid object of the action (InfinityTopoi §7.1.1). Its image under the hypersheafification L (3.1) is therefore an effective epimorphism of hypercomplete fpqc sheaves (InfinityTopoi §7.4.1), so every point `Spec R → [X/G]` lifts to X after an fpqc cover `{R → R_i}` (3.1, local lifting). Along such a lift `Spec R_i → X`, the base change of `L X → [X/G]` is `L(G ×_S Spec R_i) → Spec R_i`, by the effectivity above, the left exactness of L and `L Spec R_i = Spec R_i` (3.2): the hypersheafification of a base change of `G → S`, split by the unit. When X and `G ×_S Spec R_i` are hypercomplete fpqc sheaves, for instance when X is affine and G is affine over S (3.2, 4.1), `L X = X` and this is the base change of the atlas itself, the projection `G ×_S Spec R_i → Spec R_i`; for the constant group ℤ/2 it is not (a Check below). Then `[X/G]` has affine diagonal when S and X are affine and G is affine over S. The map `X × X → [X/G] × [X/G]` is an fpqc surjection, a point of the target lifting after a common refinement of the covers that lift its two components. The base change of the diagonal along it is `X ×_{[X/G]} X ≃ G ×_S X → X × X`, by the effectivity above, L being left exact and fixing the affines X and `G ×_S X` (3.2); this is a map of affines, hence affine. As `[X/G]` and its square are hypercomplete fpqc sheaves, affineness is local on the target (4.1), and the diagonal is affine. So the atlas is affine: at a point `Spec R → [X/G]` its base change is that of the diagonal along `X × Spec R → [X/G] × [X/G]`. If moreover G is flat over S, the atlas is flat: its base change `Spec B → Spec R` along a point becomes flat after the faithfully flat `R → ∏_i R_i`, and flatness descends along faithfully flat maps (6.1). So `[X/G]` is geometric in the sense of 6.2 when G is flat over S; the flatness of G gives the flatness of the atlas, not its surjectivity.
- The trivial group `1 = S` over S acts on every X over S through the constant bar construction, so `|1^{×_S •} ×_S X| ≃ X` and `[X/1] ≃ L X`, the hypersheafification. This is X exactly when X is a hypercomplete fpqc sheaf, as every affine is (3.2).
- For G acting on itself by translation, the bar construction is the Čech nerve of `G → S`, split by the unit e. So the realization is S, and `[G/G] ≃ L S`.

**Checks.**

- The trivial quotient is not the original prestack. For the empty prestack over `Spec ℤ`, `[∅/1] = L∅ = Spec 0`: `∅ → Spec 0` is an fpqc-local equivalence (3.3), and `Spec 0`, an affine, is a hypercomplete fpqc sheaf. At the zero ring `[∅/1]` has value a point and ∅ the empty value (2.1's Check). Both have `QCoh` the zero category (7.2).
- For G acting on itself by left translation, `|G^{×_S •} ×_S G|` is S, the final prestack when `S = Spec ℤ`. A quotient formed by taking `π₀` of each value would instead give the orbit set. That agrees here but not for `BG = [S/G]`, whose values have `π₁ = G(A)`.
- In the classical context take S = `Spec 𝔽₂` and the trivial group scheme `G = S`. Its unit is the identity of `h_S = Spec 𝔽₂`, the translation realization is `Spec 𝔽₂`, and its `QCoh` is `D∞(𝔽₂)` (7.2). Read as an absolute group prestack, G would need a unit `Spec ℤ → Spec 𝔽₂`, that is a point of `Spec 𝔽₂` at the test ring ℤ, and `Hom(𝔽₂, ℤ) = ∅`.
- For a discrete group Γ the realization of its action on the final prestack is the constant prestack BΓ.
- The hypersheafification is what makes the diagonal affine. For `Γ = ℤ/2`, the fibre of the diagonal of the constant prestack BΓ over the pair of base points at a test ring R is `Spec R × Γ = Spec R ⊔ Spec R`, a coproduct of prestacks. For `R = ℤ` its value at `ℤ × ℤ` has 2 points, whereas an affine Y has `Y(ℤ × ℤ) = Y(ℤ) × Y(ℤ)`, here of 4 points; so the realization BΓ does not have affine diagonal. Its hypersheafification `[Spec ℤ/Γ]` is also the quotient by `L Γ = Spec(ℤ × ℤ)` (3.3), affine over `Spec ℤ`, and the fibre becomes `L(Spec R ⊔ Spec R) = Spec(R × R)`, affine, as the bullet on affine diagonals asserts. Accordingly the base change of the atlas `Spec ℤ → [Spec ℤ/Γ]` along itself is `L Γ = Spec(ℤ × ℤ)`, whose value at `ℤ × ℤ` has 4 points, one for each idempotent of `ℤ × ℤ`, and not the constant prestack Γ, whose value there has 2: here X is a sheaf and `G ×_S Spec R_i = Γ × Spec ℤ` is not.
- Surjectivity of the atlas does not need G flat, and without flatness the atlas can fail to be flat. In the classical context over `S = Spec ℤ`, fix a prime ℓ and let G be the closed subgroup scheme of the constant group scheme ℤ/2 with `O(G) = ℤ × 𝔽_ℓ`: its R-points are the idempotents e of R with `ℓe = 0`, closed under `(e, e′) ↦ e + e′ − 2ee′`. G is affine and not flat over ℤ, `𝔽_ℓ` being ℓ-torsion. The atlas `S → BG` is affine and an fpqc surjection, its base change along itself being `G → S`, split by the unit; that base change is not flat, so this atlas does not make BG geometric. For `G = G_m` the same base change is `Spec ℤ[t, t⁻¹] → Spec ℤ`, free, and `BG_m` is geometric, as 6.2's Check of its heart uses.

### 7.2 Quasi-coherent sheaves on realizations and quotients

Prove: for every group prestack G over a prestack S and every action of G on a prestack X over S, in every context, `QCoh(|G^{×_S •} ×_S X|) ≃ lim_{[n] ∈ Δ} QCoh(G^{×_S n} ×_S X)` symmetric monoidally (`QCoh` carries colimits of prestacks to limits: GR-I Chapter I.3, §1.1.3, p. 144; SAG Lemma 6.2.1.12, p. 488). This limit is the ∞-category of coherently G-equivariant objects of `QCoh(X)`, and pullback along `X → |G^{×_S •} ×_S X|` is the forgetful functor. Moreover `QCoh([X/G]) ≃ QCoh(|G^{×_S •} ×_S X|)` with no hypothesis on G (SAG Proposition 6.2.3.1, p. 492; GR-I Chapter I.3, Corollaries 1.3.8 and 1.3.11, p. 147). For the action of the dual group Ĥ on the framed derived cocycle functor over `Spec Λ`, in the animated context, the limit is LanglandsParameterStacks §1.8's enhanced quasi-coherent category of the derived parameter stack, the totalization of module categories over the nerve of the action groupoid, which is the same bar construction; the statement here extends §1.8 to every action of a group prestack on a prestack and agrees with it there. The perfect objects (6.3) of `QCoh([X/G])` are its chartwise dualizable objects, which is how §1.8 defines `Perf` on its stacks: an object of the limit is dualizable exactly when each of its terms is (1.5), and its term on `G^{×_S n} ×_S X` is the pullback of its term on X along the map of the bar construction induced by any vertex `[0] → [n]`, pullback being symmetric monoidal; so it is perfect exactly when its term on X is dualizable in `QCoh(X)`.

*Proof route.* The first statement is 2.3 applied to the colimit defining the realization; the second is 3.3(a). The description of the perfect objects is 6.3 on `[X/G]`, transported along these symmetric monoidal equivalences, and 1.5 on the limit.

**Checks.**

- `QCoh([X/1]) = QCoh(X)`, since `[X/1] = L X` (7.1) and `QCoh(L X) ≃ QCoh(X)` (3.3). For `X = ∅` both are the zero category, although `[∅/1] = Spec 0` is not ∅.
- For G acting on itself by translation, `QCoh([G/G]) = QCoh(L S) = QCoh(S)`, the cosimplicial diagram being the Čech nerve of `G → S`. In the absolute case this is `D∞(ℤ)`, and for the trivial group over `Spec 𝔽₂` it is `D∞(𝔽₂)`.
- For the constant group `G = ℤ/2` over `Spec 𝔽₂`, `π₀ Map(O, O[1])` on `BG` is `H¹(ℤ/2; 𝔽₂) = 𝔽₂`, while the ordinary invariants of the atlas module `𝔽₂` see only `H⁰`.
- For `G = G_m` over ℤ the totalization is the ∞-category of comodules over `ℤ[t, t⁻¹]` in `D∞(ℤ)`, with heart the ℤ-graded abelian groups (6.2).
- Agreement with LanglandsParameterStacks §1.8 on its own Checks: for Ĥ over `S = Spec Λ` acting on itself by translation the limit is `QCoh(S) = Mod_Λ`, as §1.8 computes for the free translation quotient, and its perfect objects are `Perf(Λ)`, as §1.8 records: the term of M on the chart Ĥ is `M ⊗_Λ O(Ĥ)`, dualizable exactly when M is perfect, `Λ → O(Ĥ)` being faithfully flat (6.1); for `BĤ = [S/Ĥ]`, Ĥ being flat and affine over Λ, it is the ∞-category of `O(Ĥ)`-comodules in `Mod_Λ`, so a Λ-module without coherent coaction data is not an object, as §1.8 records. A definition dropping the nerve would give `Mod_Λ` in both cases.

*Needs:* 1.5 (dualizable objects of a limit); 2.3; 3.3; 6.1 and 6.3 (perfect = dualizable, for the perfect objects); 7.1; LanglandsParameterStacks §1.8 (the derived parameter stacks, for the agreement only).

### 7.3 Quotients by discrete groups

Let Γ be a u-small discrete group acting on a prestack X, that is a functor `BΓ → PreStk_T` with value X, and let `X/Γ` be its colimit. Prove that `QCoh(X/Γ) ≃ QCoh(X)^{hΓ}`, the limit of the induced action `BΓ → CAlg(Ĉat∞)` on `QCoh(X)`, which for finite Γ is EnhancedDerivedSheaves' `homotopyFixedPoints` (`QCoh` is the right Kan extension along the Yoneda embedding and so carries colimits of prestacks to limits: GR-I Chapter I.3, §1.1.3, p. 144; SAG Lemma 6.2.1.12, p. 488); that `X/Γ` is the realization of the action of the constant group prestack Γ (7.1); and that `QCoh([X/Γ]) ≃ QCoh(X/Γ)` (GR-I Chapter I.3, Corollary 1.3.8, p. 147). No finiteness of Γ and no covering property of `X → X/Γ` is assumed. For the lattice `Γ = ℤ^d` acting on `Spec C` through ring automorphisms, the equivalence `QCoh([Spec C/ℤ^d]) ≃ D∞(C)^{hℤ^d}` is HabiroCohomologyFoundations §1.27's, and its heart, vector bundles and perfect complexes (6.2(a), 6.3) are those of its §1.28: the heart is the C-modules with d commuting invertible operators, each semilinear for the corresponding automorphism of C, the vector bundles are the objects whose underlying C-module is finite projective, and the perfect objects those whose underlying object of `D∞(C)` is perfect. The statement here extends the first to every discrete group acting on every prestack, in every context, and agrees with it on the lattice: both are 2.3 and 3.3(a) applied to the same realization.

*Proof route.* 2.3 applied to the colimit over BΓ, and 3.3(a). For the agreement with §1.28: `Spec C/ℤ^d` is a u-small colimit of affines, so 6.2(a) applies; every point of it lifts to `Spec C` up to homotopy, its value at A being the homotopy orbits `Map_T(C, A)_{hℤ^d}`, so connectivity, perfectness and local freeness of finite rank are read on the underlying object of `D∞(C)` (6.1, 6.3); and the automorphisms act t-exactly, so a homotopy fixed point is coconnective exactly when its underlying object is (the forgetful functor has the left adjoint `M ↦ ⊕_{γ ∈ ℤ^d} γ^* M`, which preserves connectivity), and the heart is the homotopy fixed points of the heart of `D∞(C)`, an ordinary category of equivariant objects.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial group, `QCoh(X/1) = QCoh(X)`.
- For Γ = ℤ acting trivially on `Spec C`, `QCoh(Spec C/ℤ) = Fun(Bℤ, D∞(C))`, complexes with an automorphism, and `π₀ Map(O, O[1]) = H¹(ℤ; C) = C`; the coarse quotient `Spec C` has `π₀ Map(O, O[1]) = 0`. For `C = ℚ[x^{±1}]` this is the class `ℚ[x^{±1}]` that a coarse quotient loses.
- For `Γ = ℤ^d` acting on `Spec C` through ring automorphisms, `QCoh(Spec C/ℤ^d) ≃ D∞(C)^{hℤ^d}`, as in HabiroCohomologyFoundations §1.27; for `d = 0` this is `D∞(C)`, with no fixed-point factor. The unit is C with the operators `σ_i` themselves, a rank-one vector bundle in the heart, as in §1.28; for `d = 1`, `C = ℚ[x^{±1}]` and `σ(x) = 2x`, the identity of C is not σ-semilinear, `id(x · 1) = x` while `σ(x) · id(1) = 2x`, so it is no equivariant structure on C.

*Needs:* 2.3; 3.3; 6.1, 6.2 and 6.3 (the t-structure and the perfect and locally free objects, for the lattice agreement); 7.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.3 (`homotopyFixedPoints`, `CoherentActionData`); HabiroCohomologyFoundations §§1.27–1.28 (the lattice case); Mathlib `groupCohomology`.

### 7.4 Classical quotient stacks

Let k be a commutative ring, G a flat affine group scheme over k, regarded through h as a group prestack over `Spec k` (7.1; SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.13 through `GroupSpace.ofGroupScheme`), acting on a k-scheme X, and let `[X/G]` be SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.21's `actionQuotient`, an fppf stack in groupoids. Prove, in the classical context: (a) `[X/G]^♮` is `L_fppf` of the realization `|h_G^{×_k •} ×_k h_X|` over `Spec k` (2.5), so the map to it from the realization is inverted by the fpqc hypersheafification (3.1) and `QCoh([X/G]^♮) ≃ lim_{[n]∈Δ} Dqc(G^{×_k n} ×_k X)` (3.3(a), 7.2, 5.3); (b) the t-structure of 6.2(a), computed levelwise along the flat face maps of the bar construction, has heart SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.22's `Stacks.QCoh([X/G])`, the G-equivariant quasi-coherent `O_X`-modules (`QCoh.presentationEquiv`; Stacks, Sheaves on Algebraic Stacks, Definition 96.11.1 and Proposition 96.14.3, tags 06WG and 06WT); (c) for `X = Spec k` the heart is the category of comodules over the flat Hopf algebra `O(G)`; (d) if X is quasi-compact and `[X/G]^♮` has affine diagonal (4.1), for instance if X is affine, the t-exact functor `D⁺(QCoh([X/G])^♥) → QCoh([X/G])^+` extending the inclusion of the heart is an equivalence (GR-I Chapter I.3, Proposition 2.4.3 and Lemma 2.4.5, p. 156; SAG Corollary 10.4.6.8, p. 930; HNR Theorem C.1, p. 16, for the lisse-étale analogue).

*Proof route.* (a) The value of the realization at R is the nerve of the action groupoid with objects `X(R)` and arrows `(G ×_k X)(R)`, colimits of prestacks being computed pointwise, and `[X/G]` is the fppf stackification of that groupoid prestack (§1.21), so 2.5's comparison of stackification with `L_fppf` identifies `[X/G]^♮` with `L_fppf` of the realization. By 3.1 the hypersheafification inverts the map from the realization, so 3.3(a) gives `QCoh([X/G]^♮) ≃ QCoh(|h_G^{×_k •} ×_k h_X|)`; then the first statement of 7.2, and 5.3 on each `G^{×_k n} ×_k X`, h preserving fibre products (7.1). For X affine, `[X/G]^♮` has affine diagonal. Every object of `[X/G](Spec R)` is fppf-locally in the image of the groupoid prestack (§1.18 `Stack.stackification_locally_essSurj`), so every point of `[X/G]^♮ × [X/G]^♮` lifts to `h_X × h_X` after an fppf cover (2.5). The base change of the diagonal along `h_X × h_X → [X/G]^♮ × [X/G]^♮` is `h_X ×_{[X/G]^♮} h_X ≃ h_{G ×_k X} → h_X × h_X`, by the effectivity of 7.1, `L_fppf` being left exact and fixing `h_X` and `h_{G ×_k X}`, which are affines, hence fpqc sheaves (3.2) and so fppf sheaves (3.1); it is a map of affines. So the diagonal of the fppf sheaf `[X/G]^♮` is affine by 4.1 in its fppf form. (b) and (c): 6.2(a) applies to `[X/G]^♮`, whose hypersheafification is that of the realization (a), the colimit in hypercomplete fpqc sheaves of the hypersheafifications of the `h_{G^{×_k n} ×_k X}`, each a small colimit of affines by the route of 3.4(a). The face maps `G^{×_k n} ×_k X → G^{×_k (n−1)} ×_k X` are flat, so pullback along them is t-exact, the levelwise t-structure, whose connective part is that of 6.2(a), is a t-structure on the limit, and its heart is the limit of the hearts, the descent data of quasi-coherent modules, which is §1.22's presentation equivalence. (d): the functor exists by the dual of HA Theorem 1.3.3.2 (p. 97), the heart being Grothendieck abelian with enough injectives and the t-structure right complete; GR-I Lemma 2.4.5 also asks the t-structure to be compatible with filtered colimits. Both properties are 6.2(b), applied to the map f below, which is flat affine and an fpqc surjection, `[X/G]^♮` having affine diagonal; 6.2(b) does not ask `[X/G]^♮` to be an fpqc sheaf, which it need not be (Checks). As X is quasi-compact, it has a finite affine open cover `{U_i}`; put `Spec A = ⨿_i U_i` and let f be the composite `Spec A → h_X → [X/G]^♮`. f is affine: a map from an affine to a prestack with affine diagonal is affine, its base change along a point `Spec R` being the base change of the diagonal along `Spec(A ⊗ R) → [X/G]^♮ × [X/G]^♮`. f is flat: after an fppf cover `R → R′` over which a point lifts to `h_X`, the base change of f becomes the disjoint union over i of the preimages of `U_i` under the orbit map `G ×_k Spec R′ → X` of the lifted point (by the effectivity of 7.1, as in (a)), open subschemes of `G ×_k Spec R′` and so flat over R′, and flatness descends along `R → R′` (6.1). And every point of `[X/G]^♮` lifts along f after an fppf cover, first to `h_X` (§1.18 `Stack.stackification_locally_essSurj`) and then Zariski-locally to some `U_i`. So `f^*` is t-exact and kills no nonzero object of the heart, and `f_*` (4.2) is t-exact. Every heart object F therefore embeds, through the unit `F → f_* f^* F`, into `f_* I` for an injective A-module I containing `f^* F`, and `Hom(F′, f_* I[n]) = Hom(f^* F′, I[n]) = 0` for `n > 0` and F′ in the heart; GR-I Lemma 2.4.5 then gives the equivalence on bounded-below objects. Without the affine diagonal f need not be affine, and `f_*` need not be computed pointwise.

The API consists of the following facts, both used without proof in BZFN's proof of Corollary 3.22 (p. 24).

- Fibres over the classifying stack. Let `k → R` be a ring map and P a `G_R`-torsor over `Spec R`, a point `Spec R → (BG)^♮`. Then `[X/G]^♮ ×_{(BG)^♮} Spec R ≃ (contractedProduct G_R P X_R)^♮` over `Spec R`, the classical prestack (2.5) of the fppf sheaf `(P × X_R)/G_R` of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.16. Proof route: 2.5 carries the 2-fibre product of `[X/G] → BG` and the point to this fibre product of prestacks. By `actionQuotient.torsorEquiv`, an object of that 2-fibre product over a scheme T over `Spec R` is a `G_T`-torsor Q with a G-equivariant map `Q → X` and an isomorphism `Q ≅ P_T` of torsors, and a morphism is an isomorphism of torsors compatible with both. So these groupoids are discrete, equivalent to the sets of G-equivariant maps `P_T → X`. Sending local sections p of P and x of X to the equivariant map carrying p to x is invariant under the diagonal action and defines a map from `(P × X_R)/G_R` to the sheaf of equivariant maps `P → X`. It is an isomorphism where P is trivial (`contractedProduct_trivial`, compatibly with base change by `contractedProduct_baseChange`), hence an isomorphism of fppf sheaves, P being fppf-locally trivial.
- Change of group. Let `φ : H → G` be a homomorphism of flat affine group schemes over k that is a closed immersion, such that the fppf quotient sheaf `G/H` of G by translation by H is represented by a k-scheme, on which G acts by translation. Then the map `(BH)^♮ → (BG)^♮` induced by `Q ↦ pushforwardTorsor φ Q` is equivalent over `(BG)^♮` to the map `[(G/H)/G]^♮ → (BG)^♮` induced by `G/H → Spec k`. Proof route: an object of `[(G/H)/G]` over T is a `G_T`-torsor P with an equivariant map `f : P → G/H` (`actionQuotient.torsorEquiv`). The preimage under f of the unit coset is an `H_T`-torsor Q, fppf-locally a translate of H inside P because `G → G/H` is an H-torsor of fppf sheaves, and `pushforwardTorsor φ Q ≅ P` compatibly with f. Conversely Q gives `pushforwardTorsor φ Q` with the map to `G/H` induced by `G → G/H`. These are inverse equivalences of stacks over BG, and 2.5 turns them into an equivalence of prestacks. So, by the previous fact, the fibre of `(BH)^♮ → (BG)^♮` over a G-torsor P is `(contractedProduct G_R P (G/H)_R)^♮`. A G-equivariant invertible module on `G/H` is an object of the heart of `QCoh([(G/H)/G]^♮) ≃ QCoh((BH)^♮)` (7.4(b)), invertible because each of its evaluations is (6.3), whose restriction to that fibre is the module descended from it.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial group `G = Spec k`, `[X/1]` is the stack of X, `[X/1]^♮ = h_X` (2.5), the bar construction is constant with value `h_X`, and (a) is 5.3.
- For `G = G_m` over ℤ and `X = Spec ℤ`, the heart is ℤ-graded abelian groups.
- For `G = ℤ/p` over `𝔽_p` and `X = Spec 𝔽_p`, `Ext^i` of the unit in the heart is `Hⁱ(ℤ/p; 𝔽_p) = 𝔽_p` for every `i ≥ 0`, and (d) identifies it with `π₀ Map(O, O[i])` in `QCoh(BG)`.
- The comparison is not asserted on unbounded objects: for `G = G_a` over `𝔽_p`, `D(QCoh(BG)^♥)` is not left complete (SAG Warning 10.4.6.9, p. 930), while `QCoh(BG)` is (6.2).
- Quasi-compactness of X does not replace the affine diagonal in (d). For the trivial group and the plane Z with doubled origin over a field, Z is quasi-compact, but `[Z/1]^♮ = h_Z` does not have affine diagonal (6.2's Check), and the map f of the route of (d), here the chart map `U ⊔ U′ → h_Z`, is not affine: its base change along U is `U ⊔ (𝔸² ∖ 0) → U`. (d) is not asserted for it.
- `[X/G]^♮` need not be an fpqc sheaf. Over `k = ℚ` let G be the profinite constant group scheme of `Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ)`, flat and affine, with `O(G)` the colimit over the finite Galois extensions K of ℚ of the rings `∏_{Gal(K/ℚ)} ℚ`. `Spec ℚ̄` is a G-torsor over `Spec ℚ`, since `ℚ̄ ⊗_ℚ ℚ̄ ≅ ℚ̄ ⊗_ℚ O(G)`. It is trivial over the faithfully flat `ℚ → ℚ̄` and over no fppf cover: a nonzero finitely presented ℚ-algebra has a residue field finite over ℚ, which receives no ring map from ℚ̄. So its descent datum along `ℚ → ℚ̄` is not effective in `(BG)^♮`, which is not an fpqc sheaf and so not geometric in the sense of 6.2. Its atlas `Spec ℚ → (BG)^♮` is flat affine, its base change along a torsor P being P (change of group, with `H = 1`), and an fpqc surjection, so (b)–(d) reach `(BG)^♮` through 6.2(b), which needs no sheaf condition. The heart is the `O(G)`-comodules, the ℚ-vector spaces with a continuous action of `Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ)`, as (c) says.
- Fibres over the classifying stack, trivial torsor: for `P = G_R` the fibre is `X_R` (`contractedProduct_trivial`), so the base change of `[X/G]^♮ → (BG)^♮` along the atlas `Spec k → (BG)^♮` is `h_X`. For `X = Spec k` every fibre is `Spec R`, `[Spec k/G] → BG` being the identity.
- Fibres over the classifying stack, nontrivial torsor: for `k = ℚ`, `G = ℤ/2`, `X = G` with the translation action and `P = Spec ℚ(i)`, the points of the fibre over a ℚ-algebra T are the equivariant maps `P_T → G`, the trivializations of `P_T`, so the fibre is P, with no ℚ-point. Reading the fibre as `X_R`, ignoring the twist, would give `Spec ℚ ⊔ Spec ℚ`, with two (8.4's Check).
- Change of group: for `H = 1`, `G/H = G` and the fact identifies `Spec k → (BG)^♮` with `[G/G]^♮ → (BG)^♮`, whose fibre over P is `P ×^G G ≅ P`, as 7.1's translation quotient `[G/G] ≃ L S` predicts; for `H = G` both maps are the identity of `(BG)^♮`. For `G = G_m` and `H = μ_n` over ℤ, `G/H` is `G_m` through the n-th power map, so `(Bμ_n)^♮ ≃ [G_m/G_m]^♮` with `G_m` acting on the quotient through `t ↦ t^n`. The structure sheaf of the quotient `G_m` with its linearization through the character t of `G_m` becomes the tautological character of `μ_n` in the heart of `QCoh((Bμ_n)^♮)`, and with the trivial linearization it becomes O, so the linearization, not the module, determines the invertible object.

## Layer 8: Compact generation and perfect stacks

### 8.1 Qcqs schemes

Prove: for a qcqs scheme X, `QCoh(h_X)` is compactly generated by a single perfect object, and its compact, perfect and dualizable objects coincide (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18 `perfect_generator`; Stacks, Derived Categories of Schemes, Theorem 36.15.3 and Proposition 36.17.1, tags 09IS and 09M1; SAG Proposition 9.6.1.1, p. 807).

*Proof route.* The unit is compact: `Map(O, −)` is `Ω^∞` of the total direct image along the quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism `X → Spec ℤ`, which preserves small colimits (5.4(a)), and `Ω^∞` preserves filtered colimits. So every dualizable object F is compact, `Map(F, −) ≃ Map(O, F^∨ ⊗ −)`, and perfect objects, being dualizable (6.3), are compact. By `perfect_generator`, transported through 5.3, a single perfect object P generates: an object admitting no nonzero map from any shift of P is zero. Let `C₀` be the smallest stable full subcategory closed under retracts containing P; its objects are perfect, the perfect objects forming such a subcategory (6.1, 1.4). The functor `Ind(C₀) → QCoh(h_X)` extending the inclusion preserves colimits and is fully faithful, the objects of `C₀` being compact. `QCoh(h_X)` is presentable (3.4(b), whose hypothesis holds by the route of 3.4(a)), so the functor has a right adjoint (`adjointFunctorTheorem`), and the cofibre of the counit at any object admits no nonzero map from the shifts of P, hence vanishes. So the functor is an equivalence, and the compact objects of `QCoh(h_X)` are those of `Ind(C₀)`, namely `C₀` (`indCompactComparison`), which are perfect. Perfect equals dualizable by 6.3. For `DQCoh X` the equality of compact and perfect objects is Stacks 09M1.

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spec A`, the generator is `O` and compact = perfect is 1.4; the route returns `C₀` = the perfect A-modules.
- For `ℙ¹_k`, `O ⊕ O(−1)` generates: an object with no maps from any shift of `O` or `O(−1)` is zero.
- Quasi-compactness is needed: for `X = ⨿_ℕ Spec ℤ`, `QCoh(h_X) = ∏_ℕ D∞(ℤ)` is compactly generated, but `O` is dualizable and not compact, since `Hom(O, ⊕_i O_i) = ∏_i ℤ ≠ ⊕_i ℤ` for the unit `O_i` of the i-th factor. The route fails at its first step: `X → Spec ℤ` is not quasi-compact, and its total direct image, the product over ℕ, does not preserve this coproduct.

*Needs:* 1.4; 3.4 (3.4(b), presentability); 5.3; 5.4 (5.4(a), the total direct image preserves colimits); 6.1; 6.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18 (`perfect_generator`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.1 (`compactObjects`, `IsCompactlyGenerated`, the supplied Ind-completion, `indCompactComparison`), E3.2 (`adjointFunctorTheorem`).

### 8.2 Perfect stacks

Define a prestack X to be perfect if it has affine diagonal, `Perf(X)` is essentially small, and the colimit-preserving functor `Ind(Perf(X)) → QCoh(X)` extending the inclusion of `Perf(X)` is an equivalence (BZFN Definition 3.2, p. 17); `QCoh(X)` is then presentable, as the Ind-completion of a small ∞-category. Essential smallness is part of the definition because `Perf(X)`, a limit over the (u+1)-small `Pts(X)`, is not small a priori, and the supplied Ind-completion is that of a small ∞-category. Prove that X with affine diagonal is perfect exactly when `QCoh(X)` is compactly generated and its compact and dualizable objects coincide (BZFN Proposition 3.9, p. 20). Prove that for perfect X the unit `O_X` is compact. Hence the space-valued `Map(O_X, −)` commutes with filtered colimits. Moreover the derived global sections `RΓ(X, −) = q_* : QCoh(X) → QCoh(Spec ℤ) = D∞(ℤ)`, the right adjoint of pullback along the final map `q : X → Spec ℤ`, preserve small colimits, and `Map(O_X, F) ≃ Ω^∞ RΓ(X, F)`.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Perfectness depends only on `QCoh(X)` and the diagonal; it is invariant under fpqc-local equivalences between prestacks with affine diagonal (3.3).
- On a perfect stack, the compact objects are `Perf(X)`, which is idempotent complete and generates.
- Derived global sections: for perfect X, `q_*` exists by `adjointFunctorTheorem`, `q^*` preserving colimits (2.3). It is exact, and it preserves coproducts because `q^*` carries the compact generator ℤ of `D∞(ℤ)` to the compact `O_X` (`rightAdjoint_preservesCoproducts_iff`), so it preserves small colimits (`exact_preservesColimits_iff_coproducts`). The space-valued `Map(O_X, −) = Ω^∞ ∘ q_*` preserves filtered colimits, as `Ω^∞ : D∞(ℤ) → S` does, but not finite coproducts, which `Ω^∞` does not preserve (last Check).

**Checks.**

- `Spec A` is perfect in every context.
- `BG_m` over any ring R is perfect: `QCoh(BG_m)` is the graded objects of `Mod_R`, generated by the characters. For the character `O(n)` of weight n, the `Mod_R`-valued mapping object `M ↦ M_n`, the weight-n part, preserves colimits, so `Map(O(n), −) = Ω^∞(−)_n` commutes with filtered colimits and `O(n)` is compact; a graded object is dualizable exactly when it is perfect in each weight and zero in all but finitely many weights, exactly the compact objects.
- For the scheme `X = ⨿_ℕ Spec ℤ`, in the classical context, `h_X` has affine diagonal and compactly generated `QCoh` but is not perfect (8.1's Check): the definition must ask compact = dualizable. The diagonal is affine: a point of `h_X × h_X` at R is a pair of locally constant functions `f, g : Spec R → ℕ`, and the fibre of the diagonal over it is the classical prestack of the open and closed subscheme of `Spec R` where `f = g`, which is `Spec(Re)` for the idempotent e cutting it out, a direct factor of R. The coproduct of prestacks `⨿_ℕ Spec ℤ`, with value ℕ at every test ring, has the same `QCoh`, `∏_ℕ D∞(ℤ)` (2.3, 3.3), and does not have affine diagonal: over the pair of indices `(0, 1)` at ℤ the fibre is the empty prestack, whose value at the zero ring is empty, whereas an affine has a point there (2.1's Check).
- Mapping spaces are not derived global sections. On `Spec 𝔽₂`, `Map(O, 0 ⊕ 0)` is a point while `Map(O, 0) ⊔ Map(O, 0)` has two points, so the space-valued `Map(O, −)` does not preserve this coproduct although O is compact. `RΓ` gives `0 ⊕ 0 = 0` on both sides.

*Needs:* 2.3; 3.3; 4.1 (affine diagonal); 6.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.1 (the supplied Ind-completion, `compactObjects`, `IsCompactlyGenerated`), E5:abstract.4 (`idemCompletion`), E3.2 (`adjointFunctorTheorem`), E3.3 (`exact_preservesColimits_iff_coproducts`, `rightAdjoint_preservesCoproducts_iff`).

### 8.3 Permanence of perfectness

Prove the following (BZFN Lemma 3.20, Propositions 3.21 and 3.24, and Corollary 3.23, pp. 23–25).

- (a) If `f : Y → X` is affine and X is perfect, then Y is perfect; in the classical context f is assumed flat affine (4.1).
- (b) In the classical context, let `f : Y → X` be flat and representable by qcqs schemes in the sense of 5.4(b), with X perfect, and let `{L_i}_{i∈I}` be a set of invertible objects of `QCoh(Y)` whose restrictions to every base change `Y_η` along a point of X are invertible modules forming an ample family on the scheme `Y_η` (Stacks, Morphisms of Schemes, Definition 29.12.1, tag 0FXR: `Y_η` is quasi-compact and every point of it lies in the non-vanishing locus of a global section of a positive tensor power of some `L_i`, that locus being affine). Then Y is perfect. A single invertible object whose restriction to every `Y_η` is ample is the case of a one-element I, the case 8.4 uses.
- (c) In the animated and `E∞` contexts, for every morphism `p : X → Y` between perfect stacks, the base change `X ×_Y Spec A` along every point `Spec A → Y` is perfect. In the classical context the same holds for every morphism p representable by qcqs schemes with affine diagonal, meaning that every such base change is (the classical prestack of) a qcqs scheme with affine diagonal, with no flatness of p and no perfectness of X or Y assumed; affine morphisms are of this kind.
- (d) In the animated and `E∞` contexts, for perfect `X₁` and `X₂` over a prestack Y with affine diagonal, `X₁ ×_Y X₂` is perfect; for Y final this says that `X₁ × X₂` is perfect. In the classical context, if `X₂` is perfect and `X₁ → Y` is flat affine, or is flat and representable by qcqs schemes with a set of invertible objects on `X₁` as in (b), then `X₁ ×_Y X₂` is perfect, for every map `X₂ → Y`.

*Proof route.* (a) and (b): Y has affine diagonal, since `Y → Y ×_X Y` is affine (in (b) each `Y_η` has affine diagonal, Stacks, Morphisms of Schemes, Lemma 29.12.2, tag 0FXS) and `Y ×_X Y → Y × Y` is a base change of the diagonal of X. `f_*` preserves colimits and satisfies the projection formula (4.2, 5.4(b)), so `f^*` preserves compact objects (`rightAdjoint_preservesCoproducts_iff`); hence `O_Y = f^*O_X` is compact and every dualizable object of `QCoh(Y)` is compact. In case (a) let the family be `{O_Y}`. For compact M on X, `i ∈ I` and `n ≥ 0`, the object `f^*M ⊗ L_i^{⊗−n}` is dualizable, M being dualizable because X is perfect, and so compact. These objects generate: if N admits no nonzero map from any of their shifts, adjunction gives `Map(M, f_*(L_i^{⊗n} ⊗ N)[k]) = 0` for all compact M and all k, so `f_*(L_i^{⊗n} ⊗ N) = 0` because X is compactly generated; by the pointwise formula for `f_*`, `RΓ(Y_η, L_i^{⊗n} ⊗ N) = 0` on every base change `Y_η`, which forces `N|_{Y_η} = 0`, in case (a) because restriction of scalars is conservative, in case (b) by Stacks, Derived Categories of Schemes, Lemma 36.16.1 (tag 0BQR) with 5.3, whose proof uses of the ample invertible module only that the scheme is quasi-compact with affine diagonal and that each point lies in the affine non-vanishing locus of a global section of a positive power, and so applies verbatim to an ample family (the generation step of BZFN Proposition 3.21); and every point of Y factors through some `Y_η`. Let `C₀` be the smallest stable subcategory closed under retracts containing these generators; it consists of compact dualizable objects, dualizable objects being the perfect ones (6.3). The induced functor `Ind(C₀) → QCoh(Y)` is fully faithful, and its image is closed under colimits and contains the generators, hence is everything (the inclusion has a right adjoint, sending N to the presheaf `c ↦ Map(c, N)` on `C₀`, which is exact and takes small values, hence is an object of `Ind(C₀)`: for a generator `c = f^*M ⊗ L_i^{⊗−n}` the space `Map(c, N) ≃ Map(M, f_*(L_i^{⊗n} ⊗ N))` is a mapping space of `QCoh(X)`, which is presentable because X is perfect; the cofibre of the counit admits no map from the generators). So the compact objects of `QCoh(Y)` are those of `Ind(C₀)`, namely `C₀` (`indCompactComparison`; Neeman's theorem, BZFN §3.1.2, p. 20), all dualizable, and 8.2 applies. This route never pulls back along a projection `Y_η → Y`, which in the classical context need not be flat (4.2); BZFN's proof of the second half of Lemma 3.20 does.

(c): in the animated and `E∞` contexts, `X ×_Y Spec A → X` is the base change of the point, affine because Y has affine diagonal, so the base change is perfect by (a) (BZFN Corollary 3.23). In the classical context the base change is a qcqs scheme with affine diagonal, perfect by 8.1 with 8.2.

(d): in the animated and `E∞` contexts, `X₁ ×_Y X₂ → X₁ × X₂` is a base change of the diagonal of Y, hence affine, so by (a) it suffices that `X₁ × X₂` is perfect (BZFN Proposition 3.24). For a point `U = Spec A → X₁`, the map `q_U : U × X₂ → X₂` is affine, so `U × X₂` is perfect by (a) and its unit is compact (8.2). Hence the pushforward `p_{U*} : QCoh(U × X₂) → Mod_A` along the projection `p_U : U × X₂ → U`, the A-linear derived global sections, preserves small colimits. It is exact, and it preserves coproducts because `p_U^*` carries the compact generator A of `Mod_A` to the compact unit (`rightAdjoint_preservesCoproducts_iff`, `exact_preservesColimits_iff_coproducts`). It satisfies the projection formula, both sides preserving colimits in the `Mod_A` variable and agreeing at A. The squares of these pushforwards over maps of points of `X₁` are right adjointable (4.2 for the affine maps between the `U × X₂`), and the projection `π₁ : X₁ × X₂ → X₁` has a pushforward computed pointwise and preserving colimits (1.5, as in 4.2). Then the unit of `X₁ × X₂` is `π₁^*O_{X₁}`, compact; the objects `π₁^*M₁ ⊗ π₂^*M₂` for compact `M_i` are dualizable, hence compact; and they generate: if N admits no nonzero map from their shifts, then `π_{1*}(π₂^*M₂^∨ ⊗ N) = 0` for each compact `M₂` because `X₁` is compactly generated, so at every point U of `X₁`, `Map(M₂, q_{U*}(N|_{U × X₂})[k]) = 0` for all k, whence `q_{U*}(N|_{U × X₂}) = 0` because `X₂` is compactly generated, and `N|_{U × X₂} = 0` because `q_{U*}` is computed pointwise by restriction of scalars (4.2); so N = 0. Compact objects are dualizable by the Ind argument of (a), the mapping spaces from the generators being `Map(π₁^*M₁ ⊗ π₂^*M₂, N) ≃ Map(M₁, π_{1*}(π₂^*M₂^∨ ⊗ N))`, small because `QCoh(X₁)` is presentable. In the classical context, `X₁ ×_Y X₂ → X₂` is a base change of `X₁ → Y`, and its base change along a point of `X₂` is that of `X₁ → Y` along the composite point; so it is flat affine, respectively flat and representable by qcqs schemes with the pulled-back family, and (a), respectively (b), applies over the perfect `X₂`.

**Checks.**

- The identity of a perfect stack and `Spec B → Spec A` are affine and preserve perfectness.
- In the animated and `E∞` contexts, (d) with Y final makes `BG_m × BG_m = B(G_m²)` over ℤ perfect, generated by the characters of `G_m²`.
- Flatness is used in the classical context: for the closed point `Spec 𝔽_p → Spec ℤ`, base change of affine maps along it is not computed by the classical fibre product (4.2), and the route of (a) does not apply to non-flat classical maps. For the same reason the classical clause of (c) is not stated for arbitrary morphisms between perfect stacks: the derived route applies (a) to `X ×_Y Spec A → X`, the base change of a point of Y, which is affine but flat only when the point is.
- For `A = k[ε]/(ε²)` over a field k and `X₁ = X₂ = Y = Spec A` in the classical context, the classical clause of (d) applies through the flat affine identity `X₁ → Y` and returns `Y`, perfect. A route through `X₁ ×_Y X₂ → X₁ × X₂` would not: that map is the diagonal `Spec A → Spec(A ⊗ A)`, tensor product over ℤ, affine but not flat, since for its kernel I the group `Tor₁^{A⊗A}(A, A) = I/I² = Ω¹_{A/ℤ}` surjects onto `Ω¹_{A/k}`, which is `k·dε` in characteristic not 2 and `A·dε` in characteristic 2, nonzero in both.
- An ample family need not contain an ample member: the affine line Z with doubled origin over a field k carries invertible modules `L_1` and `L_{−1}` with global sections whose non-vanishing loci are the two affine charts, so `{L_1, L_{−1}}` is an ample family, whereas Z is not separated and so has no ample invertible module (Stacks, Morphisms of Schemes, Remark 29.12.3, tag 0FXT). Thus (b) applies to `h_Z → Spec k` and the single-module case does not; its conclusion agrees with 8.1 with 8.2, Z being a qcqs scheme with affine diagonal (tag 0FXS).

### 8.4 Quotient stacks in characteristic zero

Prove: over a field k of characteristic zero, for an affine group scheme G of finite type over k acting on a quasi-projective k-scheme X that carries a G-linearized ample line bundle L, the classical prestack `[X/G]^♮` is perfect; in particular `QCoh([X/G]^♮) ≃ Ind(Perf([X/G]^♮))` (BZFN Corollary 3.22, p. 24).

*Proof route.* `BGL_n` is perfect. It has affine diagonal (7.4, with `X = Spec k`), and `QCoh((BGL_n)^♮)` is `QCoh` of the geometric stack `[Spec k/GL_n]` of 7.1, `GL_n` being affine and flat over `Spec k`: the fpqc hypersheafification of `(BGL_n)^♮` is that quotient (7.4(a)), so 3.3(a) and 7.2 identify the two, and the t-structures agree, connectivity being fpqc-local (6.1). The t-structure of that geometric stack is left and right complete (6.2(b)). In characteristic zero every comodule over the coordinate Hopf algebra of `GL_n`, finite-dimensional or not, is completely reducible (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Comodule.isCompletelyReducible_of_reductive`, `GL_n` being reductive by `TauCeti.GeneralLinear.reductiveCommHopfAlgProperty_finiteTypeCoordinateHopfAlgebra`). Through the heart of 7.4(c) and ReductiveGroups Layer 1 this says that every subobject of an object of the heart is a direct summand, so every short exact sequence of the heart splits: the heart is semisimple, every object of it is injective, and `Ext^i` vanishes in the heart for `i > 0`. Every representation, the union of its finite-dimensional subrepresentations (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Subcomodule.iUnion_finiteSubcomodules_eq_univ`, over the field k), is then a sum of irreducible finite-dimensional ones. Semisimplicity of the heart does not by itself control the mapping spaces of `QCoh` (Checks): the comparison is 7.4(d), which applies with `X = Spec k`, affine, so that `(BGL_n)^♮` has affine diagonal (7.4) and the map f of its route is the atlas `Spec k → (BGL_n)^♮`. For M in the heart it gives `π₀ Map(O, M[i]) = Ext^i(O, M)` computed in the heart, hence zero for `i > 0`. So the derived invariants `RΓ(BGL_n, −) = q_*`, the right adjoint of pullback along `q : BGL_n → Spec k`, carry the heart to the heart, and they are t-exact. `q_*` is left t-exact, as the right adjoint of `q^*`, which is t-exact because every k-algebra is flat over the field k. It carries a connective object with finitely many nonzero `π_i` to a connective object, by induction along its truncations. A connective M is the limit of its truncations `τ_{≤m} M` (left completeness), and `π₀ RΓ(τ_{≤m} M)` is constant in `m ≥ 0`, the fibre `RΓ(π_m M)[m]` being concentrated in degree m; so no `lim¹` term enters `π_{−1}`, and `RΓ(M)` is connective. Hence for a finite-dimensional representation V, placed in degree zero, every M and every integer i, `π₀ Map(V[i], M) ≅ Hom(V, π_i M)` in the heart; this is a statement about `π₀`, the mapping space having higher homotopy. So every such V is compact, and it is dualizable (6.3). These V generate. Suppose `Hom(V, π_i M) = 0` for all V and i. Then every `π_i M`, being the union of its finite-dimensional subrepresentations as above, is zero, and M = 0 because the t-structure of that geometric stack is left and right complete. By the Ind argument of 8.3 (`indCompactComparison`), the compact objects are the smallest stable subcategory closed under retracts containing these V. These are the objects with finitely many nonzero `π_i`, each finite-dimensional: such an object is a finite iterated extension of the `π_i M[i]` through its truncations. They are dualizable, dualizable objects forming a stable subcategory closed under retracts. Conversely a dualizable object F is compact, `Map(F, −) = Map(O, F^∨ ⊗ −)` with O compact. So the compact and dualizable objects coincide, and 8.2 applies. A homomorphism `G → GL_n` that is a closed immersion (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AffineGroupSchemeCat.exists_isClosedImmersion_generalLinear`) gives `BG → BGL_n`. The quotient `GL_n/G` is a quasi-projective k-scheme with a `GL_n`-linearized ample line bundle `O(1)` (gap:chevalley-quasiprojective-quotient), so by 7.4 (change of group) `(BG)^♮ → (BGL_n)^♮` is `[(GL_n/G)/GL_n]^♮ → (BGL_n)^♮`, and `O(1)` is an invertible object of `QCoh((BG)^♮)`. The base change along a point `Spec R → BGL_n`, a `GL_n`-torsor P, is the twist `P ×^{GL_n} (GL_n/G)` (7.4, fibres over the classifying stack), to which `O(1)` restricts as the twisted `O(1)`: P is the frame bundle of the rank-n locally free module it defines by the descent of SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.1, hence Zariski-locally trivial, so the twist is glued from copies of `GL_n/G × U_i`, flat and quasi-projective over R, with the twisted `O(1)` ample on it. Thus 8.3(b) applies with the one-element family `{O(1)}` and BG is perfect. For `[X/G] → BG` this gluing is not available: a point `Spec R → BG` is a G-torsor P over R, which need not have sections Zariski-locally on `Spec R`, and the base change of `[X/G]` along it is the contracted product `P ×^G X` (7.4, fibres over the classifying stack). By gap:twist-quasiprojective, `P ×^G X` is a scheme, flat and quasi-projective over R, on which the module descended from L is ample. The G-linearized L is an object of the heart of `QCoh([X/G]^♮)` (7.4(b)), invertible because each of its evaluations is (6.3), and its restriction to each `P ×^G X` is that descended module. So `[X/G] → BG` is flat and representable by qcqs schemes, L restricts to an ample module on every base change, and 8.3(b) applies again with a one-element family.

**Checks.**

- `B(ℤ/p)` over ℚ is perfect. By Maschke's theorem `Hⁱ(ℤ/p; V) = 0` for `i > 0` and every representation V over ℚ, and these groups are `π₀ Map(O, V[i])` by the group-cohomology formula of 8.5, which holds over every field; so invariants are exact and `O` is compact. For a finite group this formula, and not 7.4(d), is the comparison between the heart and `QCoh`.
- Semisimplicity of the heart alone does not make invariants exact. On the constant prestack `K(ℤ, 2) × Spec ℚ`, `QCoh` is `Fun(K(ℤ, 2), Mod_ℚ)` (2.3) and its heart is equivalent to the ℚ-vector spaces, `K(ℤ, 2)` being connected and simply connected, so every object of the heart is injective. Yet `π₀ Map(O, O[2]) = H²(K(ℤ, 2); ℚ) = ℚ`, while `Ext²(O, O)` in the heart is 0: the comparison of 7.4(d) fails there, and the route above uses it.
- `BG_a` over ℚ is perfect, `G_a` being a closed subgroup of `GL₂` with quasi-projective quotient `GL₂/G_a`.
- The compact dualizable objects are not only the representations in degree zero. On `BGL₁ = BG_m` over ℚ, `O[1]` is invertible with inverse `O[−1]`, and its atlas pullback has `π₁ = ℚ` and `π₀ = 0`. For `V = ℚ`, `i = 0` and `M = O[1]`, `Map(V, M) = Ω^∞(ℚ[1])` is `K(ℚ, 1)`: its `π₀` is `0 = Hom(V, π₀ M)` and its `π₁` is ℚ. The identification of mapping spaces with Homs in the heart holds only on `π₀`.
- The characteristic hypothesis cannot be dropped: `B(ℤ/p)` over `𝔽_p` is not perfect (8.5).
- Base changes of `[X/G] → BG` are not Zariski-locally products. Take `k = ℚ`, `G = ℤ/2` and `X = G` with the translation action and `L = O`, so `[X/G] = Spec ℚ`, and the point of BG given by the torsor `P = Spec ℚ(i)` over `R = ℚ`. The base change is `P ×^G G = P = Spec ℚ(i)`, which has no ℚ-point, whereas `X × Spec ℚ = Spec ℚ ⊔ Spec ℚ` has two; the gluing used for `GL_n`-torsors would produce the latter.
- The descent datum of a twist, computed. For the same G and P, let G act on `X = ℙ¹_ℚ` through `diag(1, −1)` on `ℚ²`, with `L = O(1)` linearized through that representation. Over `ℚ(i)` the twist is `ℙ¹_{ℚ(i)}`, and the descent datum on `ℚ(i)²` is complex conjugation composed with `diag(1, −1)`, whose invariants are `ℚ·e₁ ⊕ ℚ·i e₂`. So `P ×^G X = ℙ(ℚ·e₁ ⊕ ℚ·i e₂) ≅ ℙ¹_ℚ`, with the descended `O(1)` ample, a scheme as 8.3(b) requires. Conjugation alone, without the action, would fix `ℚ²` and forget the torsor.

### 8.5 Dualizable objects that are not compact in positive characteristic

Prove: for `G = ℤ/p` over `𝔽_p`, the structure sheaf of `BG` is dualizable and not compact in `QCoh(BG)`, so `BG` is not perfect. More generally, on `BΓ × Spec k` for a finite group Γ and a field k of characteristic p dividing `|Γ|`, `O` is dualizable and not compact (HR-perf Introduction, p. 1, the analogue for the lisse-étale `D_qc` of a quasi-compact algebraic stack with quasi-finite and separated diagonal: if the stack is not tame, its structure sheaf is perfect and not compact there). Compact generation itself does not fail here (last Check). It fails in another setting and for another category: a quasi-compact, quasi-separated algebraic stack is poorly stabilized when it has a geometric point of positive characteristic whose stabilizer group scheme has a reduced identity component that is not semi-abelian, and for such a stack the lisse-étale `D_qc` is not compactly generated, `BG_a` in characteristic p being an example (HNR Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2, and §4, p. 9). That theorem is about `D_qc`, which this roadmap does not treat (Scope and ownership), and nothing here is deduced from it. `B(ℤ/p)` is not poorly stabilized, the stabilizer `ℤ/p` of its geometric points having trivial identity component.

The API consists of the following facts.

- Group cohomology as mapping spaces. Let V be a k-linear representation of a finite group Γ, an object of Mathlib's `Rep k Γ`, regarded in the heart of `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k) = Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` (2.3), which is also `QCoh` of `BG = [Spec k/Γ]` (7.1), its fpqc hypersheafification, by 3.3(a). Then `π₀ Map(O, V[i]) ≅ Hⁱ(Γ; V)` for `i ≥ 0`, Mathlib's `groupCohomology`. By 2.2's mapping-space formula the mapping space is `Ω^∞` of the shifted homotopy fixed points `V^{hΓ}[i]`. The cohomology of `V^{hΓ}` is group cohomology (EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.3, `homotopyFixedPoints` and `continuousDerivedInvariants`, Γ being finite and discrete).
- A coinduced witness. The characteristic p is prime: the characteristic of a field is prime or zero (`CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero`), and `p ≠ 0` because p divides `|Γ|`, which is positive. Cauchy's theorem gives an element g of order p in Γ (`exists_prime_orderOf_dvd_card`). Let C be the cyclic subgroup it generates, and `V = Coind_C^Γ k` (`Rep.coind` along the inclusion), with C acting trivially on k. Then `Hⁱ(Γ; V) ≅ Hⁱ(C; k) = k` for every `i ≥ 0`. The first isomorphism is Shapiro's lemma (`groupCohomology.coindIso`). The second is the cyclic computation (`Rep.FiniteCyclicGroup.groupCohomologyIsoEven`, `Rep.FiniteCyclicGroup.groupCohomologyIsoOdd`): the two maps `ρ(g) − 1` and the norm are `0` and multiplication by p, both zero on k. In degree 0 it is the invariants k.

*Proof route.* O is dualizable on every prestack (6.3). In `Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` the canonical map `⊕_{i≥0} V[i] → ∏_{i≥0} V[i]` is an equivalence. The forgetful functor to `Mod_k` is conservative and preserves sums and products, and in `Mod_k` the n-th homotopy of either side is the single term `π_n(V[n]) = V`. Hence `π₀ Map(O, ⊕_i V[i]) = ∏_i Hⁱ(Γ; V) = ∏_i k`. If O were compact, `Map(O, −)` would commute with the filtered colimit `⊕_i V[i] = colim_N ⊕_{i≤N} V[i]`, and so would `π₀`. That gives `colim_N ∏_{i≤N} k = ⊕_i k`, and the canonical map `⊕_i k → ∏_i k` is not surjective. For `Γ = ℤ/p` one may take `C = Γ` and `V = k`, so `V[i] = O[i]`.

**Checks.**

- For Γ trivial, `O` is compact: `Hⁱ = 0` for `i > 0`, and Γ has no element of order p, so there is no coinduced witness.
- For `Γ = ℤ/p` over `𝔽_p`, `Hⁱ(ℤ/p; 𝔽_p) = 𝔽_p` for every `i ≥ 0`, so the comparison `⊕ → ∏` is not surjective.
- For `Γ = S₃` over `𝔽₃`, trivial coefficients vanish in low degrees. `H¹(S₃; 𝔽₃) = Hom(S₃, 𝔽₃) = 0`, the abelianization of `S₃` having order 2. `H²(S₃; 𝔽₃)` is the part of `H²(C₃; 𝔽₃) = 𝔽₃` fixed by `S₃/C₃`, which acts by −1, so it is 0 as well. The coinduced witness from `C₃` is two-dimensional, with `Hⁱ(S₃; V) = Hⁱ(C₃; 𝔽₃) = 𝔽₃` for every i, and the comparison is `⊕_i 𝔽₃ → ∏_i 𝔽₃`, not surjective.
- Over ℚ the same Γ gives `Hⁱ = 0` for `i > 0` and `O` is compact (8.4).
- Compact generation is not what fails. `Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` is generated by the regular representation `k[Γ]`, which is compact: `Map(k[Γ], M)` is `Ω^∞` of the underlying k-module of M, induction being left adjoint to the forgetful functor, which preserves colimits and is conservative. For `Γ = ℤ/p` and `k = 𝔽_p`, `π₀ Map(k[Γ], ⊕_{i≥0} O[i])` is `π₀` of the underlying module `⊕_i k[i]`, that is k, equal to `⊕_i π₀ Map(k[Γ], O[i]) = k ⊕ 0 ⊕ 0 ⊕ ⋯`; whereas `π₀ Map(O, ⊕_i O[i]) = ∏_i 𝔽_p` is not `⊕_i π₀ Map(O, O[i]) = ⊕_i 𝔽_p`.

*Needs:* 2.2; 2.3 (`QCoh(BΓ × Spec k) ≃ Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)`); 3.3 (3.3(a), the same `QCoh` on `BG`); 6.3; 7.1 (`BG = [S/G]`); 8.2; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5:presentability.3 (`homotopyFixedPoints`, `continuousDerivedInvariants`); Mathlib `groupCohomology`, `Rep.coind`, `groupCohomology.coindIso`, `Rep.FiniteCyclicGroup.groupCohomologyIsoEven`, `Rep.FiniteCyclicGroup.groupCohomologyIsoOdd`, `CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero`, `exists_prime_orderOf_dvd_card`.

### 8.6 The positive-characteristic statement in the derived category of representations

Prove `not_preservesCoproducts_trivial`: for a field k of characteristic p and a finite group Γ whose order is divisible by p, both u-small, the trivial representation `Rep.trivial k Γ k`, placed in degree 0 of Mathlib's derived category `DerivedCategory (Rep k Γ)` by `DerivedCategory.singleFunctor`, is not compact in the sense of StableHomotopyKTheory §5.17 (`IsCompactForCoproducts`): its additive corepresented functor `preadditiveCoyoneda.obj (op K)` does not preserve the coproducts indexed by the types of the morphism universe (`PreservesColimitsOfShape` for the discrete category on such a type). Equivalently, some morphism from it to such a coproduct is not a finite sum of morphisms through the summand inclusions. This is the theorem of 8.5 read on the homotopy category of `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k)`, in which O is that object (HA Proposition 1.4.4.1(3), p. 154, for compactness; the second API fact below for the homotopy category).

The API consists of the following facts.

- In the homotopy category of a stable ∞-category with small colimits, an object is compact in the sense of StableHomotopyKTheory §5.17 exactly when it is compact in the ∞-category. By HA Proposition 1.4.4.1(3), p. 154, the latter means that every map from it to a small coproduct factors, up to homotopy, through a finite subcoproduct. In an additive category the comparison map `⊕_i Hom(K, X_i) → Hom(K, ∐_i X_i)` is always injective, composing with the morphism `∐_i X_i → X_j` that is the identity on the j-th summand and zero on the others recovering the j-th component; so it is bijective exactly when every morphism into the coproduct factors through a finite subcoproduct.
- The homotopy category of `QCoh(BΓ × Spec k) ≃ Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)` (2.3), where `Mod_k ≃ D∞(k)` (1.2), is `DerivedCategory (Rep k Γ)`, with O the trivial representation in degree 0 and `V[i]`, for a representation V, the representation V placed in Mathlib degree −i. `Rep k Γ` is Grothendieck abelian, being equivalent to the category of modules over the group algebra `k[Γ]` (`Rep.equivalenceModuleMonoidAlgebra`, `CategoryTheory.IsGrothendieckAbelian.of_equivalence`), so EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.3 gives its enhanced derived category `D∞(Rep k Γ)`, the localization of the nerve of its complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms, with homotopy category `DerivedCategory (Rep k Γ)` (`enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory`). A complex of representations is a functor from the one-object groupoid of Γ to complexes of k-modules, and a quasi-isomorphism of such complexes is one of underlying complexes; so composing with the localization of complexes of k-modules and using the universal property of the localization gives a functor `D∞(Rep k Γ) → Fun(BΓ, D∞(k))` over `D∞(k)`. Both forgetful functors to `D∞(k)` are conservative, preserve small colimits and have left adjoints that are `k[Γ] ⊗_k −` on underlying objects, the first because induction of representations is exact; the functor commutes with these left adjoints, so by `barrBeckLurie` it is an equivalence. The route uses no commutativity of `k[Γ]`, and no comparison with module spectra; for left module spectra over a discrete ring the corresponding statement is HA Remark 7.1.1.16, p. 1206.

*Proof route.* Choose g, C and `V = Coind_C^Γ k` as in 8.5, so that `Hⁱ(Γ; V) ≅ k` for every `i ≥ 0`. Let P be the complex with V in every nonpositive degree and zero differentials. Termwise it is both the direct sum and the product of the complexes `V[i]`, `i ≥ 0`. It is a coproduct of the `V[i]`, indexed by ℕ (lifted to the morphism universe), in `DerivedCategory (Rep k Γ)`, morphisms out of P into a K-injective complex being computed termwise. Morphisms out of k are computed by its bar resolution, a bounded-above complex of projective representations, so `Hom(k, P) = ∏_i Hom(k, V[i]) = ∏_i Ext^i(k, V) = ∏_i Hⁱ(Γ; V)` (`groupCohomologyIsoExt`). The morphism `f : k → P` all of whose components are nonzero is not in the image of `⊕_i Hom(k, V[i])`: an element of the image has only finitely many nonzero components. So the functor does not preserve this coproduct. The route uses Mathlib's homological algebra and the coinduced witness of 8.5, not the ∞-categorical theory; the two API facts show that the theorem and 8.5 say the same thing.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial group the hypothesis fails, p not dividing 1, and so does the conclusion: `Rep k 1` is the category of k-vector spaces, and k in degree 0 is compact, since `Hom(k, X) = H⁰(X)` and cohomology commutes with direct sums of complexes.
- For `Γ = ℤ/2` and `k = 𝔽₂`, take `V = k` (C = Γ): `Hⁱ(ℤ/2; 𝔽₂) = 𝔽₂` for every `i ≥ 0`, both maps `ρ(g) − 1` and the norm `1 + g` being zero on 𝔽₂. The morphism `k → ⊕_{i≥0} k[i]` whose i-th component is the nonzero class is not a finite sum through the summands.
- Over ℚ the hypothesis on the characteristic fails, and the trivial representation of `ℤ/p` is compact (8.4's Check on `B(ℤ/p)`): by Maschke's theorem it is a projective `ℚ[ℤ/p]`-module, so `Hom(ℚ, X) = H⁰(X)^{ℤ/p}`, which commutes with direct sums.
- Finiteness of Γ is used, and not only through divisibility of its order, whose conventional value for an infinite group is 0, divisible by every p. For `Γ = ℤ` and any field k the trivial representation is compact: it is the cokernel of the injective map `t − 1` on the free module `k[t, t⁻¹] = k[ℤ]`, so it is perfect, and morphisms out of it are computed by a two-term complex of free modules, which commutes with direct sums.
- Infinite coproducts are what the statement is about: a finite coproduct in a preadditive category is a biproduct, preserved by every additive functor, so restricting the index types to finite ones would make every object compact, the unit of `DerivedCategory (Rep 𝔽₂ (ℤ/2))` included.
- The comparison of homotopy categories needs no commutativity. For `Γ = S₃` and `k = 𝔽₃` the group algebra `𝔽₃[S₃]` is not commutative; `D∞(Rep 𝔽₃ S₃)` and `Fun(BS₃, D∞(𝔽₃))` are both monadic over `D∞(𝔽₃)` with monad `𝔽₃[S₃] ⊗_{𝔽₃} −`, and for the coinduced V of 8.5 both give `π₀ Map(k, V[i]) = Hⁱ(S₃; V) = 𝔽₃` for every `i ≥ 0`. A comparison through module spectra over the group algebra would need the comparison of `Mod_A(D(ℤ))` with module spectra that no supplier states (Scope and ownership), EnhancedDerivedSheaves' `moduleSpectra_equiv_derived` being stated for commutative rings.

*Needs:* 1.2 (`Mod_k ≃ D∞(k)`) and 2.3 (`QCoh(BΓ × Spec k) ≃ Fun(BΓ, Mod_k)`), for the comparison; 8.5 (the coinduced witness, for the route; the statement, for the comparison); StableHomotopyKTheory §5.17 (`IsCompactForCoproducts`, the notion of compactness); EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.3 (`enhancedDerived`, `enhancedDerived.homotopyCategory`, for the Grothendieck abelian category `Rep k Γ`), E0.3 (the supplied localization universal property), E5:abstract.5 (`barrBeckLurie`), for the homotopy category; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.preadditiveCoyoneda`, `CategoryTheory.Limits.PreservesColimitsOfShape`, `CategoryTheory.Discrete`, `Rep`, `Rep.trivial`, `Rep.equivalenceModuleMonoidAlgebra`, `CategoryTheory.IsGrothendieckAbelian.of_equivalence`, `HasDerivedCategory`, `DerivedCategory`, `DerivedCategory.singleFunctor`, `groupCohomologyIsoExt`, `Rep.coind`, `groupCohomology.coindIso`, `CharP.char_is_prime_or_zero`, `exists_prime_orderOf_dvd_card`.
