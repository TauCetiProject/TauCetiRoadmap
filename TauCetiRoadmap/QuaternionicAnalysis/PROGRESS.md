## QuaternionicAnalysis: 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-09 (`0b27eb0` to `7ca783d`)

The first stretch of quaternionic analysis reaches the Cauchy theorems on boxes. The [box Cauchy theorem](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy.html#TauCeti.left_cauchy_box) says that a function continuous on a closed coordinate box and left Fueter regular inside it has zero boundary flux against the 3-form `Dq`. Regularity is pointwise here, with no continuity of the derivative assumed. The right-handed version and the two-sided [Cauchy theorem](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy.html#TauCeti.cauchy_box) for `g * eᵢ * f` landed with it (TauCeti#31439). The [Cauchy–Fueter kernel](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Kernel.html#TauCeti.cauchyFueterKernel) `(q − q₀)⁻¹/‖q − q₀‖²` is regular on both sides away from its pole, and the boundary sums [vanish for a pole outside the box](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Exterior.html#TauCeti.left_cauchyFueter_box_of_notMem). The cutoff argument for an interior pole has its analytic core: the cutoff density has [mass exactly 2π²](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cutoff.html#Quaternion.integral_cauchyFueterCutoff_density), and integrating against it [recovers 2π² times the value at the pole](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Concentration.html#TauCeti.tendsto_setIntegral_cauchyFueterCutoff_density).

On the rigidity side, [left quotient rigidity](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Rigidity.html#TauCeti.leftQuotient_rigidity) shows that on a connected open set, a function with a left quotient derivative at every point is `q ↦ a + q*b`. Pointwise differentiability is the only hypothesis. The right and two-sided classifications have not landed yet. Fueter-regular functions are [harmonic](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Harmonic.html#TauCeti.LeftFueterOn.laplacian_eq_zero_of_contDiffOn), but only under an assumed `C²` hypothesis, since smoothness is not yet derived.

Underneath sit the matrices and determinants of quaternion multiplication, the quotient calculus with its exact product, inverse and chain-rule criteria, the Fueter predicates with their reflection symmetry, and the forms `dq` and [`Dq`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Forms.html#TauCeti.Quaternion.Dq). They rest on a [divergence theorem on affine parallelepipeds](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/DivergenceTheorem/Affine.html#ContinuousLinearEquiv.integral_divergence_affineFluxPullback) built over Mathlib's box version.
