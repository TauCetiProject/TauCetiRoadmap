## QuaternionicAnalysis: 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-09 (`0b27eb0` to `7ca783d`)

The first stretch of quaternionic analysis reaches the Cauchy theorems on boxes. The [box Cauchy theorem](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy.html#TauCeti.left_cauchy_box) says that a function continuous on a closed coordinate box and left Fueter regular inside it has zero boundary flux against the 3-form `Dq`. Regularity is pointwise here, with no continuity of the derivative assumed. The right-handed version and the two-sided [Cauchy theorem](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy.html#TauCeti.cauchy_box) for `g * eᵢ * f` landed with it (TauCeti#31439). The [Cauchy–Fueter kernel](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Kernel.html#TauCeti.cauchyFueterKernel) `(q − q₀)⁻¹/‖q − q₀‖²` is regular on both sides away from its pole, and the boundary sums [vanish for a pole outside the box](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Exterior.html#TauCeti.left_cauchyFueter_box_of_notMem). The cutoff argument for an interior pole has its analytic core: the cutoff density has [mass exactly 2π²](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cutoff.html#Quaternion.integral_cauchyFueterCutoff_density), and integrating against it [recovers 2π² times the value at the pole](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Concentration.html#TauCeti.tendsto_setIntegral_cauchyFueterCutoff_density).

On the rigidity side, [left quotient rigidity](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Rigidity.html#TauCeti.leftQuotient_rigidity) shows that on a connected open set, a function with a left quotient derivative at every point is `q ↦ a + q*b`. Pointwise differentiability is the only hypothesis. The right and two-sided classifications have not landed yet. Fueter-regular functions are [harmonic](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Harmonic.html#TauCeti.LeftFueterOn.laplacian_eq_zero_of_contDiffOn), but only under an assumed `C²` hypothesis, since smoothness is not yet derived.

Underneath sit the matrices and determinants of quaternion multiplication, the quotient calculus with its exact product, inverse and chain-rule criteria, the Fueter predicates with their reflection symmetry, and the forms `dq` and [`Dq`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Forms.html#TauCeti.Quaternion.Dq). They rest on a [divergence theorem on affine parallelepipeds](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Integral/DivergenceTheorem/Affine.html#ContinuousLinearEquiv.integral_divergence_affineFluxPullback) built over Mathlib's box version.

## QuaternionicAnalysis: 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-19 (`7ca783d` to `b235000`)

The Cauchy–Fueter theory now gives its classical consequences. Fueter regular functions on an open set are real analytic: on any ball of radius R where a function is regular, its Taylor series converges to it on the concentric ball of radius (√2 − 1)R/2. So regular functions are smooth, and two regular functions that agree on an open piece of a connected domain agree on all of it. The proof is the one the roadmap planned. The kernel is expanded as a power series away from its pole, the series is integrated term by term over the faces to give a [power series for each Cauchy–Fueter potential](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Analytic.html#TauCeti.hasFPowerSeriesOnBall_integral_cauchyFueterKernel_mul), and the box formula identifies the function with such a potential (TauCeti#27666). As a result, [harmonicity of regular functions](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Harmonic.html#TauCeti.LeftFueterOn.laplacian_eq_zero) no longer assumes a `C²` hypothesis.

The Cauchy–Fueter formula now holds on both sides and on both kinds of domain. The [right box formula](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/CutoffField.html#TauCeti.right_cauchyFueter_box) has `f` to the left of `Dq` and the kernel to its right. On affine parallelepipeds with an interior pole, the [left](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Representation.html#TauCeti.left_cauchyFueter_affine) and [right](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Cauchy/Representation.html#TauCeti.right_cauchyFueter_affine) formulas are stated as actual face integrals of the ordered surface form. They rest on the [raw affine face functional](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/AffineFace.html#ContinuousLinearMap.rawAffineBoundary), its [identification with the cofactor flux](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/AffineFace.html#ContinuousLinearEquiv.rawAffineBoundary_eq_cofactor), and the cofactor-form affine Cauchy theorems. That groundwork, together with the box flux sum and the radial cutoff, landed earlier and is recorded here late.

The kernel also has [derivative bounds of every order that do not depend on the pole](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Quaternion/Kernel.html#TauCeti.exists_norm_iteratedFDeriv_cauchyFueterKernel_le). They are the first ingredient of the universal derivative estimate, which is not yet proved. Along the way the analytic toolkit gained quantitative composition of power series on balls and [term-by-term integration of parameter power series](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/BochnerIntegral.html#HasFPowerSeriesOnBall.integral_bilin).
