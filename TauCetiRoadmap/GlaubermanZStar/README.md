# Roadmap: isolated involutions and Glauberman's Z* theorem

**Summary.** This roadmap proves Glauberman's Z* theorem for finite groups: an involution `t`
that commutes with no conjugate of itself other than `t` is central modulo the largest normal
subgroup of odd order, `t ∈ Z*(G)`. It builds the theory of isolated involutions and their fusion
(weak closure in a Sylow `2`-subgroup, descent to quotients, the dihedral group of two
involutions), the order induction that reduces a counterexample to a core-free group generated by
the conjugates of `t`, the Brauer–Suzuki theorem for generalized quaternion Sylow `2`-subgroups
by ordinary characters on a trivial-intersection set, the constancy of principal-`2`-block
characters on the odd part of an involution section, and the character-value alternative whose
block-section orthogonality contradiction finishes the proof. The end theorems are
`glauberman_zStar`, its Sylow weak-closure form `glauberman_zStar_sylow`, `brauer_suzuki`, and the
principal-block constancy theorem `principalBlock_involution_section_constancy`. Everything is
stated for an arbitrary finite group at the prime two; the `2'`-core and the block theory are
consumed from LocalFiniteGroupTheory and ModularCharactersAndBlocks.

Suggested home: `TauCeti/GroupTheory/ZStar/`, with the principal-block lemmas next to the
block-theory library of ModularCharactersAndBlocks.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns:

- the theory of isolated involutions: the three equivalent forms of isolation (commuting
  conjugates, weak closure of the singleton in a Sylow `2`-subgroup, conjugates inside the
  centralizer), transport under conjugation, isomorphism, restriction and quotients, fusion of
  `t` in overgroups, and the dihedral lemmas for two involutions (Layer 0);
- `Z*(G)` for the prime two, the self-contained existence form of `t ∈ Z*(G)`, their
  equivalence, their lifting and descent, and the reductions of a minimal counterexample
  (Layer 1);
- the **Brauer–Suzuki theorem**: the unique involution of a generalized quaternion Sylow
  `2`-subgroup lies in `Z*(G)`, proved with ordinary characters on a trivial-intersection set,
  and the cyclic-Sylow case from the normal `2`-complement (Layer 2);
- the comparison of the central-character criterion for the principal `2`-block with the block
  of ModularCharactersAndBlocks, and the **constancy theorem**: a principal-block character is
  constant on `s · N` for an involution `s` and an odd-order normal subgroup `N` of `C_G(s)`
  (Layer 3);
- the character-value alternative in a minimal counterexample and the block-section
  orthogonality contradiction (Layer 4);
- **Glauberman's Z* theorem** in both forms, the factorization `G = C_G(t) · O(G)`, and the
  corollaries on simple groups and on principal-block values at isolated involutions (Layer 5).

It leaves to other roadmaps, by section:

- LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1 owns the `2'`-core `O(G) = pPrimeCore 2 ⊤`, its normality,
  characteristic property, behaviour under quotients and `O(G ⧸ O(G)) = 1`; L4 owns the
  classification of `2`-groups of `2`-rank one as cyclic or generalized quaternion; L5 owns
  normal `p`-complements and the cyclic-Sylow form of Burnside's theorem.
- ModularCharactersAndBlocks M1 owns the `2`-modular system over the cyclotomic field and the
  lifting of roots of unity; M2 the comparison between modular systems; M4 the principal block
  `B₀(G)`; M5 generalized decomposition numbers and block-section orthogonality; M7 the second
  main theorem and the local third main theorem; M8 the kernel theorems for the principal block
  and the inflation of ordinary characters along the quotient by a normal subgroup of odd order.
  This roadmap consumes these at `p = 2` and adds no block theory of its own.
- CharacterTheory §1.1 owns class sums and structure constants, §3.5 column orthogonality, §4.2
  the integrality of character values, §4.3 central characters and their integrality, §4.4 the
  Galois action on irreducible characters, §5.1 the multiplicativity of central characters on
  class sums; the pinned kernel criterion `Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff` decides
  `χ(g) = χ(1)`. The character formula for structure constants is derived here (§2.5).
- InductionRestriction Layer 2 owns induced characters and Frobenius reciprocity.
- OddOrderTheorem F2 owns the general Dade isometry and its comparison with ordinary induction
  when every complement `H_a` is trivial, on `TauCeti.IsTISet` data. Layer 2 consumes that
  comparison alone; it does not consume the cyclic-factor coherence theorems of F2 nor anything of
  F0 or F3–F6, and no odd-order hypothesis enters.

Not in scope: isolated elements of odd prime order (the `Z*_p` theorem, which rests on the
classification of finite simple groups), the classification of groups with dihedral or
semidihedral Sylow `2`-subgroups, and the Feit–Thompson theorem. No layer of this roadmap assumes
the odd order theorem or the classification.

## Conventions

- **Groups.** `G` is a finite group, `[Group G] [Finite G]`, with order `Nat.card G`. An
  **involution** is an element with `orderOf t = 2`; the equivalent form `t ≠ 1 ∧ t * t = 1` is
  proved once in Layer 0 and used freely. Conjugation is written `g t g⁻¹`; the commutator is
  Mathlib's `⁅g, t⁆ = g t g⁻¹ t⁻¹`. `t^G` is the conjugacy class of `t`, `C_G(t)` is
  `Subgroup.centralizer {t}`, `Z(G)` is `Subgroup.center G`, and `⟨t^G⟩` is
  `Subgroup.normalClosure {t}`.
- **Isolation.** An involution `t` is **isolated** in `G` when the only conjugate of `t` that
  commutes with `t` is `t` itself:
  `∀ g : G, (g t g⁻¹) t = t (g t g⁻¹) → g t g⁻¹ = t`.
  For `t` in a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S`, `t` is **weakly closed in `S` as a singleton** when
  `∀ g : G, g⁻¹ t g ∈ S → g⁻¹ t g = t`. Layer 0 proves the two equivalent. Neither condition is
  `t ∈ Z(S)`: isolation implies `t ∈ Z(S)`, and the converse fails (Layer 0, Checks).
- **The core and `Z*(G)`.** `O(G)` is the largest normal subgroup of odd order,
  `pPrimeCore 2 ⊤` of LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1 (there `O_{2'} = piCore {q | q ≠ 2}`), and
  `Z*(G)` is the preimage of `Z(G ⧸ O(G))` under the quotient map. The **existence form** of
  `t ∈ Z*(G)` is
  `∃ N : Subgroup G, N.Normal ∧ Odd (Nat.card N) ∧ ∀ g : G, g t g⁻¹ t⁻¹ ∈ N`;
  Layer 1 proves it equivalent to `t ∈ Z*(G)`. The headline theorems conclude with the existence
  form so that `Suggested.lean` depends on no declaration of another roadmap; the odd order and
  normality of `N` are conclusions, never hypotheses.
- **Generalized quaternion groups.** The generalized quaternion group of order `2^n`, `n ≥ 3`,
  is Mathlib's `QuaternionGroup (2^(n-2))`, which has order `4 · 2^(n-2)` (`QuaternionGroup.card`);
  it is generated by `a 1` of order `2^(n-1)` and `xa 0` of order four, with
  `(xa 0)⁻¹ (a 1) (xa 0) = (a 1)⁻¹`. Its unique involution is `a (2^(n-2))`, the generator of its
  center. `n = 3` gives `Q₈ = QuaternionGroup 2`; `QuaternionGroup 1` is cyclic of order four and
  is excluded by `n ≥ 3`.
- **Two involutions.** For involutions `a, b` with `orderOf (a b) = m`, the subgroup `⟨a, b⟩` is
  dihedral of order `2m`, and `a (a b) a = (a b)⁻¹`.
- **Characters.** Characters are complex-valued functions `G → ℂ`; the irreducible ones form
  `TauCeti.irreducibleCharacters ℂ G`. `Cl(g)` is the conjugacy class of `g` and the
  **central character** of an irreducible `χ` on the class sum of `Cl(g)` is
  `ω_χ(g) = |Cl(g)| · χ(g) / χ(1)` (CharacterTheory §4.3), an algebraic integer. Values at
  involutions are real. `B₀(G)` is the principal `2`-block of ModularCharactersAndBlocks M4.
- **Algebraic integers.** `integralClosure ℤ ℂ` is the ring of all algebraic integers; `m` denotes
  a maximal ideal of it containing `2`. Every such `m` satisfies `m ∩ ℤ = 2ℤ`.
- **Universes.** All statements are universe-polymorphic in `G`.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 is pure group theory: isolation, its Sylow form, and the dihedral lemmas, with the
transport statements every later layer calls. Layer 1 fixes the target `Z*(G)`, its existence form,
and the order induction: it reduces a minimal counterexample to a core-free group generated by the
conjugates of `t` with no nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup, in which a Sylow `2`-subgroup either has
a unique involution or contains a second involution commuting with `t`. Layer 2 settles the
unique-involution case by the Brauer–Suzuki theorem, using only ordinary characters, Burnside's
normal complement and the Dade/induction comparison. Layer 3 supplies the block-theoretic input:
the central-character criterion for the principal `2`-block and the constancy theorem on
involution sections. Layer 4 derives the character-value alternative in the reduced counterexample
and the orthogonality contradiction. Layer 5 assembles the theorem and exports its corollaries.
`README.md` is normative; `Suggested.lean` states the four headline theorems against the pinned
APIs with the one definition they need, and nothing else.

## Layer 0: isolated involutions and fusion

### 0.1 Involutions and isolation

For `t : G`, prove `orderOf t = 2 ↔ t ≠ 1 ∧ t * t = 1` (Mathlib `orderOf_eq_prime_iff`), and
that an involution equals its inverse. Define isolation as in the Conventions: `t` is isolated when
every conjugate `g t g⁻¹` commuting with `t` equals `t`. This is the definition of Glauberman
(*Central elements in core-free groups*, 1966, Theorem 1) in the form used by Waldecker (*Isolated
Involutions in Finite Groups*, Chapter 1, p. 1). *Needs:* Mathlib `orderOf_eq_prime_iff`.

- Isolation is invariant under conjugation: if `t` is isolated then so is `g t g⁻¹`, for every `g`.
- Isolation is transported by a group isomorphism `G ≃* G'`.
- Isolation restricts: if `t ∈ H ≤ G` and `t` is isolated in `G`, then `t` is isolated in `H`.
- A central involution is isolated; in an abelian group every involution is isolated.
- If `t` is isolated then `t t^g = t (g t g⁻¹)` has odd order for every `g` (Waldecker, Lemma
  3.1 (3)); the proof is in §0.3.

**Checks.**
- In `C₂` the nonidentity element is isolated: its only conjugate is itself.
- In `S₃` the transposition `(12)` is isolated: its other conjugates `(13)` and `(23)` do not
  commute with it. In `A₅` the involution `(12)(34)` is not isolated: `(13)(24)` is conjugate to
  it (conjugation by `(234)` sends `(12)(34)` to `(13)(24)`) and commutes with it.
- In the dihedral group `D₈ = ⟨r, s⟩` of order eight the reflection `s` is not isolated:
  `r s r⁻¹ = s r²` commutes with `s` and differs from it; the rotation `r²` is central, hence
  isolated.
- In `Q₈` the element `−1` is central, hence isolated; in the Klein four-group every involution is
  isolated.
- The wrong model "`t` lies in the center of some Sylow `2`-subgroup" is not isolation: in `S₄`
  the involution `(12)(34)` is central in the Sylow `2`-subgroup `⟨(1324), (12)⟩`, yet
  `(13)(24)` is conjugate to it and commutes with it.

### 0.2 Transport and descent

Prove the following, with `t` an isolated involution of `G`.

- **Fusion in overgroups** (Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (4)): if `t ∈ H ≤ G`, then
  `t^G ∩ H = t^H`; every `G`-conjugate of `t` lying in `H` is an `H`-conjugate. Route: for
  `x = g t g⁻¹ ∈ H`, `t x` has odd order (§0.1), so `t` and `x` are conjugate in the dihedral
  group `⟨t, x⟩ ≤ H` (§0.3).
- **Descent to quotients** (Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (7)): if `N` is a normal subgroup with `t ∉ N`,
  then the image `t̄` of `t` in `G ⧸ N` is an isolated involution, and `C_{G/N}(t̄)` is the image
  of `C_G(t)`. Route: a conjugate `x̄ = ḡ t̄ ḡ⁻¹` commuting with `t̄` lifts to `x = g t g⁻¹` with
  `⁅x, t⁆ ∈ N`; the subgroup `M = ⟨x, t⟩ N` has `M ⧸ N` generated by two commuting involutions
  of `M ⧸ N` so `M = S N` for a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S` of `M` containing `t`; `x` lies in a
  conjugate `n S n⁻¹` with `n ∈ N`, so `n⁻¹ x n ∈ S` is a `G`-conjugate of `t` in `S`, hence
  equals `t` by weak closure (§0.4), and `x̄ = t̄`.
- **Lifting through an odd normal subgroup**: if `N` is normal of odd order, then `t` is isolated
  in `G` if and only if `t̄` is isolated in `G ⧸ N`. Route: descent for one direction; for the
  other, if `x = g t g⁻¹` commutes with `t` then `x̄ = t̄`, so `x ∈ t N`, and `x t ∈ N` is an
  involution or trivial in a group of odd order, hence `x = t`.
- **Product conjugacy**: if `a, b ∈ C_G(t)` are conjugate in `G`, then `t a` and `t b` are
  conjugate in `G` (a consequence of fusion in overgroups, Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (4)). Route: with `b = g a g⁻¹`, both
  `t` and `g t g⁻¹` lie in `C_G(b)`; by fusion in the overgroup `C_G(b)` there is `c ∈ C_G(b)`
  with `c g t g⁻¹ c⁻¹ = t`, and then `(c g) (t a) (c g)⁻¹ = t b`.

*Needs:* §0.1, §0.3, §0.4; Mathlib `QuotientGroup.mk'`, `Subgroup.comap`, Sylow conjugacy.

**Checks.**
- Fusion in overgroups fails without isolation: in `S₄` with `t = (12)` and `H = ⟨(12), (34)⟩`,
  the conjugate `(34)` lies in `H` but is not an `H`-conjugate of `(12)`.
- Descent needs `t ∉ N`: for `G = C₂` and `N = G` the image of `t` is trivial, not an involution.
- Product conjugacy fails without isolation: in `S₄`, `t = (12)`, `a = (34)` and `b = (12)` are
  conjugate elements of `C_G(t)`, but `t a = (12)(34)` and `t b = 1` are not conjugate.
- Product conjugacy witness: in `S₃ × C₂` with `t = (1, c)` central, `a = ((12), 1)` and
  `b = ((13), 1)`, the products `t a` and `t b` are conjugate by `((23), 1)`.

### 0.3 Two involutions and their dihedral group

For involutions `a, b` with `orderOf (a b) = m`, prove that `⟨a, b⟩` is dihedral of order `2m`:
`a (a b) a = (a b)⁻¹`, every element is `(a b)^i` or `a (a b)^i`, and the conjugation formula
`(a b)^(-j) a (a b)^j = a (a b)^(2j)` for every integer `j`. Prove:

- if `m` is odd, `a` and `b` are conjugate in `⟨a, b⟩` (Waldecker, proof of Lemma 3.1 (4)): the
  Sylow `2`-subgroups of `⟨a, b⟩` have order two and are conjugate;
- if `m = 2k`, then `w = (a b)^k` is an involution central in `⟨a, b⟩`, commuting with `a` and
  `b`;
- if `m = 2k` with `k` even, then `a (a b)^k = (a b)^(-k/2) a (a b)^(k/2)` is a conjugate of
  `a`, distinct from `a`, commuting with `a`; hence `a` is not isolated;
- if `m = 2k` with `k` odd, then `a (a b)^k = (a b)^(-(k-1)/2) b (a b)^((k-1)/2)` is a conjugate
  of `b` in `⟨a, b⟩` (from `b = a (a b)` and the conjugation formula), distinct from `a` and
  commuting with `a`; hence if `b` is conjugate to `a` in `G`, then `a` is not isolated;
- consequently, if `t` is isolated and `w` is an involution conjugate to `t`, then
  `orderOf (t w)` is odd (Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (3), stated there without proof): an even order
  `2k` falls under one of the two preceding bullets, and each contradicts isolation; and if `w`
  is an involution not conjugate to `t`, then `orderOf (t w) = 2k` with `k` odd (Lemma 3.1 (5)):
  the order is even by the first bullet and `k` is odd by the `k` even bullet.

*Needs:* Mathlib `orderOf`, `zpow` arithmetic, `Sylow` conjugacy in `⟨a, b⟩`.

**Checks.**
- `D₈`, `a = s`, `b = s r`: `a b = r` has order `4`, `k = 2`, and
  `r⁻¹ s r = s r² = a (a b)²`, a conjugate of `s` distinct from `s` and commuting with it.
- `S₃`, `a = (12)`, `b = (13)`: `a b = (132)` has order three and `(23) (12) (23) = (13)`.
- `V₄`, `a ≠ b`: `a b` has order two, `k = 1`, `w = a b` central, and the odd-`k` formula gives
  `a (a b)^1 = b`, conjugate to `b` and not to `a` in `V₄`; the odd-`k` bullet claims nothing
  unless `b` is conjugate to `a` in the ambient group.
- `GL(2, 3)`, `a = diag(1, −1)`, `r = −(1 1; 0 1)` of order six, `b = a r = (−1 −1; 0 1)`: `a` and
  `b` are involutions of determinant `−1`, hence conjugate in `GL(2, 3)`, `a b = r`, `k = 3`, and
  `a (a b)³ = a r³ = −a = diag(−1, 1)` commutes with `a`, differs from `a`, and is conjugate to
  `a`: `a` is not isolated in `GL(2, 3)`, consistently with `Z*(GL(2, 3)) = Z(GL(2, 3)) = {±1}`.
  In the dihedral group `⟨a, b⟩` alone, `a (a b)³` is conjugate to `b` and not to `a`.
- The wrong formula `(a b)^(-j) a (a b)^j = a (a b)^j` fails in `D₈` at `j = 1`:
  `r⁻¹ s r = s r²`, not `s r`.

### 0.4 Isolation and Sylow subgroups

Let `t` be an involution in a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S`. Prove the equivalence of
(i) `t` is weakly closed in `S` as a singleton, `t^G ∩ S = {t}`;
(ii) `t` is isolated, `t^G ∩ C_G(t) = {t}`
(Guralnick–Tong-Viet–Tracey, *Weakly subnormal subgroups and variations of the Baer–Suzuki
theorem*, Theorem 5.1 (i) ⇔ (ii), at `p = 2`; Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (1)–(2)). Route for
(i) ⇒ (ii): `t^S ⊆ t^G ∩ S = {t}` gives `t ∈ Z(S)`, so `S ≤ C_G(t)`; a conjugate `x` commuting
with `t` generates with `t` a `2`-subgroup of `C_G(t)`, contained in `h S h⁻¹` for some
`h ∈ C_G(t)`, so `h⁻¹ x h ∈ t^G ∩ S = {t}` and `x = t`. Route for (ii) ⇒ (i): a conjugate
`x = g t g⁻¹ ∈ S` has `t x` of odd order (§0.3), but `t x ∈ S` is a `2`-element, so `x = t`.
Derive: an isolated involution is central in every Sylow `2`-subgroup containing it, `C_G(t)`
contains a Sylow `2`-subgroup of `G`, and every `2`-subgroup normalized by `t` is centralized by
`t` (Waldecker, Lemma 3.1 (2)). *Needs:* §0.1, §0.3; Mathlib `Sylow`, `IsPGroup`.

**Checks.**

## Layer 1: `Z*(G)`, the existence form, and the order induction

### 1.1 `Z*(G)` and the existence form

Define `Z*(G)` as the preimage of `Z(G ⧸ O(G))` under the quotient map, with `O(G)` the
`2'`-core of LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1. For an involution `t` prove the equivalence of
(a) `t ∈ Z*(G)`;
(b) the existence form: some normal subgroup `N` of odd order contains every commutator
`g t g⁻¹ t⁻¹`;
(c) `G = C_G(t) · O(G)` as sets (every element is a product);
(d) every commutator `g t g⁻¹ t⁻¹` lies in `O(G)`
(Guralnick–Tong-Viet–Tracey, Theorem 5.1 (vi) ⇔ (vii) and (vii) ⇒ (iv), at `p = 2`). Route:
(a) ⇔ (d) is the definition of the center of the quotient; (d) ⇒ (b) with `N = O(G)`; (b) ⇒ (d)
since `N ≤ O(G)` by maximality of the core; (d) ⇒ (c): for `g ∈ G` the conjugate `g t g⁻¹` lies
in `t · O(G)`, so `t` and `g t g⁻¹` are involutions of `O(G) ⋊ ⟨t⟩`, whose Sylow `2`-subgroups
have order two, hence are conjugate by some `n ∈ O(G)`, and then `n⁻¹ g ∈ C_G(t)` (Theorem 5.1 states
(vi) ⇔ (vii) and proves (vi) ⇒ (vii) through a lifting lemma for centralizers of central
`p`-subgroups; the Sylow argument is the route taken here); (c) ⇒ (d): for `n ∈ O(G)` and `c ∈ C_G(t)`,
`⁅n c, t⁆ = n (c t c⁻¹) n⁻¹ t⁻¹ = ⁅n, t⁆`, which lies in `O(G)` by normality.
Prove that the subgroup generated by the commutators `⁅g, t⁆` is the normal subgroup `[G, ⟨t⟩]`
and that `⟨t^G⟩ = [G, ⟨t⟩] · ⟨t⟩`. *Needs:* LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1 (`pPrimeCore 2 ⊤`, its maximality and
`O(G ⧸ O(G)) = 1`); Mathlib `Subgroup.center`, `QuotientGroup.mk'`.

**Checks.**
- `C₂`: `O = 1`, `Z* = C₂`; the existence form holds with `N = ⊥` and every commutator trivial.
- `S₃`, `t = (12)`: `O(S₃) = A₃`, `S₃ ⧸ A₃` is abelian, so `Z*(S₃) = S₃`; the existence form
  holds with `N = A₃`, every commutator `⁅g, (12)⁆` being a `3`-cycle or trivial.
- `A₅`, `t = (12)(34)`: `O(A₅) = 1`, `Z*(A₅) = 1`; `⁅(123), t⁆ ≠ 1`, so the existence form fails:
  the only normal subgroup of odd order is trivial.
- `D₈`: `O = 1`, `Z* = Z = ⟨r²⟩`; `s ∉ Z*` and `⁅r, s⁆ = r²` is a nontrivial `2`-element.
- The Frobenius group `F₂₀ = C₅ ⋊ C₄` with `t = c²` the involution of `C₄`: `t` inverts `C₅`, so
  `t ∉ Z(G)`, but `O(G) = C₅` and `G ⧸ C₅ ≅ C₄` is abelian, so `t ∈ Z*(G)`, with `N = C₅`.
- The wrong model "`N` of even order allowed" would make `N = G` always work; the odd-order
  conclusion is what carries content.

### 1.2 Lifting and descent of the conclusion

Prove, for an involution `t`:

- if `N` is normal of odd order and the image of `t` lies in `Z*(G ⧸ N)`, then `t ∈ Z*(G)`
  (the preimage of an odd-order normal subgroup of `G ⧸ N` is normal of odd order in `G`);
- if `t ∈ Z*(G)` and `N` is normal, then the image of `t` lies in `Z*(G ⧸ N)` (the image of an
  odd-order normal subgroup is one);
- if `t ∈ Z*(G)` and `t ∈ H ≤ G`, then `t ∈ Z*(H)` (`O(G) ⊓ H` is normal of odd order in `H`);
- if `t ∈ Z*(H)` for a normal subgroup `H` containing `t^G`, then `t ∈ Z*(G)`: `O(H)` is
  characteristic in `H`, hence normal of odd order in `G`, and in `Ḡ = G ⧸ O(H)` the image `t̄`
  lies in `Z(H̄)`, a characteristic subgroup of the normal subgroup `H̄` of `Ḡ`, hence normal in
  `Ḡ`; so every conjugate of `t̄` lies in `Z(H̄)` and commutes with `t̄`, and isolation of `t̄`
  (descent, §0.2) forces it to equal `t̄`, so `t̄` is central and `⁅G, t⁆ ≤ O(H)`. This item
  assumes `t` isolated.

*Needs:* §0.2; LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1 (`O(N) = O(G) ⊓ N`, characteristic property); Mathlib
`Subgroup.map`, `Subgroup.comap`, `Subgroup.card_mul_index`.

**Checks.**
- `S₃ × C₂`, `t = ((12), 1)`, `N = A₃ × 1`: `t̄` is central in the quotient `C₂ × C₂`, and `t`
  lies in `Z*(S₃ × C₂) = S₃ × C₂`.
- Lifting needs `N` of odd order: for `G = D₈`, `N = ⟨r²⟩` and `t = s`, the image of `s` is
  central in `D₈ ⧸ N ≅ V₄`, but `s ∉ Z*(D₈) = ⟨r²⟩`.
- The last item needs isolation: in `S₄` with `t = (12)(34)` and `H = V₄`, the normal subgroup
  `H` contains `t^G` and `t ∈ Z*(V₄) = V₄`, but `t ∉ Z*(S₄) = 1`; `t` is not isolated in `S₄`.

### 1.3 The minimal counterexample

Set up well-founded induction on `Nat.card G`, with strict inequalities for proper subgroups and
for quotients by nontrivial normal subgroups. A **minimal counterexample** is a finite group `G`
with an isolated involution `t ∉ Z*(G)` such that every proper subgroup and every proper quotient
satisfies the Z* theorem for all its isolated involutions (Waldecker, Hypothesis 4.1). Prove, for
a minimal counterexample (Waldecker, Lemma 4.4 (1)–(2)):

1. `O(G) = 1`: otherwise the image of `t` is isolated in `G ⧸ O(G)` (§0.2), lies in
   `Z*(G ⧸ O(G))` by minimality, and lifts (§1.2).
2. `⟨t^G⟩ = G`: otherwise `H = ⟨t^G⟩` is a proper normal subgroup with `t ∈ Z*(H)` by minimality,
   and §1.2 gives `t ∈ Z*(G)`. Consequently `G ⧸ [G, G]` is generated by the image of `t`, so
   `[G, G] = G` or `|G : [G, G]| = 2`.
3. `t` is not central, and no nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup exists: if `N ⊴ G` is a nontrivial
   `2`-group, `t` centralizes `N` (§0.4), hence so do all conjugates of `t`, so `N ≤ Z(G)` by 2.
   Pick an involution `w ∈ N`; then `t ∉ ⟨w⟩`, the image of `t` in `G ⧸ ⟨w⟩` is isolated (§0.2)
   and lies in `Z*(G ⧸ ⟨w⟩)` by minimality. The preimage `X` of `O(G ⧸ ⟨w⟩)` has the central
   subgroup `⟨w⟩` as a Sylow `2`-subgroup, so `X = ⟨w⟩ × O(X)` by Burnside's normal complement
   (L5), and `O(X) ≤ O(G) = 1`; hence `O(G ⧸ ⟨w⟩) = 1` and `⁅G, t⁆ ≤ ⟨w⟩ ≤ Z(G)`. Every
   conjugate of `t` then commutes with `t`, so `t` is central by isolation, a contradiction.
4. For a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S` containing `t`: either `t` is the unique involution of `S`, or
   `S` contains an involution `s ≠ t`; in the second case `s` commutes with `t` (`t ∈ Z(S)`) and
   is not conjugate to `t` (isolation).
5. Every proper subgroup `H` containing `t` satisfies `t ∈ Z*(H)`, so `⁅H, t⁆ ≤ O(H)`
   (Waldecker, Lemma 4.2 (1)–(2)).

*Needs:* §0.2, §0.4, §1.1, §1.2; LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1 and L5; Mathlib
`Subgroup.normalClosure`, `WellFounded` induction on `Nat.card`.

**Checks.**
- No group of order at most `7` is a counterexample: each is abelian or `S₃`, where `Z*` is
  everything.
- `A₅` is not a counterexample although `Z*(A₅) = 1`: it has no isolated involution.
- Reduction 3 at work: `O(S₄) = 1` and `V₄` is a nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup of `S₄`, so `S₄`
  is not a minimal counterexample for any involution; consistently, `S₄` has no isolated
  involution (`(12)` commutes with its conjugate `(34)`, and `(12)(34)` with `(13)(24)`).
- Reduction 2's dichotomy on `[G, G]`: for `G = S₃` with `t = (12)`, `⟨t^G⟩ = S₃` and
  `|S₃ : A₃| = 2`; for a perfect group generated by the conjugates of an involution,
  `[G, G] = G`.

### Examples

`S₃`, `F₂₀`, `D₈` and `A₅` as above; `S₃ × C₂` for lifting and descent.

## Layer 2: the Brauer–Suzuki theorem

The theorem (Brauer–Suzuki, *On finite groups of even order whose 2-Sylow group is a quaternion
group*, 1959, p. 1757): if a Sylow `2`-subgroup of `G` is generalized quaternion, then the
image of its unique involution is central in `G ⧸ O(G)`; in particular `G` is not nonabelian
simple. The proof here follows the ordinary-character argument of Suzuki (*Group Theory II*,
Chapter VI, §2.2, Example 3) on a trivial-intersection set, through the Dade/induction comparison
of OddOrderTheorem F2. No block theory and no odd-order theorem enters this layer
(ModularCharactersAndBlocks is consumed here only for the ordinary-character inflation law of
M8), and Layer 5 is not imported here.

### 2.1 Generalized quaternion groups

With the convention of this roadmap, write `Q = QuaternionGroup (2^(n-2))`, `n ≥ 3`. Prove from
Mathlib's presentation: `Nat.card Q = 2^n`; `a 1` has order `2^(n-1)` and `X = ⟨a 1⟩` is cyclic of
index two; every `xa i` has order four; `z = a (2^(n-2))` is the unique involution and
`Z(Q) = ⟨z⟩`; the unique subgroup `U` of order four of `X` is `⟨a (2^(n-3))⟩`, it is normal in
`Q`, `C_Q(U) = X`, and `Q ⧸ X` acts on `U` by inversion; the commutator subgroup `[Q, Q]` is
`⟨a 2⟩`, of index four, since `(xa 0)⁻¹ (a 1) (xa 0) (a 1)⁻¹ = (a 1)⁻²` and `Q ⧸ ⟨a 2⟩` is generated
by two commuting involutions. For `n ≥ 4` the subgroup `X` is the
unique cyclic subgroup of index two; for `n = 3` there are three, `⟨a 1⟩`, `⟨xa 0⟩`, `⟨xa 1⟩`,
and any of them serves as `X`. Transport these statements along an isomorphism
`S ≃* Q` to a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S`. *Needs:* Mathlib `QuaternionGroup.card`,
`QuaternionGroup.orderOf_a`, `QuaternionGroup.orderOf_xa`, `MulEquiv`.

**Checks.**
- `n = 3`: `QuaternionGroup 2` has `4 · 2 = 8` elements, its involution is `a 2`, and
  `xa 0`, `xa 1`, `a 1` all have order four.
- `n = 4`: `QuaternionGroup 4` has `16` elements, involution `a 4`, `U = ⟨a 2⟩`, and `⟨a 1⟩` is
  its only cyclic subgroup of index two since every `xa i` has order `4 < 8`; `[Q, Q] = ⟨a 2⟩`
  has order four and index four, and for `n = 3` it is `⟨a 2⟩ = Z(Q₈)` of order two.
- `QuaternionGroup 1` has order four and contains `xa 0` of order four, so it is cyclic; the
  hypothesis `n ≥ 3` excludes it, and `n - 2` is never the natural-number junk value `0` here.
- The wrong model `DihedralGroup (2^(n-1))` has the same order and a cyclic subgroup of index
  two but `2^(n-1) + 1` involutions, so "unique involution" discriminates it.

### 2.2 Cyclic Sylow subgroups and the rank-one case

If a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S` of `G` is cyclic, then `G` has a normal `2`-complement, which is
`O(G)` (LocalFiniteGroupTheory L5, from Mathlib `MonoidHom.ker_transferSylow_isComplement'`), so
`G ⧸ O(G) ≅ S` is abelian and the involution of `S` lies in `Z*(G)` (Waldecker, Theorem 2.19).
Combine with LocalFiniteGroupTheory L4: if a Sylow `2`-subgroup has a unique involution then it is
cyclic or generalized quaternion, so the cyclic case here and §2.4 together give: the unique
involution of a Sylow `2`-subgroup of `2`-rank one lies in `Z*(G)`. *Needs:* LocalFiniteGroupTheory
L4, L5; Mathlib `MonoidHom.transferSylow`.

**Checks.**
- `F₂₀ = C₅ ⋊ C₄`: Sylow `C₄`, complement `C₅ = O(G)`, involution in `Z*`, not in `Z`.
- `C₆`: Sylow `C₂`, `O = C₃`, `Z* = C₆`.
- A nonabelian simple group has no cyclic Sylow `2`-subgroup: `A₅` has Sylow `V₄`, consistently.

### 2.3 The trivial-intersection configuration

Let `S` be a generalized quaternion Sylow `2`-subgroup of `G` of order `2^n`, `n ≥ 3`, with
`X`, `U`, `z` as in §2.1 transported to `S`. Put `C = C_G(U)`, `H = N_G(U)` and
`K = {k ∈ C | 4 ∣ orderOf k}`. Prove:

- `S ≤ H`, `C ⊴ H`, `S ⊄ C`, and `|H : C| = 2`: `H ⧸ C` embeds in `Aut(U) ≅ C₂` and `S`
  inverts `U`.
- `C_S(U) = X` is a Sylow `2`-subgroup of `C`: a Sylow `2`-subgroup `T` of `C` containing `X`
  lies in some conjugate `S'` of `S`, so `T = X` or `T = S'`; the latter would put `U` in
  `Z(S')`, of order two.
- `C` has a normal `2`-complement, `C = O(C) ⋊ X` (§2.2), and `H = O(C) ⋊ S` with `H ⧸ O(C) ≅ S`.
- Every subgroup of order four of a cyclic Sylow `2`-subgroup of `C` is `U`; hence for `k ∈ C`
  with `4 ∣ orderOf k`, the `2`-part `k₂` of `k` generates a cyclic group containing `U`, and
  `C_G(k) ≤ C_G(k₂) ≤ C_G(U) = C ≤ H`.
- `K` is a trivial-intersection set for `H`, `TauCeti.IsTISet K H`, and `N_G(K) = H`: if
  `k ∈ K ∩ g K g⁻¹` then `⟨k₂⟩` contains both `U` and `g U g⁻¹`, so `U = g U g⁻¹` and `g ∈ H`.
  The claim is about `K`, not about `U`: distinct conjugates of `U` may share the involution `z`.
- `K` is the preimage in `C` of the elements of order at least four of `C ⧸ O(C) ≅ X`, a union of
  cosets of `O(C)`; `G`-fusion in `K` is controlled by `H`.
- All involutions of `G` are conjugate to `z`, each lying in a conjugate of `S`; and a product of
  two involutions of `G` is trivial or of odd order: if `x y` has even order `2k` for involutions
  `x ≠ y`, then `(x y)^k` is an involution commuting with `x` and distinct from `x` (§0.3), so
  `⟨x, (x y)^k⟩` is a Klein four-group, which no `2`-subgroup of `G` contains, every nontrivial
  `2`-subgroup of `G` lying in a conjugate of `S` and having one involution. In particular no
  element of `K`, whose orders are divisible by four, is a product of two involutions.

Hence the Dade data of OddOrderTheorem F2 hold with `L = H`, `A = K` and every `H_a = 1`.
*Needs:* §0.3, §2.1, §2.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsTISet`; Mathlib Sylow conjugacy,
`Subgroup.centralizer`, `Subgroup.normalizer`.

**Checks.**
- `G = Q₈`, `X = ⟨i⟩ = U`: `C = X`, `H = G`, `K = {i, −i}`, `|H : C| = 2`, and `K` is trivially
  trivial-intersection.
- `G = SL(2, 3)`, `U = ⟨i⟩`: `C_G(i) = ⟨i⟩` (the class of `i` has six elements), so `C = U`,
  `H = Q₈`, `K = {i, −i}`, `O(C) = 1`; the three conjugates of `U` all contain `−1`, which is why
  the trivial-intersection claim is made for `K` and not for `U`.
- `G = 𝔽₃² ⋊ Q₈` (the action of `Q₈ ≤ SL(2, 3)` on `𝔽₃²`): `C = C_G(⟨i⟩) = ⟨i⟩` since `i − 1` is
  invertible on `𝔽₃²`, and `K = {i, −i}` again; `O(G) = 𝔽₃²`, and `−1` acts as `−1` on `𝔽₃²`, so
  `−1 ∉ Z(G)` while `−1 ∈ Z*(G)`.
- The wrong model `K' = C ∖ {1}` is not a trivial-intersection set for `H`: in `SL(2, 3)` with
  `C = ⟨i⟩`, an element `g` of order three has `g C g⁻¹ = ⟨j⟩`, and `{i, −1, −i}` meets
  `{j, −1, −j}` in `−1`; the definition of `K` excludes the involution.
- Products of two involutions: in `Q₈` and `SL(2, 3)` the only involution is `z` and `z · z = 1`;
  in `𝔽₃² ⋊ Q₈` the involutions are the `(v, −1)` with `v ∈ 𝔽₃²`, and
  `(v, −1) (w, −1) = (v − w, 1)` has order one or three. The wrong model `D₈` has `s · s r = r`
  of order four, a product of two involutions; `D₈` contains the Klein four-group `⟨s, r²⟩`.

### 2.4 Characters supported on `K` and the induction isometry

Let the notation be as in §2.3 with `m = n − 1`, so `|X| = 2^m`, and identify the irreducible
characters of `H ⧸ O(C) ≅ S` (inflated to `H` along `H → H ⧸ O(C)`, ModularCharactersAndBlocks
M8): four linear
characters, among them `ε_X` with kernel `X`, and `2^(m-1) − 1` characters `χ_j` of degree two,
`1 ≤ j ≤ 2^(m-1) − 1`, with `χ_j(a i) = ζ^(ij) + ζ^(-ij)` for a primitive `2^m`-th root of unity
`ζ` and `χ_j(xa i) = 0`. Prove:

- a class function of `S` vanishes on `{1, z} ∪ (S ∖ X)` exactly when its inflation to `H`
  vanishes off `K`; the space of such class functions has dimension `2^(m-1) − 1`, the number
  of `S`-classes `{a i, a (−i)}` of elements of `X` of order at least four;
- for `m ≥ 3`: `α_{j,j'} = χ_j − χ_{j'}` with `j ≠ j'`, `j ≡ j' (mod 2)`, and `β_j = 1_S + ε_X − χ_j` with
  `j` even vanish on `{1, z} ∪ (S ∖ X)`, with norms `‖α_{j,j'}‖² = 2` and `‖β_j‖² = 3`;
- for `m = 2` (`S = Q₈`): the functions vanishing on `{1, z} ∪ (S ∖ X)` are the multiples of
  `f = 1_S + ε_X − ε₂ − ε₃`, where `ε₂, ε₃` are the two linear characters with kernels the other
  cyclic subgroups of index two; `‖f‖² = 4` and `f` takes the value `4` at the two elements of
  order four of `X`;
- for the inflations of these functions to `H`, ordinary induction to `G` is an isometry
  (OddOrderTheorem F2 with every `H_a = 1`), and Frobenius reciprocity (InductionRestriction
  Layer 2) gives `⟨Ind β_j, 1_G⟩ = 1` and `⟨Ind f, 1_G⟩ = 1`;
- the induced functions are supported on the conjugates of `K` and agree with the inflated
  functions on `K` (every `H_a = 1`); in particular they vanish at `z` and at `1`.

Then prove the signed decompositions: `Ind α_{j,j'}` is a difference of two distinct irreducible
characters, `Ind β_j = 1_G ± ψ₁ ± ψ₂` and `Ind f = 1_G ± ψ₁ ± ψ₂ ± ψ₃` with distinct nontrivial
irreducibles, and the degree and value constraints these impose: the degrees satisfy
`1 ± ψ₁(1) ± ψ₂(1) (± ψ₃(1)) = 0`, the values at `z` the same relation, and the values on `K`
the relations read off from `β_j` and `f`. Use the Galois action of CharacterTheory §4.4 to move
between the `χ_j` of a fixed parity and to compare the constituents of different `α_{j,j'}`.
*Needs:* §2.3; OddOrderTheorem F2; InductionRestriction Layer 2; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M8;
CharacterTheory §4.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ClassFunction.characterPairing`.

**Checks.**
- `S = Q₈`, `X = ⟨i⟩`: `f(1) = 1 + 1 − 1 − 1 = 0`, `f(−1) = 0`, `f(i) = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4`,
  `f(j) = 1 − 1 + 1 − 1 = 0`, `f(k) = 1 − 1 − 1 + 1 = 0`; `‖f‖² = 4`.
- `S = Q₁₆`, `m = 3`, `X = ⟨a 1⟩` of order eight: `β_2 = 1 + ε_X − χ_2` has `β_2(1) = 0`,
  `β_2(z) = 1 + 1 − 2 = 0` since `χ_2(a 4) = 2`, `β_2(xa i) = 1 − 1 − 0 = 0`, and
  `β_2(a 1) = 2 − (ζ² + ζ⁻²) = 2`; the wrong choice `β_1` has `β_1(z) = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4 ≠ 0`.
- `G = SL(2, 3)`, `H = Q₈`: `Ind f = 1_G + λ + λ̄ − ψ₃`, with `λ, λ̄` the nontrivial linear
  characters and `ψ₃` the character of degree three; check at `1`: `1 + 1 + 1 − 3 = 0`; at `−1`:
  `1 + 1 + 1 − 3 = 0`; at an element of order three: `1 + ω + ω² − 0 = 0`; at `i`:
  `1 + 1 + 1 − (−1) = 4 = f(i)`.
- The wrong model "`Ind` is an isometry on all class functions of `H`" fails: `‖Ind 1_H‖²` is
  the number of `(H, H)`-double cosets, which is `3` for the normal subgroup `H = Q₈` of

### 2.5 The contradiction and the theorem

Write `P(G)` for the statement "if a Sylow `2`-subgroup of `G` has a unique involution `z`, then
the existence form of `z ∈ Z*(G)` holds". §2.2 proves `P(G)` when that Sylow subgroup is cyclic.
Prove `P(G)` for generalized quaternion Sylow `2`-subgroups by induction on `Nat.card G`, through
the targets below (Suzuki, *Group Theory II*, Chapter VI, §2.2, Example 3; the targets follow the
stages of the formalization of that example cited in References). Throughout, `G` is a
counterexample of least order, with `S`, `X`, `U = ⟨u⟩`, `z = u²`, `C`, `H`, `K` as in §2.3.

**Reduction to a simple group.** Prove:

- `O(G) = 1`: otherwise `S O(G) ⧸ O(G) ≅ S` is a generalized quaternion Sylow `2`-subgroup of the
  smaller group `G ⧸ O(G)`, so the image of `z` lies in `Z*(G ⧸ O(G))` and `z ∈ Z*(G)` by §1.2.
  Hence `Z*(G) = Z(G)` and `z ∉ Z(G)`.
- Every nontrivial normal subgroup `M` of `G` contains `z`: `S ⊓ M` is a Sylow `2`-subgroup of
  `M`, and a nontrivial subgroup of `S` contains its unique involution `z`; if `z ∉ M`, then `|M|`
  is odd and `M ≤ O(G) = 1`.
- `G` is nonabelian simple: for a proper nontrivial normal `M`, the Sylow `2`-subgroup `S ⊓ M` of
  `M` has the unique involution `z`, so it is cyclic or generalized quaternion
  (LocalFiniteGroupTheory L4); by §2.2, or by `P(M)` for the smaller group `M`, `z ∈ Z*(M)`, and
  `O(M) ≤ O(G) = 1` (L1), so `z ∈ Z(M)`. The Sylow `2`-subgroup `P` of the abelian characteristic
  subgroup `Z(M)` of `M` is a nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup of `G`, hence `P ≤ S`
  (`IsPGroup.le_sylow_of_normal`), and `z` is the unique involution of `P`; conjugation by any
  `g ∈ G` preserves `P` and so fixes `z`, against `z ∉ Z(G)`.
- Consequently every nontrivial irreducible character `ψ` of `G` is faithful, so `ψ(z) ≠ ψ(1)`
  (`Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff`, the kernel being normal) and `ψ(1) ≥ 2`.

**The vanishing class function.** Prove:

- the character formula for structure constants: for conjugacy classes `C₁ ∋ g₁`, `C₂ ∋ g₂` and
  `g ∈ G`, the number of pairs `(x, y) ∈ C₁ × C₂` with `x y = g` is
  `(|C₁| |C₂| / |G|) ∑_χ χ(g₁) χ(g₂) conj(χ(g)) / χ(1)`, the sum over the irreducible characters:
  multiply `ω_χ(K₁) ω_χ(K₂) = ∑ₖ a₁₂ₖ ω_χ(Kₖ)` (CharacterTheory §5.1) by `χ(1) conj(χ(g))`, sum
  over `χ`, and apply `ω_χ(K_j) = |C_j| χ(g_j) / χ(1)` (§4.3) and column orthogonality (§3.5);
- hence `Φ = ∑_χ (χ(z)² / χ(1)) χ` vanishes at every element of even order (the formula with
  `C₁ = C₂ = z^G`, the class of all involutions, `χ(z)` being real, and §2.3: no element of even
  order is a product of two involutions), and for every class function `ζ` supported on elements
  of even order, `∑_χ (χ(z)² / χ(1)) ⟨χ, ζ⟩ = ⟨Φ, ζ⟩ = 0`.

**The three relations.** Let `ζ = Ind β_j` (`n ≥ 4`, `j` even) or `ζ = Ind f` (`n = 3`), with
`ζ = 1_G + ∑ᵢ εᵢ ψᵢ` as in §2.4, and put `dᵢ = ψᵢ(1)`, `eᵢ = ψᵢ(z)`, `fᵢ = dᵢ − eᵢ`. Prove:

- `1 + ∑ᵢ εᵢ dᵢ = 0` (`ζ(1) = 0`) and `1 + ∑ᵢ εᵢ eᵢ = 0` (`ζ(z) = 0`, as `z` is conjugate to no
  element of `K`); hence `∑ᵢ εᵢ fᵢ = 0`;
- `1 + ∑ᵢ εᵢ eᵢ² / dᵢ = 0` (`ζ` is supported on the conjugates of `K`); with
  `eᵢ² / dᵢ = dᵢ − 2 fᵢ + fᵢ² / dᵢ` and the two preceding relations, `∑ᵢ εᵢ fᵢ² / dᵢ = 0`;
- `fᵢ > 0` and `4 ∣ fᵢ`: `eᵢ ≠ dᵢ` by faithfulness, `|eᵢ| ≤ dᵢ` since `ψᵢ(z)` is a sum of `dᵢ`
  signs, and in a representation affording `ψᵢ` the `−1`-eigenspace of `z = u²` is the sum of the
  `i`- and `(−i)`-eigenspaces of `u`, which have equal dimension because `u` is conjugate to
  `u⁻¹` in `S` (§2.1); so `fᵢ` is twice an even number. Write `fᵢ = 4 cᵢ` with `cᵢ ≥ 1`.

**The case `n ≥ 4`.** With `ζ = Ind β₂ = 1_G + ε₁ ψ₁ + ε₂ ψ₂`: `ε₁ f₁ + ε₂ f₂ = 0` with
`f₁, f₂ > 0` forces `ε₂ = −ε₁` and `f₁ = f₂`; then `ε₁ f₁² (1/d₁ − 1/d₂) = 0` gives `d₁ = d₂`,
and the degree relation reads `1 + ε₁ (d₁ − d₂) = 1 ≠ 0`. This contradiction proves `P(G)` for
`n ≥ 4`.

**The case `n = 3`.** Here `S = Q₈`, `X = U`, `C = O(C) × U`, `K = u O(C) ∪ u⁻¹ O(C)`,

conjugates of `K` and `0` elsewhere. Prove, in order:

1. *Integer values.* `ζ` is fixed by the Galois action of CharacterTheory §4.4, since `g` is
   conjugate into `K` exactly when `g^a` is, for `a` prime to `|G|`; so each `σ` permutes

   Hence every `ψᵢ` is fixed by every `σ`, is rational-valued, and is integer-valued (§4.2); so
   `ψᵢ(g^a) = ψᵢ(g)` for every `a` prime to `|G|`.
2. *The value at `u`.* `ψᵢ(u) = εᵢ`. Route: Frobenius reciprocity with `f = 4` on `K` gives
   `∑_{k ∈ K} ψᵢ(k) = 2 |O(C)| εᵢ`; for `v ∈ O(C)` and `a` prime to `|G|` with `a ≡ 1 (mod 4)` and

   so the integers `aᵥ = ψᵢ(u v)` satisfy `∑ᵥ aᵥ = |O(C)| εᵢ` and `aᵥ = a_{v⁻¹}`. Inversion fixes
   only `1` in `O(C)`, so `ψᵢ(u) = a₁ ≡ |O(C)| εᵢ ≡ 1 (mod 2)` and `∑ᵥ aᵥ² ≡ 1 (mod 2)`. The sum
   `∑ᵥ aᵥ²` is `|O(C)| m` for an integer `m`: `|ψᵢ|²` restricted to `C` is a character of `C`, and
   the sum of a character of `C` over a coset of `O(C)` is `|O(C)|` times a `ℤ[i]`-combination of
   its multiplicities along `C ⧸ O(C) ≅ U`. So `m` is odd and `∑ᵥ (aᵥ − εᵢ)² = (m − 1) |O(C)|`.
   If `m ≥ 3`: every integer `a` has `a² + εᵢ a ≥ 0`, and
   `∑_{g ∈ G} (ψᵢ(g)² + εᵢ ψᵢ(g)) = |G|` (`Representation.char_orthonormal` with `ψᵢ` real, and
   orthogonality to `1_G`), while the `|G : H|` disjoint conjugates of `K` contribute

   `dᵢ² + εᵢ dᵢ > 0`: a contradiction. So `m = 1`, `aᵥ = εᵢ` for all `v`, and `ψᵢ(u) = εᵢ`.
3. *Fusion of elements of order four.* All elements of order four of `G` are conjugate, so
   `ψᵢ(y) = εᵢ` for every `y` of order four. Route: the transfer `V : G →* Abelianization S`
   (`MonoidHom.transfer` along `Abelianization.of`) is trivial, `G` being perfect

   of `z` or of `1`, trivial since `z ∈ [S, S]` (§2.1); an orbit of size one contributes the image
   of a `G`-conjugate of `u` lying in `S`; and the number of orbits of size one is odd, since
   `|G : S|` is odd. If every `G`-conjugate of `u` in `S` lay in `⟨u⟩ = {1, u, z, u z}`, each such
   factor would be the image of `u` and `V(u)` would be an odd power of it, nontrivial as

   element of order four of `S` is inverted by the elements of order four outside its cyclic

   shows that all six elements of order four of `S` are conjugate to `u`; and every element of
   order four of `G` lies in a conjugate of `S`.
4. *The restriction congruence.* `⟨ψᵢ|_S, 1_S⟩ = (dᵢ + eᵢ + 6 εᵢ) / 8` is an integer, `S` having
   one element of order one, one of order two and six of order four; with `fᵢ = 4 cᵢ` this reads
   `dᵢ = 4 ⟨ψᵢ|_S, 1_S⟩ − 3 εᵢ + 2 cᵢ`, so `dᵢ ≡ εᵢ (mod 4)` whenever `cᵢ` is even.
5. *The final arithmetic.* Let `g = gcd(c₁, c₂, c₃)` and `cᵢ = g wᵢ`. From `∑ᵢ εᵢ cᵢ = 0` the
   number of odd `wᵢ` is even, and it is not zero, so exactly one `wᵢ` is even, say `w₁`; then

   `ε₂ d₂ + ε₃ d₃ ≡ 0 (mod 4)`; but the degree relation gives
   `ε₂ d₂ + ε₃ d₃ = −1 − ε₁ d₁ ≡ −2 (mod 4)`. This contradiction proves `P(G)` for `n = 3`.

## Layer 3: principal-block stability on involution sections

### 3.1 The central-character criterion for the principal `2`-block

Define `InPrincipalTwoBlock m χ`, for `m` an ideal of the algebraic integers and `χ : G → ℂ`,
as: `χ` is an irreducible character and, for every `g`, the central-character value
`|Cl(g)| · χ(g) / χ(1)` is an algebraic integer `z` with `z − |Cl(g)| ∈ m`. (The denominator is
`χ(1) ≥ 1`, the degree of an irreducible character.) For `m` maximal with `2 ∈ m`, prove
`InPrincipalTwoBlock m χ ↔ χ ∈ B₀(G)`: intersect `m` with the ring of integers of the cyclotomic
field `F = ℚ(ζ_e)` of M1, obtaining a prime of `F` above `2`; complete there to a `2`-modular
system, lift roots of unity (M1), and compare with the fixed system by M2; M4 characterizes the
block partition by congruence of reduced central characters on integral class sums, and the
principal block as the block of the trivial character, whose central character is `g ↦ |Cl(g)|`.
Prove that the criterion is independent of the choice of `m` among maximal ideals containing
`2`. *Needs:* CharacterTheory §4.3; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M1, M2, M4; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.irreducibleCharacters`, `TauCeti.Representation.centralCharacter`; Mathlib
`integralClosure`, `Ideal.IsMaximal`.

- The trivial character satisfies the criterion for every `m`: `z = |Cl(g)|`.
- The criterion is invariant under conjugation of `χ` by a group automorphism and under the
  Galois action of CharacterTheory §4.4 (the principal block is Galois-stable).
- For `χ ∈ B₀(G)`, `O(G) ≤ ker χ` (M8), so `χ(g n) = χ(g)` for `n ∈ O(G)`.

**Checks.**
- `C₂`, the sign character, `g = s`: `z = −1`, `−1 − 1 = −2 ∈ m`; the sign character is in the
  principal block, and `C₂` has one `2`-block.
- `S₃`, the character of degree two, `g` a transposition: `|Cl(g)| = 3`, `χ(g) = 0`, `z = 0`,
  and `0 − 3 = −3 ∉ m` since `m ∩ ℤ = 2ℤ`; it is not in the principal block (it has defect
  zero, in agreement with M8's `S₃` example).
- `S₃`, the sign character: at a `3`-cycle `z = 2 · 1 / 1 = 2`, difference `0`; at a
  transposition `z = −3`, difference `−6 ∈ m`; it is in the principal block.
- The wrong model "a maximal ideal containing `3`" puts the degree-two character of `S₃` in the
  principal block (`−3 ∈ m`), which is the correct `3`-block statement and the wrong `2`-block
  one; the ideal discriminates the prime.

### 3.2 Constancy on the odd part of an involution section

Prove `principalBlock_involution_section_constancy`: for `m` maximal with `2 ∈ m`, `χ` with
`InPrincipalTwoBlock m χ`, an involution `s`, a normal subgroup `N` of `C_G(s)` of odd order and
`v ∈ N`, `χ(s v) = χ(s)`. This is constancy on `s · N`; it does not say that `N` lies in the
kernel of `χ`. It is the block-theoretic step of the proof of the Z* theorem (Glauberman 1966).
Route, in the modular system of M1 with `p = 2`:

1. `s` is a `2`-element and `v` is `2`-regular and commutes with `s`, so M5 gives
   `χ(s v) = ∑_φ d^s_{χφ} φ(v)` over the irreducible Brauer characters `φ` of `C_G(s)`.
2. By the second main theorem (M7), `d^s_{χφ} ≠ 0` only for `φ` in a block `b` of `C_G(s)` with
   `b^G = B₀(G)`; by the local third main theorem (M7) with `P = ⟨s⟩` and
   `H = C_G(s) = P · C_G(P) ≤ N_G(P)`, that block is `B₀(C_G(s))`.
3. By M8, every simple module of `B₀(C_G(s))` is trivial on `O(C_G(s))`, which contains `N`
   (L1 maximality), so `φ(v) = φ(1)` for the `φ` that occur.
4. Hence `χ(s v) = ∑_φ d^s_{χφ} φ(1) = χ(s · 1) = χ(s)`, the formula of step 1 at `v = 1`.

*Needs:* §3.1; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M1, M5, M7, M8; LocalFiniteGroupTheory L1.

**Checks.**
- `N = ⊥`: `χ(s · 1) = χ(s)`, trivially.
- `C₆ = C₂ × C₃`, `s = (c, 1)`, `N = 1 × C₃ ≤ C_G(s) = G`: the characters in the principal
  `2`-block are those trivial on `C₃`, and for them `χ(s n) = χ(s)`; the character
  `(c, ω) ↦ ω` is not in the principal block and has `χ(s n) = ω ≠ 1 = χ(s)`.
- `S₃ × C₂`, `s = (1, c)`, `N = A₃ × 1`, `χ = sgn ⊗ ε` with `ε` the sign of `C₂`:
  `χ(s) = −1` and `χ(s n) = sgn(3-cycle) · ε(c) = −1`; for `χ = std ⊗ ε`, which is not in the
  principal block, `χ(s) = −2` but `χ(s n) = 1`; the block hypothesis is needed.
- The hypotheses `2 ∈ m` and maximality are needed: with a maximal ideal above `3`, the
  criterion selects the `3`-block, and the `S₃ × C₂` example above fails for `std ⊗ ε`.

## Layer 4: character values and the contradiction

Throughout this layer `G` is a minimal counterexample as in §1.3 with `O(G) = 1`, `⟨t^G⟩ = G`,
no nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup, and a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S ∋ t` containing an involution
`s ≠ t`; by §1.3, `s` commutes with `t` and is not conjugate to `t`, and every proper subgroup
`H ∋ t` has `⁅H, t⁆ ≤ O(H)`. The statements below carry these hypotheses explicitly; none is
asserted for arbitrary commuting involutions.

### 4.1 Constancy on products of conjugates

Let `χ ∈ B₀(G)` (equivalently `InPrincipalTwoBlock m χ`, §3.1). Prove that
`χ(t' s') = χ(t s)` for every conjugate `t'` of `t` and `s'` of `s`. Route: conjugate the pair to
`(t, h)` with `h` a conjugate of `s`. Since `t` and `h` are not conjugate, `orderOf (t h) = 2k`
with `k` odd (§0.3), and `w = (t h)^k` is an involution commuting with `t` and `h`.

1. `w` is not central (no nontrivial normal `2`-subgroup), so `C_G(w)` is a proper subgroup
   containing `t`, hence `⁅C_G(w), t⁆ ≤ N := O(C_G(w))`, a normal subgroup of odd order of
   `C_G(w)`.
2. `(t h)² = ⁅t, h⁆ ∈ N`; so `v = (t h)^(k+1) = w · (t h) ∈ N`, and `μ = t w` is an involution
   with `μ h = t (t h)^(k-1) t = (t h)^(1-k) ∈ N`, of odd order. By §0.3, `μ` is conjugate to `h`
   in `⟨μ, h⟩`, hence to `s`; and `t μ = w`.
3. The constancy theorem (§3.2) at the involution `w` and the odd normal subgroup `N ⊴ C_G(w)`
   gives `χ(t h) = χ(w v) = χ(w)`.
4. `s` and `μ` are conjugate involutions in `C_G(t)`, so product conjugacy (§0.2) gives
   `t s ∼ t μ = w`, and `χ(w) = χ(t s)`.

*Needs:* §0.2, §0.3, §1.3, §3.2.

**Checks.**
- `k` is odd by §0.3 alone; the odd-core package only needs `(t h)² ∈ N`: if `t h` has order
  two (`k = 1`), then `w = t h`, `v = 1`, `μ = h`, and every step is an identity.
- In a group that satisfies Z* the conclusion holds for the same reason: in `S₃ × C₂` with
  `t = (1, c)`, `s = ((12), 1)`, `χ = sgn ⊗ ε`, every `t' s'` is `(transposition, c)` with value
  `(−1)(−1) = 1 = χ(t s)`.
- The hypothesis that `t` is isolated is used in step 4 and in `k` odd: in `S₄` with `t = (12)`
  and `s = (34)`, `χ` the character of degree three, `χ((12)(34)) = −1` but
  `χ((12)(13)) = χ((132)) = 0`, so constancy on products of conjugates fails.

### 4.2 The class-sum identity

With `χ ∈ B₀(G)` as in §4.1, prove `χ(t s) · χ(1) = χ(t) · χ(s)`. Route: the central character
is multiplicative on class sums (CharacterTheory §4.3, §1.1):
`ω_χ(t) ω_χ(s) = ∑_u a_u ω_χ(u)` with `a_u` the structure constants, which after multiplying by
`χ(1)²` reads `|Cl(t)| |Cl(s)| χ(t) χ(s) = χ(1) ∑_{x ∈ Cl(t), y ∈ Cl(s)} χ(x y)`; by §4.1 every
`χ(x y)` equals `χ(t s)`, and the sum has `|Cl(t)| |Cl(s)|` terms. Apply the same identity to the
involution `t s ∈ S`, which commutes with `t`, differs from `t`, and is not conjugate to `t`, to
obtain `χ(s) · χ(1) = χ(t) · χ(t s)`. *Needs:* §4.1; CharacterTheory §1.1, §4.3; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.classSum_mul`, `TauCeti.Representation.centralCharacter_classSumCenter_mul_character_one`.

**Checks.**
- `V₄`, any linear `χ`: `χ(t s) χ(1) = χ(t) χ(s)` is multiplicativity.
- `S₃ × C₂`, `t = (1, c)`, `s = ((12), 1)`, `χ = std ⊗ 1`: `χ(t s) = 0`, `χ(1) = 2`,
  `χ(t) = 2`, `χ(s) = 0`: `0 = 0`.
- `S₄`, `t = (12)`, `s = (34)`, `χ` of degree three: `χ(t s) χ(1) = −3` but `χ(t) χ(s) = 1`;
  `t` is not isolated, and the identity fails.
- The class sizes in the identity are what make the central character multiplicative: in `S₃`
  the class sum `C` of the `3`-cycles satisfies `C² = 2 · 1 + C`; for the character of degree
  two, `ω_χ(C) = 2 · (−1) / 2 = −1` satisfies `(−1)² = 2 + (−1)`, while the wrong normalization
  `χ(g) / χ(1) = −1/2` gives `1/4 ≠ 3/2`.

### 4.3 The character-value alternative

With `χ ∈ B₀(G)` nontrivial, prove `χ(s) = 0 ∨ χ(t) = −χ(1)`. Route: the two identities of §4.2
give `χ(s) χ(1)² = χ(t) χ(t s) χ(1) = χ(t)² χ(s)`, so `χ(s) (χ(1)² − χ(t)²) = 0`. If
`χ(t) = χ(1)`, then `t` acts trivially in a representation affording `χ`
(`Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff`), so `⟨t^G⟩ = G` acts trivially and `χ` is the trivial
character. *Needs:* §4.2; Tau Ceti `Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff`; Mathlib
`Subgroup.normalClosure`.

**Checks.**
- The trivial character has `χ(s) = 1 ≠ 0` and `χ(t) = 1 ≠ −1`: the alternative is for
  nontrivial `χ` only.
- `⟨t^G⟩ = G` is needed to exclude `χ(t) = χ(1)`: in `S₃ × C₂` with `t = (1, c)`,
  `χ = sgn ⊗ 1` has `χ(t) = χ(1) = 1`, and `⟨t^G⟩ = 1 × C₂ ≠ G`.
- `χ(1)² − χ(t)² = (χ(1) − χ(t))(χ(1) + χ(t))` over `ℂ`; both factors are integers since `χ(t)`
  is a sum of `±1`.

### 4.4 The orthogonality contradiction

Apply block-section orthogonality (M5) to `B₀(G)` for the pairs `(s, t)` and `(s, 1)`: the
`2`-parts `s` and `t` are not conjugate (isolation), and `s ≠ 1`, so
`∑_{χ ∈ B₀} χ(s) χ(t) = 0` and `∑_{χ ∈ B₀} χ(s) χ(1) = 0` (values at involutions are real, so no
conjugation is needed). Adding, `∑_{χ ∈ B₀} χ(s) (χ(t) + χ(1)) = 0`. By §4.3 every nontrivial
term is zero, and the trivial character contributes `1 · (1 + 1) = 2 ≠ 0`. Hence no minimal
counterexample has a second involution in `S`. Verify that the trivial character lies in
`B₀(G)` and is counted once (§3.1). *Needs:* §3.1, §4.3; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M5.

**Checks.**
- The relation is false for conjugate `2`-parts: in `S₃` with `g = h = (12)`,
  `∑_{B₀} χ(g)² = 1 + 1 = 2 ≠ 0`.
- `S₃ × C₂`, `g = (1, c)`, `h = ((12), 1)`: the principal block consists of
  `1, sgn ⊗ 1, 1 ⊗ ε, sgn ⊗ ε` (the characters trivial on `O = A₃ × 1`), and
  `∑ χ(g) χ(h) = 1 − 1 − 1 + 1 = 0`.
- Over a field of characteristic two the final `2 ≠ 0` would fail; the sum is in `ℂ`.

### Examples

`S₃ × C₂`, `S₄` and `V₄` as computed above.

### Dependencies

Layers 0, 1, 3; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M5; CharacterTheory §1.1, §4.3; Tau Ceti
`Representation.char_eq_finrank_iff`.

## Layer 5: assembly and applications

### 5.1 The theorem

Prove `glauberman_zStar`: for an isolated involution `t` of a finite group `G`, the existence
form of `t ∈ Z*(G)` holds (Glauberman 1966, Theorem 1). Route: a minimal counterexample
(§1.3) has a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S ∋ t` with either a unique involution, excluded by §2.2 and
§2.5, or a second involution `s`, excluded by §4.4. Prove `glauberman_zStar_sylow`: for `t` in
a Sylow `2`-subgroup `S` and weakly closed in `S` as a singleton, the same conclusion, by §0.4.
Prove `t ∈ Z*(G)` in the definitional form and `G = C_G(t) · O(G)` by §1.1. *Needs:* §0.4, §1.1,
§1.3, §2.2, §2.5, §4.4.

**Checks.**
- `C₂`: `N = ⊥`. `S₃`, `t = (12)`: `N = A₃`. `Q₈`, `t = −1`: `N = ⊥`. `V₄`: `N = ⊥` for each
  involution.
- `A₅`: `(12)(34)` is not isolated and the conclusion fails for it; the hypothesis is not
  removable.
- `F₂₀` and `𝔽₃² ⋊ Q₈`: `N = C₅`, respectively `N = 𝔽₃²`, and `t ∉ Z(G)`; the conclusion is
  `Z*`, not `Z`.
- The hypotheses are finiteness, `orderOf t = 2` and isolation only; no solvability, simplicity
  or Sylow structure is assumed.

### 5.2 Corollaries and transport

Prove:

- if `O(G) = 1`, every isolated involution is central;
- a nonabelian finite simple group has no isolated involution, and no involution weakly closed
  as a singleton in a Sylow `2`-subgroup (Waldecker, Chapter 1, p. 1);
- for `t` isolated and `χ ∈ B₀(G)`, `χ(t) = ±χ(1)`: `O(G) ≤ ker χ` (M8) and `t` is central
  modulo `O(G)`, so `t` acts as a scalar `±1`; and for an involution `s` commuting with `t`,
  `χ(t s) χ(1) = χ(t) χ(s)`;
- transport: the conclusion for `t` in `G` gives the conclusion for `g t g⁻¹`, for the image of
  `t` in every quotient, and for `t` in every subgroup containing it (§1.2).

*Needs:* §1.2, §5.1; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M8.

**Checks.**
- `S₃`, `t = (12)`, `χ = sgn`: `χ(t) = −1 = −χ(1)`; `χ` of degree two is not in `B₀` and has
  `χ(t) = 0`.
- `A₅` is simple and `(12)(34)` is not isolated, as the corollary predicts.
- `S₃ × C₂`, `t = (1, c)`, `s = ((12), 1)`, `χ = sgn ⊗ ε`: `χ(t s) χ(1) = 1 = χ(t) χ(s)`.

### Examples

`S₃`, `A₅`, `S₃ × C₂`, `F₂₀`, `𝔽₃² ⋊ Q₈`.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–4; ModularCharactersAndBlocks M8.

## Downstream consumers

No roadmap on main consumes this roadmap yet. LocalFiniteGroupTheory names Glauberman's Z*
theorem and the Brauer–Suzuki theorem, in its closing section, as the local theorems a later
roadmap should supply on top of its material; this roadmap is that supply. The natural consumers
are the local analysis of involution centralizers and the classification of groups with dihedral
or semidihedral Sylow `2`-subgroups, which use `glauberman_zStar` and its corollary that a
nonabelian simple group has no isolated involution.
