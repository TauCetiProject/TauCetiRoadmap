# Topological sheaf cohomology, compact supports and closed-subset duality

Construct the topological sheaf-cohomology interfaces for arbitrary compact Hausdorff spaces and locally compact Hausdorff spaces: cover-refinement Čech cohomology, bounded hypercohomology, ordinary open/compact sheaf comparison, pure homotopy K-sheaves, compact supports, finite quotient descent, closed-subset topological duality and finite interval-diagram gluing. Build these on native sheaves, modules, complexes and derived functors. The definitive specification is this README; `Suggested.lean` supplies interface sketches rather than an exhaustive checklist.

## Scope, coefficients and ownership

Use arbitrary commutative coefficient rings R for sheaves of R-modules, the open/compact equivalence, bounded comparisons, support operations and the topological duality below. State the actual smallness, site, exactness and flatness hypotheses for each categorical construction. The finite-group invariant comparison requires the group order to be a unit in R; R=ℚ is its principal characteristic-zero specialization. Real vector spaces do not replace rational coefficients, and a real representation's dimension and orientation module are separate data.

An unqualified space in TS1–TS3 is locally compact Hausdorff; the cover-colimit Čech/derived comparison in TS1 is stated for compact Hausdorff spaces. The purely cover-theoretic raw-refinement, refinement-homotopy and small-cofinal cohomological-colimit interfaces in TS1.1–TS1.2 are constructed for arbitrary topological spaces; the subsequent compact-derived and support comparisons retain their stated compactness and local-compactness hypotheses. The support comparison on a noncompact space uses its compact one-point compactification and extension by zero. TS4 permits Hausdorff finite-dimensional topological manifolds without second countability, paracompactness or smooth structure; it explicitly constructs the additional local-homology and orientation statements needed in that generality. It uses ordinary singular chains, not a new manifold or homology category.

Use the native site category `CategoryTheory.Sheaf (Opens.grothendieckTopology X) (ModuleCat.{u} R)` for X and R in Type u. Its native equivalence with `TopCat.Sheaf` supplies the topological wrapper. Use native `ModuleCat`, `CochainComplex`, `HomologicalComplex`, homology, cones, shifts and derived-category localization. Compact indexing is `TopologicalSpace.Compacts X`, ordered by inclusion. Reuse native Čech and alternating-face complexes, sheaf cohomology, exact sequences, site restriction and extension by zero. The module-valued and underlying abelian comparisons are constructions here, with their explicit universe/sheafification/Ext hypotheses. `SheafOfModules` over a ring sheaf is a separate ringed-space interface; these targets use constant-R module-valued sheaves.

[ComplexKahlerGeometry K2](../ComplexKahlerGeometry/README.md#k2-dolbeault-calculus-and-the-local-lemmas) owns analytic resolutions, fine/acyclic sheaves and all-abelian-sheaf Čech/derived comparison on paracompact Hausdorff manifolds. [CharacteristicClasses C1.2](../CharacteristicClasses/README.md) owns singular support cochains and constant-coefficient Čech/singular comparison on paracompact Hausdorff spaces of CW type. TS1 owns the arbitrary compact Hausdorff extension and its bounded-complex, compact-index and refinement-independent cohomological interfaces. Compact Hausdorff spaces and noncompact paracompact manifolds are distinct input classes. On compact manifolds construct the natural comparison with CKG's fixed-cover augmentations and cohomological refinement maps. TS3.7 compares with CC's actual fixed-cover complexes and its singular resolution. These overlap adapters identify the resulting maps; analytic fine resolutions remain with CKG and constant-coefficient singular/CW comparisons remain with CC.

[AlgebraicTopology](../AlgebraicTopology/README.md) Stages2–3 supplies ordinary singular/relative chains, local coefficients, subdivision, excision and binary open-cover Mayer–Vietoris with first map (i_U,−i_V). Stage6.0 supplies native extension by zero from ℕ complexes and integer reindexing. Ordinary6.1–6.2 supplies cochains and cup/cap conventions;6.3–6.4 supplies orientation and duality on its stated manifold carriers. TS4 owns the closed-complement relative Mayer–Vietoris construction and its sign bridge, and extends the local-orientation/vanishing/Čech-duality statements to its stated Hausdorff topological manifolds. On common carriers it uses the same local homology fibres and transports as AT. [DGAInfinity Layer0](../DGAInfinity/README.md) owns general signed-complex/tensor interfaces; consume the implemented `TauCeti.Algebra.Homology.Monoidal.Braiding` Koszul braiding at matching ring/module universes. TS0/TS5 own only the additional independent-parity and finite-flag adapters.

Generic finite quotient sheaf comparison belongs to TS3, including nonfree and ineffective actions and arbitrary coefficient sheaves with the stated invertibility. Orbifold atlas geometry, orbibundles, integration, weighted orbifold fundamental classes and weighted Poincaré duality belong to [Orbifolds](../Orbifolds/README.md). Its singular/de Rham/Čech atlas adapters are specializations requiring their own geometric data. They are not inputs to TS3. Implicit atlases, obstruction spaces, global virtual traces, stable-domain or stable-map moduli, virtual fundamental classes and Gromov–Witten axioms are outside this roadmap.

Use native graded families and complexes; no second generic DG category is introduced. TS5 fixes its flag-order and orientation-line specialization against this adapter.

## TS1. Compact-space Čech cohomology and bounded hypercohomology

1. For an indexed open cover U of X, use native `cechComplexFunctor` on the presheaf and actual tuple intersections. Construct the ordered-subset version, including orientation lines/signs, degenerate/repeated indices and its comparison to the native tuple complex. For a finite cover of size n, the ordered version vanishes above n−1. Prove the empty-cover convention separately. Retain raw refinement functions as native formal-coproduct morphisms with their inclusions. Construct induced cochain maps, identity and composition laws, and an explicit refinement cochain homotopy between any two functions refining the same covers. Prove compatibility with coefficient maps, further refinements, restriction and the ordered/tuple comparison, without division by factorials.

2. Fix a small cover system. For compact X use finite indexed covers in `Σ n : ℕ, (ULift.{u} (Fin n) → Opens X)` satisfying the covering condition, with repeated opens and the empty family retained. For general X use point-neighborhood covers U:X→Opens X with x∈U x; choosing a member containing each point proves cofinality among all open covers. Use subtype indices for open subspaces and prove common-refinement/cofinality maps. Raw refinement functions remain actual maps. After proving their cohomological independence, use the directed preorder ordered by existence of refinement and its strict cohomology diagram. Define Čech cohomology as its filtered colimit in ModuleCat.{u} R. Prove independence of the small cofinal system and its natural comparison maps. Compactness supplies finite subcovers and the simultaneous finite-intersection shrinkings. The raw category of all refinement functions has no filteredness assertion: the repeated-index example retains its distinct parallel maps. Chain representatives use fixed-cover complexes, their refinement homotopies and derived global sections.

3. For every R-module sheaf on compact Hausdorff X, construct natural all-degree `cechDerivedComparison` to TS0 derived global sections and its underlying-abelian comparison. Prove the compact shrinking/refinement and flasque-resolution argument, augmented Čech-row exactness after cofinal refinement, and the acyclic-cover theorem with actual intersection-acyclicity. Own the compact Hausdorff sheafification lemma for presheaves: a presheaf with zero sheafification has zero cover-colimit Čech cohomology in every degree. Prove it by local annihilation of each representative section, finite simultaneous shrinkings/refinements of the tuple intersections and the ordered/tuple comparison; deduce the Čech comparison for a presheaf and its sheafification by exactness and the locally zero kernel/cokernel. Include degree0 gluing, empty/point, restriction, coefficients, finite coproducts and compatibility with the existing long exact sequence and site Mayer–Vietoris. On compact manifolds compare the fixed-cover/resolution maps with CKG K2's written comparison; CKG's analytic resolutions are unnecessary for the compact-space proof.

4. For bounded-below sheaf complexes construct the finite-cover double complexes, the totalization sign and the cover-refinement homotopies. Define Čech hypercohomology by the directed colimit of their cohomology, not by an unconstructed strict chain-colimit. Prove comparison with TS0 derived hypercohomology. Export both spectral sequences from the double complex with their stated pages, including `E1^(p,q)=Čech^q(X;F^p)⇒H^(p+q)(X;F)` and `E2^(p,q)=H^p(X;mathcalH^q(F))⇒H^(p+q)(X;F)`, and strong convergence after translating the uniform lower degree bound: every total-degree diagonal is finite. For the second sequence first obtain the presheaf H^q(F), apply TS1.3's sheafification lemma to identify its cover-colimit Čech groups with those of mathcalH^q(F), and then use `cechDerivedComparison`. The refinement homotopy lowers Čech filtration by one for the sheaf-degree filtration, so refinement maps agree only on E_r for r≥2 there; E0/E1 are not functorial on the cover preorder. For the internal-degree filtration the homotopy preserves filtration, so maps agree on E_r for r≥1, but not E0. Define pagewise filtered colimits only from those first functorial pages (from E1 for the displayed E1-sequence and from E2 for the displayed E2-sequence), then pass their differentials by exactness of filtered ModuleCat colimits. For total degree m and uniform lower bound b, prove page stabilization uniformly for r>m−b+1, and compare the finite-diagonal abutment filtrations. This identifies exactly where the common lower bound is used. Prove the bounded-complex version, coefficient naturality, cone/long-exact-sequence comparison and truncation compatibility. For unbounded homotopy K-complexes use only the locally lower-bounded/pure route in TS2; no unrestricted unbounded hyperdescent assertion is inferred.

## TS2. Open/compact sheaves and pure homotopy K-sheaves

1. A K-presheaf is an actual `(Compacts X)ᵒᵖ ⥤ ModuleCat R`. Construct its extension to open U by `lim_(K⊂U) F(K)`. A K-sheaf satisfies F(∅)=0, the left-exact sequence `0→F(K∪L)→F(K)⊕F(L)→F(K∩L)` and the neighborhood-continuity isomorphism. The last map in this displayed sequence is not required surjective. Express continuity by the compact-neighborhood diagram of TS0 and prove the equivalent open-neighborhood formula. Construct

   `alphaCompact(F)(K)=colim_(open U⊃K) F(U)` and
   `alphaOpen(G)(U)=lim_(compact K⊂U) G(K)`.

   Prove `alphaCompact ⊣ alphaOpen` on presheaves by the actual limit/colimit maps, and prove the ordinary sheaf/K-sheaf equivalence on locally compact Hausdorff X, with actual unit/counit, restriction, singleton-stalk and coefficient maps. Finite-intersection neighborhood cofinality and the genuine limit/colimit maps prove these assertions; the two indexing categories themselves are not asserted equivalent. Prove continuity on filtered decreasing compact families and finite compact-cover exactness. Compare the open stalk with singleton evaluation in the compact model.

2. A complex of K-presheaves is an actual functor to native R-module cochain complexes. Construct the homotopy K-sheaf axioms: F(∅) acyclic, the finite binary Mayer–Vietoris total complex acyclic, and compact-neighborhood continuity a quasi-isomorphism. Use first map (restriction,restriction) and second map restriction_left−restriction_right. In horizontal degrees p=0,1,2 the qth total term uses internal degree q−p; take D=d_internal+(-1)^k delta on an internal degree-k element and compare with the actual native cones. Retain the TS0 independent-parity conversion. Prove the ensuing exact sequence and stability under finite sums, shifts, cones, finite filtrations and exact filtered colimits with displayed hypotheses. Soft K-sheaves have surjective compact restrictions; prove their degree0 complexes and the appropriate soft resolutions supply the axioms. Do not identify every ordinary K-sheaf placed in degree0 with a homotopy K-sheaf.

3. For a finite compact cover of compact X construct the finite ordered-intersection Čech complex of a K-presheaf and the double complex for a K-complex. Establish refinement homotopies. For every open-set presheaf P on compact Hausdorff X, prove open/compact cover comparison and coefficient naturality. Prove the compact transform's required compact-neighborhood continuity in its construction, and prove objectwise quasi-isomorphism invariance by finite filtration. For an open-set presheaf P define its compact transform by the same compact-neighborhood colimit as αCompact, and construct the comparison from the finite-cover complexes by compact-neighborhood cofinality. If P has zero sheafification, prove its cover-colimit Čech groups vanish by finite local annihilation and refinement; do not claim the pointwise compact transform itself is zero. For every continuity K-presheaf G, also prove the compact-cover comparison with αOpen G through the actual unit isomorphism G≅αCompact(αOpen G); the input G need not be an ordinary sheaf on opens. For a homotopy K-sheaf, prove evaluation at X maps quasi-isomorphically to every finite-cover total complex, by binary union/intersection induction, including X=∅. Consequently its evaluation computes its compact-cover hypercohomology. For the continuity K-presheaf G, use the actual isomorphism G≅αCompact(αOpen G); the open-side sheafification lemma from TS1.3 and compact-neighborhood cofinality identify the relevant cover-colimit groups. Prove finite local shrinking annihilates tuple representatives, then use the uniform lower bound and truncation induction for complex-valued K-presheaves. This comparison does not follow from an ordinary-sheaf-only theorem. Restrict this statement to each compact subspace for noncompact X. Cover cohomology descends through the directed refinement preorder as in TS1; no speculative open/K derived-category equivalence is needed.

4. Define the actual weak local lower-vanishing condition: for every x there are an open neighborhood U and an integer N such that H^i(F(K))=0 for all compact K⊂U and i≤N. Define purity by this condition AND H^i(F({x}))=0 for i≠0. Prove that on each compact subspace the finite binary descent gives a uniform lower bound, then induct upwards using continuity and singleton detection to prove H^i(F(K))=0 for all i<0. Construct H^0F as an actual K-sheaf and prove

   `H^i(F(X)) ≅ Čech^i(X; alphaOpen(H^0F))`

   for compact X. Give its maps, coefficient and restriction naturality, degree0 normalization and the finite total-complex version for a complex of pure homotopy K-sheaves. The homology of evaluations is not by definition sheaf hypercohomology; these are the comparison isomorphisms.

5. Construct pullback of homotopy K-sheaves along open and closed embeddings i:Y↪X by `(i*F)(K)=F(i(K))`. Prove compactness, finite-cover descent, the actual neighborhood-continuity/cofinality maps and composition. Use compact-neighborhood sandwiching for closed embeddings; no evaluation on a noncompact intersection with an open set is used. Prove stalkwise quasi-isomorphism detection under the local lower bounds by cones/truncations. Prove the open/closed purity criterion for a closed Z and its open complement by choosing, for compact K, a compact neighborhood L of K∩Z inside K and using K=L∪(K\int_K L); the exact continuity maps control L before binary descent. Export bounded-flat/K-flat tensor and pairing comparisons, with the actual hypotheses preserving quasi-isomorphisms, and the rank-one-free orientation-line specialization.

## TS4. Local topological orientations and duality for arbitrary closed subsets

### TS4.0. The local-homology extension

#### TS4.0a. Topological chart carriers

Use Hausdorff spaces with native local homeomorphisms/charted-space data locally modeled on ℝⁿ, or on the closed half-space with a specified boundary subset carried to its coordinate hyperplane. No smooth atlas, global triangulation, second countability or paracompactness is required. Use supplied boundary charts to state the manifold-with-boundary case. Dimension0 has empty boundary. In dimension1 the tame pieces of the discrete boundary are locally empty or full.

#### TS4.0b. Local orientation and boundary transport

Reuse AT ordinary singular/relative chain complexes and their coefficient systems. Prove the local ball/sphere and half-space relative calculations, all chart-excision and restriction maps, compatibility on overlapping charts and independence of charts. Only after computing these groups, characterize the boundary as the points with vanishing local top homology (handle low dimensions separately); invariance of the local groups under chart changes proves that the coordinate hyperplane is intrinsic. Do not use this characterization to construct the charts or calculate their local homology. First work integrally. On AT carriers use its identical local-homology fibre formula and path transport. For all other carriers extend those same constructions by the chart argument. The local orientation line on the interior is `H_n(M,M\{x};ℤ)`. Construct its locally constant sheaf and the associated native fundamental-groupoid local system, and identify their path transport using subdivision of paths through simply connected charts. This builds the nonsmooth, non-second-countable extension here. A boundary point's absolute local top homology is not its orientation fiber. Extend the interior line by j_* across the half-space boundary; prove it remains locally isomorphic to ℤ, with j the interior inclusion. Define the relative orientation sheaf by j!. Construct the underlying exact stalk sequence `0→j!o_int→j_*o_int→i_*o_D→0`, deriving the boundary map from the pair connecting homomorphism. The last map is odd for the independent parities n and n−1: express the even-map super sequence with target `Π i_*o_D` and prove the comparison after forgetting parity. Do not silently declare the unshifted boundary map even. Pin the coordinate simplex/local sphere generator and the outward-first half-space boundary sign by comparison with AT's existing ordinary generators. Start with AT Stage 2.5 reference classes and Stage 3 chains, pairs and excision. Prove the AT6.3 agreement in TS4.3 after TS4.2 establishes the common carrier; it is a downstream comparison, not a chart or local-homology input.

#### TS4.0c. Coefficients and orientation pairing

Construct the local sign pairing `o_M⊗o_M≅ℤ`, its dual line and coefficient extension `o_(M,R)=o_(M,ℤ)⊗_ℤ R` to every commutative R; tensor with L over R in the displayed orientation-before-L order. An ordinary local coefficient system L has independent parity0; the order swap from AT's L⊗o to o⊗L is then unsigned. Represent the local-system/sheaf dictionary by actual functors and natural isomorphisms, while retaining the same fibre formula and transport on the overlap. A fundamental class has coefficients in o_M^dual. Normalize the ordered generator pairing to +1 after forgetting parity; interchanging odd orientation factors still carries the super sign, so do not impose an unshifted symmetric super pairing. Pin tensor and dual evaluation order in the comparison with the ordinary AT pairing. Prove the orientation/local-system-to-sheaf dictionary and the local multiplication comparison for any native locally constant R-module system L: top local homology is `o_M⊗L`, with the actual coefficient-extension map on relative simplex generators. ## TS5. Finite interval diagrams and pure gluing

1. For a finite poset S use its interval category: objects (s,t) with s≤t, and a unique map (s,t)→(s',t') when s≤s'≤t'≤t. A diagram is an actual strict functor into native R-module complexes; its composition identities are data of that functor. Define the finite flag total complex by

   `Tot(A)^n = ⊕_[s0<...<sp, p≥0] A_(s0,sp)^(n+p)`.

   In the flag direction, deleting an inner vertex uses the identity on its endpoint complex, deleting the first uses A_(s0,sp)→A_(s1,sp), and deleting the last uses A_(s0,sp)→A_(s0,s(p−1)). The horizontal boundary is the alternating sum of these maps. With internal degree q and no independent parity, fix `D=d_internal+(-1)^q partial_flag`. Construct its comparison with native finite biproduct/alternating-face totalization and prove D²=0 from the strict interval identities. The flag degree is −p, so both terms raise total degree by1. Finiteness bounds flag length; object complexes need not be termwise bounded.

2. Retain the independent parity of TS0. Interpret each flag orientation line on the RIGHT with cochain degree−p and parity p. For source parity σ the super differential has flag coefficient (−1)^σ. Prove the conjugation by (−1)^(pτ), τ=σ−q, to the displayed native total differential, including endpoint-deletion maps, simplex orientation and interval structure maps. This fixes a basis/order convention for Pardon's A.7 construction, whose deletion/internal signs are read through its A.0.3 super convention; construct that comparison rather than asserting an unspecified named homotopy-colimit model. Prove orientation-line and tensor-order changes with their actual signs.

3. Prove functoriality for interval-diagram maps, homotopies, shifts, cones, finite biproducts and finite filtrations. Objectwise quasi-isomorphisms induce a quasi-isomorphism of totals by the finite flag-length filtration. If S has a unique maximum s_top and every map A_(s,t)→A_(s,t') for s≤t'≤t is a quasi-isomorphism, prove the natural inclusion A_(s_top,s_top)→Tot(A) a quasi-isomorphism, with its explicit cone filtration. Include the empty-poset and singleton cases. Construct the product-poset shuffle map `Tot(A)⊗Tot(B)→Tot(A⊗B)`, its naturality, signs, associativity/unit and comparison to the nerve-product subdivision. Construct the product comparison by shuffle/Alexander–Whitney maps and their actual finite normalized-nerve homotopies, so this structural chain comparison does not require flat coefficients. Separately state flat/K-flat hypotheses whenever tensoring quasi-isomorphisms or passing to a derived tensor comparison. The operation is a finite flag complex; it is not an ordinary categorical colimit or a new general infinity category.

4. Prove that finite interval totals of homotopy K-sheaves remain homotopy K-sheaves, using the finite filtration and TS2 closure laws, with actual compact restriction and continuity maps. ## Proof order and verification targets

TS0 consumes AT6.0 and the implemented native Koszul braiding, with DGAInfinity Layer0 for its general complex interfaces, and supplies the additional scalar comparison, compact neighborhoods and independent-parity signs. TS1 constructs cover maps and refinement homotopies BEFORE its filtered cohomology colimit and compact derived comparison. TS3's extension/support and proper finite-fiber statements use TS0–TS2; finite quotient cohomology then adds averaging with invertible order. It has no Orbifolds input.

TS4.0 starts from AT Stage 2.5's ordinary reference classes/local coefficient carriers and Stage 3 chains, pairs and excision; it does not consume AT's later Stage 6.3–6.4 orientation/cap duality. Prove chart laws and local homology first, then the intrinsic-boundary characterization, orientation comparisons and upper-vanishing floor. TS4.1 uses these local calculations for purity and the explicit orientation morphism; TS4.2 consumes TS1–TS3 and that purity. Only after TS4.2 does TS4.3 compare with AT Stage 6.3–6.4 and CC support theory on their common hypotheses. AT duality is a downstream overlap check, not an input to TS4.0 or purity. There is no dependency from VFCs, stable-map moduli, weighted orbifold PD or the speculative general derived open/K equivalence.

Acceptance includes actual maps and their compatibility, not just abstract group isomorphisms.
