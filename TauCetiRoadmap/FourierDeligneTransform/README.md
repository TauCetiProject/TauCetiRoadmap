# Roadmap: the Fourier–Deligne transform

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the ℓ-adic Fourier transform of Deligne, Katz and Laumon on the étale cohomology of schemes in characteristic `p`. It starts from the finite Fourier transform of `Λ`-valued functions on finite vector spaces and from the six-operation, Frobenius and perverse foundations of EtaleCohomologyFoundations. It then constructs kernel transforms between compactifiable schemes with their composition law and trace functions, and the character sheaves `ℒ_χ` of smooth connected commutative groups over finite fields obtained from Lang torsors, in particular the Artin–Schreier sheaves `ℒ_ψ` and the Kummer sheaves. With these it defines the transform `FT_ψ` on a vector bundle over any `𝔽_p`-scheme. The end theorems are the equality `FT_! = FT_*` through local acyclicity at infinity; inversion `FT^{E^∨} ∘ FT^E ≅ [−1]^*(−r)` as explicit data; duality; functoriality in the base and in linear maps, with external products, convolution and Plancherel; the trace-function identity and the Frobenius exchange; and perverse `t`-exactness over a field. On the affine line it proves, after classifying the simple perverse sheaves on a curve, the exchange of their three types, the theory of Fourier sheaves, the Gauss-sum transform of Kummer sheaves, Laumon's description of the local monodromy of the transform, and the definition and tame values of the three local Fourier transforms.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns:

- the finite Fourier transform of functions with values in a commutative ring carrying a nondegenerate additive character, and its inversion, Plancherel and convolution identities (Tau Ceti's orthogonality and Poisson summation over linear codes, the finite Fourier lemma of AlgebraicCodingTheory Layer 3 (a completed roadmap), cover primitive characters of finite rings into domains on `R^ι` with the dot product; the version here allows any coefficient ring with a nondegenerate character and an abstract vector space with its dual);
- kernel transforms `Φ_𝒦` between separated finitely presented schemes over a qcqs base, their composition isomorphism with its associativity, unit and base-change coherence, their constructibility, and the function–sheaf dictionary for them: over a finite field the trace function of `Φ_𝒦 K` is the finite kernel transform of the trace functions of `K` and `𝒦`;
- the Lang torsor of a smooth connected commutative group scheme of finite type over `𝔽_q`, the character sheaves `ℒ_χ`, the Artin–Schreier sheaves `ℒ_ψ(f)` and the Kummer sheaves `ℒ_χ(g)`, with their tensor, pullback, base-extension and trace laws, their vanishing cohomology and the local behaviour of `ℒ_ψ(f)` on the line, with the comparison of the Galois group, inertia and upper-numbering filtration of a henselian discrete valuation ring with those of its completion;
- the Euler–Poincaré formula for lisse sheaves on smooth affine curves over an algebraically closed field with the coefficients of this roadmap (free modules over finite local rings, finite fields, `E` and `ℚ̄_ℓ`) and with compact supports, derived from the formula with `𝔽_ℓ` coefficients and ordinary cohomology (§2.7); the rational-coefficient form on all smooth curves, proper ones included, is EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves §5.2 (`curveEulerPoincareSwan`), with its descent to a perfect base in CongruenceSpectralGap §4.2b, and on affine curves it is the rational case of §2.7, which those roadmaps can import from here;
- the classification of the simple perverse sheaves on a smooth curve over a field with field coefficients, the one-dimensional case of the classification of simple perverse sheaves (§5.2), whose form in every dimension is EtaleDualityAndPerverseSheaves §2.10, which can import the curve case from here;
- the Fourier–Deligne transform on a vector bundle over an `𝔽_p`-scheme and every statement of Layers 3–5.

## Conventions

- `p` is a prime and `𝔽_p = ℤ/p`. The algebra of Layer 0 allows any commutative ring. In §§1.1–1.2 `Λ` is any torsion commutative ring (killed by some `N ≥ 1`), the generality of the compact-support direct image of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8; from §1.3 on, a torsion coefficient ring `Λ` is a nonzero finite commutative ring, adic and rational coefficients are those of the next item, and Layer 5 fixes coefficient fields in its introduction. An additive character is a group homomorphism `ψ : 𝔽_p → Λ^×` (Mathlib's `AddChar (ZMod p) Λ`). It is **nondegenerate** when `ψ(1) − 1` is a unit of `Λ`. Then `ψ(a) − 1` is a unit for every `a ≠ 0`, `p` is a unit of `Λ`, and the characteristic `N` of `Λ` is prime to `p`, so `N` is invertible on every `𝔽_p`-scheme (§0.1). For a finite local `Λ` of residue characteristic `ℓ ≠ p`, nondegenerate means nontrivial (KL85 lines 442–446).
- Adic coefficients are the ring of integers `O_E` of a finite extension `E/ℚ_ℓ`, `ℓ ≠ p`, containing a primitive `p`-th root of unity, with a nontrivial `ψ : 𝔽_p → O_E^×`; every reduction `ψ mod λ^n` is nondegenerate. `E`, `O_E` and `ℚ̄_ℓ` objects are those of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.4.1–F.4.6, and every adic statement is the realization of the torsion statement at each level.
- `D⁺(X, Λ)` is the bounded-below derived category of étale `Λ`-module sheaves of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.1; `Rf_!` is the compact-support direct image of its §0.8 on separated finitely presented morphisms between qcqs schemes; `⊠_S` is `p_1^*(−) ⊗^L p_2^*(−)`. Complexes are cohomological, `Hⁱ(K[r]) = H^{i+r}(K)`; `Λ(1) = Λ ⊗_{ℤ/N} μ_N` (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.3), so `Λ(−r)[−2r]` sits in degree `2r`.
- Frobenius. Over `𝔽_q` the arithmetic Frobenius is `z ↦ z^q`; the geometric Frobenius `Frob` is its inverse and acts on `Λ(−1)` by `q` (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.10.1–F.10.2). For `x ∈ X(𝔽_{qⁿ})`, `Frob_x` is the geometric `qⁿ`-Frobenius acting on the stalk at a geometric point over `x`.
- The trace function `t_{K,n} : X(𝔽_{qⁿ}) → Λ` of `K ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(X, Λ)` on a separated finite-type `X` over `𝔽_q` sends `x` to the Lefschetz number `L(Frob_x, K_x̄)` of `Frob_x` on the perfect complex `K_x̄`, the local term of InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups §IG.0.T2 (LAU87 lines 242–244 for `ℚ̄_ℓ`). Witness: `t_{Λ(−1)[−1], n} = −qⁿ`, since `Frob_x` acts on `Λ(−1)` by `qⁿ` and the shift puts it in odd degree.
- Lang torsor. For a commutative group `G` over `𝔽_q`, written additively, with `q`-Frobenius `F`, the Lang map is `L = F − id`. The character sheaf `ℒ_χ` of `χ : G(𝔽_q) → Λ^×` is the `χ`-isotypic part of `L_*Λ` for the translation action `(a·s)(t) = s(t + a)`; equivalently the push-out of the Lang torsor along `χ^{−1}` (LAU87 lines 306–312). The Galois group acts on functions `s` on a geometric fibre by `(g·s)(t) = s(g^{−1}t)`; for `x ∈ G(𝔽_{qⁿ})` with norm `N(x) = x + F(x) + ⋯ + F^{n−1}(x) ∈ G(𝔽_q)`, the arithmetic `qⁿ`-Frobenius acts on `L^{−1}(x)(𝔽̄_q)` by `t ↦ Fⁿ(t) = t + N(x)`, so the geometric one sends `s` to `t ↦ s(t + N(x)) = χ(N(x)) s(t)`. With this choice `Frob_x` acts on `ℒ_χ` by `χ(N(x))`. Witness: for `G = 𝔾_a` over `𝔽_3` and `x = 1`, the trace is `ψ(1)`, a primitive cube root of unity; the Lang map `id − F` with the same isotypic part, or `F − id` with the `χ^{−1}`-isotypic part, gives `ψ(−1) = ψ(1)²`. The convention of KATZ01 lines 418–432 (Lang map `x ↦ x − x^q`, monodromy through `ψ`) defines the same sheaf, since `id − F = [−1] ∘ (F − id)`.
- Fourier–Deligne transform. For a finite locally free `E` of constant rank `r` on `S`, `V(E) = Spec_S Sym(E^∨)` is Tau Ceti's total space `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.totalSpaceScheme`, covariant in `E`, whose points over an `S`-scheme `T` are the sections of `E` pulled back to `T`, with projections `pr`, `pr'` from `V(E) ×_S V(E^∨)` and pairing `⟨ , ⟩`. Then `FT_ψ(K) = Rpr'_!(pr^*K ⊗ ⟨ , ⟩^*ℒ_ψ)[r]` (LAU87 Définition (1.2.1.1), LAU87 lines 378–382). Witness on `𝔸¹`: `FT_ψ(Λ_{𝔸¹}) ≅ σ'_*Λ(−1)[−1]`, with trace `−q` at `0'` and `0` elsewhere, matching `−Σ_{x ∈ 𝔽_q} ψ(Tr(x x'))`.
- `[−1]` is the inverse on `V(E)`; `σ`, `σ'` are zero sections. Inversion reads `FT^{E^∨}_ψ ∘ FT^E_ψ ≅ [−1]^*(−r)`, the double dual being identified with `E` by the negative of the canonical map (LAU87 lines 396–404).
- The finite Fourier transform of a function `t` on a finite vector space `W` over `𝔽_q` is `(𝔉_ψ t)(w') = Σ_{w ∈ W} t(w) ψ(Tr_{𝔽_q/𝔽_p} w'(w))`, without normalizing factor or sign. The sheaf transform has trace `(−1)^r 𝔉_ψ(t_K)` (§3.8).
- Henselian traits follow EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.3a; local Galois groups `G_s`, inertia `I_s` and wild inertia `P_s` at a closed point `s` of a curve are those of the henselization at `s`, and their upper-numbering filtrations, breaks and Swan conductors are those of the completion at `s`, through the comparison of §2.7. On the line, `A = Spec k[x]` and `A' = Spec k[x']` with pairing `x x'`, compactified by `D ⊃ A` and `D' ⊃ A'` at `∞` and `∞'` (LAU87 lines 584–605).

## How to read the build

Layer 0 is algebra: nondegenerate additive characters with values in a finite ring and the finite Fourier transform of functions, which is the trace-function shadow of everything later. Layer 1 builds kernel transforms on `D⁺` over a qcqs base, their composition coherence, constructibility and the function–sheaf dictionary through trace functions. Layer 2 constructs Lang torsors and character sheaves, proves their multiplicativity, traces and vanishing cohomology, and specializes to Artin–Schreier and Kummer sheaves; after matching the ramification of a henselian trait with that of its completion and carrying the Euler–Poincaré formula for lisse sheaves on curves to the coefficients used here, it treats the local behaviour of `ℒ_ψ(f)` on the line. Layer 3 defines the transform on a vector bundle as a kernel transform with kernel `⟨ , ⟩^*ℒ_ψ`, proves `FT_! = FT_*` by local acyclicity at infinity, inversion, duality, functoriality, coefficient change, the trace identity and Frobenius exchange. Layer 4 proves perverse `t`-exactness over a field and the induced equivalence of hearts. Layer 5 works on the affine line over a perfect field: the simple perverse sheaves on a curve and their three types on the line, Fourier sheaves, Kummer sheaves and Gauss sums, the local monodromy of the transform and the local Fourier transforms with their tame values.

## Layer 0: additive characters and the finite Fourier transform

### 0.1 Nondegenerate additive characters

Define a nondegenerate additive character of a commutative ring `Λ` as an additive character `ψ : 𝔽_p → Λ^×` such that `ψ(1) − 1` is a unit. Prove: `ψ(a) − 1` is a unit for every `a ∈ 𝔽_p^×`; `p` is a unit of `Λ` and `Φ_p(ψ(1)) = 0` for the `p`-th cyclotomic polynomial; for every finite field `k` of characteristic `p` and `y ∈ k`, `Σ_{x ∈ k} ψ(Tr_{k/𝔽_p}(x y))` equals `|k|` if `y = 0` and `0` otherwise (orthogonality, proved by multiplying by `ψ(Tr(x_0 y)) − 1` for an `x_0` with `Tr(x_0 y) = 1`, which exists by `FiniteField.trace_to_zmod_nondegenerate`). For nonzero `Λ`, a nondegenerate character is primitive in Mathlib's sense, `AddChar.IsPrimitive`: for `a ≠ 0` the value at `1` of `x ↦ ψ(a x)` is `ψ(a)`, and `ψ(a) − 1` is a unit, hence nonzero in a nonzero ring. (KL85 lines 442–446 for the local case.)
*Needs:* Mathlib `AddChar`, `AddChar.IsPrimitive`, `Algebra.trace`, `FiniteField.trace_to_zmod_nondegenerate`; Tau Ceti `Submodule.sum_addChar_dotProduct`, only as the domain-valued case the orthogonality must agree with.

**API.**
- The inverse character `ψ^{−1}` and the scaled characters `a ↦ ψ(c a)` for `c ∈ 𝔽_p^×` are nondegenerate.
- For a ring map `h : Λ → Λ'`, `h ∘ ψ` is nondegenerate (units map to units), so nondegeneracy survives reduction modulo every ideal, in particular modulo every maximal ideal.
- For finite `Λ`, `ψ` is nondegenerate if and only if its reduction modulo every maximal ideal is nontrivial; for local `Λ` of residue characteristic `ℓ ≠ p` this is nontriviality of `ψ`, because the `p`-torsion of `Λ^×` meets the `ℓ`-group `1 + 𝔪` trivially. For local `Λ` of residue characteristic `p` no character is nondegenerate, although nontrivial ones may exist.
- For a finite field `k` of characteristic `p`, the composite `ψ_k = ψ ∘ Tr_{k/𝔽_p}` satisfies the orthogonality above, and when `Spec Λ` is connected (in particular nonempty, so `Λ ≠ 0`) every additive character of `k` with values in `Λ^×` is `x ↦ ψ_k(c x)` for a unique `c ∈ k` (LAU87 (0.2), LAU87 lines 148–149, for `ℚ̄_ℓ`-valued characters); for `Λ = Λ_1 × Λ_2` the pairs `(ψ_{1,k}(c_1 x), ψ_{2,k}(c_2 x))` with `c_1 ≠ c_2` are not of this form.
- The characteristic `N` of a finite `Λ` with a nondegenerate character is prime to `p`.

**Checks.**
- `Λ = ℤ/7`, `p = 3`, `ψ(1) = 2`: `2³ = 8 ≡ 1`, `ψ(1) − 1 = 1` is a unit and `ψ(1) + ψ(2) + ψ(0) = 2 + 4 + 1 = 7 ≡ 0`; a definition demanding only `ψ ≠ 1` agrees here because `ℤ/7` is local.
- `Λ = ℤ/7 × ℤ/7`, `ψ(1) = (2, 1)`: `ψ` is nontrivial but `ψ(1) − 1 = (1, 0)` is not a unit, and `Σ_a ψ(a) = (0, 3) ≠ 0`; nontriviality is the wrong condition for non-local rings.
- `Λ = ℤ/5`, `p = 3`: `3 ∤ 4`, so `(ℤ/5)^×` has no element of order `3` and no nondegenerate character exists; the degenerate case is an empty class, not a theorem about the trivial character.
- `Λ = ℤ/49`, `p = 3`, `ψ(1) = 18`: `18³ = 5832 = 119 · 49 + 1`, and `18 − 1 = 17` is a unit mod `49`; a nontrivial character into a non-reduced local ring is nondegenerate.
- `Λ = ℤ/9`, `p = 3`, `ψ(1) = 4`: `4³ = 64 = 7 · 9 + 1`, so `ψ` is a nontrivial character into a local ring, but `ψ(1) − 1 = 3` is not a unit and `ψ(0) + ψ(1) + ψ(2) = 1 + 4 + 16 = 21 ≡ 3 ≠ 0`; nontriviality suffices only when the residue characteristic differs from `p`.
- `Λ = 0`: `ψ(1) − 1 = 0` is a unit of the zero ring, so the unique character is nondegenerate, but every character into the zero ring is trivial, so it is not primitive in Mathlib's sense and every `c ∈ k` gives the same character of `k`; the primitivity clause needs `Λ ≠ 0` and the classification needs `Spec Λ` connected, hence nonempty.

### 0.2 The finite Fourier transform

For a finite field `k` of characteristic `p`, a finite-dimensional `k`-vector space `W` with dual `W^∨ = Module.Dual k W`, a commutative ring `Λ` and a nondegenerate `ψ`, define `𝔉_ψ : (W → Λ) → (W^∨ → Λ)` by `(𝔉_ψ t)(w') = Σ_{w ∈ W} t(w) ψ_k(w'(w))`. Prove inversion `𝔉_ψ^{W^∨}(𝔉_ψ^W t)(w) = |W| · t(−w)`, identifying `W^{∨∨}` with `W` canonically; Plancherel `Σ_{w'} 𝔉_ψ t_1(w') 𝔉_ψ t_2(w') = |W| Σ_w t_1(w) t_2(−w)`; the convolution identity `𝔉_ψ(t_1 * t_2) = 𝔉_ψ t_1 · 𝔉_ψ t_2` for `(t_1 * t_2)(w) = Σ_{u + v = w} t_1(u) t_2(v)`; for a linear `f : W_1 → W_2` and `f_! t(w_2) = Σ_{f(w_1) = w_2} t(w_1)`, `𝔉_ψ(f_! t) = 𝔉_ψ(t) ∘ f^∨`; translation `𝔉_ψ(t(· − e)) = ψ_k(· (e)) · 𝔉_ψ t`; and `𝔉_{ψ^{−1}} t = (𝔉_ψ t) ∘ [−1]`. (LAU87 lines 383–391 for the definition; the identities are the function forms of LAU87 (1.2.2.1)–(1.2.2.8), LAU87 lines 401–471.)
*Needs:* §0.1; Mathlib `Module.Dual`, finite sums; Tau Ceti `Submodule.sum_addChar_dotProduct`, only as the domain-valued case the subspace identity must agree with.

**API.**
- `𝔉_ψ` is `Λ`-linear, and multiplication by `|W|` is invertible on functions because `p` is a unit, so `𝔉_ψ` is a linear equivalence with inverse `|W|^{−1} [−1]^* 𝔉_ψ^{W^∨}`.
- `𝔉_ψ` of the indicator of `0` is the constant `1`; `𝔉_ψ` of the constant `1` is `|W|` times the indicator of `0`; for a subspace `U ⊂ W`, `𝔉_ψ` of the indicator of `U` is `|U|` times the indicator of `U^⊥`.
- Compatibility with base change of finite fields: for `k ⊂ k'`, `𝔉_{ψ}` on `W ⊗ k'` uses `ψ_{k'} = ψ_k ∘ Tr_{k'/k}`.
- For `Λ = ℂ`, `k = 𝔽_p`, `W = ZMod p` and `ψ = ZMod.stdAddChar`, `𝔉_ψ Φ (k) = ZMod.dft Φ (−k)`, and the inversion above is then Mathlib's `ZMod.dft_dft`.

**Checks.**
- `W = 𝔽_3`, `Λ = ℤ/7`, `ψ(1) = 2`, `t` the indicator of `{1}`: `𝔉_ψ t (w') = ψ(w')`, so `𝔉_ψ t = (1, 2, 4)` at `w' = 0, 1, 2`; applying `𝔉_ψ` again at `w = 2` gives `1 + 2 · 4 + 4 · 2 = 17 ≡ 3 = |W| · t(−2)`, while at `w = 1` it gives `1 + 2·2 + 4·4 = 21 ≡ 0`. A transform without the sign `t(−w)` would put the mass at `1`.
- `W = 0`: `𝔉_ψ` is the identity and `|W| = 1`; a normalization by `|W|^{−1/2}` would be undefined in `ℤ/7`.
- With the degenerate `ψ(1) = (2, 1)` into `ℤ/7 × ℤ/7`, the second component of `𝔉_ψ` is `t ↦ Σ t`, which is not injective for `|W| > 1`: inversion needs nondegeneracy.
- Comparison with Mathlib: for `N = p = 3` and `Φ` the indicator of `1`, `ZMod.dft Φ k = stdAddChar(−k)`, while `𝔉_ψ Φ (k) = stdAddChar(k)`; the two differ by `k ↦ −k`, which is the stated identity and not an equality of functions.
- Comparison with Tau Ceti's code orthogonality `Submodule.sum_addChar_dotProduct`: `W = 𝔽_3²` with its dual identified by the dot product, `Λ = ℤ/7`, `ψ(1) = 2`, `U` the line spanned by `(1, 1)`. Then `𝔉_ψ(1_U)(w') = Σ_{s ∈ 𝔽_3} ψ(s(w'_1 + w'_2))` is `3` on `U^⊥ = {(0, 0), (1, 2), (2, 1)}` and `1 + 2 + 4 = 7 ≡ 0` elsewhere, the value of the code sum for `C = U`. Since `(1, 1) · (1, 1) = 2 ≠ 0`, `U ≠ U^⊥`, and a formula with `1_U` in place of `1_{U^⊥}` would give `3` at `(1, 1)`, where the sum is `ψ(0) + ψ(2) + ψ(1) = 0`.

### Examples

For `k = 𝔽_5`, `W = k`, `Λ = ℤ/11`, `ψ(1) = 3` (order `5` in `(ℤ/11)^×`): the quadratic function `t(w) = ψ(w²)` has `𝔉_ψ t(0) = Σ_w ψ(w²) = 1 + 2·3 + 2·3⁴ = 1 + 6 + 162 = 169 ≡ 4`, and `4² = 16 ≡ 5 = |k|·(−1/5)`, the Legendre symbol `(−1/5) = 1`; this is the quadratic Gauss-sum identity reduced mod `11`.

## Layer 1: kernel transforms and trace functions

### 1.1 Kernel transforms

Let `S` be a qcqs scheme, `Λ` a torsion ring, `X`, `Y` separated finitely presented `S`-schemes, `p_X`, `p_Y` the projections of `X ×_S Y`, `𝒦 ∈ D⁺(X ×_S Y, Λ)` of finite Tor amplitude and `a ∈ ℤ`. Define the kernel transform `Φ_𝒦 : D⁺(X, Λ) → D⁺(Y, Λ)`, `Φ_𝒦(K) = Rp_{Y!}(p_X^*K ⊗^L 𝒦)[a]`, a triangulated functor. Prove base change: for `g : S' → S` with base changes `g_X`, `g_Y`, the canonical map `g_Y^* Φ_𝒦(K) ≅ Φ_{𝒦'}(g_X^* K)` with `𝒦'` the pullback of `𝒦`, an isomorphism by proper base change on the compactified factor; and the relative Künneth isomorphism `R(f ×_S g)_!(K ⊠_S L) ≅ Rf_!K ⊠_S Rg_!L` for separated finitely presented `f`, `g` over `S` and `L` of finite Tor amplitude, from base change and the projection formula. (Base change: LAU87 Proposition (1.2.2.9), LAU87 lines 472–474, and BRY86 Proposition 9.4 1), BRY86 lines 3449–3450 and 3460, both for the Fourier kernel and proved by proper base change; BRY86 Définition 9.1, BRY86 line 3394, for the Fourier kernel as the motivating instance; Künneth: the compact-support Künneth formula as used at LAU87 lines 459–461. Kernel transforms at this generality are this roadmap's own.)
*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§G.1–G.3, 0.7–0.8, F.1, F.2.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.1–0.4; gap:projection-formula-qcqs (on Noetherian `S` with `Dᵇ_ctf` inputs, EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.2.7 suffices).

**API.**
- A morphism of kernels `𝒦_1 → 𝒦_2` induces a natural transformation `Φ_{𝒦_1} → Φ_{𝒦_2}`, functorially, and distinguished triangles of kernels give triangles of functors.
- For `f : X → Y` the graph kernel `(id, f)_!Λ` on `X ×_S Y` (shift `0`) gives `Φ ≅ Rf_!`; for `g : Y → X` the kernel `(g, id)_!Λ` on `X ×_S Y` gives `Φ ≅ g^*`; for `M ∈ D⁺(Y, Λ)` of finite Tor amplitude, `𝒦 ⊗^L p_Y^*M` is again a kernel and `Φ_{𝒦 ⊗^L p_Y^*M} ≅ Φ_𝒦(−) ⊗^L M` by the projection formula.
- Base-change isomorphisms satisfy the identity and composition laws for `S'' → S' → S`.
- Finite Tor amplitude of `𝒦` is preserved by base change and by `Rp_!` along separated finitely presented maps.

**Checks.**
- `X = Y = S`, `𝒦 = Λ_S`, `a = 0`: `Φ_𝒦` is the identity of `D⁺(S, Λ)`; a definition with `Rp_{Y*}` agrees here but not for `S = Spec k̄`, `X = 𝔸¹_S`, `Y = S`, `𝒦 = Λ`, where `Φ(Λ) = Λ(−1)[−2]` (compact support) versus `Λ` (ordinary).
- `Λ = ℤ/4`, `X = Y = S = Spec k` with `k` separably closed and `𝒦 = ℤ/2` (infinite Tor amplitude over `ℤ/4`): `Φ_𝒦(ℤ/2) = ℤ/2 ⊗^L_{ℤ/4} ℤ/2` has cohomology `ℤ/2` in every degree `≤ 0` and leaves `D⁺`; the Tor hypothesis is needed. The same kernel arises by tensoring `𝒦 = Λ` with `p_Y^*M` for `M = ℤ/2`, so the tensoring of the API needs `M` of finite Tor amplitude.
- Graph kernels: for the closed immersion `i : {0} → 𝔸¹_k`, the graph kernel of `i` sends `Λ` to the skyscraper `i_*Λ`, and the transposed one sends `Λ_{𝔸¹}` to `Λ` on the point.

### 1.2 Composition of kernels

For `𝒦 ∈ D⁺(X ×_S Y)` and `𝒦' ∈ D⁺(Y ×_S Z)` of finite Tor amplitude with shifts `a`, `a'`, define `𝒦' ∘ 𝒦 = Rp_{XZ!}(p_{XY}^*𝒦 ⊗^L p_{YZ}^*𝒦')` with shift `a + a'`. Construct the composition isomorphism `c_{𝒦',𝒦} : Φ_{𝒦'} ∘ Φ_𝒦 ≅ Φ_{𝒦'∘𝒦}` from base change, composition of `Rf_!` and the projection formula, and prove: it is natural in `𝒦` and `𝒦'`; for three composable kernels the two composites `Φ_{𝒦''}Φ_{𝒦'}Φ_𝒦 ≅ Φ_{𝒦''∘𝒦'∘𝒦}` agree with the associator `(𝒦''∘𝒦')∘𝒦 ≅ 𝒦''∘(𝒦'∘𝒦)` of kernels; the diagonal kernel `Δ_!Λ` with shift `0` is a two-sided unit, with unitors compatible with `c`; and `c` is compatible with base change along every `S' → S`. (LAU87 lines 413–417 and 440–444 for the pattern of proof: the proofs of (1.2.2.1) and (1.2.2.4) compose a Fourier kernel with a Fourier or a graph kernel by proper base change and the projection formula; the coherence statements are this roadmap's own.)
*Needs:* §1.1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8 (composition and refinement coherence of `Rf_!`), §F.2.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.3–0.4; gap:projection-formula-qcqs.

**API.**
- The composite kernel `𝒦' ∘ 𝒦` has finite Tor amplitude, is functorial in both kernels and commutes with base change along `S' → S`.
- Composition with graph kernels: `𝒦 ∘ Γ_g` and `Γ_h ∘ 𝒦` are the pullback of `𝒦` along `g × id` and the compact direct image along `id × h`.
- The associator of composite kernels satisfies the pentagon identity for four composable kernels.

**Checks.**
- Graph kernels compose to the graph kernel of the composite, and `c` becomes the composition isomorphism `Rg_! Rf_! ≅ R(gf)_!` of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8; a construction with an arbitrary sign or choice would fail this on `f = g = id`.
- For `𝒦 = Δ_!Λ` the left unitor composed with `c` is the identity of `Φ_{𝒦'}` for every `𝒦'`.
- Shifts add: `Φ_{Λ[1]}` on `X = Y = S` composed with itself is `[2]`, and `c` carries the Koszul sign of exchanging the two shifts, so that associativity holds for three shifted kernels.

### 1.3 Constructibility

For Noetherian `S`, finite `Λ` with characteristic invertible on `S` and `𝒦 ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(X ×_S Y, Λ)`, prove that `Φ_𝒦` sends `Dᵇ_ctf(X, Λ)` to `Dᵇ_ctf(Y, Λ)`, and `𝒦' ∘ 𝒦 ∈ Dᵇ_ctf`. Under change of coefficients `Λ → Λ'`, `Φ_𝒦` on `Dᵇ_ctf(X, Λ)` commutes with derived scalar extension. (KL85 §2.1; the finiteness theorems for constructible complexes that LAU87 lines 172–182 rely on.)

**Checks.**

### 1.4 The function–sheaf dictionary for kernels

Let `X`, `Y` be separated of finite type over `𝔽_q`, `Λ` finite of characteristic prime to `p`, `K ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(X, Λ)` and `𝒦 ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(X ×_{𝔽_q} Y, Λ)`, with trace functions `t_{K,n}` and `t_{𝒦,n}` as in the Conventions. Prove, for every `n ≥ 1` and `y ∈ Y(𝔽_{qⁿ})`, `t_{Φ_𝒦 K, n}(y) = (−1)^a Σ_{x ∈ X(𝔽_{qⁿ})} t_{K,n}(x) t_{𝒦,n}(x, y)`, and for `𝒦' ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(Y ×_{𝔽_q} Z, Λ)` the matrix-product formula `t_{𝒦'∘𝒦, n}(x, z) = Σ_{y ∈ Y(𝔽_{qⁿ})} t_{𝒦,n}(x, y) t_{𝒦',n}(y, z)` for the composite kernel of §1.2 (the shifts `a`, `a'` are separate data and contribute the sign `(−1)^{a+a'}` to the composite transform). The same holds for `E`-coefficients. Route: the pullback, tensor and Fubini laws of local terms (InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups §IG.0.T2, and §IG.0.T3 for `E`) applied to `p_X^*K ⊗^L 𝒦` and to `Rp_{Y!}`, and `t_{M[a]} = (−1)^a t_M` from the additivity of perfect-complex traces. (LAU87 (1.1.1.1)–(1.1.1.4), LAU87 lines 249–262, are the laws used; LAU87 Théorème (1.2.1.2), LAU87 lines 389–391, applies them to the Fourier kernel in the same way. The statement for arbitrary kernels is this roadmap's own.)
*Needs:* InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups §§IG.0.T2–IG.0.T3; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.10.3 (`PerfectComplex.lefschetzNumber`); §§1.1–1.3.

**Checks.**
- Graph kernel: for `f : X → Y` and `𝒦 = (id, f)_!Λ`, `a = 0`, `t_{𝒦,n}(x, y)` is `1` when `y = f(x)` and `0` otherwise, and the dictionary returns the Fubini identity of §IG.0.T2. For `f : 𝔸¹ → Spec 𝔽_q` and `K = Λ`, it reads `Σ_{x ∈ 𝔽_q} 1 = q = t_{Λ(−1)[−2]}`; a trace function built on arithmetic Frobenius would give `q^{−1}` on the right.
- Shift sign: `X = Y = Spec 𝔽_q`, `𝒦 = Λ`, `a = 1`: `Φ_𝒦(K) = K[1]` has trace `−t_K`, which is `(−1)^a t_K`; a dictionary without the sign gives `+t_K`.
- Perfect complexes: `Λ = ℤ/4`, `X = Y = Spec 𝔽_q`, `𝒦 = Λ`, `a = 0`, `K = [ℤ/4 —2→ ℤ/4]` in degrees `0`, `1` with trivial Frobenius: `t_{Φ_𝒦 K} = t_K = 1 − 1 = 0`, although `H⁰ = H¹ = ℤ/2`, which is not free over `ℤ/4`, so a trace on cohomology modules is undefined; the dictionary is stated with Lefschetz numbers of perfect complexes.

## Layer 2: character sheaves

In §§2.1–2.4, `G` is a smooth connected commutative group scheme of finite type over `𝔽_q`, written additively, `F` its `q`-Frobenius and `A = G(𝔽_q)`. In §§2.1–2.6 and 2.8, `Λ` is a finite ring (or `O_E` by realization); §2.7 states its own coefficients, which include `E` and `ℚ̄_ℓ`.

### 2.1 The Lang torsor

Prove that the Lang map `L = F − id : G → G` is a finite étale `A`-torsor for the translation action of `A ⊂ G` on the source: `L` is a homomorphism; its differential is `−id` because `F` kills invariant differentials, so `L` is étale; its image is an open subgroup, hence `G` by connectedness, so `L` is surjective (this is ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.3.7 (a) on `𝔽̄_q`-points); and its kernel is the constant group `A`, which gives `G ×_{L,G,L} G ≅ G × A`. For `x ∈ G(𝔽_{qⁿ})`, the geometric Frobenius `Frob_x` acts on the fibre `L^{−1}(x)(𝔽̄_q)` as translation by `−N(x)`, where `N = 1 + F + ⋯ + F^{n−1} : G(𝔽_{qⁿ}) → A` is the norm, because `Fⁿ(t) = t + N(x)` on that fibre. (LAU87 lines 299–309, recalling SGA45-ST.)
*Needs:* ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.3.7; Tau Ceti `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.qPowerFrobenius` (the `q`-Frobenius `F` of `G`, a homomorphism of group schemes over `𝔽_q` because iterated absolute Frobenius commutes with the group law, `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.iterateFrobenius_naturality`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.2; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.GrpObj`, `FiniteField.norm_surjective`; Tau Ceti `MonoidHom.commutatorMap_prod_range` for the telescoping identity on points.

**API.**
- For a homomorphism `φ : G → G'` of such groups, `L_{G'} ∘ φ = φ ∘ L_G`, so the Lang torsor of `G'` pulled back along `φ` is the push-out of that of `G` along `φ|_A`.
- `Fⁿ − id = (F − id) ∘ (1 + F + ⋯ + F^{n−1})`: the `qⁿ`-Lang torsor of `G ⊗ 𝔽_{qⁿ}` maps to the base change of the `q`-Lang torsor, inducing the norm `G(𝔽_{qⁿ}) → A` on groups. On `𝔽̄_q`-points this is the telescoping identity of Tau Ceti's `MonoidHom.commutatorMap_prod_range`, written there multiplicatively for the Lang map `t ↦ t / F(t)`, which is `−L`.
- The norm `G(𝔽_{qⁿ}) → G(𝔽_q)` is surjective.
- `L` commutes with the `q`-Frobenius, which gives the Frobenius structure of §2.2.

**Checks.**
- `G = 𝔾_a`: `L(t) = t^q − t`, kernel `𝔽_q`, and for `x ∈ 𝔽_q` the fibre is `{t : t^q − t = x}`; with `q = 3`, `x = 1` the arithmetic Frobenius sends `t` to `t + 1`.
- `G = 𝔾_m`: `L(t) = t^{q−1}`, kernel `μ_{q−1} = 𝔽_q^×`; the Lang torsor is the Kummer cover of order `q − 1`.
- Disconnected non-example: for the constant group `ℤ/2` over `𝔽_q`, `F = id` and `L = 0` is not surjective (ReductiveGroupsPartII *lang_not_connected* for `μ_2`, `q` odd).
- `G = 0`: `L` is the identity of a point and `A` is trivial.

### 2.2 Character sheaves

For a character `χ : A → Λ^×`, define `ℒ_χ` on `G` as the torsor–character sheaf of the Lang torsor with `χ^{−1}` (gap:torsor-character-sheaf), which is the `χ`-isotypic part of `L_*Λ` for `(a·s)(t) = s(t + a)`. For an `𝔽_q`-scheme `X` and `f : X → G` put `ℒ_χ(f) = f^*ℒ_χ`. Prove: `ℒ_χ` is lisse of rank one with monodromy through `A`, hence finite; a rigidification `ℒ_χ|_{0} ≅ Λ`; the multiplicativity `m^*ℒ_χ ≅ pr_1^*ℒ_χ ⊗ pr_2^*ℒ_χ` compatible with the rigidification, associative and commutative; `ℒ_χ ⊗ ℒ_{χ'} ≅ ℒ_{χχ'}`; `ℒ_χ^∨ ≅ ℒ_{χ^{−1}}` and `[−1]^*ℒ_χ ≅ ℒ_{χ^{−1}}`; `ℒ_1 ≅ Λ`; `ℒ_χ|_{G ⊗ 𝔽_{qⁿ}} ≅ ℒ_{χ ∘ N}`; `φ^*ℒ_{χ'} ≅ ℒ_{χ' ∘ φ}` for homomorphisms `φ : G → G'`; `ℒ_χ(f + g) ≅ ℒ_χ(f) ⊗ ℒ_χ(g)` for `f, g : X → G`; and a canonical Frobenius isomorphism `F^*ℒ_χ ≅ ℒ_χ`. (LAU87 lines 310–312 for the construction as a lisse sheaf of rank one; LAU87 (1.1.3.1)–(1.1.3.3) and (1.1.3.6), LAU87 lines 313–339, for the rigidification, the multiplicativity compatible with it, `ℒ_χ^∨ ≅ ℒ_{χ^{−1}}`, the base extension and `ℒ_χ(f)`; KATZ01 lines 426–440 for the Artin–Schreier case. The other laws are derived here. Associativity and commutativity: the two isomorphisms to be compared are isomorphisms between rank-one lisse sheaves on `G × G × G` (resp. `G × G`), which is connected because `G` is connected with a rational point, so they differ by a constant unit of `Λ`, and both are compatible with the rigidification, so that unit is `1`. `ℒ_χ ⊗ ℒ_{χ'} ≅ ℒ_{χχ'}` is the tensor law of gap:torsor-character-sheaf, and `ℒ_1 ≅ Λ` its push-out law along `A → 1`. `φ^*ℒ_{χ'} ≅ ℒ_{χ' ∘ φ}` is its pullback and push-out laws applied to the §2.1 API, by which the Lang torsor of `G'` pulled back along `φ` is the push-out of that of `G` along `φ|_A`. `[−1]^*ℒ_χ ≅ ℒ_{χ^{−1}}` is the case `φ = [−1]`, and `F^*ℒ_χ ≅ ℒ_χ` the case `φ = F`, which is the identity on `A`. `ℒ_χ(f + g) ≅ ℒ_χ(f) ⊗ ℒ_χ(g)` is the multiplicativity pulled back along `(f, g) : X → G × G`.)
*Needs:* §2.1; gap:torsor-character-sheaf; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1–F.0.2, F.0.4.

**API.**
- Translation: for `a ∈ G(𝔽_q)`, `t_a^*ℒ_χ ≅ ℒ_χ ⊗ π^*(ℒ_χ|_a)`, where `ℒ_χ|_a` is the line on `Spec 𝔽_q` on which `Frob` acts by `χ(a)`.
- Coefficient change along `h : Λ → Λ'` carries `ℒ_χ` to `ℒ_{h ∘ χ}`.
- For an `O_E`-valued `χ`, the reductions form a normalized adic system (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.1).
- `ℒ_χ(0) ≅ Λ_X` and `ℒ_χ(−f) ≅ ℒ_χ(f)^∨`.

**Checks.**
- Convention witness: `G = 𝔾_a` over `𝔽_3`, `Λ = ℤ/7`, `χ = ψ` with `ψ(1) = 2`: `Frob_1` acts on `ℒ_ψ` at `x = 1` by `2`, not by `ψ(−1) = 4`; the opposite Lang map or the `χ^{−1}`-isotypic part gives `4`.
- `G = 𝔾_m` over `𝔽_5`, `Λ = ℤ/3`, `χ` the quadratic character: `ℒ_χ` is the sheaf of the cover `t² = x`, with trace `+1` at `x = 1, 4` and `−1` at `x = 2, 3`.
- `χ = 1`: `ℒ_1` is constant, whereas `L_*Λ` has rank `|A|`; the isotypic part, not the whole pushforward, is the character sheaf.

### 2.3 Traces of character sheaves

Prove that for `x ∈ G(𝔽_{qⁿ})`, `Frob_x` acts on `ℒ_χ` by `χ(N_{𝔽_{qⁿ}/𝔽_q}(x))`, so `t_{ℒ_χ, n} = χ ∘ N`, and `t_{ℒ_χ(f), n} = χ ∘ N ∘ f`. The proof is the fibre computation of §2.1. (LAU87 (1.1.3.3), LAU87 lines 328–330; LAU87 Remarque (1.1.3.5), LAU87 lines 332–335.)

### 2.4 Vanishing of cohomology

Prove that if the elements `χ(a) − 1`, `a ∈ A`, generate the unit ideal of `Λ`, then `RΓ_c(G_{𝔽̄_q}, ℒ_χ) = 0`, and, when moreover the characteristic of `Λ` is prime to `p`, `RΓ(G_{𝔽̄_q}, ℒ_χ) = 0`. Route: by multiplicativity and the automorphism `(x, y) ↦ (x + y, y)` of `G × G`, `RΓ_c(G, ℒ_χ) ⊗ ℒ_χ ≅ RΓ_c(G, ℒ_χ) ⊗ Λ` as complexes on `G_{𝔽̄_q}`; the Lang torsor is connected, so its monodromy `π_1(G_{𝔽̄_q}) → A` is surjective, and comparing the two actions on cohomology gives `(χ(a) − 1) Hⁱ = 0` for all `a`; the same argument with `Rpr_{2*}` and ordinary Künneth, which rests on smooth base change and so on the characteristic of `Λ` being prime to `p`, handles `RΓ`. Deduce `Σ_{x ∈ G(𝔽_{qⁿ})} χ(N x) = 0` (orthogonality) by the trace formula when the characteristic of `Λ` is prime to `p`; the identity holds for every finite `Λ`, since translating by a `y` of norm `a` (the norm is onto, §2.1) multiplies the sum by `χ(a)`. (LAU87 (1.1.3.4), LAU87 lines 330–331, recalling SGA45-ST.)
*Needs:* §§2.1–2.3; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.8 and F.2.1 (`Rf_!` and its base change for arbitrary torsion coefficients) and gap:projection-formula-qcqs, for the vanishing of `RΓ_c` over every finite `Λ`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.2.4, F.2.6–F.2.7, F.3 and InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups §IG.0.T2, for `RΓ` and the trace formula when the characteristic of `Λ` is prime to `p`.

**Checks.**
- `G = 𝔾_a`, `ψ` nondegenerate: `RΓ_c(𝔸¹_{𝔽̄_p}, ℒ_ψ) = 0`; the trivial character instead has `H²_c = Λ(−1)`, so the hypothesis is needed.
- `Λ = ℤ/7 × ℤ/7`, `ψ(1) = (2, 1)`: the unit-ideal hypothesis fails and `RΓ_c(𝔸¹_{𝔽̄_3}, ℒ_ψ) = (0, (ℤ/7)(−1)[−2])`, nonzero; nontriviality of `χ` alone does not suffice.
- `G = 𝔾_m` over `𝔽_5`, quadratic `χ` into `ℤ/3`: `χ(2) − 1 = −2 = 1` is a unit, and `Σ_{x ∈ 𝔽_5^×} χ(x) = 1 − 1 − 1 + 1 = 0`.
- Coefficients of characteristic `p`: `G = 𝔾_m` over `𝔽_4`, `Λ = 𝔽_4`, `χ : 𝔽_4^× → 𝔽_4^×` the identity, so `χ(ω) − 1 = ω²` is a unit and `RΓ_c(𝔾_{m,𝔽̄_2}, ℒ_χ) = 0`. But `ℒ_χ` is an eigensheaf of the pushforward of `𝔽_4` along `t ↦ t³`, so `H¹(𝔾_{m,𝔽̄_2}, ℒ_χ)` is an eigenspace of `μ_3` acting by `t ↦ ζ t` on `H¹(𝔾_{m,𝔽̄_2}, 𝔽_4) = (𝔽̄_2[t, t^{−1}]/{g² − g}) ⊗_{𝔽_2} 𝔽_4`; the classes of `c t`, `c ∈ 𝔽̄_2`, are independent modulo `g² − g` (a relation `c t = g² − g` forces `g` to have infinitely many terms unless `c = 0`), `μ_3` acts on their span `𝔽̄_2 t` by multiplication by `ζ`, and `𝔽̄_2 t ⊗_{𝔽_2} 𝔽_4 ≅ 𝔽̄_2 ⊕ 𝔽̄_2` puts an infinite-dimensional piece in each of the two nontrivial eigenspaces. So `RΓ ≠ 0`, and the characteristic hypothesis is needed for `RΓ`.

### 2.5 Artin–Schreier sheaves

For a nondegenerate `ψ : 𝔽_p → Λ^×`, let `ℒ_ψ` be the character sheaf of `𝔾_{a,𝔽_p}` and, for any `𝔽_p`-scheme `X` and `f ∈ Γ(X, O_X)`, `ℒ_ψ(f) = f^*ℒ_ψ`. Prove: `ℒ_ψ(f + g) ≅ ℒ_ψ(f) ⊗ ℒ_ψ(g)`; `ℒ_ψ(c f) ≅ ℒ_{ψ(c ·)}(f)` for `c ∈ 𝔽_p`; `ℒ_ψ(f^p) ≅ ℒ_ψ(f)` and `ℒ_ψ(f^p − f) ≅ Λ_X` canonically; for `X` over `𝔽_q`, `ℒ_ψ` on `𝔾_{a,𝔽_q}` is the character sheaf of `ψ ∘ Tr_{𝔽_q/𝔽_p}`, through the map of Lang torsors `t ↦ t + t^p + ⋯ + t^{q/p}`; and `t_{ℒ_ψ(f), n}(x) = ψ(Tr_{𝔽_{qⁿ}/𝔽_p} f(x))`. (LAU87 lines 343–345 and 370–371 for the definition; KATZ01 lines 430–440 for `ℒ_ψ(f)`, its trace function and `ℒ_ψ(0) ≅ Λ_X`. The laws are the §2.2 laws for `G = 𝔾_{a,𝔽_p}`, `A = 𝔽_p`. Additivity is `ℒ_χ(f + g) ≅ ℒ_χ(f) ⊗ ℒ_χ(g)`. `ℒ_ψ(c f) ≅ ℒ_{ψ(c ·)}(f)` is `φ^*ℒ_{χ'} ≅ ℒ_{χ' ∘ φ}` for `φ = [c]`. `ℒ_ψ(f^p − f) ≅ Λ_X` is the same law for the Lang map `φ = L`, `L(t) = t^p − t`, which vanishes on `A`: `f^p − f = L ∘ f` and `L^*ℒ_ψ ≅ ℒ_1 ≅ Λ`; then `ℒ_ψ(f^p) ≅ ℒ_ψ(f)` by additivity. The identification over `𝔽_q` is the base-extension law `ℒ_χ|_{G ⊗ 𝔽_q} ≅ ℒ_{χ ∘ N}` (LAU87 (1.1.3.3), LAU87 lines 328–329), the norm of `𝔾_a` being `Tr_{𝔽_q/𝔽_p}` and the map of Lang torsors that of the §2.1 API, `t^q − t = L(t + t^p + ⋯ + t^{q/p})`.)
*Needs:* §§2.1–2.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§2.9, 2.11.

**API.**

### 3.2 The transform

Define `FT_ψ = FT_{ψ,!} : D⁺(V, Λ) → D⁺(V', Λ)` as the kernel transform with kernel `ℒ_ψ⟨ , ⟩` and shift `r`, and `FT_{ψ,*}(K) = Rpr'_*(pr^*K ⊗ ℒ_ψ⟨ , ⟩)[r]` with its forget-supports map `FT_{ψ,!} → FT_{ψ,*}` (LAU87 Définition (1.2.1.1), LAU87 lines 378–382; KL85 (2.1.1)–(2.1.2), KL85 lines 372–377). Prove `FT_ψ(σ_*M) ≅ π'^*M[r]` for `M ∈ D⁺(S, Λ)`: `pr^*σ_*M ≅ (σ × id)_*π'^*M` by proper base change, then the projection formula for the closed immersion `σ × id` and the restriction `Λ` of the kernel to `σ(S) ×_S V'` (§3.1); for `M = Λ` this is the case `s = 0` of LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.1), LAU87 lines 477–480. Prove `FT_{ψ^{−1}} ≅ [−1]^* ∘ FT_ψ`, from `ℒ_{ψ^{−1}}(f) ≅ ℒ_ψ(−f)` (§2.5), `⟨e, −e'⟩ = −⟨e, e'⟩` (§3.1) and base change along the automorphism `[−1]` of `V'`.
*Needs:* §§1.1, 2.5, 3.1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8.

**API.**
- `FT_ψ` is triangulated and commutes with shifts and Tate twists.
- `FT_ψ(K ⊗ π^*M) ≅ FT_ψ(K) ⊗ π'^*M` for `M ∈ D⁺(S, Λ)` of finite Tor amplitude.
- `FT_{ψ(c·)} ≅ [c]^* ∘ FT_ψ` for `c ∈ 𝔽_p^×`, where `[c]` is multiplication by `c` on `V'`.
- `FT_ψ` is the kernel transform of §1.1 with kernel `ℒ_ψ⟨ , ⟩` on `V ×_S V'` and shift `r`.

**Checks.**
- `r = 0`: `FT_ψ` is the identity.
- On `𝔸¹_{𝔽_q}`: `FT_ψ(i_{0*}Λ) = Λ_{𝔸¹}[1]`, trace `−1` everywhere, equal to `−Σ_x δ_0(x) ψ(x x')`.
- With the trivial (degenerate) character, `FT(K) = π'^*Rπ_!K[r]`, which forgets everything but `Rπ_!K`.

### 3.3 Local acyclicity at infinity and `FT_! = FT_*`

Let `j × 1 : 𝔸¹ ×_{𝔽_p} 𝔸¹ → ℙ¹ ×_{𝔽_p} 𝔸¹` be the inclusion and `ℒ = (j × 1)_!ℒ_ψ(x y)`. Prove: the projection `ℙ¹ ×_{𝔽_p} 𝔸¹ → ℙ¹` is universally locally acyclic relative to `ℒ` in the sense of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.2.3, and its strict-local comparison maps remain isomorphisms after tensoring `ℒ` with any `Λ`-module (strong local acyclicity) (KL85 Théorème (2.4.4), KL85 lines 567–568; LAU87 Théorème (1.3.1.2), LAU87 lines 526–529). Deduce that for every qcqs `𝔽_p`-scheme `S`, every `E` and every `K ∈ D⁺(V, Λ)` the forget-supports map `FT_{ψ,!}(K) → FT_{ψ,*}(K)` is an isomorphism (KL85 Théorème (2.4.1), KL85 lines 447–449; LAU87 Théorème (1.3.1.1), LAU87 lines 521–525). Route (KL85 lines 450–554): Zariski-locally `E = O_S^r`; the map factors variable by variable, reducing to `r = 1`; it is enough to treat `j_!(pr_1^*(−) ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x y)) → Rj_*(…)` over `∞ × 𝔸¹`; translation `y ↦ y + a` reduces from `∞ × 0` to `∞ × 𝔾_m`; there the Artin–Schreier cover `T^p − T = x y`, finite Galois with group `𝔽_p`, totally ramified over `∞ × 𝔾_m`, with smooth total space, decomposes its pushforward of `Λ` as `Λ ⊕ ⊕_{ψ' ≠ 1} (j × 1)_!ℒ_{ψ'}(x y)` (KL85 Lemme (2.4.2), KL85 lines 514–521), and near `∞ × 𝔾_m` the composite of the cover with `pr_1` factors as a radicial surjection followed by a smooth map, so smooth base change and topological invariance apply.
*Needs:* §§2.5, 3.2; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.7, F.0.4, F.2.2, F.2.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.11; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.totalSpaceFreeIso` and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.totalSpaceSchemeBiprodIso` for the reduction to `E = O_S^r` and to `r = 1`.

**Checks.**
- `K = Λ_V` on `S = Spec k̄`, `r = 1`: `FT_!(Λ) = σ'_*Λ(−1)[−1]` by proper base change, and the theorem gives the same for `FT_*`, although the ordinary cohomology of the fibre of `pr'` over `0'` is `RΓ(𝔸¹, Λ)[1] = Λ[1]`; `Rpr'_*` is not computed on fibres, and a fibrewise definition of `FT_*` would put `Λ[1]` at `0'`.
- The constant kernel `Λ` (trivial `ψ`) fails: `Rpr'_!Λ = Λ(−1)[−2]` while `Rpr'_*Λ = Λ`; nondegeneracy is used.
- On the Artin–Schreier cover for `p = 2`, `T² − T = x y` has the single nontrivial `ψ'`, and the decomposition reads `f_*Λ = Λ ⊕ (j × 1)_!ℒ_ψ(x y)` when `2` is a unit of `Λ`.

### 3.4 Inversion

Prove: `Rpr_!ℒ_ψ⟨ , ⟩ ≅ σ_*Λ(−r)[−2r]` on `V` (fibres off the zero section vanish by §2.4 and Künneth, and over the zero section `Rπ_!Λ = Λ(−r)[−2r]` by the projective-bundle formula of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.9 for `ℙ¹`, localization and Künneth); `FT_ψ(π^*M[r]) ≅ σ'_*M(−r)` for `M ∈ D⁺(S, Λ)` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.2.2), LAU87 lines 419–432); and construct the inversion isomorphism `ι_K : FT^{V'}_ψ(FT^V_ψ K) ≅ [−1]^*K(−r)`, natural in `K ∈ D⁺(V, Λ)` and compatible with base change, through the cartesian diagram of LAU87 lines 407–417. Hence `FT_ψ` is an equivalence of triangulated categories with quasi-inverse `[−1]^* FT^{V'}_ψ(−)(r)`, equivalently `FT_{ψ^{−1}} ∘ FT_ψ ≅ (−r)` (LAU87 Théorème (1.2.2.1) and Corollaire (1.2.2.3), LAU87 lines 401–434). Prove also `Rπ'_!FT_ψ(K) ≅ σ^*K(−r)[−r]` (LAU87 Corollaire (1.2.2.5), LAU87 lines 445–449) and `σ'^*FT_ψ(K) ≅ Rπ_!K[r]`; the second is proper base change of `Rpr'_!` along `σ'`, the kernel restricting to `Λ` on `V ×_S σ'(S)` (§3.1), and is the case `f = π : V → S` (transpose `σ'`, transform on the zero bundle the identity) of LAU87 Théorème (1.2.2.4), LAU87 lines 435–444.
*Needs:* §§2.4, 3.1–3.3; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.7–0.9, F.2.1, F.2.7; gap:projection-formula-qcqs.

**Checks.**
- `r = 1`, `S = Spec 𝔽_q`: `FT(FT(i_{0*}Λ)) = FT(Λ[1]) = i_{0*}Λ(−1)`, trace `q` at `0`, matching `𝔉 𝔉 δ_0 = q δ_0` and the signs `(−1)^{2r}`.
- Degenerate non-example: for the trivial character, `FT(FT(i_{0*}Λ)) = Λ_{𝔸¹}(−1)`, not supported at `0`.
- Sign witness: for `r = 1` and the skyscraper at `a ∈ 𝔽_q^×`, `FT(FT(i_{a*}Λ)) ≅ i_{(−a)*}Λ(−1)`; without `[−1]^*` the support would be `a`, which differs from `−a` for `p` odd.
- Zero-section values, `r = 1`: `σ'^*FT(Λ[1]) = σ'^*σ'_*Λ(−1) = Λ(−1) = Rπ_!(Λ[1])[1]` and `σ'^*FT(i_{a*}Λ) = σ'^*ℒ_ψ(a x')[1] = Λ[1] = Rπ_!(i_{a*}Λ)[1]`, while the shift `[−r]` of the first isomorphism would give `Λ(−1)[−2]` and `Λ[−1]`; on the other side `Rπ'_!FT(i_{0*}Λ) = Rπ'_!Λ[1] = Λ(−1)[−1] = σ^*(i_{0*}Λ)(−1)[−1]`, and `Rπ'_!FT(i_{a*}Λ) = 0 = σ^*(i_{a*}Λ)` for `a ≠ 0` by §2.4.

### 3.5 Duality

For `S` Noetherian, `K ∈ Dᵇ_ctf(V, Λ)` and `M ∈ D⁺(S, Λ)`, prove the bifunctorial isomorphism `RHom(FT_ψ K, π'^!M) ≅ FT_{ψ^{−1}}(RHom(K, π^!M))(r)`, from the sheafified Verdier adjunction for `pr'`, `pr^! = pr^*(r)[2r]`, smooth base change for `RHom`, `ℒ_ψ^∨ = ℒ_{ψ^{−1}}` and §3.3 (LAU87 Théorème (1.3.2.1), LAU87 lines 553–560). For `M = Λ_S` this is `D_{V'/S}(FT_ψ K) ≅ FT_{ψ^{−1}}(D_{V/S}K)(r)` (KL85 Corollaire (2.1.5)(ii), KL85 lines 390–391); over `S = Spec k` it is `D(FT_ψ K) ≅ FT_{ψ^{−1}}(DK)(r)` (LAU87 Corollaire (1.3.2.2), LAU87 lines 563–565).
*Needs:* §§3.3–3.4; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.5–F.6; gap:smooth-exceptional-pullback.

**Checks.**
- `S = Spec 𝔽_q`, `r = 1`, `K = i_{a*}Λ` with `a ∈ 𝔽_q^×`: `D(FT_ψ K) = ℒ_ψ(a x')^∨(1)[1] = ℒ_{ψ^{−1}}(a x')(1)[1] = FT_{ψ^{−1}}(DK)(1)`; with `FT_ψ` on the right the traces would be `ψ(a x')` instead of `ψ(−a x')`, different at `x' = 1` for `p = 3`.
- `K = Λ_{𝔸¹}[1]`: `D(FT K) = D(σ'_*Λ(−1)) = σ'_*Λ(1)` and `FT_{ψ^{−1}}(Λ(1)[1])(1) = σ'_*Λ(1)`.
- `r = 0`: duality reduces to `D_S`, and the twist `(r)` is trivial.

### 3.6 Functoriality

Prove, for the data of this layer, with one of the two inputs of finite Tor amplitude wherever two inputs are tensored (`K_1` or `K_2` for external products, convolution and Plancherel, `M` or `L` for linear group actions), as in the Künneth formula of §1.1 and the projection formula of gap:projection-formula-qcqs, so that every complex below lies in `D⁺`; every object of `Dᵇ_ctf` qualifies:

- base change: for `g : S' → S`, `g'^* FT_ψ(K) ≅ FT_ψ(g^*K)` (KL85 Corollaire (2.1.5)(i), KL85 lines 388–389; LAU87 Proposition (1.2.2.9), LAU87 lines 472–474), and for separated finitely presented `g`, `FT_ψ(Rg_{V!}K_1) ≅ Rg_{V'!}FT_ψ(K_1)` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.5), LAU87 lines 508–518);
- linear maps: for `f : E_1 → E_2` of ranks `r_1`, `r_2` and `K_1 ∈ D⁺(V(E_1))`, `FT_ψ(Rf_!K_1) ≅ f^{∨*}FT_ψ(K_1)[r_2 − r_1]` (LAU87 Théorème (1.2.2.4), LAU87 lines 435–444);
- external products: `FT^{E_1 ⊕ E_2}_ψ(K_1 ⊠_S K_2) ≅ FT^{E_1}_ψ K_1 ⊠_S FT^{E_2}_ψ K_2` (LAU87 lines 459–461);
- convolution: `K_1 * K_2 = Rs_!(K_1 ⊠_S K_2)` and `FT_ψ(K_1 * K_2) ≅ FT_ψ K_1 ⊗^L FT_ψ K_2[−r]` (LAU87 Définition (1.2.2.6) and Proposition (1.2.2.7), LAU87 lines 452–463);
- Plancherel: `Rπ'_!(FT_ψ K_1 ⊗^L FT_ψ K_2) ≅ Rπ_!(K_1 ⊗^L [−1]^*K_2)(−r)` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.2.8), LAU87 lines 464–471);
- sub-bundles: for a sub-bundle `F ⊂ E` of rank `s` with orthogonal `F^⊥ ⊂ E^∨`, `FT_ψ(Λ_{V(F)}[s]) ≅ Λ_{V(F^⊥)}(−s)[r − s]` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.1), LAU87 lines 477–480);
- translation: for `e ∈ V(S)`, `FT_ψ(τ_{e*}K) ≅ FT_ψ(K) ⊗ ℒ_ψ⟨e, ·⟩` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.2), LAU87 lines 483–487);
- quadratic forms, in every characteristic: for a symmetric isomorphism `a : E → E^∨` with `q(e) = ⟨e, a e⟩` and `q'(e') = ⟨a^{−1}e', e'⟩`, `[2]^* FT_ψ(ℒ_ψ(q)) ≅ ℒ_ψ(−q') ⊗ π'^*Rπ_!ℒ_ψ(q)[r]`, from the identity `q(e) + ⟨e, 2e'⟩ = q(e + a^{−1}e') − q'(e')`, valid for every symmetric `a`, and the automorphism `(e, e') ↦ (e + a^{−1}e', e')` of `V ×_S V'` over `V'` (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.3), LAU87 lines 488–494, which has no parity hypothesis);
- linear group actions: for a quasi-separated `S`-group `H` of finite type (automatic over a Noetherian `S`), so that `H` is a qcqs base for the transform, acting linearly on `E` by `m`, with contragredient action `m'`, `M ∈ D⁺(H, Λ)` and `L ∈ D⁺(V, Λ)`, one of them of finite Tor amplitude, an isomorphism `m^*K ≅ M ⊠_S L` induces `m'^*FT_ψ(K) ≅ M ⊠_S FT_ψ(L)`, through the automorphism `(pr_H, m)` of `H ×_S V` over `H`, the linear-map formula above, base change along `H → S` and the projection formula (LAU87 Proposition (1.2.3.4), LAU87 lines 495–507, for `ℚ̄_ℓ` coefficients).

*Needs:* §§3.2–3.4; §§1.1–1.2; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.8, F.2.1, F.2.7; gap:projection-formula-qcqs; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.totalSpaceSchemeBiprodIso` for external products and `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.isAddMonHom_totalSpaceScheme_map` for linear maps.

**Checks.**
- Shift witness for linear maps: `E_1 = O_S` (`r_1 = 1`), `E_2 = 0`, `K_1 = i_{0*}Λ`: `Rf_!K_1 = Λ_S` and `f^∨ = σ'`, so `Λ_S = σ'^*(Λ[1])[s]` forces `s = −1 = r_2 − r_1`; the shift `r_1 − r_2` gives `Λ_S[2]`.
- Convolution of two skyscrapers `i_{a*}Λ * i_{b*}Λ = i_{(a+b)*}Λ` has transform `ℒ_ψ⟨a + b, ·⟩[r]`, equal to `ℒ_ψ⟨a, ·⟩[r] ⊗ ℒ_ψ⟨b, ·⟩[r] [−r]`.
- Quadratic forms for `p = 2`: `q` is additive and `[2] = 0`, so the left side is `π'^*Rπ_!ℒ_ψ(q)[r]` by §3.4. For `r = 1`, `a = 1`, `ℒ_ψ(x²) ≅ ℒ_ψ(x)` (§2.5) has `Rπ_! = 0` and both sides vanish; for `r = 2` and `a` the antidiagonal matrix, `q = 2x_1x_2 = 0` and `q' = 0`, and both sides are `π'^*Λ(−2)[−2]`. A version restricted to odd `p` would lose these cases, where the formula holds.
- Tor hypothesis: `Λ = ℤ/9`, `p = 2`, `ψ(1) = −1` (nondegenerate, since `−2 = 7` is a unit), `S = Spec k̄`, `E = E_1 = E_2 = O_S` and `K_1 = K_2 = (ℤ/3)_{𝔸¹}`. Then `K_1 ⊠_S K_2 = (ℤ/3 ⊗^L_{ℤ/9} ℤ/3)_{𝔸²}` and `K_1 ⊗^L [−1]^*K_2 = (ℤ/3 ⊗^L_{ℤ/9} ℤ/3)_{𝔸¹}` have cohomology `ℤ/3` in every degree `≤ 0` and leave `D⁺`, so neither side of the external-product, convolution or Plancherel formula is defined. With `K_2 = Λ_{𝔸¹}`, of finite Tor amplitude, both sides of the external-product formula are `σ'_*(ℤ/3)(−2)[−2]`, both sides of the convolution formula are `σ'_*(ℤ/3)(−2)[−3]` and both sides of Plancherel are `(ℤ/3)(−2)[−2]`.
- Group actions with the trivial group `H = S`, on `𝔸¹_{k̄}` with `Λ = ℤ/9` and `ψ(1) = −1` as above: the formula becomes `FT_ψ(π^*M ⊗ L) ≅ π'^*M ⊗ FT_ψ(L)`; for `M = ℤ/3` (infinite Tor amplitude over `ℤ/9`) and `L = Λ_{𝔸¹}` both sides are `σ'_*(ℤ/3)(−1)[−1]`, while `M = ℤ/3` with `L = (ℤ/3)_{𝔸¹}` gives `M ⊠_S L ∉ D⁺`, an instance the hypothesis excludes.

### 3.8 Trace functions and Frobenius

## Layer 4: perverse `t`-exactness

### 4.1 `t`-exactness over a field

Let `k` be a field of characteristic `p`, `S = Spec k`, `E` of dimension `r`. Coefficients are finite rings `Λ` with nondegenerate `ψ`, `O_E` with both integral perversities, `E`, or `ℚ̄_ℓ`. Prove that `FT_ψ : Dᵇ_c(V) → Dᵇ_c(V')` is `t`-exact for the middle perversity, and for `O_E` coefficients also for the perversity `p⁺`. Route: `pr` is smooth of pure relative dimension `r`, so `pr^*[r]` is `t`-exact; tensoring with the lisse rank-one `ℒ_ψ⟨ , ⟩` preserves both aisles, since stalks and costalks `i_x^!(K ⊗ ℒ) ≅ i_x^!K ⊗ ℒ|_x` change by an invertible module; `pr'` is affine, so `Rpr'_!` is left and `Rpr'_*` right `t`-exact (these exactness properties of smooth and affine morphisms are gap:perverse-exactness-affine-smooth); hence `FT_!` lands in `ᵖD^{≥0}` and `FT_*` in `ᵖD^{≤0}`, and `FT_! = FT_*` (§3.3). For `p⁺`, duality (§3.5) exchanges `p` and `p⁺` and `FT_{ψ^{−1}}` is `t`-exact for `p`. (LAU87 Théorème (1.3.2.3), LAU87 lines 566–575; KL85 Corollaire (2.1.5)(iii), KL85 lines 392–393; KW01 Ch. III, by locator.)

### 5.3 Fourier sheaves

A constructible sheaf `ℱ` on `A` is elementary if `H⁰_c(A_{k̄}, ℱ) = 0` and `H²_c(A_{k̄}, ℱ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(t x)) = 0` for all `t ∈ k̄`; it is Fourier if it is a middle extension and `H⁰(A_{k̄}, ℱ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(t x)) = 0 = H²_c(A_{k̄}, ℱ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(t x))` for all `t`; an irreducible Fourier sheaf is a Fourier sheaf whose restriction to a dense open of lissity is geometrically irreducible. (ESDE (7.3.3)–(7.3.6) and Theorem 7.3.8, ESDE lines 7235–7312; the trace identity is Theorem 7.3.8 (4), ESDE lines 7307–7312, for elementary `ℱ`.)

**API.**

### 5.4 The Kummer transform and Gauss-sum lines

Let `χ` be a character of `μ_N(k)` with `χ(ζ) − 1` a unit, `ℒ_χ` its Kummer sheaf on `U = 𝔾_m ⊂ A` and `j : U → A`, `j' : U' → A'`. Define `G(χ, ψ) = H¹_c(U_{k̄}, ℒ_χ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x))` with its `Gal(k̄/k)`-action. Prove: `H^i_c(U_{k̄}, ℒ_χ ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x)) = 0` for `i ≠ 1` and `G(χ, ψ)` has rank one; for `k = 𝔽_q` and `χ : 𝔽_q^× → Λ^×`, `Frob` acts on `G(χ, ψ)` by `−Σ_{x ∈ 𝔽_q^×} χ(x) ψ(Tr_{𝔽_q/𝔽_p} x)`; and `FT(j_!ℒ_χ[1]) ≅ j'_!ℒ_{χ^{−1}}[1] ⊗ G(χ, ψ)`, with `G(χ, ψ)` regarded as a geometrically constant sheaf. Route: `j_!ℒ_χ[1]` is of type (T3); the action of `𝔾_m` by homotheties and §3.6's group-action formula give `FT(j_!ℒ_χ[1]) ≅ j'_!ℒ_{χ^{−1}}[1] ⊗ (stalk at 1)`, and the stalk is `G(χ, ψ)` by proper base change. (LAU87 Lemme (1.4.3.1) and Proposition (1.4.3.2), LAU87 lines 651–678; SGA45-ST (4.3), by locator.)
*Needs:* §§2.6–2.7, 3.6, 3.8, 5.2.

**API.**
- `G(χ, ψ)` commutes with extension of `k` and is functorial in `Λ`.
- `G(χ, ψ(c·)) ≅ G(χ, ψ) ⊗ ℒ_χ(c)^{−1}` for `c ∈ 𝔽_p^×`, where `ℒ_χ(c)` is the stalk line at `c`.
- `G(χ, ψ)^∨ ≅ G(χ^{−1}, ψ^{−1})(1)`, from Poincaré duality on `U_{k̄}`.

**Checks.**
- Trace witness over `𝔽_q`: `t_{FT}(x') = Σ_x χ(x) ψ(Tr(x x')) = χ^{−1}(x') g(χ, ψ)` for `x' ≠ 0` and `0` at `x' = 0`, equal to the trace `(−χ^{−1}(x'))(−g(χ, ψ))` of the right side.
- Inversion: `G(χ, ψ) ⊗ G(χ^{−1}, ψ)` is the line on which `Frob` acts by `χ(−1) q`, matching Mathlib's `gaussSum_mul_gaussSum_eq_card` with `mul_gaussSum_inv_eq_gaussSum` (`g(χ, ψ) g(χ^{−1}, ψ) = χ(−1) q` for a domain target).
- `q = 5`, `Λ = ℤ/11` reduction field `𝔽_11`, `ψ(1) = 3`, `χ` quadratic: `g = Σ_{x ≠ 0} χ(x) ψ(x) = 3 − 9 − 27 + 81 ≡ 3 + 2 + 6 + 4 = 15 ≡ 4`, and `g² = 16 ≡ 5 = χ(−1)·5`.

### 5.5 Local monodromy of the transform

Let `K` be perverse on `A`, `K' = FT(K)`, `U`, `U'` the open sets of lissity of `K`, `K'` (where `a_x = 0`, §5.1), `S = A ∖ U`, `S' = A' ∖ U'`, `F = H^{−1}(K)|_U`, `F' = H^{−1}(K')|_{U'}`, `ā : A → D` the inclusion, and `F_∞ = F_{η̄_∞}` the local monodromy of `F` at `∞` with its slope parts `F_{∞,[0,1[}`, `F_{∞,1}`, `F_{∞,]1,∞[}`. Module invariants `r`, `s` of local representations are ranks and Swan conductors (§5.1). Prove, with the nonnegative invariants of §5.1 (LAU87 Proposition (2.3.1.1) and Corollaires (2.3.1.2)–(2.3.1.3), LAU87 lines 933–974, with the signs changed accordingly):

- the generic rank: `rk(K') = Σ_{s ∈ S} deg(s) a_s(K) + s(F_{∞,]1,∞[}) − r(F_{∞,]1,∞[})`, and the same with `K`, `K'` exchanged;
- the stalk ranks: for a closed point `s' ≠ 0'`, `rk_{s'}(K') = rk(K') − r(F_{∞,1}) + s(F_{∞,1} ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s'))`, and `rk_{0'}(K') = rk(K') − r(F_{∞,[0,1[}) + s(F_{∞,[0,1[})`;
- the lisse locus: `s' ≠ 0'` lies in `U'` exactly when `s(F_{∞,1} ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s')) = r(F_{∞,1})`, and `0' ∈ U'` exactly when `F_{∞,[0,1[} = 0`; in particular `U' = A' ∖ {0'}`, `rk(K') = Σ_s deg(s) a_s(K)` and `rk_{0'}(K') = rk(K') − r(F_∞) + s(F_∞)` when `F_∞` is nonzero with all slopes `< 1`, and `U' = A'` with `rk(K') = Σ_s deg(s) a_s(K) + s(F_∞) − r(F_∞)` when all slopes exceed `1`;
- vanishing cycles at a finite point `s'`: for `pr' : D ×_k A' → A'`, the vanishing cycles of `pr^*ā_!K ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x x')[1]` at `s'` vanish on `A × s'`, their stalk at `(∞, s')` is concentrated in degree `−1`, and `R^{−1}Φ_{s'}(K') ≅ R^{−1}Φ(pr^*ā_!K ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x x')[1])_{(∞, s')}` as `G_{s'}`-modules (LAU87 Proposition (2.3.2.1), LAU87 lines 988–1000), of rank `r(F_{∞,1}) − s(F_{∞,1} ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s'))` for `s' ≠ 0'` and `r(F_{∞,[0,1[}) − s(F_{∞,[0,1[})` for `s' = 0'` (LAU87 Corollaire (2.3.2.3), LAU87 lines 1004–1007);
- the sequence at `0'`: `0 → H⁰_c(A_{k̄}, K) → F'_{η̄_{0'}} → R^{−1}Φ_{0'}(K') → H¹_c(A_{k̄}, K) → 0` is an exact sequence of `G_{0'}`-modules, its outer terms are the stalks `H^{−1}(K'_{0̄'})` and `H⁰(K'_{0̄'})` with `I_{0'}` acting trivially (LAU87 (2.3.2), LAU87 lines 975–987); §5.6 identifies the third term with a local Fourier transform of `F_∞`;
- vanishing cycles at `∞'`: for `pr' : D ×_k D' → D'`, the vanishing cycles at `∞'` vanish on `U × ∞'`, their stalks at `(s, ∞')` for `s ∈ S ∪ {∞}` are concentrated in degree `−1`, and `F'_{η̄_{∞'}} ≅ ⊕_{s ∈ S} Ind(R^{−1}Φ_{(s,∞')}) ⊕ R^{−1}Φ_{(∞,∞')}` as `G_{∞'}`-modules, the induction going from the Galois group of `∞' ⊗ k(s)` (LAU87 Proposition (2.3.3.1), LAU87 lines 1017–1034).

Route: proper base change gives `rk_{s'}(K') = χ_c(A_{k̄}, K ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s'))` for a rational `s'` (after extending `k` to `k(s')` in general), the Euler characteristic of the complex, since `K'_{s̄'} = RΓ_c(A_{k̄}, K ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s'))[1]` and `H^i_c(A_{k̄}, K ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x s'))` vanishes outside degrees `0` and `1` (witness: `K = Λ[1]` and `s' = 0'` give `rk_{0'}(K') = −1 = χ_c(A_{k̄}, Λ[1])`); §5.1's Euler–Poincaré formula and the twisting formula evaluate it; the vanishing statements are the local acyclicity of §3.3, once for `D × A' → A'` and once with the roles of `A` and `A'` exchanged; the identification at `∞'` uses `RΨ = RΦ` there because the kernel vanishes on `D × ∞'`.
*Needs:* §§3.3, 3.4, 4.2, 5.1, 5.2; LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §§0.3–0.7; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.2.1, F.3a.

Prove that the naive transform `NFT_ψ` of §5.3 restricts to an autoequivalence of the category of lisse sheaves on `A` all of whose breaks at `∞` exceed `1`, with quasi-inverse `[−1]^* NFT_ψ(−)(1)`. Route: such a sheaf `ℱ` is Fourier, so `FT(ℱ[1]) ≅ NFT_ψ(ℱ)[1]` (§5.3), and the lisse-locus criterion above with `S = ∅` and all slopes of `F_∞` above `1` shows that `G = NFT_ψ(ℱ)` is lisse on `A'`. After extending `k` to `k̄`, the criterion with the roles of `A` and `A'` exchanged, applied to `G[1]`, whose transform `[−1]^*ℱ(−1)[1]` is lisse on `A`, shows that `G` has no part of slope `< 1` at `∞'` and that its slope-one part `G_1` satisfies `s(G_1 ⊗ ℒ_ψ(x' s)) = r(G_1)` for every `s ≠ 0`; the slope-one twisting lemma of §5.1 then forces `G_1 = 0`. No stationary-phase formula is used. (ESDE §7.8 Class (1), ESDE lines 7877–7889, stated there over an algebraically closed field; KATZ01 lines 888–893.)
*Needs:* §§2.7–2.8, 5.1, 5.3; the lisse-locus criterion above.

**Checks.**
- `K = Λ[1]`: `S = ∅`, `F_∞` trivial, so `rk(K') = 0` and `rk_{0'}(K') = 0 − 1 + 0 = −1`, matching `K' = i_{0'*}Λ(−1)`; the sequence at `0'` reads `0 → 0 → 0 → Λ(−1) → H²_c(𝔸¹_{k̄}, Λ) = Λ(−1) → 0`.
- `K = i_{0*}Λ`: `a_0 = 1`, `F = 0`, so `rk(K') = 1` and `U' = A'`, matching `K' = Λ[1]`; a formula with `−Σ a_s` would give a negative rank.
- `K = ℒ_ψ(x²)[1]`, `p` odd: `S = ∅` and `F_∞` is purely of slope `2` with `r = 1`, `s = 2`, so `U' = A'` and `rk(K') = 2 − 1 = 1`, matching §5.3's second Check.
- `K = j_!ℒ_χ[1]`, `χ` nontrivial: `a_0 = 1`, `F_∞ = ℒ_{χ^{−1}}(π)` is tame, so `U' = A' ∖ {0'}`, `rk(K') = 1`, `rk_{0'}(K') = 0`, and since `H⁰_c = H¹_c = 0` the sequence gives `F'_{η̄_{0'}} ≅ R^{−1}Φ_{0'}(K')`, both being `V'_{χ^{−1}} ⊗ G(χ, ψ)`, the monodromy at `0'` of `ℒ_{χ^{−1}}(x') ⊗ G(χ, ψ)` from §5.4.

### 5.6 Local Fourier transforms

Let `T`, `T'` be henselian traits over `k` with residue field `k` (spectra of henselian discrete valuation rings `R ⊃ k` with `R/𝔪 = k`, so `k{π} ⊂ R ⊂ k[[π]]` as in LAU87 (2.1.1), LAU87 lines 684–688; `R` need not be excellent) and uniformizers `π`, `π'`, `𝒢`, `𝒢'` the categories of continuous finite-dimensional `Λ`-representations of their Galois groups, `V_!` the extension by zero to `T` of the sheaf on the generic point `η` defined by `V ∈ 𝒢`. Define the local Fourier transforms `𝔉^{(0,∞')}`, `𝔉^{(∞,0')}`, `𝔉^{(∞,∞')} : 𝒢 → 𝒢'` as the complexes of vanishing cycles, for the projection `pr' : T ×_k T' → T'`, of `pr^*(V_!) ⊗ ℒ_ψ(π/π')`, `pr^*(V_!) ⊗ ℒ_ψ(π'/π)` and `pr^*(V_!) ⊗ ℒ_ψ(1/(π π'))` at the point over `(t, η̄')`, each sheaf `ℒ_ψ(π/π')`, `ℒ_ψ(π'/π)`, `ℒ_ψ(1/(π π'))` living on `T ×_k η'`, `η ×_k T'`, `η ×_k η'` respectively and extended by zero, and `𝔉^{(·,·)}(V)` as the first cohomology module (LAU87 Définition (2.4.2.3), LAU87 lines 1091–1100). Prove: the comparison with global vanishing cycles (LAU87 Lemme (2.4.2.1), LAU87 lines 1063–1081): for a perverse `K` on `A` and an isomorphism of `K` near `0` (resp. `∞`) with `V_![1]` (resp. `V[1]`), the vanishing cycles of §5.5 at `(0, ∞')`, `(∞, 0')`, `(∞, ∞')` are identified with the local ones shifted by `2`; in particular the third term of the sequence at `0'` of §5.5 is `R^{−1}Φ_{0'}(K') ≅ 𝔉^{(∞,0')}(F_∞)` as `G_{0'}`-modules (LAU87 Lemme (2.4.2.1)(ii) with Proposition (2.3.2.1)(iii), LAU87 lines 993–995); for every `V` arising as the local monodromy at `0` or `∞` of a perverse sheaf on `A`, the complexes are concentrated in degree `1` and supported on the closed point over `η'` (LAU87 Proposition (2.4.2.2), LAU87 lines 1084–1090); compatibility with extension of `k` inside `k̄` (LAU87 (2.5.2), LAU87 lines 1157–1165); and the tame values (LAU87 Proposition (2.5.3.1), LAU87 lines 1170–1182): `𝔉^{(0,∞')}(Λ) = Λ`, `𝔉^{(∞,0')}(Λ) = Λ(−1)`, `𝔉^{(∞,∞')}(Λ) = 0`, and for a nontrivial Kummer module `V_χ = ℒ_χ(π)`, with `V'_χ = ℒ_χ(π')`, `𝔉^{(0,∞')}(V_χ) ≅ V'_χ ⊗ G(χ, ψ)`, `𝔉^{(∞,0')}(V_χ) ≅ V'_χ ⊗ G(χ^{−1}, ψ)` and `𝔉^{(∞,∞')}(V_χ) = 0`, with `G` unramified. Consequently, for `K = j_!F[1]` with `F` lisse on a dense open `U ⊂ A` and `F_∞` unramified, `𝔉^{(∞,0')}(F_∞) ≅ F_∞(−1)` and the sequence at `0'` of §5.5 reads `0 → H¹_c(A_{k̄}, j_!F) → F'_{η̄_{0'}} → F_∞(−1) → H²_c(A_{k̄}, j_!F) → 0`, the sequence of §5.5 with the tame value above. (LAU87 lines 2620–2628 give the three-term form without `H²_c` under the hypotheses of LAU87 Corollaire (4.3.1.1), LAU87 lines 2565–2567, which include `F` irreducible, unramified at `∞` and not geometrically constant; these make `H²_c(A_{k̄}, j_!F) = (F_η̄)_{π_1(U_{k̄})}(−1)` vanish.) When `k` is finite, every tamely ramified `V` is, after finite extensions of `k` and of `Λ`, a successive extension of unramified twists of Kummer modules and of the trivial module, each the local monodromy of a perverse sheaf on `A`. The input is that a topological generator `σ` of `I/P` acts with eigenvalues that are roots of unity of order prime to `p`: for `E`, and for `ℚ̄_ℓ` after descending `V` to a finite `E` (a continuous homomorphism from a compact group to `GL_n(ℚ̄_ℓ)` lands in some `GL_n(E)`, by Baire's theorem), because `V` is quasi-unipotent (LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §1.1, stated for ℓ-adic representations); for a finite field `Λ` without that theorem, because `GL(V)` is finite. Over a finite extension `𝔽_Q` of `k` with every eigenvalue `α` satisfying `α^Q = α`, a Frobenius lift `φ` with `φ σ φ^{−1} ≡ σ^Q` modulo `P` preserves each generalized eigenspace `V_α` of `σ` and the kernels of the powers of `u − 1`, where `σ = α u` on `V_α` with `u` unipotent, since `u^Q − 1` is `u − 1` times a unit when `Q` is invertible in `Λ`. On the graded pieces `I` acts through a character of `I/P` of order prime to `p`, a Kummer character, and what remains is unramified. With the compatibility (2.5.2) and the change of coefficient field, the complexes are therefore concentrated in degree `1` for every tame `V`.
*Needs:* §§3.6, 5.4–5.5; LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles §§0.3–0.7, 1.1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.3a.

**API.**
- Each `𝔉^{(·,·)}` is a triangulated functor on the level of complexes and commutes with tensoring by unramified modules.
- They commute with extension of `k` inside `k̄` and with change of the coefficient field.
- Replacing `ψ` by `ψ(c·)`, `c ∈ 𝔽_p^×`, composes `𝔉^{(0,∞')}` and `𝔉^{(∞,∞')}` with the substitution `π' ↦ c^{−1}π'` and `𝔉^{(∞,0')}` with `π' ↦ c π'`.

**Checks.**
- Trivial module at `(∞, 0')`: the global object `Λ_A[1]` has transform `i_{0'*}Λ(−1)`, whose vanishing cycles at `0'` are `Λ(−1)` (§5.5's first Check), so `𝔉^{(∞,0')}(Λ) = Λ(−1)`, not `Λ`.
- Kummer at `(0, ∞')`: from §5.4, the local monodromy of `j'_!ℒ_{χ^{−1}}(x')` at `∞'` is `ℒ_{χ^{−1}}(1/π') = ℒ_χ(π')`, so the value is `V'_χ ⊗ G(χ, ψ)`; the character `χ^{−1}` instead of `χ` would contradict §5.4's trace witness.
- `𝔉^{(∞,∞')}` vanishes on tame modules: slopes `≤ 1` contribute nothing at `∞'`, consistent with the first rank formula of §5.5 having only the `]1, ∞[` part.
- Unramified `F_∞` with `H²_c ≠ 0`: for `F = Λ` on `U = A`, `F' = 0` because `FT(Λ[1]) = i_{0'*}Λ(−1)`, and the sequence at `0'` reads `0 → 0 → 0 → Λ(−1) → H²_c(A_{k̄}, Λ) = Λ(−1) → 0`. The three-term form of LAU87 lines 2620–2628 would make `Λ(−1)` a quotient of `F'_{η̄_{0'}} = 0`; it applies only when `H²_c(A_{k̄}, j_!F) = 0`, as for `F` irreducible and not geometrically constant.
