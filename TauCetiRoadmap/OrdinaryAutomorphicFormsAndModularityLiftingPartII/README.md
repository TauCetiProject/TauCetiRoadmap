# Roadmap: ordinary automorphic forms and ordinary modularity lifting, part II — dyadic unitary and symplectic ordinary lifting

**Summary.** This Part II continues *Ordinary automorphic forms and ordinary modularity lifting* (OrdinaryAutomorphicFormsAndModularityLifting), which stops at the rank-two ordinary theory: Hida families of modular, Hilbert and quaternionic forms, nearly ordinary deformation rings and the Skinner–Wiles theorems for GL₂ at odd primes. It builds the two higher-rank ordinary R = T theorems that the modularity of abelian surfaces over ℚ uses (Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, *Modularity theorems for abelian surfaces*, §§5–6). The end theorems are:

1. **The dyadic unitary theorem** (BCGP25 Theorem 5.7.14). Let F be a CM field with F/F⁺ unramified at every finite place, every place of F⁺ above 2 or under the ramification of the seed split in F, n ≥ 2 and n[F⁺:ℚ] ≡ 0 mod 4, and let `ρ̄ : G_F → GL_n(𝔽̄₂)` come from an ordinary RACSDC automorphic representation, with nearly adequate image containing a regular semisimple element and, for n even, strongly residually odd at some real place. Then the ordinary deformation ring `R^{T,ord}` is finite over the weight algebra Λ; and if every irreducible component of Spec `R^{T,ord}` has dimension at least dim Λ (the dimension bound 3.2(b), BCGP25 Lemma 5.7.7, whose printed proof gives only dim Λ − 1), then for sufficiently deep tame level its reduced quotient is the localized universal ordinary Hecke algebra of a definite unitary group.
2. **The symplectic theorem for p > 2** (BCGP25 Theorem 6.3.4). Let p > 2 split completely in the totally real field F⁺ and let π be an ordinary cuspidal automorphic representation of `GSp₄(𝔸_{F⁺})` whose residual representation is GSp₄-reasonable and tidy, ordinary of weight 2 at p, with the seed point on a unique ordinary component. Then the deformation ring of the problem selected by π is finite over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`, and its map onto the corresponding Hida–Hecke algebra has nilpotent kernel, so the two have the same reduced quotient.

The two theorems are proved separately: for p = 3 the image Sp₄(𝔽₃) ⊂ GL₄(𝔽₃) is not adequate (BCGP25 Remark 6.4.5), so the GSp₄ problem is patched with Whitmore's Taylor–Wiles places, while for p = 2 the GL₄ route works under near adequacy.

## Conventions

- E/ℚ_p is a finite coefficient field with ring of integers O, uniformizer ϖ (also written λ) and residue field k, enlarged when needed; CNL_O is the category of complete Noetherian local O-algebras with residue field k, and CNL_Λ that of Λ-algebras in it.
- F is an imaginary CM field with maximal totally real subfield F⁺, c ∈ Gal(F/F⁺) the nontrivial element, `δ_{F/F⁺}` the quadratic character of F/F⁺, and c_v a complex conjugation at an infinite place v. S_p (S_2) is the set of places of F⁺ above p (above 2) and S_∞ the set of infinite places; d = [F⁺:ℚ].
- 𝒢_n = (GL_n × GL_1) ⋊ {1, j} with `j(g, a)j^{−1}` = (`a·ᵗg^{−1}`, a), 𝒢_n^0 = GL_n × GL_1, multiplier ν with ν(j) = −1, and Ĝ_n(A) = ker(𝒢_n(A) → 𝒢_n(k)). Polarized lifts are representations of `G_{F⁺}` (BCGP25 writes G_F in §5.4; source issue below).
- ε is the p-adic cyclotomic character. GSp₄-valued lifts have similitude `ε^{−1}`; ad ρ̄ = 𝔤𝔰𝔭₄ (dimension 11) and ad⁰ρ̄ = 𝔰𝔭₄ (dimension 10).
- Local Artin maps send uniformizers to geometric Frobenius elements; for v ∤ p this gives `ε(Art_{F_v}(ϖ_v)) = q_v^{−1}`. The Hecke polynomial at a split place w is `Σ_j (−1)^j q_w^{j(j−1)/2} T^j_w X^{n−j}`.
- Λ = O⟦T(l)⟧ (l = 2) is the weight algebra of the unitary theory, where T(l) is the kernel of `∏_{v|2} T(O_{F⁺_v}) → ∏_{v|2} T(k(v))`, T the diagonal torus of GL_n; dim Λ = 1 + nd. `Λ_{GSp₄,v} = O⟦(O^×_{F⁺_v}(p))²⟧` and `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺} = ⊗̂_{v|p} Λ_{GSp₄,v}`. Completed group algebras are the native carrier `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`.
- "Reduced R = T" means that the map from the deformation ring onto the Hecke algebra is surjective with nilpotent kernel.
- Geraghty's paper is cited in the numbering of his preprint (Ger19); the published numbers are recorded in the source manifest.

**Checks.**

- Geometric Frobenius normalization: for p = 7 and v ∤ 7 with q_v = 2, `ε(Art_{F_v}(ϖ_v)) = 2^{−1} ≡ 4` mod 7, whereas the arithmetic normalization would give 2; the two differ, so the convention is pinned.
- Unrestricted characters at F_v = ℚ₃, O = ℤ₂ and trivial residual character have ad⁰ = 0 but ad = 𝔽₂. The pro-2 completion ℚ₃×(2) = C₂ × ℤ₂ gives R^□ = ℤ₂⟦u,X⟧/(u² + 2u); its special fibre is 𝔽₂⟦u,X⟧/(u²), with u nonzero and u² = 0. Thus it is not formally smooth, consistently with dim H²(F_v, ad) = dim H⁰(F_v, ad∨(1)) = 1; the trace-zero test would incorrectly predict smoothness.
- The multiplier convention: for n = 4 the ordinary problem of Layer 3 has multiplier `ε^{1−n}δ_{F/F⁺}^n = ε^{−3}`, since `δ_{F/F⁺}^4 = 1`.
- dim Λ: for n = 2 and F⁺ real quadratic with 2 split, T(l) ≅ ((1 + 2ℤ₂)^×)^4 ≅ ({±1} × ℤ₂)^4, so dim Λ = 1 + 4 = 5 = 1 + nd.

## How to read the build

Layer 1 sets up polarized deformation theory at p = 2 over a weight algebra and the cohomological input for Taylor–Wiles: Thorne's complex with its corrected cohomology in degree 3, near adequacy, and the one dual Selmer class that survives every Taylor–Wiles datum once ρ̄ stays absolutely irreducible over the relevant quadratic extension (for instance under near adequacy). Layer 2 specializes Geraghty's ordinary Hida theory for definite unitary groups to l = 2. Layer 3 defines the ordinary global problem, bounds the dimension of its components, constructs the Hecke-valued Galois representation, the sufficiently deep levels and the Taylor–Wiles level structures at p = 2. Layer 6 proves Theorem 6.3.4.

## Layer 1: Dyadic polarized deformation problems over a weight algebra

For p = 2 polarized deformation theory has two features absent for odd p. Conjugation by the GL_1 factor of 𝒢_n is not absorbed by scalar matrices, because elements of 1 + m_A need not be squares; strict equivalence therefore uses the full kernel Ĝ_n, and the classes µ_v measure the difference. And a scalar dual Selmer class survives: the quadratic extension of F⁺ inside `F(ζ_{2^∞})` gives a class that no Taylor–Wiles datum of large level kills, nonzero as soon as ρ̄ stays absolutely irreducible over the corresponding quadratic extension of F, and Thorne's complex has one-dimensional cohomology in degree 3. The O-coefficient theory at p = 2 is the contract gap:two-adic-polarized-presentation on GlobalGaloisDeformations; this layer states its forms over a weight algebra.

### 1.1 Polarized deformation problems up to Ĝ_n-conjugacy

Let E/ℚ₂ be finite with ring of integers O, uniformizer ϖ and residue field k. Let F be an imaginary CM field such that F/F⁺ is unramified at every finite place and every place of F⁺ above 2 splits in F. Let S be a finite set of places of F⁺ containing S_2 and S_∞, and let `G_{F⁺,S} ⊃ G_{F,S}` be the Galois groups of the maximal extension F(S) of F unramified outside S. Fix a continuous r̄ : `G_{F⁺,S} → 𝒢_n(k)` with `r̄^{−1}(𝒢_n^0(k)) = G_{F,S}` whose restriction ρ̄ to `G_{F,S}`, projected to GL_n, is absolutely irreducible, and a continuous χ : `G_{F⁺,S} → O^×` lifting ν ∘ r̄ with χ(c_v) = −1 for every v ∈ S_∞. For each v ∈ S fix Λ_v ∈ CNL_O and put `Λ = ⊗̂_{v∈S} Λ_v`.

Define a lifting of `r̄|G_{F⁺_v}` to A `∈ CNL_{Λ_v}` as a continuous r_v : `G_{F⁺_v} → 𝒢_n(A)` reducing to `r̄|G_{F⁺_v}` with `ν ∘ r_v = χ|G_{F⁺_v}`; the functor Lift^□_v is represented by `R^□_v ∈ CNL_{Λ_v}`. A local deformation problem D_v ⊂ Lift^□_v is a representable subfunctor stable under conjugation by Ĝ_n(A). A global deformation problem is a tuple 𝒮 = (F, r̄, O, χ, S, `{Λ_v}_{v∈S}`, `{D_v}_{v∈S}`). A lift of r̄ to A ∈ CNL_Λ is a continuous r : `G_{F⁺,S} → 𝒢_n(A)` reducing to r̄ with ν ∘ r = χ; it is of type 𝒮 if `r|G_{F⁺_v} ∈ D_v(A)` for every v ∈ S. Two lifts are strictly equivalent if they are conjugate by an element of Ĝ_n(A), the whole kernel including its GL_1 factor. For T ⊂ S a T-framed lift of type 𝒮 is `(r, (α_v)_{v∈T})` with α_v ∈ Ĝ_n(A), and (r, (α_v)) ~ (a `r a^{−1}`, (a α_v)) for a ∈ Ĝ_n(A). Def_𝒮 and Def^T_𝒮 : CNL_Λ → Set are the functors of strict equivalence classes of lifts, resp. T-framed lifts, of type 𝒮. When Λ_v = O for v ∈ S ∖ T, put `R^loc_{𝒮,T} = ⊗̂_{v∈T} R_v`, a Λ-algebra through `⊗̂_{v∈T} Λ_v ≅ Λ` (BCGP25 §5.4, pp. 121–122; BCGP25 Lemma 5.1.2 and Remark 5.1.3, pp. 115–116).

- Def_𝒮 and Def^T_𝒮 are represented by R_𝒮 and R^T_𝒮 in CNL_Λ, because ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible and the centralizer of r̄ in Ĝ_n is the image of the scalars (Tho17 Lemmas 2.8 and 2.10; the proofs do not use p odd). With O-coefficients this is the contract gap:two-adic-polarized-presentation; here the coefficient rings are the Λ_v.
- For T ≠ ∅, Def^T_𝒮 → Def_𝒮 is a torsor under `(∏_{v∈T} Ĝ_n)/Ĝ_m`, so R^T_𝒮 is formally smooth over R_𝒮 of relative dimension (n² + 1)#T − 1; for T = ∅, R^T_𝒮 = R_𝒮 (Tho17 Lemma 2.11; with O-coefficients, gap:two-adic-polarized-presentation).
- (r, (α_v)) `↦ (α_v^{−1} r|G_{F⁺_v} α_v)_v` defines a CNL_Λ-morphism `R^loc_{𝒮,T} → R^T_𝒮`.
- At a place v split in F as ṽṽ^c every lifting takes values in 𝒢_n^0(A), and r_v ↦ ρ_v (the GL_n-projection of `r_v|G_{F_ṽ}`) identifies liftings of `r̄|G_{F⁺_v}` with liftings of `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}`, compatibly with conjugation.
- If every element of 1 + m_A has a square root (for example for p odd), conjugation by (1, a) ∈ Ĝ_n(A) equals conjugation by (µ·1_n, 1) with `µ² = a^{−1}`, so Def_𝒮 agrees with the functor of lifts up to 1 + M_n(m_A)-conjugacy of GlobalGaloisDeformations §8.1.
- Adding a Taylor–Wiles datum Q gives 𝒮_Q with `R_{𝒮_Q}` an O[Δ_Q]-algebra and `R_{𝒮_Q}` `⊗_{O[Δ_Q]} O ≅ R_𝒮`.

**Checks.**

- For T = ∅ the framed ring is R_𝒮 itself, and the framing torsor is the trivial group.
- For n = 4 and #T = 3 the relative dimension of R^T_𝒮 over R_𝒮 is (16 + 1)·3 − 1 = 50; the GL_n-framing count n²#T − 1 = 47 omits the GL_1 factor of Ĝ_n and is wrong.
- For γ = (B, b)j ∈ 𝒢_n(A) ∖ 𝒢_n^0(A) and a ∈ 1 + m_A, conjugation by (1, a) sends γ to `(a^{−1}B, b)j` and fixes 𝒢_n^0(A) pointwise; over A = k[ε] with p = 2 and a = 1 + ε, a is not a square ((x + yε)² = x²), so this conjugation is not induced by a scalar matrix.
- For p = 2, n even and r̄(c_v) GL_n(k)-conjugate to (1_n, 1)j at a real place v, the functor of lifts up to 1 + M_n(m_A)-conjugacy differs from Def_𝒮: the one-dimensional µ_v of 1.2 is identified to zero by Ĝ_n-conjugacy and not by GL_n-conjugacy.

### 1.2 Thorne's complex, the dyadic dual Selmer group and Taylor–Wiles presentations over Λ

Keep the data of 1.1 with p = 2, let ad r̄ = M_n(k) = Lie GL_n ⊂ 𝔤_n r̄ = Lie 𝒢_n with the adjoint action of `G_{F⁺,S}` through r̄, so that 0 → ad r̄ → 𝔤_n r̄ → k → 0 is exact, and let L_v ⊂ H¹(F⁺_v, ad r̄) be the image of the tangent space of D_v. The classes µ_v = ker(H¹(F⁺_v, ad r̄) → H¹(F⁺_v, 𝔤_n r̄)) and the dual Selmer group `H¹_{𝒮^⊥,T} ⊂` H¹(F(S)/F⁺, ad r̄(1)), cut out by µ_v^⊥ at v ∈ T and by L_v^⊥ at v ∈ S ∖ T, are those of PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §4.4, *Taylor–Wiles data when F contains ζ_p, including p = 2*; they depend only on the residual data, so they are the same for the Λ-coefficient problem. Let `C^•_{𝒮,T}` be Thorne's complex and `h^i_{𝒮,T}` the dimensions of its cohomology.

With O-coefficients, (2) and (3) are the contract gap:two-adic-polarized-presentation on GlobalGaloisDeformations (Tho17 Lemmas 2.12, 2.13 and 2.15, Corollary 2.14). Prove the following for the Λ-coefficient problems of 1.1, deducing (2) and (3) from that contract because Thorne's complex depends only on r̄ and the subspaces L_v (BCGP25 §5.4, (5.4.1)–(5.4.3), pp. 122–123, and Remark 5.4.4, pp. 123–124; Tho17 Lemma 2.17):

1. µ_v is the image of the connecting map H⁰(F⁺_v, k) → H¹(F⁺_v, ad r̄), so dim_k µ_v ≤ 1; µ_v ⊂ L_v; µ_v = 0 if v splits in F. At a real place v, dim_k µ_v = 1 if r̄(c_v) is GL_n(k)-conjugate to (1_n, 1)j, and µ_v = 0 if n is even and r̄(c_v) is conjugate to (Ψ_n, 1)j, Ψ_n the antidiagonal matrix.
2. For T ≠ ∅: `h⁰_{𝒮,T} = 0`, `h^i_{𝒮,T} = 0` for i ≥ 4, `h²_{𝒮,T} = h¹_{𝒮^⊥,T}`, and `h³_{𝒮,T}` = h⁰(F(S)/F⁺, ad r̄(1)) = 1. Moreover `h¹_{𝒮,T}` = dim_k `m_{R^T_𝒮}/(m_{R^loc_{𝒮,T}}, m²_{R^T_𝒮})`, whatever the coefficient rings Λ_v.
3. If S = T ⊔ Q ⊔ S_∞ with S_2 ⊂ T, D_v = Lift^□_v at v ∈ S_∞ and Q a Taylor–Wiles datum with #Q = q, the Euler characteristic is `χ_{𝒮_Q,T} = 1 − #T − q + dn(n−1)/2`.
4. (Taylor–Wiles presentation over Λ.) Assume (i) χ(c_v) = −1 and D_v = Lift^□_v for v ∈ S_∞; (ii) F = F⁺(√−1); (iii) if n is even, r̄(c_v) is GL_n(k)-conjugate to (1_n, 1)j for some v ∈ S_∞; (iv) ρ̄(G_F) is nearly adequate (1.3); and let T = S ∖ S_∞. Put `q = h¹_{𝒮^⊥,T} − 1` and g = q + #T − 1 − dn(n−1)/2. Then for each N ≥ 1 there are Taylor–Wiles data `(Q, (α_v)_{v∈Q})` of level N with #Q = q and a surjection of `R^loc_{𝒮,T}`-algebras, hence of Λ-algebras, `R^loc_{𝒮,T}⟦X_1, …, X_g⟧ ↠ R^T_{𝒮_Q}` (BCGP25 Proposition 5.5.6, p. 126).
5. (Comparison with conjugate self-dual Selmer groups.) Restriction gives an exact sequence 0 → H¹(F/F⁺, k) → H¹(F(S)/F⁺, ad r̄) → `H¹(F(S)/F, ad r̄)^{Gal(F/F⁺)}` → H²(F/F⁺, k) → H²(F(S)/F⁺, ad r̄), and the restriction map is surjective as soon as some real place v has r̄(c_v) conjugate to (1_n, 1)j (automatic for n odd; strong residual oddness for n even).

The route: (1) is the long exact sequence of 0 → ad r̄ → 𝔤_n r̄ → k → 0, Ĝ_n-stability of D_v and Tho17 Lemma 2.17. For (2) and (3), the complex `C^•_{𝒮,T}` is built from cochains of r̄ and the cocycle spaces of the D_v over k[ε], so it is the same for a Λ-coefficient problem and the O-coefficient problem with the same tangent spaces, and the identification of h¹ with the relative cotangent dimension (Tho17 Lemma 2.12) is a statement about k[ε]-points, on which Λ acts through k. In the contract, (2) is Tho17 Lemma 2.15 by Poitou–Tate, with h⁰ = 0 because T ≠ ∅, h^i = 0 for i ≥ 4 because global and archimedean cohomology agree in degrees ≥ 3 (Tho17, proof of Lemma 2.13, p. 9), and h³ = 1 because at p = 2 the element j acts on Lie GL_n by X ↦ −ᵗX = ᵗX, so the scalars 1_n are `G_{F⁺}`-invariant in ad r̄(1) while ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible. (3) is Tho17 Corollary 2.14 with ℓ¹_v = n(n+1)/2 at real places and n² + 1 at v ∈ Q. (4) is the argument of PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §§4.4, 5.4 with relaxed adequacy replaced by near adequacy through 1.3, the coefficient rings entering only through `R^loc_{𝒮,T}` (KT17 §4; BCGP25 §5.4). (5) is inflation–restriction with coefficients ad r̄ and `(ad r̄)^{G_F} = k`; surjectivity holds because H²(F/F⁺, k) ≅ Ĥ⁰(Gal(ℂ/ℝ), k) maps to Ĥ⁰(Gal(ℂ/ℝ), ad r̄) = `(ad r̄)^{c_v}/(1 + c_v)ad r̄` by 1 ↦ 1_n, and writing r̄(c_v) = (B, 1)j with B symmetric, 1_n ∈ (1 + c_v)ad r̄ exactly when B is alternating.

### 1.3 Weak adequacy with the full adjoint, and near adequacy

Let k be a finite field, V a finite-dimensional k-vector space, ad = End_k(V), ad⁰ the trace-zero endomorphisms, H ⊂ GL(V) a subgroup with set of semisimple elements H^ss, and for g ∈ H^ss and α ∈ k̄ let `e_{g,α}` be the projection of V ⊗ k̄ onto the generalized α-eigenspace of g. Let U = {w ∈ ad ⊗ k̄ : tr(gw) = 0 for all g ∈ H^ss}. Prove that U = {w : `tr(e_{g,α}w) = 0` for all g ∈ H^ss and α}, that U is an H-submodule of ad⁰ ⊗ k̄, and that the following are equivalent: (1) every simple k̄[H]-submodule W ⊂ ad ⊗ k̄ has σ ∈ H^ss and an eigenvalue α of σ with `tr(e_{σ,α}W) ≠ 0`; (3) ad is spanned over k by H^ss; (U) U = 0. Deduce that BCGP's nearly adequate subgroups (weakly adequate, H¹(H, k) = 0 and H¹(H, ad) = 0; BCGP25 Definition 5.3.3) are exactly the relaxed-adequate subgroups of PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §5.4 when k contains the eigenvalues of the elements of H (BCGP25 Lemma 5.3.1 and proof, p. 120; Definitions 5.3.2–5.3.3 and Remark 5.3.4, pp. 120–121). The trace condition (1) runs over the simple submodules of the full adjoint ad ⊗ k̄, as the trace condition of relaxed adequacy does, rather than over those of ad⁰ ⊗ k̄ as in ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.9; since U ⊂ ad⁰ ⊗ k̄, both forms are equivalent to (U), also when p divides dim V.

The route: each `e_{g,α}` is a polynomial in g and `g = Σ_α α e_{g,α}` for semisimple g, which identifies the two descriptions of U; H-stability follows from `tr(g·hwh^{−1}) = tr(h^{−1}gh·w)` with `h^{−1}gh ∈ H^ss`; g = 1 gives U ⊂ ad⁰ ⊗ k̄; nondegeneracy of the trace form gives (3) ⇔ (U); and (1) says that U, an H-submodule, meets the socle of ad ⊗ k̄ trivially, hence (1) ⇔ (U). For the comparison with relaxed adequacy, whose trace condition is on simple k[H]-submodules of ad, run the same argument over k: when k contains the eigenvalues every `e_{g,α}` is defined over k, `U_k = {w ∈ ad : tr(gw) = 0 for all g ∈ H^ss}` satisfies `U_k ⊗ k̄ = U`, and the condition over k is `U_k = 0`, which is (U).

**Checks.**

- U always lies in ad⁰ ⊗ k̄, because 1 ∈ H^ss; for H = {1} ⊂ GL₂(k), U = ad⁰ ⊗ k̄ is three-dimensional and H is not weakly adequate.
- GL₂(𝔽₂) ≅ S₃ acts absolutely irreducibly on 𝔽₂², but its semisimple elements are 1, A and A² with A of order 3 (the involutions are unipotent), spanning only 𝔽₂[A] ≅ 𝔽₄ of dimension 2 < 4; absolute irreducibility does not give weak adequacy, and (1) fails for the simple submodule U ≠ 0.
- A₅ ≅ SL₂(𝔽₄) acting on its four-dimensional projective representation V over 𝔽₂ is nearly adequate (BCGP25 Lemma 8.2.2, p. 182) and not adequate in the sense of Tho17 Definition 2.20, since H¹(A₅, ad/k) ≅ H²(A₅, k) ≅ k from the Schur multiplier ℤ/2 (BCGP25 Remark 8.2.3, p. 182).

*Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.9; PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §5.4 (*Adequacy relaxed to the vanishing of H¹(H, ad)*); Mathlib `Matrix.trace`, `Module.End.IsSemisimple`, `Submodule.span`.

### 1.4 The unavoidable dyadic dual Selmer class

Keep the data of 1.1 with p = 2 and let T = S ∖ S_∞. Let K⁺_m be the maximal totally real subfield of `ℚ(ζ_{2^m})`, choose the least m with K⁺_m ⊄ F⁺ (so `K⁺_{m−1} ⊂` F⁺), let L⁺ = F⁺K⁺_m, a totally real quadratic extension of F⁺ unramified outside 2, and let ψ ∈ H¹(F(S)/F⁺, ad r̄(1)) be the inflation of the nonzero class of H¹(L⁺/F⁺, k) through the scalars k ⊂ ad r̄. Prove that `ψ ∈ H¹_{𝒮_Q^⊥,T}` for every Taylor–Wiles datum `(Q, (α_v)_{v∈Q})` of level N ≥ m (each v ∈ Q outside S, split completely in `F(ζ_{2^N})`, with ρ̄(Frob_v) semisimple with eigenvalue α_v). Prove moreover that if `ρ̄|G_{F·L⁺}` is absolutely irreducible, then ψ ≠ 0, so that `h¹_{𝒮_Q^⊥,T} ≥ 1` for all such data and `h¹_{𝒮^⊥,T} ≥ 1`; the hypothesis holds when H¹(ρ̄(G_F), k) = 0, in particular when ρ̄(G_F) is nearly adequate (1.3), since then ρ̄(G_F) has no subgroup of index 2 and `ρ̄(G_{F·L⁺}) = ρ̄(G_F)` (BCGP25 Lemma 5.5.3 and proof, pp. 125–126, with the nonvanishing hypothesis added; source issue below).

The route: nonvanishing first. If ψ = ∂X with X ∈ ad r̄, then X is fixed by `G_{F·L⁺} = G_F ∩ G_{L⁺}`, on which ψ vanishes, so X is a scalar when `ρ̄|G_{F·L⁺}` is absolutely irreducible; a scalar has ∂X = 0 (c acts on 1_n by −1_n = 1_n), while ψ(g) = 1_n for `g ∉ G_{L⁺}`. Membership: ψ is trivial at infinite places because L⁺ is totally real, and at v ∈ Q because v splits completely in `F(ζ_{2^N}) ⊃ L⁺`. At a finite v ∈ S let Δ_v be the image of H¹(F⁺_v, k) → H¹(F⁺_v, ad r̄); then ψ_v ∈ Δ_v and µ_v ⊂ Δ_v, since µ_v is represented by a cocycle vanishing on G_F and sending c to 1_n. The Tate pairing on Δ_v × Δ_v is n times that of H¹(F⁺_v, k), by tr(1_n) = n (`Matrix.trace_one`). If n is even it vanishes, so ψ_v ∈ Δ_v ⊂ µ_v^⊥. If n is odd, the pairing on H¹(F⁺_v, k) is the Hilbert symbol of the classes of F/F⁺ and L⁺/F⁺, trivial at places prime to 2 (both extensions are unramified there or F/F⁺ is unramified and L⁺/F⁺ unramified outside 2) and at places above 2 (where F/F⁺ splits).

**Checks.**

## Layer 2: Ordinary Hecke algebras of definite unitary groups at p = 2

Geraghty's ordinary Hida theory for definite unitary groups (Ger19 §2) fixes an arbitrary prime l (Ger19 §2, opening paragraph, p. 4); only its construction of Galois representations over Hecke algebras (Ger19 §2.7) uses lemmas of Clozel–Harris–Taylor that need l > 2, and that step is replaced in 3.3. This layer specializes the algebraic modular forms of AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §§7.4–7.5 to the definite unitary group at l = 2 and constructs the ordinary objects that the l-odd sections of PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §§3.2–3.13 do not cover. Throughout, BCGP25 Hypothesis 5.7.1 holds: F/F⁺ is unramified at every finite place, every place of F⁺ above 2 splits in F, and nd ≡ 0 mod 4.

### 2.1 The universal ordinary Hecke algebra at p = 2

Let G/F⁺ be the definite unitary group of PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructurePartII §3.1 (*The definite unitary group attached to a CM field*), with ι_ṽ : `G(O_{F⁺_v}) ≅ GL_n(O_{F_ṽ})` at split v, chosen places ṽ | v for v | 2, and Ĩ_2 the embeddings F → E inducing them. For `λ ∈ (ℤ^n_+)^{Ĩ_2}` let M_λ be the tensor product over τ ∈ Ĩ_2 of the O-lattices of highest weight λ_τ; for a set R ⊂ T ∖ S_2 of split places and characters χ_v : Iw(ṽ)/Iw_1(ṽ) ≅ (k(ṽ)^×)^n → O^× put `M_{λ,{χ_v}} = M_λ ⊗ ⊗_{v∈R} O(χ_v)`. For U = ∏U_v with `U_v ⊂ ι_ṽ^{−1}Iw(ṽ)` (v ∈ R) and an O-module A, `S_{λ,{χ_v}}(U, A)` is the carrier S(U, `M_{λ,{χ_v}}` ⊗ A) of AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §7.4, the level acting through its components at S_2 ∪ R; sufficient smallness of a level is that of AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §7.5 (torsion-free projection to one finite place) and is assumed only where stated. Define the following (BCGP25 §5.7, from Hypothesis 5.7.1 to the definition of the universal ordinary Hecke algebra, pp. 130–132; Ger19 §§2.2–2.4).

- For 0 ≤ b ≤ c, the level `U(l^{b,c})` replaces U_v (v | 2) by `ι_ṽ^{−1}Iw(ṽ^{b,c})`, the matrices upper triangular modulo ṽ^c and unipotent upper triangular modulo ṽ^b. For (b, c) = (0, 0) this is the hyperspecial level `GL_n(O_{F_ṽ})`. The U-operators, the enlarged Hecke algebra and every ordinary construction below are defined only at Iwahori depth c ≥ 1 (Ger19 Definitions 2.3.1 and 2.3.4, pp. 10–11).
- At split w ∉ T, T^j_w is the unweighted double coset operator of `ι_w^{−1}diag(ϖ_w 1_j, 1_{n−j})`. At v | 2 and for c ≥ 1, with `α^j_{ϖ_ṽ}` = diag(ϖ_ṽ `1_j, 1_{n−j}`), `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` = `(w₀λ)(α^j_{ϖ_ṽ})^{−1}[U(l^{b,c}) ι_ṽ^{−1}(α^j_{ϖ_ṽ}) U(l^{b,c})]`, the weighted Hecke operator of §7.4 for the coefficient action of M_λ extended to the monoid generated by `U(l^{b,c})` and the α^j. The diamond operators ⟨u⟩, `u ∈ T(O_{F⁺,2})`, act through the torus. At depth c = 0 the same normalized double coset of `GL_n(O_{F_ṽ})` is the spherical operator `T^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` (Ger19 Definition 2.3.2, p. 11), a different operator. For 1 ≤ j ≤ n − 1 the inclusion `S(U(l^{0,0}), A) ⊂ S(U(l^{0,1}), A)` does not intertwine it with `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}`: on constant functions of weight 0 the first acts by the number of points of the Grassmannian `Gr(j, n)(k(ṽ))` and the second by `q_ṽ^{j(n−j)}` (first Check); for j = n both are translation by the central element `ϖ_ṽ1_n`.
- For c ≥ 1, `T^T_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{b,c}), A) ⊂ T̃^T_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{b,c}), A)` are the O-subalgebras of End_O generated by the T^j_w, `(T^n_w)^{−1}` and ⟨u⟩, respectively by these and the `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` (Ger19 Definition 2.3.4, p. 11). For A one of O, E/O, `O/ϖ^γ`, `ϖ^{−γ}O/O`, the commutative algebra T̃^T is finite over O, hence the product of its localizations; a maximal ideal of T̃^T is ordinary when it contains no `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}`, and the ordinary idempotent e ∈ T̃^T is the idempotent of the product of the ordinary localizations; with `U(l) = ∏_{v|2} ∏_j U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}`, e = lim_r `U(l)^{r!}`. Put `S^{ord} = eS`, `T̃^{T,ord} = eT̃^T`, and let `T^{T,ord}` be the image of T^T in `T̃^{T,ord}`, that is `eT^T ⊂ eT̃^T`; since eT̃^T acts faithfully on eS, this is also the image of T^T in `End_O(eS)` (Ger19 Definitions 2.4.1–2.4.2, p. 12). The smaller algebra T^T, not T̃^T, carries the universal ordinary Hecke algebra below and the Galois surjection of 3.3.
- `S(U(l^∞), E/O) = colim_{c≥1} S(U(l^{c,c}), E/O)` and `T(U(l^∞), O) = lim_{c≥1} T(U(l^{c,c}), O)`, likewise for T̃ and for the ordinary variants.
- Λ = O⟦T(l)⟧. For λ = 0, `u ↦ (∏_{τ∈Ĩ_2} ∏_{i=1}^n τ(u_i)^{1−i})⟨u⟩` makes `T^{T,ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)` a Λ-algebra, the universal ordinary Hecke algebra `T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)`. The twist is the character `(w₀ν)^{−1}` with ν_τ = (n − 1, …, 1, 0).

Its API:

- For 0 ≤ b ≤ c with c ≥ 1, the operators T^j_w, `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` and ⟨u⟩ on `S(U(l^{b,c}), A)` commute with each other, and for b ≤ b′, c ≤ c′ the inclusion `S(U(l^{b,c}), A) ⊂` `S(U(l^{b′,c′}), A)` is equivariant for all of them: the matrices with blocks `1_j`, X, 0, `1_{n−j}`, X running over representatives of `M_{j,n−j}(O_{F_ṽ}/ṽ)`, are coset representatives of `Iw(ṽ^{b,c})` modulo `Iw(ṽ^{b,c}) ∩ α^j Iw(ṽ^{b,c}) (α^j)^{−1}` for every such (b, c) (Ger19 Lemma 2.3.3 and proof, p. 11). The normalization by `(w₀λ)(α^j)^{−1}` makes `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` preserve integral forms (Ger19, remark after Definition 2.3.1, p. 11, from Lemma 2.2.2).
- For c ≥ 1, e is an idempotent of `T̃^T_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{b,c}), A)` and commutes with `T^T_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{b,c}), A)`; it need not lie in T^T (second Check). eS is the largest O-submodule on which every `U^j_{λ,ϖ_ṽ}` is bijective, U(l) is topologically nilpotent on (1 − e)S, and `T^{T,ord}` and `T̃^{T,ord}` act faithfully on eS; on `S(U(l^∞), E/O)` there is a unique idempotent restricting to e at each level c ≥ 1 (Ger19 §2.4, pp. 13–14).
- The limit algebras with coefficients E/O and O are naturally isomorphic, with or without ord (Ger19 Lemma 2.4.7).
- For split w ∉ T the Hecke polynomial is `Σ_j (−1)^j q_w^{j(j−1)/2} T^j_w X^{n−j}`; it is the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius in 3.3.

**Checks.**

- Depth zero is excluded from the operator compatibility. Take n = 2, λ = 0, R = ∅ and F⁺ = ℚ(√105) ⊂ F = ℚ(√−7, √−15): F/F⁺ is unramified at every finite place (3, 5 and 7 already have ramification index 2 in F⁺, and 2 is unramified), 2 splits completely in F with completions ℚ₂, and nd = 4, so Hypothesis 5.7.1 holds. With K = GL₂(ℤ₂), `I = Iw(2^{0,1})` and a = diag(2, 1), the cosets count `[K : K ∩ aKa^{−1}] = 3` (the points of P¹(𝔽₂); modulo 4, 96/32) and `[I : I ∩ aIa^{−1}] = 2` (modulo 4, 32/16). The constant function 1 is a form of level `U(l^{0,0})`; the spherical double coset sends it to 3, while after inclusion into level `U(l^{0,1})` the operator `U^1_{0,2}` sends it to 2. At depths c = 1 and c′ = 2 both indices are 2, as the coset representatives of Ger19 Lemma 2.3.3 predict, and the inclusion is equivariant.
- The ordinary idempotent lies in T̃^T and not in T^T. Let n = 2, F⁺_v = ℚ₂ at v | 2, λ = 0, and let π be ι-ordinary with π_v an unramified principal series. Then `π_v^{Iw(ṽ^{0,1})}` is two-dimensional; the T^j_w, `(T^n_w)^{−1}` (w ∉ T) and ⟨u⟩ act on the π-part by scalars (the ⟨u⟩ trivially, π_v being unramified), while `U^1_{0,ϖ_ṽ}` has as eigenvalues the two roots of `X² − tX + 2s`, with t and s the eigenvalues of the spherical operators `T^1_{0,ϖ_ṽ}` and `T^2_{0,ϖ_ṽ}`, and `U^2_{0,ϖ_ṽ}` acts by the unit s. Ordinarity makes t a unit, so the polynomial is X(X − t) modulo ϖ and Hensel gives roots α, β ∈ O with α a unit and β of valuation v(2). For t = s = 1 the roots are ≡ 27 and 6 mod 32 (27² − 27 + 2 = 704 = 22·32 and 6² − 6 + 2 = 32), with unit difference. Hence e = (U^1 − β)/(α − β) acts on this plane as diag(1, 0), which no scalar operator equals; `T^{T,ord}` acts on eS by the scalars of π, with identity e.
- For n = 1 the factor `∏_τ τ(u_1)^{1−1}` is 1, so the twisted and untwisted Λ-structures coincide; a nontrivial twist first appears for n = 2, as `u ↦ ∏_τ τ(u_2)^{−1}`.
- For n = 2 the Hecke polynomial at split w ∉ T is X² − T^1_w X + q_w T^2_w.
- If n = 2 and F⁺ has two places above 2 with F⁺_v = ℚ₂ (d = 2, so nd = 4), then T(l) ≅ ({±1} × ℤ₂)^4, Spec Λ has 16 irreducible components, one for each character of {±1}^4, each of dimension 5 = dim Λ, and Λ[1/2] is regular.
- The normalization is needed exactly when the scalar `(w₀λ)(α^j_{ϖ_ṽ}) = ∏_τ τ(ϖ_ṽ)^{λ_{τ,n−j+1}+⋯+λ_{τ,n}}` is not a unit. For n = 2, λ = (1, 0) and j = 1 it is ϖ⁰ = 1, while for j = n it is `∏_τ τ(ϖ_ṽ)^{Σ_iλ_{τ,i}}`, a non-unit when `Σ_{τ,i}λ_{τ,i} ≠ 0`; with the unnormalized operator the weight-independence isomorphism of 2.2 would not carry U^n_λ to U^n_0.

### 2.2 Hida theory at p = 2

Keep 2.1 with `U(l^{b₀,b₀})` sufficiently small for some b₀ ≥ 1 and U_v hyperspecial at split v ∉ T. Prove (BCGP25 §5.7, pp. 132–133; Ger19 Proposition 2.5.3, Corollary 2.5.4, Lemma 2.4.4, Proposition 2.6.1, Lemma 2.6.4 and the proof of Corollary 3.1.4):

1. The Pontryagin dual of `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O)` is finite free over `O⟦T(l^{b₀})⟧`; `T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)` is a finite Λ-algebra and, when `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O)` is nonzero, a finite faithful `O⟦T(l^{b₀})⟧`-algebra (Ger19 Corollary 2.5.4, whose statement omits the nonvanishing), and it and the finite-level algebras `T^{T,ord}` and `T̃^{T,ord}` at every level `U(l^{b,c})` with c ≥ 1 are reduced (Ger19 Lemma 2.4.4).
2. For a dominant λ, r ≥ 1 and c ≥ r, composing with the projection of M_λ onto its lowest weight line gives a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism `S^{ord}_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{c,c}), ϖ^{−r}O/O)` ≅ `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^{c,c}), ϖ^{−r}O/O)` carrying U^j_λ to U^j_0 and twisting ⟨u⟩ by (w₀λ)(u).
3. The primes ℘_λ ⊂ Λ attached to dominant weights λ are Zariski dense in Spec Λ.
4. `Hom_O(T^{T,ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)/℘_λ, ℚ̄₂)` = `Hom_O(T^{T,ord}_{λ,{χ_v}}(U(l^{1,1}), O), ℚ̄₂)` naturally (BCGP25 (5.7.4)).

The route is Geraghty's, at l = 2: exactness of A ↦ S(U, A) and freeness over group rings follow from sufficient smallness (AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §7.5), the projection to the lowest weight line is B_n(O)-equivariant, and no step of Ger19 §§2.2–2.6 uses the parity of l.

**Checks.**

- For n = 1, `U^1_{λ,ϖ}` acts on every algebraic character by a unit, so every character is ordinary and `T^{T,ord}` is the full Hecke algebra.
- For λ = 0 the isomorphism of (2) is the identity and the twist (w₀λ)(u) of ⟨u⟩ is 1; for n = 2 and λ_τ = (1, 0) at one τ it twists ⟨u⟩ by τ(u_2), the lowest weight of M_λ.
- Every O-algebra map `T^{T,ord}(U(l^∞), O) → ℚ̄₂` above some ℘_λ comes from a classical ordinary form of weight λ and level `U(l^{1,1})`, by (4); a map above a prime of Λ that is not of the form ℘_λ need not.
- `T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)` is semilocal and finite over Λ, hence the product of its localizations at its finitely many maximal ideals.
- The nonvanishing in (1) is needed. If `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O) = 0`, the rank r = dim `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^{b₀,b₀}), k)` of Ger19 Proposition 2.5.3 is 0, the Hecke algebra is the zero ring, which is finite over Λ, and the kernel of `O⟦T(l^{b₀})⟧ → 0` is the whole ring, so it is not faithful. The constant functions do not prevent this for n ≥ 2: on them `U^j_{0,ϖ_ṽ}` acts by `q_ṽ^{j(n−j)}`, a non-unit for 1 ≤ j ≤ n − 1, so they have no ordinary part.
- Faithfulness over Λ fails. The scalar −1_n lies in T(l), since −1 ≡ 1 modulo every place above 2. If U_v ∋ −1_n at every finite v ∤ 2 (for instance U_v hyperspecial off R and `U_v = ι_ṽ^{−1}Iw(ṽ)` on R), then −1_n is a global central element and `f(g·(−1_n)_2) = f(g·(−1_n)^{(2)})`, so [−1_n] ∈ Λ acts on `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O)` by the sign `ε₀ = (−1)^{dn(n−1)/2}∏_{v∈R}∏_j χ_{v,j}(−1) = ∏_{v∈R}∏_j χ_{v,j}(−1)` (nd ≡ 0 mod 4 makes the twist contribute 1). The nonzero element [−1_n] − ε₀ of Λ then acts by zero, so `Λ → T^{T,ord}` is not injective, although it is finite.

### 2.3 Ordinary constituents and ordinary RACSDC representations

Keep 2.1 and fix ı : ℚ̄₂ ≅ ℂ. For `λ ∈ (ℤ^n_+)^{Ĩ_2}`, A_λ = colim_U S_λ(U, ℚ̄₂) is a semisimple admissible `ℚ̄₂[G(𝔸^∞_{F⁺})]`-module. Prove that the irreducible submodules of ıA_λ are the finite parts of descents to G of automorphic representations π of GL_n(𝔸_F) of weight λ, isobaric sums of conjugate self-dual representations, that after localization at a non-Eisenstein maximal ideal only RACSDC π contribute, and that an irreducible submodule meets `S^{ord}_λ(U(l^{b,b}), ℚ̄₂)` for some b ≥ 1 exactly when π is ι-ordinary, that is `(ı^{−1}(⊗_{v|2} π_v))^{ord} ≠ 0` (BCGP25 §5.7, the paragraph defining A_λ, pp. 132–133).

## Layer 3: The ordinary global problem and the Hecke-valued Galois representation

### 3.1 The ordinary global problem S_χ and `R^{T,ord}`

Assume Hypothesis 5.7.1 and let π be an ordinary RACSDC automorphic representation of GL_n(𝔸_F) with `ρ̄ = r̄_{π,2}` : G_F → GL_n(k) absolutely irreducible. By Tho17 Lemma 2.2 (BCGP25 Lemma 5.1.2, p. 115) ρ̄ extends to r̄ : `G_{F⁺} → 𝒢_n(k)` with `ν ∘ r̄ = ε̄^{1−n}δ^n_{F/F⁺}` and `r̄^{−1}(𝒢_n^0(k)) = G_F`, unique up to 𝒢_n^0(k)-conjugacy. Let T ⊃ S_2 be a finite set of finite places of F⁺ split in F, R ⊂ T ∖ S_2, and `χ_v = (χ_{v,1}, …, χ_{v,n})` characters of `O^×_{F_ṽ}` trivial modulo ϖ for v ∈ R; assume BCGP25 Hypothesis 5.7.5: T contains the places under the ramification of π, and for v ∈ R, `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` is trivial and `π_ṽ^{Iw(ṽ)} ≠ 0`. Put S = T ∪ S_∞, Λ_v = O for v ∉ S_2 and `Λ_v = Λ_{GL_n,v} = O⟦I^{ab}_{F_ṽ}(2)^n⟧` for v ∈ S_2. Define the global problem (BCGP25 §5.7, Hypothesis 5.7.5, (5.7.6) and Definition 5.7.11, pp. 133–134)

S_χ = (F, r̄, O, `ε^{1−n}δ^n_{F/F⁺}`, S, {Λ_v}, `{D^{χ_v}_v}_{v∈R} ∪ {D^△_v}_{v∈S_2} ∪` `{Lift^□_v}_{v∈S∖(R∪S_2)}`),

where `D^{χ_v}_v` is the problem of lifts with inertial characteristic polynomial ∏_j `(X − χ_{v,j}(Art^{−1}σ)^{−1})` (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.29) and D^△_v is Geraghty's ordinary problem (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §1.9 over §1.3). Local class field theory gives `⊗̂_{v∈S_2} Λ_v ≅ Λ`, so `R_{S_χ}` and `R^loc_{S_χ,T}` are Λ-algebras. For R = ∅ write `S^{T,ord} = S_χ` and `R^{T,ord} = R_{S_χ}`.

Its API:

- `R_{S_χ}` represents deformations of type S_χ (1.1) and `R^loc_{S_χ,T} = ⊗̂_{v∈R}R^{χ_v}_v ⊗̂ ⊗̂_{v∈S_2}R^△_v ⊗̂ ⊗̂_{v∈T∖(R∪S_2)}R^□_v`.

- For a finite CM extension F′/F satisfying the same hypotheses, restriction gives a map `R^{T′,ord}_{F′}` → `R^{T,ord}_F` over the norm map `Λ_{F′} → Λ_F`.

### 3.2 The dimension bound

In the setting of 3.1 (so S = T ⊔ S_∞, S_2 ⊂ T and every place of T splits in F), prove:

(a) (The obstruction count.) Every irreducible component of Spec `R_{S_χ}` has dimension at least dim Λ − 1 = nd.

(b) (BCGP25 Lemma 5.7.7 and proof, pp. 133–134, with the printed 1 + [F:ℚ]n corrected, in the case R = ∅ that Theorem 5.7.14 uses.) Assume moreover R = ∅ and H¹(ρ̄(G_F), k) = 0 (for instance ρ̄(G_F) nearly adequate, 1.3). Then every irreducible component of Spec `R^{T,ord}` has dimension at least dim Λ = 1 + nd.

The route of (a): `R^loc_{S_χ,T}` has every component of dimension at least 1 + n²#T + dn(n+1)/2 ((5.7.8)): `R^{χ_v}_v` has components of dimension n² + 1 (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.29), R^□_v for v ∤ 2 is O-flat and equidimensional of dimension n² + 1 (Sho18 Theorem 2.5; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.13), and every component of R^△_v has dimension at least 1 + n² + [F⁺_v:ℚ₂]n(n+1)/2 (gap:dyadic-flag-ring-dimension; only this lower bound is used). Write `R^T_{S_χ} = P/J` with `P = R^loc_{S_χ,T}⟦x_1, …, x_r⟧`, `r = h¹_{S_χ,T}` (1.2(2)), and m_P the maximal ideal of P. The obstruction to lifting the universal T-framed lift from P/J to P/m_P J, relative to the local lifts given by `R^loc_{S_χ,T} → P`, is a class `o ∈ H²_{S_χ,T} ⊗_k J/m_P J` whose adjoint (J/m_P J)^∨ → `H²_{S_χ,T}` is injective (CHT08 Lemma 2.2.11 and Corollary 2.2.12, run with Thorne's complex `C^•_{S_χ,T}`), so J is generated by `h²_{S_χ,T}` elements. Krull's principal ideal theorem in each component of P, a complete local domain, gives every component of Spec `R^T_{S_χ}` dimension at least dim R^loc + h¹ − h². By 1.2(2)–(3) with q = 0, h¹ − h² = h⁰ − h³ − χ = −1 − (1 − #T + dn(n−1)/2); the −1 is `h³_{S_χ,T} = 1`, the term −h⁰(ad r̄(1)) of CHT08 Corollary 2.3.5. Hence every component of Spec `R^T_{S_χ}` has dimension at least (n² + 1)#T − 1 + nd, and subtracting the (n² + 1)#T − 1 framing variables (1.1) gives nd.

A sufficient route to (b) is to exhibit a minimal torsion relation in the scalar direction that cuts no dimension; the obstruction count of (a) alone does not provide it. Keep the notation of (a), with R = ∅. Under H¹(ρ̄(G_F), k) = 0 the class ψ of 1.4 is nonzero, and `(ad r̄/k)^{G_F} = 0`, since it injects into H¹(ρ̄(G_F), k), `(ad r̄)^{G_F}` being k; so Thorne's complex with trace-zero coefficients, whose Tate dual is (ad r̄/k)(1), has vanishing h³, and the long exact sequence for 0 → ad⁰ r̄ → ad r̄ → k → 0 (the trace) carries `h³_{S_χ,T} = 1` to the trace quotient: the relation lost in (a) is in the scalar direction. Let `M = ∏_{v∈T} µ_{2^∞}(F_ṽ)`, for ζ ∈ M let `s_ζ = ∏_{v∈T} det ρ_v(Art_{F_ṽ}(ζ_v))`, formed from the universal local liftings in `R^loc_{S_χ,T}`, an element of 2-power order, and let M_χ ⊂ M be the tuples ζ for which s_ζ becomes 1 in `R^T_{S_χ}`, so that s_ζ − 1 ∈ J. The diagonal tuples lie in M_χ. For `ζ ∈ µ_{2^∞}(F)` placed at every ṽ, the image of s_ζ is `det ρ^univ(Art_F(a_ζ))`, a_ζ the idele with ζ at every ṽ (v ∈ T) and 1 elsewhere. Since ζζ^c = 1, Hilbert 90 gives y ∈ F^× with y/y^c = ζ, and `a_ζ = y·z^{−1}·u` with `z ∈ 𝔸^×_{F⁺}` and u a unit at every finite place outside S times an archimedean idele: at a split v the components of y at (ṽ, ṽ^c) are `(y_ṽ, ζ^{−1}y_ṽ) = (ζ^{−1}y_ṽ)·(ζ, 1)`; at an inert v, F_v/F⁺_v is unramified, so `y ∈ F⁺_v^×·O^×_{F_v}`; and the signs of z at the real places can be chosen with `δ_{F/F⁺}(z) = 1`, so that `z = f·N_{F/F⁺}(w)` with f ∈ F⁺^× and w ∈ 𝔸_F^×. As det ρ^univ is unramified outside S and `det ρ^univ·(det ρ^univ)^c = χ^n|G_F = ε^{n(1−n)}` (the multiplier of det ∘ r, below), `det ρ^univ(Art_F(a_ζ)) = (ε^{n(1−n)}(Art_F(w)))^{−1} = ε(Art_{F⁺}(z))^{n(n−1)}` =: c, a constant of `(ℤ₂^×)²` independent of the lift (n(n−1) is even). The same computation for `r_{π,2}`, whose determinant satisfies the same identity, gives `∏_{v∈T} det r_{π,2}(Art_{F_ṽ}(ζ)) = c`, a root of unity; the roots of unity of ℤ₂^× are ±1 and −1 is not a square there, so c = 1 and s_ζ − 1 ∈ J. Through these determinants P is an algebra over the finite local O-algebra O[M]; Spec P is the union over the characters θ of M, with values in O after enlarging E, of the closed sets P_θ on which M acts through θ, every irreducible component of Spec P lies in some P_θ, and on a component inside P_θ the element s_ζ − 1 is the constant θ(ζ) − 1, which is 0 when θ(ζ) = 1 and a nonzero element of ϖO otherwise. The two inputs are: (★) some ζ ∈ M_χ has s_ζ − 1 ∉ m_P J, so that s_ζ − 1 extends to a minimal generating set of J; and (C) every irreducible component of Spec P/ϖ lies on an irreducible component of Spec P contained in a P_θ with θ(ζ) = 1. Given (★), an irreducible component C of Spec `R^T_{S_χ}` not contained in V(ϖ) lies on a component D of Spec P with θ(ζ) = 1 (on any other, s_ζ − 1 is a nonzero constant of ϖO that vanishes on C), and given (C) so does a component contained in V(ϖ); modulo the prime of D the ideal J is generated by the other h² − 1 generators, so Krull's theorem in the complete local domain of D, of dimension at least 1 + n²#T + dn(n+1)/2 + h¹, gives C dimension at least that bound minus h² − 1, and the count of (a) with one relation fewer gives (b). (C) holds in the prepared situation of 4.3 (T = S_2 ⊔ {v₁} ⊔ R): the components of Spec P are products of local components (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §4.6; BCGNT25 Lemma 3.2.4), at v | 2 every `R^△_v/Q` is irreducible and O-flat with the same special fibre for every minimal prime Q of Λ_v (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.24), at v ∈ R the functors `D^{χ_v}_v` and D^1_v agree on Artinian k-algebras (3.1), and at v₁ the lifts are sums of characters (§2.29). At the unprepared field of (b), (C) at v | 2 is gap:dyadic-flag-ring-special-fibre, and at v ∈ T ∖ S_2 it is part of this target: it would follow from the Breuil–Mézard identity for l ≠ p, which identifies the special-fibre cycles of fixed-type rings whose types are congruent modulo ϖ, but Sho18 Theorem 4.6 proves that identity for l > 2 only. (★) is the step the sources do not supply, and it concerns the scalar direction: det : 𝒢_n → 𝒢_1, (g, a) ↦ (det g, a^n), j ↦ j, is a homomorphism (both sides of `j(g, a)j^{−1}` = (`a·ᵗg^{−1}`, a) map to (a^n·det `g^{−1}`, a^n)) whose derivative on ad r̄ = M_n(k) is the trace; det ∘ r is a lift of the rank-one problem of multiplier `χ^n δ^{n+1}_{F/F⁺}` with all lifts allowed at T; the map of rank-one presentations P_1 → P sends J_1 into J. ### 3.3 The Galois representation over the ordinary Hecke algebra

In the setting of 3.1, suppose U_v is hyperspecial for v ∉ T, `U_v ⊂ ι_ṽ^{−1}Iw(ṽ)` for v ∈ R, and `π_ṽ^{U_v} ≠ 0` for every v ∈ T ∖ (R ∪ S_2) (for instance U_v sufficiently deep in the sense of 3.5). Then π contributes to `S^{ord}_{λ_π,{1}}(U(l^{b,c}), ℚ̄₂)` for some 0 ≤ b ≤ c with c ≥ 1 (2.3: at the places of R by Hypothesis 5.7.5, off T because π is unramified there, above 2 because π is ordinary), hence by 2.2(2) to `S^{ord}_{0,{1}}(U(l^∞), E/O)`; let m be the maximal ideal of `T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)` so obtained, transported from `T^{T,ord}_{{1}}(U(l^∞), O)` through `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), k)` = `S^{ord}_{0,{1}}(U(l^∞), k)`, the χ_v being trivial modulo ϖ. Prove that there is a surjection of Λ-algebras `R_{S_χ}` → `T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)_m`, and that the corresponding r_m : `G_{F⁺}` → `𝒢_n(T^{T,ord}_{{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), O)_m)`, unique up to strict equivalence, is characterized by: for every finite place v ∉ T split as ww^c in F, r_m is unramified at w and w^c and r_m(Frob_w) has characteristic polynomial `X^n + ⋯ + (−1)^j q_w^{j(j−1)/2}T^j_w X^{n−j} + ⋯ + (−1)^n q_w^{n(n−1)/2}T^n_w` (BCGP25 Proposition 5.7.9 and proof, p. 134, with the fixed-vector hypothesis at T ∖ (R ∪ S_2) added; source issue below).

The route: glue the 2-adic Galois representations of the ordinary constituents (2.3, which states them at l = 2) along the reduced finite-level Hecke algebras and pass to the limit as in Ger19 Proposition 2.7.4. The 𝒢_n-valued extension of the residual representation uses Tho17 Lemma 2.2 in place of CHT08 Lemma 2.1.4, and its lift and uniqueness up to strict equivalence use Tho17 Lemma 2.4 in place of CHT08 Lemma 2.1.12, which needs residue characteristic > 2 and enters Ger19 Propositions 2.7.3–2.7.4 through CHT08 Propositions 3.4.2 and 3.4.4 (Ger19 §2.7, opening discussion, p. 22, and proofs of Propositions 2.7.3–2.7.4, pp. 25–26). The multiplier `ε^{1−n}δ^n_{F/F⁺}` of r_m is 2.3(ii′) applied to each constituent, whose Galois representation is irreducible because m is non-Eisenstein; the sign of the glued pairing, an element of {±1} in each reduced finite-level localized Hecke algebra, is read off in the product of the characteristic-zero fields of its constituents, into which that algebra embeds, so it is the common sign of the constituents and passes to the limit. At p = 2 this multiplier is not determined by r̄, both candidates `ε^{1−n}δ^n_{F/F⁺}` and `ε^{1−n}δ^{n+1}_{F/F⁺}` being trivial modulo 2 (Tho17, proof of Theorem 4.1, pp. 19–20). The condition at v | 2 is Ger19 Corollary 3.1.4, checked on the dense classical points of 2.2(3)–(4); at v ∈ R the inertial characteristic polynomial is as in Ger19 Proposition 2.7.4(5). Surjectivity holds because T_m is generated by the T^j_w, coefficients of characteristic polynomials of r_m, and by the diamond operators, the image of Λ.

**Checks.**

- The map is one of Λ-algebras for the twisted Λ-structure of 2.1; the untwisted structure differs from it by the character `(w₀ν)^{−1}`, which is trivial for n = 1 and nontrivial for n = 2 `(u ↦ ∏_τ τ(u_2)^{−1})`, so for n ≥ 2 only the twisted structure can be matched with `Λ_{GL_n,v}` on the deformation side.
- For n = 2 the characteristic polynomial is X² − T^1_w X + q_w T^2_w, the Hecke polynomial of 2.1.
- For n = 2 the two candidate multipliers `ε^{−1}` and `ε^{−1}δ_{F/F⁺}` take the values −1 and +1 at c_v and agree modulo 2; only the first is the multiplier of S_χ, and a Galois representation over `T_m` with the second would not be of type S_χ. The choice is made by 2.3(ii′), not by r̄.
- A point of T_m above ℘_λ gives an ordinary RACSDC π′ of weight λ (2.2(4), 2.3) with `r_{π′,2}` the specialization of r_m.
- The fixed-vector hypothesis cannot be dropped: if v ∈ T ∖ (R ∪ S_2) is a place at which `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` is ramified (so π is ramified at ṽ and v ∈ T by Hypothesis 5.7.5, while v ∉ R because R requires ρ̄ trivial) and `U_v = GL_n(O_{F_ṽ})`, then every lift of `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` is ramified, so by local–global compatibility (AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.5) no RACSDC π′ with `r̄_{π′,2} ≅ ρ̄` has a U_v-fixed vector, `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O)` has no constituent with residual representation ρ̄, and no maximal ideal m with r̄_m ≅ r̄ exists; the printed proposition asserts one.

*Needs:* 3.1; 2.2; 2.3, including (ii′) for the multiplier; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.4; AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.3 (*The polarization sign of the constructed system*) and AG2.5.

### 3.4 Uniform local fixed vectors

Let w ∤ 2 be a finite place of the CM field F and π a RACSDC automorphic representation of GL_n(𝔸_F). Prove that there is a compact open subgroup `U_w ⊂ GL_n(O_{F_w})`, depending only on `r̄_{π,2}|G_{F_w}`, such that every RACSDC π′ with `r̄_{π′,2} ≅ r̄_{π,2}` has `(π′_w)^{U_w} ≠ 0` (BCGP25 Lemma 5.7.12 and proof, p. 134).

The route: Spec R^□[1/2] of `r̄_{π,2}|G_{F_w}` has finitely many components, on each of which the restriction to inertia of the Weil–Deligne representation is constant (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.24). By local–global compatibility and temperedness (AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII AG2.5), `WD(r_{π′,2}|G_{F_w})^{F-ss}` ≅ rec(π′_w `⊗ |det|^{(1−n)/2}`) with π′_w generic, so only finitely many restrictions to inertia occur. By gap:inertial-types-and-block-levels (i) they meet finitely many Bernstein blocks, and by (ii) each block has a compact open U with fixed vectors in every irreducible member; U_w is the intersection.

**Checks.**

- U_w depends only on `r̄_{π,2}|G_{F_w}`, not on π′.
- The conductor of the seed π alone does not bound the others: for n = 1 and q_w ≡ 1 mod 4, characters of k(w)^× of order 4 are ≡ 1 modulo the maximal ideal and give lifts of conductor 1 of the trivial residual character, while the seed may be unramified.
- The trivial and Steinberg representations of GL₂(F_w) lie in the same Bernstein block [T, 1] while their parameters have monodromy N = 0 and N ≠ 0; the block is determined by the restriction to inertia without N, which is why (i) of the gap forgets the monodromy.

### 3.5 Sufficiently deep tame level

In the setting of 3.1 let v ∈ T ∖ S_2 and ṽ | v the chosen place. Define a compact open subgroup U_v ⊂ G(F⁺_v) ≅ GL_n(F_ṽ) to be sufficiently deep if every RACSDC π′ of GL_n(𝔸_F) with `r̄_{π′,2} ≅ ρ̄` has `(π′_ṽ)^{U_v} ≠ 0`, and a level U to be sufficiently deep at T if U_v is sufficiently deep for every v ∈ T ∖ S_2 (BCGP25 Definition 5.7.13, p. 134).

Its API:

- Sufficiently deep subgroups exist and can be chosen depending only on `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` (3.4).
- If U′_v ⊂ U_v and U_v is sufficiently deep, so is U′_v, since `(π′_ṽ)^{U_v} ⊂ (π′_ṽ)^{U′_v}`; in particular the intersection of two sufficiently deep subgroups is sufficiently deep.
- If U is sufficiently deep at T, every ℚ̄₂-point of `R^{T,ord}` that is automorphic, unramified outside T and ordinary above 2 occurs in `S^{ord}(U(l^∞))`.

**Checks.**

- If every lift of `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` is unramified, then `GL_n(O_{F_ṽ})` is sufficiently deep, since every π′_ṽ is then unramified and has spherical vectors.
- For n = 1, trivial `ρ̄|G_{F_ṽ}` and q_v ≡ 1 mod 4, the characters of k(ṽ)^× of order 4 give lifts of conductor 1, so `O^×_{F_ṽ}` is not sufficiently deep while `1 + ϖ_ṽO_{F_ṽ}` is (every lift is tamely ramified with ramification through k(ṽ)^×(2), by LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.24).
- The subgroup K_1(ṽ^c) attached to the conductor c of the seed `r_{π,2}|G_{F_ṽ}` alone need not be sufficiently deep: another lift may have larger conductor at ṽ (3.4, second Check).

*Needs:* 3.4; 3.1.

### 3.6 Taylor–Wiles level structures for ordinary forms at p = 2

In the setting of 3.3, suppose moreover that `U(l^{1,1})` is sufficiently small (2.1), let m be the maximal ideal of 3.3, with `r̄_m ≅ r̄`, and let H be the Pontryagin dual of `S^{ord}_{0,{χ_v}}(U(l^∞), E/O)_m`. ## Layer 4: The dyadic ordinary R = T theorem

For p = 2 one cannot choose an auxiliary place at which every deformation is unramified, so BCGP use an auxiliary place v₁ with regular semisimple residual Frobenius, patch, produce automorphic lifts on every choice of local components by Ihara avoidance, and then remove v₁ by a solvable base change over which all those lifts are unramified at v₁. Throughout this layer the six hypotheses of BCGP25 Theorem 5.7.14 are: (1) there is an ordinary RACSDC automorphic representation π of GL_n(𝔸_F) with `r̄_{π,2} ≅ ρ̄` for a continuous ρ̄ : G_F → GL_n(𝔽̄₂), n ≥ 2; (2) F/F⁺ is unramified at every finite place, and every place of F⁺ above 2 or under a place where π is ramified splits in F; (3) nd ≡ 0 mod 4; (4) ρ̄(G_F) is nearly adequate (1.3); (5) ρ̄(G_F) contains a regular semisimple element; (6) if n is even, `(ρ̄, ε^{1−n}δ^n_{F/F⁺})` is strongly residually odd at some infinite place of F⁺. T is a finite set of finite places of F⁺, all split in F, containing S_2 and the places under the ramification of π.

### 4.1 Solvable preparation and the auxiliary place

Assume (1)–(6). Prove that there is a solvable CM extension F′/F, linearly disjoint over F from the field cut out by ρ̄, such that, with T′ the places of F′⁺ above T and E enlarged: (1)–(6) hold over F′ with `ρ̄(G_{F′}) = ρ̄(G_F)`; F′ = F′⁺(√−1); `ρ̄|G_{F′_ṽ}` is trivial for v ∈ T′; [F′⁺_v:ℚ₂] > n(n−1)/2 + 1 for v | 2; and for v ∈ T′ ∖ S_2, `π_{F′,ṽ}^{Iw(ṽ)} ≠ 0`, q_v ≡ 1 mod 2^N with 2^N > n and O containing a primitive 2^N-th root of unity, where 2^N ∥ q_v − 1, and every lift of `ρ̄|G_{F_u}` (u the place of F under ṽ, v ∈ T ∖ S_2, the original field) becomes unipotently ramified on restriction to `G_{F′_w}` for every place w of F′ above u (BCGP25 Lemma 5.6.2, p. 128). Moreover there are infinitely many finite places v₁ ∉ T′ of F′⁺, split in F′, with `ρ̄(Frob_{ṽ₁})` regular semisimple; for such v₁ and any torsion-free compact open `U_{v₁}`, every level `U(l^{b,c})` with that component is sufficiently small, and Hypotheses 5.7.1 and 5.7.5 hold for T′ ∪ {v₁} (BCGP25 proof of Theorem 5.7.14, first two paragraphs, and Remark 5.7.15, pp. 135–136).

The route: all conditions except F′ = F′⁺(√−1) are local; choose local extensions trivializing ρ̄ at T, raising the local degrees above 2, making every lift of the original local representations at T ∖ S_2 unipotently ramified (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.24, the extension of BCGP25 Lemma 5.6.2), giving Iwahori-fixed vectors and the roots of unity, and realize them by a solvable CM extension disjoint from the field of ρ̄ (PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructure §2.5 with prescribed completions from PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems §2.16); ordinarity and the RACSDC property survive (PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructure §3.47). Adjoining √−1 does not change the image, since a nearly adequate group has H¹(H, k) = 0 and so no subgroup of index 2. Hypothesis (5) and Chebotarev give v₁, and a torsion-free `U_{v₁}` gives sufficient smallness (AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §7.5).

**Checks.**

- The unipotence clause is about lifts of the original `ρ̄|G_{F_u}`, restricted to F′: a lift `ψ ⊕ 1^{n−1}` of the trivial `ρ̄|G_{F′_ṽ}`, with ψ a tame character through `k(ṽ)^× → µ_{2^N} ⊂ O^×`, is ≡ 1 mod ϖ but has inertial characteristic polynomial `(X − ψ(σ))(X − 1)^{n−1} ≠` (X − 1)^n; it is a lift over F′ that is not a restriction from F, and the clause does not cover it. These are exactly the characters used in 4.3.
- Since `ρ̄(G_{F′}) = ρ̄(G_F)`, near adequacy, the regular semisimple element and strong residual oddness are checked once over F.
- For p odd the usual auxiliary place makes every deformation unramified there; at p = 2 every lift at v₁ is only a sum of characters (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.29), which 4.6 uses to remove v₁.

*Needs:* 1.3; 2.1; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§2.24, 2.29; PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructure §2.5; PotentialModularityAndCompatibleSystems §2.16; PotentialAutomorphyInfrastructure §3.47; AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups §7.5.

### 4.2 Ring–module patching over a weight algebra with torsion

Let Λ be a complete Noetherian local O-algebra that is finite free over a power series subring Λ⁰ = O⟦w_1, …, w_s⟧, so dim Λ = dim Λ⁰ = 1 + s. Let q, j, g ≥ 0, h = q + j, S_∞ = Λ⟦X_1, …, X_h⟧ (X_1, …, X_q the Taylor–Wiles variables, `X_{q+1}`, …, X_h the framing variables), S⁰_∞ = Λ⁰⟦X_1, …, X_h⟧, a_∞ = (X_1, …, X_h), and for N ≥ 1 let S_N = `S_∞/((1 + X_i)^{2^N} − 1)_{i≤q}`. Let R^loc be a complete Noetherian local Λ-algebra, R_∞ = R^loc⟦Y_1, …, Y_g⟧, R a complete Noetherian local Λ-algebra with a surjection R_∞ ↠ R of R^loc-algebras, and H ≠ 0 an R-module finite over Λ. Suppose that for each N ≥ 1 there are a complete Noetherian local R^loc-algebra R_N with a map S_∞ → R_N factoring through S_N, a surjection of R^loc-algebras R_∞ ↠ R_N, an isomorphism R_N/a_∞ ≅ R compatible with the maps from R_∞, and an R_N-module H_N finite free over S_N with H_N/a_∞ ≅ H compatibly with these actions. Prove that there are a map of Λ-algebras S_∞ → R_∞, an R_∞-module H_∞ finite free over S_∞, a surjection R_∞/a_∞ ↠ R and an isomorphism H_∞/a_∞ ≅ H compatible with it; and that if dim R_∞ ≤ dim S_∞ then `depth_{R_∞} H_∞` = dim R_∞ and `Supp_{R_∞} H_∞` is a union of irreducible components of Spec R_∞ of dimension dim S_∞. The same holds for two such systems with compatible identifications modulo ϖ, producing patched data compatible modulo ϖ.

The route: S_N modulo the standard cutoffs (ϖ^m and powers of the maximal ideal) is a finite ring because Λ is finite over Λ⁰, so the extraction of a compatible subsystem is DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§5.2–5.4, which uses no condition on p; freeness of given rank over each finite quotient passes to the limit. H_∞ is then finite free over S⁰_∞, a regular local ring with dim S⁰_∞ = dim S_∞, and the numerical depth comparison of DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §5.5 (first part, for a regular local ring and a free module) gives `depth_{R_∞} H_∞` ≥ `depth_{S⁰_∞} H_∞` = dim S_∞ ≥ dim R_∞; a maximal Cohen–Macaulay module over a Noetherian local ring is supported on a union of components of maximal dimension (Tay08 Lemma 2.3). This is the patching of Tho12 Theorem 8.6 and KT17 §3, Proposition 3.1 (abstract patching over a ring Λ of CNL_O with S_∞ = Λ⟦S_1, …, S_q⟧), over Λ.

**Checks.**

- For q = j = 0, S_∞ = Λ, S_N = Λ for every N and H_N = H; the conclusion says that H is finite free over Λ and, when dim R_∞ ≤ dim Λ, of maximal depth over R_∞.
- For n = 2 and F⁺ = ℚ(√105), F = ℚ(√−7, √−15) (F/F⁺ is unramified at every finite place, 2 splits completely in F since −7 ≡ −15 ≡ 105 ≡ 1 mod 8, and nd = 4), the two places above 2 have F⁺_v = ℚ₂ and Λ = O⟦({±1} × ℤ₂)^4⟧ = O[{±1}^4]⟦w_1, …, w_4⟧ is finite free of rank 16 over Λ⁰ = O⟦w_1, …, w_4⟧ but is not a domain ((1 − [−1])(1 + [−1]) = 0) and not a power series ring, so DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra Layer 8, which requires Λ = O⟦w_1, …, w_s⟧, does not apply directly, while the reduction to Λ⁰ does. One place with F⁺_v = ℚ₂ alone is no instance: it forces F⁺ = ℚ, which the standing hypotheses exclude (1.4).
- If dim R_∞ = dim S_∞ + 1, the maximal-depth conclusion fails, since `depth_{R_∞} H_∞` ≤ dim S_∞ < dim R_∞.

*Needs:* DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§5.2–5.5, 6.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`.

### 4.3 Patched modules for the unipotent and distinct-character problems

In the situation of 4.1 (over F′, written F), write T = S_2 ⊔ {v₁} ⊔ R, choose for v ∈ R pairwise distinct characters `χ_{v,1}`, …, `χ_{v,n}` of `O^×_{F_ṽ}` trivial modulo ϖ, and let S_1, S_χ be the problems of 3.1. ### 4.4 Components of the patched rings

In the situation of 4.3, prove for each minimal prime Q of Λ (BCGP25 proof of Theorem 5.7.14, list (1)–(5), p. 137; BCGNT25 Lemma 3.2.4 and proof, pp. 33–34): (1) the generic points of `R_{χ,∞}/Q` and `R_{1,∞}/Q` have characteristic 0; (2) the irreducible components of Spec `R_{χ,∞}/Q` and of Spec `R_{1,∞}/Q` biject with products over v ∈ R ∪ {v₁} of irreducible components C_v of the local rings; (3) the irreducible components of Spec `R_{χ,∞}/(Q, ϖ)` = Spec `R_{1,∞}/(Q, ϖ)` biject with products of irreducible components C̄_v of the special fibres of the local rings; (4) the irreducible components of Spec `R_{χ,∞}/Q` biject with those of Spec `R^□_{v₁}`; (5) for each component C̄ = ⊗C̄_v of Spec `R_{1,∞}/(Q, ϖ)` there are components C_v of Spec R^1_v (v ∈ R) and components `C^1_{v₁}`, …, `C^s_{v₁}` of Spec `R^□_{v₁}` (s ≥ 1) such that the components of Spec `R_{1,∞}/Q` generalizing C̄ are exactly the `C^i_{v₁} ⊗ ⊗_{v∈R} C_v`.

The route: components of a completed tensor product of local rings whose components are geometrically irreducible of characteristic 0 are products of local components, in the generic and the special fibre (BLGHT11 Lemma 3.3 as used in BCGNT25 Lemma 3.2.4; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §4.6), and power series variables do not change this. Above 2, R^△_v/Q is geometrically irreducible with generically reduced special fibre (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.24). At v ∈ R, `R^{χ_v}_v` is irreducible with characteristic-0 generic point and every prime of R^1_v minimal over ϖ contains a unique minimal prime (§2.29), `R^1_v/ϖ = R^{χ_v}_v/ϖ`. At v₁ the lifting ring has finitely many components, all of characteristic 0 (§2.13); unique generalizations come from DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.7.

**Checks.**

### 4.5 Component support by Ihara avoidance

In the situation of 4.3, prove (BCGP25 proof of Theorem 5.7.14, from "Fix for the moment a minimal prime Q" to the existence of π_Y, pp. 137–138): (a) for every minimal prime Q of Λ and every set X of irreducible components C_v of Spec R^1_v (v ∈ R) there is an irreducible component `C_{X,v₁}` of Spec `R^□_{v₁}` such that the component `C_X = C_{X,v₁} ⊗ ⊗_{v∈R} C_v` of Spec `R_{1,∞}/Q` (4.4(2)) lies in the support of `H_{1,∞}`; with R^loc_X the quotient of `R^loc_{S_1,T}/Q` by the prime of `C_{X,v₁} ⊗ ⊗_{v∈R} C_v` and `R_X = R_{S_1}` `⊗_{R^loc_{S_1,T}}` R^loc_X, Spec R_X lies in the support of H_1, (R_X)_red is a quotient of `T^{T,ord}_{{1}}(U(l^∞), O)_{m₁}`, R_X is a finite Λ-algebra and every component of Spec R_X has dimension at least dim Λ; (b) for every choice Y of irreducible components C_v of the local rings at v ∈ R ∪ S_2 there is an ordinary RACSDC π_Y with `r̄_{π_Y,2} ≅ ρ̄`, `r_{π_Y,2}` unramified outside T, and `r_{π_Y,2}|G_{F_ṽ}` lying on C_v and on no other component for each v ∈ R ∪ S_2.

### 4.8 The dyadic ordinary reduced R = T theorem

Assume (1)–(6), let `R^{T,ord}` be as in 4.7 and `T^{T,ord}(U(l^∞))_m` the localized universal ordinary Hecke algebra (2.1) with the surjection of 3.3. ## Layer 5: GSp₄ deformation problems and the selected ordinary problem

The GSp₄ side for p > 2. Whitmore's Taylor–Wiles places need only semisimple Frobenius, which allows the weaker big-image condition "GSp₄-reasonable" in place of BCGP21's vastness; the ordinary condition at p is the flag-image ring R^△_v, which is not residually p-distinguished in general, so the problem is cut down to the component through the automorphic seed.

### 5.1 GSp₄ deformation problems over a weight algebra and the framing ring

Let F⁺ be totally real, p a prime, S_p the places above p and ρ̄ : `G_{F⁺} → GSp₄(k)` continuous and absolutely irreducible with similitude `ν ∘ ρ̄ = ε̄^{−1}`. Let S ⊃ S_p be a finite set of finite places containing those where ρ̄ ramifies; for v ∈ S fix Λ_v ∈ CNL_O and put `Λ = ⊗̂_{v∈S} Λ_v`. Define a lift of `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` to A `∈ CNL_{Λ_v}` as a continuous ρ : `G_{F⁺_v} → GSp₄(A)` reducing to ρ̄ with `ν ∘ ρ = ε^{−1}`, with lifting functor D^□_v represented by R^□_v; a local problem is a subfunctor D_v represented by a quotient of R^□_v and stable under ĜSp₄(A) = ker(GSp₄(A) → GSp₄(k)); a global problem is 𝒮 = (ρ̄, S, {Λ_v}, {D_v}), with lifts of `G_{F⁺,S}` of type 𝒮, strict equivalence by ĜSp₄(A)-conjugacy, and T-framed lifts `(ρ, (γ_v)_{v∈T}) up` to `(aρa^{−1}, (aγ_v))` (BCGP25 §6.1, Definitions 6.1.1–6.1.4 and (6.1.5), pp. 140–141).

Its API:

- D^□_v(k[ε]) ≅ Z¹(F⁺_v, Lie(ker ν)) = Z¹(F⁺_v, 𝔰𝔭₄) through ρ(σ) = (1 + εφ(σ))ρ̄(σ), and two liftings are ĜSp₄(k[ε])-conjugate iff their classes in H¹(F⁺_v, ad ρ̄) agree.
- D_𝒮 and D^T_𝒮 are represented by R_𝒮 and R^T_𝒮, since the centralizer of the image in ĜSp₄ is Ĝ_m (GlobalGaloisDeformations §3.3; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §1.17).
- For T ≠ ∅, D^T_𝒮 → D_𝒮 is a torsor under `(∏_{v∈T} ĜSp₄)/Ĝ_m`, so R^T_𝒮 is formally smooth over R_𝒮 of relative dimension 11#T − 1; with the framing ring 𝒯 = `Λ⟦x_1, …, x_{11#T−1}⟧`, a representative of the universal deformation gives R^T_𝒮 ≅ R_𝒮 ⊗̂_Λ 𝒯 (as in LocalGaloisDeformationRings §F.2.6).
- When Λ_v = O off T, `R^loc_{𝒮,T} = ⊗̂_{v∈T} R_v` maps to R^T_𝒮 by (ρ, (γ_v)) `↦ (γ_v^{−1}ρ|G_{F⁺_v}γ_v)_v`.
- Composing with GSp₄ → GL₄ (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.5) maps D_𝒮 to the GL₄ deformation functor with the induced conditions.

**Checks.**

- For #T = 1 the framing ring has 10 variables; for T = ∅, R^T_𝒮 = R_𝒮.
- dim ad ρ̄ = 11 and dim ad⁰ρ̄ = 10, so for #T = 3 the framing ring has 32 variables over Λ; GL₄-framings would give 16·3 − 1 = 47.
- The global lifts are representations of `G_{F⁺}`; with the source's G_F of Definition 6.1.4 read as a CM field the problem would not be defined (source issue).

*Needs:* LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§1.17, F.2.6; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.5; GlobalGaloisDeformations §3.3; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.12.

### 5.2 GSp₄-reasonable residual representations

Let p ≥ 3, k a finite field of characteristic p, F a number field and ρ̄ : G_F → GSp₄(k) continuous and absolutely GSp₄-irreducible (its image lies in no proper parabolic subgroup over k̄). Let 𝔰𝔭₄ be the Lie algebra of Sp₄ over k (Mathlib `LieAlgebra.Symplectic.sp`) with the adjoint action of GSp₄(k), 𝔰𝔭₄^∨ its dual, and for semisimple h ∈ GSp₄(k) let 𝔷_h = Lie `Z(Z_{GSp₄}(h)) ∩ 𝔰𝔭₄`. Define a subgroup H ⊂ GSp₄(k) to satisfy the reasonableness conditions if (a) H⁰(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0 and (b) for every nonzero simple k[H]-submodule W ⊂ 𝔰𝔭₄^∨ there are a semisimple h ∈ H, w ∈ W and z ∈ 𝔷_h with w(z) ≠ 0; and ρ̄ to be GSp₄-reasonable if for all sufficiently large n the group `H_n = ρ̄(G_{F(ζ_{p^n})})` satisfies (a) and (b) and, when ζ_p lies in the field cut out by 𝔰𝔭₄^∨, also H¹(H_n, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0 (WHI22 Definitions 3.15 and 3.19 and Lemma 3.20, pp. 21–24; WHI22 v4 renames the notion).

Its API:

- If `H¹(ρ̄(G_{F(ζ_p)}), k) = 0` the groups H_n are independent of n, so the conditions can be checked on H_1 (WHI22 Lemma 3.20).
- A vast ρ̄ (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11) is GSp₄-reasonable: an enormous image gives (a) by absolute irreducibility and (b) with h regular semisimple, for which 𝔷_h is the Cartan subalgebra through h, every root is nontrivial on h and the trace form is nondegenerate on the Cartan subalgebra for p > 2.
- If `H¹(ρ̄(G_{F(ζ_p)}), k) = 0` and `ρ̄(G_{F(ζ_p)})` is GSp₄-adequate (WHI22 Definition 3.15), ρ̄ is GSp₄-reasonable.
- Condition (a) excludes images of H_n in a proper Levi subgroup, whose centre gives invariants in 𝔰𝔭₄ ≅ 𝔰𝔭₄^∨.
- A GSp₄-reasonable ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible as a four-dimensional representation (BCGP25 Hypothesis 6.3.1 (1), p. 146). If a k̄-subspace 0 ≠ W ≠ V is ρ̄(G_F)-stable, so is W^⊥, and W ∩ W^⊥ is a stable isotropic subspace, hence zero because ρ̄(G_F) lies in no proper parabolic; so V ⊗ k̄ = V₁ ⊕ V₂ is an orthogonal sum of stable non-degenerate planes, and the image lies in the stabilizer `M = GL₂ ×_{det} GL₂` of the decomposition. A semisimple h ∈ H_n lies in a maximal torus of M, which is a maximal torus of GSp₄, and the centre of the connected centralizer `Z_{GSp₄}(h)` lies in every maximal torus of it, so 𝔷_h ⊂ 𝔪 = 𝔰𝔭(V₁) ⊕ 𝔰𝔭(V₂). Being defined over k, 𝔷_h lies in the H_n-stable k-subspace 𝔪_k = ∩_σ σ(𝔪), σ ∈ Gal(k̄/k), of dimension at most 6 < 10, and (b) fails for every simple k[H_n]-submodule of the annihilator of 𝔪_k in 𝔰𝔭₄^∨.
- Let F′/F be finite, E the field cut out by ρ̄ and L ⊂ E the field cut out by 𝔰𝔭₄^∨, so that LF′ is the field cut out by 𝔰𝔭₄^∨ for `ρ̄|G_{F′}`. If `E(ζ_{p^∞})` and `F′(ζ_{p^∞})` are linearly disjoint over `F(ζ_{p^∞})`, then `ρ̄(G_{F′(ζ_{p^n})})` = H_n for all large n; if moreover ζ_p ∈ LF′ only when ζ_p ∈ L, then `ρ̄|G_{F′}` is GSp₄-reasonable whenever ρ̄ is. Both conditions hold when F′ is linearly disjoint from `E(ζ_{p^∞})` over F; the first alone does not suffice (first Check).

**Checks.**

- Base change by ζ_p can destroy reasonableness. Let p = 5 and H = (SL₂(𝔽₅) × SL₂(𝔽₅)) ⋊ C₂ ⊂ Sp₄(𝔽₅), acting on an orthogonal sum V₁ ⊕ V₂ of two symplectic planes, C₂ swapping them. As an H-module 𝔰𝔭₄ ≅ 𝔰𝔭₄^∨ is A ⊕ B with A = 𝔰𝔩₂ ⊕ 𝔰𝔩₂, induced from `𝔰𝔩₂ ⊠ 1`, and B = V₁ ⊗ V₂, both absolutely irreducible, so (a) holds; (b) holds for A with h = (diag(2, 3), 1), whose 𝔷_h is the Cartan line of the first factor, and for B with h = (diag(2, 3), 1)σ, σ the swap, which has order 8 and the four distinct eigenvalues ±√2, ±√3 in 𝔽₂₅, so 𝔷_h is a two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra meeting A in a line only. By Shapiro's lemma and the Künneth formula `H¹(H, A) ≅ H¹(SL₂(𝔽₅), 𝔰𝔩₂) ≅ 𝔽₅ ≠ 0`. Hence if H_n = H for large n and ζ₅ ∉ L, ρ̄ is GSp₄-reasonable, while over F′ = F(ζ₅), for which the disjointness over `F(ζ_{5^∞})` is automatic, ζ₅ ∈ LF′ and the H¹ clause fails: `ρ̄|G_{F′}` is not GSp₄-reasonable.
- If ρ̄ has image in the diagonal torus, H⁰(H_n, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) contains the dual of the Cartan subalgebra, so (a) fails and ρ̄ is not GSp₄-reasonable.
- Reasonableness is stronger than GSp₄-irreducibility. H = SL₂(k) × SL₂(k) acting on an orthogonal sum V₁ ⊕ V₂ of symplectic planes stabilizes only 0, V₁, V₂ and V, none isotropic, so it lies in no proper parabolic; and 𝔰𝔭₄ ≅ Sym²V = 𝔰𝔩₂ ⊕ 𝔰𝔩₂ ⊕ V₁ ⊗ V₂ has no H-invariants for p ≥ 3, so (a) holds. But every 𝔷_h lies in 𝔰𝔩₂ ⊕ 𝔰𝔩₂, which the dual of V₁ ⊗ V₂ kills, so (b) fails and H does not satisfy the reasonableness conditions, as the irreducibility bullet of the API predicts for this reducible image. With the swap adjoined (first Check) the image is irreducible and h = (diag(2, 3), 1)σ reaches V₁ ⊗ V₂.
- If `ρ̄(G_{F(ζ_3)}) ⊇ Sp₄(𝔽₃)`, then, Sp₄(𝔽₃) being perfect and enormous (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11), every H_n contains Sp₄(𝔽₃) and ρ̄ is vast, hence GSp₄-reasonable; yet Sp₄(𝔽₃) ⊂ GL₄(𝔽₃) is not adequate as a subgroup of GL₄, because H¹(Sp₄(𝔽₃), ∧²) ≠ 0 (BCGP25 Remark 6.4.5, p. 151), so reasonableness is a symplectic condition.
- For regular semisimple h in the diagonal torus of GSp₄ with p > 2, the trace form on 𝔷_h = 𝔱 is 2(xx′ + yy′), nondegenerate; for p = 2 it vanishes, which is why p ≥ 3 is assumed.

*Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §§7.5, 7.9, 7.11; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.Symplectic.sp`, `Matrix.symplecticGroup`, `groupCohomology`.

### 5.3 Taylor–Wiles places for GSp₄-reasonable residual representations

Let p ≥ 3, 𝒮 = (ρ̄, S, {Λ_v}, {D_v}) a GSp₄ problem (5.1) over a totally real F⁺, T ⊂ S with S_p ⊂ T, and n ≥ 1. Let L be the field cut out by 𝔰𝔭₄^∨ and suppose that `H = ρ̄(G_{F⁺(ζ_{p^n})})` satisfies (a) and (b) of 5.2 and that either ζ_p ∉ L or H¹(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0. Let `q ≥ h¹(G_{F⁺,S}, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1))`. Prove that there is a set Q of Taylor–Wiles places of level n (v ∉ S, q_v ≡ 1 mod p^n, ρ̄ unramified at v with ρ̄(Frob_v) semisimple, with the local problem of gap:gsp4-semisimple-taylor-wiles-places) with #Q = q and `H¹_{𝒮_Q^⊥,T}(𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1)) = 0` (WHI22 Proposition 3.18 and proof, pp. 22–23, with the hypothesis (a) that its proof uses).

The route: (i) WHI22 Lemma 3.16: if H⁰(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0 and either ζ_p ∉ L or H¹(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0, then `H¹(Gal(L(ζ_{p^n})/F⁺), 𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1)) = 0` (the proof is that of BCGP21 Lemma 7.5.9); this step is part of this target, since ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11 states it only under ζ_p ∉ L or enormous image. (ii) By WHI22 Lemma 3.14 and the dual condition L_v^⊥ = H¹_ur(F_v, M_v^∨(1)) of the gap, a class [ψ] is killed by adding v exactly when its image in H¹_ur(F_v, N_v^∨(1)) is nonzero, N_v = Lie `Z(M_{ρ̄(Frob_v)}) ∩ 𝔰𝔭₄`; this quotient detects classes and is not L_v^⊥. (iii) By (i), ψ restricts to a nonzero `G_{F⁺(ζ_{p^n})}`-equivariant homomorphism f : `G_{L(ζ_{p^n})} → 𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1)`; condition (b) for a simple submodule of the span of its image gives semisimple σ₀, and σ = σ₀ or τσ₀ with ψ(σ)(z) ≠ 0. (iv) Chebotarev gives infinitely many v with ρ̄(Frob_v) = ρ̄(σ), ψ(Frob_v) = ψ(σ) and Frob_v `∈ G_{F⁺(ζ_{p^n})}`; induct on the dimension.

**Checks.**

- The Frobenius at a Taylor–Wiles place need only be semisimple; for ρ̄(Frob_v) = 1 one has M = GSp₄, N_v = 𝔰𝔭₄ ∩ Lie Z(GSp₄) = 0 and v detects no class, so such places are useless though allowed.
- For a reasonable image H with ζ_p ∈ L that is not enormous, Lemma 3.16 applies through H¹(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) = 0, while the statement of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11 does not; this is why (i) is proved here.
- Without (a), H⁰(H, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨) ≠ 0 (for instance a torus image) and the inflation–restriction kernel `H¹(Gal(L(ζ_{p^n})/F⁺), 𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1))` need not vanish, so the restriction to `G_{L(ζ_{p^n})}` of a nonzero class can be zero.

*Needs:* 5.1; 5.2; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11; gap:gsp4-semisimple-taylor-wiles-places.

### 5.4 Ordinary GSp₄ seed data

Let p > 2 split completely in the totally real field F⁺ and fix ı : ℂ ≅ ℚ̄_p. Define an ordinary GSp₄ seed datum as: an ordinary cuspidal automorphic representation π of `GSp₄(𝔸_{F⁺})` of general type (ModularityAndLanglandsExtensions §3.9) with central character |·|², which is either regular algebraic (π_∞ essentially discrete series, with weights `λ_v = (k_v, l_v; 2)` in `X^*(T)^+`, that is 0 ≥ k_v ≥ l_v and k_v + l_v even in the conventions of BCGP25 §§1.8.7–1.8.8 and 1.8.12) or of weight 2 in the sense of BCGP25 Definition 1.8.16 (`λ_v = (1, 1; 2)` at every v | ∞, not G-dominant, with π_∞ a non-degenerate limit of discrete series), with its Galois representation `ρ_{π,p}` (gap:gsp4-galois-representations-totally-real) and residual representation ρ̄; a finite set R of finite places prime to p containing the prime-to-p places where π ramifies; an ordinary p-stabilization (α_v, β_v) of `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` for each v | p; and a place w₀; such that (BCGP25 §6.3, Hypothesis 6.3.1, Remarks 6.3.2–6.3.3 and the choice of w₀, pp. 146–147): (1) ρ̄ is GSp₄-reasonable (5.2), hence absolutely irreducible (5.2, API), so that 5.1 applies to it; (2) ρ̄ is tidy (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11); (3) for v | p, `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` is ordinary of weight 2 with p-stabilization (α_v, β_v) compatible with a fixed ordinary p-stabilization of π_v; (4) for v | p, `ρ_{π,p}|G_{F⁺_v}`, viewed through the stabilization as a point of R^△_v (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.29), lies on a unique irreducible component of Spec R^△_v; (5) for v ∈ R, `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` is trivial, q_v ≡ 1 mod p (mod 9 if p = 3) and `π_v^{Iw(v)} ≠ 0`; (6) w₀ is an unramified place of residue characteristic > 5 with `q_{w₀} ≢ 1` mod p such that no two eigenvalues of `ρ̄(Frob_{w₀})` have ratio `q_{w₀}`. Put S = S_p ∪ R ∪ {w₀}.

Its API:

- A place w₀ as in (6) exists: tidiness gives `h ∈ ρ̄(G_{F⁺})` with ν(h) ≠ 1 and no two eigenvalues of ratio ν(h); Chebotarev gives w₀ with `ρ̄(Frob_{w₀})` conjugate to h, and since `ν(h) = q_{w₀}^{±1}` and the set of eigenvalue ratios is stable under inversion, `q_{w₀} ≢ 1` and no ratio equals `q_{w₀}`.
- (6) gives `H⁰(G_{F⁺_{w₀}}, ad⁰ρ̄(1)) = 0`, so `H²(G_{F⁺_{w₀}}, ad⁰ρ̄) = 0` by local duality and `R^□_{w₀}` is formally smooth over O (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §1.4, in the fixed-similitude form of §1.17).
- For a solvable totally real F′⁺/F⁺ in which p splits completely and which is linearly disjoint over F⁺ from `E(ζ_{p^∞})`, E the field cut out by ρ̄, the base change of π with the induced data is a seed datum after the local conditions at R are rearranged: the image of ρ̄, hence tidiness, is unchanged, and reasonableness is preserved by the last API bullet of 5.2. Disjointness from E alone does not preserve reasonableness (5.2, first Check).

**Checks.**

- If `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` is residually p-distinguished, condition (4) holds automatically, since Spec R^△_v[1/p] is irreducible (BCGP25 Remark 6.3.2).
- For p = 3 a place v ∈ R must have q_v ≡ 1 mod 9: q_v = 19 is allowed and q_v = 7 is not.
- A place w₀ with `q_{w₀} ≡ 1` mod p is excluded by (6), since every eigenvalue has ratio 1 with itself. At an unramified place w₀ ∤ p with this congruence, put `h = ρ̄(Frob_{w₀})`; the cyclotomic twist is trivial, so `H⁰(G_{F⁺_{w₀}}, ad⁰ρ̄(1)) = ker(Ad(h) − 1 : 𝔰𝔭₄ → 𝔰𝔭₄)`. For h = I₄ this has dimension 10. It need not contain the standard Cartan: over 𝔽₃ with q = 7, take `U = [[1,1],[0,1]]`, `h = diag(U,U^{−t})`, `J = [[0,I₂],[−I₂,0]]` and `X = diag(1,0,−1,0)`. Then h³ = I₄, h · J · ᵗh = J, and X ∈ 𝔰𝔭₄ but `[h,X] = 2E₁₂ + E₄₃ ≠ 0`, where Eᵢⱼ are the matrix units. Nevertheless `Z = h − h^{−1} = 2E₁₂ + E₄₃` is a nonzero invariant: `ᵗZJ + JZ = 0` and `[h,Z] = 0`. Thus unramifiedness does not imply semisimplicity of h.
- If π is unramified at every prime-to-p place, R may be empty and S = S_p ∪ {w₀}.
- The weight convention. In BCGP25 G-dominance is 0 ≥ k₁ ≥ k₂ and ρ = (−1, −2; 0), so the weight-two seed (1, 1; 2) of Theorem 1.8.17 is not dominant (1 > 0) and enters only through the second alternative, with Galois diagonal α, β, `β^{−1}ε^{−1}`, `α^{−1}ε^{−1}` (BCGP25 (1.8.19)). The range k_v ≥ l_v ≥ 2 read in these coordinates would exclude (1, 1; 2) and admit (2, 2; 2), which is neither dominant nor of the form λ with λ + ρ dominant ((2, 2) + ρ = (1, 0)), and for which the Hodge–Tate formula of BCGP25 Theorem 1.8.13 returns the repeated weights 1, 0, 1, 0; (0, 0; 2) is dominant and regular, with Hodge–Tate weights −1, 0, 1, 2.

*Needs:* 5.2; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §7.11; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§1.4, 1.17, 6.29; ModularityAndLanglandsExtensions §3.9; gap:gsp4-galois-representations-totally-real.

### 5.5 The selected ordinary problems S_1 and `S_{1,π}`

Let (π, (α_v, β_v), R, w₀) be an ordinary GSp₄ seed datum with S = S_p ∪ R ∪ {w₀}. For v | p let D^△_v be the ordinary problem of LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.29 over `Λ_{GSp₄,v}` with unramified characters χ_1(Frob_v) = α_v and χ_2(Frob_v) = β_v; for v ∈ R let D^1_v be the unipotently ramified problem (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.22 with trivial characters); let `D^□_{w₀}` be all lifts. Define S_1 = (ρ̄, S, `{Λ_{GSp₄,v}}_{v∈S_p} ∪ {O}_{v∈S∖S_p}`, `{D^△_v}_{v∈S_p} ∪ {D^1_v}_{v∈R} ∪` `{D^□_{w₀}}`) over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`; for v | p let `R^{△,π}_v` be the quotient of R^△_v by the minimal prime of the unique component through `ρ_{π,p}|G_{F⁺_v}` (seed condition (4)); and let `S_{1,π}` replace D^△_v by the problem of `R^{△,π}_v` (BCGP25 §6.3, from "By the above hypotheses" to the definition of `T_{S_{1,π}}`, p. 147).

Its API:

- `R_{S_1} ↠ R_{S_{1,π}}` is a surjection of `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`-algebras, and `ρ_{π,p}` with its stabilizations is a ℚ̄_p-point of Spec `R_{S_{1,π}}`.
- If `ρ̄|G_{F⁺_v}` is residually p-distinguished for every v | p, D^△_v is the B-ordinary problem `D^{B,α_v}_v` (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.27) and `R^{△,π}_v` is the maximal reduced O-flat quotient of R^△_v.

**Checks.**

The following Checks test the selection on the model ring B = O⟦x, y⟧/(xy) in place of R^△_v, whose minimal primes are (x) and (y).

- Orientation. A seed point x ↦ 0, y ↦ ϖ lies on V(x) and not on V(y), since y ↦ ϖ ≠ 0, so it selects B/(x); the O-point x ↦ ϖ, y ↦ 0 is then a lift of type S_1 that is not of type `S_{1,π}`, since it does not factor through B/(x).
- Uniqueness. The O-point x ↦ 0, y ↦ 0 has kernel (x, y), which contains both (x) and (y), so it lies on both components and selects none; seed condition (4) excludes such a seed, and "the component through π" is then defined.
- Selection is scheme-theoretic, not pointwise. The k[ε]-point x ↦ ε, y ↦ 0 has as its only point the closed point, which lies on the selected component, yet it does not factor through B/(x) since ε ≠ 0; a definition of `S_{1,π}` by the components on which the underlying points lie would wrongly admit it. In the residually p-distinguished case `R_{S_{1,π}}` and `R_{S_1}` have the same reduced O-flat quotient (API above), and the distinction disappears after passing to it.

*Needs:* 5.1; 5.4; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§2.22, 6.27, 6.29.

### 5.6 The Galois representation over the GSp₄ Hida–Hecke algebra

In the setting of 5.5, let M¹ be the finite free `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`-module of gap:hilbert-siegel-hida-module-free and `T_{S_1}` the Hida–Hecke algebra acting faithfully on it. Prove that there is a continuous ρ : `G_{F⁺} → GSp₄(T_{S_1})` of type S_1, unique up to strict equivalence, whose Frobenius characteristic polynomials at v ∉ S are the Hecke polynomials Q_v(X), giving a surjection `R_{S_1} ↠ T_{S_1}` of `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`-algebras; the same holds for the Taylor–Wiles variants `M^{1,Q}`, compatibly with Δ_Q. Put `T_{S_{1,π}} = R_{S_{1,π}} ⊗_{R_{S_1}} T_{S_1}` (BCGP25 §6.3, p. 147; WHI22 Theorems 7.7–7.8 and proofs, pp. 64–65; BCGP21 Theorem 7.9.4).

The route: embed `T_{S_1}` into a ring of tuples (a, (a_π)) `⊂ k × ∏_π O_{E_π}` indexed by the classical ordinary π of large regular weight contributing to M¹ (gap:hilbert-siegel-ordinary-classicality), glue their Galois representations (gap:gsp4-galois-representations-totally-real) and pass to Artinian quotients. The flag-image condition D^△_v is a scheme-theoretic image, not understood on Artinian rings; but `T_{S_1}` is O-flat (it acts faithfully on the free module M¹) and, by gap:hilbert-siegel-ordinary-classicality, reduced and embedded in `∏_π O_{E_π}`: a nilpotent Hecke operator acts by zero on each semisimple specialization `M¹ ⊗_{Λ,κ} E`, so it maps M¹ into `∩_κ ℘_κM¹ = 0`. Hence `R^□_v → T_{S_1}` factors through R^△_v once every composite `R^□_v → O_{E_π}` does, that is once each `ρ_π|G_{F⁺_v}` is ordinary with stabilization lifting (α_v, β_v) (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.3); only the `O_{E′}`-points of D^△_v are used.

**Checks.**

- `T_{S_1}` is finite over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`, acting faithfully on a finite free module.
- The k-coordinate of a tuple (a, (a_π)) is determined by the others, so the factoring through R^△_v is tested on `O_{E′}`-points only.
- For F⁺ = ℚ and ρ̄ residually p-distinguished, the construction is that of BCGP21 Theorem 7.9.4 with `D^{B,α_p}`.
- Classicality of points alone would not give reducedness. Let Λ = O⟦x⟧, M = Λ² and T = Λ[N]/(N²) with N = [[0, 1], [0, 0]]: M is free, T acts faithfully and is finite over Λ, and every ℚ̄_p-point of T is a point of Λ, yet N ≠ 0 is nilpotent. The contract excludes it, since N acts on each `M/℘_κM = E²` as a nonzero nilpotent, not semisimply.

*Needs:* 5.5; gap:hilbert-siegel-hida-module-free; gap:hilbert-siegel-taylor-wiles-coinvariants for the Taylor–Wiles variants; gap:hilbert-siegel-ordinary-classicality; gap:gsp4-galois-representations-totally-real; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.29; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.3.

## Layer 6: The symplectic ordinary R = T theorem for p > 2

Throughout, p > 2 splits completely in the totally real field F⁺, (π, `(α_v, β_v)_{v|p}`, R, w₀) is an ordinary GSp₄ seed datum (5.4) with residual representation ρ̄ and S = S_p ∪ R ∪ {w₀}, `S_{1,π}` is the selected problem (5.5), and `T_{S_{1,π}} = R_{S_{1,π}} ⊗_{R_{S_1}} T_{S_1}` (5.6). Since p > 2 splits completely, `O^×_{F⁺_v}(p) = 1 + pℤ_p ≅ ℤ_p` and `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}` ≅ `O⟦x_1, …, x_{2d}⟧` is a power series ring.

### 6.1 The automorphic ordinary component dominates the local weight space

Prove that for every v | p the composite Spec `R^{△,π}_v` → Spec `Λ_{GSp₄,v}` is surjective; consequently some minimal prime of `R^{△,π}_v/(p)` contains the minimal prime of `R^{△,π}_v` and no other minimal prime of R^△_v, and `R^{△,π}_v` has relative dimension 16 over O (BCGP25 proof of Theorem 6.3.4, first paragraph, p. 148; Lemma 6.2.6, pp. 145–146).

The route: let `Q_π ⊂ T_{S_1}` be a minimal prime contained in the prime of π; `R^△_v → T_{S_1}/Q_π` factors through `R^{△,π}_v` by seed condition (4). The composite Spec `T_{S_1}/Q_π` → Spec `Λ_{GSp₄,v}` is surjective because `T_{S_1}/Q_π` is finite and torsion-free over the domain `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`, so lying over applies. This is the hypothesis of LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.35 (BCGP25 Lemma 6.2.6), whose conclusion is the second sentence.

**Checks.**

- The hypothesis of Lemma 6.2.6 is checked globally, not by a local analysis of R^△_v.
- For p > 2 split completely `Λ_{GSp₄,v}` = O⟦x, y⟧ is a domain; at p = 2 it would have torsion and the lying-over argument would need each component separately.
- Relative dimension 16 over O above p is 14 over `Λ_{GSp₄,v}`, matching the count of 6.2.

*Needs:* 5.5; 5.6; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.35.

### 6.2 Patched Hida modules for the selected problem

Enlarge E to contain µ_p (µ_9 if p = 3) and fix, for v ∈ R, characters `χ_v = (χ_{v,1}, χ_{v,2})` nontrivial and trivial mod λ with `χ_{v,1} ≠ χ_{v,2}^{±1}`; let S_χ be S_1 with `D^{χ_v}_v` (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §2.22) in place of D^1_v. Let `q = h¹(G_{F⁺,S}, 𝔰𝔭₄^∨(1))` and, by WHI22 Lemma 7.10 built from 5.3, fix 0 ≤ t ≤ 2q and Taylor–Wiles data Q_N of level N with #Q_N = q and `Δ_{Q_N}` having t cyclic factors. Let `Δ_∞ = ℤ_p^t ↠ Δ_{Q_N}`, S_∞ = 𝒯⟦Δ_∞⟧ with 𝒯 the framing ring over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}` (5.1) and augmentation ideal a_∞, R^1_∞ and R^χ_∞ power series rings in g = t − 4d + #S − 1 variables over `R^loc_{S_1,S}` and `R^loc_{S_χ,S}`, `R_{∞,π} = R^1_∞` `⊗_{R^loc_{S_1,S}}` `R^loc_{S_{1,π},S}`, and `R^χ_{∞,π} = R^χ_∞` `⊗_{R^loc_{S_χ,S}}` `R^loc_{S_{χ,π},S}`, where `S_{χ,π}` replaces D^△_v in S_χ by the problem of `R^{△,π}_v`. Prove that there are `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`-algebra maps `S_∞ → R^1_∞ → R_{S_1}` and `S_∞ → R^χ_∞ → R_{S_χ}`, S_∞-modules M^1_∞ and M^χ_∞ finite free over S_∞ with M^·_∞/a_∞ ≅ M^· (5.6), compatible actions of R^·_∞, and identifications M^1_∞/λ ≅ M^χ_∞/λ compatible with R^1_∞/λ ≅ R^χ_∞/λ; that `depth_{R^·_∞} M^·_∞` = dim S_∞ and every irreducible component of `Supp_{R^·_∞} M^·_∞` has dimension dim S_∞; and that every irreducible component of Spec `R_{∞,π}` and of Spec `R^χ_{∞,π}` has dimension dim S_∞ (BCGP25 proof of Theorem 6.3.4, pp. 148–149; WHI22 §7.3, Lemma 7.10 and Proposition 7.11 with proof, pp. 66–68). Nothing is claimed about the dimension of R^·_∞ itself, whose factors above p are the full rings R^△_v.

The route: the Taylor–Wiles data are those of WHI22 Lemma 7.10 (#Q_N = q, with t the number of cyclic factors of `Δ_{Q_N}`, which varies with N and is fixed by passing to a subsequence); the modules `M^{·,Q_N}` are finite free over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}[Δ_{Q_N}]` with coinvariants M^· (gap:hilbert-siegel-taylor-wiles-coinvariants) and carry the Galois representations of 5.6. Since `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}` is a power series ring, the patching is DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra Layer 8 (§§8.2–8.5) with complexes concentrated in one degree and δ = 1. Depth and support: depth over R^·_∞ is computed through the local map from S_∞, over which M^·_∞ is free, so it is dim S_∞; every minimal prime P of the support is associated, so dim S_∞ = depth ≤ dim R^·_∞/P, and `R^·_∞/Ann M^·_∞` embeds in the finite S_∞-algebra `End_{S_∞}(M^·_∞)`, so dim R^·_∞/P ≤ dim S_∞. Dimensions of the selected rings: S_∞ is formally smooth over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}` of relative dimension t + 11#S − 1; above p the domain `R^{△,π}_v` has relative dimension 16 over O, that is 14 over `Λ_{GSp₄,v}` (6.1; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.35); at v ∈ R the unipotent and distinct-character rings are equidimensional, resp. irreducible, of relative dimension 10 over O with characteristic-zero generic points (§2.22); at w₀ the ring is O⟦x_1, …, x_10⟧ by the fixed-similitude smoothness criterion of §§1.4, 1.17; and components of the completed tensor product are products of local components, with additive relative dimensions (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §4.6). So every component of `R_{∞,π}` and `R^χ_{∞,π}` has relative dimension g + 10#S + 4d over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`, which equals t + 11#S − 1 for g = t − 4d + #S − 1.

**Checks.**

- For F⁺ = ℚ, #S = 3 and t = 7: g = 7 − 4 + 3 − 1 = 5, every component of `R_{∞,π}` has relative dimension 5 + 30 + 4 = 39 and S_∞ has 7 + 33 − 1 = 39.
- Depth over R^·_∞ equal to dim S_∞ is not maximal Cohen–Macaulayness over R^·_∞ unless dim R^·_∞ = dim S_∞, which would need every component of the O-flat quotient of R^△_v, not only `R^{△,π}_v`, to have relative dimension 16; this is not supplied for residual representations that are not p-distinguished and is not claimed. In the model R = O⟦x, y⟧/(ϖx, ϖy) with M = O (x, y ↦ 0), depth_R M = 1 equals the dimension of the O-flat quotient O, while dim R = 2.
- The number of places in Q_N is q, while t, the number of cyclic factors of `Δ_{Q_N}`, can be up to 2q because the Levi centres at semisimple Frobenius have rank up to 2; using t for #Q_N would miscount.
- With I = ∅ the patched modules are free over S_∞; there is no defect, unlike the Klingen case #I = 1 of BCGP21 where they are only balanced.

*Needs:* 5.1; 5.3; 5.5; 5.6; 6.1; gap:hilbert-siegel-taylor-wiles-coinvariants; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§1.4, 1.17, 2.22, 6.35; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra Layer 8, §§4.6, 6.3.

### 6.3 The symplectic ordinary reduced R = T theorem

Prove that the map `R_{S_{1,π}} → T_{S_{1,π}}` has nilpotent kernel, so `(R_{S_{1,π}})_red ≅ (T_{S_{1,π}})_red`: R = T holds up to nilpotents on exactly the chosen local components above p (BCGP25 Theorem 6.3.4 and proof, pp. 148–149, with the conclusion corrected; Remark 6.3.2, p. 147; source issue below). The reduced ring is `T_{S_{1,π}}` itself exactly when `T_{S_{1,π}}` is reduced, which its definition as `R_{S_{1,π}} ⊗_{R_{S_1}} T_{S_1}` does not guarantee although `T_{S_1}` is reduced. This is the headline of Layers 5–6.

The route: it suffices that the support of M¹_∞ contains every irreducible component of Spec `R_{∞,π}`. (i) The seed: `ρ_{π,p}` with its stabilizations is a point of `Supp_{R_{S_1}} M¹` lying on Spec `R_{S_{1,π}}`, so Supp M¹_∞ contains the component C_π of Spec `R_{∞,π}` through it. (ii) By 6.1 (LocalGaloisDeformationRings §6.35) the special fibre of C_π contains an irreducible component Z of Spec `R_{∞,π}/λ` lying on no other component of Spec R^1_∞, the factors at R (§2.22) and at w₀ (fixed-similitude formal smoothness, §§1.4, 1.17) contributing single components. (iii) Ihara avoidance: Z ⊂ Supp M¹_∞/λ = Supp M^χ_∞/λ; a component of Supp M^χ_∞ generalizing Z has special fibre through Z, so its factor above p is `R^{△,π}_v`; the χ-side local rings at R are irreducible (§2.22), so `R^χ_{∞,π}` is irreducible after enlarging E and M^χ_∞ has full support on it (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §9.6). Reducing modulo λ, Supp M¹_∞/λ contains Spec `R_{∞,π}/λ`. (iv) Every component of Supp M¹_∞ and of Spec `R_{∞,π}` has dimension dim S_∞, so M¹_∞ is supported on a component of `R_{∞,π}` as soon as it is supported at a special-fibre point lying on that component alone; such points exist factor by factor (6.1 above p, §2.22 at R, §§1.4, 1.17 at w₀). (v) Hence Spec `R_{S_{1,π}} ⊂` Supp M¹, and since `T_{S_1}` acts faithfully on M¹ the kernel is nilpotent (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §6.5).

**Checks.**

- The theorem is "minimal at p": it identifies R and T only on the components selected by the seed; a component of Spec R^△_v not through the seed is not claimed.
- Nonvanishing of M^χ_∞ alone does not suffice in (iii): it gives some component of Supp M^χ_∞, possibly with a factor above p other than `R^{△,π}_v`; the seed point and Z are what select `R^{△,π}_v`.
- In the residually p-distinguished case the theorem is a special case of BCGP21 and WHI22 Theorem 7.12.
- Selecting a component can create nilpotents in the Hecke side. Let Λ = O⟦u⟧ and T = Λ[z]/(z(z − u)), reduced and free over Λ with basis 1, z (it embeds in Λ × Λ by z ↦ (0, u)), and let B = O⟦x, y⟧/(xy) → T send x ↦ z², y ↦ z − u (xy ↦ z·z(z − u) = 0). The quotient selected by the minimal prime (x) is T ⊗_B B/(x) = Λ[z]/(z², uz), in which z ≠ 0 and z² = 0. So nilpotence of the kernel identifies `(R_{S_{1,π}})_red` with `(T_{S_{1,π}})_red`, not with `T_{S_{1,π}}`; for 4.8 the target `T^{T,ord}(U(l^∞))_m` is reduced (2.2(1)) and the stronger form holds there.

*Needs:* 6.1; 6.2; 5.5; LocalGaloisDeformationRings §§1.4, 1.17, 2.22, 6.35; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §§6.1, 6.5, 9.6.

### 6.4 Finiteness of the selected symplectic ordinary deformation ring

Prove that `R_{S_{1,π}}` is a finite `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}`-algebra (BCGP25 Theorem 6.3.4, p. 148). This is a headline of the roadmap.

The route: `T_{S_1}`, and hence its quotient `T_{S_{1,π}}`, is finite over `Λ_{GSp₄,F⁺}` (5.6); by 6.3 the kernel of `R_{S_{1,π}} → T_{S_{1,π}}` is nilpotent, so `R_{S_{1,π}}/m_Λ R_{S_{1,π}}` has finite length and `R_{S_{1,π}}` is finite (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §4.1).

**Checks.**

- Finiteness is what BCGP25 Proposition 7.5.10 uses at p > 2: for a minimal prime Q of R_S through ρ, `R_{S_1}/Q` factors through `R_{S_{1,π}}` and is finite over `Λ_{GSp₄,ℚ}`.
- A ring with nilpotent kernel onto a finite Λ-algebra need not be finite unless it is complete Noetherian local; `R_{S_{1,π}}` is, which is why §4.1 applies.
- For F⁺ = ℚ, `Λ_{GSp₄,ℚ}` = O⟦x, y⟧ and finiteness means `R_{S_{1,π}}` is a finite O⟦x, y⟧-module.

*Needs:* 6.3; 5.6; DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §4.1.
