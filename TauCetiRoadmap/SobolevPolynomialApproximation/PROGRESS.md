## SobolevPolynomialApproximation: 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-11 (`7530630` to `1c6a1a4`)

The roadmap's first piece of its own landed: the space [`polynomialsDegLE`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/MvPolynomial/PolynomialSpace.html#TauCeti.polynomialsDegLE) of real polynomial functions of total degree at most `m` on Euclidean space, defined as the image of the formal bounded-degree polynomials under [evaluation](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/MvPolynomial/PolynomialSpace.html#TauCeti.polynomialEvalₗ) (TauCeti#34779). Evaluation is [injective](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/MvPolynomial/PolynomialSpace.html#TauCeti.polynomialEvalₗ_injective), so the space is [linearly equivalent](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/MvPolynomial/PolynomialSpace.html#TauCeti.restrictTotalDegreeEquivPolynomialsDegLE) to its formal counterpart, and its [dimension](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/MvPolynomial/PolynomialSpace.html#TauCeti.finrank_polynomialsDegLE) is the binomial coefficient `(m + n).choose n`. Small sanity lemmas pin down the conventions the README asks for: degree zero means exactly the constants, and a coordinate function belongs exactly when the degree bound is positive. The statements are for an arbitrary finite coordinate type rather than only `Fin n`.

This is §1.2 of Layer 1, and these are still functions on the whole space. Their Sobolev classes on a domain, the seminorms, the polynomial Poincaré lemma and the kernel of the top seminorm are next in line.

## SobolevPolynomialApproximation: 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-19 (`1c6a1a4` to `b235000`)

The headline is the density of functions smooth up to the boundary on star-shaped domains. If an open set is star-shaped with respect to every point of a ball, restrictions of test functions on the whole space are [dense](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/StarConvex.html#TauCeti.Wkp.denseRange_restrictL_ofTestFunctionₗ_top_of_forall_starConvex) in `W^{k,p}` for every order `k` and every finite `p`. A [convex version](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/StarConvex.html#TauCeti.Wkp.denseRange_restrictL_ofTestFunctionₗ_top_of_convex) follows (TauCeti#41404). This is §5.2, and it does not need the domain to be bounded. The same work shows why `p = ∞` is excluded: on a nonempty interval, smooth functions are [not dense](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Interval.html#TauCeti.Wkp.not_dense_contDiff_representatives_Ioo_top) in `W^{k,∞}`.

Most of the rest is Layer 1. The [polynomial Poincaré lemma](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/MvPolynomial/PoincareLemma.html#MvPolynomial.exists_pderiv_eq) says that a family of polynomials of degree at most `m` whose mixed partial derivatives agree is the gradient of a polynomial of degree at most `m + 1`. It is proved over any `ℚ`-algebra, by homogeneous components and Euler's identity. [Order truncation](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Basic.html#TauCeti.Wkp.toOrderL) and the [Sobolev seminorms](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Seminorm.html#TauCeti.Wkp.seminorm) arrived with their full API. That API includes the √2 comparisons between the graph norm and the Euclidean combination of the seminorms (an [upper](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Seminorm.html#TauCeti.Wkp.norm_le_sqrt_two_mul) and a [lower](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Seminorm.html#TauCeti.Wkp.sqrt_le_sqrt_two_mul_norm) bound), and the [factor](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Wkp/Seminorm.html#TauCeti.Wkp.seminorm_ofExponentLEL_le) by which a seminorm can grow when the exponent is lowered. [Partial](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Calculus/IteratedGradient/Contraction.html#TauCeti.IteratedGradient.contractL) and [full](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Calculus/IteratedGradient/Contraction.html#TauCeti.IteratedGradient.fullContractL) contractions of iterated gradients now exist too, with their homogeneity and norm bounds. Together these finish §1.1, §1.3 and §1.4, and the algebraic half of §1.6.

The Sobolev classes of polynomials, the kernel of the top seminorm and the quotient by polynomials are still missing. Nothing has started on the averaged Taylor polynomial.
