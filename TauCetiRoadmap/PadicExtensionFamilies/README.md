# Roadmap: ramification polygons, slopes and families of p-adic field extensions

A finite extension `L/K` of nonarchimedean local fields factors canonically as an unramified
extension followed by a totally ramified one, and the totally ramified step is generated by a root
of an Eisenstein polynomial `φ`. Almost all of the ramification of `L/K` can be read off `φ`:
the Newton polygon of the *ramification polynomial* `ρ(x) = α^{-n} φ(αx + α)` (the **ramification
polygon**), the residue-field polynomials attached to its segments (the **residual polynomials** of
Ore, Greve and Pauli), and the **indices of inseparability** of Fried and Heiermann. Over the Galois
closure the same data become the higher ramification filtration in its upper numbering, which the
LMFDB displays as **slopes**: visible, hidden, and Galois, with the **slope content** `[s_1, …, s_k]_t^u`
and the **Galois mean slope** that assembles, prime by prime, into the **Galois root discriminant**
of a number field. Finally the extensions with a fixed ramification polygon form a **family**, carried
by a generic Eisenstein polynomial (Monge, Pauli–Sinclair), with a **mass** that refines Serre's
mass formula, and Krasner's count of the extensions of a given degree *and* discriminant.

This roadmap builds exactly that dictionary. It is the roadmap that the LMFDB section of `p`-adic
fields (`lf`: fields and families) is held against, together with the Galois root discriminant
(`grd`) of the number-field section; every invariant those pages display or search on is either a
definition here, or consumed by name from a named supplier, or handed, with its owner named, to a
neighbouring roadmap. The carrier is Tau Ceti's local-field vocabulary at the pin: a base
`K` with `[Field K] [ValuativeRel K] [TopologicalSpace K] [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField K]`, an
extension `L` with the same structure and `[Algebra K L] [ValuativeExtension K L]`, Eisenstein
polynomials over `𝒪[K]` in Mathlib's `Polynomial.IsEisensteinAt 𝓂[K]`, and the ramification
filtration, Herbrand functions and upper numbering of
`TauCeti.LocalFieldsRamification` (`TauCeti/NumberTheory/LocalField/`). Nothing below defines a
second valuation, a second filtration, a second different, or a second Newton polygon.

## Pinned conventions

1. **The carrier.** `K` carries `[Field K] [ValuativeRel K] [TopologicalSpace K]
   [IsNonarchimedeanLocalField K]`, spelled out, as in Tau Ceti's local-field modules; an extension
   `L` carries the same four instances together with `[Algebra K L] [ValuativeExtension K L]`;
   finiteness is `finite_of_valuativeExtension`. The residue characteristic is `p := ringChar 𝓀[K]`
   and the residue cardinality `q := Nat.card 𝓀[K]`, written inline. Both characteristics are
   allowed except where a statement says `FinitePadicExtension K p` (Layer 4's jump sets, the label
   prefix, Layer 7) or `Algebra.IsSeparable K L` (Layers 1–3 and 5–6 throughout: in equal
   characteristic an Eisenstein polynomial may be inseparable, and its roots have no breaks).
2. **Valuations.** `v_L` is `TauCeti.normalizedValuation L`, with `v_L(π_L) = 1`; `addVal` is
   `IsDiscreteValuationRing.addVal 𝒪[L]`. Every slope, break, ram and mean is a rational number
   and is a `ℚ`; the Newton polygon's real heights are read through `(↑) : ℚ → ℝ`.
3. **Artin and Swan.** For a finite Galois `M/K` with `G = Gal(M/K)` and upper filtration
   `G^u = upperRamificationGroup K M u`, `u ∈ [-1, ∞)`: a **Swan slope** of `M/K` is an upper break
   `u > 0` (`UpperJump K M u`); its **Artin slope** is `u + 1`. Following the LMFDB, write
   `G^{(s)} := G^{s-1}` for `s ≥ 0` and `G^{(s+)} := ⋂_{ε > 0} G^{(s+ε)}`; a Galois Artin slope `s > 1`
   has **multiplicity** `log_p [G^{(s)} : G^{(s+)}]`. The LMFDB columns `slopes`, `visible`,
   `hidden`, `top_slope` of `lf_fields` are **Artin**; the columns `slopes`, `means`, `rams` of
   `lf_families` are **Swan**, and `visible` there is Artin. Artin slope `0` is the unramified
   step and Artin slope `1` the tame step; neither is ever listed among the wild slopes.
4. **Orientation of the ramification polygon.** The ramification polygon of `φ` with root `α` is
   `Polynomial.newtonPolygon ρ v` for `ρ(x) = α^{-n} φ(αx + α)` and `v` the additive normalized
   valuation of `L`, in the orientation of Newton polygons' Convention 3: points `(i, v_L(ρ_i))`,
   slopes increasing from left to right, a slope `m` corresponding to a root of valuation `-m`. The
   LMFDB plots the reflection `(-i, v_L(ρ_i))`. **Rams are the negatives of the Newton slopes.**
   `ρ(0) = 0`, so the polygon starts at abscissa `1`; its finite slopes count `n − 1` roots.
5. **Rams, slopes and means are indexed `1, …, w`,** `w = v_p(e)`, nondecreasing, with the
   convention `r_0 = s_0 = m_0 = 0`. The `k`-th ram is the negative of the slope of the
   ramification polygon on the interval `[p^{w-k}, p^{w-k+1}]` of abscissae (Layer 1 proves the
   polygon is linear there; the faces are read from `p^w` down to `1`, since a Newton polygon's
   slopes increase to the right, so the smallest ram sits on the face ending at `p^w` and the
   largest on `[1, p]`); the
   `k`-th Swan slope is `s_k = s_{k-1} + (r_k − r_{k-1})/(ε p^{k-1})`; the `k`-th mean is
   `m_k = Σ_{j ≤ k} (p−1) s_j / p^{k+1-j}`, equivalently `(p−1) s_k = p m_k − m_{k-1}`. A ram or
   slope repeated `d` times is listed `d` times. The **small rams** are `r_k^* = (p−1) r_k/(ε p^k)`
   and the **tiny rams** `r_k' = r_k/(ε p^k)`, with `r_k^* = m_k − m_{k-1}` and `r_k' = s_k − m_k`.
6. **Wild exponent, height, mean.** For `L/K` finite separable with `e`, `f`, `c =
   discriminantExponent K L`: the **height** `H(L/K) := c/f − e + 1 ∈ ℕ` (faithful by
   `inertiaDegree_mul_ramificationIndex_sub_one_le_discriminantExponent`) and the **mean**
   `m(L/K) := H(L/K)/e ∈ ℚ`, so `c = f(e − 1 + e m)`. For `L/K` totally ramified, `H(L/K)` is
   Mass formula's wild exponent `c(L)`; the symbol `c` below always means the discriminant
   exponent, as on the LMFDB page, and the wild exponent is written `H` or `i_0`.
7. **Residual polynomials** follow Ore's normalization as the LMFDB states it: for a segment with
   endpoints at abscissae `k` and `k + l`, heights `v_α(ρ_k)` and `v_α(ρ_{k+l})`, and slope `h/m`
   in lowest terms with `h = (v_α(ρ_k) − v_α(ρ_{k+l})) m / l`,
   `A(x) = Σ_{j=0}^{l/m} \overline{ρ_{jm+k} α^{jh − v_α(ρ_k)}} x^j ∈ 𝓀[L][x]`, where `v_α` is
   `v_L` and the bar is the residue map. The constant term of each residual polynomial is the
   leading coefficient of the next, and `x^k A(x^m)` is additive when `h/m > 0`.
8. **Slopes of a non-Galois extension** are defined through a Galois closure and proved
   independent of it (Layer 2). They coincide with the slopes of the lower convex envelope of the
   points `(n_E, c_E)` over the subextensions `E` of `L/K` (the slope polygon), which is how the
   LMFDB computes them; the envelope statement is a theorem, not the definition.
9. **Families are sets of isomorphism classes** of subextensions of `SeparableClosure K`,
   represented as in Mass formula by representative sets (`IsRepresentativeSet`-style predicates),
   never by `Quotient`. A **Herbrand invariant** is the triple `(ε, f, (s_1, …, s_w))` of a tame
   index, a residue degree and a nondecreasing list of positive rationals, with rams and means as
   derived presentations; the LMFDB writes it `[s_1, …, s_w]_ε^f`, `(r_1, …, r_w)_ε^f`,
   `⟨m_1, …, m_w⟩_ε^f`, and omits a subscript `ε = 1` or superscript `f = 1`.
10. **Masses.** The mass of a member `L` of a family over `K` with unramified subfield `K_f` is
    `1/#Aut(L/K_f)`, summed over the `K_f`-isomorphism classes of members; the absolute mass is
    `1/#Aut(L/ℚ_p)` summed over the `ℚ_p`-isomorphism classes. Layer 6 proves the relation
    between the two; neither is defined through the other.
11. **The label prefix** of a `p`-adic field `L` (a `FinitePadicExtension L p`) is the quadruple
    `(p, f, e, c)` of `L/ℚ_p`; of a family over `K` it is `(label of K, f, e, c)` relative to `K`.
    Nothing else about a label is defined here.
12. **Indices of inseparability** use Heiermann's definition with `K' = L^{ur}`, `n = [L : K']`,
    `ν = v_p(n)`, `\bar v_p(k) = min(v_p(k), ν)`, and the absolute index `e_L = v_L(p)` in mixed
    characteristic, `e_L = ∞` (no correction) in equal characteristic. The **jump set** uses Miki's
    decomposition of `ζ_p` with the conditions read on `v_K(α_i − 1)`.

## Layer 0: the canonical tower and the invariants of the page header

Standing hypotheses: `K`, `L` as in Convention 1, `L/K` finite. Write `n = [L : K]`,
`e = ramificationIndex K L`, `f = inertiaDegree K L`, `p = ringChar 𝓀[K]`, `q = Nat.card 𝓀[K]`,
and, when `L/K` is separable, `c = discriminantExponent K L`.

### 0.1 The tame index and the wild exponent

Define `wildExponent K L := padicValNat p e` (written `w`) and
`tameRamificationIndex K L := e / p^w` (written `ε`), so `e = ε p^w` with `p ∤ ε`. Prove
`isTamelyRamified_iff_wildExponent_eq_zero`, multiplicativity of both in a tower

### 0.2 The canonical tower and the relative Eisenstein polynomial

no new definition, that `L^{ur}/K` is unramified of degree `f`, that `L/L^{ur}` is totally
ramified of degree `e`, and that `L^{ur}` is the unique such intermediate field. Prove that every
uniformizer `α` of `L` has minimal polynomial over `𝒪[L^{ur}]` Eisenstein of degree `e`
(`IsTotallyRamified.isEisensteinAt_minpoly` on `L/L^{ur}`, with the intermediate-field adapters

uniformizer generating `L`. This is the pair *Unramified subfield / Relative Eisenstein polynomial*
of the page. Prove that the uniformizers of `L` are exactly the roots in `L` of the Eisenstein
polynomials of degree `e` over `𝒪[L^{ur}]`, so that every invariant defined from "an Eisenstein
polynomial of `L/L^{ur}`" below is quantified over this set. *Rests on:* Tau Ceti
`Unramified/Factorization.lean`, `Eisenstein/TotallyRamified.lean`,

### 0.3 Height and mean

### 0.6 Extensions of degree `n` by their unramified part

Fix `n ≥ 1` and `f ∣ n`, and let `K_f` be the unramified extension of `K` of degree `f`, unique up
to `K`-isomorphism and Galois with `Gal(K_f/K) ≅ ℤ/f` generated by Frobenius (Local fields and
ramification Layer 2). Write `𝒯(K_f, n/f)` for the set of `K_f`-isomorphism classes of totally
ramified extensions of `K_f` of degree `n/f`; `Gal(K_f/K)` acts on it by transport of structure,
`τ · [L]` being the class of `L` with `K_f` acting through `τ⁻¹`. Prove that choosing a
`K`-isomorphism `K_f ≅ L^{ur}` (0.2) and viewing `L` over it induces a bijection from the set of
`K`-isomorphism classes of extensions `L/K` of degree `n` with `inertiaDegree K L = f` onto the
orbit set `𝒯(K_f, n/f)/Gal(K_f/K)`: two choices differ by an element of `Gal(K_f/K)`, a
`K`-isomorphism `L ≅ L'` carries `L^{ur}` onto `L'^{ur}` (0.2's uniqueness), and a totally ramified
`L/K_f` of degree `n/f` has `L^{ur} = K_f` over `K` (`eq_unramifiedExtension_inertiaDegree_iff`),
which gives surjectivity. Two `K_f`-classes are `K`-isomorphic exactly when they lie in one orbit.
Prove the automorphism count: restriction `Aut_K(L) → Gal(L^{ur}/K) ≅ Gal(K_f/K)` has kernel
`Aut_{K_f}(L)` and image the stabilizer `Stab([L])` of the class of `L`, the elements of
`Gal(K_f/K)` that extend to automorphisms of `L`; so `#Aut_K(L) = #Aut_{K_f}(L) · #Stab([L])`, the
orbit of `[L]` has `f / #Stab([L])` elements, and

```text
Σ_{[L]_K : f(L/K) = f} 1/#Aut_K(L) = (1/f) · Σ_{[L] ∈ 𝒯(K_f, n/f)} 1/#Aut_{K_f}(L)
```

in `ℝ≥0∞`. Every degree-`n` class of `K` arises for exactly one `f ∣ n`. Neither separability nor
characteristic zero is used; the separable classes in `𝒯(K_f, n/f)` are the classes of Mass
formula's `σ_{K_f}(n/f)`. 6.3, 6.5 and 6.7 apply this to families and to Monge's count. *Rests
on:* 0.2, 0.5; Local fields and ramification Layer 2 (unramified extensions, Frobenius); Tau Ceti

`isTotallyRamified_unramifiedExtension_inertiaDegree`).

## Layer 1: the ramification polygon and residual polynomials

Standing hypotheses: `K` as in Convention 1, `φ ∈ 𝒪[K][X]` Eisenstein of degree `n ≥ 1`,
`L = K(α)` for a root `α` of `φ` with the structures of Convention 1 (so `L/K` is totally ramified
of degree `n` and `α` is a uniformizer, Tau Ceti `Eisenstein/PowerBasis.lean`), and `L/K`
separable. Write `n = ε p^w`. For a general finite separable `L/K`, every notion of this layer is
applied to `L/L^{ur}` with `φ` an Eisenstein polynomial of 0.2, and invariance under the choice of
`φ` is part of each milestone.

### 1.1 The ramification polynomial and its coefficients

Define `ramificationPolynomial φ α : L[X]` by `ρ(x) = α^{-n} · φ(αx + α)`, that is
`C (α⁻¹ ^ n) * (φ.map (algebraMap 𝒪[K] L)).comp (C α * X + C α)`. Prove: `ρ` is monic of degree
`n`; `ρ(0) = 0`; `ρ_i = α^{-n} Σ_{h ≥ i} C(h, i) φ_h α^h` for `1 ≤ i ≤ n`; and, since the terms
`φ_h α^h` with `h < n` have pairwise distinct valuations modulo `n` (`addVal_sum_eisenstein_powerBasis`)
and the `h = n` term has the residue of `h = 0`, the exact valuation formula

```text
v_L(ρ_i) = min_{i ≤ h ≤ n} ( n · v_K(C(h, i) φ_h) + h ) − n        (1 ≤ i ≤ n),
```

with `v_L(ρ_1) = d(L/K) − n + 1 = height K L` (the Eisenstein derivative formula) and
`v_L(ρ_n) = 0`. Prove that the roots of `ρ` in a splitting field `M` are `(α' − α)/α` for the
conjugates `α'` of `α`, each simple. *Rests on:* Tau Ceti `addVal_sum_eisenstein_powerBasis`,
`natCast_differentExponent_eq_iInf_of_eisenstein_adjoin_eq_top`; Mathlib `Polynomial.comp`,
`Polynomial.coeff_comp`.

### 1.2 The ramification polygon

Define `ramificationPolygon φ α := Polynomial.newtonPolygon ρ v_L` (Newton polygons Layer 2, in
Convention 4's orientation), its height function `N : ℕ → WithTop ℝ`, and its finite slope
multiset `Polynomial.newtonSlopes ρ v_L` of cardinality `n − 1`. Prove:

- **Independence.** Two uniformizers `α, α'` of `L` (equivalently two Eisenstein polynomials of
  `L/K`) give the same polygon: `α' = α u` with `u ∈ 𝒪[L]ˣ`, the roots `(σα' − α')/α'` and
  `(σα − α)/α` have the same valuations, and a polygon is determined by its slope multiset
  together with its endpoints (Newton polygons 0.6).
- **Vertices at powers of `p`.** The abscissae of the vertices of the polygon lie in
  `{p^0, p^1, …, p^w} ∪ {n}`, and `N` is affine on each `[p^{k-1}, p^k]` and on `[p^w, n]`,
  where `N = 0` (the tame segment, absent when `ε = 1`; the whole polygon is flat when `w = 0`). The proof is the valuation formula
  of 1.1: `v_L(ρ_i) ≥ n · v_K(C(h, i))` is governed by `v_p(C(h, i))`, which vanishes only when
  `i`'s base-`p` digits are dominated by `h`'s, so the lower envelope touches the heights only at
  `p`-powers (Greve–Pauli, *Ramification polygons*, Prop. 4.3 and Thm. 4.5).
- **The endpoints** are `(1, height K L)` on the left and `(n, 0)` on the right (the LMFDB's
  reflected plot shows them the other way round).
- **Rams.** Define `rams φ α : List ℚ` of length `w` by
  `r_k := (N(p^{w-k}) − N(p^{w-k+1})) / (p^{w-k+1} − p^{w-k})`, `k = 1, …, w` (Convention 5: the
  faces are read from abscissa `p^w` down to `1`, which is what makes the list nondecreasing).
  Prove `0 < r_1 ≤ … ≤ r_w`, and the **slope dictionary**: the Newton slope multiset of `ρ` is
  `{−r_k with multiplicity p^{w-k+1} − p^{w-k} : k = 1, …, w} + {0 with multiplicity n − p^w}`.
  Hence (Newton polygons 3.3) `v_L((α' − α)/α) = r_k` for exactly `p^{w-k+1} − p^{w-k}` conjugates
  `α' ≠ α` and `= 0` for `n − p^w` of them, and `#{α' : v_L((α' − α)/α) ≥ t} = p^{#{k : r_k ≥ t}} − 1`
  for every `t > 0`. A run `r_k = ⋯ = r_{k+d-1}` of length `d` is a single face, spanning
  `[p^{w-k-d+1}, p^{w-k+1}]` with integer endpoint heights, so Newton polygons 6.1 gives
  `p^{w-k-d+1}(p^d − 1) r_k ∈ ℕ` (2.6 sharpens this to `(p^d − 1) r_k ∈ ℕ`).
- **Wild segments.** `wildSegments φ α := (rams φ α).dedup.length`, the number of segments of
  positive slope; it is `≤ w`.

*Rests on:* 1.1; Newton polygons 0.6, 2.1, 3.3, 6.1 (`Polynomial.newtonPolygon`,
`Polynomial.newtonSlopes`, `newtonSlopes_X_sub_C`, `newtonSlopes_mul`, `card_newtonSlopes`).

**Checks.**

- (computation) For `x⁴ − 2` over `ℚ_2` (prefix `2.1.4.11`): `ρ = x⁴ + 4x³ + 6x² + 4x`, heights
  `(1, 8), (2, 4), (3, 8), (4, 0)`, envelope through `(1, 8), (2, 4), (4, 0)`, Newton slopes
  `{−4, −2, −2}`, rams `(2, 4)` and two wild segments. For `x⁸ − 2` (prefix `2.1.8.31`) the rams
  are `(2, 4, 8)`.
- (repeated ram) For `x⁴ + 2x + 2` over `ℚ_2` (prefix `2.1.4.4`) the single wild face runs from
  `(1, 1)` to `(4, 0)` with slope `−1/3`: rams `(1/3, 1/3)`, one wild segment, and
  `(p² − 1) · 1/3 = 1 ∈ ℕ`.
- (tame boundary) For `x³ − 5` over `ℚ_5` (`w = 0`) the polygon is the flat tame segment, the
  rams list is empty and there are no wild segments.
- (rejection) `x² + 2x + 4` and `x² + x + 2` over `ℚ_2` fail `Polynomial.IsEisensteinAt`, so no

### 1.3 Residual polynomials of the segments

For a segment `S` of the polygon with abscissae `k < k + l` and slope `−h/m` (Convention 7; in
`Suggested.lean` a segment is the data `k l h m` satisfying the predicate
`IsPolygonSegment ρ k l h m`, stated on the coefficient valuations) define
`residualPolynomial φ α k l h m : 𝓀[L][X]` by Ore's formula
`A(x) = Σ_{j=0}^{l/m} \overline{ρ_{jm+k} α^{jh − v_L(ρ_k)}} x^j`, where `h/m` is the slope in
lowest terms (`m = 1`, `h = 0` on the tame segment). Prove: `deg A = l/m`; `A(0) ≠ 0` and the
leading coefficient is nonzero; the constant term of the residual polynomial of a segment is the
leading coefficient of that of the next; for `h/m > 0`, `x^k A(x^m)` is an additive polynomial
over `𝓀[L]` (its exponents are the `p`-powers `p^{k'}` for `k'` ranging over the `p`-power
abscissae on `S`, by 1.2), so that `A` has the form `Σ a_j x^j` with `a_j ≠ 0` only when
`k + jm` is a power of `p`; and on the tame segment `A(x) = Σ_{j=0}^{n−p^w} \overline{ρ_{p^w+j}} x^j`
has `x^{p^w} A(x) ≡ \overline{ρ}` modulo the ideal of higher-valuation terms. Define
`residualPolynomials φ α : List 𝓀[L][X]` listing the segments from abscissa `n` down to `1`, the
order the LMFDB stores them (left to right in its reflected plot): the tame segment `[p^w, n]`
first when `ε > 1`, then the wild segments by increasing ram. *Rests on:* 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `Polynomial.sum`,
`IsLocalRing.residue`.

### 1.4 The equivalence class of residual polynomials and the associated inertia

For `δ ∈ 𝓀[K]ˣ` and residual polynomials `A_1, …, A_ℓ` with slopes `h_i/m_i`, define
`γ_{δ,ℓ} = δ^{-h_ℓ deg A_ℓ}`, `γ_{δ,i} = γ_{δ,i+1} δ^{-h_i deg A_i}`, and the orbit
`𝒜(φ) = {(γ_{δ,1} A_1(δ^{h_1} x), …, γ_{δ,ℓ} A_ℓ(δ^{h_ℓ} x)) : δ ∈ 𝓀[K]ˣ}`. Prove that
`𝒜(φ)` depends only on the extension `L/K`, not on `φ` or `α` (replacing `α` by `αu` multiplies
the `j`-th coefficient of the `i`-th residual polynomial by `\bar u^{jh_i − v(ρ_k)}`, and the
`δ`-orbit absorbs the residue of the unit; Greve–Pauli, Thm. 6.3). This is the
**equivalence class of residual polynomials** of `L/K`, the LMFDB's `residual_polynomials` up
to the stored representative, and the datum that defines a subfamily (6.6). Define
`associatedInertia φ α : List ℕ`, the list over the segments of `associatedInertiaOf A_i`, the
lcm of the degrees of the irreducible factors of `A_i` over `𝓀[L]`, prove it is an invariant of
`L/K`, and prove that each
entry divides the residue degree over `K` of the splitting field of `φ` (the roots of `A_i` are
the residues of the ratios of root differences, which generate the residue extension of the
segment's subfield of the splitting field; Greve–Pauli, Thm. 7.1). *Rests on:* 1.3; Mathlib

## Layer 2: breaks of a separable extension and the slope polygon

Standing hypotheses: `K`, `L` as in Convention 1, `L/K` finite separable, `M/K` finite Galois
with `L ⊆ M` (a tower `K ⊆ L ⊆ M` of local fields, `[Algebra L M] [IsScalarTower K L M]`), `G =
Gal(M/K)`, `H = Gal(M/L) = fixingSubgroup L ≤ G`, with the upper filtration `G^u` of Tau Ceti
and the lower filtration `G_t`.

### 2.1 The orbit-count function

The Herbrand functions `ψ_{L/K} = φ_{M/L} ∘ ψ_{M/K}` and `φ_{L/K} = ψ_{L/K}^{-1}` of `L/K`, their
tower law and the open-subgroup identity are those of
[Local fields and ramification](../LocalFieldsRamification/README.md) 4a.2, applied with
`A = 𝒪[K]`; this milestone supplies the integrand of their integral formula as a finite-level
count. Define `embeddingOrbitCount K L M (u) := (G^u ⊔ H).index` for `u ∈ [-1, ∞)`: the number
of orbits of `G^u` on `G/H = Hom_K(L, M)`. Prove it is nondecreasing in `u`, equal to `1` at
`u = −1` and to `f(L/K)` on `(−1, 0]`, equal to `[L : K]` for `u` large, and independent of `M`
(two Galois closures restrict to a common one, and `map_restrictNormalHom_upperRamificationGroup`
carries `G^u` onto the quotient's `G^u`). Prove that it is 4a.2's integrand: for every
`K`-embedding of `M` into `AlgebraicClosure K`, with `L` identified with its image and
`G_L ≤ G_K` its fixing subgroup, `embeddingOrbitCount K L M u = [G_K : G_K^u G_L]` (restriction
`G_K → G` is onto with kernel `G_M ≤ G_L`, carries `G_K^u` onto `G^u` by 4a.1 and `G_L` onto
`H`, so the index of `G_K^u G_L` is that of its image `G^u H`). Hence
`ψ_{L/K}(u) = (1/f) ∫_0^u embeddingOrbitCount K L M t dt` for `u ≥ 0`, with derivative
`[L : K]/f = e` beyond the largest break. *Rests on:* Tau Ceti `upperRamificationGroup`,
`map_restrictNormalHom_upperRamificationGroup`; Local fields and ramification 4a.1, 4a.2;
Mathlib `Subgroup.index`, `AlgEquiv.restrictNormalHom`.

### 2.2 Upper and lower breaks of `L/K`

Define `IsUpperBreak K L u` (`u ≥ −1`; `IsUpperBreak K L M u` in `Suggested.lean`, with the
closure explicit) by `G^u H ≠ G^{u'} H` for every `u' > u`, the
left-continuity convention of Convention 3 (`G^u` is constant on `(u − ε, u]` for small `ε`, so
the orbit count can only grow to the right of `u`). Define `IsLowerBreak K L t := IsUpperBreak K L (φ_{L/K} t)`,
with `φ_{L/K}` of Local fields and ramification 4a.2.
Prove: the upper breaks of `L/K` form a finite set; for `L/K` Galois they are Tau Ceti's
`UpperJump K L u`; the breaks `u > 0` are exactly the `u` at which the orbit count jumps, and at
such a jump the ratio `embeddingOrbitCount(u+)/embeddingOrbitCount(u)` is a power of `p` (`G^u`
is a `p`-group for `u > 0`, `lowerRamificationGroup_one` and `natCard_lowerRamificationGroup_one`);
the break `u = −1` occurs exactly when `f > 1` and the break `u = 0` exactly when `ε > 1`. Define
`swanSlopes K L : Multiset ℚ` as the multiset of positive upper breaks `u`, each with multiplicity
`log_p (embeddingOrbitCount(u+)/embeddingOrbitCount(u))`, where `embeddingOrbitCount(u+)` is the
constant value of the orbit count on `(u, u + δ)` for small `δ > 0`, and
`artinSlopes K L := swanSlopes.map (· + 1)`; the multiset does not depend on `M`.
Prove `card (swanSlopes K L) = w`. For `L/K` Galois, prove the multiplicity of `u` is
`log_p [G^{(u+1)} : G^{(u+1)+}]` in the notation of Convention 3, and that the Swan slopes are
integers when `G` is abelian (Hasse–Arf, Local fields and ramification Layer 3). *Rests on:* 2.1;
Local fields and ramification 4a.2;
Tau Ceti `UpperJump`, `upperJump_iff_upperJump_or_upperJump_inverseHerbrand`,
`natCard_lowerRamificationGroup_one`.

**Checks.**

- (Galois) `ℚ_2(i)`: Swan slopes `[1]`, Artin `[2]`; `ℚ_2(√2)`: Swan `[2]`, Artin `[3]`; for

- (non-Galois) `ℚ_2(∜2)`: Swan `[2, 3]`, Artin `[3, 4]`, two slopes for `w = 2`;

### 2.3 The discriminant exponent as an upper-numbering integral

For every intermediate field `E` of `M/K` with `H_E = Gal(M/E)` prove

```text
c(E/K) = ∫_{-1}^{∞} ( [E : K] − [G : G^u H_E] ) du,
```

where `c(E/K) = discriminantExponent K E` for the structure of 0.2's adapters. Proof: Hilbert's
formula and the tower law of differents give `e(M/E) · d(E/K) = Σ_{σ ∉ H_E} i_G(σ)`
(`differentExponent_eq_finsum_lowerRamificationGroup`, `differentExponent_tower`, with
`i_G = IsLocalRing.lowerIndex`); regroup by level, `Σ_{σ ∉ H_E} i_G(σ) = Σ_{t ≥ 0} (#G_t − #(G_t ∩ H_E))`;
multiply by `f(E/K)/e(M/E)` and use `#G_t − #(G_t ∩ H_E) = #G_t (1 − [G : G_t H_E]/[G : H_E])`;
finally substitute `u = φ_{M/K}(t)`, `du = dt · #G_t/#G_0`, which turns the sum over `t` into the
integral over `u` with `G_t = G^{φ(t)}` (`upperRamificationGroup_herbrand`). This is Serre's
Prop. 4 of IV §1 together with the change of variables of IV §3, not a statement about Artin
conductors. Corollary, with `E = L`: `c(L/K) = f · ∫_{-1}^{∞} (e − ψ_{L/K}'(u)) du`, which 2.6
evaluates as `f(e − 1 + e m_w)`. *Rests on:* Tau Ceti `Different/Hilbert.lean`,
`Different/Tower.lean`, `LowerIndex.lean`, `Herbrand/Basic.lean` (`coe_herbrand`); Mathlib
`intervalIntegral`.

### 2.4 The slope polygon theorem

Define `slopePolygon K L` as the lower convex envelope (`TauCeti.lowerConvexEnvelope`, Newton
polygons 0.1, indexed by the degree `n_E ∈ ℕ`) of the finite point set
`{(n_E, c(E/K)) : K ⊆ E ⊆ L}`. Prove:

- its vertices are the points `(n_{E_u}, c(E_u/K))` for the fields `E_u := L ∩ M^{G^u}`
  (the fixed field of `G^u H` in `M`, independent of `M`; `G^u` is left-continuous, so `E_u` is
  the vertex *before* the jump at `u`), `u` ranging over the upper breaks `u_1 < ⋯ < u_m` of
  `L/K`, together with `L` itself; `E_{u_1} = K`, and `(1, 0)`, `(f, 0)`, `(fε, f(ε−1))` are
  vertices;
- the segment starting at the vertex of the break `u` has slope `u + 1`: the segment from
  `E_{u_k}` to `E_{u_{k+1}}` (to `L` when `k = m`) has slope `u_k + 1`, by 2.3 applied to
  `E_{u_k}` and `E_{u_{k+1}}`, whose orbit-count functions agree with that of `L` up to `u_k`,
  respectively `u_{k+1}`, and are constant beyond, so that the two integrals differ by
  `(u_k + 1)(n_{E_{u_{k+1}}} − n_{E_{u_k}})` (witness `ℚ_2(∜2)`: breaks `2, 3`, vertices
  `E_2 = ℚ_2`, `E_3 = ℚ_2(√2)`, `L`, slopes `3, 4`);
- every other `(n_E, c(E/K))` lies on or above the envelope, by 2.3 and
  `[G : G^u H_E] ≤ [G : G^u H]` for `H ≤ H_E`;
- hence **the multiset of slopes `> 1` of `slopePolygon K L`, each counted `log_p(n_{E'}/n_E)` times
  for its segment from `(n_E, ·)` to `(n_{E'}, ·)`, is `artinSlopes K L`**, the slope `1` segment
  has width `f(ε − 1)` and the slope `0` segment width `f − 1`.

This is the LMFDB definition of the visible slopes (knowl `lf.slopes`), proved equal to Deligne's.
Define `visibleArtinSlopes K L := artinSlopes K L` and `visibleSwanSlopes K L := swanSlopes K L`;
the adjective records that they are read from `L` alone. *Rests on:* 2.1–2.3; Newton polygons
0.1, 0.6.

### 2.5 Rams are lower breaks: the polygon-to-break theorem

For `L/K` totally ramified with Eisenstein polynomial `φ` and root `α`, prove that the positive
lower breaks of `L/K` (2.2) are exactly the distinct entries of `rams φ α`, and that for each lower
break `t` of `L/K`, `#{σ ∈ Hom_K(L, M) : v_M(σα − α)/e(M/L) − 1 ≥ t} = p^{#{k : r_k ≥ t}}`. The
proof identifies the root valuations of 1.2, `v_L((σα − α)/α) = v_M(σα − α)/e(M/L) − 1`, with the
`G_t`-orbit structure. Two lower numberings are in play: `t` for `M/K` (Tau Ceti's `G_t`) and the
lower numbering of `L/K`, which is `ψ_{L/K} = φ_{M/L} ∘ ψ_{M/K}` (Local fields and ramification
4a.2); they agree only when
`e(M/L) = 1`. First prove the **non-normal sum formula**: for `σ ∈ G_0` (every coset of `H` meets
`G_0`, `L/K` being totally ramified) and `α` a uniformizer of `L`,
`v_M(σα − α) = Σ_{τ ∈ σH} i_G(τ)` with `i_G = IsLocalRing.lowerIndex` (the argument of Serre IV §1
Prop. 3, which uses `𝒪_M = 𝒪_L[x]` and never that `H` is normal; Tau Ceti's
`ramificationIndex_mul_lowerIndex_eq_sum` is its normal case). With `m := max_{τ ∈ σH} i_G(τ)` and
`i_G(τs) = min(i_G(τ), i_H(s))` for `τ` attaining the maximum and `s ∈ H`, the sum is
`Σ_{s ∈ H} min(m, i_H(s)) = e(M/L)(φ_{M/L}(m − 1) + 1)` (Serre IV §3, the computation behind
Herbrand's theorem). Hence `v_L((σα − α)/α) = φ_{M/L}(m − 1)`, and since `σ ∈ G_t H` exactly when
`m ≥ t + 1` (`mem_lowerRamificationGroup_iff_le_lowerIndex`), the root valuation is
`≥ φ_{M/L}(t)` exactly when `σH` lies in the `G_t`-orbit of `H`. A jump of `G_t H` at `t` is
therefore the ram `φ_{M/L}(t) = ψ_{L/K}(φ_{M/K}(t))`, a lower break of `L/K` in the sense of 2.2
(Deligne, loc. cit., A.6.2; Greve–Pauli, Thm. 4.5 in the Galois case, where `φ_{M/L} = id`;
witness `ℚ_2(∜2)` with its `D_4`-closure: `|G_t| = 8, 8, 4, 4, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1`, `H` a reflection,
`G_t H` jumps at `t = 3, 7`, `φ_{M/L}(3) = 2`, `φ_{M/L}(7) = 4`, the rams `(2, 4)`). Consequently
the **Herbrand function of `L/K` is read off the polygon**: `ψ_{L/K}` is the piecewise affine
function through `(0, 0), (s_1, r_1), …, (s_w, r_w)` with slopes `ε p^{k-1}` on `[s_{k-1}, s_k]`
and `ε p^w` beyond, where `s_k` are the Swan slopes of 2.2 listed with multiplicity; the LMFDB's
*Herbrand function of a family* is the graph of `φ_{L/K} = ψ_{L/K}^{-1}` through `(r_k, s_k)`.
For a general separable `L/K` the statement holds for `L/L^{ur}` (1.5) and the breaks of `L/K`
and of `L/L^{ur}` coincide for `u > 0` (`comap_restrictScalarsHom_upperRamificationGroup` with
`ψ_{L^{ur}/K} = id`). *Rests on:* 1.2, 2.1, 2.2; Local fields and ramification 4a.2; Tau Ceti
`LowerIndex.lean`,
`RamificationGroup.lean`.

### 2.6 The dictionary: slopes, rams, means

With `r_k` the rams (1.2), `s_k` the Swan slopes (2.2) and `m_k` defined by
`m_k := Σ_{j=1}^{k} (p−1) s_j / p^{k+1-j}` (the **means**), prove the four identities of the
LMFDB knowl `lf.herbrand_invariant`:

```text
s_k = (p m_k − m_{k-1})/(p − 1),      m_k = Σ_{j ≤ k} (p−1) s_j / p^{k+1-j},
r_k = ε p^k (m_k − m_{k-1})/(p − 1),  m_k = Σ_{j ≤ k} (p−1) r_j / (ε p^j),
```

together with `s_k − s_{k-1} = (r_k − r_{k-1})/(ε p^{k-1})`, `r_k^* = m_k − m_{k-1}`,
`r_k' = s_k − m_k`, and the **height identity** `height K L = e · m_w`, equivalently
`c = f(e − 1 + e m_w)`; the identities are pure arithmetic once 2.5 identifies `r_k` with the
breakpoints of `ψ_{L/K}`. Write `V_k` for the vertex field of degree `ε p^k` over `L^{ur}` of the
slope polygon (2.4): `V_k = L ∩ M^{G^v}` for `v ∈ (s_k, s_{k+1}]`, defined when `k = w` or
`s_k < s_{k+1}`, with `V_0` the maximal tamely ramified subextension. Prove that `m_k` is the mean
(0.3) of `V_k/L^{ur}`, i.e. that the LMFDB's *slope polygon* — the envelope of
`{(e', e' m(E/L^{ur}))}` over `L^{ur} ⊆ E ⊆ L` — is the shear `(n, c) ↦ (n, c − n + 1)` of
`slopePolygon L^{ur} L`, with vertices at abscissae `ε p^k` and heights `ε p^k m_k`. Prove the
**denominator theorem**: for a run `r_k = ⋯ = r_{k'}` of length `d = k' − k + 1`,
`[V_{k'} : V_{k-1}] = p^d` and `V_{k'}/V_{k-1}` has the single ram `r_k` (the tower law of Local
fields and ramification 4a.2 gives `ψ_{L/K} = ψ_{L/V_{k'}} ∘ ψ_{V_{k'}/V_{k-1}} ∘ ψ_{V_{k-1}/K}`, and `ψ_{L/V_{k'}}` is the
identity below the first break of `L/V_{k'}`), so its ramification polygon is the single face from
`(1, H(V_{k'}/V_{k-1}))` to `(p^d, 0)` and `(p^d − 1) r_k = H(V_{k'}/V_{k-1}) ∈ ℕ`: the denominator
of a ram divides `p^d − 1`, `d` its multiplicity (knowl `lf.rams`), sharpening 1.2's
`p^{w-k-d+1}(p^d − 1)`. *Rests on:* 0.3, 2.1, 2.4, 2.5; Local fields and ramification 4a.2.

**Checks.**

- (means) `ℚ_2(ζ_8)`: means `⟨1/2, 5/4⟩` from Swan `[1, 2]` and rams `(1, 3)`, with
  `s_2 = 2 = 2·(5/4) − 1/2`; `ℚ_2(∜2)`: `⟨1, 2⟩`; `F_{x⁴+2x+2}`: `⟨1/6, 1/4⟩`.
- (height identity) `c = f(e − 1 + e m_w)`: `11 = 3 + 4·2` for `ℚ_2(∜2)`, `9 = 3 + 4·(3/2)` for
  `F_{x⁴+2x²+2}`, `4 = 3 + 4·(1/4)` for `F_{x⁴+2x+2}`, `5 = 2 + 3·1` for `ℚ_3(∛3)`,

- (tame boundary) `ℚ_5(∛5)`: no means, `height = 0` and `c = f(e − 1) = 2`.

### 3.3 The Galois mean slope

from 2.3 with `E = M`: `c(M/K)/[M:K] = ∫_{-1}^{∞} (1 − 1/#G^u) du`, where `#G^u = t p^k` on

Tau Ceti `discriminantExponent`.

## Layer 4: indices of inseparability and jump sets

### 4.1 Indices of inseparability

For `L/K` finite separable with `K' = L^{ur}`, `n = [L : K'] = ε p^ν`, a uniformizer `π_L` with
minimal polynomial `f(X) = X^n + c_{n-1} X^{n-1} + ⋯ + c_0` over `K'`, and `e_L = v_L(p) ∈ ℕ∞`
(Convention 12), define for `0 ≤ j ≤ ν`

```text
i_j^{π_L} := min { n v_{K'}(c_h) + h − n : 0 ≤ h ≤ n − 1, \bar v_p(h) ≤ j }   (∞ if empty),
i_j       := min { i_{j'}^{π_L} + (j' − j) e_L : j ≤ j' ≤ ν }.
```

Define `indicesOfInseparability K L : List ℕ∞` as `[i_0, …, i_ν]` (`Suggested.lean` declares the
entries on the Eisenstein polynomial `φ` of `π_L`: `indexOfInseparabilityAt φ j = i_j^{π_L}` and
`indexOfInseparability φ j = i_j`). Prove (Heiermann,
*De nouveaux invariants numériques*, §1, Thm. 1.1 and Prop. 1.2; Fried for equal characteristic):
`i_j` is independent of `π_L`; `0 = i_ν < i_{ν-1} ≤ ⋯ ≤ i_1 ≤ i_0 < ∞`; `i_0 = height K L`
(the different exponent minus `n − 1`, by the Eisenstein derivative formula); and the **polygon
relation**: in the ramification polygon of `L/K'` with heights `N`, `N(p^j) ≤ i_j` for every `j`,
with equality at every vertex, so the points `(p^j, i_j)` lie on or above the polygon and include
its vertices (the LMFDB's black dots), and the rams are determined by the indices:
`r_k = (i_{w-k}^{\ast} − i_{w-k+1}^{\ast})/(p^{w-k+1} − p^{w-k})` where `i^{\ast}` is the lower
convex envelope of `j ↦ i_j` on the `p`-adic scale (Convention 5's indexing: `r_1` is read from
the last two indices, `r_w` from the first two; `x⁴ − 2` has `[8, 4, 0]` and rams
`((4−0)/2, (8−4)/1) = (2, 4)`). Prove the independence from the choice of `K'`
(`K' = L^{ur}` is canonical) and the behaviour under unramified base change
(`i_j` is unchanged). *Rests on:* 1.1, 1.2, 0.2; Tau Ceti
`natCast_differentExponent_eq_iInf_of_eisenstein_adjoin_eq_top`, `natCastValuation`.

### 4.2 Jump sets

For `K` with `FinitePadicExtension K p` containing a primitive `p`-th root of unity `ζ_p`, with
`e = v_K(p)` and `r = v_p(e)`, define a **Miki decomposition** of `ζ_p` as a tuple
`(α_0, …, α_r)` of units with `ζ_p = α_0^{p^r} α_1^{p^{r-1}} ⋯ α_{r-1}^p α_r` such that each `α_i`
satisfies one of: `v_K(α_i − 1) < pe/(p−1)` and `p ∤ v_K(α_i − 1)`; or `v_K(α_i − 1) = pe/(p−1)`
and `α_i` is not a `p`-th power in `K`; or `α_i = 1`. Prove existence (Miki, Crelle 328, Thm. 1)
and that the sequence `(v_K(α_i − 1))_{α_i ≠ 1}` is independent of the decomposition (Pagano,
*Jump sets in local fields*, Thm. 1.3). Define `jumpSet K : List ℕ` of length `r + 1` by
`u_i := v_K(α_i − 1)` when `α_i ≠ 1` and by the recursion `u_{i+1} = ρ(u_i)`,
`ρ(x) = min(px, x + e)`, for the undefined entries, and prove it is strictly increasing with
`u_0 ≥ 1` and `u_r ≤ pe/(p−1)`. Prove Pagano's characterization: `u_i` are the **jumps** of the
`ℤ_p`-filtered module `U^1(K)` with the filtration `U^i(K)` of Local fields and ramification Layer 1
and the `p`-power map, the levels `i` at which `U^i/U^{i+1}` is not generated by `p`-th powers of
`U^{⌈i/p⌉}` together with `U^{i+1}` after accounting for the shift `ρ`. The jump set is undefined
(the page prints `undefined`, the column stores `[]`) when `ζ_p ∉ K`; the Lean form is
`Option (List ℕ)` with `none` exactly in that case. *Rests on:* Local fields and ramification
Layer 1 (`unitFiltration`); Mathlib `IsPrimitiveRoot`.

**Checks.**

- (filled entry) `ℚ_2(i)`: `−1 = i²·1`, `α_0 = i` of level `1`, `α_1 = 1`, so the second entry is
  `ρ(1) = min(2, 3) = 2`: jump set `[1, 2]`.
- (both levels) `ℚ_2(√2)`: `−1 = (1 + √2)²(−3 + 2√2)` with levels `1` and `3`: jump set `[1, 3]`,
  of length `r + 1 = 2` with `3 ≤ pe/(p − 1) = 4`.
- (undefined) `ℚ_3(∛3)` contains no primitive cube root of unity: the jump set is `none`.

## Layer 5: Krasner's count by degree and discriminant

Standing hypotheses: `K` as in Convention 1, `n ≥ 1`, `p`, `q` as above, `v_K(h) :=
natCastValuation K h` for `h ≠ 0` in `K` and `v_K(h) = ∞` otherwise; `σ_K(n)` is Mass formula's
`totallyRamifiedOfDegree K n` and `c(L)` its wild exponent (`= height K L` by 0.3), so the
discriminant exponent is `n − 1 + c(L)`.

### 5.1 Ore's conditions

For `j ∈ ℕ` write `j = a n + b` with `0 ≤ b < n`. Define `IsOreAdmissible K n j` as:
either `b = 0` and `a = v_K(n)` (so `(n : K) ≠ 0`), or `b > 0`, `v_K(b) ≤ a` and `a + 1 ≤ v_K(n)`.
Prove **Ore's theorem**: `σ_K(n)` contains an `L` with `c(L) = j` if and only if
`IsOreAdmissible K n j`. The forward direction is the Eisenstein derivative formula: for an
Eisenstein generator with coefficients `φ_h`, `c(L) = min_{1 ≤ h ≤ n} t_h` with
`t_h := n(v_K(φ_h) − 1 + v_K(h)) + h` and `t_n = n v_K(n)`, the `t_h` are pairwise incongruent
modulo `n` for `h < n`, so the minimum `a n + b` with `b > 0` is attained at `h = b` and forces
`v_K(φ_b) = a + 1 − v_K(b) ≥ 1`, while `b = 0` forces `h = n`. The converse exhibits
`X^n + π^{a+1-v_K(b)} X^b + π` (`b > 0`) and `X^n + π` (`b = 0`, `j = n v_K(n)`). Over `ℚ_p`
this is Ore's condition `min(v_p(b) n, v_p(n) n) ≤ j ≤ v_p(n) n` (Pauli–Roblot, §2, stated
there over a general `K` with `v_K`). *Rests on:* Tau Ceti
`natCast_differentExponent_eq_iInf_of_eisenstein_adjoin_eq_top`, `addVal_sum_eisenstein_powerBasis`;
Mass formula Layer 0.

### 5.2 The discriminant box

Define `eisensteinBox K n j ⊆ (Fin n → K)` as the set of coefficient vectors `a` with
`v_K(a_0) = 1`, and, writing `j = an + b`: if `b > 0`, `v_K(a_b) = a + 1 − v_K(b)` exactly and
`v_K(a_h) ≥ ν_h` for `0 < h < n`, `h ≠ b`; if `b = 0`, `v_K(a_h) ≥ ν_h` for all `0 < h < n`,
where `ν_h := max(1, a + 1 − v_K(h) + [h < b])`. Prove: `eisensteinBox K n j ⊆ eisensteinSet K n`
(Mass formula Layer 3), the boxes for distinct `j` are disjoint, their union over admissible `j` is
the Eisenstein region up to the null set of inseparable polynomials, a polynomial with coefficient
vector in the box has every root generating a member of `σ_K(n)` with `c = j`, and the measure is

```text
μ(eisensteinBox K n j) = q^{-1}(1 − q^{-1}) · (q^{-(a+1−v_K(b))}(1 − q^{-1}))^{[b>0]} · Π_{h ≠ 0, b} q^{-ν_h}
```

for the Haar measure normalized on the integer box (Mass formula Layer 2's box volumes). *Rests
on:* 5.1; Mass formula Layers 2–3 (`eisensteinSet`, `measure_eisensteinSet`, box volumes).

### 5.3 Krasner's count

Prove **Krasner's theorem** (Krasner 1966; Pauli–Roblot, §2, in the form below): for
admissible `j`,

```text
#{ L ∈ σ_K(n) : c(L) = j } = n · q^{n + j} · μ(eisensteinBox K n j) / (1 − q^{-1}),
```

a natural number. The proof integrates Mass formula Layer 3's root-count identity
`Σ_L rootCount L = n` over the box, uses that `rootCount L` vanishes off the box when `c(L) ≠ j`
(by 5.2), and divides by the integral of the root count, `q^{-(n+j)}(1 − q^{-1})`, which is
constant on `{c = j}`; countability and measurability are Mass formula's. Prove the **closed form
over `ℚ_p`** (`v_K = v_p`): with `j = an + b`,

```text
#{c = j} = n q^{Σ_{i=1}^{a} n/p^i}                      if b = 0 (then a = v_p(n)),
#{c = j} = n (q − 1) q^{Σ_{i=1}^{a} n/p^i + ⌊b/p^{a+1}⌋}  if b > 0,
```

by evaluating `Σ_h ν_h` (Pauli–Roblot, §2; `krasnerClosedForm p q n j` in `Suggested.lean`, whose
values at `(2, 2, 4, j)` and `(3, 3, 3, j)` are closed by `decide +kernel`; the form is checked
below on `ℚ_2`, `n = 2, 4, 8`, and `ℚ_3`, `n = 3, 9`, against the mass formula). Prove the **finiteness of extensions of bounded discriminant**: for `n`
fixed and every `D`, only finitely many `L ∈ σ_K(n)` have `c(L) ≤ D`, and, in mixed
characteristic, `σ_K(n)` is finite (each `c(L) ≤ n v_K(n)`); hence for `K` a `p`-adic field
there are finitely many extensions of each degree inside `SeparableClosure K` — Krasner's
finiteness theorem, which Mass formula states only through its finiteness dichotomy. Prove the
**consistency theorem** `Σ_j #{c = j} q^{-j} = n`, by summing the formula, which is Mass formula's
Theorem 1 regrouped by `c`; and the isomorphism-class form, through the orbit count,
`Σ_{[L] : c = j} 1/#Aut_K(L) = #{c = j}/n`. *Rests on:* 5.2; Mass formula Layers 3–4 (root-count
integral, Theorem 1, orbit count, `exists_card_aroots_eq`).

### 5.4 The total count of extensions of given degree

Prove Krasner's **total count**: `#σ_K(n) = Σ_{j admissible} #{c = j}`, a finite sum of the
box counts of 5.3, and for `K = ℚ_p` a finite sum of the closed forms; record the values `#σ_{ℚ_2}(2) = 6`, `#σ_{ℚ_3}(3) = 21`,
`#σ_{ℚ_2}(4) = 92`. Prove that the number of all (not necessarily totally ramified) extensions of
`K` of degree `n` inside the separable closure is `Σ_{ef = n} #σ_{K_f}(e)` with `K_f` the
unramified extension of degree `f`, each `L` arising from its canonical tower exactly once
(0.2's uniqueness of `L^{ur}`). *Rests on:* 5.3, 0.2.

### 6.2 Compatibility with the base field

### 6.4 The Eisenstein diagram and the generic polynomial

For a compatible `I` with `w ≥ 1`, write `σ = je + i` with `0 ≤ i < e` for the monomial
`π^{j+1} x^i`, `h(σ) := σ/e`, and the **index** of `i` as `k(i) := w − v_p(gcd(i, p^w))`
(`k(0) := 0`). The **bands** are `B_k := {m_k ≤ h < s_k}` for `1 ≤ k ≤ w` and `B_0 := {h = 0}`.
Classify the points `P_σ`, `0 ≤ σ ≤ e s_w`, with `k = k(i)`:

- `σ = 0` is the **D-point**, *critical* when `gcd(ε, q^f − 1) > 1`;
- `σ > 0` with `h(σ) < m_k` is a **Z-point**;
- `σ > 0` with `h(σ) = m_k`, band `k` geometric (`r_k^* ≠ e_K`) and `k` final in its segment
  (`k = w` or `s_{k+1} > s_k`) is an **A-point**;
- `σ > 0` with `m_k ≤ h(σ) < s_k`, not an A-point, is a **B-point**;
- `σ > 0` with `h(σ) ≥ s_k` is a **C-point**, *critical* when `h(σ) ∈ {s_1, …, s_w}`.

Define `α(I) := #A`, `β(I) := #B`, `γ(I) := #critical C`, `δ(I) ∈ {0,1}` the number of critical
D-points (`alphaPoints K I`, `betaPoints K I`), all computable functions of `I` and `e_K`, `q^f`. Define the **generic polynomial**
`genericPolynomial K I` over the polynomial ring in variables `a_σ` (A-points), `b_σ` (B-points),
`c_σ` (critical C-points), `d_0`:

```text
f_I(x) = x^e + d_0 π + Σ_{A} a_σ π^{j+1} x^i + Σ_{B} b_σ π^{j+1} x^i + Σ_{C critical} c_σ π^{j+1} x^i,
```

with `d_0 = 1` when the D-point is not critical, `π` a uniformizer of `K`. Prove: every
specialization with `a_σ ∈ 𝒪_{K_f}ˣ` lifting a nonzero residue, `b_σ, c_σ ∈ 𝒪_{K_f}` lifting
residues, `d_0` lifting a nonzero residue, is Eisenstein over `𝒪_{K_f}` with Herbrand invariant
`I` (the ramification polygon of `f_I` is read off the point types: the A-points are the vertices,
by 1.1's valuation formula); and **Monge's theorem** that every member of `I/K` is generated over
`K_f` by a root of such a specialization with the coefficients drawn from a fixed set of lifts `ℛ`
of `𝓀[K_f]`, the **semicanonical polynomials** (Monge, *A family of Eisenstein polynomials*,
main theorem; Pauli–Sinclair, the enumeration theorem). Prove the count of the *rams* on the
diagram: the number of drawn points in `B_k` of index `≤ k` is `⌈r_k⌉`, and the number of C-points
on or below the top edge of `B_k` is `⌊r_k⌋` (knowl `lf.eisenstein_diagram`). *Rests on:* 6.1,
6.2, 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `MvPolynomial`.

**Checks.**

- (geometric ram) `[1]` over `ℚ_2` (`r_1 = 1`, `r_1^* = 1/2 ≠ e_K`): `P_0` is the D-point, not
  critical; `P_1` (`h = 1/2 = m_1`) the one A-point; `P_2` (`h = 1 = s_1`) a critical C-point.
  So `α = 1`, `β = 0`.
- (arithmetic ram) `[2]` over `ℚ_2` (`r_1 = 2`, `r_1^* = 1 = e_K`): `P_1` a Z-point, `P_3`
  (`h = 3/2`) the one B-point, `P_2` a C-point and `P_4` a critical C-point. So `α = 0`, `β = 1`.
- (mass) These give `(q − 1)^α q^β = 1` and `2` with `q = 2`, `f = 1`: the masses of 6.5.

### 6.5 Masses and the comparison with Serre

Define the **relative mass** `relMass K I := Σ_{[L]} 1/#Aut(L/K_f)`, the sum over the
`K_f`-isomorphism classes of members of `family K I` (a finite sum, each `L ⊇ K_f` by 0.2 and
the uniqueness of unramified extensions), and the **absolute mass**
`absMass K I := Σ_{[L]} 1/#Aut(L/ℚ_p)` over the `ℚ_p`-isomorphism classes. Prove
`relMass K I = f · Σ_{[L]_K} 1/#Aut(L/K)` (the `K_f`-classes inside one `K`-class form a torsor
under `Gal(K_f/K)/image`, and `#Aut(L/K) = #Aut(L/K_f) · #image`), hence `relMass = f · absMass`
when `K = ℚ_p` (knowl `lf.family_mass`). Prove the **family mass formula**

```text
relMass K I = (q^f − 1)^{α(I)} q^{f β(I)}
```

### 6.7 Monge's count of isomorphism classes over `ℚ_p`

For `K = ℚ_p` prove the closed formula for the number `N_p(e, f)` of `ℚ_p`-isomorphism classes
of extensions with ramification index `e` and residue degree `f` (Monge, *Determination of the
number of isomorphism classes*, main theorem; `mongeCount p e f`), in the form the LMFDB
evaluates it:

```text
N_p(e, f) = (1/f) Σ_{i=0}^{v_p(e)} Σ_{f' | f} Σ_{e' | e} T(i, f', e'),
T(i, f', e') = [ (p^{f'} − 1)/ε'' ∈ ℤ ] · φ(ε'') φ(f/f') / φ(p^i) · S(φ(p^i), e', f', v_p(e')) · Δ(φ(p^i) e' f', v_p(e''), i),
```

where `e'' := e/(φ(p^i) e')` must be an integer, `e'' = p^{v_p(e'')} ε''`, `φ` is Euler's function,
`S(n_0, e, f, s) := 1 + Σ_{i=1}^{s} p^i (p^{ϵ(i) n_0 e f} − p^{ϵ(i−1) n_0 e f})` with
`ϵ(i) := Σ_{j=1}^{i} p^{-j}`, and `Δ(m, s, i)` is `1` for `s = i = 0`, `(p^m − 1) p^{m(s−1)}` for
`s > i = 0`, `(p − 1)(p^m − 1) p^{m(s−1)+i−1}` for `s > i > 0`, `(p − 1) p^{ms+s−1}` for
`s = i > 0`, and `0` otherwise. State it as a theorem about the number of isomorphism classes
(Krasner's total count 5.4 divided along orbits); its acceptance values are
`N_2(2,1) = 6`, `N_2(4,1) = 48`, `N_3(3,1) = 9`, `N_2(8,1) = 1544`, `N_5(5,1) = 25`,
`N_2(2,2) = 10`, `N_3(9,1) = 753`, with the degree totals `Σ_{ef = n} N_p(e, f)` equal to

0.6, 5.4, 6.5; Mathlib `Nat.totient`, `Nat.divisors`.

### 6.8 Completeness of a totally ramified list by its mass

Let `K` be as in Convention 1 of characteristic `0`, `q = Nat.card 𝓀[K]`, `n ≥ 1`, and write
`H(L) = height K L` for `L ∈ σ_K(n)`, Mass formula's wild exponent `c(L) = d(L) + 1 − n` (0.3).
Prove: **if `S` is a finite set of members of `σ_K(n)`, pairwise not `K`-isomorphic, with
`Σ_{L ∈ S} 1/(#Aut_K(L) · q^{H(L)}) = 1`, then every totally ramified extension of `K` of degree
`n` is `K`-isomorphic to a member of `S`.** In Tau Ceti's vocabulary the conclusion is
`IsRepresentativeSet n S`; an abstract totally ramified `L/K` of degree `n` is separable in
characteristic `0` and so is `K`-isomorphic to a member of `σ_K(n)`. Prove the converse too: every
representative set is finite (5.3) and has mass `1` (Serre's Theorem 2), so for a finite pairwise
non-isomorphic `S ⊆ σ_K(n)` completeness is equivalent to mass `1`. Proof: extend `S` to a
representative set `R ⊇ S` by choosing a member in each class containing no element of `S`; by
Theorem 2 in its representative-set form, `Σ_{L ∈ R} = 1`; hence `Σ_{L ∈ R ∖ S} = 0` in `ℝ≥0∞`, and
every term is positive, so `R = S`. ⚠ The argument uses no finiteness, but in equal characteristic
with `p ∣ n` the set `σ_K(n)` has infinitely many classes (Mass formula's finiteness dichotomy),
Theorem 2 is a convergent series of positive terms, and no finite `S` has mass `1`; the criterion
is therefore stated in characteristic `0`, where `σ_K(n)` is finite and lists of mass `1` exist.
*Rests on:* 0.3, 0.5, 5.3; Mass formula Layer 4 (Theorem 2; Tau Ceti `main`,
`MassFormula/Representative.lean`: `IsRepresentativeSet`, `exists_isRepresentativeSet`,
`tsum_one_div_natCard_algEquiv_mul_natCard_residueField_pow_wildExponent_eq_one_iff`).

- (quartic over `ℚ_2`) The counts `4, 8, 16, 16, 16, 32` at `j = 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8` give the `48`

## Layer 7: the Galois root discriminant

Standing hypotheses: `F` a number field (`[NumberField F]`), `N := normalClosure ℚ F (AlgebraicClosure F)`
(`galoisClosure F`) with its `NumberField` instance from `normalClosure.is_finiteDimensional`,
`G = Gal(N/ℚ)`.

### 7.1 Definition and basic properties

Define `galoisRootDiscriminant F : ℝ := NumberField.rootDiscr N`, that is
`|discr N|^{1/[N : ℚ]}` (knowl `nf.galois_root_discriminant`). Prove: it is `rootDiscr F` when
`F/ℚ` is Galois; it depends only on the isomorphism class of `F`; `rootDiscr F ≤ galoisRootDiscriminant F`
(the root discriminant does not decrease in a tower, from the tower law of discriminants and
`N_{F/ℚ}(𝔡_{N/F}) ⊇ 1`); `galoisRootDiscriminant F = 1` only for `F = ℚ`; and the LMFDB's display
form `Π_p p^{e_p}` with `e_p := v_p(disc N)/[N : ℚ] ∈ ℚ`, as the exact value whose decimal
approximation the page prints. *Rests on:* Mathlib `NumberField.rootDiscr`, `normalClosure`,
`NumberField.discr`; Number-field arithmetic 5.10 (tower law).

### 7.2 The local Galois mean slopes of a number field

For a prime `p` define `galoisMeanSlopeAt F p : ℚ := v_p(discr N)/[N : ℚ]`. Prove, through the
completion at a prime `𝔓 | p` of `N` (Number-field arithmetic 5.1, 5.2, 5.6, 6.3): `N_𝔓/ℚ_p` is
Galois with group the decomposition group `D_𝔓`, all `𝔓 | p` give isomorphic completions, and

```text
galoisMeanSlopeAt F p = galoisMeanSlope ℚ_p N_𝔓   (3.3),
```

since `v_p(disc N) = g_p · c(N_𝔓/ℚ_p)` with `g_p · [N_𝔓 : ℚ_p] = [N : ℚ]`. Prove that `N_𝔓` is
the compositum inside `AlgebraicClosure ℚ_p` of the Galois closures over `ℚ_p` of the completions
`F_𝔭`, `𝔭 | p` (it is the `ℚ_p`-span of the `G`-conjugates of `F`; 5.3 semi-local structure), and
in particular, when `F` has a single prime above `p`, that it is the Galois closure of `F_𝔭` and
`galoisMeanSlopeAt F p` is the LMFDB `gms` of the `p`-adic field `F_𝔭`. Prove that
`galoisMeanSlopeAt F p = 0` exactly when `p` is unramified in `F`, and the tame value
`1 − 1/t_p` with `t_p` the tame degree of `N_𝔓/ℚ_p` when `p ∤ e_𝔭` for all `𝔭 | p`. *Rests on:*
3.3; Number-field arithmetic 5.1–5.3, 5.6, 6.3, 6.5.

### 7.3 The product formula

Prove `galoisRootDiscriminant F = Π_{p | disc F} p^{galoisMeanSlopeAt F p}`, a finite product over
`(discr F).natAbs.primeFactors` (the primes ramified in `N` are those ramified in `F`), and its
logarithmic form `log galoisRootDiscriminant F = Σ_p galoisMeanSlopeAt F p · log p`. Prove the
bound `galoisMeanSlopeAt F p ≤ 1 − 1/e_p + v_p(e_p)`, `e_p` the ramification index of `p` in
`N`, from the wild upper bound `d ≤ e − 1 + v_{N_𝔓}(e)` with `v_{N_𝔓}(e_p) = e_p v_p(e_p)`, so
that `galoisRootDiscriminant F < Π_{p | disc F} p^{1 + v_p([N:ℚ])}`. *Rests on:*
7.1, 7.2; Tau Ceti `Different/Wild.lean`; Mathlib `Nat.factorization_prod_pow_eq_self`.

### Boundary and off-hypothesis checks

- `x² + 4x + 2` over `ℚ_2` is Eisenstein; `x² + 2x + 4` is not (`4 ∈ 𝓂²`), and `x² + x + 2` is
  not (`1 ∉ 𝓂`): `Polynomial.IsEisensteinAt` rejects both, and no polygon is formed for them.
- The ramification polygon of an unramified extension is the point `(1, 0)`; `rams = []`,
  `wildSegments = 0`, `height = 0`, and the slope content of `ℚ_2(√5)/ℚ_2` is `[]^2`.
- A tamely ramified `L/K` has `visibleSwanSlopes = []`, `topSlope = 1`, `height = 0`, and the
  residual polynomial of its single segment has degree `e − 1` over the residue field; the
  slope polygon of `ℚ_5(∛5)` is the segment `(1,0)–(3,2)` of slope `1`.
- Krasner's formula at a non-admissible `j` is `0`, not a positive number: `ℚ_2`, `n = 4`,
  `j = 2` and `j = 4`.
- `IsCompatible` rejects `[3]` over `ℚ_2` with `e = 2`, and accepts it over `ℚ_2(√2)` (`e_K = 2`,
  bound `p e_K/(p−1) = 4`).
- `jumpSet` is `none` for `ℚ_3(∛3)` and for every `K` without `ζ_p`, and `some [1, 2]` for
  `ℚ_2(i)`.
- `galoisRootDiscriminant ℚ = 1`, and `rootDiscr F < galoisRootDiscriminant F` for `F = ℚ(∜2)`
  (`2^{11/4} < 8`).

```text
Tau Ceti LocalField (pin) ──► Layer 0 ──► Layer 1 ──► Layer 2 ──► Layer 3
NewtonPolygons L0–3, 6 ─────────────┘        │          │            │
                                              ▼          ▼            ▼
                                           Layer 4    Layer 5      Layer 7
MassFormula L0, 3, 4 ────────────────────────────────► Layer 5 ──► Layer 6 ◄── Layers 1–3
NumberFieldArithmetic L5–6 ───────────────────────────────────────► Layer 7
```

Layer 0 first. Layer 1 needs Layer 0 and Newton polygons. Layer 2 needs Layers 0–1, the Tau
Ceti Herbrand package and Local fields and ramification 4a.1–4a.2; 2.1–2.3 can be built in
parallel with Layer 1 and meet it at 2.5. Layer 3 needs Layer 2. Layer 4 needs Layers 1–2 (4.1) and only Local fields and ramification Layer 1
(4.2), so 4.2 is independent of everything else here. Layer 5 needs Layer 0 and Mass formula, not
Layers 1–4. Layer 6 needs Layers 1–3 and 5. Layer 7 needs 3.3 and Number-field arithmetic and is
otherwise independent of Layers 4–6.
