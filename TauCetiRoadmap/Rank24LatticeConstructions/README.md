# Roadmap: Niemeier glue data and explicit Leech constructions

The 23 rootful even unimodular lattices of rank 24 can be constructed uniformly: take an
orthogonal sum `R` of positive ADE root lattices, identify its discriminant quadratic module in
standard component coordinates, and take the inverse image of a concrete Conway--Sloane glue
subgroup `H ≤ A_R`.  This roadmap makes every row of that construction explicit and checked.  It
then constructs the rootless Leech lattice from the extended binary Golay code by Construction B
and an odd shift.

The outputs are 24 named lattices with proofs of the properties used downstream.  For each rootful
row the output is positive-definite, even, unimodular, of rank 24, and has exactly the displayed ADE
root sublattice.  The Leech output is positive-definite, even, unimodular, of rank 24, has minimum
norm `4`, and therefore has no roots.

This is a construction roadmap, not a classification roadmap.  It does **not** prove that an
arbitrary even positive-definite unimodular rank-24 lattice is isometric to one of these 24; prove
Venkov's equi-Coxeter theorem; prove uniqueness from a root system or uniqueness of a rootless
lattice; use theta series or harmonic theta series; identify Mathieu, Conway, or umbral groups; or
make any categorical construction.  In particular, the `A₁²⁴` Golay overlattice is rootful and is
not the Leech lattice.  The categorical questions motivated by these objects belong to a separate
research programme and are not milestones here.

Suggested home: `TauCeti/LinearAlgebra/IntegralLattice/Rank24/`, split into component glue
coordinates, the checked Niemeier table, rootful constructions, and the Golay/Leech construction.

## Dependencies and standing conventions

- This roadmap consumes the complete
  [integral-lattices roadmap](../IntegralLattices/README.md): `IntegralLattice`, its literal dual
  carrier, finite discriminant bilinear and quadratic modules, orthogonal sums, ADE discriminant
  coordinates, `ofIsotropicSubgroup`, the isometry
  `A_(R_H) ≅ H^⊥/H`, and the Lagrangian/unimodularity criterion.  It does not introduce another
  lattice, quotient, finite quadratic module, or gluing construction.
- It consumes the [algebraic-coding-theory roadmap](../AlgebraicCodingTheory/README.md): additive
  and linear codes, the tetracode and hexacode, the extended binary and ternary Golay codes, their
  checked weights and duality, Construction A, and code-to-discriminant coordinate isometries.
  Code names in this roadmap refer to those actual finite subobjects, never to a predicate
  asserting their parameters.
- An ADE component is the subtype of `TauCeti.DynkinType` carrying both `Valid` and
  `IsSimplyLaced`, with the positive Cartan matrix,
  roots of squared norm `2`, and Bourbaki node numbering.  A reducible root system is an **ordered**
  finite list of components.  The ordering is part of the glue table: glue digits are meaningless
  without it.  Reordering is implemented by an explicit coordinate equivalence and lattice
  isometry, not by identifying function types.
- For an integral lattice `L`, a root is `x : L.carrier` with `L.form x x = 2`.  Its root
  sublattice is the `ℤ`-span, in the ambient rational vector space, of all such vectors.  To say
  that a constructed overlattice has root system `R` means both that every norm-`2` vector lies in
  the embedded carrier of `R` and that the roots of `R` span that carrier.  Merely comparing the
  number of roots is not enough.
- The discriminant quadratic form is the dependency's half-norm form
  `q(x+L)=B(x,x)/2 mod ℤ`.  Conway--Sloane usually records the squared norm of a shortest coset
  representative.  A glue word is quadratically isotropic exactly when half that displayed norm is
  zero in `ℚ/ℤ`, equivalently when its displayed squared norm is even.
- The component alphabets and labels are exactly those of Conway--Sloane Chapter 4 §3 and Chapter
  16 §1.  For `A_n`, the alphabet is `ℤ/(n+1)` and `[i]` is the class `i`, represented by a shortest
  fundamental-weight vector of norm `i(n+1-i)/(n+1)`.  For `D_n`, use
  `[0]=0`, `[1]=s=(1/2,…,1/2)`, `[2]=v=(0,…,0,1)`, and
  `[3]=c=(1/2,…,1/2,-1/2)`.  When `n` is odd this is the cyclic group of order four generated by
  `[1]`; when `n` is even it is the Klein four group, with `[1]+[2]=[3]`.  The shortest norms are
  `n/4, 1, n/4` for labels `1,2,3`.  The `E₆`, `E₇`, and `E₈` alphabets are respectively
  `ℤ/3`, `ℤ/2`, and the trivial group; their nonzero shortest norms are `4/3`, `3/2`, and none.
- A bracketed digit string lists one label per component in the displayed order.  In a pattern
  such as `[1(012)]`, cyclically rotate only the parenthesized block and use every resulting word
  as a generator.  `[even permutations of (0123)]` means all twelve even permutations of the
  **positions**.  Pattern expansion is executable Lean data with constructor equations, not an
  unconstrained choice.  Each row also records a span mode.  For 22 rows it is `additive`: take the
  additive subgroup generated by the expanded words.  For `D₄^6` alone it is `F4-linear`: first
  take the scalar orbit under the pinned `F₄`, then take additive closure.  Thus the source's
  bracket typography is preserved without falsely interpreting its `F₄` basis as a binary basis.
  In neither mode are the printed words asserted to be a minimal additive generating set.
- All finite computations are proof-producing.  Decidable equality and `Fintype` instances may be
  installed locally to enumerate a glue subgroup, but an unchecked native boolean, a string parser,
  or a cardinality entered as a structure field is not a theorem.

### Tau Ceti and neighboring roadmaps

Tau Ceti's `LinearAlgebra/RootSystem/` development owns `DynkinType`, its rank and Cartan matrix,

## Layer 1: component glue coordinates and coset minima

Build a reusable, typed coordinate language before entering the 23 rows.

- For each component `T : {t : DynkinType // t.Valid ∧ t.IsSimplyLaced}`, construct its positive
  integral root lattice from Tau Ceti's Cartan
  data.  Prove that the simple-root basis has Gram matrix `T.cartanMatrix`, that the carrier has the
  expected rank, that the form is positive-definite and even, and that the norm-`2` vectors are
  exactly the roots supplied by the root-system development and span the carrier.
- Construct a finite quadratic module `GlueAlphabet(T)` using the component alphabets and labels
  fixed above, including the parity-dependent addition law for `D_n`.  Give explicit label
  representatives in the dual lattice and an isometry
  `A_(Q_T) ≅ GlueAlphabet(T)`.  Check every label's order, pairing, half-norm value, and shortest
  representative norm.  These are refinements of the ADE calculations in the integral-lattices
  dependency, not a second proof with a disconnected quotient.
- For an ordered component list, form the dependent coordinate product of alphabets and the
  orthogonal sum of root lattices.  Construct the induced discriminant-form isometry and prove its
  naturality under componentwise isometries and coordinate permutations.
- Define the minimum squared norm `μ_T(a)` of a vector in the dual coset labelled by `a`, with the
  formulas fixed above.  Prove **attainment and minimality**, not just a lower bound.  Deduce for a
  word `w=(w_i)` that the minimum of its coset in an orthogonal sum is
  `μ(w)=∑ i, μ_(T_i)(w_i)`.

  This is the heaviest item in the roadmap and the integral-lattices dependency does not supply
  it: that roadmap computes the discriminant form on chosen representatives, whereas here every
  coset needs its actual shortest vector.  It is stated as `IsLeast`, and it is proved family by
  family in the standard coordinate models, not by a general argument:

  - `A_n` in the model `{x∈ℤ^{n+1} : ∑x_i=0}` with `A_n^∨/A_n` generated by
    `[1]=e₁-(1/(n+1))(1,…,1)`.  The coset of `[i]` is minimized by a vector with `n+1-i`
    coordinates equal to `-i/(n+1)` and `i` coordinates equal to `(n+1-i)/(n+1)`, of norm
    `i(n+1-i)/(n+1)`; minimality is a rearrangement argument on the multiset of coordinates.
  - `D_n` in the model `{x∈ℤ^n : ∑x_i even}`.  The coset `[2]=v` is minimized by any `±e_j`, of
    norm `1`; the two spinor cosets are minimized by the `2^{n-1}` vectors with all coordinates
    `±1/2` and the prescribed sign parity, of norm `n/4`.
  - `E₆` and `E₇`: the nonzero cosets are the minuscule ones, minimized by the `27`, `27` and `56`
    minuscule weights, of norms `4/3` and `3/2`.  Conclude from the Weyl-orbit description of the
    minuscule weights, which is where the root-system dependency is used.
  - `E₈`: the discriminant group is trivial, so there is nothing to minimize.

  Conway--Sloane Chapter 4 §6 is the source for all four calculations.  Without attainment the
  `μ(w) ≥ 4` row checks below are vacuous and the root-sublattice theorem in Layer 3 has a gap, so
  no row may be discharged before this item is complete.
- Define the three table-pattern constructors: a literal word, cyclic expansion of a marked block,
  and even-permutation expansion.  The decoder verifies word length and label range before
  producing a typed coordinate.  Prove expansion/decoding lemmas and naturality under the indicated
  coordinate permutations.

Acceptance at this layer includes the odd and even `D_n` label additions, because treating both
alphabets as a cyclic `ZMod 4` silently gives wrong subgroups in nine table rows.

the source-row identifier.  The Lean data, not a parser for the typography below, is the
implementation input.  Prove by finite
calculation that each row has total rank `24`, all components have the displayed common Coxeter
number, the discriminant group is additively equivalent to the displayed product of `ZMod`
factors, and the expansion and selected span mode are valid.

`0↦0`, `1=s↦ω`, `2=v↦1`, and `3=c↦ω²`.  The ordinary additive closure of `[111111]` and the five
rotations of `[0(02332)]` has binary rank `5` and order `32`; it is **not** the row subgroup.
Taking the `F₄` scalar orbit is equivalently accomplished by adjoining `[222222]` and then taking
additive closure.  Prove that this enlarged subgroup has order `64`, is closed under multiplication
by `ω`, and maps to the dependency's actual hexacode.  This row-specific lemma is required in
addition to the uniform table checks.

For every row, independently prove:

1. the decoded words, expanded according to the row's span mode, generate a subgroup of
   cardinality `|H|`;
2. every subgroup word has quadratic value zero;
3. `|H|² = |A_R|`, and hence, using nondegeneracy, `H=H^⊥`;
4. every nonzero subgroup word satisfies `μ(w) ≥ 4`;
5. direct finite enumeration reproduces the displayed number of roots from the component formulae
   `#A_n=n(n+1)`, `#D_n=2n(n-1)`, `#E₆=72`, `#E₇=126`, and `#E₈=240`.

The proof objects for these five checks are stored beside the data and are re-evaluated when the
table changes.  A hand-entered proof that the expected order equals itself, or checking only the
listed generators rather than their generated subgroup, does not discharge a row.

## Layer 3: constructing the 23 rootful lattices

For a table row `r`, transport its checked subgroup through the inverse of the orthogonal ADE
discriminant isometry and define

`N_r = R_r.ofIsotropicSubgroup(H_r)`.

- Use the gluing dependency to prove `N_r` is even and
  `A_(N_r) ≅ H_r^⊥/H_r`.  Use the checked Lagrangian equality to prove unimodularity.  Prove
  positive-definiteness from the unchanged rational form and rank `24` from the component list.
- Prove the literal carrier formula
  `x ∈ N_r ↔ x ∈ R_r^∨ ∧ (x mod R_r) ∈ H_r`, together with representative and generator lemmas
  which make each named lattice computable.
- Combine the coset-minimum theorem with `μ(w) ≥ 4` to prove that a norm-`2` vector of `N_r` lies in
  `R_r`.  Prove that the component roots still span `R_r`; conclude that the root sublattice of
  `N_r` equals the embedded root-lattice carrier.  This is the required root-system verification,
  not a consequence of evenness, unimodularity, determinant, or the displayed root count.
- Give stable names to all 23 lattices and their property theorems.  Provide a finite eliminator so
  a theorem uniform over the table can be specialized without writing a second 23-case match.
- Prove that the 23 **displayed root-system descriptions** are pairwise distinct as ordered
  multisets up to component reordering.  Do not turn this finite data fact into a claim that the
  lattices exhaust or classify any ambient class.

The one-component rows `D₂₄` and `A₂₄` test respectively noncyclic component glue and a proper
subgroup of a cyclic discriminant group.  The three-component row `E₈^3` tests trivial glue.  All
three must go through the same API as the remaining rows rather than being replaced by ad hoc
lattice constants.

## Layer 4: code identifications and mixed-component acceptance tests

The long digit patterns are cross-checked against independently constructed codes and the mixed
alphabet is exercised before the generic table theorem is accepted.

- For `A₁^24`, use the following destination-to-source permutation from the coding dependency's
  `Fin 12 ⊕ Fin 12` order (identified with `Fin 24`) to the table order:
  `[0,1,2,6,5,13,10,15,17,4,18,19,11,7,14,12,3,8,23,20,22,9,21,16]`; all scalings are one.
  Prove that the subgroup generated by
  `[1(00000101001100110101111)]` is carried to the actual `extendedBinaryGolay.toAddSubgroup`.
  Package the coordinate map as an isometry of the complete finite quadratic modules, exhibit the
  base-lattice discriminant isometry, and prove that the row-23 lattice is isometric to
  `golayConstructionA`.  This theorem explicitly distinguishes that rootful Construction A
  lattice from the Leech construction in Layer 6.
- For `A₂^12`, exhibit the coordinate permutation and nonzero coordinate scalings which carry the
  table pattern `[2(11211122212)]` to the actual extended ternary Golay code.  In destination order
  these are respectively
  `[0,4,7,10,9,5,1,8,6,11,3,2]` and `[1,2,2,1,1,1,1,2,2,1,2,1]`.
  Prove equality after transport and recover the row-22 overlattice through the general code/glue
  comparison.  Package the map as a finite-quadratic-module isometry; the scalings are `±1`, so
  their squares are one and they preserve both the half-norm and polar pairing.  A bare field-
  monomial equivalence is insufficient.
- For `D₄^6`, identify the labels `0,1,2,3` with the additive group of the pinned `F₄` and prove
  that their `F₄`-linear span is carried to the hexacode subgroup in the traced-Hermitian
  discriminant coordinates.  The coordinate order is unchanged and the destination scalings are
  `[ω,ω,1,ω²,ω²,1]`.  Package this as a finite-quadratic-module isometry.  Each nonzero scale `u`
  satisfies `u³=1`, so both Hamming half-norm and the traced-Hermitian polar term
  `Tr(x y²)/2` are preserved.  Verify quadratic isotropy from that actual coordinate form, not from
  Hermitian self-duality alone.
- For the mixed row `A₁₁ D₇ E₆`, compute the order and quadratic value of `[111]`, prove its cyclic
  subgroup has order `12`, and construct the resulting lattice end to end.  This exposes any
  accidental assumption that all component alphabets are modules over one field.

These comparisons may prove equality only after displaying the coordinate equivalence and proving
quadratic-form preservation.  “Both are Golay”, an opaque additive equivalence, or “isomorphic to
the hexacode” is not a formal identification.

## Layer 5: functoriality and the actions used by the table

Develop only the action API needed to make coordinate choices and pattern symmetries harmless.

- A componentwise root-lattice isometry plus a permutation of equal-type components induces an
  isometry of the orthogonal root lattice and its discriminant module.  If it carries `H` to `H'`,
  use the naturality theorem from the gluing dependency to construct an isometry `R_H ≅ R'_(H')`.
- Specialize this to cyclic shifts and the even permutations occurring in the table.  Prove that
  expanding a parenthesized pattern and then permuting coordinates gives the same generated
  subgroup as permuting the seed and then expanding it.
- A code coordinate permutation preserving one of the named codes induces the corresponding
  lattice isometry through the `A₁`, `A₂`, or `D₄` discriminant identification.  Keep code
  monomial, diagram, component-permutation, and lattice actions distinct until the required
  compatibility is proved.

Do not identify full stabilizers with Mathieu, Conway, or umbral groups.  The output of this layer
is the induced-action mechanism and the concrete symmetries used in verification, not an
automorphism-group classification.

## Layer 6: the Leech lattice by Construction B and an odd shift

Let `C=G₂₄` on the dependency's fixed coordinate type `Ω = Fin 12 ⊕ Fin 12`.  Work in the rational
ambient space `Ω → ℚ` with form

`B(x,y)=(∑ i, x_i y_i)/2`.

Thus the dependency's Construction A lattice is the usual `2^(-1/2) P₂(C)` without adjoining
`√2` to the scalar field.

### The Construction-B half-lattice

Define `K` to be the sublattice of Construction A consisting of its integer vectors `x` whose
coordinate sum is divisible by `4`.  Equivalently, if `c` is the `0/1` lift of `x mod 2` and
`x=c+2z`, then `∑z_i` is even; the equivalence uses the doubly-evenness of `C` and must be proved.

- Prove that coordinate-sum divided by two gives a surjection from Construction A to `ZMod 2` with
  kernel `K`.  Hence `K` has index `2` and discriminant `4`, since the ambient Construction A
  lattice is unimodular.
- Prove directly that `K` is even and integral and identify its dual quotient.  These statements
  also follow from the index calculation and the dependency APIs, but the explicit quotient class
  is needed for the shift.

Choose the distinguished coordinate `∞ = Sum.inl 0` and set

`s_∞=-3/2`,  `s_i=1/2` for `i≠∞`.

Prove `B(s,s)=4`, `B(s,K)⊆ℤ`, `2s∈K`, and `s∉K`.  Let `H_s≤A_K` be generated by `s+K`.  Prove it
has order two and is quadratic-isotropic; since `#A_K=4`, it is Lagrangian.  Define the Leech
lattice by the existing gluing operation

`Leech = K.ofIsotropicSubgroup(H_s)`.

This also proves the carrier decomposition `Leech = K ∪ (s+K)`, evenness, unimodularity, and
positive-definiteness.

### Congruence model and absence of roots

Prove the following exact second description.  A vector `x : Ω → ℚ` belongs to `Leech` exactly when
there are integers `a_i`, an integer `m∈{0,1}`, and integers `b_i` such that

`x_i=a_i/2`,  `a_i=m+2b_i`,  `(b_i mod 2)_i∈G₂₄`,  and  `∑a_i≡4m (mod 8)`.

In these coordinates `B(x,x)=∑a_i²/8`.  Prove the lower bound `∑a_i²≥32` for every nonzero member
by the following exhaustive cases:

1. `m=0` and the Golay word is zero: every `a_i` is divisible by four, and the sum congruence rules
   out a single coordinate `±4`;
2. `m=0` and the word is nonzero: at least eight coordinates are `2 mod 4`, by Golay minimum
   distance `8`;
3. `m=1` and the word is zero: the unique coordinatewise absolute-value-one choice is
   `(1,…,1)`, whose sum is `0`, not `4`, modulo eight;
4. `m=1` and the word is nonzero: the unique coordinatewise absolute-value-one choice has sum
   `24-2 wt(c)≡0 (mod 8)`, using divisibility of Golay weights by four, so some coordinate has
   absolute value at least three.

In every case the first admissible raw squared norm is at least `32`, hence the lattice norm is at
least `4`.  The shift `s` attains norm `4`.  Conclude that the minimum is exactly `4`, the set of
norm-`2` vectors is empty, and the root sublattice is zero.

The proof must use the checked Golay code and its weight theorems from the coding dependency.  It
must not invoke a theta-series coefficient, sphere-packing optimality, or uniqueness of a rootless
rank-24 lattice.

## Ordering and completion criterion

Layer 1 fixes typed alphabets and coset minima; without them, the table cannot be interpreted and
the root-subsystem proofs have a gap.  Layer 2 enters and checks all finite data.  Layer 3 applies
the general gluing theorem uniformly.  Layer 4 independently checks the three code rows and one
mixed row.  Layer 5 supplies the coordinate-change invariance used by those comparisons.  Layer 6
then uses the binary Golay API but a genuinely different Construction-B-and-shift construction.

The roadmap is complete only when every one of the 23 table rows has all five finite checks, its
named glued lattice, and a literal root-sublattice theorem, and when the Leech construction has the
congruence equivalence and the explicit minimum-norm proof.  A verified table without constructed
overlattices, 23 even-unimodularity proofs without exclusion of new roots, or a lattice called
“Leech” without the four-case rootlessness argument is not completion.
