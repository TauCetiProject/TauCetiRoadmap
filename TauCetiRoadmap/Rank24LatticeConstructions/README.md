# Roadmap: Niemeier glue data and explicit Leech constructions

**Summary.** Conway and Sloane tabulate 23 root systems `R` of total rank 24, each an ordered
list of simply-laced (ADE) components sharing one Coxeter number, together with explicit glue
words generating a subgroup `H` of the discriminant group `A_R = R^∨/R` of the root lattice of
`R`. Gluing gives the lattice `N = {x ∈ R^∨ : x + R ∈ H}`. With the Leech lattice, which has no
roots, these are Niemeier's even unimodular positive-definite lattices of rank 24. This roadmap
makes every row of that construction explicit and checked, and builds the Leech lattice from the
extended binary Golay code by Construction B and an odd shift. It constructs the 24 lattices and
does not prove that they exhaust the even unimodular positive-definite lattices of rank 24.

Lattices sit in rational vector spaces with a rational symmetric form; a root is a vector of norm
2, and discriminant forms take half-norms modulo `ℤ`. The headline theorems are:

- For each of the 23 rows, the glue subgroup `H` generated by the row's words has the displayed
  order `|H|`, with `|H|² = |A_R|`; it is isotropic and equals its orthogonal complement in `A_R`;
  and every nonzero glue word has shortest norm at least 4, where the shortest norm of a word is
  the least norm of a vector of `R^∨` in its class.
- For each row, the glued lattice `N` is even, unimodular, positive definite and of rank 24, and
  its vectors of norm 2 are exactly the roots of `R`, which span `R`. The root system is proved
  by excluding new roots through the shortest-norm bound, not read off from a root count.
- Let `C` be the extended binary Golay code on 24 coordinates, `K` the lattice of integer vectors
  `x` with `x mod 2 ∈ C` and `∑ xᵢ ≡ 0 (mod 4)` under the form `(∑ xᵢ yᵢ)/2`, and
  `s = (−3/2, 1/2, …, 1/2)`. Then `K^∨/K` is elementary abelian of order 4 with the class of `s`
  isotropic, and the Leech lattice `Λ = K ∪ (s + K)` is even, unimodular, positive definite, of
  rank 24, with minimum 4 and no roots.

The lattice substrate (integral lattices, discriminant quadratic modules, gluing along isotropic
subgroups, ADE discriminant forms) is the
[integral-lattices foundation](../../Completed/IntegralLattices/README.md), and the Golay codes,
the hexacode and Construction A are the
[algebraic-coding foundation](../../Completed/AlgebraicCodingTheory/README.md); Tau Ceti
implements both, and most of the construction here. The ADE data are Tau Ceti's Dynkin types,
classified in [RootSystems](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md).
[CFSGBasicProperties](../CFSGBasicProperties/README.md) uses this Leech lattice for the Conway
groups, and [IntegralLattices](../IntegralLattices/README.md) separates the 24 lattices by their
root systems.

Layer 1, the component coordinates and coset minima, is Tau Ceti's and forms no layer here; the
other layers keep their numbers. Layer 2 states the finite checks as single theorems over the
table; Layer 3 the unimodularity, root theorem, names and eliminator of the 23 lattices; Layer 4
the comparison of the `A₂¹²` row lattice with the ternary Golay code; Layer 5 the pattern and Golay
symmetries as isometries of the row lattices; Layer 6 the discriminant group of the
Construction-B lattice along which the Leech lattice is glued.

## Conventions

1. **Lattices.** An integral lattice is Tau Ceti's `IntegralLattice V`: a full `ℤ`-lattice `L`
   (`Submodule.IsLattice ℚ`) in a finite-dimensional `ℚ`-vector space `V` with a symmetric
   `ℚ`-bilinear form `B`, integral on `L`. The norm of `x` is `B(x, x)` (`IntegralLattice.norm`),
   the dual is the literal `L^∨ = {x : B(x, L) ⊆ ℤ}` (`IntegralLattice.dualCarrier`), `L` is even
   when every norm is even and unimodular when `L = L^∨`. The discriminant group is `A_L = L^∨/L`
   and, for `L` even, its quadratic form is the half-norm `q(x + L) = B(x, x)/2 mod ℤ`
   (`IntegralLattice.discriminantQuadraticModule`). For an isotropic subgroup `H ≤ A_L` the glued
   lattice is `{x ∈ L^∨ : x + L ∈ H}` (`IntegralLattice.ofIsotropicSubgroup`). Tau Ceti names
   are written without their `TauCeti.` prefix.
2. **Displayed norms.** Conway and Sloane record the norm of a shortest representative of a
   coset, not its half-norm. A glue word is quadratically isotropic exactly when half its
   displayed norm lies in `ℤ`, that is, when its displayed norm is even.
3. **Components.** An ADE component is a Tau Ceti `DynkinType` that is valid and simply laced
   (`DynkinType.Component`), with the positive Cartan matrix, roots of norm 2 and Bourbaki node
   numbering. Its root lattice is spanned by the simple roots with Gram matrix the Cartan matrix
   (`DynkinType.rootLattice`). A reducible root system is an **ordered** finite list of
   components, and the order is part of the glue table: glue digits mean nothing without it.
   Reordering is an explicit equivalence of coordinates together with a lattice isometry, never
   an identification of function types.
4. **Roots.** A root of an integral lattice `L` is a vector of `L` of norm 2, and the root
   sublattice is the `ℤ`-span of the roots in the ambient space. A lattice `N ⊇ R` *has root
   system* `R` when every root of `N` lies in the embedded carrier of `R` and the roots of `R` span
   that carrier. Comparing numbers of roots is not enough.
5. **Alphabets and labels** (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 4 §3 and Chapter 16 §1). For `Aₙ` the
   alphabet is `ℤ/(n+1)`, and the label `[i]` is the class `i`, represented by the `i`-th
   fundamental weight, a shortest vector of the class, of norm `i(n+1−i)/(n+1)`. For `Dₙ` the labels
   are `[0] = 0`, `[1] = s = (1/2, …, 1/2)`, `[2] = v = (0, …, 0, 1)` and
   `[3] = c = (1/2, …, 1/2, −1/2)`: for `n` odd the alphabet is the cyclic group of order 4
   generated by `[1]`, and for `n` even it is the Klein four group with `[1] + [2] = [3]`; the
   shortest norms of the labels `1, 2, 3` are `n/4, 1, n/4`. The `E₆`, `E₇` and `E₈` alphabets are
   `ℤ/3`, `ℤ/2` and the trivial group, with nonzero shortest norms `4/3`, `3/2` and none. A word
   `w = (wᵢ)` over an ordered list has shortest norm `μ(w) = ∑ᵢ μ_{Tᵢ}(wᵢ)` and quadratic value
   `μ(w)/2 mod ℤ`. Tau Ceti's glue alphabets (`DynkinType.glueAlphabet`, with labels
   `DynkinType.glueLabelEquiv` and shortest norms `DynkinType.glueMinimum`) are these.
6. **Patterns and span modes.** A bracketed digit string lists one label per component in the
   displayed order. In a pattern such as `[1(012)]` only the parenthesized block is rotated
   cyclically, and every rotation is a generator; `[even permutations of (0123)]` means the twelve
   words obtained by the even permutations of the **positions**. Each row has a span mode. For 22
   rows it is additive: `H` is the additive subgroup generated by the expanded words. For `D₄⁶`
   alone it is `𝔽₄`-linear, with the `D₄` labels read in `𝔽₄ = {0, 1, ω, ω²}` as `0 ↦ 0`,
   `1 = s ↦ ω`, `2 = v ↦ 1`, `3 = c ↦ ω²`: each expanded word is replaced by its three nonzero
   scalar multiples, and then the additive closure is taken. In neither mode are the printed words
   a minimal generating set. The expansion of a pattern is a definite finite set of words (Tau
   Ceti's `GluePattern.expand`), not a choice.
7. **Finite checks.** Every finite statement is a theorem with a proof, over the decoded glue
   words and their generated subgroups; an unchecked native evaluation, a string parser, or a
   cardinality entered as a field of a row is not a theorem. Each check is one theorem quantified
   over the table index, so that every row is covered by the same statement and editing the data
   leaves nothing to re-enter by hand. Checking only the listed generators, rather than the
   subgroup they generate, does not establish a check.
8. **Leech coordinates.** The coordinate type is `Ω = Fin 24` with distinguished coordinate
   `∞ = 0`, and the ambient space is `Ω → ℚ` with the form `B(x, y) = (∑ᵢ xᵢ yᵢ)/2`. The
   Construction-A lattice of a binary code `C` is `{x ∈ ℤ^Ω : x mod 2 ∈ C}`, which is the usual
   `2^(−1/2) P₂(C)` without adjoining `√2`. The Golay code is Tau Ceti's `BinaryGolay.code`, in
   its systematic coordinates `[I₁₂ | B]` assembled with `Fin.append`. Code names refer to the
   actual finite subobjects (`BinaryGolay.code`, `TernaryGolay.code`, `Hexacode.code ω`), never
   to a predicate asserting their parameters.

### 2.1 The order of each glue subgroup

Prove `natCard_glueSubgroup`: for every row `r`, the subgroup `H_r` has exactly the displayed
order, `Nat.card H_r = r.glueOrder` (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 16 §1, Table 16.1; the
generators are those of Conway–Sloane 1982, Table V). It is a single theorem over the index; a
proof that the expected order equals itself, or a count of the listed generators, does not
establish it. With `|H|² = |A_R|` it gives `|H_r|² = |A_R|`.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `RootfulNiemeierType.glueSubgroup`, `RootfulNiemeierType.glueOrder`; the
row-by-row orders `RootfulNiemeierType.natCard_glueSubgroup_of_ne` (the 19 rows other than `A₁²⁴`,
`A₂¹²`, `D₄⁶` and `A₁₁ D₇ E₆`), `RootfulNiemeierType.natCard_glueSubgroup_a1TwentyFourth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.natCard_glueSubgroup_a2Twelfth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.natCard_glueSubgroup_d4Sixth` and
`RootfulNiemeierType.natCard_glueSubgroup_a11d7e6`.

**Checks.**

- `D₁₂²`: in the Klein alphabet `[12] + [21] = [33]`, and `H = {0, [12], [21], [33]}` has order
  4. Reading the even-rank `D₁₂` alphabet as `ℤ/4` instead makes `[12]` of order 4, and `[12]`,
  `[21]` then generate all 16 elements of `(ℤ/4)²`. A cyclic reading of the even `Dₙ` alphabets
  changes the generated subgroup in all nine rows with an even-rank `D` component (rows 1, 2, 5,
  7, 9, 13, 14, 18, 19).
- `D₆⁴`: the twelve words given by the even permutations of `(0123)` generate a subgroup of order
  16. All 24 permutations generate a subgroup of order 64 that is not isotropic: it contains
  `[0123] + [1023] = [1100]`, of shortest norm `3/2 + 3/2 = 3`.
- `D₄⁶`: the additive span of the printed words `[111111]` and the five rotations of
  `[0(02332)]` has order 32 and does not contain `[222222] = ω² · [111111]`; the `𝔽₄`-linear span
  has order 64. Reading row 19 additively gets the order wrong by a factor of 2.

### 2.2 The Lagrangian property

Prove `isLagrangian_glueSubgroup`: for every row `r`, `H_r` is a quadratic Lagrangian of `A_R`:
it is isotropic for the discriminant quadratic form and equals its orthogonal complement `H_r^⊥`
for the discriminant bilinear form (Chenevier 2020, §5.1 (a), (c) and §5.3). The route: `H_r` is
isotropic, `A_R` is nondegenerate, so `|H_r| · |H_r^⊥| = |A_R|`, and §2.1 with `|H|² = |A_R|`
forces `H_r = H_r^⊥`.

*Needs:* §2.1; Tau Ceti `RootfulNiemeierType.isIsotropic_glueSubgroup`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.glueOrder_sq_eq_natCard_glueModule`,
`DynkinType.isNondegenerate_pi_glueAlphabet`; or, row by row,
`RootfulNiemeierType.isLagrangian_glueSubgroup_of_ne`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.isLagrangian_glueSubgroup_a1TwentyFourth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.isLagrangian_glueSubgroup_a2Twelfth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.isLagrangian_glueSubgroup_d4Sixth` and
`RootfulNiemeierType.isLagrangian_glueSubgroup_a11d7e6`.

**Checks.**

- `A₂₄`: the pairing on `ℤ/25` is `b([i], [j]) = −ij/25 mod ℤ`, so
  `H^⊥ = {[a] : 25 ∣ 5a} = {0, [5], [10], [15], [20]} = H`. The whole group `ℤ/25` is not
  isotropic: `[1]` has shortest norm `24/25`, which is not even.

### 2.3 Glue words of shortest norm at least four

Prove `four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_glueSubgroup`: for every row `r` and every nonzero
`w ∈ H_r`, `μ(w) = ∑ᵢ μ_{Tᵢ}(wᵢ) ≥ 4` (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 16 §1, with the coset
minima of Chapter 4 §§6–8). Since `μ(w)` is the least norm of a vector of `R^∨` in the class `w`,
attained (`DynkinType.isLeast_sum_glueMinimum`), the statement says that every vector of `R^∨`
whose class is a nonzero element of `H_r` has norm at least 4. Without attainment of the minima
this bound would say nothing about the glued lattice.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `DynkinType.glueMinimum`; the row-by-row bounds
`RootfulNiemeierType.four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_glueSubgroup_of_ne`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_glueSubgroup_a1TwentyFourth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_glueSubgroup_d4Sixth`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_glueSubgroup_a11d7e6`; for `A₂¹²`,
`four_le_sum_glueMinimum_of_mem_a2TwelfthGlueSubgroup` transferred by
`RootfulNiemeierType.map_glueSubgroup_a2Twelfth` and
`RootfulNiemeierType.le_sum_glueMinimum_of_map_glueModuleIsometry`.

**Checks.**

- `A₁₇ E₇`: `[31]` has `μ = 3·15/18 + 3/2 = 4`, and its multiples `[60]`, `[91]`, `[12 0]`,
  `[15 1]` have `μ = 4, 6, 4, 4`. The bound is attained, so a strict inequality would be false.
- `A₁₁ D₇ E₆`: `[111]` has `μ = 11/12 + 7/4 + 4/3 = 4`, and `k · [111]` has `μ = 6` for
  `k ≡ 5, 7 (mod 12)` and `μ = 4` for the other nonzero `k`. The odd `D₇` alphabet must be cyclic
  here: `2 · [111] = [2 2 2]` has `μ = 20/12 + 1 + 4/3 = 4`, whereas a Klein reading of `D₇` would
  give `[2 0 2]` with `μ = 3`, which is not even.

### Examples

- Row 3, `E₈³`: `A_R` and `H` are trivial and `N = R = E₈³`. Row 1, `D₂₄`, glues along the
  spinor class of the Klein alphabet of `D₂₄`, of shortest norm `24/4 = 6`. Row 4, `A₂₄`, glues
  along the proper subgroup of order 5 of the cyclic group `ℤ/25`. These three rows go through
  the same definitions as the others.
- Row 19, `D₄⁶`: the `𝔽₄`-linear span of the printed words is carried onto the hexacode by the
  coordinate scalings `[ω, ω, 1, ω², ω², 1]`, and it is closed under multiplication by `ω`, which
  on `D₄` labels is the permutation `1 ↦ 3 ↦ 2 ↦ 1`.

## Layer 3: constructing the 23 rootful lattices

For a row `r`, `R_r` is the orthogonal sum of the root lattices of its components
(`RootfulNiemeierType.rootLattice`), and `N_r` is `RootfulNiemeierType.lattice r`, the lattice
`R_r.ofIsotropicSubgroup(H_r)` obtained by transporting `H_r` through the discriminant isometry
`DynkinType.glueWordIsometry` (`RootfulNiemeierType.lattice_def`). It satisfies
`x ∈ N_r ↔ x ∈ R_r^∨ ∧ (x mod R_r) ∈ H_r` (`RootfulNiemeierType.mem_lattice_carrier_iff`), is
spanned by `R_r` and representatives of the glue generators
(`RootfulNiemeierType.lattice_carrier_eq_sup_span`), has discriminant module `H_r^⊥/H_r`
(`DynkinType.gluedLatticeDiscriminantIsometry`), and is even, positive definite and of rank 24
(`RootfulNiemeierType.isEven_lattice`, `RootfulNiemeierType.isPosDef_lattice`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.finrank_lattice`).

### 3.1 Unimodularity of the 23 lattices

Prove `isUnimodular_lattice`: for every row `r`, `N_r` is unimodular, `N_r = N_r^∨` (Niemeier
1973; Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 16 §1). Together with evenness, positive definiteness and
rank, every `N_r` is an even unimodular positive-definite lattice of rank 24. The route: a glued
lattice is unimodular exactly when its glue subgroup is Lagrangian, or exactly when `|H|² = |A_R|`
(`RootfulNiemeierType.isUnimodular_lattice_iff`), and §2.1 or §2.2 supplies this.

*Needs:* §2.1 or §2.2; Tau Ceti `RootfulNiemeierType.isUnimodular_lattice_iff`.

**Checks.**

- `A₂₄`: `|A_R| = 25` and `|H| = 5`, so `R` has index 5 in `N` and the discriminant of `N` is
  `|A_R|/|H|² = 25/5² = 1`, not `|A_R|/|H| = 5`: gluing divides the discriminant by the square of
  the index. The order-5 subgroup is the largest isotropic subgroup of `ℤ/25`, since `[1]` has
  shortest norm `24/25`.

### 3.2 No new roots

Prove `image_vectorsOfNorm_two_lattice`: for every row `r`, the vectors of norm 2 of `N_r` are,
as ambient vectors, exactly the vectors of norm 2 of `R_r`; and prove
`span_vectorsOfNorm_two_lattice`: they span the carrier of `R_r`, so that `N_r` has root system
`R_r` in the sense of Convention 4 (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 16 §1 and Chapter 4 §6). The
route: a vector `x ∈ N_r` outside `R_r` lies in a nonzero class `w ∈ H_r`, so its norm is at least
`μ(w) ≥ 4` by `DynkinType.isLeast_sum_glueMinimum` and §2.3; the roots of `R_r` span `R_r`
(`DynkinType.span_vectorsOfNorm_two_pi_rootLattice`). This is the root-system verification; it
is not a consequence of evenness, unimodularity, the determinant or the displayed root count.

*Needs:* §2.3; Tau Ceti `DynkinType.image_vectorsOfNorm_gluedLattice_eq`,
`DynkinType.span_vectorsOfNorm_two_gluedLattice`, `RootfulNiemeierType.lattice_def`.

**Checks.**

- Gluing `D₈` along its spinor class `[1]`, of shortest norm `8/4 = 2` and so isotropic, gives
  `E₈`, with 240 roots instead of the 112 of `D₈`. An isotropic glue word of shortest norm 2 adds
  roots, which is why the bound of §2.3 is needed and evenness and unimodularity are not enough.
- Root counts do not determine root systems: the rows `D₁₆ E₈` and `E₈³` both have 720 roots,
  `A₁₇ E₇` and `D₁₀ E₇²` both 432, `A₁₁ D₇ E₆` and `E₆⁴` both 288, `A₉² D₆` and `D₆⁴` both 240,
  and `A₅⁴ D₄` and `D₄⁶` both 144. A theorem comparing root counts would not tell these apart.

### 3.3 Names and the finite eliminator

Give each of the 23 lattices a stable name, an abbreviation of `N_r` named after its root system
(the `D₂₄` lattice, …, the `A₁²⁴` lattice), with its evenness, unimodularity, positive
definiteness, rank and root-system theorems specialised to it. Prove the finite eliminator: a
predicate on the rows holds for every row if and only if it holds for each of the 23
constructors, so that a theorem uniform over the table specialises to a named row without a
second 23-case match. The eliminator is a convenience lemma: it is the case analysis on the 23
constructors of `RootfulNiemeierType`, stated once. The one-component rows `D₂₄` and `A₂₄`, which
test noncyclic component glue and a proper subgroup of a cyclic discriminant group, and the
trivially glued `E₈³` are named through the same definition `N_r`, not as separate lattice
constants.

### 4.1 The row-`A₂¹²` lattice and the ternary Golay code

## Layer 5: functoriality and the actions used by the table

A componentwise isometry of root lattices together with a permutation of components of equal
type induces an isometry of the orthogonal sums and of their discriminant modules, and if it
carries `H` to `H'` it induces an isometry `R_H ≅ R'_{H'}` of the glued lattices
(`DynkinType.gluedLatticeIsometry`, `DynkinType.gluedLatticePiIsometry`,
`DynkinType.gluedLatticePiCongrLeftIsometry`). Expanding a pattern and then permuting positions
by one of its symmetries gives the same decoded words as permuting first
(`GluePattern.decodedWords_comp`). This layer uses only these actions and the concrete
symmetries of the table; it does not determine full automorphism groups.

### 5.1 Pattern symmetries of the rows

For a row `r`, a cyclic pattern or an even-permutation pattern of `r` has a symmetry group
(`GluePattern.symmetries`): the powers of the rotation of its marked block, or the even
permutations of its positions, acting on the positions of components of one type. Prove:

1. For every row `r` other than `A₅⁴ D₄`, every such pattern of `r` and every `g` in its symmetry
   group, permuting the positions of glue words by `g` maps `H_r` onto `H_r`, and the
   corresponding permutation of the components of `r` is an isometry of `N_r` onto itself. The
   rows concerned are `D₈³`, `E₆⁴`, `D₆⁴`, `A₈³`, `A₆⁴`, `D₄⁶`, `A₄⁶`, `A₃⁸`, `A₂¹²` and `A₁²⁴`;
   in `D₄⁶` the rotation of the last five positions fixes the other printed word `[111111]`.
2. For `A₅⁴ D₄`, let `ρ` move the `A₅` components in positions 2, 3, 4 to positions 3, 4, 2, and
   let `τ` be the automorphism of the `D₄` alphabet permuting the labels `1 ↦ 2 ↦ 3 ↦ 1`, which is
   multiplication by `ω²` in the `𝔽₄` labels of Convention 6 and is induced by a triality isometry
   of the `D₄` root lattice (`DynkinType.typeD4ScalarIsometry`). Then `ρ` followed by `τ` on the
   fifth coordinate maps `H` onto `H`, and `ρ` together with that triality isometry is an isometry
   of `N_{A₅⁴ D₄}` onto itself; so are its powers. The rotation `ρ` alone, which the pattern
   `[2(024)0]` indicates, does not preserve `H`.

(Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 16 §1 for the patterns; Ebeling, Chapters 1 and 3 for gluing.)

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `GluePattern.symmetries`, `GluePattern.decodedWords_comp`,
`RootfulNiemeierType.glueSubgroup_def`, `DynkinType.gluedLatticePiCongrLeftIsometry`,
`DynkinType.gluedLatticePiIsometry`, `DynkinType.typeD4ScalarIsometry` (for item 2),
`RootfulNiemeierType.lattice_def`.

**Checks.**

- `A₅⁴ D₄`: `ρ` carries `[33001] ∈ H` to `[30301]`, and `[30301] − [30302] = [00003]` has
  shortest norm 1, which is odd, so `[30301] ∉ H`. With `τ`, the three words `[33001]`, `[30302]`,
  `[30033]` are permuted cyclically and the rotations of `[2(024)0]` among themselves, so `H` is
  preserved. Composing `ρ` with the other 3-cycle `1 ↦ 3 ↦ 2 ↦ 1` does not preserve `H`; it
  preserves it only with `ρ⁻¹`.
- `D₆⁴`: the transposition of the first two components is not a symmetry: it carries
  `[0123] ∈ H` to `[1023]`, and `[1023] − [0123] = [1100]` has shortest norm 3, so `[1023] ∉ H`.

### 5.2 Golay-code automorphisms

Prove: for every permutation `σ` of `Fin 24` carrying `BinaryGolay.code` onto itself, there is an

permutation by `σ`. For every `u : Fin 12 → (ℤ/3)ˣ` and every permutation `σ` of `Fin 12` such

`N_{A₂¹²}` that permutes its `A₂` components and acts on each by `±1`, and whose induced action on

## Layer 6: the Leech lattice by Construction B and an odd shift

In the coordinates of Convention 8, let `C = BinaryGolay.code` and let `K` be the Construction-B
lattice `BinaryGolay.constructionBLattice`: the vectors `x` of the Construction-A lattice
`BinaryGolay.constructionALattice` (integer vectors with `x mod 2 ∈ C`) with `∑ xᵢ ≡ 0 (mod 4)`.
Equivalently, writing `x = c + 2z` with `c` the 0/1 lift of `x mod 2`, the sum `∑ zᵢ` is even;
the equivalence uses that `C` is doubly even (`ConstructionB.val_add_two_mul_mem_lattice_iff`).
Half the coordinate sum is a surjection of the Construction-A lattice onto `ℤ/2` with kernel `K`
(`ConstructionB.sumHalf`), so `K` has index 2 and discriminant 4, the Construction-A lattice
being unimodular; `K` is even. The odd shift is `s = (−3/2, 1/2, …, 1/2)` (`leechShift`), with
`B(s, s) = 4`, `B(s, K) ⊆ ℤ`, `2s ∈ K` and `s ∉ K`. Its class generates the subgroup
`H_s = leechShiftSubgroup` of order 2, isotropic and hence Lagrangian since `|A_K| = 4`, and the
Leech lattice is `Λ = K.ofIsotropicSubgroup(H_s)` (`leechLattice`), so that `Λ = K ∪ (s + K)`
(Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 7 §5 and Chapter 10; Wilson, §5.4). It is even, unimodular,
positive definite and of rank 24. A vector `x : Ω → ℚ` lies in `Λ` exactly when `x = a/2` with
`a = m·𝟙 + 2b`, `m ∈ {0, 1}`, `b mod 2 ∈ C` and `∑ aᵢ ≡ 4m (mod 8)`; then `B(x, x) = ∑ aᵢ²/8`, and
`∑ aᵢ² ≥ 32` for `x ≠ 0` by the Golay weights alone, so `Λ` has minimum 4, attained by `s`, and no
roots (`mem_leechLattice_carrier_iff_exists`, `BinaryGolay.thirtyTwo_le_sum_sq`,
`minimum_leechLattice`, `vectorsOfNorm_two_leechLattice`). No theta series, sphere-packing bound
or uniqueness of a rootless lattice enters.

### 6.1 The discriminant group of the Construction-B lattice

Let `e_∞` be the coordinate vector at `∞`. Prove
`two_nsmul_discriminantGroup_constructionBLattice`: twice every element of `A_K = K^∨/K` vanishes,
so that `A_K`, of order 4, is an elementary abelian group. Prove that it has basis the classes of
`2e_∞` and of `s`; that the discriminant quadratic form of `K` vanishes on both classes and takes
the value `1/2` on their sum; and that the discriminant bilinear form pairs the two classes to
`1/2` (Conway–Sloane, *SPLAG*, Chapter 7 §5). Prove
`isIsotropic_constructionBLattice_iff_of_natCard_eq_two`: consequently a subgroup of order 2 of
`A_K` is isotropic if and only if it is `H_s` or the subgroup generated by the class of `2e_∞`.
The class of `s` is the one the gluing above uses.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `BinaryGolay.constructionBLattice`,
`BinaryGolay.natCard_discriminantGroup_constructionBLattice`, `leechShiftClass`,
`two_nsmul_leechShiftClass`, `leechShiftClass_ne_zero`, `leechShiftSubgroup`,
`isIsotropic_leechShiftSubgroup`, `BinaryGolay.constructionBLattice_carrier_le`,
`BinaryGolay.isDoublyEven_code`.

**Checks.**

- `B(2e_∞, 2e_∞) = 2` and `B(s, s) = 4`, so both classes have quadratic value 0, and
  `B(2e_∞, s) = −3/2`, which is `1/2` modulo `ℤ`. The sum is the class of `s + 2e_∞ =
  (1/2, …, 1/2)`, of norm `24 · (1/4)/2 = 3`, quadratic value `1/2`. The naive shift
  `(1/2, …, 1/2)` lies in `K^∨` but glues `K` to an odd lattice containing a vector of norm 3;
  this is why `s_∞ = −3/2`.
- `4e_∞ ∈ K` and `2 · (1/2, …, 1/2) = 𝟙 ∈ K` (the all-ones word lies in `C` and `∑ = 24`), so all
  three nonzero classes have order 2: `A_K` is not cyclic of order 4.
- Gluing along the class of `2e_∞` gives back the Construction-A lattice, which contains `2e_∞`
  of norm 2: it is rootful, as the `A₁²⁴` row lattice is, and only the class of `s` gives a
  rootless lattice.

### Examples

- `s` itself: `a = (−3, 1, …, 1)`, `m = 1`, `b = (−2, 0, …, 0)` with `b mod 2 = 0 ∈ C`,
  `∑ aᵢ = 20 ≡ 4 (mod 8)`, and `∑ aᵢ² = 9 + 23 = 32`, norm 4.
- `𝟙/2` is not in `Λ`: here `m = 1`, `b = 0`, and `∑ aᵢ = 24 ≡ 0`, not `4 (mod 8)`. This is the
  case `m = 1`, Golay word zero, of the four-case bound: the only choice with every `|aᵢ| = 1` is
  excluded, so some `|aᵢ| ≥ 3`.
- `(2, 2, 0, …, 0) ∈ Λ`: `a = (4, 4, 0, …, 0)`, `m = 0`, `b mod 2 = 0`, `∑ aᵢ = 8 ≡ 0`, norm
  `32/8 = 4`. By contrast `(2, 0, …, 0)`, of norm 2, has `∑ aᵢ = 4 ≢ 0 (mod 8)` and is not in `Λ`.

### Dependencies

Tau Ceti: Construction A and Construction B of the binary Golay code, the odd shift and its
class, and the Leech lattice. No earlier layer of this roadmap and no other roadmap.

The integral lattices, their duals, discriminant quadratic modules, gluing along isotropic
subgroups and the ADE discriminant forms are the
[integral-lattices foundation](../../Completed/IntegralLattices/README.md); the linear and additive
codes, the tetracode and hexacode, the binary and ternary Golay codes with their weights and
duality, Construction A and the code-to-discriminant isometries are the
[algebraic-coding foundation](../../Completed/AlgebraicCodingTheory/README.md). This roadmap
introduces no second lattice, quotient, finite quadratic module, gluing construction or Dynkin
type. The Cartan–Killing classification of irreducible root systems is in
[RootSystems](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md); it does not imply a classification
of rank-24 lattices.

Not in scope, and not milestones of this roadmap: that an arbitrary even unimodular
positive-definite lattice of rank 24 is isometric to one of the 24; Venkov's equi-Coxeter
theorem; uniqueness of a lattice with a given root system, or of a rootless one; theta series and
harmonic theta series (the rank-24 theta identities are in [ThetaSeries](../ThetaSeries/README.md),
Layer 8); the identification of stabilizers or automorphism groups with Mathieu, Conway or umbral
groups (the Conway groups are in [CFSGBasicProperties](../CFSGBasicProperties/README.md)); the
separation of the 24 lattices by their root systems
([IntegralLattices](../IntegralLattices/README.md), 6E); the mass-formula completeness argument of
Conway–Sloane 1982; and any categorical construction, which belongs to a separate research
programme. The `A₁²⁴` Golay overlattice is rootful and is not the Leech lattice.
