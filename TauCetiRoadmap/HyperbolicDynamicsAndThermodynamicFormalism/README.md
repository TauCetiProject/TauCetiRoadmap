# Roadmap: hyperbolic dynamics and thermodynamic formalism

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the uniformly hyperbolic theory of diffeomorphisms and flows on manifolds and joins it to the thermodynamic formalism of ClassicalErgodicTheory in the Bowen–Ruelle theorem. From ClassicalErgodicTheory it takes pressure, the variational principles, equilibrium states, Gibbs measures, expansive homeomorphisms and flows and the Bowen property; on top of these it proves Bowen's uniqueness criterion under weak specification with bounded gluing times (expansivity, weak specification and the Bowen property give a unique, ergodic, fully supported Gibbs equilibrium state) for homeomorphisms and flows, which reaches transitive systems that are not mixing, together with upper semicontinuity of entropy for expansive flows and the behaviour of equilibrium states under time changes. On the hyperbolic side it defines hyperbolic sets and Anosov systems through a continuous Riemannian metric, proves metric independence, the characterisation, uniqueness and continuity of the splitting and adapted metrics, the Hadamard–Perron theorem and local stable and unstable manifolds, and shows that locally maximal hyperbolic sets carry a hyperbolic local product structure, from which expansivity, closing, spectral decomposition and specification follow abstractly. The end theorems are Bowen's theorem (a Hölder potential on a basic set of a C¹ diffeomorphism, in particular on a transitive Anosov diffeomorphism, has exactly one equilibrium state), the Bowen–Ruelle theorem for basic sets of C¹ flows with its invariance under constants and flow coboundaries, uniqueness of the Bowen–Margulis measure, and the Bowen–Ruelle characterisation of the SRB measure of a hyperbolic attractor of a flow generated by a C² vector field as the equilibrium state of the geometric potential, Lebesgue-almost every point of the attractor's basin being generic for it; for a transitive Anosov flow generated by a C² vector field the basin is the whole manifold, so the generic points have full volume. Hyperbolic toral automorphisms, suspensions of Anosov diffeomorphisms and geodesic flows in negative curvature are the examples.

**Route.** The summit is reached through Bowen's criterion rather than Markov partitions. Climenhaga and Thompson prove the criterion for homeomorphisms and for flows with a specification property whose gluing times are only bounded (ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem A and Theorem 5.5). With bounded gluing times, transitivity in forward time (Conventions) suffices: a nonempty system transitive in forward time with hyperbolic local product structure has this weak specification at every scale, so neither a mixing hypothesis nor the reduction of non-mixing flows to constant-roof suspensions is needed. The smooth theory enters only through Bowen's abstract axioms for a homeomorphism with hyperbolic local product structure (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, lines 66–83) and their flow analogue (BowenRuelle1975, lines 96–108), which locally maximal hyperbolic sets satisfy. This keeps each layer general: the uniqueness theorems hold for every compact metric system with the abstract properties, and the examples are not special-cased.

## Conventions

- Logarithms are natural. Topological entropy is Mathlib's `Dynamics.coverEntropy` T Set.univ, valued in EReal, with value ⊥ on the empty space; h_top(g) denotes `Dynamics.coverEntropy` (g 1) Set.univ for a flow g. Pressure is ClassicalErgodicTheory's: P_T(φ) is `topologicalPressure` T φ (§5.4) and P_g(F) is `flowTopologicalPressure` g F (§5.5), the pressure of the time-one map for the potential F_1 below, both in EReal. Measure entropy hμ(T) is GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.2's `measureEntropy`, valued in [0, ∞] and cast to EReal when compared with pressure; the measure entropy of μ for a flow g is hμ(g^1). Free energies hμ(T) + ∫φ dμ are computed in EReal.
- Pressure has the potential added: P_T(φ) is the exponential growth rate of Σ exp(S_nφ) over separated sets, so P_T(0) = h_top(T) (ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.4). The inverse-temperature form of statistical mechanics is P_T(−βH) for an energy H and inverse temperature β; equilibrium states of −βH are Gibbs states at temperature 1/β. Witness: on the full shift on k symbols, P(c) = log k + c for a constant c, so the inverse-temperature form of the constant energy H = 1 gives P(−β) = log k − β.
- Birkhoff sums are Mathlib's `birkhoffSum T φ n`, the sum of φ∘T^k over 0 ≤ k < n; S_0φ = 0. For a flow g and potential F, the orbit integral over [0, t] is ∫₀ᵗ F(g^s x) ds, and F_t denotes the function x ↦ ∫₀ᵗ F(g^s x) ds, so F_1 is the time-one average of BowenRuelle1975, lines 191–192.
- Dynamical distances use n terms for maps and closed time intervals for flows: d_n(x, y) = max over 0 ≤ k < n of d(T^k x, T^k y) for n ≥ 1 and d_0 = 0, matching Mathlib's `Dynamics.dynEntourage`, and d_t(x, y) = sup over 0 ≤ s ≤ t of d(g^s x, g^s y) for real t ≥ 0, so that the flow distance d_0 is d itself. Bowen balls B_n(x, ε) and B_t(x, ε) are ClassicalErgodicTheory's (Conventions), always with radius ε > 0, and they are the open balls of these distances (§1.1): the map ball B_0(x, ε) is the whole space, while the flow ball B_0(x, ε) is the ordinary ε-ball. Witness: for the doubling map of ℝ/ℤ, B_2(0, 1/8) is the arc of half-width 1/16.
- Separated sets: a finite set is (n, U)-separated when it is a dynamical net in the sense of Mathlib's `Dynamics.IsDynNetIn` for the entourage U, as in ClassicalErgodicTheory's Conventions, that is, the balls of the n-step dynamical entourage about its points are pairwise disjoint; on a metric space U is {d < ε}. For a flow, a finite set E is (T, ε)-separated when the open d_T-balls of radius ε about its points are pairwise disjoint. Disjoint balls of radius ε force d_T(x, y) ≥ ε, and the classical convention d_T(x, y) > ε at scale 2ε forces disjoint balls at scale ε, so the two conventions differ by at most a factor two in the scale, which the limit ε → 0 does not see; a Check at a fixed scale is read with the disjoint-ball convention. A maximal family with pairwise disjoint open ε-balls is 2ε-spanning, not ε-spanning (the ε-ball of any further point meets one of them), so a covering argument built on it uses the radius 2ε; witness in §11.5.
- Invariant measures: M(T) is ClassicalErgodicTheory's `invariantProbabilities` T (§5.1), the Borel probability measures μ with `MeasurePreserving T μ μ`, carrying the weak topology of `MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure`; for a flow g, M(g) is its `flowInvariantProbabilities` g, the probability measures preserved by every g^t. Ergodicity of a map is Mathlib's `Ergodic`; ergodicity of a flow is `ErgodicVAdd ℝ X μ` for the additive action `Flow.toAddAction`. Equilibrium states are ClassicalErgodicTheory's `IsEquilibriumState` and `IsFlowEquilibriumState` (§8.1), invariant probabilities whose free energy equals the pressure in EReal; ES_T(φ) and ES_g(F) denote their sets, and a measure of maximal entropy is an equilibrium state of the zero potential.
- The full shift on k symbols carries the metric d(x, y) = 2^(−N(x,y)), N(x, y) the least |i| with x_i ≠ y_i, and d(x, x) = 0; it has diameter 1, and for the left shift the Bowen ball B_n(x, 1/2), n ≥ 1, is the cylinder fixing x₋₁, …, x_n.
- Transitivity is taken in forward time: a continuous map T, invertible or not, is transitive when for all nonempty open U, V there is n ≥ 0 with T^n(U) ∩ V nonempty, and a continuous flow g is transitive when there is a real t ≥ 0 with g^t(U) ∩ V nonempty (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, Proposition 4.2, lines 387–392, uses the forward notion for continuous maps). In Mathlib's terms this is `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive` ℕ X for the action `Flow.toAddAction` of the ℕ-flow `Flow.fromIter` of T, also when T is a homeomorphism, and `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive` for the nonnegative-time flow `Flow.restrictNonneg` g, whose acting monoid is `AddSubmonoid.nonneg` ℝ. The same predicate for the ℤ-flow `Homeomorph.flow` of a homeomorphism or for the ℝ-flow g itself is transitivity in signed time, the notion of ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.5 for flows; it is weaker and is never a hypothesis here. Witness: the monotone shift, the left shift (Tx)_i = x_(i+1) on X = {x ∈ {0,1}^ℤ : x_i ≤ x_(i+1) for all i}, has a dense ℤ-orbit, formed by the sequences with exactly one switch from 0 to 1, so it is transitive in signed time; but for U = {x_0 = 1} and V = {x_0 = 0} every T^n(U) with n ≥ 0 lies in U, so it is not transitive. It satisfies (A2) and (A3) of §6.1, its only periodic points are 0^ℤ and 1^ℤ, and both point masses are measures of maximal entropy (§§6.2, 6.8, 10.1). When periodic points (periodic orbits) are dense the two notions agree, by the argument of §10.1. Topological mixing of maps is ClassicalErgodicTheory's (§5.2); that of flows is defined in §2.1.
- Manifolds are finite-dimensional real smooth manifolds without boundary, Hausdorff and σ-compact, modelled with corners I on a finite-dimensional normed space E; compactness is assumed where stated. Diffeomorphisms of class C^r (r ≥ 1) are Mathlib's `Diffeomorph I I M M r`. A C^r flow is a `Flow ℝ M` whose map (t, x) ↦ g^t x is C^r on ℝ × M; its generator is X(x) = d/dt g^t x at t = 0.
- In Layers 7–10, norms on tangent spaces come from a continuous Riemannian metric, Mathlib's `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric` on the tangent bundle, registered through `Bundle.RiemannianBundle` with the predicate `IsContinuousRiemannianBundle`. Because hyperbolicity does not depend on this choice (§7.3), any continuous metric may be used, and smooth metrics (`Bundle.ContMDiffRiemannianMetric`) are the special case. In Layer 11, which differentiates unstable Jacobians in time, norms, distances and volume come from a smooth Riemannian metric, as in BowenRuelle1975, line 92: for a merely continuous metric t ↦ log Jac(Dg^t|E^u_x) need not be differentiable. Distances on M are the truncated distance d(x, y) = min(1, ρ(x, y)), where ρ is the Riemannian extended distance (`Manifold.riemannianEDist`) of a fixed smooth Riemannian metric, which exists on every manifold of the previous item (`TauCeti.nonempty_contMDiffRiemannianMetric`). ρ is the infimum of the lengths of C¹ paths, so it is ⊤ between points of different components and is not a real-valued metric on a disconnected M; d is a real-valued metric inducing the topology of M and equal to ρ wherever ρ < 1, so every statement at scales below 1 reads the same for both. On a compact set any two such distances are bi-Lipschitz equivalent (a compact set meets finitely many components; within one of them the two Riemannian distances are comparable, and across two both truncated distances are 1), which changes contraction constants but not rates. The truncated Riemannian distance of a continuous Riemannian metric is likewise comparable on each compact chart region with the Euclidean distance of the chart, so it too is bi-Lipschitz equivalent on compact sets to that of a smooth metric; the statements of Layers 8 and 10, which see the distance only through local product structure, contraction rates and Hölder continuity, may therefore be read with the truncated distance of the continuous metric that gives the norms, and their formal statements are made so. Witness: for the period-two saddle orbit {c, d} of the three-copy example of §8.4, c and d lie in different copies of ℝ², ρ(c, d) = ⊤, and the truncated distance between c and d is 1.
- Rates: a hyperbolic set of a diffeomorphism has constants C > 0 and 0 < λ < 1 with |Df^n v| ≤ Cλ^n|v| on E^s; a hyperbolic set of a flow has C > 0 and κ > 0 with |Dg^t v| ≤ Ce^(−κt)|v| on E^s for t ≥ 0. Stable means contracted in forward time; unstable means contracted in backward time. Witness: for the linear map diag(2, 1/2) of ℝ² at the fixed point 0, E^s is the second coordinate axis with λ = 1/2 and C = 1.
- The geometric potential of a C² flow generated by a C² vector field, for a smooth Riemannian metric (Layer 11), is φ^u(x) = −d/dt log Jac(Dg^t|E^u_x) at t = 0, with the sign making ∫₀ᵀ φ^u(g^t x) dt = −log Jac(Dg^T|E^u_x). Witness: for the geodesic flow of a surface of curvature −1 with the Sasaki metric, Jac(Dg^t|E^u) = e^t and φ^u = −1.
- Weak specification has bounded, not fixed, gluing times, as in ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 295–303 (flows) and lines 1661–1669 (maps); specification with gluing times all equal to a fixed τ is called strong specification. ClassicalErgodicTheory's specification (`HasSpecification`, `HasFlowSpecification`, §§8.4–8.5) asks for periodic shadowing points and, on a nonempty space, implies strong and hence weak specification (§2.2); it is not assumed here.

## How to read the build

Layers 1–5 are topological and measure-theoretic and use no manifolds; they add to ClassicalErgodicTheory's pressure, variational principles and equilibrium states what the uniqueness theory of transitive, possibly non-mixing systems needs. Layer 1 fixes Bowen balls and the expansivity notions, Layer 2 mixing of flows and weak specification, Layer 3 the measure entropy of expansive flows, Layer 4 time changes of flows, and Layer 5 proves Bowen's uniqueness criterion under weak specification. Layer 6 abstracts uniform hyperbolicity as a local product structure with exponential contraction and derives the hypotheses of Layer 5 from it. Layers 7 and 8 are smooth: hyperbolic sets, their linear theory, invariant manifolds, and the theorem that locally maximal hyperbolic sets satisfy Layer 6's axioms. Layer 9 gives the examples, Layer 10 the Bowen and Bowen–Ruelle theorems, and Layer 11 the SRB theory of C² flows. Layers 1–5 can be built in parallel with Layer 7.

## Layer 1: Bowen balls and expansivity

This layer fixes the dynamical distances, identifies ClassicalErgodicTheory's Bowen balls as their open balls, and adds what the uniqueness theory uses beyond ClassicalErgodicTheory's expansive homeomorphisms and Bowen–Walters expansive flows: the two-sided sets Γ_ε, entropy-expansiveness with its finiteness theorem, and the orbit-segment form of expansivity for flows.

### 1.1 Dynamical distances and Bowen balls

Define, for a map T of a pseudometric space (X, d), the dynamical distance d_n(x, y) as the maximum of d(T^k x, T^k y) over 0 ≤ k < n (zero for n = 0), and for a flow g on X and real t ≥ 0 the dynamical distance d_t(x, y) as the supremum of d(g^s x, g^s y) over 0 ≤ s ≤ t, so that d_0 = d. The Bowen balls are ClassicalErgodicTheory's (Conventions): B_n(x, ε) is the set of y with d(T^k x, T^k y) < ε for every k < n, and B_t(x, ε) the set of y with d(g^s x, g^s y) < ε for every s ∈ [0, t]. Prove that for ε > 0 they are the open balls of radius ε of d_n and d_t: for maps the maximum is over finitely many times, and for flows s ↦ d(g^s x, g^s y) is continuous on the compact interval [0, t] (a `Flow` is jointly continuous), so its supremum is attained and is below ε exactly when every value is. The closed balls B̄_n(x, ε) and B̄_t(x, ε) are the closed balls of d_n and d_t (BowenRuelle1975, lines 163–164, separate maps with times k ∈ [0, n], and lines 99–101 use closed flow balls with s ∈ [0, T]; ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 170–175, defines d_t with s ∈ [0, t] and both the open and the closed d_t-balls, and its separated sets are separated in the closed sense, d_t(x, y) > δ, line 945; the conventions differ by a shift of n by one and by boundary points, which no limit sees).

Its API:

- For ε > 0 the Bowen ball B_n(x, ε) is the ball at x of Mathlib's `Dynamics.dynEntourage` T {(a, b) | d(a, b) < ε} n, so every metric statement transfers to Mathlib's entourage statements and back.
- For ε > 0, B_0(x, ε) is the whole space and B_1(x, ε) is the ball of radius ε; B_(n+1)(x, ε) ⊆ B_n(x, ε), the flow balls B_t(x, ε) decrease as t increases, and all balls increase with ε.
- T maps B_(n+1)(x, ε) into B_n(Tx, ε); for a semiconjugacy h with h∘S = T∘h and h Lipschitz with a constant L > 0, h maps B^S_n(x, ε) into B^T_n(hx, Lε). Every Lipschitz map has a positive Lipschitz constant, and L = 0 is excluded because the ball of radius 0 is empty.
- For a continuous flow on a compact metric space, t > 0 and n ≥ 1: the flow ball B_(nt)(x, ε) lies in the time-t ball B^(g^t)_n(x, ε), and conversely B^(g^t)_n(x, δ) lies in B_(nt)(x, ε) whenever δ > 0 is such that d(y, z) < δ implies d(g^s y, g^s z) < ε for every s ∈ [0, t] (such δ exists by uniform continuity of (s, y) ↦ g^s y on the compact [0, t] × X); every s ∈ [0, nt] is kt + r with 0 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 and r ∈ [0, t].
- For a homeomorphism and ε > 0 the two-sided set Γ_ε(x) = {y : d(T^k x, T^k y) ≤ ε for every k ∈ ℤ} is the intersection over n ≥ 0 of T^n(B̄_(2n+1)(T⁻ⁿx, ε)), where B̄ denotes the closed d_(2n+1)-ball: y lies in the n-th set iff T⁻ⁿy is ε-close to T⁻ⁿx for d_(2n+1), that is, iff the orbits are ε-close at the times −n, …, n. It is closed, and compact when X is compact (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 1687–1688, for the definition on a compact metric space). For a flow, Γ_ε(x) = {y : d(g^t x, g^t y) ≤ ε for every real t} (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 355–358, where compactness is noted for compact X), closed and compact in the same way.

**Checks.**

- For a rotation of ℝ/ℤ (an isometry), B_n(x, ε) equals the ε-ball for every n ≥ 1; a definition that summed the distances along the orbit instead of maximising would shrink with n.
- For the doubling map x ↦ 2x of ℝ/ℤ and ε = 1/8, B_n(0, 1/8) is the arc of half-width 2⁻ⁿ⁻²: half-width 1/8 at n = 1 and 1/16 at n = 2. The convention with k ≤ n would give 1/16 already at n = 1.
- At n = 0 the ball is the whole space, matching `Dynamics.dynEntourage` at time zero, so Mathlib's covering numbers at time zero are 1 on a nonempty set; a convention using k ≤ n would give the ε-ball instead.
- On the full two-shift with the metric of the Conventions and the left shift, let y have its only 1 at coordinate −1. Then d(T^k 0, T^k y) = 2^(−k−1) ≤ 1/2 for every k ≥ 0, while d(T⁻¹0, T⁻¹y) = 1, so y ∉ Γ_(1/2)(0). The set T^1(B̄_3(T⁻¹0, 1/2)) tests the times −1, 0, 1 and excludes y, whereas T⁻ⁿ(B̄_(2n+1)(T^n 0, 1/2)), translated the other way, tests the times n, …, 3n and contains y for every n.
- For the identity of ℚ, Γ_1(0) = [−1, 1] ∩ ℚ is closed but not compact (rationals tending to √2/2 have no limit in it), so the compactness clause needs X compact. The identity of ℝ is no counterexample: Γ_1(0) = [−1, 1] is compact.
- For a constant map h of the two-point space {a, b} to a point (L = 0 is a Lipschitz constant), with n = 1 and ε = 1, the image {pt} of B_1(a, 1) does not lie in the empty ball B_1(pt, 0) but lies in B_1(pt, 1), the containment with the positive constant L = 1.
- For the unit-speed rotation flow of ℝ/ℤ, t = 1 and ε = 1/4, the time-one map is the identity: B^(g^1)_0(x, δ) is the whole circle while the flow ball B_0(x, 1/4) is the arc of half-width 1/4, so the converse containment fails at n = 0; for n ≥ 1 and δ ≤ 1/4 both balls are arcs of half-width δ and 1/4 and it holds.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory Conventions (Bowen balls); Mathlib `Dynamics.dynEntourage`, `Dynamics.mem_ball_dynEntourage`, `Flow`.

### 1.2 Entropy-expansive maps

ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.3 supplies expansive homeomorphisms: T is expansive with constant c > 0 when any two distinct points x ≠ y have some n ∈ ℤ with d(T^n x, T^n y) > c, equivalently Γ_c(x) = {x} for every x (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 1687–1690); their topological entropy is finite and computed at every scale below c. Define a uniformly continuous map T of a compact metric space to be entropy-expansive at scale ε > 0 when `Dynamics.coverEntropy` T Φ_ε(x) = 0 for every x, where Φ_ε(x) is the set of points whose forward orbit stays within ε of that of x (BowenEntropyExpansive1972, lines 55–56); for a homeomorphism of a compact space the two-sided set Γ_ε(x) of §1.1 may be used instead (BowenEntropyExpansive1972, Corollary 2.3, line 188). Prove that an entropy-expansive map at scale ε of a compact metric space has finite entropy, equal to its spanning-set entropy at the entourage {d ≤ ε}: `Dynamics.coverEntropy` T Set.univ = `Dynamics.coverEntropyEntourage` T Set.univ {d ≤ ε} < ∞ (BowenEntropyExpansive1972, Theorem 2.4, lines 192–193, for the equality; the entropy at the fixed entourage {d ≤ ε} of the compact space is finite by Mathlib's `Dynamics.coverEntropyEntourage_finite_of_isCompact_invariant`).

Its API:

- An expansive homeomorphism with constant c is entropy-expansive at every scale ε < c, since Γ_ε(x) = {x}.
- An expansive homeomorphism of a compact metric space has finitely many points of each period: two distinct points of Fix(T^n) are separated beyond c at some integer time, which periodicity moves into [0, n), so Fix(T^n) is (n, c)-separated, and separated sets of a compact space are finite.
- Entropy-expansiveness at scale ε passes to every smaller scale, and to T^k, k ≥ 1, at a smaller scale.

**Checks.**

- The identity of [0, 1] is entropy-expansive at every scale although it is not expansive (Φ_ε(x) is a closed interval, on which the identity has entropy 0), so finiteness of entropy is the conclusion for the wider class, while finiteness of the sets of periodic points needs expansivity itself: every point of [0, 1] is fixed.
- The full two-shift with the metric of the Conventions is expansive with constant 1/2 (distinct points differ at some coordinate n, and then d(T^n x, T^n y) = 1), hence entropy-expansive at ε = 1/4. Two points are {d ≤ 1/4}-close for T^0, …, Tⁿ⁻¹ iff they agree on the coordinates −1, …, n, so the minimal covers have 2ⁿ⁺² elements and the entropy at that entourage is log 2, the topological entropy.
- Let X be the one-point compactification of the disjoint union over m ≥ 1 of the full shifts on 2^m symbols, the m-th piece scaled to diameter 2^(−m) and placed at distance 2^(−m) from the added point ∞, with T the shift on each piece and fixing ∞. For every ε > 0, Φ_ε(∞) contains every piece lying in the ε-ball about ∞, and those pieces have entropies m log 2 for all large m, so T is entropy-expansive at no scale; its entropy is ∞. The hypothesis cannot be dropped from the finiteness theorem.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.3 (expansive homeomorphisms); Mathlib `Dynamics.coverEntropy`, `Dynamics.coverEntropyEntourage`, `Dynamics.coverEntropyEntourage_finite_of_isCompact_invariant`; Layer 1.1.

### 1.3 Bowen–Walters expansive flows

ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5 supplies Bowen–Walters expansive flows (`IsExpansiveFlow`): a continuous flow g on a metric space is expansive when for every η > 0 there is ε > 0 such that whenever d(g^t x, g^(s(t)) y) < ε for all real t, for some continuous s : ℝ → ℝ with s(0) = 0, then y = g^u x for some |u| < η (BowenWalters1972; ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 359–362); such flows on compact metric spaces have finite entropy. Prove, for a Bowen–Walters expansive flow g on a compact metric space:

- for every η > 0 there is ε > 0 with Γ_ε(x) ⊆ g^([−η, η])x for every x, taking s the identity (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 356–366);
- the fixed points of g are isolated (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 380–381, citing Bowen and Walters);
- for ε as in the first item, every time-t map, t ≠ 0, is entropy-expansive at scale ε for the metric d_(|t|) of §1.1, and every invariant measure is almost expansive in the sense of ClimenhagaThompson2016, Definition 2.7 (lines 371–375): Γ_ε(x) = Γ_ε(x; g^(|t|), d_(|t|)), the two-sided set of g^t and of its inverse, lies in an orbit segment, on which g^t has entropy 0 (BowenEntropyExpansive1972, Example 1.6, lines 117–133; ClimenhagaThompson2016, Proposition 3.3, lines 513–530); with §1.2 this gives the finiteness of h_top(g) recorded by ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5;
- expansivity is preserved by time reversal (`Flow.reverse`) and by rescaling g(at), a > 0.

**Checks.**

- The flow of a periodic orbit on a circle is expansive: points ε-close for all time lie on the same orbit within time ε/(speed).
- The linear flow of irrational slope on ℝ²/ℤ² is not expansive: two points on a short transverse segment stay at the same distance for all time without lying on a common short orbit segment, and Γ_ε(x) contains that segment.
- The identity flow of the circle is not expansive (every point is a non-isolated fixed point); the identity flow of a finite set is, its fixed points being isolated.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5 (`IsExpansiveFlow`, finite entropy); Mathlib `Flow`, `Flow.reverse`, `Dynamics.coverEntropy`; Layers 1.1, 1.2.

### Examples

The full shift on k symbols is expansive and entropy-expansive with entropy log k; the identity of [0, 1] is entropy-expansive and not expansive; the constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift and the flow on a periodic orbit are expansive flows, and the irrational linear flow on the torus is not.

### Dependencies

ClassicalErgodicTheory Conventions (Bowen balls), §§5.3, 8.5; Mathlib's topological entropy and dynamical entourages (`Mathlib/Dynamics/TopologicalEntropy/`) and `Flow`.

## Layer 2: Mixing and weak specification

### 2.1 Topological mixing of flows

ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.2 defines topological mixing of a map (`IsTopologicallyMixing`). Define a continuous flow g to be topologically mixing when for all nonempty open U, V there is t₀ with g^t(U) ∩ V nonempty for every t ≥ t₀ (the continuous-time form of the notion for homeomorphisms of BowenRuelle1975, line 165, footnote 2, and for diffeomorphisms of BCS2022, lines 43–44; it is the hypothesis on Anosov flows in TsujiiZhang2023, Theorem 1.1, line 125, and Theorem 1.2, line 156).

Its API:

- Mixing implies transitivity in forward time (Conventions): `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive` for the ℕ-flow `Flow.fromIter` of a mixing map or homeomorphism and for the nonnegative-time flow `Flow.restrictNonneg` of a mixing flow, and hence also the weaker signed-time predicate for the ℤ- or ℝ-flow.
- Mixing of maps and of flows is invariant under topological conjugacy (for flows, the conjugate flow `Homeomorph.conjFlow`) and passes to factors; a homeomorphism T is mixing iff T^k is mixing for one, equivalently every, k ≥ 1 (T^j of a nonempty open set is open and nonempty).
- A flow is mixing iff one, equivalently every, time-t map with t > 0 is mixing: the forward implication restricts to the times nt, and the converse covers [0, t] by finitely many short intervals of offsets.

**Checks.**

- The flow on a periodic orbit of period p is transitive and not mixing: for a short arc U, g^t(U) ∩ U is empty for t near p/2 + pℤ; its time-p map is the identity, not mixing, as the third item requires.
- The linear flow of irrational slope on ℝ²/ℤ² is minimal, hence transitive, but not mixing: g^t(U) is a translate of U, and for small balls U, V the translates meet V only for t in a union of bounded intervals recurring with gaps.
- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of any homeomorphism of a nonempty compact metric space is not mixing: a thin flow box U at height 1/4 meets a flow box V at height 3/4 only at times in 1/2 + ℤ up to the box widths, so not at the large integer times. On the empty space the suspension is empty and mixing holds vacuously, since there are no nonempty open sets.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.2 (`IsTopologicallyMixing`); Mathlib `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive`, `Flow.fromIter`, `Flow.toAddAction`, `Flow.restrictNonneg`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.conjFlow`.

### 2.2 Weak specification

Define weak specification of a homeomorphism T of a compact metric space (`HasWeakSpecification`): for every δ > 0 there is τ ∈ ℕ such that for every finite list of orbit segments (x_1, n_1), …, (x_k, n_k) there are a point y and gluing times τ_1, …, τ_(k−1) ≤ τ with d_(n_j)(T^(N_(j−1)+τ_(j−1)) y, x_j) < δ for 1 ≤ j ≤ k, where N_j is the sum of n_i over i ≤ j plus the sum of τ_i over i < j and N_0 = τ_0 = 0 (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 1661–1669). Define it for a continuous flow with real segment lengths and real gluing times in [0, τ] in the same way (`HasFlowWeakSpecification`; ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 295–303). Strong specification asks that all gluing times equal τ. The list may be empty (k = 0), and then both definitions ask only for a point y: the empty space has neither weak nor strong specification, and a nonempty space meets the case k = 0 with any point. A segment of length 0 imposes nothing for a map (d_0 = 0) and closeness at the single time N_(j−1) + τ_(j−1) for a flow (the flow distance d_0 is d).

Its API:

- Strong specification implies weak specification. On a nonempty space, ClassicalErgodicTheory's specification with periodic shadowing (`HasSpecification`, §8.4; `HasFlowSpecification`, §8.5) implies strong specification, with τ the constant M of that property at a scale ε < δ, and hence weak specification. The empty list is met by any point. For k ≥ 1 put s_j = n_1 + ⋯ + n_(j−1) + (j − 1)M and apply the property to the points T^(−s_j) x_j (g^(−s_j) x_j for a flow) on the closed time intervals [s_j, s_j + n_j], which for a map is one step longer than the j-th segment, so that a segment of length 0 still gives an interval with s_j ≤ s_j + n_j; consecutive intervals are exactly M apart, the period p = s_k + n_k + M is admissible, and the shadowing point y meets the inequalities of the definition with every gluing time equal to M. The periodicity of y, and for a flow the time error of its period, are not used.
- Weak specification of a nonempty system implies transitivity in forward time (Conventions): given nonempty open U, V, glue a point of U to a later point of V. In particular the monotone shift of the Conventions, transitive only in signed time, has no weak specification.
- Weak specification is invariant under topological conjugacy of compact systems (with δ transported by uniform continuity; for flows, the conjugate flow `Homeomorph.conjFlow`).
- For a flow, weak specification at scale δ for the segments of length at least T₀ implies it for all segments at the same scale, with τ + T₀ in place of τ: a segment (x, t) with t < T₀ is the initial part of (x, T₀), a point shadowing the longer segment shadows the shorter one, and the unused time T₀ − t joins the following gluing time. Example: for T₀ = 1 and τ = 3, the segments (x₁, 0.2), (x₂, 5) are handled by shadowing (x₁, 1), (x₂, 5) with a gluing time g ≤ 3, which leaves the gluing time 0.8 + g ≤ 4 after the first given segment. The statement used by the uniqueness proof is a different one, which passes tail specification at all scales from a collection of orbit segments to its enlargements (ClimenhagaThompson2016, Lemma 2.10, lines 448–463).

**Checks.**

- The swap of two points a, b has weak specification with τ = 1 for δ < d(a, b): choose each gluing time 0 or 1 to match the phase. It has no strong specification: the segments (a, 1), (a, 1) need 1 + τ even and the segments (a, 1), (b, 1) need it odd. It has no specification with periodic shadowing either: it has no fixed point, so no point y with T^p y = y for odd p.
- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift has weak specification with gluing times in [0, τ₀ + 1] (§6.6), but not ClassicalErgodicTheory's `HasFlowSpecification`: its periods are integers, and for p = n + 1/2 and ε < 1/2 no |τ| ≤ ε makes p + τ a period.
- The full shift has strong specification: at scale 2^(−m), gluing time τ = 2m + 1 leaves room to concatenate the required central words.
- A disjoint union of two full shifts has no weak specification, since it is not transitive. An irrational rotation does have weak specification (choose the gluing time so the orbit lands near the next starting point, using minimality), which shows that expansivity in Bowen's criterion is an independent hypothesis.
- Nonemptiness is needed for the comparison with ClassicalErgodicTheory's specification: on the empty space its specification and flow specification (§§8.4–8.5) hold vacuously, since their conclusion is reached only through a segment index i₀ and a point x_(i₀), and there is no point; weak specification fails there, the empty list of segments having no shadowing point y.
- The comparison shadows each segment of a map at one more time than the segment has: with intervals [a_j, a_j + n_j − 1] placed M apart the gluing times would be M − 1, which is −1 for the one-point space, where M = 0 is admissible, and a segment of length 0 would give the interval [a_j, a_j − 1], excluded by the supplier's requirement that each interval have its left end at most its right end.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §§8.4–8.5 (`HasSpecification`, `HasFlowSpecification`, for the comparison); Mathlib `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive`, `Flow.fromIter`, `Flow.restrictNonneg`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.conjFlow`; Layer 1.1.

### Examples

The full shift is mixing with strong specification; the swap of two points and the constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift have weak specification and are not mixing; the irrational rotation is minimal with weak specification but neither expansive nor mixing.

### Dependencies

Layer 1; ClassicalErgodicTheory §§5.2, 8.4–8.5; Mathlib `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive`, `Flow.fromIter`, `Flow.restrictNonneg`.

## Layer 3: Measure entropy of expansive flows

### 3.1 Entropy of expansive flows

Prove, for a Bowen–Walters expansive continuous flow g on a compact metric space: there is ε > 0 such that hμ(g^1) = hμ(g^1, ξ) for every μ ∈ M(g^1) and every finite Borel partition ξ of diameter less than ε for d_1; and μ ↦ hμ(g^1) is upper semicontinuous on M(g^1), hence on its closed subset M(g) (`upperSemicontinuousOn_measureEntropy_of_isExpansiveFlow`; ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem 3.2 and Propositions 3.3–3.4, lines 504–535; BowenEntropyExpansive1972, Example 1.6, lines 117–133). By §1.3 every point x has Γ_ε(x) = Γ_ε(x; g^1, d_1) inside an orbit segment, so h(Γ_ε(x; g^1, d_1), g^1) = 0 and ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem 3.2 (lines 504–509), applied to the homeomorphism g^1 of (X, d_1), gives the equality for every g^1-ergodic measure. For general μ ∈ M(g^1) use ArithmeticDynamics §2.7's ergodic decomposition x ↦ μ_x of g^1 on the standard Borel space X: hμ(g^1) = ∫⁻ hμ_x(g^1) dμ(x), and the identification g_ξ(x) = hμ_x(g^1, ξ) of the Shannon–McMillan–Breiman limit with ∫ g_ξ dμ = hμ(g^1, ξ) gives hμ(g^1, ξ) = ∫ hμ_x(g^1, ξ) dμ(x); the two integrands agree almost everywhere. Upper semicontinuity follows by the route of ClassicalErgodicTheory §6.2 for expansive homeomorphisms (Walters1982, Theorem 8.2): for ν ∈ M(g^1) choose ξ of d_1-diameter below ε with ν-null boundaries (ArithmeticDynamics §2.7); the atoms of each join ξ^n have boundaries in the union of the g^(−k)(∂ξ), k < n, which are ν-null, so ν' ↦ H_ν'(ξ^n) is continuous at ν (`MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_measure_of_null_frontier_of_tendsto`), and on M(g^1) the function ν' ↦ hν'(g^1) = hν'(g^1, ξ) = inf_n H_ν'(ξ^n)/n is an infimum of functions continuous at ν.

**Checks.**

- For the flow on a periodic orbit of period 1, g^1 is the identity of the orbit: every g^1-invariant measure has entropy 0 and every partition rate is 0, so the equality holds for every partition; M(g) is the single time measure.
- For the constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift and μ the product of the Bernoulli measure (1/2, 1/2) with Lebesgue measure on the fibre, hμ(g^1) = log 2, computed by a partition of d_1-diameter below ε into products of cylinders with height intervals. The trivial partition {X} has rate 0 ≠ log 2: the diameter hypothesis is needed.
- For the linear flow of irrational slope on ℝ²/ℤ² (not expansive) every entropy is 0, so this example says nothing about necessity.

*Needs:* ArithmeticDynamics §2.7 (null-boundary partitions, the Shannon–McMillan–Breiman limit and its identification on ergodic components, ergodic decomposition with entropy integration); GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.2 (`measureEntropy`, `partitionEntropyRate`); ClassicalErgodicTheory §§5.1, 3.4 (M(g^1), M(g) closed), 6.2 (the route); Mathlib `MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_measure_of_null_frontier_of_tendsto`, `UpperSemicontinuous`; Layer 1.3.

### Examples

The constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift has hμ(g^1) = log 2 for the product measure, computed by a small partition; periodic orbits have zero entropy.

### Dependencies

Layer 1; ArithmeticDynamics §2.7; GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic §4.2; ClassicalErgodicTheory §§3.4, 5.1, 6.2; Mathlib's portmanteau theorem.

## Layer 4: Time-t maps and rescaling of flows

### 4.1 Time-t maps, rescaling and flow coboundaries

Prove, for a continuous flow g on a compact metric space with h_top(g) < ∞ and a continuous F (BowenRuelle1975, lines 190–199, for the time-one map: P_g(F) = P_(g^1)(F_1), ∫F dμ = ∫F_1 dμ for μ ∈ M(g), and P_g(F) as the supremum of the free energy over M(g); Remark 4.5, lines 409–413, for the rescaling of time):

- for t > 0, P_(g^t)(F_t) = t P_g(F), that is `topologicalPressure` (g t) F_t = t · `flowTopologicalPressure` g F; equivalently, for the rescaled flow g_a(s) = g(as) with a > 0, P_(g_a)(aF) = a P_g(F);
- for t > 0 and μ ∈ M(g), μ ∈ ES_g(F) iff μ is an equilibrium state of F_t for the map g^t (`IsEquilibriumState` (g t) F_t μ); ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.1 proves the case t = 1;
- for a > 0, ES_(g_a)(aF) = ES_g(F);
- if F' = F + c + L_gψ for a real c and a continuous ψ differentiable along the flow with continuous derivative L_gψ, then P_g(F') = P_g(F) + c and ES_g(F') = ES_g(F) (ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5); when g is a C¹ flow on a manifold, every C¹ function ψ qualifies, with L_gψ = dψ(X). For a merely continuous flow a C¹ function need not be differentiable along the flow, and only the explicit hypothesis applies.

For the first item rescale time in ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.5's separated-set form of the flow pressure: a set is (T, ε)-separated for g_a iff it is (aT, ε)-separated for g, and the orbit integral of aF along g_a over [0, T] is that of F along g over [0, aT], so P_(g_a)(aF) = a P_g(F); by definition P_(g_a)(aF) is the pressure of the time-one map g^a of g_a for the time-one average of aF along g_a, which is F_a. The second combines Abramov's formula (ClassicalErgodicTheory §6.4, hμ(g^t) = t hμ(g^1)), ∫F_t dμ = t∫F dμ for μ ∈ M(g) and the first item: the free energy of μ for (g^t, F_t) is t times its free energy for (g, F), and the supremum over the larger set M(g^t) is t P_g(F) by the variational principle (ClassicalErgodicTheory §7.1). The third follows in the same way, since M(g_a) = M(g) and the free energy of μ for (g_a, aF) is a times that for (g, F). The last is ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5's flow-coboundary statement; for a C¹ flow, t ↦ ψ(g^t x) is C¹ with derivative dψ(X(g^t x)), which is continuous in x.

**Checks.**

- For a constant c, F_1 = c and P_g(c) = h_top(g) + c; for the rescaled flow g'(t) = g(2t), P_g'(2F) = 2P_g(F), while using F instead of 2F gives P_g'(F) = 2P_g(F/2), which differs from 2P_g(F) exactly when P_g(F/2) ≠ P_g(F). For F = c ≠ 0 the two values are 2h_top(g) + c and 2h_top(g) + 2c; on the roof-one suspension of the full two-shift with F = 1 they are 2 log 2 + 1 and 2 log 2 + 2. For a flow coboundary F = L_gψ (fourth item) both equal 2h_top(g), so such an F does not detect the wrong scaling.
- On a periodic orbit of period p, ∫₀^p L_gψ(g^s x) ds = ψ(g^p x) − ψ(x) = 0, so the coboundary has zero integral for the flow-invariant measure.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift, F = 1 and F' = 1 + L_gψ have the same equilibrium state, the measure of maximal entropy, and pressure log 2 + 1.
- For the flow on a periodic orbit of period 1 and t = 1, every point mass of the orbit is an equilibrium state of F_1 for g^1 but is not in M(g): the second item is an equivalence for g-invariant μ only.
- For the unit-speed rotation flow of ℝ/ℤ and ψ(s) = sin(2πs), L_gψ = 2π cos(2πs). For the flow conjugate to this rotation by a homeomorphism which, in a local coordinate near 0, is s ↦ s^(1/3), the orbit of 0 is t ↦ t^(1/3) near t = 0, so the smooth function equal to that coordinate near 0 has difference quotients t^(−2/3) along the orbit and is not differentiable along the flow: the C¹ clause needs a C¹ flow.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §§5.4–5.5 (`topologicalPressure`, `flowTopologicalPressure` and its separated-set form), 6.4 (Abramov's formula), 7.1, 8.1 (`IsEquilibriumState`, `IsFlowEquilibriumState`), 8.5 (flow coboundaries); Layer 1.1.

### Examples

For the constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift and F = c, P_g(c) = log 2 + c, and speeding the flow up by 2 doubles the pressure of 2F and keeps the equilibrium states.

### Dependencies

Layer 1; ClassicalErgodicTheory §§5.4–5.5, 6.4, 7.1, 8.1, 8.5.

## Layer 5: Bowen's uniqueness criterion under weak specification

### 5.1 Lower Gibbs bounds for flows

ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4 defines Gibbs measures of a homeomorphism (`IsGibbsMeasure`). Define μ ∈ M(g), for a continuous flow g on a compact metric space and a continuous φ with P_g(φ) finite, to have the lower Gibbs property at scale ε (`HasLowerGibbsProperty`) with constant K ≥ 1 when μ(B_t(x, ε)) · exp(t P_g(φ) − ∫₀ᵗ φ(g^s x) ds) ≥ K⁻¹ for every x and every t ≥ 0 (BowenRuelle1975, Theorem 3.3, lines 272–277; ClimenhagaThompson2016, Lemma 4.16, lines 1251–1253).

Its API:

- Large times suffice: if the inequality holds for t ≥ T₀ with constant K, it holds for every t ≥ 0 with constant K e^(2T₀(|P_g(φ)| + max|φ|)), because B_t(x, ε) ⊇ B_T₀(x, ε) for t ≤ T₀ (§1.1) and both exponents change by at most T₀(|P_g(φ)| + max|φ|).
- Larger scales are free: the lower Gibbs property at scale ε₀ with constant K gives it at every scale ε ≥ ε₀ with the same constant, because B_t(x, ε) ⊇ B_t(x, ε₀); so the property at every sufficiently small scale gives it at every scale ε > 0. Since μ(B_t(x, ε₀)) ≤ 1, it also gives ∫₀ᵗ φ(g^s x) ds − t P_g(φ) ≤ log K for all x and t ≥ 0.
- A measure with the lower Gibbs property at arbitrarily small scales gives positive mass to every nonempty open set, which contains some B_0(x, ε).
- μ has the lower Gibbs property for φ iff it has it for φ + c with the same constant, and for φ + L_gψ with the constant multiplied by e^(2 sup|ψ|): the orbit integral changes by ψ(g^t x) − ψ(x) and the pressure does not change (ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5).

**Checks.**

- For the flow on a periodic orbit of period p and its time measure, P_g(φ) is the orbit average (1/p)∫₀^p φ, ∫₀ᵗ φ(g^s x) ds = tP_g(φ) + O(p max|φ|), and B_t(x, ε) is an orbit arc of measure comparable to ε/p for every t, so the lower Gibbs property holds.
- For the constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift, φ = 0 and the product μ of the Bernoulli measure (1/2, 1/2) with Lebesgue measure on the fibre, P = log 2 and B_t([s, x], ε) contains, for ε = 2^(−m) small, the product of a height interval of length comparable to ε with the cylinder fixing the coordinates −m, …, ⌊t⌋ + m, of measure comparable to ε 2^(−t−2m); times e^(t log 2) this stays above a positive constant. With the opposite sign, exp(−tP), the product would be comparable to 4^(−t) and tend to 0.
- The time measure of a periodic orbit of the same suspension is not lower Gibbs for φ = 0: the Bowen balls of points off the orbit have measure 0.
- Only the lower bound passes to large scales. For the full two-shift, φ = 0 and the Bernoulli measure, at ε = 1 the ball B_n(x, 1) is the cylinder fixing x_0, …, x_(n−1), of measure 2⁻ⁿ, and times exp(n log 2) the product is 1; at ε = 2, above the diameter 1, B_n(x, 2) is the whole shift, of measure 1, and the product is 2^n. The lower bound holds there with K = 1 while the upper Gibbs bound fails, which is why the two-sided Gibbs property of maps is asked only at small scales. For the roof-one suspension of the same shift and its product measure, a scale above the diameter gives the product e^(t log 2) = 2^t in the same way.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §§5.5, 8.4–8.5 (`flowTopologicalPressure`, `IsGibbsMeasure` for maps, flow coboundaries); Layer 1.1.

### 5.2 Bowen's theorem for homeomorphisms with weak specification

Prove (`existsUnique_isEquilibriumState_of_hasWeakSpecification`) that if X is a nonempty compact metric space, T an expansive homeomorphism of X (ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.3) with weak specification (§2.2), and φ a continuous potential with the Bowen property (`HasBowenProperty`, ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4), then φ has exactly one equilibrium state μ_φ; μ_φ is ergodic, is a Gibbs measure (`IsGibbsMeasure`, ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4), and gives positive mass to every nonempty open set (ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem 5.5, lines 1717–1724, with the decomposition consisting of all orbit segments and P^⊥_exp(φ) = −∞ by expansivity; Proposition 6.16, line 1955, for ergodicity; Lemma 6.13, lines 1908–1910, for the lower Gibbs bound and Proposition 6.17, lines 1963–1965, for the upper bound, the Bowen property replacing the supremum over the ball by the value at x). This is ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4's theorem with specification with periodic shadowing weakened to weak specification, which it implies (§2.2). Lemma 6.13 gives the lower bound μ(B_n(x, ρ)) ≥ Q e^(−nP + S_nφ(x)) for n ≥ N(M) only; for 1 ≤ n < N(M), B_n(x, ρ) ⊇ B_(N(M))(x, ρ) gives it with Q replaced by Q e^(−2N(M)(|P_T(φ)| + max|φ|)). The scales are free: for ε below an expansivity constant and the scale of the Bowen property and δ < ε/40, the proof gives the lower bound at every ρ ∈ (5δ, ε/8] and the upper bound at every γ ∈ (4δ, ε/4] (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 1880–1887), so both hold at every scale r ≤ ε/8 after choosing δ < r/5, and by uniqueness the measure does not depend on these choices. The route constructs μ as a limit of weighted orbit averages over maximising separated sets, proves the lower Gibbs bound by gluing with specification, deduces that every equilibrium state is absolutely continuous with respect to μ, and proves ergodicity of μ by a second gluing; absolute continuity and ergodicity give uniqueness.

**Checks.**

- For the full shift with φ depending on x_0, the unique equilibrium state is the Bernoulli measure with weights e^(φ(a))/Σ_b e^(φ(b)), and it is Gibbs: for φ = 0 and ε = 1/2, B_n(x, 1/2) is the cylinder fixing x₋₁, …, x_n, of measure 2^(−(n+2)), and exp(n log 2) = 2^n, so the product is 1/4 for all x and n.
- The swap of two points satisfies every hypothesis but not ClassicalErgodicTheory's specification (§2.2); its unique invariant measure is the equilibrium state, ergodic but not mixing, so ergodicity is the right conclusion and mixing is not claimed.
- The disjoint union of two copies of the full two-shift is expansive and every locally constant potential has the Bowen property, but weak specification fails and the equilibrium states of φ = 0 form a segment, joining the Bernoulli measures (1/2, 1/2) of the two copies, each of entropy log 2 = h_top: the specification hypothesis is needed. The two pieces must have equal entropy: for the full two-shift beside the full three-shift, h_top = log 3, a combination tμ₂ + (1 − t)μ₃ has entropy at most t log 2 + (1 − t) log 3, and the Bernoulli measure (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) of the three-shift is the only equilibrium state of φ = 0.
- The lower bound needs the small times treated separately: for the full two-shift, φ = 0 and a lower bound proved only for n ≥ N, the case n = 1 reads μ(B_1(x, 1/2)) = 1/8 ≥ Q e^(−log 2), which follows from B_1 ⊇ B_N with Q replaced by Q 2^(−2N).

### 5.3 Bowen's criterion for flows with weak specification

Prove (`existsUnique_isFlowEquilibriumState_of_hasFlowWeakSpecification`) that if X is a nonempty compact metric space, g a Bowen–Walters expansive continuous flow on X (`IsExpansiveFlow`, ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5) with weak specification (§2.2), and φ a continuous potential with the flow Bowen property (`HasFlowBowenProperty`, ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5), then φ has exactly one equilibrium state μ_φ for g (`IsFlowEquilibriumState`); μ_φ is ergodic for the flow, has the lower Gibbs property (§5.1) at every scale ε > 0, and gives positive mass to every nonempty open set (ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem A, lines 140–147, uniform case, expansivity giving P^⊥_exp(φ) = −∞ since §1.3 puts every Γ_ε(x) in an orbit segment; Proposition 4.19, line 1489, ergodicity; Lemma 4.16, lines 1251–1253, lower Gibbs bound for t ≥ T(M) at every sufficiently small scale, extended to all t ≥ 0 and then to every scale ε > 0 by §5.1; Proposition 4.15, lines 1243–1244, existence, also given by §3.1 and ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.2). This is ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5's theorem with specification with periodic shadowing weakened to weak specification, which it implies (§2.2); the roof-one suspension of the full two-shift satisfies the weaker hypothesis and not the stronger one. Positivity on open sets follows from the lower Gibbs bound at arbitrarily small scale, since by uniqueness the measure does not depend on the scale.

**Checks.**

- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift is expansive with weak specification, with gluing times in [0, τ₀ + 1] as in §6.6, τ₀ the connecting time at the given scale and the extra unit absorbing the phase; τ₀ grows as the scale shrinks, since at a scale that distinguishes the base coordinates −k, …, k at matched heights a shadowing point fixes the base coordinates −k, …, n + k of a first segment starting at height 0 with integer length n, so a second arbitrary segment can only start after a gluing time above 2k. It is not mixing; its unique measure of maximal entropy is the flow-ergodic product measure.
- The flow on a periodic orbit satisfies the hypotheses, and its unique invariant measure is the equilibrium state of every potential.

## Layer 6: Abstract hyperbolic dynamics

Uniform hyperbolicity enters the uniqueness theory only through a local product structure with exponential contraction. This layer states those axioms for homeomorphisms (Bowen's Axiom A*) and for flows, and derives expansivity, closing, density of periodic points, spectral decomposition, weak specification and the Bowen property of Hölder potentials, so that Layer 5 applies.

### 6.1 Homeomorphisms with hyperbolic local product structure

Define, for a homeomorphism f of a compact metric space X and δ > 0, the local stable and unstable sets W^s_δ(x) = {y : d(f^n x, f^n y) ≤ δ for all n ≥ 0} and W^u_δ(x) = {y : d(f⁻ⁿ x, f⁻ⁿ y) ≤ δ for all n ≥ 0} (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, lines 66–72). Say f has hyperbolic local product structure (`HasHyperbolicLocalProductStructure`) when (A2) for every δ > 0 there is e(δ) > 0 such that W^s_δ(x) ∩ W^u_δ(z) is nonempty whenever d(x, z) < e(δ), and (A3) there are δ* > 0, 0 < λ < 1 and c ≥ 1 with d(f^n x, f^n y) ≤ cλ^n d(x, y) for y ∈ W^s_(δ*)(x) and d(f⁻ⁿ x, f⁻ⁿ y) ≤ cλ^n d(x, y) for y ∈ W^u_(δ*)(x), for all n ≥ 0. Say f satisfies Axiom A* when moreover (A1) its periodic points are dense (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, lines 74–83).

Its API:

- Two points whose orbits stay δ*-close at all times n ∈ ℤ coincide: if d(f^n y, f^n y') ≤ δ* for every n ∈ ℤ, then f^n y' ∈ W^u_(δ*)(f^n y), so (A3) gives d(y, y') ≤ cλ^n d(f^n y, f^n y') ≤ cλ^n δ* for every n ≥ 0, hence y = y'.
- For 2δ ≤ δ* and d(x, z) < e(δ), the intersection W^s_δ(x) ∩ W^u_δ(z) is a single point [x, z]: two such points stay 2δ-close at all times, so they coincide by the previous item. The bracket is continuous, [x, x] = x, and f[x, z] = [fx, fz] whenever both sides are defined.
- f⁻¹ has hyperbolic local product structure with the roles of W^s and W^u exchanged, and so does f^k for k ≥ 1 with constant λ^k.
- The definitions depend on the metric only up to bi-Lipschitz equivalence, which changes c and e but not λ.

**Checks.**

- The full two-shift with d(x, y) = 2^(−N(x,y)) has W^s_(1/2)(x) = {y : y_i = x_i for i ≥ 0}, contraction d(T^n x, T^n y) ≤ 2⁻ⁿ d(x, y) on it (c = 1, λ = 1/2), and bracket [x, z] equal to z on the negative coordinates and x on the nonnegative ones whenever x_0 = z_0; its periodic points are dense, so it satisfies Axiom A*.
- The identity of a finite set satisfies Axiom A*: for δ below the least distance both local sets are singletons, and (A2) holds with e(δ) at most that distance.
- An irrational rotation fails (A3): W^s_δ(x) is the δ-ball and distances are preserved; the identity of the circle fails (A3) for the same reason, although it satisfies (A2).

*Needs:* Layer 1.1.

### 6.2 Expansivity, shadowing and closing

Prove, for a homeomorphism f of a compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure:

- f is expansive with constant δ* (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, Lemma 2.3, lines 96–97): this is §6.1's coincidence of points whose orbits stay δ*-close at all times;
- shadowing: for every β > 0 there is α > 0 such that every α-pseudo-orbit (x_n)_(n∈ℤ), d(f x_n, x_(n+1)) < α, is β-shadowed by some point y, d(f^n y, x_n) ≤ β for all n, and the shadowing point is unique when 2β ≤ δ*, two shadowing points staying 2β-close along their whole orbits (Bowen1975, Chapter 3; KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 18.1);
- closing: for every ε > 0 there are η > 0 and R ∈ ℕ such that every y with d(f^m y, y) < η for some m ≥ R has a point z with f^m z = z and d(f^k z, f^k y) ≤ ε for 0 ≤ k ≤ m (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, 2.5, lines 101–104);
- if f is transitive in forward time (for all nonempty open U, V some n ≥ 0 has f^n(U) ∩ V nonempty, Conventions), its periodic points are dense, so f satisfies Axiom A*.

Shadowing of finite pseudo-orbits is proved by iterating the bracket along the pseudo-orbit, as in BowenPeriodicPoints1971's proof of 2.10 (lines 171–200), with the errors summed as a geometric series by (A3); compactness gives bi-infinite pseudo-orbits and expansivity with constant δ* gives uniqueness for 2β ≤ δ*. Closing applies shadowing to the periodic pseudo-orbit y, fy, …, f^(m−1)y, y, …. For the last item, a homeomorphism of a nonempty compact metric space that is transitive in forward time is either a single finite orbit (if X has an isolated point p, its forward orbit is dense and contains the isolated point f⁻¹p, so p is periodic) or has no isolated points and, by Baire's theorem, a point whose forward orbit is dense; in both cases every nonempty open U meets f^n(U) for arbitrarily large n, and closing turns such a long return of a point of U into a periodic point near it. Forward time is needed: transitivity in signed time does not give returns.

**Checks.**

- On the full two-shift a pseudo-orbit is a sequence of points whose consecutive symbols agree up to α, and the shadowing point reads off the zeroth symbols; the closing lemma produces the periodic sequence repeating the first m symbols of y.
- On the identity of a finite set every pseudo-orbit with α below the least distance is constant, and shadowing is trivial.
- The irrational rotation (no (A3)) has the pseudo-orbit adding α/2 extra rotation at each step, which no orbit shadows at scale β < 1/4 for long times; it also has no periodic points.
- On the full two-shift with the metric of the Conventions (diameter 1, δ* = 1/2) and β = 1, every point 1-shadows every pseudo-orbit, so uniqueness fails above the expansivity scale; at β = 1/4, 2β = 1/2 = δ* and the shadowing point is unique.
- The monotone shift of the Conventions, with the metric of the full shift of the Conventions, has hyperbolic local product structure with c = 1 and λ = 1/2: W^s_(1/2)(x) is the set of y ∈ X with y_i = x_i for i ≥ 0, on which d(T^n x, T^n y) = 2⁻ⁿ d(x, y), and when x_0 = z_0 the sequence equal to z on the negative and to x on the nonnegative coordinates is again nondecreasing, so it lies in W^s_(1/2)(x) ∩ W^u_(1/2)(z). Its ℤ-action has a dense orbit, but its only periodic points are 0^ℤ and 1^ℤ: the cylinder {x₋₁ = 0, x_0 = 1} is a single nonperiodic point. The last item therefore fails for transitivity in signed time; in forward time the monotone shift is not transitive.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.3 (expansive homeomorphisms); Layer 6.1.

### 6.3 Spectral decomposition and specification

Prove, for a homeomorphism f of a nonempty compact metric space X satisfying Axiom A* (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, 2.6–2.10, lines 105–168, and Remark 4.4, lines 421–426):

- X is a finite disjoint union of closed sets X_1, …, X_m, permuted by f, with f^r : X_i → X_i C-dense whenever f^r maps X_i to itself, where f is C-dense when for every periodic point p its global unstable set W^u(p) = {y : d(f⁻ⁿ y, f⁻ⁿ p) → 0 as n → ∞} is dense (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, lines 71–72 and 105–106); f is transitive iff the permutation is a single cycle;
- for C-dense f and every ε > 0 there is M(ε) ∈ ℕ such that for every k ≥ 1, all points x_1, …, x_k and all integers a_1 ≤ b_1 < a_2 ≤ b_2 < … < a_k ≤ b_k with a_(i+1) − b_i > M(ε) for i < k (at least M(ε) integers between consecutive intervals, Bowen's M(ε)-delayed specification, lines 155–163) there is a periodic point y with d(f^n y, f^n x_i) < ε for all i and all n ∈ [a_i, b_i] (strong specification with periodic shadowing points, BowenPeriodicPoints1971, Theorem 2.10, lines 166–168);
- if f is transitive, it has weak specification at every scale, and h_top(f) > 0 unless X is finite.

The third item glues orbit segments by applying the second item to f^m on the piece X_1, at a scale ε' such that d(a, b) < ε' implies d(f^i a, f^i b) < ε for 0 ≤ i < m, with constant M = M(ε') counted in steps of f^m. A segment (x_j, n_j) is the tail of the f-segment of length r_j + n_j starting at f^(−r_j) x_j ∈ X_1, 0 ≤ r_j < m, which the f^m-segment of length L_j = max(1, ⌈(r_j + n_j)/m⌉) covers; glue these f^m-segments so that the first time of each exceeds the last time of the previous one by M + 1 steps of f^m, and move the shadowing point forward by r_1. The gluing time of f after the j-th segment is then mM + (mL_j − r_j − n_j) + r_(j+1), which lies in [mM, mM + 2m − 1] ⊆ [0, m(M + 2)] and in the residue class mod m that moves the end of one segment into the piece containing the start of the next.

**Checks.**

- The swap of two points satisfies Axiom A* with m = 2 pieces, each fixed by f², and has weak but not strong specification, as in §2.2.
- The full shift is C-dense with m = 1, and its strong specification is the second item.
- The disjoint union of two full shifts satisfies Axiom A* with two pieces fixed by f; the permutation is not a cycle, f is not transitive, and weak specification fails.
- The empty space is excluded: its homeomorphism satisfies Axiom A* and is transitive in forward time, both vacuously (Mathlib's `AddAction.IsTopologicallyTransitive` quantifies over nonempty open sets), and it decomposes into m = 0 pieces, whose permutation has no cycle; it has no weak specification (§2.2). Both the first and the third item fail there.
- The gluing times scale with m: let f be the product of the full two-shift σ with the rotation of ℤ/2, for the maximum of the shift metric and the discrete metric of ℤ/2, so that m = 2, X_1 = Σ × {0} and f² on X_1 is σ². At δ = 2^(−k) a point shadowing two segments of X_1 matches the base coordinates −k, …, n_1 + k − 1 of the first and those from n_1 + τ − k on of the second, so gluing two arbitrary segments needs τ ≥ 2k. The route's scale is ε' = 2^(−k−1), at which a σ²-segment occupying the σ²-times 0, …, L − 1 fixes the coordinates −k − 1, …, 2(L − 1) + k + 1 and one starting at the σ²-time t₀ fixes those from 2t₀ − k − 1 on; arbitrary segments are compatible iff these ranges are disjoint, that is t₀ − (L − 1) ≥ k + 2, so the least admissible delay is M = k + 1 (a_2 − b_1 > M, the route's excess of M + 1 steps of σ²): the bound m(M + 2) = 2k + 6 covers 2k, while M + m = k + 3 is below 2k once k ≥ 4.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.2 (topological mixing of maps); Layers 2.2, 6.1, 6.2.

### 6.4 Hölder potentials have the Bowen property

Prove that for a homeomorphism f of a compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure and a Hölder φ (`HolderWith` C θ φ with θ > 0), φ has the Bowen property (ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 341–342, for the statement; Bowen1975, Chapter 1). Choose ε with the bracket of ε-close points defined and within δ of both. If d(f^k x, f^k y) < ε for 0 ≤ k < n, then z = [x, y] satisfies f^k z = [f^k x, f^k y] for k < n, so d(f^k x, f^k z) ≤ cλ^k δ and d(f^k z, f^k y) ≤ cλ^(n−1−k) δ, hence d(f^k x, f^k y) ≤ 2cδ λ^(min(k, n−1−k)): this is the exponential closeness under which ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4 proves that Hölder potentials have the Bowen property, and summing the two geometric series directly gives |S_nφ(x) − S_nφ(y)| ≤ 2C(cδ)^θ/(1 − λ^θ).

**Checks.**

- On the full two-shift with θ = 1, the potential x ↦ x_0 is Lipschitz and Bowen with K = 0 at ε = 1/2: y ∈ B_n(x, 1/2) forces y_i = x_i for −1 ≤ i ≤ n, so the Birkhoff sums of x and y over 0 ≤ k < n agree.
- A constant potential is Hölder with C = 0 and Bowen with K = 0.
- On the full two-shift, the continuous potential φ(x) = 1/m, where m is the least index i ≥ 1 with x_i = 1 (φ = 0 if there is none), is neither Bowen nor Hölder: for every m ≥ 1, x = 0^ℤ and the point y whose only 1 is at index n + m stay 2^(−m)-close at the times 0, …, n − 1, while S_nφ(y) − S_nφ(x) = 1/(m + 1) + ⋯ + 1/(n + m) is unbounded in n, so the Bowen property fails at every scale; and its variation on cylinders of length m is 1/m, not exponentially small.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HolderWith`; ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.4 (`HasBowenProperty`); Layer 6.1.

### 6.5 Flows with hyperbolic local product structure

Define, for a continuous flow g without fixed points on a compact metric space and δ > 0, the local strong stable set W^s_δ(x) of points y with d(g^t x, g^t y) ≤ δ for all t ≥ 0 and d(g^t x, g^t y) → 0, and the local strong unstable set W^u_δ(x) symmetrically in backward time (BowenRuelle1975, lines 96–103). Say g has hyperbolic local product structure (`HasFlowHyperbolicLocalProductStructure`) when (F2) there is δ₀ > 0 such that for every δ ∈ (0, δ₀] there is e(δ) > 0 such that whenever d(x, z) < e(δ) there is a unique v ∈ [−δ, δ] with W^s_δ(g^v x) ∩ W^u_δ(z) nonempty, and (F3) there are δ* > 0, κ > 0 and c ≥ 1 with d(g^t x, g^t y) ≤ ce^(−κt) d(x, y) for y ∈ W^s_(δ*)(x) and d(g^(−t) x, g^(−t) y) ≤ ce^(−κt) d(x, y) for y ∈ W^u_(δ*)(x), for all t ≥ 0. The stable and unstable sets are those of BowenRuelle1975, lines 96–108; the axioms (F2) and (F3) are this roadmap's flow analogue of Bowen's (A2) and (A3) (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, lines 74–80), and §8.5 proves them for locally maximal hyperbolic sets of C¹ flows.

Its API:

- For δ ∈ (0, δ₀] with 2δ ≤ δ* and d(x, z) < e(δ), the intersection W^s_δ(g^v x) ∩ W^u_δ(z) at the time shift v = v(x, z) of (F2) is a single point [x, z]: two such points stay 2δ-close at all times and converge to each other in both time directions, so (F3) forces them to coincide. The bracket and the time shift are continuous (the uniform rate of (F3) keeps the convergence clauses in the limit), [x, x] = x and v(x, x) = 0. If moreover 4δ ≤ δ*, 2δ ≤ δ₀ and e(δ) ≤ e(2δ) (shrink e(δ) if necessary), then g^t[x, z] = [g^t x, g^t z] and v(g^t x, g^t z) = v(x, z) whenever d(g^s x, g^s z) < e(δ) for every s between 0 and t. For |τ| below a bound depending only on δ (uniform continuity of the flow on [−1, 1] × X), g^τ[x, z] lies in W^s_(2δ)(g^(v+τ) x) ∩ W^u_(2δ)(g^τ z); the time shift is unique at the scale 2δ because d(g^τ x, g^τ z) < e(2δ), and the point because 4δ ≤ δ*; steps of that length cover the times between 0 and t. Closeness at the two end times alone is not enough (Checks).
- The reversed flow `Flow.reverse` has hyperbolic local product structure with W^s and W^u exchanged; the rescaled flow g(at), a > 0, has it with κ replaced by aκ.
- Let W^cs_δ(x) be the union of g^v W^s_δ(x) over |v| ≤ δ (the local weak stable set). For δ ∈ (0, δ₀] with 2δ ≤ δ*, every z with d(x, z) < e(δ) lies in W^u_δ(w) for the point w = [x, z], and there is e'(δ) ∈ (0, e(δ)] such that w ∈ W^cs_δ(x) whenever d(x, z) < e'(δ). The point w lies in W^s_δ(g^v x) with |v| ≤ δ, and for v > 0 that set need not lie in g^v W^s_δ(x): g^(−v) w must also stay δ-close to x at the times in [0, v), which are backward times for w (Checks). For z close to x, uniformly in x by compactness, w is so close to g^v x (the bracket and v are continuous, [x, x] = x and v(x, x) = 0) that d(g^(−u) g^v x, g^(−u) w) ≤ δ for 0 ≤ u ≤ δ, whence g^(−v) w ∈ W^s_δ(x).

**Checks.**

- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift: W^s_δ([s, x]) consists of the points [s, y] with y ∈ W^s(x) of the shift, the bracket combines coordinates as in §6.1 at matched heights, and v is the height difference.
- The flow on a periodic orbit: W^s_δ(x) = W^u_δ(x) = {x}, and every z near x is g^v x for a unique small v, so (F2) and (F3) hold.
- The bound δ ≤ δ₀ in (F2) is needed: for the unit-speed flow on a periodic orbit of period 1 and δ = 0.6, the point z = g^(0.45)x is g^v x for v = 0.45 and for v = −0.55, both in [−0.6, 0.6], so the time shift is not unique however small e(δ) is taken relative to the orbit; δ₀ = 1/4 works, since two times in [−1/4, 1/4] differ by less than the period.
- The linear flow of irrational slope on ℝ²/ℤ² satisfies (F3) vacuously but fails (F2): distances are preserved for all time, so the convergence condition reduces both strong sets to {x}, and (F3) only tests y = x, where it reads 0 ≤ 0; a point z near x off the orbit segment g^([−δ, δ])x is not g^v x for any small v, so W^s_δ(g^v x) ∩ W^u_δ(z) is empty.
- Equivariance of the bracket needs closeness at the intermediate times. Take the constant-roof suspension of the cat map A = (2 1; 1 1), with [s, w] = [s − 1, Aw], the metric ds² + e^(2as) du² + e^(−2as) dv² of §11.1 (a = log((3 + √5)/2), u and v the coordinates along the unit eigenvectors e_u = (φ, 1)/√(1 + φ²) and e_v = (−1, φ)/√(1 + φ²), where φ = (1 + √5)/2), an even k ≥ 2, n = (F_(k+1), F_k) a pair of consecutive Fibonacci numbers, q = n − (n·e_v)e_v its projection to the unstable line and y = A^(−k)q. The identities F_(k+1) − φF_k = (−1/φ)^k and φF_(k+1) + F_k = φ^(k+1) give n·e_v = −φ^(−k)/√(1 + φ²) and |y| = φ^(1−k)/√(1 + φ²), that is −0.0293 and 0.0474 for k = 6 and n = (13, 8). For x = [0, w] and z = [0, w + y] the backward orbits are e^(−at)|y| apart along e_u, so x ∈ W^u_δ(z) and [x, z] = x with time shift 0; but g^k z = [0, A^k w + q] = [0, A^k w − (n·e_v)e_v], whose forward orbit is e^(−at)|n·e_v| from that of g^k x along e_v, so [g^k x, g^k z] = g^k z ≠ g^k x. Both brackets are defined at a scale δ of the first item once |y| < min(δ, e(δ)), which a large k achieves for every admissible e(δ). In between the orbits are far apart: A^(k/2)y = (φ², φ)/(1 + φ²) ≈ (0.724, 0.447) is congruent mod ℤ² to e_v/√(1 + φ²), at flat distance 1/√(1 + φ²) ≈ 0.526 from ℤ² for every k, and since the metric dominates e^(−2a|s|) times the flat metric of the fibres while a path that climbs to height ±h and returns has length at least 2h, the two points at time k/2 are at least min over h ≥ 0 of max(2h, 0.526e^(−ah)) ≈ 0.428 apart, so the hypothesis of the equivariance clause fails at every scale δ with e(δ) ≤ 0.428.
- The sets W^s_δ(g^v x) and g^v W^s_δ(x) differ. In the same suspension and metric the plane u = const carries the metric ds² + e^(−2as) dv² of curvature −a², onto which the metric projects without increasing lengths, so the point at stable coordinate b from [s, w] at the same height is at distance (2/a) asinh(a|b|e^(−as)/2) from it; for small δ, W^s_δ([s, w]) is therefore the stable segment of coordinate half-width ℓe^(as) with ℓ = (2/a) sinh(aδ/2) (ℓ ≈ 0.1000 at δ = 0.1). Hence W^s_δ(g^v x) has half-width ℓe^(a(s+v)) and g^v W^s_δ(x) has ℓe^(as), ratio e^(av) ≈ 1.101 at v = 0.1: a point of the first need not lie in the second, which is why the third item shrinks e(δ) to e'(δ); for z close to x the stable coordinate of [x, z] is far below ℓe^(as).

*Needs:* Mathlib `Flow`, `Flow.reverse`; Layer 1.1.

### 6.6 Expansivity, closing and weak specification for flows

Prove, for a continuous flow g on a compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure:

- g is Bowen–Walters expansive (BowenWalters1972; BowenEntropyExpansive1972, Example 1.6*, lines 134–135, for basic sets of Axiom A flows);
- closing: for every ε > 0 there are η > 0 and R > 0 such that every y with d(g^T y, y) < η for some T ≥ R has a periodic point z, of period T' with |T' − T| ≤ ε (T' ∈ `Flow.periods` g z), with d(g^t z, g^t y) ≤ ε for 0 ≤ t ≤ T (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 18.1);
- if g is transitive in forward time (for all nonempty open U, V some t ≥ 0 has g^t(U) ∩ V nonempty, Conventions), its periodic orbits are dense;
- if the space is nonempty and g is transitive in forward time, g has weak specification at every scale.

Expansivity follows from (F3) as in §6.2, the time shift absorbing the reparametrisation. Closing iterates the bracket along the closed pseudo-orbit and adjusts the time shift at the closing point. For density of periodic orbits, a flow without fixed points has no isolated points, so on a nonempty space forward transitivity and Baire's theorem give a point whose forward orbit is dense, every nonempty open set returns to itself at arbitrarily large times, and closing turns a long return into a periodic orbit. For weak specification, choose finitely many δ-balls covering X and, by transitivity, connecting orbit segments of length between 1 and τ₀ between any two of them (the point with dense forward orbit above passes from any ball to any other at arbitrarily large times, so the lower bound 1 costs nothing); concatenating the given segments with these connections gives a pseudo-orbit with finitely many jumps, which the bracket iteration shadows up to a time shift of size at most δ at each junction; the shifts are absorbed into the gluing times, which for δ < 1 stay in [0, τ₀ + 1], the lower bound 1 on the connecting times keeping them nonnegative.

**Checks.**

- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the full two-shift is transitive and not mixing, and has weak specification with gluing times in [0, τ₀ + 1], but no strong specification. The height [s, x] ↦ s mod 1 is a continuous map to ℝ/ℤ that advances at unit speed along the flow; by uniform continuity there is δ > 0 such that points less than δ apart have heights less than 1/8 apart in ℝ/ℤ. Write a segment as (point, length). For the segments ([0, a], 0), ([0, b], 1) a shadowing point y has height within 1/8 of 0 and g^τ y has height within 1/8 of 0, so a common gluing time τ lies within 1/4 of ℤ; for the segments ([0, a], 1/2), ([0, b], 1), g^(1/2+τ) y must have height within 1/8 of 0, so τ lies within 1/4 of 1/2 + ℤ. The distances of any real τ to ℤ and to 1/2 + ℤ add up to 1/2, so no τ does both, at this δ and at every smaller one.
- The flow on a periodic orbit is transitive, its periodic orbit is dense, and weak specification holds with gluing times at most one period.
- The identity flow of a finite set has fixed points and is excluded from the definition; the disjoint union of two periodic orbits has hyperbolic local product structure but is not transitive and has no weak specification.
- The flow on the empty space has no fixed points, hyperbolic local product structure and transitivity in forward time, all vacuously, but no weak specification (§2.2), which asks for a point even for the empty list of segments: the last item needs the space nonempty.
- The constant-roof suspension (roof 1) of the monotone shift of the Conventions has hyperbolic local product structure, with the bracket at matched heights as for the full two-shift (§6.5), and a dense orbit; but its only periodic orbits are the suspensions of 0^ℤ and 1^ℤ, and it has no weak specification. For the flow boxes U over {x_0 = 1} and V over {x_0 = 0} at heights in (1/4, 3/4), g^t(U) never meets V for t ≥ 0, because the zeroth symbol of T^k x is x_k ≥ x_0 = 1 for k ≥ 0: it is transitive only in signed time, and the two items above fail for that notion.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5 (`IsExpansiveFlow`); Tau Ceti `Flow.periods`; Layers 2.1, 2.2, 6.5.

### 6.7 Hölder potentials have the Bowen property for flows

Prove that for a continuous flow g on a compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure and a Hölder φ, φ has the Bowen property for g (BowenRuelle1975, Appendix, Lemma A.2, lines 681–701, for C¹ potentials on hyperbolic sets, whose argument uses only the bracket and the exponential estimates; ClimenhagaThompson2016, lines 341–342). Fix a small δ and take ε ≤ e(δ) of (F2). For y ∈ B_t(x, ε), z = [x, y] lies on W^s_δ(g^v x) with |v| ≤ δ and on W^u_δ(y); the orbit integrals of φ over [0, t] along x and g^v x differ by at most 2|v| max|φ| ≤ 2δ max|φ|, and those along g^v x, z and z, y differ by at most the geometric integrals ∫₀^∞ C(cδ)^θ e^(−κθs) ds each, as in §6.4. No bound of |v| by a multiple of d(x, y) is used: the abstract axioms give none.

**Checks.**

- A constant potential is Bowen with K = 0.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the full two-shift, a potential of the zeroth symbol and the height that is Lipschitz in the height is Hölder and Bowen.
- For a C¹ flow on a manifold and a C¹ function ψ, the orbit integral of L_gψ over [0, t] is ψ(g^t x) − ψ(x), so L_gψ has the flow Bowen property (`HasFlowBowenProperty`) with K = 4 sup|ψ| whether or not the flow is hyperbolic: the Bowen property of coboundaries does not need this section.
- On the circle, the flow conjugate to the unit-speed rotation by a homeomorphism equal to s ↦ s³ near 0 satisfies (F2) and (F3) with singleton strong sets, and d(x, g^v x) = |v|³ for x = 0 and small v: the time shift is not bounded by a multiple of the distance, while |v| ≤ δ still holds and the bound 2δ max|φ| is what the proof uses.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HolderWith`; ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5 (`HasFlowBowenProperty`); Layer 6.5.

### 6.8 Unique equilibrium states for transitive hyperbolic systems

Prove, with transitivity in forward time as in the Conventions: (a) (`existsUnique_isEquilibriumState_of_hasHyperbolicLocalProductStructure`) if f is a transitive homeomorphism of a nonempty compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure and φ is Hölder, then φ has a unique equilibrium state, which is ergodic, has the Gibbs property at every sufficiently small scale (§5.2) and is positive on nonempty open sets; for φ = 0 this is the unique measure of maximal entropy, and h_top(f) > 0 unless X is finite (BowenPeriodicPoints1971, 8.1, lines 749–750, for the entropy of Bowen's measure, and Remark 4.4, lines 421–426); (b) (`existsUnique_isFlowEquilibriumState_of_hasFlowHyperbolicLocalProductStructure`) if g is a transitive continuous flow on a nonempty compact metric space with hyperbolic local product structure and φ is Hölder, then φ has a unique equilibrium state for g, which is flow-ergodic, has the lower Gibbs property (§5.1) at every scale ε > 0 (§5.3) and is positive on nonempty open sets. Both are Layer 5 applied with §§6.2–6.4, respectively §§6.6–6.7.

**Checks.**

- For the full shift and φ of the zeroth coordinate, (a) gives the Bernoulli measure of §5.2.
- For the swap of two points (finite X), h_top = 0 and the unique measure is the equilibrium state of every potential.
- For the flow on a periodic orbit, (b) gives the normalised time measure for every Hölder potential, and the entropy is 0.
- Transitivity in signed time is not enough. The monotone shift of the Conventions has hyperbolic local product structure (§6.2) and a dense ℤ-orbit; it has n + 1 words of length n, so h_top = 0, and an invariant probability gives every point of the single wandering orbit of switch sequences the same mass, hence mass 0, so M(T) is the segment between the point masses at 0^ℤ and 1^ℤ. Every member has free energy 0 = P_T(0), so φ = 0 has infinitely many equilibrium states. Its roof-one suspension likewise has the time measures of its two periodic orbits as distinct equilibrium states of F = 0.

*Needs:* Layers 5.2, 5.3, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4, 6.6, 6.7.

### Examples

## Layer 7: Hyperbolic sets and their linear theory

Throughout, M is a finite-dimensional smooth manifold without boundary and the norm |v| of a tangent vector comes from a continuous Riemannian metric (`Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric` on the tangent bundle). The splitting is not required to be continuous in the definitions: continuity is a theorem (§7.5).

### 7.1 Hyperbolic sets of diffeomorphisms and Anosov diffeomorphisms

Define a compact set Λ ⊆ M with f(Λ) = Λ to be a hyperbolic set (`IsHyperbolicSet`) of a C¹ diffeomorphism f (`Diffeomorph I I M M 1`) for a continuous Riemannian metric when there are C > 0, 0 < λ < 1 and, for every x ∈ Λ, complementary subspaces E^s_x and E^u_x of T_xM with Df_x(E^s_x) = E^s_(fx) and Df_x(E^u_x) = E^u_(fx), such that |D(f^n)_x v| ≤ Cλ^n|v| for v ∈ E^s_x and |D(f⁻ⁿ)_x v| ≤ Cλ^n|v| for v ∈ E^u_x, for all n ≥ 0. Call f Anosov when M is compact and M itself is a hyperbolic set (Smale1967, Part I, Section 1.3, lines 381–473; KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4).

Its API:

- A closed subset Λ' ⊆ Λ with f(Λ') = Λ' of a hyperbolic set Λ is hyperbolic with the restricted splitting and the same constants. Finite unions are treated in §7.4, after uniqueness of the splitting.
- A fixed point p is a hyperbolic set {p} iff the linear map Df_p has no complex eigenvalue of modulus 1, with E^s_p and E^u_p the sums of the generalised eigenspaces of eigenvalues of modulus less than and greater than 1.
- Hyperbolicity is invariant under C¹ conjugacy: if h is a C¹ diffeomorphism, h(Λ) is hyperbolic for h∘f∘h⁻¹ with the pushed-forward splitting.

**Checks.**

- For the linear map diag(2, 1/2) of ℝ², the set {0} is hyperbolic with E^s the second axis, E^u the first, C = 1 and λ = 1/2; for diag(2, 1) it is not, the eigenvalue 1 giving a vector that is neither contracted nor expanded.
- The identity of a manifold of positive dimension has no nonempty hyperbolic set: |Df^n v| = |v| forces E^s = E^u = 0, which is impossible when T_xM ≠ 0.
- The rate λ = 1 must be excluded: with λ = 1 allowed, every isometry with any splitting would qualify.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Diffeomorph`, `mfderiv`, `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric`.

### 7.2 Hyperbolic sets of flows and Anosov flows

Define a compact g-invariant set Λ ⊆ M to be a hyperbolic set (`IsFlowHyperbolicSet`) of a C¹ flow g when its generator X (`flowGenerator`, the velocity of t ↦ g^t x at t = 0) has no zero on Λ and there are C > 0, κ > 0 and, for every x ∈ Λ, subspaces E^s_x, E^u_x with T_xM = E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x) ⊕ E^u_x, invariant under every Dg^t, such that |Dg^t v| ≤ Ce^(−κt)|v| for v ∈ E^s_x and |Dg^(−t) v| ≤ Ce^(−κt)|v| for v ∈ E^u_x, for all t ≥ 0. Call g Anosov when M is compact and M is a hyperbolic set (BowenRuelle1975, lines 10–18; FH2019, Chapter 5, for hyperbolic sets of flows, and Chapter 8, for Anosov flows).

Its API:

- A closed invariant subset of a hyperbolic set is hyperbolic; Dg^t(X(x)) = X(g^t x), and |X| is bounded above and below on Λ.
- A periodic orbit is a hyperbolic set iff the derivative of its return map, acting on T_xM/ℝX(x), has no eigenvalue of modulus 1.
- Hyperbolicity is invariant under C¹ conjugacy of flows: for a C¹ diffeomorphism h, h(Λ) is a hyperbolic set of the conjugate flow `Homeomorph.conjFlow` along h, with the pushed-forward splitting.

**Checks.**

- The flow of a constant vector field on ℝ²/ℤ² is not Anosov: Dg^t is the identity, so E^s = E^u = 0 and T_xM would be one-dimensional.
- The constant-roof suspension of diag(2, 1/2) at the fixed point 0 (a periodic orbit of period 1 in the mapping torus of the linear map) is a hyperbolic set with E^s, E^u the two axes transported along the orbit and κ = log 2.
- A zero of the generator is excluded: the origin of the linear field (x, −y) on ℝ² is a hyperbolic equilibrium but not a hyperbolic set of the flow in this sense (BowenRuelle1975, line 11).

*Needs:* Mathlib `Flow`, `mfderiv`, `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.conjFlow`.

### 7.3 Independence of the metric

Prove that if Λ is a hyperbolic set of f for one continuous Riemannian metric with constants (C, λ), then it is hyperbolic with the same splitting for any other continuous Riemannian metric, with constants (CK₁K₂, λ), where K₁ bounds the ratio of the new norm to the old and K₂ the ratio of the old to the new on the tangent spaces over the compact set Λ; and the same for flows with (CK₁K₂, κ) (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4; BowenRuelle1975, lines 412–413, for the metric independence of the derived objects). Compactness of the unit sphere bundle over Λ and continuity of the inner products (`Continuous.inner_bundle`) give K₁ and K₂.

**Checks.**

- Multiplying a metric by the constant 4 multiplies norms by 2, so K₁ = 2, K₂ = 1/2 and K₁K₂ = 1: C and λ are unchanged, as they must be since the ratios |Df^n v|/|v| do not change.
- On ℝ² at the fixed point 0 of diag(2, 1/2), the metric with Gram matrix (2 1; 1 1) has K₁ = ((3 + √5)/2)^(1/2) and K₂ = ((3 − √5)/2)^(−1/2), so K₁K₂ = (3 + √5)/2 ≈ 2.618, and the rate λ = 1/2 persists.
- On a noncompact invariant set the conclusion fails: two metrics whose ratio is unbounded along an orbit can destroy the uniform constant.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric`, `Continuous.inner_bundle`; Layers 7.1, 7.2.

### 7.4 Characterisation and uniqueness of the splitting

Prove, for a hyperbolic set Λ of f and x ∈ Λ, that E^s_x = {v : |D(f^n)_x v| → 0 as n → ∞} = {v : sup_(n≥0) |D(f^n)_x v| < ∞}, and E^u_x is the corresponding set for backward iterates; hence any two splittings witnessing hyperbolicity coincide, and a finite union of hyperbolic sets Λ₁, …, Λ_k of f is hyperbolic, with constants the largest C_i and the largest λ_i, the splittings agreeing on overlaps. For a hyperbolic set of a flow, E^s_x = {v : |Dg^t v| → 0 as t → ∞}, E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x) = {v : sup_(t≥0) |Dg^t v| < ∞}, and symmetrically for E^u (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4). Writing v = v^s + v^u, the unstable component grows at least like C⁻¹λ⁻ⁿ|v^u|, so boundedness forces v^u = 0; for flows the flow direction stays bounded above and below.

**Checks.**

- For diag(2, 1/2) at 0: (1, 0) grows like 2^n, (0, 1) decays like 2⁻ⁿ, and (1, 1) is unbounded, so E^s is exactly the second axis.
- For the constant-roof suspension of diag(2, 1/2), the flow direction ∂_t is bounded but does not decay, so it lies in E^s ⊕ ℝX and not in E^s.
- The boundedness characterisation needs hyperbolicity: for a rotation of the plane every vector is bounded and none decays.
- For the diffeomorphism of ℝ² ⊔ ℝ² equal to diag(2, 1/2) on the first copy and to (1/3)·Id on the second, the saddle {0₁} (C = 1, λ = 1/2) and the sink {0₂} (C = 1, λ = 1/3) have hyperbolic union with C = 1 and λ = 1/2 = max(1/2, 1/3), and dim E^s is 1 at 0₁ and 2 at 0₂.

*Needs:* Layers 7.1, 7.2.

### 7.5 Continuity of the splitting

Prove, for a hyperbolic set Λ of f: the set of tangent vectors v with base point x ∈ Λ and v ∈ E^s_x is closed in the total space of TM, and likewise for E^u, and for E^s ⊕ ℝX and E^u ⊕ ℝX in the flow case; x ↦ dim E^s_x is locally constant on Λ; and the projections onto E^s_x along E^u_x have norms bounded uniformly on Λ (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4; BowenRuelle1975, line 13, where continuity is part of the definition). A limit of vectors v_k ∈ E^s_(x_k) satisfies |D(f^n) v| ≤ Cλ^n|v| by continuity of D(f^n), so it lies in E^s_x by §7.4; the two dimensions are upper semicontinuous by closedness and add up to dim M, hence are locally constant; uniform boundedness of the projections follows by compactness.

**Checks.**

- For a hyperbolic toral automorphism the splitting is constant in the flat trivialisation, the simplest continuous case.
- On a disjoint union of a hyperbolic fixed point of saddle type in ℝ² and a hyperbolic sink in ℝ², dim E^s takes the values 1 and 2: locally constant, not constant.
- Closedness uses the uniform constants: for the maps diag(1 + t, 1/(1 + t)) the stable line at 0 is the second axis for every t > 0, but the rate tends to 1 as t → 0 and the limit identity has stable subspace 0, so no statement uniform in t survives.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric`; Layers 7.1, 7.2, 7.4.

### 7.6 Adapted metrics

Prove that for a hyperbolic set Λ of a C¹ diffeomorphism f of a manifold M (Hausdorff and σ-compact, as in the Conventions, but not necessarily compact; Λ is compact by definition) with rate λ and every λ' with λ < λ' < 1, there is a continuous Riemannian metric on M for which |Df_x v| ≤ λ'|v| for v ∈ E^s_x and |Df⁻¹_x v| ≤ λ'|v| for v ∈ E^u_x, for every x ∈ Λ; and for a hyperbolic set of a C¹ flow with rate κ and every 0 < κ' < κ, a continuous Riemannian metric with |Dg^t v| ≤ e^(−κ't)|v| on E^s and |Dg^(−t) v| ≤ e^(−κ't)|v| on E^u for all t ≥ 0, x ∈ Λ (BowenRuelle1975, lines 19–24, for flows with the estimate for t ≥ t₀; KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4). On Λ define the new norm on E^s by the series Σ_(n≥0) λ'⁻²ⁿ|D(f^n) v|² (for flows, the integral ∫₀^∞ e^(2κ's)|Dg^s v|² ds), similarly on E^u backward, with E^s and E^u declared orthogonal; for a flow, the flow direction ℝX(x) is declared orthogonal to both and keeps the norm |X(x)| of the original metric, which is continuous on Λ because X is, so that the form is an inner product on all of T_xM = E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x) ⊕ E^u_x (the statement constrains E^s and E^u only); it is continuous by §7.5 and dominated convergence, and C = 1 follows by shifting the series. Extend the resulting continuous inner product from Λ to M by Tietze extension in finitely many local trivialisations covering Λ and a partition of unity subordinate to them and to the complement of Λ (`SmoothPartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate`, whose hypotheses on M are that it is Hausdorff and σ-compact), mixing with the original metric away from a neighbourhood of Λ on which the extension stays positive definite.

**Checks.**

- For diag(2, 1/2) at 0 with λ' = 3/4 the Euclidean metric is already adapted (C = 1, rate 1/2).
- For the geodesic flow of a closed surface of curvature −1, the Sasaki metric is adapted with C = 1 and κ = 1: unstable Jacobi fields grow exactly like e^t in the norm (|J|² + |J'|²)^(1/2) (§9.3).
- The spectral radius need not be an admissible rate: for the linear map of ℝ³ equal to 2 on one axis and to the Jordan block J = (1/2 1; 0 1/2) on the complementary plane, J^n has entries 2⁻ⁿ and n2¹⁻ⁿ, so 2^n|J^n(0, 1)| = (4n² + 1)^(1/2) is unbounded and λ = 1/2 satisfies the hypothesis with no constant C, while every λ ∈ (1/2, 1) does. For λ' = 3/4 the inner product ⟨v, w⟩' = ⟨Dv, Dw⟩ with D = diag(1/8, 1) on the plane gives J the operator norm of (1/2 1/8; 0 1/2), which is ((65/64)^(1/2) + 1/8)/2 ≈ 0.566 ≤ 3/4, so the adapted metric of the statement exists there.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Bundle.ContinuousRiemannianMetric`, `SmoothPartitionOfUnity.exists_isSubordinate`; Layers 7.1, 7.2, 7.5.

### 7.7 Inverses, iterates and time reversal

Prove: if Λ is a hyperbolic set of f, it is a hyperbolic set of f⁻¹ with E^s and E^u exchanged and the same constants, and of f^k (k ≥ 1) with the same splitting and rate λ^k; if Λ is a hyperbolic set of a flow g, it is one for the reversed flow `Flow.reverse` with E^s and E^u exchanged, and for the rescaled flow g(at), a > 0, with rate aκ; in particular inverses of Anosov diffeomorphisms and reversals of Anosov flows are Anosov (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4). Each time-t map of an Anosov flow preserves the splitting E^s ⊕ ℝX ⊕ E^u with the stated rates on E^s and E^u and bounded distortion on ℝX; on a nonempty compact manifold it is not an Anosov diffeomorphism, for any real t, since Dg^t X(x) = X(g^t x) and |X| is bounded above and below. On the empty manifold every flow and every diffeomorphism is Anosov vacuously, so nonemptiness is needed.

**Checks.**

- For the cat map A = (2 1; 1 1), A⁻¹ = (1 −1; −1 2) has the same eigenlines with the roles of the eigenvalues (3 ± √5)/2 exchanged.
- For f^2 with f = diag(2, 1/2), the rate is 1/4 = λ².
- The time-1 map of the constant-roof suspension of an Anosov diffeomorphism fixes the flow direction (|Dg^1 X| = |X|), so it fails the definition of §7.1 although the flow is Anosov.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Flow.reverse`; Layers 7.1, 7.2.

### Examples

The linear maps diag(2, 1/2) and the cat matrix give hyperbolic fixed points and Anosov automorphisms with explicit splittings and adapted metrics; constant-roof suspensions give hyperbolic periodic orbits of flows.

### Dependencies

Mathlib's diffeomorphisms, `mfderiv`, continuous Riemannian metrics and partitions of unity. Independent of Layers 1–6.

## Layer 8: Invariant manifolds and local product structure

In this layer M is Hausdorff and σ-compact, compact only where stated, and carries a fixed smooth Riemannian metric, which exists under these hypotheses (`TauCeti.nonempty_contMDiffRiemannianMetric`); distances are the truncated distance d = min(1, ρ) of the Conventions, ρ its Riemannian extended distance (`Manifold.riemannianEDist`), which is ⊤ between different components. So d is a real-valued metric, a hyperbolic set with the restricted distance is a compact metric space even when it meets several components, and d = ρ at the small scales at which the local statements of this layer are made. Any two such distances are bi-Lipschitz equivalent on a compact set, so the choice changes constants only. The charts at the points x of Λ are built from finitely many extended charts (`extChartAt`) whose domains cover a compact neighbourhood of Λ: the one containing x, translated so that x goes to 0 and composed with a linear isomorphism carrying the splitting of T_xM to a coordinate splitting; finiteness makes the C¹ estimates of §8.1 uniform in x.

### 8.1 The Hadamard–Perron theorem for sequences

Prove the uniform Hadamard–Perron theorem (`exists_isHadamardPerronFamily`): let V be a finite-dimensional real inner product space, the orthogonal sum of a subspace E^u and E^s = (E^u)^⊥ (the source's ℝ^d = ℝ^k ⊕ ℝ^(d−k), whose summands are orthogonal), write z = (x, y) for the components, let the box B^u(r₀) × B^s(r₀) be the set of z with |x| < r₀ and |y| < r₀ (`hadamardPerronBox`), let μ > max(1, λ), and for each n ∈ ℤ let f_n(x, y) = (A_n x + g_n(x, y), B_n y + h_n(x, y)) be a C¹ map of the box into V with f_n(0) = 0, where A_n, B_n are linear with |A_n x| ≥ μ|x| (that is, A_n is invertible with |A_n⁻¹| ≤ μ⁻¹) and |B_n| ≤ λ. There is γ₀ > 0 such that for 0 < γ < γ₀ there is δ₀ > 0 such that, if g_n, h_n and their derivatives have norm below δ < δ₀ on the box for all n, then λ' = λ + (1 + γ⁻¹)δ and μ' = (μ − (1 + γ)δ)/(1 + γ), which tend to λ and μ as γ, δ → 0, satisfy max(1, λ') < μ', and there is a unique family (W^+_n)_(n∈ℤ) of graphs W^+_n = {x + φ_n(x) : |x| < r₀} (`hadamardPerronGraph`) of C¹ maps φ_n from the ball of radius r₀ of E^u to that of E^s with φ_n(0) = 0 and |Dφ_n| < γ such that (`IsHadamardPerronFamily`) (i) f_n(W^+_n) ∩ (B^u(r₀) × B^s(r₀)) = W^+_(n+1); (ii) |f_n(y) − f_n(y')| > μ'|y − y'| for distinct y, y' ∈ W^+_n; and (iii) if a point z has points z_L, L ≥ 0, of the box with z_0 = z, f_(n−L)(z_L) = z_(L−1) for every L ≥ 1 and |z_L| < Cν^(−L)|z| for every L ≥ 0, for some C > 0 and some ν with λ' < ν < μ', then z ∈ W^+_n (ClimenhagaPesin2016, Theorem 11.1, lines 1822–1840, with λ' and μ' at lines 1853–1855; KatokHasselblatt1995, Theorem 6.2.8, cited at ClimenhagaPesin2016, lines 281–283); and (iv) the unstable cones are invariant with expansion: if two points of the box have difference (Δx, Δy) with |Δy| ≤ γ|Δx|, the difference (Δx', Δy') of their images under f_n satisfies |Δy'| ≤ γ|Δx'| and |Δx'| ≥ (μ − (1 + γ)δ)|Δx|, where μ − (1 + γ)δ > 1 + γ > 1 because μ' > 1. Uniqueness is among the families of graphs through 0 with these properties. When E^u ≠ 0 the condition φ_n(0) = 0 follows from (iii) and continuity, since the points of the constructed graphs other than 0 decay in backward time; when E^u = 0 each graph is a single point, (ii) and (iii) are empty, and only the condition through 0 singles out the family (Checks). Clause (iv) is the cone step inside the proof of (iii) and of the source's constants (ClimenhagaPesin2016, lines 1847–1855, where μ' = (μ − δ(1 + γ))/(1 + γ) is the same bound for the full norm of a vector of the cone); its proof is one application of the mean value inequality: |Δg_n|, |Δh_n| ≤ δ(|Δx| + |Δy|) ≤ δ(1 + γ)|Δx|, so |Δx'| ≥ (μ − (1 + γ)δ)|Δx| and |Δy'| ≤ (λγ + (1 + γ)δ)|Δx| ≤ γ|Δx'| once δ(1 + γ)² ≤ γ(μ − λ), which the choice of δ₀ in the source (line 1852) implies, together with μ − (1 + γ)δ > 1 + γ. The source gives the maps for n ≤ 0 only, and states (iii) with the inverses f_(n−L)⁻¹ ∘ ⋯ ∘ f_(n−1)⁻¹(z), which for invertible f_n are the points z_L above. The form for n ∈ ℤ follows by applying the source's form to each shifted sequence (f_(m+k))_(m≤0), k ∈ ℤ, and comparing the resulting families by uniqueness. The form of (iii) above needs no inverse. The graphs pass through 0, the common fixed point of the f_n, because the graph transform preserves graphs through 0. Let q_L ∈ W^+_(n−L) be backward images, inside the graphs, of the point q_0 of W^+_n with the same E^u-coordinate as z; they exist by (i), and |q_L| ≤ μ'^(−L)|q_0| by (ii) applied with y' = 0. If z ∉ W^+_n, then z − q_0 is a nonzero vector of E^s, so by forward invariance of the unstable cones {|Δy| ≤ γ|Δx|} no z_L − q_L lies in them; on the complementary cones a forward step multiplies the norm of the E^s-component of the difference by at most λ + (1 + γ⁻¹)δ, which is the source's λ' (ClimenhagaPesin2016, line 1854). Hence |z − q_0| ≤ λ'^L (Cν^(−L)|z| + μ'^(−L)|q_0|) for every L, which tends to 0 because λ' < ν and λ' < μ', a contradiction. The stable family is a separate statement with further hypotheses: assume in addition that λ < 1 and that each f_n is the restriction of a C¹ diffeomorphism F_n of ℝ^d with F_n(0) = 0 whose inverse, written in the coordinates of E^s ⊕ E^u, is (y, x) ↦ (B_n⁻¹y + h̃_n(x, y), A_n⁻¹x + g̃_n(x, y)), with B_n invertible and h̃_n, g̃_n of C¹ norm below δ on B^u(r₀) × B^s(r₀). The theorem applied to the backward-indexed sequence of the F_n⁻¹, with E^s in the role of E^u and the rates 1/λ > max(1, 1/μ), gives a unique family of graphs W^−_n of C¹ maps B^s(r₀) → B^u(r₀) with F_n⁻¹(W^−_(n+1)) ∩ (B^u(r₀) × B^s(r₀)) = W^−_n, on which F_n⁻¹ expands distances (ClimenhagaPesin2016, Remark 11.2, lines 1841–1843). The proof is the graph transform: it is a contraction on the space of sequences of 1-Lipschitz graphs, and its fixed point is C¹ by the invariant cone family for the derivatives.

**Checks.**

- For linear maps (g_n = h_n = 0) the unstable graphs are E^u itself: φ_n = 0, with expansion exactly μ when A_n = μ·Id.
- With λ ≥ 1 allowed (only λ < μ and μ > 1 required) the theorem produces strong unstable manifolds for dominated splittings, as needed for flows, where the complement contains the neutral flow direction.
- With μ = λ the cone argument fails: for f_n = Id on ℝ² every line through 0 is invariant, so uniqueness of the family is lost.
- The condition through 0 is needed when E^u = 0: for V = E^s, every f_n the identity, λ = 1 and μ = 2, each constant family W^+_n = {p} with |p| < r₀ satisfies (i), while (ii) and (iii) are empty (a backward sequence of z ≠ 0 is constant, and |z| < Cν^(−L)|z| fails for large L because ν > λ' > 1); so without φ_n(0) = 0 the family would not be unique.
- The stable statement needs invertibility: f_n(x, y) = (2x, 0) satisfies every hypothesis of the unstable statement (A_n = 2, B_n = 0, zero nonlinear parts, μ = 2, λ = 1/2), has the unstable graphs y = 0, and maps each vertical line {x} × ℝ to a single point, so it has no inverse sequence and receives no stable conclusion. Clause (iii), stated with backward sequences, still applies to it: z = (x, 0) with x ≠ 0 has z_L = (2^(−L)x, 0), and |z_L| = 2^(−L)|z| < 2ν^(−L)|z| for every L ≥ 0 and every ν < 2, so z lies on y = 0 as (iii) asserts; a point (x, y) with y ≠ 0 is not in the image of f_(n−1), has no z_1, and is not on the graph. A clause written with f_(n−1)⁻¹ would be undefined here.

*Needs:* Mathlib calculus (`fderiv`, contraction mapping `ContractingWith`). Tau Ceti's Lyapunov–Perron construction of the local stable manifold of a hyperbolic equilibrium of an ordinary differential equation (`ContinuousLinearMap.lyapunovPerronSolution`, `ContinuousLinearMap.localStableGraphMap`) is the continuous-time analogue of this graph transform; it concerns an equilibrium, not a sequence of maps, and is not consumed.

### 8.2 Local stable and unstable manifolds of hyperbolic sets of diffeomorphisms

Prove that for a hyperbolic set Λ of a C^r diffeomorphism f of M (r ≥ 1) there are r₀ > 0, K ≥ 1, C' > 0 and λ < λ' < 1 and, for every x ∈ Λ, a C^r embedding φ^s_x of the closed ball of radius r₀ of E^s_x (for the adapted norm of §7.6) into M, the graph parametrisation in the chart at x, with φ^s_x(0) = x and derivative at 0 the inclusion of E^s_x, depending continuously on x in the C¹ topology of embeddings (read in the charts of this layer's introduction, where each plaque is the graph of a map between fixed coordinate balls: uniform convergence of the graph maps and of their derivatives on the closed ball), such that the local stable plaques 𝒲^s_r(x) = φ^s_x(closed ball of radius r), 0 < r ≤ r₀, satisfy: f(𝒲^s_r(x)) ⊆ 𝒲^s_(λ'r)(fx); d(f^n y, f^n y') ≤ C'λ'^n d(y, y') for y, y' ∈ 𝒲^s_r(x) and n ≥ 0; and W^s_(r/K)(x) ⊆ 𝒲^s_r(x) ⊆ W^s_(Kr)(x), where W^s_ε(x) = {y : d(f^n x, f^n y) ≤ ε for all n ≥ 0}. Symmetrically for the unstable plaques 𝒲^u_r(x), tangent to E^u_x, and the sets W^u_ε(x) of backward orbits (KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4; ClimenhagaPesin2016, Theorem 11.1, for the sequence version). The metric cutoffs W^s_ε(x) are compact neighbourhoods of x in the plaques but need not be smooth discs; the plaques carry the smooth structure. Apply §8.1 in the charts of this layer's introduction along each orbit, with the adapted metric of §7.6 so that the linear parts satisfy the hypotheses uniformly, the stable statement of §8.1 applying because f is a diffeomorphism; the inclusions W^s_(r/K)(x) ⊆ 𝒲^s_r(x) come from §8.1's characterisation of the points whose iterates stay in the boxes, and C^r regularity follows by the same graph transform on jets.

**Checks.**

- For the cat map on 𝕋² with the flat metric (its matrix is symmetric, so the eigenlines are orthogonal), W^s_ε(x) is, for small ε, the closed ε-segment of the stable line through x, and the plaques are segments of the same line.
- For the linear map B = (1/2 1; 0 1/2) of ℝ², the fixed point 0 is a hyperbolic set with E^s = ℝ², and W^s_ε(0) = {y : |B^n y| ≤ ε for all n ≥ 0}. The unit vector v proportional to (1, √7 − 2) has |v| = |Bv| = 1 and |B²v| ≈ 0.7646, so at εv the constraints n = 0 and n = 1 are both active, with normals v and BᵀBv of determinant √7/4 ≈ 0.661 ≠ 0: W^s_ε(0) has a corner at εv for every ε > 0, while the plaques, closed discs of an adapted inner product, are smooth.
- For the time-one map of a flow (not hyperbolic) the set {y : d(f^n x, f^n y) ≤ ε, n ≥ 0} contains a segment of the orbit of x and is not tangent to a contracting subspace: hyperbolicity is used.
- At a hyperbolic fixed point the theorem is the local stable manifold theorem of the fixed point: for diag(2, 1/2) at 0 the local stable manifold is the ε-segment of the second axis.

### 8.3 Strong and weak local manifolds of hyperbolic sets of flows

The weak plaque has corners along the boundary of the product and is not claimed to be a smooth closed disc. Moreover there are charts straightening the weak plaques: for every sufficiently small ε > 0 and every x ∈ Λ there is a C^r map φ_x from the closed ε-ball of T_xM (for the inner product that makes E^s_x, ℝX(x) and E^u_x orthogonal, agrees with the metric of §7.6 on E^s_x and E^u_x and gives X(x) length 1; it depends continuously on x ∈ Λ because X is continuous and has no zero on Λ) into M, a C^r diffeomorphism onto its image, with φ_x(0) = x and derivative the identity of T_xM at 0, carrying the ε-ball of E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x) into 𝒲^cs_r₀(x) and the ε-ball of E^u_x ⊕ ℝX(x) into 𝒲^cu_r₀(x), with C¹ norms of φ_x and of its inverse bounded uniformly in x ∈ Λ; in particular φ_(g^t x)⁻¹ ∘ g^t ∘ φ_x, defined near 0, has derivative Dg^t_x at 0 (BowenRuelle1975, A.1, lines 643–652). Then flow the strong plaques for |v| ≤ r. The inclusion 𝒲^s_r(x) ⊆ W^s_(Kr)(x) is the contraction on the plaques. The inclusion W^s_(r/K)(x) ⊆ 𝒲^s_r(x) is not a case of §8.1(iii) for the time −1 maps: there λ = 1, so λ' > 1 and (iii) asks for a rate ν > 1, while the points of W^s_ε(x) converge with no rate. It follows from the cone step §8.1(iv) for the time-one maps. Let y ∈ W^s_(r/K)(x) and let w be the point of the weak stable plaque 𝒲^cs_r(x) with the same E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x)-coordinate as y in the chart at x, which exists for K large because that plaque is a graph over a neighbourhood of 0 in E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x). ### 8.4 Local product structure of locally maximal hyperbolic sets of diffeomorphisms

Call a set Λ locally maximal for a homeomorphism f (`IsLocallyMaximal`) when there is an open U ⊇ Λ, an isolating neighbourhood, such that every point whose whole orbit lies in U lies in Λ; for Λ with f(Λ) = Λ, such as a hyperbolic set of f, this says that Λ is the intersection of f^n(U) over n ∈ ℤ, which is the set of points whose whole orbit lies in U. The condition is topological; it is used here for hyperbolic sets.

Its API:

- Λ is locally maximal with isolating neighbourhood U iff every subset S ⊆ U with f(S) = S is contained in Λ; the whole orbit of a point is such a set. Forward invariance f(S) ⊆ S does not suffice (first Check).
- If Λ is locally maximal for f, it is locally maximal for f⁻¹ with the same U; if moreover f(Λ) = Λ, it is locally maximal for f^k, k ≥ 1, with the isolating neighbourhood U ∩ f(U) ∩ ⋯ ∩ f^(k−1)(U), which contains Λ = f^j(Λ) ⊆ f^j(U) for 0 ≤ j < k, and a point x whose f^k-orbit lies in it has f^(kn−j)x ∈ U for all n ∈ ℤ and 0 ≤ j < k, that is at every integer time, so x ∈ Λ; a C¹ diffeomorphism h carries a locally maximal hyperbolic set Λ to a locally maximal hyperbolic set h(Λ) of h∘f∘h⁻¹ with isolating neighbourhood h(U).
- Local maximality does not depend on the metric, and it is what places the bracket of the theorem below in Λ.

**Checks.**

- A hyperbolic sink {p} of a diffeomorphism of ℝ² is locally maximal with U a small ball around p, since the forward images of U shrink to p; the only subsets S ⊆ U with f(S) = S are ∅ and {p}, as such an S lies in every f^n(U), n ≥ 0, whereas S = U satisfies f(S) ⊆ S without lying in {p}, so the first item of the API needs f(S) = S. It is a hyperbolic set with E^u = 0, although no Anosov diffeomorphism of a compact manifold of positive dimension has E^u = 0 (§8.6), so the local API holds where the global nontriviality theorem does not apply.
- The neighbourhood for f^k must be shrunk: let f act on three copies of ℝ² by diag(2, 1/2) on the first and by diag(2, 1/2) from the second copy to the third and from the third to the second, with fixed saddle a = 0₁ and period-two saddle orbit {c, d} = {0₂, 0₃}. Then Λ = {a} is locally maximal for f with U the union of small balls around a and c, since the orbit of c leaves U at d; for f² the point c is fixed in U, so the intersection of the f²ⁿ(U) contains c, while U ∩ f(U) excludes c and isolates {a} for f².
- In the same three-copy example the period-two saddle orbit {c, d} is a hyperbolic periodic orbit, hence locally maximal. Its points lie in different copies of ℝ², so ρ(c, d) = ⊤ for the Riemannian extended distance ρ, while the truncated distance of the Conventions between c and d is 1: with it, f restricted to {c, d} is the swap of two points of §2.2, a compact metric space with weak specification with τ = 1 at every scale below 1, whereas ρ restricted to {c, d} is not a real-valued metric.
- Invariance is needed for iterates: for f = −Id on ℝ², Λ = {(1, 0)} and U the open ball of radius 1/2 about (1, 0), every point of U has first coordinate above 1/2 and y, −y never both do, so no orbit {y, −y} lies in U and Λ is locally maximal for f, vacuously; but f² is the identity, each point is a whole f²-orbit, every open set containing (1, 0) contains other points, and no open set isolates Λ for f². Here f(Λ) = {(−1, 0)} ≠ Λ and U ∩ f(U) = ∅.
- Without local maximality the bracket can leave Λ: for a horseshoe with hyperbolic fixed point p and a transverse homoclinic orbit O of p, the set {p} ∪ O is hyperbolic but not locally maximal, and the bracket of two points of O near p, one close to the local stable and one close to the local unstable manifold of p, is a horseshoe point outside {p} ∪ O.

### 8.5 Local product structure of locally maximal hyperbolic sets of flows

Call a set Λ locally maximal for a flow g (`IsFlowLocallyMaximal`) when there is an open U ⊇ Λ, an isolating neighbourhood, such that every point whose whole orbit lies in U lies in Λ; for an invariant Λ, such as a hyperbolic set of §7.2, this says that Λ is the intersection of g^t(U) over t ∈ ℝ, which is the set of points whose whole orbit lies in U (BowenRuelle1975, line 29, condition (d) of a basic hyperbolic set). The condition is topological; it is used here for hyperbolic sets.

Its API:

- Λ is locally maximal for g with isolating neighbourhood U iff every subset S ⊆ U with g^t(S) ⊆ S for every real t (Mathlib's `IsInvariant`, which forces g^t(S) = S, applying it at −t) is contained in Λ; the whole orbit of a point is such a set.
- Local maximality depends only on the orbits as sets: Λ is locally maximal with the same U for the reversed flow t ↦ g^(−t) and for every rescaled flow t ↦ g^(at), a > 0; a homeomorphism h carries it to the locally maximal set h(Λ) of the conjugate flow t ↦ h ∘ g^t ∘ h⁻¹ (`Homeomorph.conjFlow`), with isolating neighbourhood h(U), and when h is a C¹ diffeomorphism and Λ a hyperbolic set, h(Λ) is a locally maximal hyperbolic set (§7.2). In particular local maximality does not depend on the metric, and it is what places the bracket of the theorem below in Λ.
- For an invariant Λ and t > 0, the set U_t of points x with g^s x ∈ U for every s ∈ [0, t] is open, its complement being the projection to M of the closed set of (s, x) ∈ [0, t] × M with g^s x ∉ U, along the compact factor [0, t]; it contains Λ; and a point whose g^t-orbit lies in U_t has its whole g-orbit in U (write each time as nt + s with n ∈ ℤ and 0 ≤ s < t), so it lies in Λ. Hence Λ is locally maximal for the homeomorphism g^t in the sense of §8.4, with isolating neighbourhood U_t. A nonempty hyperbolic set Λ of a C¹ flow g (§7.2) is not a hyperbolic set of the diffeomorphism g^t, since for x ∈ Λ the vector Dg^(nt) X(x) = X(g^(nt) x) stays bounded above and below for n ∈ ℤ (§7.2) while a hyperbolic splitting of g^t sends every nonzero vector either to 0 or to infinity under forward iteration; so the theorem of §8.4 does not apply to g^t. The hypothesis is needed: for the linear field (x, −y) on ℝ² (§7.2, third Check), {0} is invariant and locally maximal for the flow, isolated by any ball around 0, and it is a hyperbolic set of g^t = diag(e^t, e^(−t)), although it is not a hyperbolic set of the flow, the generator vanishing there.

### 8.6 Nontriviality of the stable and unstable bundles

Prove: (a) if f is a C¹ Anosov diffeomorphism of a compact manifold and E^s_x = 0 or E^u_x = 0 at some x, then the connected component of x is a single point; hence on a compact manifold whose components have positive dimension both bundles are nonzero everywhere; (b) if g is a C¹ Anosov flow on a compact manifold and E^s_x = 0 or E^u_x = 0 at some x, then the component of x has dimension 1 and is a periodic orbit; hence on a compact manifold of dimension at least 2 both bundles are nonzero. The proof derives them from §§7.5, 7.7, 8.2 (whose plaques are those of KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 6.4) and 8.3 and one pinned Mathlib theorem, and every intermediate step is part of this target. By §7.7 (inverse and reversal exchange E^s and E^u) it suffices to treat E^u_x = 0. Dimensions are locally constant (§7.5), so E^u = 0 on the component K of x, which is open and closed and, the manifold being compact, one of finitely many components.

For (a), each y ∈ K has dim E^s_y = dim M, so the stable plaque 𝒲^s_r₀(y) of §8.2 is a full-dimensional embedded disc around y and a neighbourhood of y; finitely many such plaques cover K. Since f permutes the finitely many components, some power f^p maps K onto K, and f^(pk)(K) is covered by the images of the plaques, whose diameters are at most 2C'Kλ'^(pk)r₀ → 0 (each plaque lies in W^s_(Kr₀)(y), §8.2, so it has diameter at most 2Kr₀ before the contraction acts). A set covered, for each k, by the same finite number m of sets of diameter tending to 0 has at most m points, so K is finite and, being connected, a point.

For (b), T_yM = E^s_y ⊕ ℝX(y) on K, so the interior of each weak stable plaque 𝒲^cs_r₀(y) of §8.3 is a neighbourhood of y; choose finitely many, N_1, …, N_m with centres y_i, covering K. A point of N_i is g^v z with |v| ≤ r₀ and z in the strong plaque at y_i, so d(g^t(g^v z), g^(t+v)y_i) ≤ L·C'e^(−κ't)·Kr₀, where L bounds the Lipschitz constants of the g^v with |v| ≤ r₀. Chaining overlapping N_i through the connected K gives times w_i with |w_i| ≤ 2mr₀ and d(g^t y_i, g^(t+w_i)y_1) → 0 as t → ∞. Hence the distance from each point of g^t(K) = K to the orbit arc {g^s y_1 : |s − t| ≤ 3mr₀} tends to 0 uniformly as t → ∞, and for t_k → ∞ with g^(t_k)y_1 → z the set K lies in the compact arc A = {g^s z : |s| ≤ 3mr₀}. In a chart at a point of K (`extChartAt`), A is the image of a subset of ℝ under a map differentiable on it, so if dim M ≥ 2 the image has dense complement (`DifferentiableOn.dense_compl_image_of_dimH_lt_finrank`), which a nonempty open subset of K cannot have. Hence dim M = 1 on K (dimension 0 is excluded by X ≠ 0), and the compact connected one-dimensional K, invariant and contained in one orbit, is that periodic orbit.

**Checks.**

- The hyperbolic toral automorphism of 𝕋² given by the cat matrix has dim E^s = dim E^u = 1.
- An Anosov flow on a circle (the flow on a single periodic orbit) has E^s = E^u = 0 and dim M = 1, the exception in (b).
- A hyperbolic sink {p} of a diffeomorphism of ℝ² is a hyperbolic set with E^u = 0, but it is not Anosov on a compact manifold; the conclusion is about Anosov systems only.

*Needs:* Mathlib `extChartAt`, `DifferentiableOn.dense_compl_image_of_dimH_lt_finrank`; Layers 7.1, 7.2, 7.5, 7.7, 8.2, 8.3.

### 8.7 Hölder continuity of the bundles

Prove that for a hyperbolic set Λ of a C² diffeomorphism or C² flow (C^(1+α) suffices), the bundles E^s and E^u are Hölder continuous on Λ: there are θ ∈ (0, 1] and C such that for x, y ∈ Λ in a common chart domain, the distance between E^u_x and E^u_y read in the chart, measured as the distance between the graphs representing them over a fixed complement, is at most C d(x, y)^θ (BowenRuelle1975, line 362, citing Pugh; KatokHasselblatt1995, Section 19.1). The proof compares the backward invariant cone images at x and y over n steps. Let L > 1 be a common Lipschitz constant of f and f⁻¹ on a neighbourhood of Λ (for a flow, of g^1 and g⁻¹, with λ = e^(−κ)), so that d(f^(∓k)x, f^(∓k)y) ≤ L^k d(x, y) while these points stay in the neighbourhood, and let Df be α-Hölder there, 0 < α ≤ 1 (α = 1 in the C² case): the images agree to within C (L^n d(x, y))^α at step n and contract at rate λ^n, and optimising n gives θ = α log λ⁻¹ / (α log L + log λ⁻¹), which lies in (0, α); the Hölder constant of Df enters only the constant C.

**Checks.**

- For a hyperbolic toral automorphism E^u is constant in the flat chart, Hölder for every θ.
- For the cat map with the flat metric, A and A⁻¹ both have operator norm (3 + √5)/2 = λ⁻¹, so L = λ⁻¹ and α = 1 give θ = log λ⁻¹ / (2 log λ⁻¹) = 1/2 in the route, consistent with the previous Check. Reading L as a Lipschitz constant of Df would be wrong: Df is constant, so every L > 0 qualifies, and L = λ makes the denominator 0.
- For the geodesic flow of a surface of curvature −1, E^u is smooth (horocyclic), the best case.
- For C¹ systems without Hölder derivative the bundles are only continuous in general; this is why the geometric potential of §11.1 is Hölder only for C² (or C^(1+α)) systems.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HolderWith`; Layers 7.1, 7.2, 7.5, 8.2, 8.3.

The sink (1/2 1; 0 1/2) shows that metric cutoffs can have corners where plaques are smooth.

### 9.1 Hyperbolic toral automorphisms

Prove that f_A is an analytic diffeomorphism and that it is Anosov for the flat metric with E^s and E^u the (constant, in the flat trivialisation) images of the sums of the real generalised eigenspaces of A for eigenvalues of modulus less than and greater than 1 (Smale1967, Part I, Section 1.3, lines 381–473). ### 9.3 Geodesic flows in negative curvature

Let (M, g) be a smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary with its Levi-Civita connection ∇, let π : TM → M be the projection, and let φ^t(v) = γ_v'(t) be the geodesic flow of TM on its open domain, γ_v the geodesic with initial velocity v. Define the connection map K : T(TM) → TM of ∇: for v ∈ TM and ξ ∈ T_v(TM), K_v(ξ) = D_sV(0), where V is a C¹ curve in TM with V(0) = v and V'(0) = ξ, read as a vector field along its base curve c = π ∘ V, and D_s is the covariant derivative along c (`CovariantDerivative.alongCurve`). In a chart, for v = (x, w) and ξ with base and fibre components (a, b), K_v(ξ) = b + Γ_x(w, a), Γ the Christoffel map; this depends on ξ only, so K_v is well defined, linear, and valued in T_(π(v))M. Prove (KatokHasselblatt1995, Chapter 17, for the horizontal–vertical splitting used for geodesic flows):

- (a) K is a smooth vector-bundle map from T(TM) to TM over π, and ξ ↦ (Dπ_v ξ, K_v ξ) is a smooth vector-bundle isomorphism from T(TM) onto the pull-back of TM ⊕ TM along π; so T_v(TM) is the direct sum of the horizontal subspace ker K_v and the vertical subspace ker Dπ_v, identified with T_(π(v))M by Dπ_v and by K_v respectively.
- (b) For v ∈ TM, ξ ∈ T_v(TM) and t in the domain of γ_v, Dπ(Dφ^t_v ξ) = J(t) and K(Dφ^t_v ξ) = D_tJ(t), where J is the Jacobi field along γ_v with J(0) = Dπ_v ξ and D_tJ(0) = K_v ξ.

For (a), in a chart the map is (a, b) ↦ (a, b + Γ_x(w, a)), block lower triangular with identity diagonal blocks, hence invertible, with entries smooth in (x, w). For (b), choose a smooth curve V with V(0) = v and V'(0) = ξ and consider the surface f(s, t) = π(φ^t(V(s))), whose curves t ↦ f(s, t) are geodesics, defined for s near 0 on every compact subinterval of the domain of γ_v because that domain is open (`TauCeti.Manifold.contMDiffOn_maximalIntegralCurve_geodesicSpray`). Since ∂_t f(s, t) = φ^t(V(s)), the symmetry lemma for the torsion-free ∇ (`CovariantDerivative.alongCurve_curveVelocity_comm`) gives D_t ∂_s f(0, t) = D_s ∂_t f(0, t) = K(Dφ^t_v ξ), which at t = 0 is D_sV(0) = K_v ξ; so the variation field is the Jacobi field J of the statement.

Its API:

- K_v vanishes exactly on the horizontal subspace and Dπ_v exactly on the vertical one; on the vertical subspace K_v is inverse to the vertical lift w' ↦ d/ds (v + sw') at s = 0, so a vertical vector is determined by its K-component.
- The geodesic spray at v is the horizontal vector with Dπ-component v: the velocity of a geodesic is parallel along it, so K_v of the spray is 0.
- The flip σ(v) = −v carries a vector with components (w, w') at v to the vector with components (w, −w') at −v: σ ∘ V has the same base curve as V and D_s(−V) = −D_sV.

## Layer 10: Basic sets and the Bowen–Ruelle theorem

### 10.1 Basic sets and attractors

Define a basic set (`IsBasicSet`) of a C¹ diffeomorphism f as a nonempty locally maximal hyperbolic set Λ on which f is transitive in forward time (Conventions: for nonempty relatively open U, V ⊆ Λ some n ≥ 0 has f^n(U) ∩ V nonempty), and a basic set (`IsFlowBasicSet`) of a C¹ flow as a nonempty locally maximal hyperbolic set on which the flow is transitive in forward time (some t ≥ 0 has g^t(U) ∩ V nonempty); call a basic set Λ of a flow an attractor (`IsFlowAttractor`) when it has an open neighbourhood U with g^t(U) ⊆ U for all t ≥ T₀ and Λ the intersection of g^t(U) over t ≥ 0, and define its basin W^s_Λ as the set of points y with d(g^t y, Λ) → 0 (BowenRuelle1975, lines 25–34 and 94–95, whose basic hyperbolic sets have a dense orbit and, as a further axiom, dense periodic orbits; the first two items below show that the two definitions agree).

Its API:

- Periodic points (orbits) are dense in a basic set (§6.2, §6.6 and §§8.4–8.5), so Bowen–Ruelle's density axiom is a theorem; a basic set also has a point with dense forward orbit (the Baire argument of §§6.2 and 6.6), so it has a dense orbit.
- Conversely, a nonempty locally maximal hyperbolic set Λ on which f is transitive in signed time and whose periodic points are dense is a basic set: if f^n(U) ∩ V is nonempty for some n < 0, a periodic point p of period q ≥ 1 in the open set V ∩ f^n(U) satisfies f⁻ⁿp ∈ U, so p lies in f^(kq+n)(U) ∩ V, and kq + n ≥ 0 for large k. The same argument with a periodic orbit of positive period (a hyperbolic set of a flow has no fixed points) applies to flows. Without density of periodic points the implication fails (Checks).
- A transitive Anosov diffeomorphism or flow of a nonempty compact manifold M is a basic set Λ = M, and for flows an attractor with U = M and basin M.
- Basic sets of f are basic sets of f⁻¹, and basic sets of a flow are basic sets of its reversal.
- For a hyperbolic set Λ of a flow, x ∈ Λ and ε > 0, the local strong stable set W^s_x(ε) is the set of y ∈ M with d(g^t y, g^t x) ≤ ε for all t ≥ 0 and d(g^t y, g^t x) → 0, and W^s_Λ(ε) is the union of the W^s_x(ε) over x ∈ Λ (BowenRuelle1975, Proposition 4.4(b), lines 381–383); unlike the sets of §6.5, y ranges over M and not over Λ. Every y ∈ W^s_x(ε) lies in the closed flow Bowen ball B_x(ε, T) for every T ≥ 0, and in the basin.
- If Λ is an attractor of a C¹ flow, then for every small ε > 0 there is α > 0 such that the closed α-neighbourhood of Λ lies in W^s_Λ(ε), and the basin W^s_Λ is the union over t ≥ 0 of the sets g^(−t)(W^s_Λ(ε)); in particular the basin is open (BowenRuelle1975, lines 402–406 and 441, which take the neighbourhood from HirschPalisPughShub1970, Section 5.1). Route: for small r the strong unstable plaques 𝒲^u_r(x), x ∈ Λ, of §8.3 lie in Λ, because the backward orbit of a point of 𝒲^u_r(x) stays within the attractor's open neighbourhood U, so the point lies in every g^t(U), t ≥ 0. For y within α of x ∈ Λ, read in the chart of §8.3 at x the unstable plaque 𝒲^u_r(x) as the graph of a map k from the closed ball E^u_x(r) to E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x), and the weak stable plaques 𝒲^cs_r(z), z ∈ 𝒲^u_r(x), as graphs of maps h_z from a ball of E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x) to E^u_x with small Lipschitz constants, depending continuously on z. Write y = (y^cs, y^u) and z(u) = (k(u), u); since h_(z(u))(k(u)) = u, the map u ↦ u − h_(z(u))(y^cs) + y^u sends E^u_x(r) into itself when α is small, and a fixed point (`TauCeti.brouwerFixedPoint_closedBall`) gives z ∈ 𝒲^u_r(x) ⊆ Λ with y ∈ 𝒲^cs_r(z). Then y = g^v y' with |v| ≤ r and y' ∈ 𝒲^s_r(z), and g^v z ∈ Λ. For r ≤ ε/(KL), with K of §8.3 and L the Lipschitz constant of the maps g^s, |s| ≤ r₀, on the strong stable plaques at points of Λ constructed in §8.5's proof, y ∈ W^s_(g^v z)(ε): the distance d(g^t y, g^t g^v z) = d(g^(t+v) y', g^(t+v) z) is at most Kr when t + v ≥ 0, because 𝒲^s_r(z) ⊆ W^s_(Kr)(z) (§8.3); at the backward times t + v ∈ [v, 0), which occur for 0 ≤ t < −v when v < 0 and about which the plaque says nothing, it is at most L d(y', z) ≤ LKr; and it tends to 0 by §8.3's contraction. A point of the basin comes within α of Λ at some time t, so it lies in g^(−t)(W^s_Λ(ε)); conversely every point of g^(−t)(W^s_Λ(ε)) converges to Λ.

**Checks.**

- A hyperbolic periodic orbit of a flow is a basic set, and it is an attractor iff its unstable bundle is zero.
- On the flat torus ℝ²/ℤ² with coordinates (θ, r), the flow of θ' = 1, r' = −sin(2πr)/(2π) has the hyperbolic periodic orbit Λ = {r = 0}, with stable multiplier e^(−t). It is an attractor with U = {|r| < 1/4}; the larger U = {|r| < 1/2} satisfies g^t(U) ⊆ U, but the intersection of its images is U itself, since backward orbits tend to the repelling circle r = 1/2 without reaching it. For ε < 1/4, W^s_(θ,0)(ε) = {(θ, r) : |r| ≤ ε}, the θ-coordinates moving in step, so W^s_Λ(ε) = {|r| ≤ ε}, of volume 2ε, contained in B_Λ(ε, T) for every T; the basin {r ≠ 1/2} is the increasing union of the g^(−t)({|r| ≤ ε}).
- For a hyperbolic periodic orbit of saddle type, such as the orbit of [0, 0] in the constant-roof suspension of the cat map, W^s_Λ(ε) is a piece of the two-dimensional weak stable surface of the orbit and has volume 0 in the three-manifold: without the attractor hypothesis W^s_Λ(ε) need not contain a neighbourhood of Λ.
- The union of two distinct hyperbolic fixed points of a diffeomorphism is locally maximal and hyperbolic but not transitive, hence not a basic set.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map, Λ = M is a basic set and an attractor with basin M.
- The empty set is compact, invariant, locally maximal with U = ∅, hyperbolic and transitive, all vacuously, and carries no invariant probability measure; nonemptiness excludes it, so that §§10.2–10.4 have a measure to speak of.
- Let Λ₂ be a horseshoe of a C¹ diffeomorphism f of the two-sphere, a locally maximal hyperbolic set with isolating neighbourhood U₂ on which f is conjugate to the full two-shift, and let Λ ⊆ Λ₂ be the points whose itineraries are nondecreasing, on which f is conjugate to the monotone shift of the Conventions. Λ is hyperbolic, and locally maximal: the points of Λ₂ whose itinerary reads 1, 0 at times 0, 1 form a compact set disjoint from Λ, and removing from U₂ a compact neighbourhood N of it that misses Λ leaves an open set whose maximal invariant set lies in Λ₂, never meets N, and so is Λ. Its ℤ-action has a dense orbit, but f|Λ is not transitive in forward time, so Λ is not a basic set; its only periodic points are two fixed points, and both point masses are measures of maximal entropy (§6.8), which §10.4 excludes for basic sets. A definition with transitivity in signed time would make Λ a basic set and §10.2 false for φ = 0.

### 10.2 Bowen's theorem for diffeomorphisms

Prove (`existsUnique_isEquilibriumState_of_isBasicSet`) that if Λ is a basic set of a C¹ diffeomorphism f of a manifold M of the Conventions (Hausdorff and σ-compact, not necessarily compact; Λ is compact by definition), with the truncated Riemannian distance, and φ : Λ → ℝ is Hölder continuous, then φ has a unique equilibrium state μ_φ for f restricted to Λ; μ_φ is ergodic, has the Gibbs property at every sufficiently small scale, gives positive mass to every nonempty relatively open subset of Λ, and μ_φ = μ_φ' whenever φ' = φ + c + ψ∘f − ψ for a real c and a continuous ψ on Λ (Bowen1975, Chapter 4, Theorem 4.1; ClimenhagaThompson2016, Theorem 5.5, for the criterion used). In particular a transitive C¹ Anosov diffeomorphism of a nonempty compact manifold and a Hölder potential on M have a unique equilibrium state; the C² case is a specialisation. By §8.4 and §10.1, f restricted to Λ, a nonempty compact metric space, satisfies Axiom A*; §6.8(a) applies, and ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.2 gives the invariance.

**Checks.**

- For the cat map and φ = 0 the unique equilibrium state is Haar measure, of entropy log((3 + √5)/2) (§9.1).
- For the cat map and the constant potential −log((3 + √5)/2), minus the logarithm of its unstable Jacobian for the flat metric of 𝕋², the equilibrium state is again Haar measure, with pressure 0.
- For a hyperbolic fixed point (a one-point basic set) the unique equilibrium state is the point mass, of entropy 0, for every φ. For the linear map diag(2, 1/2) of the noncompact ℝ² and Λ = {0}, isolated by any ball around 0 (the only whole orbit in it is that of 0), the restricted system is a point, the Gibbs product ν(B_n) exp(nP − S_nφ) equals 1 for P = φ(0), and Λ is finite, as the finite alternative of §10.4 allows: compactness of M is not needed.

### 10.3 The Bowen–Ruelle theorem for flows

Prove (`existsUnique_isFlowEquilibriumState_of_isFlowBasicSet`) that if Λ is a basic set of a C¹ flow g on a manifold M of the Conventions (Hausdorff and σ-compact, not necessarily compact; Λ is compact by definition), with the truncated Riemannian distance, and F : Λ → ℝ is Hölder continuous, then F has a unique equilibrium state ν_(g,F) for g restricted to Λ; ν_(g,F) is ergodic for the flow, positive on nonempty relatively open subsets of Λ, satisfies the lower Gibbs bound at every scale: for every ε > 0 there is C_ε > 0 with ν_(g,F)(B_T(x, ε)) ≥ C_ε exp(−T P(g|Λ, F) + ∫₀ᵀ F(g^t x) dt) for all x ∈ Λ and T ≥ 0, B_T(x, ε) the flow Bowen ball of g restricted to Λ (the lower Gibbs property of §5.1 at every scale ε > 0, given by §6.8(b); the bound holds a fortiori for the closed balls B_x(ε, T) of §11.4, which contain these), and ν_(g,F) = ν_(g,F') whenever F' = F + c + L_gψ for a real c and a continuous ψ differentiable along the flow with continuous derivative (in particular for ψ of class C¹ on M); moreover ν_(g,F) is the equilibrium state of F_t for g^t among g-invariant measures for every t > 0, and ν_(g_a, aF) = ν_(g,F) for the rescaled flow g_a(t) = g(at), a > 0 (BowenRuelle1975, Theorem 3.3, lines 272–277, and Proposition 3.1, lines 213–222; TsujiiZhang2023, lines 106–109 and Definition 3.5, lines 485–495, for the statement in the consumers' form). In particular a transitive C¹ Anosov flow on a nonempty compact manifold and a Hölder F have exactly one equilibrium state, and C² flows are a specialisation. By §8.5 and §10.1, g restricted to Λ, a nonempty compact metric space, has hyperbolic local product structure and is transitive; §6.8(b) applies. The flow g|Λ is Bowen–Walters expansive by §6.6, so h_top(g|Λ) < ∞ (ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5), and §4.1 gives the invariance and time-change statements.

**Checks.**

- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map and F = 0, ν_(g,0) is the product of Haar measure with Lebesgue measure on the fibre, of entropy log((3 + √5)/2) for g^1.
- For a hyperbolic periodic orbit of period p, ν_(g,F) is the normalised time measure for every F, with P = (1/p)∫₀^p F along the orbit.
- The lower Gibbs bound at a large scale, on the same periodic orbit O: for ε > 0 choose s_ε ∈ (0, p/2] with d(g^s y, y) < ε for all y ∈ O and |s| ≤ s_ε (uniform continuity of the flow on [−p, p] × O); then B_T(x, ε) contains the arc {g^s x : |s| ≤ s_ε}, of measure 2s_ε/p, for every T, while writing T = kp + r with 0 ≤ r < p gives −TP + ∫₀ᵀ F(g^t x) dt = ∫₀^r F(g^t x) dt − rP ∈ [−2p max|F|, 2p max|F|]. So C_ε = (2s_ε/p) e^(−2p max|F|) works for every ε > 0; for ε above the diameter of O the ball is all of O, of measure 1, and C_ε = e^(−2p max|F|). A statement made only at small scales would say nothing at these ε.
- For F' = F + 5 + L_gψ the equilibrium state is unchanged while the pressure increases by exactly 5.

*Needs:* ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.5 (finite entropy of expansive flows); Layers 4.1, 6.6, 6.8, 8.5, 10.1; Layers 9.1 and 9.2 for the Checks.

### 10.4 Bowen–Margulis and Bowen measures

Prove that a basic set Λ of a C¹ flow (in particular M itself for a transitive Anosov flow on a nonempty compact manifold) has a unique measure of maximal entropy, the Bowen–Margulis measure ν_(g,0), which is ergodic and positive on nonempty relatively open subsets of Λ, and that h_top(g|Λ) > 0 unless Λ is a single periodic orbit (`pos_coverEntropy_of_isFlowBasicSet`); that a basic set of a C¹ diffeomorphism has a unique measure of maximal entropy, Bowen's measure, with h_top > 0 unless Λ is finite (`pos_coverEntropy_of_isBasicSet`); and that for a hyperbolic toral automorphism, ClassicalErgodicTheory's `toralEndomorphism` A with det A = ±1 and no complex eigenvalue of modulus 1, Bowen's measure is Haar measure (`isEquilibriumState_toralEndomorphism_zero_iff`; BowenRuelle1975, Remark 3.4, lines 324–325, and lines 77–78; BowenPeriodicPoints1971, Remark 4.4, lines 421–426, and Proposition 7.1, line 667). Uniqueness is §10.2 and §10.3 with zero potential. Positivity of entropy uses weak specification and expansivity of the restricted system, which §8.4 and §10.1 give through §6.3 for diffeomorphisms (whose third item is the positivity) and §8.5 and §10.1 give through §6.6 for flows: gluing two disjoint orbit segments in all binary patterns produces exponentially many separated orbit segments, unless Λ is a single orbit.

**Checks.**

- For the cat map, Bowen's measure is Haar measure and the entropy is log((3 + √5)/2) > 0.
- For a hyperbolic periodic orbit of a flow, the entropy is 0 and the Bowen–Margulis measure is the time measure: the exception is necessary.
- For the geodesic flow of a closed surface of curvature −1, the Bowen–Margulis measure is the Liouville measure (§11.6), of entropy 1.

*Needs:* Layers 6.3, 6.6, 8.4, 8.5, 9.1, 10.1, 10.2, 10.3.

### Examples

Haar measure for the cat map, the product measure for its constant-roof suspension, and the Liouville measure for hyperbolic surfaces are explicit Bowen–Margulis measures.

## Layer 11: SRB measures of C² flows

Throughout this layer g is a C² flow on a compact manifold M whose generator X is a C² vector field (the C² flows of BowenRuelle1975, line 93), and norms, distances, Jacobians and the volume m (`TauCeti.riemannianVolume`) are those of a fixed smooth Riemannian metric (`Bundle.ContMDiffRiemannianMetric`; BowenRuelle1975, line 92), distances being truncated at 1 as in the Conventions. Hyperbolic sets, basic sets and attractors are those of Layers 7 and 10, which do not depend on the metric (§7.3).

### 11.1 The geometric potential

Define, for a hyperbolic set Λ of g and x ∈ Λ, the unstable Jacobian λ_t(x) as the absolute value of the determinant of Dg^t : E^u_x → E^u_(g^t x) computed in orthonormal bases of the inner products of the smooth metric, and the geometric potential φ^u(x) = −d/dt log λ_t(x) at t = 0, which exists because t ↦ Dg^t restricted to E^u_x is C¹ and the metric is smooth (BowenRuelle1975, Section 4, lines 346–358). Concretely, let E^u_x be the span of the tangent vectors v ∈ T_xM with |Dg^(−t)v| → 0 as t → ∞ (`flowUnstableSubspace`; at x ∈ Λ this set is already a subspace and equals E^u_x, by §7.4); let λ_t(x) be the square root of the ratio of the Gram determinant of the images under Dg^t of a basis of E^u_x to the Gram determinant of that basis (`unstableJacobian`; a change of basis multiplies both determinants by the same square, so the ratio does not depend on the basis, and it is the absolute determinant above); and let φ^u(x) = −d/dt log λ_t(x) at t = 0 (`geometricPotential`). These formulas define functions on all of M; only their values on Λ are used.

Its API:

- λ_(t+s)(x) = λ_t(g^s x) λ_s(x), and ∫₀ᵀ φ^u(g^t x) dt = −log λ_T(x) (BowenRuelle1975, lines 351–357).
- φ^u is continuous and Hölder continuous: x ↦ E^u_x is Hölder (§8.7), and φ^u(x) is a C¹ function of the plane E^u_x because the generator is C² and the metric smooth (BowenRuelle1975, Lemma 4.1, lines 360–363).
- For a second smooth Riemannian metric, let ρ(x) be the ratio of its volume element on E^u_x to that of the first, ψ = log ρ, and λ'_t, φ'^u the new unstable Jacobian and potential. Then λ'_t(x) = λ_t(x) ρ(g^t x)/ρ(x), so ψ is differentiable along the flow with continuous derivative L_gψ and φ'^u = φ^u − L_gψ; by §4.1 the two potentials have the same pressure and the same equilibrium states (BowenRuelle1975, Remark 4.5, lines 409–413). Rescaling time by a replaces φ^u by aφ^u.

**Checks.**

- For the geodesic flow of a closed surface of curvature −1, λ_t = e^t and φ^u = −1.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map A, let a = log((3 + √5)/2) and take on ℝ × ℝ² the metric ds² + e^(2as) du² + e^(−2as) dv², where u and v are the coordinates along the unstable and stable eigenlines of A; it is a⁻² times the pull-back of Tau Ceti's metric e^(2z) dx² + e^(−2z) dy² + dz² of the model geometry Sol (supplier contracts) under (x, y, z) = (au, av, as), and the constant factor changes neither λ_t nor φ^u. It is invariant under the lattice translations and under (s, w) ↦ (s − 1, Aw), so it descends to the mapping torus; it is smooth but not flat, and its volume form is ds du dv. For it λ_t = e^(at) on E^u and φ^u = −a ≈ −0.9624, so P(φ^u) = h_top + φ^u = 0, consistent with §11.5.
- Rescaling that metric by e^(2u(s)) with u(s) = 0.1 sin(2πs) gives λ'_t = e^(at + u(s+t) − u(s)) and φ'^u = −a − 0.2π cos(2πs), which differs from −a by the flow derivative of ψ = u, as the metric-change item requires, and the pressure stays 0. A merely continuous conformal factor is not admissible: with u(s) = |sin(2πs)| the right and left t-derivatives of log λ'_t at s = 0 are a + 2π and a − 2π, and φ'^u is undefined there.
- The sign is fixed by ∫₀ᵀ φ^u = −log λ_T: the opposite convention would make P(φ^u) = 2 h_top in the previous example instead of 0.
- The square root in the Gram formula is needed: for the metric of the second Check and the unit vector e_u along the unstable line at x, the Gram determinants of Dg^t e_u and of e_u are e^(2at) and 1, so λ_t = e^(at); without the square root the potential would be −2a ≈ −1.925 and P(φ^u) = log((3 + √5)/2) − 2a = −a < 0, contradicting P(φ^u) = 0 for this transitive Anosov flow (§11.5).

*Needs:* Mathlib `mfderiv`, `Bundle.ContMDiffRiemannianMetric`; Layers 4.1 (coboundary invariance, for the metric change), 7.2, 7.5, 8.7.

### 11.2 The Grassmann bundle and the lifted unstable bundle

For 0 ≤ q ≤ dim M let G_q(TM) be the Grassmann bundle of q-dimensional subspaces of the tangent spaces of M, with projection π(x, E) = x; as a topological space over M, compact over compact sets, it is GeometricCurrents G6's. Prove:

- (i) G_q(TM) is a smooth manifold of dimension dim M + q(dim M − q), compact when M is, and π is a smooth submersion. Over the domain of a chart of M, the chart coordinates of x together with the graph coordinates of E read in the chart, over a q-dimensional coordinate subspace, form a chart (on the fibre these are the graph charts of `TauCeti.finiteGrassmannian.graphChart`, smooth by GeneralizedCohomology G4.0b); if a chart change has derivative with block form (A B; C D) for the two splittings, the plane with graph matrix L goes to the plane with graph matrix (C + DL)(A + BL)⁻¹, a smooth function of the base point and of L where A + BL is invertible.
- (ii) For a flow g generated by a C² vector field X, the maps G_q g^t(x, E) = (g^t x, Dg^t_x E) form a C¹ flow on G_q(TM), generated by a C¹ vector field covering X, and Φ(x, E) = −d/dt log Jac(Dg^t|E) at t = 0, the Jacobian taken in orthonormal bases of the smooth metric, is a C¹ function on G_q(TM) whose integral along the G_q g-orbit of (x, E) over [0, T] is −log Jac(Dg^T|E); on the planes E^u_x it is the geometric potential of §11.1.
- (iii) If Λ is a hyperbolic set of g with dim E^u_x = q for every x ∈ Λ (for instance the subset of a hyperbolic set where this dimension is q, which is closed, open and invariant by §7.5), then Λ* = {(x, E^u_x) : x ∈ Λ} is a hyperbolic set of the flow G_q g, with stable dimension dim E^s_x + q(dim M − q) and unstable dimension q at each point (BowenRuelle1975, Lemma A.3, lines 702–715).

For (i), glue the products of chart domains with the fibre charts along these transition maps. For (ii), (t, x) ↦ Dg^t_x is C¹ because X is C², and Φ is a C¹ expression in the derivative of X and the metric; the integral identity is the cocycle property of Jacobians, as in §11.1. For (iii), as in BowenRuelle1975, lines 706–715: Λ* is compact by continuity of E^u (§7.5) and invariant, and the generator of G_q g does not vanish on it because it covers X. In the charts of §8.3 a plane E near E^u_x is the graph of a linear map L : E^u_x → E^s_x ⊕ ℝX(x), and Dg^t carries it to the graph of (Dg^t on E^s ⊕ ℝX) ∘ L ∘ (Dg^t on E^u)⁻¹, of norm at most C e^(−κt)|L| since Dg^t is bounded on E^s ⊕ ℝX and expands E^u; so the fibre directions at E^u_x join the lifted stable directions in the stable bundle, the tangent planes of the unstable plaques form the unstable bundle, and the dimensions dim E^s + q(dim M − q), q and 1 add up to dim G_q(TM).

**Checks.**

- For q = 0 and for q = dim M every fibre is a point, G_q(TM) is a copy of M and Λ* a copy of Λ; the dimension formula gives dim M in both cases.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map with the metric of §11.1 and the orthonormal frame e_s = ∂_s, e_u = e^(−as)∂_u, e_v = e^(as)∂_v, Dg^T multiplies e_u by e^(aT), e_v by e^(−aT) and fixes e_s, so for q = 1 the line spanned by e_u + b e_v + c e_s goes to the line spanned by e_u + b e^(−2aT) e_v + c e^(−aT) e_s: in the fibre ℝP² the coordinates (b, c) at E^u contract at the rates 2a and a. The stable dimension of Λ* is 1 + 2 = 3, the unstable 1 and the flow 1, and 3 + 1 + 1 = 5 = 3 + 1·(3 − 1) = dim G_1(TM); on E^u, Φ = −a, the geometric potential of §11.1.
- The lift {(x, E^s_x)} of the stable bundle is invariant as well, but there the line spanned by e_v + b e_u + c e_s goes to the line spanned by e_v + b e^(2aT) e_u + c e^(aT) e_s: its fibre directions expand, its unstable dimension is 3 and not 1, and it is not the set to which §11.3 applies.
- If the generator is only C¹, x ↦ Dg^t_x is only continuous, G_q g is only a continuous flow and Φ only a continuous function; hyperbolic sets (§7.2) and item (a) of §11.3 need a C¹ flow and a C¹ function, which is why this layer assumes a C² generator.

*Needs:* GeometricCurrents G6 (the topological Grassmann bundle); GeneralizedCohomology G4.0b (smooth graph charts on the fibre); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.finiteGrassmannian`, `TauCeti.finiteGrassmannian.graphChart`; Mathlib `extChartAt`, `mfderiv`, `ContMDiff`; Layers 7.2, 7.5, 8.3, 11.1.

### 11.3 Bounded distortion of unstable Jacobians

Prove, for a hyperbolic set Λ of g, with q = dim E^u_x at the point x ∈ Λ under consideration:

- (a) Bowen property near a hyperbolic set: for a hyperbolic set Λ' of a C¹ flow g' on a compact manifold M' with a smooth Riemannian metric (Λ for g, or the set Λ* of §11.2 for G_q g), a C¹ function ψ on M' and θ > 0, there is δ > 0 such that |∫₀ᵀ ψ(g'^t y) dt − ∫₀ᵀ ψ(g'^t x) dt| < θ whenever x ∈ Λ', y ∈ M', T ≥ 0 and d(g'^t y, g'^t x) < δ for 0 ≤ t ≤ T (BowenRuelle1975, Lemma A.2, lines 681–701, stated for C¹ flows). Here y need not lie in Λ', unlike §6.7, and the C² hypothesis of this layer is not used.
- (b) Distortion of planes in unstable cones: for θ > 0 there are δ > 0 and η > 0 such that, for x ∈ Λ, T ≥ 0, y ∈ M with d(g^t y, g^t x) < δ for 0 ≤ t ≤ T, and a q-dimensional subspace E* ⊆ T_yM whose images Dg^t E*, read in the charts of §8.3 at g^t x (BowenRuelle1975, A.1), are graphs of linear maps E^u_(g^t x) → E^s_(g^t x) ⊕ ℝX(g^t x) of norm less than η for every t ∈ [0, T], one has e^(−θ) ≤ Jac(Dg^T|E*)/λ_T(x) ≤ e^θ. Both δ and η depend on θ.
- (c) Chart slices: for θ > 0 there is ε > 0 such that for x ∈ Λ, an integer n ≥ 0 and a point y = φ_x(v) of the chart φ_x of §8.3 at x whose chart iterates stay in the ε-balls up to time n, the tangent plane E* at y to the slice φ_x(v + E^u_x(ε)) satisfies the hypotheses of (b) with T = n, hence e^(−θ) ≤ Jac(Dg^n|E*)/λ_n(x) ≤ e^θ (BowenRuelle1975, Lemma A.4, lines 718–733).

For (a), the chart of §8.3 at x gives a point z on the weak stable plaque of x with time shift |s| ≤ C₀δ and d(g'^k y, g'^k z) ≤ C₂δγ^(n−k), and the integrals are compared step by step, the time-one integrals of ψ being Lipschitz because g' and ψ are C¹ and M' is compact. For (b), apply (a) to the hyperbolic set Λ* of the C¹ flow G_q g on the compact manifold G_q(TM) (§11.2(iii)), with any smooth Riemannian metric on G_q(TM), and the C¹ function Φ of §11.2(ii), whose integrals over [0, T] along the orbits of E^u_x and E* are −log λ_T(x) and −log Jac(Dg^T|E*); the cone hypothesis, read in the charts of §8.3 and the graph coordinates of §11.2(i), keeps the orbit of E* within the δ of (a) of that of E^u_x (BowenRuelle1975, lines 729–738). For (c), the closeness of the iterated slice planes follows from the chart estimates (A.3)–(A.5) of BowenRuelle1975, lines 655–676.

**Checks.**

- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map with the metric of §11.1, x = y and E* spanned by e_u + b e_v, where e_u and e_v are the unit vectors along the unstable and stable lines at x: Dg^T E* is spanned by e^(aT)e_u + b e^(−aT)e_v, so Jac(Dg^T|E*)/λ_T(x) = (1 + b²e^(−4aT))^(1/2)/(1 + b²)^(1/2), which decreases from 1 to (1 + b²)^(−1/2) as T → ∞. For b = 0.1 the limit 0.99504 is below e^(−0.001) ≈ 0.99900, although the base orbits coincide: the plane tolerance η must depend on θ, and the ratio stays in [e^(−θ), e^θ] for all T ≥ 0 exactly when b² ≤ e^(2θ) − 1.
- For E* = E^u_y with y ∈ Λ the ratio compares λ_T(y) with λ_T(x), which is the Bowen property of φ^u (§6.7).
- Without the C² hypothesis φ^u need not be Hölder and the ratios can grow without bound along long orbit segments.

### 11.4 The volume lemmas

Prove, for a hyperbolic set Λ of g with the Riemannian volume m of the smooth metric (`TauCeti.riemannianVolume`) and closed flow Bowen balls B_x(ε, T) in M: (a) for small ε there is c_ε ≥ 1 with c_ε⁻¹ ≤ m(B_x(ε, T)) λ_T(x) ≤ c_ε for all x ∈ Λ and T > 0; (b) for small ε, δ there is d > 0 with m(B_y(δ, T)) ≥ d·m(B_x(ε, T)) whenever x ∈ Λ, y ∈ B_x(ε, T) and T > 0 (BowenRuelle1975, Lemmas 4.2–4.3, lines 364–375, proved in the Appendix A.5–A.6, lines 740–808). ### 11.5 The pressure of the geometric potential

Prove, for a basic set Λ of g: (a) P(g|Λ, φ^u) is the limsup as T → ∞ of (1/T) log m(B_Λ(ε, T)) for small ε, where B_Λ(ε, T) is the union of the B_x(ε, T) over x ∈ Λ, and hence P(g|Λ, φ^u) ≤ 0; (b) if the local stable set W^s_Λ(ε) of §10.1 has positive volume, in particular if Λ is an attractor, in particular if g is a transitive Anosov flow on a nonempty compact manifold with Λ = M, then P(g|Λ, φ^u) = 0 and the equilibrium state μ⁺ = ν_(g,φ^u) satisfies hμ⁺(g^1) = −∫φ^u dμ⁺ (BowenRuelle1975, Proposition 4.4, lines 377–407). For (a), let E be a maximal (T, δ)-separated set of Λ in the sense of the Conventions (pairwise disjoint open d_T-balls of radius δ about its points). Distinct points of E are at d_T-distance at least δ, and E is 2δ-spanning for Λ, since the open δ-ball of any further point of Λ meets that of some x ∈ E. Hence, for δ ≤ 3ε, the closed balls B_x(δ/3, T), x ∈ E, are pairwise disjoint and lie in B_Λ(ε, T), and B_Λ(ε, T) ⊆ ∪_(x∈E) B_x(2δ + ε, T); §11.4(a) at the radii δ/3 and 2δ + ε, both chosen small, turns the two volume bounds into multiples of the partition sum of φ^u over E, with constants independent of T, and ClassicalErgodicTheory §5.5's separated-set form of the flow pressure gives (a). (BowenRuelle1975, lines 386–390, separate classically by d_T(x, y) > δ, for which a maximal set is δ-spanning and the radius δ + ε suffices.) For (b), B_Λ(ε, T) ⊇ W^s_Λ(ε) for every T (§10.1), so m(B_Λ(ε, T)) ≥ m(W^s_Λ(ε)) > 0 and the limsup of (a) is at least 0; for an attractor W^s_Λ(ε) contains the closed α-neighbourhood of Λ (§10.1), which has positive volume.

**Checks.**

- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map with the metric of §11.1, P(φ^u) = log((3 + √5)/2) − log((3 + √5)/2) = 0 and μ⁺ is the product measure; another smooth metric changes φ^u by a flow coboundary (§11.1) and leaves P(φ^u) = 0 and μ⁺ unchanged.
- For the geodesic flow of a hyperbolic surface, P(φ^u) = h_top − 1 = 0, so h_top = 1.
- For a hyperbolic periodic orbit with nonzero unstable bundle (not an attractor), P(φ^u) = −(1/p) log λ_p < 0, consistent with (a) and with the strict inequality for non-attractors; its W^s_Λ(ε) has volume 0 (§10.1).
- The covering radius is 2δ + ε, not δ + ε. For the attracting circle Λ = {r = 0} of §10.1's Check, on which d_T is the circle distance, take an integer m ≥ 2, δ = 1/(3.9m) and ε = 1.01δ: the set E = {(j/m, 0) : 0 ≤ j < m} has pairwise disjoint δ-balls in Λ and is maximal, every point of Λ lying within 1.95δ < 2δ of E. The point y = (1/(2m), ε) lies in W^s_Λ(ε) ⊆ B_Λ(ε, T) for every T > 0, but its distance at time 0 to the nearest points (0, 0) and (1/m, 0) of E is (1.95² + 1.01²)^(1/2)δ ≈ 2.196δ > δ + ε = 2.01δ; it lies in B_(0,0)(2δ + ε, T), of radius 3.01δ, for every T.

### 11.6 The SRB theorem

Prove (`srb_of_isFlowAttractor`) that if Λ is an attractor of g with basin W^s_Λ, then for m-almost every x ∈ W^s_Λ and every continuous u : M → ℝ, (1/T)∫₀ᵀ u(g^t x) dt → ∫u dμ⁺ as T → ∞, where μ⁺ = ν_(g,φ^u) is the equilibrium state of the geometric potential (BowenRuelle1975, Theorem 5.1, lines 421–469). In particular (`srb_of_isFlowBasicSet_univ`), when g is a transitive Anosov flow on a nonempty compact manifold, m-almost every point of M is generic for μ⁺; μ⁺ is the unique g-invariant probability measure whose generic points have positive volume, hence the unique one whose generic points have full volume; and every g-invariant probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to m equals μ⁺ (BowenRuelle1975, Remark 5.2, lines 470–472). The proof is Bowen–Ruelle's (BowenRuelle1975, lines 426–469). Fix u, δ > 0, and ε small for §§11.4–11.5 and such that |u(a) − u(b)| < δ/4 whenever d(a, b) ≤ ε, and let α be as in §10.1, so that the closed α-neighbourhood B_Λ(α) of Λ lies in W^s_Λ(ε). For n ≥ N choose successively finite sets S_n of points of Λ whose time averages up to time n deviate from ∫u dμ⁺ by more than δ/2, maximal subject to all the balls B_x(ε, k), x ∈ S_k, N ≤ k ≤ n, being pairwise disjoint. A point y ∈ B_Λ(α) whose average up to time n deviates by more than δ lies in W^s_z(ε) ⊆ B_z(ε, n) for some z ∈ Λ, whose average then deviates by more than δ/2, so by maximality B_z(ε, n) meets some B_x(ε, k) with x ∈ S_k, N ≤ k ≤ n, and d_k(y, x) ≤ d_k(y, z) + d_k(z, x) ≤ ε + 2ε: these points are covered by the balls B_x(3ε, k). The volume lemma (§11.4(a)) at radius 3ε, P(φ^u) = 0 (§11.5) and the lower Gibbs bound (§10.3) at radius ε bound the volume of the cover by a constant times the μ⁺-measure of the disjoint union of the balls B_x(ε, k), which lies in the set of points whose average up to some time k ≥ N deviates by more than δ/4; that μ⁺-measure tends to 0 as N → ∞ by flow-ergodicity of μ⁺ and the pointwise ergodic theorem (ProbabilisticAndMetricNumberTheory §4.1 applied to g^1 and u_1). So the points of B_Λ(α) whose averages deviate by more than δ infinitely often form an m-null set; the corresponding subset of the basin is invariant and every point of the basin enters B_Λ(α) (§10.1), so it is m-null as well, the g^t being diffeomorphisms; a countable dense set of u finishes.

**Checks.**

- For the geodesic flow of a closed surface of curvature −1, φ^u = −1 is constant, so μ⁺ is the measure of maximal entropy (equilibrium states are unchanged by adding constants, ClassicalErgodicTheory §8.2), that is the Bowen–Margulis measure of §10.4; since the Liouville measure is invariant and absolutely continuous (KatokHasselblatt1995, Chapter 17), μ⁺ is also the Liouville measure: Bowen–Margulis, SRB and Liouville measures coincide.
- For the constant-roof suspension of the cat map, μ⁺ is the product of Haar and fibre Lebesgue measure. For the metric of §11.1, whose volume form is ds du dv, this is the normalised Riemannian volume itself; for that metric rescaled by e^(2u(s)) with u(s) = 0.1 sin(2πs) the volume has density proportional to e^(3u(s)), which the flow does not preserve, so for that metric μ⁺ is not the normalised volume but lies in its measure class. For every smooth metric Lebesgue-almost every orbit equidistributes to μ⁺.
- Two distinct measures cannot both have generic points of positive volume on a transitive Anosov flow, since a common generic point would force equality; the uniqueness clause is that observation.
- The covering radius is 3ε, not 2ε. For the attracting circle of §10.1's Check and ε < 1/4, the distance from a point (θ, r) to the orbit of (θ', 0) is largest at time 0, so every closed Bowen ball B_z(ε, k) about a point z of Λ is the closed ε-disc about z. For z = (0, 0), x = (2ε, 0) and y = (0, ε) ∈ W^s_z(ε), the balls about z and x meet at (ε, 0), while d(y, x) = √5 ε ≈ 2.236ε > 2ε; the orbits of y and x stay within √5 ε ≤ 3ε of each other.
