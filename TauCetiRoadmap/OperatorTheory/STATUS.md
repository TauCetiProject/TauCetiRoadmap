### Named results

- **The spectral theorem for bounded normal operators** — a normal operator is the integral `a = ∫ z dP(z)` of the coordinate function against a unique projection-valued measure on its spectrum. See [`ContinuousLinearMap.borelCalculus_restrict_id`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/BorelCalculus/Basic.html#ContinuousLinearMap.borelCalculus_restrict_id).
- **Polar decomposition for bounded operators** — every bounded operator between complete Hilbert spaces factors as `T = U|T|`, with `U` the unique partial isometry vanishing on `ker T`, in any dimension. See [`ContinuousLinearMap.polarFactor_comp_modulus`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Polar/Bounded.html#ContinuousLinearMap.polarFactor_comp_modulus).
- **The Gohberg–Krein theory of continuous Toeplitz operators** — for a continuous symbol `a`, `T_a` is Fredholm exactly when `a` never vanishes, its index is minus the winding number, and it is invertible exactly when, in addition, that winding number is zero. See [`TauCeti.index_toeplitz_toLp`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Fredholm.html#TauCeti.index_toeplitz_toLp) and [`TauCeti.isUnit_toeplitz_toLp_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Fredholm.html#TauCeti.isUnit_toeplitz_toLp_iff).
- **Rosenblum's theorem** — a bounded operator intertwining two self-adjoint operators with disjoint spectra, bounded or not, is zero, so the Sylvester equation has at most one bounded solution. See [`LinearPMap.Intertwines.eq_zero_of_disjoint_spectrum`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/Rosenblum.html#LinearPMap.Intertwines.eq_zero_of_disjoint_spectrum).
- **The pairwise `π/2` Davis–Kahan `sin Θ` theorem** — if selected spectra of symmetric `A` and `B` are `δ` apart, then `δ·N(sin Θ) ≤ (π/2)·N(B − A)` in every unitarily invariant norm, in finite dimension. See [`TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.mul_map_starProjection_comp_starProjection_le_pi_div_two_mul_map_sub`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm/SinTheta/Pairwise.html#TauCeti.UnitarilyInvariantSeminorm.mul_map_starProjection_comp_starProjection_le_pi_div_two_mul_map_sub).

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Block Toeplitz operators** — [the compression of multiplication by a matrix-valued `L∞` symbol](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Hardy/Block/Toeplitz.html#TauCeti.blockToeplitz) to `r` copies of H². The symbol map is isometric, and a block Brown–Halmos theorem holds. For continuous symbols the semicommutators are compact. Matrix-symbol Fredholm theory and block finite sections build on it.
- **The winding number of a nowhere-zero circle function** — [defined](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Fourier/Winding.html#ContinuousMap.windingNumber) as the circle degree of the normalized symbol. It classifies symbols up to homotopy and measures the Toeplitz index.
- **Yosida approximants of a self-adjoint operator** — bounded, self-adjoint and commuting, they [converge to the operator on its domain](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/LinearPMap/Yosida.html#IsSelfAdjoint.tendsto_symmetricYosidaApproximation_apply) and are the route to the generated unitary group.

## The frontier

- **The domain-aware `π/2` Sylvester bound** — the last item of spectral-subspace perturbation; the finite pairwise bound, Rosenblum uniqueness and the domain-aware Sylvester equation exist.
- **Fredholm theory of matrix Toeplitz symbols** — a continuous matrix symbol gives a Fredholm operator exactly when it is pointwise invertible, with index minus the winding of its determinant. Block carriers and compact semicommutators are in place. The block shift-algebra comparison, the finite Hilbert-product index theorem and the Smith-form factorization remain.
- **The spectral measure of an unbounded self-adjoint operator** — transport the Cayley transform's spectral measure to `ℝ`, showing the point `1` carries no mass, then prove the resolvent formula. Self-adjoint Part E and most of the unbounded-operators roadmap wait on this.
