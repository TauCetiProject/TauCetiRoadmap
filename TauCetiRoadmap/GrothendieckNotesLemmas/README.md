# Roadmap: general lemmas in commutative algebra, topology and linear algebra

**Summary.** Twenty-eight general targets on Mathlib's native carriers, in five independent
layers. Layer 1: binomial coefficients modulo a prime (`p ^ e ∣ n` iff `p` divides every
`n.choose k` with `0 < k < p ^ e`, for `p` prime; `(p ^ a − 1).choose k ≡ (−1) ^ k mod p` for
`k ≤ p ^ a − 1`), the kernel of the Hasse derivatives of orders `0 < k < p ^ e` over a commutative
ring of prime characteristic `p` and their linearity over `R[X ^ (p ^ e)]`, the criterion
`IsCoprime (X − C a) q ↔ IsUnit (q.eval a)` over a commutative ring, the determinant `1` of the
symmetric Pascal matrix, `(u v) ^ p = u ^ p v ^ p` for an odd prime `p` in a group where
`⁅v, u⁆` commutes with `u` and `v` and `⁅v, u⁆ ^ p = 1`, and the perfectness of the normal
closure of a perfect subgroup. Layer 2: flatness of finite products, bijectivity of
`M ⊗ Π Pᵢ → Π (M ⊗ Pᵢ)` for `M` finitely presented, Noetherian rings as the rings whose finitely
generated modules are finitely presented, the range of `f.rTensor N` in the coordinates of a basis
of `N`, adic completeness for a nilpotent ideal, a retraction of `T ⊗[S] Ω[S⁄R] → Ω[T⁄R]` when `T`
is formally smooth over `S`, rational multiples of an indivisible lattice vector, injective =
divisible over a principal ideal domain with quotients of injectives injective, the associated
primes of `F ⁄ pF` for `F` flat, `(K ⁄ A)[t] ≅ A ⁄ (t)` for `t ≠ 0` over a domain `A` with fraction
field `K`, and finite length of finitely generated torsion modules over a Noetherian ring of Krull
dimension at most one. Layer 3: the Leibniz rule for integer powers of units, and the commutator
of a derivation with a multiplication. Layer 4: the exponential law for the compact-open topology
with `X` locally compact or `R1Space`, and an openness criterion in Noetherian quasi-sober spaces.
Layer 5: right zeros of finite monoids, the Binet–Cauchy and Lagrange identities, traces of odd
powers of a difference of idempotent matrices, common denominators in the Grothendieck group of a
commutative monoid, and simple modules over an algebraically closed field, finite-dimensional over
it, determined up to isomorphism by their traces. Each statement carries its exact hypotheses in
its target below.

## Conventions

- **Characteristic `p`.** Statements in prime characteristic carry `[Fact p.Prime] [CharP R p]`.
  Witness: over `ZMod 3`, `hasseDeriv 1 (X ^ 3) = 3 X ^ 2 = 0` (1.3, Checks).
- **Hasse derivatives** are Mathlib's: `hasseDeriv k (X ^ n) = n.choose k • X ^ (n − k)`, so
  `hasseDeriv 1` is the ordinary derivative and `hasseDeriv 2 (X ^ 2) = 1` in every
  characteristic (1.4, Checks).
- **Commutators** are Mathlib's `⁅a, b⁆ = a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹`; the formula of 1.7 uses `⁅v, u⁆`.
  Witness: in the Heisenberg group, `⁅E₂₃, E₁₂⁆` has corner entry `−1` (1.7, Checks).
- **The Pascal matrix** is indexed from `0`: its `(i, j)` entry is `(i + j).choose i`, with
  `i, j : Fin n`. Witness: for `n = 2` it is `[[1, 1], [1, 2]]` (1.6, Checks).
- **Sums over pairs** in 5.2 run over ordered pairs `(i, j)` of `s × s`, which doubles the sum over
  `i < j`. Witness: `a = (1, 0)`, `b = (0, 1)` give `2` on both sides (5.2, Checks).
- **Associated primes** are Mathlib's `Submodule.IsAssociatedPrime`: a prime equal to the radical
  of `N.colon {x}` for some `x`; no Noetherian hypothesis is attached. Witness: the associated
  prime of `ℤ ⁄ 4` over `ℤ` is `(2)`, the radical of the annihilator `(4)` of `1` (2.9, Checks).
- **Universes.** A statement quantifying over all modules, or asserting injectivity, carries
  `[Small.{v} R]` for modules in universe `v`, as Mathlib's `Module.Baer.of_injective` does.
- **Function spaces** carry the compact-open topology (Mathlib's instance on `C(X, Y)`), and
  `ContinuousMap.curry f x y = f (x, y)`.

## How to read the build

The five layers are independent of one another, and within a layer the only dependencies are
1.1 → 1.3 → 1.4 and 3.1 → 3.2, and, in 2.8 and 2.9, the later statement on the earlier one.
Layer 1 is arithmetic of binomial coefficients and polynomials in prime characteristic, two
determinantal or group identities that rest on Vandermonde's convolution and on `n.choose 2`, and
perfect subgroups. Layer 2 is
module theory: flatness, finite presentation, injective modules, adic completeness, Kähler
differentials, associated primes, the module `K ⁄ A` and torsion modules of finite length.
Layer 3 extends Mathlib's and Tau Ceti's derivation API. Layer 4 is general topology. Layer 5
collects finite monoids, two identities, traces, Grothendieck groups, and simple modules recognised
by their traces. The smallest targets are 1.5, 1.7, 1.8, 2.1, 2.5, 2.11, 3.1 and 3.2; 1.7, 2.5,
2.11 and the finite-product form of 2.1 follow from the pins in two lines.

## Layer 1: binomial coefficients, Hasse derivatives, a determinant, commutators

### 1.1 Prime powers dividing `n`

Prove `Nat.pow_dvd_iff_forall_dvd_choose`: for `p` prime and all `e, n`, `p ^ e ∣ n` if and only
if `p ∣ n.choose k` for every `0 < k < p ^ e` (own statement, a consequence of Lucas's theorem,
Lucas 1878). Route: Lucas's theorem `Choose.choose_modEq_prod_range_choose` in both
directions. If `p ^ e ∣ n`, the lowest `e` base-`p` digits of `n` vanish while some digit of
`0 < k < p ^ e` among them does not, so a factor `0.choose d` with `d > 0` kills the product.
Otherwise let `j < e` index the lowest nonzero digit `d` of `n`: then
`n.choose (p ^ j) ≡ d ≢ 0`. *Needs:* Mathlib `Choose.choose_modEq_prod_range_choose`,
`Choose.choose_modEq_choose_mod_mul_choose_div`.

**Checks.**

- `p = 2`, `n = 6`: `2 ∣ 6` and `6.choose 1 = 6` is even; `4 ∤ 6`, and indeed
  `6.choose 2 = 15` is odd.
- The bound is strict: with `k ≤ p ^ e` the right side would fail at `n = p ^ e`, since
  `(p ^ e).choose (p ^ e) = 1`, although `p ^ e ∣ p ^ e`.
- Primality is needed: for `p = 4`, `e = 1`, `n = 4`, `4 ∣ 4` but `4.choose 2 = 6` is not
  divisible by `4`.

### 1.2 The binomial coefficients of `p ^ a − 1`

Prove `Nat.choose_pow_sub_one_modEq_neg_one_pow`: for `p` prime and `k ≤ p ^ a − 1`,
`(p ^ a − 1).choose k ≡ (−1) ^ k [ZMOD p]`; in particular `p` divides none of them (own
statement, from Lucas's theorem). Route: Lucas's
theorem, every base-`p` digit of `p ^ a − 1` being `p − 1`, and `(p − 1).choose j ≡ (−1) ^ j`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `Choose.choose_modEq_choose_mod_mul_choose_div`.

**Checks.**

- `p = 3`, `a = 1`: `2.choose k = 1, 2, 1`, congruent to `1, −1, 1` modulo `3`.
- `p = 2`, `a = 2`: `3.choose k = 1, 3, 3, 1`, all odd, as `(−1) ^ k` is odd.
- The hypothesis `k ≤ p ^ a − 1` is needed: for `k = p ^ a` the coefficient is `0 ≢ ±1`; and the
  neighbour `p ^ a + 1` fails, since `4.choose 1 = 4 ≡ 1`, not `−1`, modulo `3`.

### 1.3 The kernel of the Hasse derivatives in characteristic `p`

Prove `Polynomial.forall_hasseDeriv_eq_zero_iff_exists_expand`: for a commutative ring `R` of prime
characteristic `p` and `f : R[X]`, `hasseDeriv k f = 0` for every `0 < k < p ^ e` if and only if
`f = expand R (p ^ e) g` for some `g` (own statement). Route: the coefficient of `X ^ (n − k)` in
`hasseDeriv k f` is `n.choose k • f.coeff n`; a binomial coefficient prime to `p` is a unit of `R`,
so the condition says that `p ^ e` divides every exponent of `f` (1.1). *Needs:* Mathlib
`Polynomial.hasseDeriv`, `Polynomial.expand`; 1.1.

**Checks.**

- Over `ZMod 3`, `f = X ^ 3`, `e = 1`: `hasseDeriv 1 f = 3 X ^ 2 = 0` and
  `hasseDeriv 2 f = 3 X = 0`, and `f = expand 3 X`.
- Over `ZMod 3`, `f = X ^ 4`: `hasseDeriv 1 f = 4 X ^ 3 = X ^ 3 ≠ 0`, and `4` is not a multiple of
  `3`.
- The bound is strict: `hasseDeriv 3 (X ^ 3) = 1 ≠ 0`, so an inclusive bound `k ≤ p ^ e` would
  exclude `X ^ 3 = expand 3 X`; and in characteristic `0` the statement fails
  (`hasseDeriv 1 (X ^ 3) = 3 X ^ 2 ≠ 0` over `ℚ`).

### 1.4 Linearity over polynomials in `X ^ (p ^ e)`

Prove `Polynomial.hasseDeriv_expand_mul`: in prime characteristic `p`, for `k < p ^ e`,
`hasseDeriv k (expand R (p ^ e) f * g) = expand R (p ^ e) f * hasseDeriv k g`. In particular
`hasseDeriv k` maps multiples of `X ^ (p ^ e)` to multiples of `X ^ (p ^ e)` and descends to
`R[X] ⧸ (X ^ (p ^ e))` (own statement). Route: the Leibniz rule `Polynomial.hasseDeriv_mul` and 1.3.
*Needs:* Mathlib `Polynomial.hasseDeriv_mul`, `Polynomial.expand`; 1.3.

**Checks.**

- Over `ZMod 2`, `e = 1`, `k = 1`, `f = g = X`: `hasseDeriv 1 (X ^ 3) = 3 X ^ 2 = X ^ 2`, and
  `X ^ 2 · hasseDeriv 1 X = X ^ 2`.
- The bound is needed: for `k = p ^ e = 2`, `f = X`, `g = 1`, `hasseDeriv 2 (X ^ 2) = 1` while the
  right side is `X ^ 2 · 0 = 0`.
- Characteristic `p` is needed: over `ℚ`, `hasseDeriv 1 (X ^ 2 · 1) = 2 X ≠ 0`.

### 1.5 Coprimality with `X − a`

Prove `Polynomial.isCoprime_X_sub_C_iff`: over a commutative ring, `IsCoprime (X − C a) q` if and
only if `q.eval a` is a unit (own statement). Two consequences follow by specialisation and are part
of this target: at `a = 0`, `X` and `q` are coprime iff `q.coeff 0` is a unit; for `q = X − C b`,
the coprimality of `X − C a` and `X − C b` is equivalent to `a − b` being a unit, which completes
Mathlib's one-directional `Polynomial.isCoprime_X_sub_C_of_isUnit_sub`. Route: write
`q = q.eval a + (X − C a) h`; conversely evaluate a Bézout relation at `a`. *Needs:* Mathlib
`Polynomial.eval`, `IsCoprime`.

**Checks.**

- Over `ℤ`, `a = 1`, `q = X + 1`: `q.eval 1 = 2` is not a unit, and indeed `(X − 1, X + 1)`
  contains `2` and `ℤ[X] ⧸ (X − 1, X + 1) ≅ ℤ ⁄ 2 ≠ 0`.
- The condition is "unit", not "nonzero": the same example has `q.eval 1 = 2 ≠ 0`.
- Over `ℤ`, `a = 0`, `q = X + 1`: `q.coeff 0 = 1`, and `(X + 1) − X = 1` is a Bézout relation.

### 1.6 The symmetric Pascal determinant

Prove `Matrix.det_pascal`: over a commutative ring, the matrix `((i + j).choose i)_{i, j < n}` has
determinant `1` (own statement). Route: Vandermonde's convolution writes it as `L Lᵀ` with
`L = (i.choose j)` lower unitriangular. *Needs:* Mathlib `Nat.add_choose_eq`, `Matrix.det`.

**Checks.**

- `n = 2`: `[[1, 1], [1, 2]]`, determinant `1`; `n = 3`: `[[1, 1, 1], [1, 2, 3], [1, 3, 6]]`,
  determinant `1 · 3 − 1 · 3 + 1 · 1 = 1`.
- `n = 0`: the empty matrix has determinant `1`.
- Indexing from `0` matters: the matrix `((i + j).choose i)_{1 ≤ i, j ≤ 2}` is `[[2, 3], [3, 6]]`,
  of determinant `3`.

### 1.7 Odd prime powers of a product with central commutator

In a group, if `d = ⁅v, u⁆ = v u v⁻¹ u⁻¹` commutes with `u` and with `v`, then for an odd prime
`p` with `d ^ p = 1`, `(u v) ^ p = u ^ p v ^ p` (the class-two case of the Hall–Petrescu formula).
This follows in two lines from the pins: Tau Ceti's binomial formula of nilpotency class two (see
Scope) at `n = p` gives `(u v) ^ p = u ^ p v ^ p d ^ (p.choose 2)`, and `p ∣ p.choose 2` by
Mathlib's `Nat.Prime.dvd_choose_self`, as `0 < 2 < p` for `p` odd, so `d ^ (p.choose 2) = 1`.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `Commute.mul_pow_eq_pow_mul_pow_mul_commutatorElement_pow_choose_two`; Mathlib
`Nat.Prime.dvd_choose_self`, `commutatorElement`, `Commute`.

**Checks.**

- The order of the commutator matters in the formula behind the route. In the Heisenberg group of
  upper unitriangular `3 × 3` integer matrices, with `u = 1 + E₁₂` and `v = 1 + E₂₃`, `(u v) ²`
  has corner entry `3`, `u ² v ²` has `4` and `⁅v, u⁆` is central with corner `−1`: `3 = 4 − 1`.
  With `⁅u, v⁆` the formula would predict `5`. For `n = 3` the corners are `6 = 9 − 3`, so over
  `ℤ` (where `d ³ ≠ 1`) `(u v) ³ ≠ u ³ v ³`, while over `ZMod 3` the entries vanish, `d ³ = 1`, and
  `(u v) ³ = 1 = u ³ v ³`, as 1.7 states.
- Commutation of `d` with `u` and `v` is needed: in `𝔖₃` with `u = (1 2)`, `v = (2 3)`,
  `d = ⁅v, u⁆` is a 3-cycle, so `d ³ = 1`, but it does not commute with `u`, and
  `(u v) ³ = 1 ≠ u v = u ³ v ³`.
- `p = 2` is excluded: in the quaternion group `Q₈`, `d = ⁅j, i⁆ = −1` is central with `d ² = 1`,
  but `(i j) ² = −1 ≠ 1 = i ² j ²`.

### 1.8 The normal closure of a perfect subgroup

Prove `Subgroup.isPerfect_normalClosure`: in a group `G`, if a subgroup `H` is perfect
(`Group.IsPerfect H`, that is `⁅H, H⁆ = H`), then so is its normal closure `N`, the smallest normal
subgroup containing `H` (own statement). Route: the commutator subgroup `⁅N, N⁆` is normal, since
`N` is, and contains `⁅H, H⁆ = H`; hence it contains `N`, by minimality of the normal closure.
*Needs:* Mathlib `Group.IsPerfect`, `Subgroup.isPerfect_iff`, `Subgroup.normalClosure`,
`Subgroup.normalClosure_le_normal`.

**Checks.**

- `H = ⊥` is perfect and its normal closure is `⊥`.
- The normal closure can be larger than `H` and is still perfect: in the wreath product
  `(𝔄₅ × 𝔄₅) ⋊ C₂`, the swap conjugates `H = 𝔄₅ × 1` to `1 × 𝔄₅`, and the normal closure is
  `𝔄₅ × 𝔄₅`, perfect, of order `3600 ≠ 60`.
- Perfectness of `H` is needed: in `𝔖₃`, the normal closure of `⟨(1 2)⟩` is `𝔖₃`, whose
  commutator subgroup is `𝔄₃`; and the statement is not about subgroups of a perfect group, since
  `𝔄₄ ≤ 𝔄₅` is not perfect (its commutator subgroup is the Klein group).

### Examples

- `Nat.pow_dvd_iff_forall_dvd_choose` at `p = 5`, `e = 1`, `n = 10`: `10, 45, 120, 210` are all
  multiples of `5`.
- Over `ZMod 2`, the Hasse derivatives of orders `1, 2, 3` all vanish on `X ^ 4 + X ^ 8`.
- 1.8 in `𝔖₅`: the normal closure of the perfect subgroup `𝔄₅` is `𝔄₅` itself; in `GL₃(𝔽₂)`,
  simple of order `168`, every perfect subgroup other than `1` has normal closure the whole group.

`commutatorElement`, `Group.IsPerfect`, `Subgroup.normalClosure`. Tau Ceti:

1.1 → 1.3 → 1.4.

## Layer 2: modules, flatness, finite presentation, injective modules

### 2.1 Flatness of finite products

Over a commutative semiring `R`, for `ι` finite, `Π i, M i` is flat if and only if every `M i`
is. This follows in two lines from the pins: after choosing a `Fintype ι`, Mathlib's
`DirectSum.linearEquivFunOnFintype` identifies `⨁ i, M i` with `Π i, M i`, and
`Module.Flat.directSum_iff`, transported along it by `Module.Flat.of_linearEquiv` in each
direction, is the statement; Tau Ceti's instance `Module.Flat.pi` is its direction from the
factors. Prove `Module.Flat.prod_iff`: `M × N` is flat iff `M` and `N` are (own statement). The
direction from the factors is Tau Ceti's instance `Module.Flat.prod` when `R` is a commutative
ring and `M`, `N` are additive groups; over a commutative semiring it is part of this target. The
converse holds because a direct summand of a flat module is flat (`Module.Flat.of_retract`).
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `Module.Flat.pi`, `Module.Flat.prod`; Mathlib `Module.Flat.of_linearEquiv`,
`Module.Flat.of_retract`, `Module.Flat.directSum_iff`, `DirectSum.linearEquivFunOnFintype`.

**Checks.**

- `ℤ × ℤ ⁄ 2` is not flat over `ℤ`, because its factor `ℤ ⁄ 2` is not (multiplication by `2` is
  injective on `ℤ` but becomes `0` on `ℤ ⁄ 2 ⊗ ℤ`).
- `ι` empty: the product is the zero module, which is flat, and the right side holds vacuously.
- Finiteness is needed for the direction from the factors: over `R = k[x, y₁, y₂, …] ⁄ (x yᵢ)`,
  `k` a field, the annihilator of `x` is `(y₁, y₂, …)`, which is not finitely generated, so the
  ideal `(x)` is not finitely presented, `R` is not coherent, and some power `R ^ A`, for an index
  set `A` depending on `R`, is not flat although `R` is (Stacks, Tag 05CZ).

Not a target: arbitrary products. For a commutative ring `R`, every product of flat `R`-modules is
flat if and only if every power `R ^ A` is flat, if and only if `R` is coherent, that is every
finitely generated ideal is finitely presented (Chase 1960; Stacks, Tag 05CZ). In particular all
products of flat modules are flat over a Noetherian ring (Stacks, Tag 05CY). The direction from
coherence is 2.2 applied to the finitely generated ideals; stating the theorem needs coherent rings,
which this roadmap does not define.

### 2.2 Tensor products with products

Prove `Module.FinitePresentation.piRightHom_bijective`: for `M` finitely presented over a
commutative ring and any family `P` of modules, `TensorProduct.piRightHom` from `M ⊗ Π i, P i` to
`Π i, M ⊗ P i` is bijective (Stacks, Tag 059K, (1) ⇒ (2)). Route: a presentation
`R ^ m → R ^ n → M → 0`, the case of finite free modules, and the five lemma. *Needs:* Mathlib
`TensorProduct.piRightHom`, `Module.FinitePresentation`.

**Checks.**

- `M = R`: both sides are `Π i, P i`.
- `ι` finite: the map is bijective for every `M`, so the statement has content only for infinite
  `ι`.
- Finite presentation is needed: for `M = ℚ` over `ℤ` and `P n = ℤ ⁄ 2 ^ n`, the target is
  `Π n, ℚ ⊗ ℤ ⁄ 2 ^ n = 0`, while `ℚ ⊗ Π n, ℤ ⁄ 2 ^ n ≠ 0` because `(1, 1, 1, …)` has infinite
  order.

### 2.3 Noetherian rings by finite presentation

Prove `isNoetherianRing_iff_forall_finitePresentation`: a ring `R` is Noetherian if and only if
every finitely generated `R`-module in universe `v` is finitely presented, for `R` small in universe
`v` (own statement; the forward direction is Mathlib's `Module.finitePresentation_of_finite`,
Stacks, Tag 00IK). Route: the forward direction is that declaration; conversely, for an ideal `I`,
`Shrink.linearEquiv` puts a copy of `R ⧸ I` in universe `v`, and
`Module.FinitePresentation.fg_ker` applied to the surjection `R → R ⧸ I` makes `I` finitely
generated. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsNoetherianRing`, `Module.Finite`, `Module.FinitePresentation`,
`Module.finitePresentation_of_finite`, `Module.FinitePresentation.fg_ker`, `Shrink.linearEquiv`.

**Checks.**

- `R = ℤ`: every finitely generated abelian group is `ℤ ^ r ⊕ ⨁ ℤ ⁄ dᵢ`, presented by a diagonal
  matrix.
- `R = k[x₁, x₂, …]` is not Noetherian, and `R ⧸ (x₁, x₂, …) ≅ k` is finitely generated but not
  finitely presented.
- The universe hypothesis is what lets the converse form `R ⧸ I` in universe `v`; without it the
  right side quantifies over modules among which the cyclic module `R ⧸ I` need not occur.

### 2.4 The range of `rTensor` in coordinates

Prove `TensorProduct.mem_range_rTensor_iff_equivFinsuppOfBasisRight`: for a basis `𝒞` of `N` and
`f : M →ₗ[R] P`, an element `x` of `P ⊗ N` lies in the range of `f.rTensor N` if and only if every
coordinate `TensorProduct.equivFinsuppOfBasisRight 𝒞 x i ∈ P` lies in the range of `f` (own
statement). Route: in the coordinates of `𝒞`, `f.rTensor N` is `Finsupp.mapRange f`. *Needs:*
Mathlib `TensorProduct.equivFinsuppOfBasisRight`, `LinearMap.rTensor`, `Module.Basis`.

**Checks.**

- `R = ℤ`, `f = 2 •` on `ℤ`, `N = ℤ ²` with its standard basis: `(2, 4)` is in the range and
  `(2, 3)` is not.
- `f` surjective: every `x` is in the range; `f = 0`: only `x = 0`.
- The basis is of the right factor `N`: the coordinates live in `P`, the codomain of `f`; the
  statement says nothing about a basis of `P`.

### 2.5 Completeness for a nilpotent ideal

Every module is `I`-adically complete when the ideal `I` is nilpotent. This follows in two lines
from Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.isAdicComplete_of_pow_smul_eq_bot`, which asks `I ^ k • ⊤ = ⊥`: a
nilpotent `I` has `I ^ k = ⊥` for some `k`, and `⊥ • ⊤ = ⊥`. Behind it, `M` is Hausdorff and every
Cauchy sequence is eventually constant. *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.isAdicComplete_of_pow_smul_eq_bot`; Mathlib `IsAdicComplete`, `IsNilpotent`.

**Checks.**

- `R = ℤ ⁄ 4`, `I = (2)`: `I ² = 0`, and `ℤ ⁄ 4` is `(2)`-adically complete.
- `I = 0` gives the trivial case.
- Nilpotence is needed: `(p)` in `ℤ` is not nilpotent, and `ℤ` is not `(p)`-adically complete,
  since its completion `ℤ_p` is uncountable.

### 2.6 The split first fundamental sequence

Prove `KaehlerDifferential.exists_retraction_mapBaseChange`: for `R → S → T` with `T` formally
smooth over `S` (`[Algebra.FormallySmooth S T]`, whose two fields are
`Module.Projective T Ω[T⁄S]` and `Subsingleton (Algebra.H1Cotangent S T)`), the map
`KaehlerDifferential.mapBaseChange R S T : T ⊗[S] Ω[S⁄R] → Ω[T⁄R]` has a `T`-linear retraction
(Stacks, Tag 031K). Route:
the Jacobi–Zariski sequence `H¹(L_{T⁄S}) → T ⊗[S] Ω[S⁄R] → Ω[T⁄R] → Ω[T⁄S] → 0`
(`Algebra.H1Cotangent.exact_δ_mapBaseChange`, `KaehlerDifferential.exact_mapBaseChange_map`) gives
injectivity, and projectivity splits the surjection onto `Ω[T⁄S]`. *Needs:* Mathlib
`KaehlerDifferential.mapBaseChange`, `Algebra.H1Cotangent`,
`Algebra.H1Cotangent.exact_δ_mapBaseChange`, `KaehlerDifferential.exact_mapBaseChange_map`,
`Algebra.FormallySmooth`.

**Checks.**

- The hypothesis is on `T ⁄ S`, not on `S ⁄ R`: for `R = k`, `S = k[t]` (smooth over `k`) and
  `T = k` with `t ↦ 0`, the map is `k dt → Ω[k⁄k] = 0`, not injective; here `T` is not formally
  smooth over `S`, as `H¹(L_{T⁄S}) = (t) ⁄ (t ²) ≠ 0`.
- `T = S`: `S` is formally smooth over itself (`Ω[S⁄S] = 0`, `H¹ = 0`), and the map is the
  isomorphism `S ⊗[S] Ω[S⁄R] ≅ Ω[S⁄R]`.
- Projectivity of `Ω[T⁄S]` alone does not suffice, the vanishing of `H¹` in formal smoothness is
  needed: for `R = k` of characteristic `≠ 2`, `S = k[t]`, `T = k[t] ⁄ (t ²)`, `Ω[T⁄S] = 0` is
  projective but `H¹(L_{T⁄S}) = (t ²) ⁄ (t ⁴) ≠ 0`, and the map `T dt → Ω[T⁄k] = T dt ⁄ (2 t dt)`
  kills `t dt ≠ 0`.

### 2.7 Rational multiples of an indivisible vector

Prove `AddSubgroup.rat_smul_mem_iff`: let `P` be an additive subgroup of a `ℚ`-vector space and
`x ∈ P` such that `x = m • y` with `y ∈ P` and `m : ℤ` forces `m` to be a unit. Then `q • x ∈ P` if
and only if `q` is an integer (own statement). Route: for `q = a ⁄ b` in lowest terms with
`q • x ∈ P`, a Bézout relation `s a + t b = 1` puts `(1 ⁄ b) • x` in `P`, so `b` is a unit. *Needs:*
Mathlib `AddSubgroup`, `Module ℚ`.

**Checks.**

- `P = ℤ ⊆ ℚ`, `x = 1`: `(1 ⁄ 2) • 1 ∉ ℤ`.
- Indivisibility is needed: `x = 2` is `2 • 1`, and `(1 ⁄ 2) • 2 = 1 ∈ ℤ`.
- `P = ℤ ²`, `x = (1, 2)`: indivisible, `(3 ⁄ 2) • x = (3 ⁄ 2, 3) ∉ P`, while `(2, 4) = 2 • x ∈ P`.

### 2.8 Injective modules over a principal ideal domain

Prove `Module.injective_iff_forall_smul_surjective`: over a principal ideal domain, a module `Q`
is injective if and only if it is divisible; and `Module.Injective.quotient`: over a principal ideal
domain, every quotient of an injective module is injective (own statements in this generality;
Mathlib's `Module.Baer.of_divisible` is the case of `ℤ`). Both carry `[Small.{v} R]` for a module
in universe `v`. Route: divisibility gives Baer's criterion by Tau Ceti's
`Module.Baer.of_surjective_smul`, hence injectivity by `Module.Baer.injective`; conversely, over a
domain `r • ·` is injective on `R`, so only `s = 0` kills `r ≠ 0`, and Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.exists_linearMap_apply_eq_iff_forall_smul_eq_zero` extends `r ↦ x` to a linear map
`f : R → Q`, and `x = r • f 1`. A quotient of a divisible module is divisible, which gives the second statement from the
first. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `Module.Baer.of_surjective_smul`,
`TauCeti.exists_linearMap_apply_eq_iff_forall_smul_eq_zero`; Mathlib `Module.Baer`,
`Module.Baer.injective`, `Module.Baer.of_injective`, `IsPrincipalIdealRing`, `Submodule.mkQ`.

**Checks.**

- `ℚ ⁄ ℤ` is divisible, hence injective over `ℤ`; `ℤ` is not, since `2 • ·` misses `1`.
- The quotient `ℚ → ℚ ⁄ ℤ` of the injective `ℤ`-module `ℚ` is injective, as stated.
- `IsDomain` cannot be dropped from the equivalence: over the principal ideal ring `ℤ ⁄ 4`, the
  module `ℤ ⁄ 4` is injective (Baer's criterion on the ideals `0`, `(2)`, `(1)`) but `2 • ·` is not
  surjective; and its quotient `ℤ ⁄ 2` is not injective (the map `(2) → ℤ ⁄ 2`, `2 ↦ 1`, does not
  extend).

### 2.9 Flat modules modulo a prime

Prove `Module.Flat.isSMulRegular_quotient_smul_top`: if `F` is flat over `A`, `p` is a prime of `A`
and `a ∉ p`, then `a` is a nonzerodivisor on `F ⁄ pF`, because `F ⁄ pF ≅ (A ⁄ p) ⊗ F` and `a` is
regular on the domain `A ⁄ p`. Prove `Submodule.IsAssociatedPrime.comap_eq_of_flat`: for `A → B`
and a `B`-module `F` flat over `A`, every associated prime `P` of the `B`-module `F ⁄ (pB)F`
satisfies `P ∩ A = p`. The second uses the first: an element of `P ∩ A` outside `p` would kill a
nonzero class. Both are own statements. *Needs:* Mathlib `Module.Flat`, `IsSMulRegular`,
`TensorProduct.quotTensorEquivQuotSMul`,
`Submodule.IsAssociatedPrime`, `Ideal.map`, `Ideal.comap`.

**Checks.**

- `A = B = F = ℤ`, `p = (2)`, `a = 3`: `3` acts as `1` on `ℤ ⁄ 2`, regular; the only associated
  prime of `ℤ ⁄ 2` is `(2)`, lying over `(2)`.
- Primality is needed: `p = (4)`, `a = 2 ∉ p`, and `2 · 2 = 0` in `ℤ ⁄ 4`. Flatness is needed:
  `F = ℤ ⁄ 2`, `p = (0)`, `a = 2` kills `F ⁄ pF = ℤ ⁄ 2`; and `A = ℤ`, `B = F = ℤ ⁄ 4`, `p = (0)`
  gives the associated prime `(2)` of `ℤ ⁄ 4`, over `(2) ≠ (0)`.
- The conclusion is `P ∩ A = p`, not `P = pB`: for `A = ℤ`, `B = F = ℤ[i]`, `p = (5)`,
  `ℤ[i] ⁄ 5 ≅ 𝔽₅ × 𝔽₅` has the two associated primes `(2 + i)` and `(2 − i)`, neither equal to
  `5ℤ[i]`, both over `(5)`.

### 2.10 The torsion of `K ⁄ A`

Let `A` be a domain with fraction field `K`, and write `K ⁄ A` for
`K ⧸ LinearMap.range (Algebra.linearMap A K)`. Prove
`IsFractionRing.nonempty_torsionBy_linearEquiv`: for `t ≠ 0`, the submodule `(K ⁄ A)[t]` of elements
killed by `t` (`Submodule.torsionBy`) is isomorphic to `A ⁄ (t)` (own statement). No principal ideal
hypothesis is made. When `A` is a principal ideal domain, `K ⁄ A` is moreover injective by Tau
Ceti's instance `Submodule.injective_quotient_of_divisionRing` (see Scope), and the two facts
together are the input of the duality `M ↦ Hom(M, K ⁄ A)` of finite-length modules. Route: the map
`a ↦ a ⁄ t mod A` has kernel `tA`, as `a ⁄ t ∈ A` iff `t ∣ a`, and image `(K ⁄ A)[t]`, as `t k ∈ A`
writes `k = b ⁄ t`. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsFractionRing`, `Algebra.linearMap`, `Submodule.torsionBy`.

**Checks.**

- `A = ℤ`, `K = ℚ`: `(ℚ ⁄ ℤ)[6] = (1 ⁄ 6)ℤ ⁄ ℤ ≅ ℤ ⁄ 6`, of order `6`; a map `a ↦ a t mod A`
  would be zero, and `a ↦ a ⁄ t` without the quotient by `A` would not be killed by `t`.
- `t ≠ 0` is needed: `(K ⁄ A)[0]` is all of `ℚ ⁄ ℤ`, which is torsion and not isomorphic to
  `ℤ ⁄ (0) = ℤ`. A unit `t` gives `0 ≅ A ⁄ A`.
- No principal ideal hypothesis: over `A = k[x, y]` and `t = x`, `(K ⁄ A)[x] = x⁻¹ A ⁄ A` is
  isomorphic to `A ⁄ (x) ≅ k[y]`, infinite-dimensional over `k`; there `K ⁄ A` is divisible but
  not injective, since `0 → A → K → K ⁄ A → 0` would otherwise give `A` injective dimension at
  most `1`, whereas `Ext² (A ⁄ (x, y), A) ≅ k ≠ 0` by the Koszul resolution.

### 2.11 Finitely generated torsion modules have finite length

Over a Noetherian commutative ring `R` of Krull dimension at most one (`Ring.KrullDimLE 1 R`), a
finitely generated module `M` that is torsion (`Module.IsTorsion`: every element is killed by a
nonzerodivisor) has finite length; in particular, over a principal ideal domain such a module is
Artinian, by `isFiniteLength_iff_isNoetherian_isArtinian` (closest source Stacks, Tag 00PF, the
finite length of `M ⁄ xM` over a Noetherian domain of dimension `1`). This follows in two lines
from the pins: Mathlib's `Submodule.annihilator_top_inter_nonZeroDivisors` gives a nonzerodivisor
`x`, the product of nonzerodivisors killing a finite set of generators, in the annihilator of `⊤`,
which is `Module.annihilator R M` by `Submodule.annihilator_top`; Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.isFiniteLength_of_nonZeroDivisor_mem_annihilator` then gives the finite length. *Needs:*
Tau Ceti `TauCeti.isFiniteLength_of_nonZeroDivisor_mem_annihilator`; Mathlib `Module.IsTorsion`,
`IsFiniteLength`, `Submodule.annihilator_top_inter_nonZeroDivisors`, `Submodule.annihilator_top`,
`isFiniteLength_iff_isNoetherian_isArtinian`, `Ring.KrullDimLE`, `IsNoetherianRing`.

**Checks.**

- `R = ℤ`, `M = ℤ ⁄ 12`: length `3`, from `0 ⊂ 6ℤ ⁄ 12 ⊂ 3ℤ ⁄ 12 ⊂ ℤ ⁄ 12`, with factors
  `ℤ ⁄ 2`, `ℤ ⁄ 2`, `ℤ ⁄ 3`.
- Each hypothesis is needed: `ℤ` is finitely generated and not torsion, and has infinite length;
  `ℚ ⁄ ℤ` is torsion and not finitely generated, and has the infinite chain of subgroups
  `(1 ⁄ 2 ^ n)ℤ ⁄ ℤ`; over `k[x, y]`, of dimension `2`, the torsion module `k[x, y] ⁄ (x)` is
  finitely generated and contains the infinite descending chain `(y ^ n)`.
- `R` a field (dimension `0`): a torsion module is `0`, of length `0`. Over the zero ring every
  module is `0`.

### Examples

- 2.2 with `M = ℤ ⁄ n` over `ℤ`: `ℤ ⁄ n ⊗ Π Pᵢ ≅ Π (Pᵢ ⁄ n Pᵢ)`.
- 2.8 over `k[t]`: `k(t) ⁄ k[t]` is divisible, hence an injective `k[t]`-module; by 2.10 its

- 2.11 over the Dedekind domain `ℤ[√−5]`: `ℤ[√−5] ⁄ (2, 1 + √−5)` is torsion and of length `1`.

## Layer 3: derivations

### 3.1 Integer powers of units

Prove `Derivation.leibniz_zpow_units`: for a derivation `D` of a commutative algebra `A`, a unit
`u` of `A` and `n : ℤ`, `D ↑(u ^ n) = n • ↑(u ^ (n − 1)) • D ↑u` (own statement). It extends
Mathlib's `Derivation.leibniz_zpow`, which is stated over fields, and completes the Leibniz API of
Mathlib's `Derivation.leibniz_pow` and Tau Ceti's `Derivation.leibniz_prod` (finite products).
Route: `Derivation.leibniz_pow` for `n ≥ 0`; for `n < 0`, apply `D` to `u ^ n · u ^ (−n) = 1`.
*Needs:* Mathlib `Derivation`, `Derivation.leibniz_pow`, `Derivation.leibniz_zpow`; Tau Ceti
`Derivation.leibniz_prod`.

**Checks.**

- `n = −1`: `D (u⁻¹) = −u⁻² D u`; on `ℚ[x, x⁻¹]` with `D = d ⁄ dx` and `u = x` this is `−x⁻²`,
  where an exponent `n` in place of `n − 1` would give `−x⁻¹`.
- `n = 0`: `D 1 = 0`, and the right side is `0 • u⁻¹ • D u = 0`.
- `n = −2` on `ℚ[x, x⁻¹]` with `D = d ⁄ dx` and `u = x` gives `−2 x⁻³`; a unit of the base, such
  as `u = 2` in `ℚ[x, x⁻¹]`, has `D u = 0` and both sides vanish.

### 3.2 Commutators with multiplications

Prove `Derivation.lie_mulLeft`: for a derivation `D` of a commutative `R`-algebra `A` into itself
and `a ∈ A`, the commutator of `D` with `LinearMap.mulLeft R a` in `Module.End R A` is
`LinearMap.mulLeft R (D a)`. Prove `Derivation.mulLeft_mul_mul_mulLeft_inv`: for a unit `u`,
`u ∘ D ∘ u⁻¹ = D − (u⁻¹ D u)` as endomorphisms, multiplications written as `LinearMap.mulLeft`.
Both are own statements. *Needs:* Mathlib `Derivation`, `LinearMap.mulLeft`, `Module.End`; 3.1.

**Checks.**

- `A = k[x]`, `D = d ⁄ dx`, `a = x`: the commutator is the identity, `LinearMap.mulLeft (D x)` with
  `D x = 1`.
- `u` a unit of `k`: `D u = 0`, and conjugation fixes `D`.
- The sign: on `k[x, x⁻¹]` with `u = x`, `x · D (x⁻¹ · 1) = −x⁻¹ = (D − x⁻¹ D x) 1`; the opposite
  sign would give `+x⁻¹`.

### Examples

- `Derivation.leibniz_zpow_units` on `k[x, x⁻¹]` with `D = d ⁄ dx` gives `D (x ^ n) = n x ^ (n − 1)`
  for every `n : ℤ`.
- `Derivation.lie_mulLeft` gives the Weyl relation `⁅d ⁄ dx, x⁆ = 1` on `k[x]`.

### Dependencies

Mathlib `Derivation` and `LinearMap.mulLeft`; Tau Ceti `Derivation.leibniz_prod`. Within the
layer, 3.2 uses 3.1 for `D (u⁻¹)`.

## Layer 4: topology

### 4.1 The exponential law

Prove `ContinuousMap.continuous_curry_of_locallyCompactSpace` and
`ContinuousMap.continuous_curry_of_r1Space`: currying `C(X × Y, Z) → C(X, C(Y, Z))` is continuous
for the compact-open topologies when `X` is locally compact, or when `X` is an `R1Space`
(preregular), with no hypothesis on `Y`. Prove `ContinuousMap.continuous_uncurry_of_r1Space`:
uncurrying is continuous when `X` is an `R1Space` and `Y` is locally compact. Together they make
`ContinuousMap.curry` a homeomorphism `C(X × Y, Z) ≃ₜ C(X, C(Y, Z))` for `X` preregular and `Y`
locally compact. These are own statements. Mathlib's `ContinuousMap.continuous_curry` asks for
`LocallyCompactSpace (X × Y)` and its `Homeomorph.curry` for both factors locally compact. *Needs:*
Mathlib `ContinuousMap.curry`, `ContinuousMap.uncurry`, `R1Space`, `LocallyCompactSpace`.

**Checks.**

- `X = ℚ`, `Y = [0, 1]`: `X` is Hausdorff, hence `R1Space`, and not locally compact, so the
  homeomorphism is new here, outside Mathlib's `Homeomorph.curry`.
- `X = Y = Z = ℝ`: all hypotheses hold and the statements reduce to Mathlib's.
- The convention `curry f x y = f (x, y)`: for `f (x, y) = x − y` on `ℝ × ℝ`, `curry f 1 0 = 1`,
  where the swapped convention would give `−1`; and `uncurry (curry f) = f`.

Not a target: compatibility with suspension and loops. For compactly generated spaces the based
exponential law `F(X ∧ Y, Z) ≅ F(X, F(Y, Z))` is a natural homeomorphism, and with `ΣX = X ∧ S¹`,
`ΩY = F(S¹, Y)` it gives `F(ΣX, Y) ≅ F(X, ΩY)` (May, *A Concise Course in Algebraic Topology*,
Ch. 8, §§1–2, pp. 57–58). In the compact-open setting of 4.1, `S¹` being locally compact, the
homeomorphism restricts to the maps killing `X ∨ S¹`; identifying these with `C(ΣX, Y)` needs the
quotient `X × S¹ → ΣX` to induce an embedding, clear for `X` compact Hausdorff.

### 4.2 An openness criterion

Prove `NoetherianSpace.isOpen_of_stableUnderGeneralization`: in a Noetherian quasi-sober space, a
set `E` stable under generalisation is open as soon as every `x ∈ E` has an open neighbourhood `W`
with `W ∩ closure {x} ⊆ E`. Mathlib has this only for prime spectra, under a constructibility
hypothesis (`PrimeSpectrum.isOpen_of_stableUnderGeneralization_of_isConstructible`). Route: by EGA
0, Corollaire 9.2.6 (EGA III₁, p. 16), a subset `E` of a Noetherian space is open if and only if,
for every irreducible closed `Y` meeting `E`, `E ∩ Y` contains a nonempty open subset of `Y`. Such a
`Y` has a generic point `η` by quasi-sobriety; stability gives `η ∈ E`, and the hypothesis at `η`
gives the nonempty open subset `W ∩ Y` of `Y` inside `E`. *Needs:* Mathlib
`TopologicalSpace.NoetherianSpace`, `QuasiSober`, `StableUnderGeneralization`.

**Checks.**

- In `Spec ℤ`, `E = {(0)}` is stable under generalisation and not open; the hypothesis fails at
  `x = (0)`, since every nonempty open set contains closed points and `closure {x}` is the whole
  space.
- In `Spec ℤ`, `E = Spec ℤ ∖ {(2)}` is open, and for a closed point `x ≠ (2)` the set `W = E`
  works, as `closure {x} = {x}`.
- In the Sierpiński space `{0, 1}` with `{1}` open, `E = {1}` is stable under generalisation and
  open, with `W = {1}`; `E = {0}` is not stable under generalisation, as `1` generalises `0`.

### Examples

- 4.1 for `X = Y = [0, 1]` recovers the classical exponential law on compact spaces.
- 4.2 on `Spec` of a Noetherian ring for `E` the complement of `V(I)`.

### Dependencies

Mathlib general topology and the compact-open topology.

## Layer 5: finite monoids, identities, traces, Grothendieck groups

### 5.1 Right zeros of finite monoids

Prove `Monoid.exists_forall_mul_eq_self_iff_of_finite`: a finite monoid `M` has a right zero, an
element `p` with `u p = p` for every `u`, if and only if any two principal right ideals meet
(`∀ u v, ∃ u' v', u u' = v v'`) and every monoid morphism from `M` to a group in the universe of `M`
is trivial (own statement). Route: finiteness and the meeting condition give `p` in every principal
right ideal; `E = p M` satisfies `u E = E` for every `u`, so left multiplication is a morphism to
the permutations of `E`, trivial by hypothesis. *Needs:* Mathlib `Monoid`, `MonoidHom`,
`Equiv.Perm`.

**Checks.**

- A nontrivial finite group satisfies the first condition and has no right zero.
- `{1, a, b}` with `a`, `b` left zeros (`x y = x` for `x ≠ 1`) has only trivial morphisms to groups
  (`f a = f (a a) = f a ²`) and no right zero (`b a = b`, `a b = a`); here `a M = {a}` and
  `b M = {b}` do not meet.
- `(ℤ ⁄ n, ·)`: `0` is a right zero, and both conditions hold.

### 5.2 The Binet–Cauchy and Lagrange identities

Prove `Finset.sum_sum_mul_sub_mul_mul_mul_sub_mul`: over a commutative ring,
`∑ i, ∑ j, (a i b j − a j b i)(c i d j − c j d i) = 2 ((∑ a c)(∑ b d) − (∑ a d)(∑ b c))`, sums over
a finite set `s` (own statement in this form). This is the `2 × 2` Cauchy–Binet formula, Tau
Ceti's `Matrix.pairMinor_mul`, for the matrix with rows `a`, `b` times the matrix with columns `c`,
`d`; it differs in summing over the ordered pairs of a finite set of any index type, with no
`Fintype` or `LinearOrder`, which doubles the sum over `i < j`. Lagrange's identity over a
commutative ring is its case `c = a`, `d = b`. Prove `RCLike.sum_sum_norm_mul_sub_mul_sq`: over
`ℝ` or `ℂ`,
`∑ i, ∑ j, ‖a i b j − a j b i‖ ² = 2 ((∑ ‖a i‖ ²)(∑ ‖b i‖ ²) − ‖∑ conj (a i) b i‖ ²)`, which gives
the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with its defect (own statement in this form). *Needs:* Tau Ceti
`Matrix.pairMinor_mul`; Mathlib `RCLike`, `Finset.sum_mul_sq_le_sq_mul_sq`.

**Checks.**

- `a = (1, 0)`, `b = (0, 1)`: the ordered pairs `(1, 2)` and `(2, 1)` contribute `1` each, and the
  right side is `2 (1 · 1 − 0) = 2`.
- `a = b`: both sides of Lagrange's identity are `0`.
- The conjugate is needed: over `ℂ`, `a = (1, i)` and `b = i a` give `0` on the left and
  `2 (2 · 2 − |2 i| ²) = 0` on the right; without `conj`, `∑ a i b i = i − i = 0` would give `8`.

### 5.3 Traces of odd powers of a difference of idempotents

Prove `Matrix.trace_sub_pow_odd_of_isIdempotentElem`: for idempotent square matrices `P`, `Q` over a
commutative ring, `tr ((P − Q) ^ (2k + 1)) = tr P − tr Q` for every `k : ℕ`, with no hypothesis on
`2`. A route that does not divide by `2`: `C = (P − Q) ²` commutes with `P` and `Q`,
`(P − Q) C = (P − Q) − (P Q P − Q P Q)`, and `tr (D P Q P) = tr (D Q P Q)` whenever `D` commutes
with `P` and `Q`. For orthogonal projections on a Hilbert space with `P − Q` in a Schatten class,
and an odd power of `P − Q` of trace class, the common value is the index of the pair
(Avron–Seiler–Simon 1994, Theorem 4.1); the matrix identity over a commutative ring, with no
hypothesis on `2`, is an own statement. *Needs:* Mathlib `Matrix.trace`, `Matrix.trace_mul_comm`,
`IsIdempotentElem`.

**Checks.**

- Over `ℤ`, `P = [[1, 0], [0, 0]]`, `Q = [[0, 0], [1, 1]]`: `P − Q = [[1, 0], [−1, −1]]` has
  trace `0 = 1 − 1`, and `(P − Q) ² = 1`, so every odd power is `P − Q`; the even powers have trace
  `2`, so the statement fails for even exponents.
- Idempotence is needed: `P = (2)`, `Q = (0)` of size one give `tr P ³ = 8 ≠ 2`.
- Over `𝔽₂`, where a proof by anticommutation divides by `2`: `P = 1`, `Q = 0` of size one give
  `1 = 1 − 0` for every exponent, and the example above reduced modulo `2` has trace `0`.

### 5.4 Common denominators in the Grothendieck group

Prove `GrothendieckGroup.exists_eq_div_of_and_eq_div_of`: any two elements `x`, `y` of the
Grothendieck group of a commutative monoid `M` are `x = of a ⁄ of c` and `y = of b ⁄ of c` with
`a, b, c : M`, where `of` is `Algebra.GrothendieckGroup.of`. In particular every element is
`of a ⁄ of b` (own statement). Route: `Submonoid.LocalizationMap.surj` for the top submonoid, then
the product of the two denominators. *Needs:* Mathlib `Algebra.GrothendieckGroup`,
`Algebra.GrothendieckGroup.of`, `Submonoid.LocalizationMap.surj`.

**Checks.**

- `M = (ℕ, ·)`: `0` absorbs, the group is trivial, and `of 2 = of 3`; the common-denominator
  statement does not make `of` injective without cancellation.
- `M = Multiplicative ℕ`: the group is `Multiplicative ℤ`, and the classes of `1` and `−2` have the
  common denominator `of 2`: they are `of 3 ⁄ of 2` and `of 0 ⁄ of 2`.
- `M = ({1, e}, ·)` with `e ² = e`: the group is trivial, and `of e = of 1`, with `c = e` in
  `Submonoid.LocalizationMap.eq_iff_exists`.

### 5.5 Simple modules are determined by their traces

Prove `IsSimpleModule.nonempty_linearEquiv_of_trace_eq`: let `k` be an algebraically closed field,
of any characteristic, `A` a `k`-algebra, not necessarily finite-dimensional, and `S`, `T` simple
`A`-modules that are finite-dimensional over `k` (with `IsScalarTower k A S` and
`IsScalarTower k A T`). If `LinearMap.trace k S (Module.toModuleEnd k S a)` equals
`LinearMap.trace k T (Module.toModuleEnd k T a)` for every `a : A`, then `S` and `T` are
isomorphic as `A`-modules (own statement in this generality; closest source Lang, *Algebra*,
Chapter XVII, Corollary 3.8, the semisimple form in characteristic zero, which is Tau Ceti's
`IsSemisimpleModule.nonempty_linearEquiv_of_trace_eq`). This target differs from that statement in
asking `k` algebraically closed and `S`, `T` simple, and in allowing any characteristic, where the
semisimple form fails (Checks). Route: if `S ≄ T`, Schur's lemma
(`IsSimpleModule.algebraMap_end_bijective_of_isAlgClosed`) makes every endomorphism of `S × T` a
pair of scalars, so the Jacobson density theorem for the semisimple module `S × T`
(`Module.Finite.toModuleEnd_moduleEnd_surjective`) yields `a : A` acting as a rank-one idempotent
on `S` and as `0` on `T`, of traces `1` and `0`. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsSimpleModule`, `IsAlgClosed`,
`LinearMap.trace`, `Module.toModuleEnd`, `IsSimpleModule.algebraMap_end_bijective_of_isAlgClosed`,
`Module.Finite.toModuleEnd_moduleEnd_surjective`.

**Checks.**

- `A = k[X]`: the simple modules are `k[X] ⁄ (X − λ)`, on which `X` has trace `λ`, so distinct `λ`
  are told apart, as the statement requires.
- Simplicity cannot be weakened to semisimplicity in characteristic `p`: over `k = 𝔽̄_p` and
  `A = k`, the semisimple modules `k ^ p` and `0` have the same trace `p · a = 0` for every `a`,
  and are not isomorphic. In characteristic `0` the trace of `1` is the dimension.
- `IsAlgClosed` cannot be dropped in characteristic `p`: for `k = 𝔽_p(t)` and the purely
  inseparable `L = k(t ^ (1 ⁄ p))`, the field trace `L → k` is identically `0`, so the two simple
  modules `L × 0` and `0 × L` of `A = L × L` have equal (zero) traces and are not isomorphic,
  being killed by different idempotents.

### Examples

- 5.2 for `ι = Fin 3` and real vectors: `‖a‖ ² ‖b‖ ² − ⟨a, b⟩ ² = ‖a × b‖ ²`, the cross product
  collecting the three pairs `i < j`, each counted twice on the left of 5.2.
- 5.5 for `A = k[G]` with `G` a finite group: two irreducible representations over an algebraically
  closed field with the same character are isomorphic, in every characteristic.

another.
