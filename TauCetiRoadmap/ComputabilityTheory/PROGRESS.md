## ComputabilityTheory: 2026-10-27 to 2026-11-08 (`64b097e` to `d2083f0`)

Two of the roadmap's three priority constructions from Soare's Chapter VII landed in this first window. Soare's [low simple set](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Simple/Low.html#TauCeti.lowSimpleSet) is built as a finite-injury construction and proved both [simple](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Simple/Low.html#TauCeti.simple_lowSimpleSet) and [low](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Simple/Lowness.html#TauCeti.low_lowSimpleSet), so its jump is no stronger than the halting problem's; its degree therefore lies [strictly between 0 and 0′](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Simple/Lowness.html#TauCeti.degree_lowSimpleSet_mem_Ioo). The original [Friedberg–Muchnik theorem](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/FriedbergMuchnik/Basic.html#TauCeti.friedberg_muchnik), two Turing-incomparable c.e. sets, comes with its witness-and-restraint stage machine, a correspondence with Soare's markers, and the [2^e − 1 injury bound](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/FriedbergMuchnik/Basic.html#TauCeti.FriedbergMuchnik.finite_injurySet_and_ncard_le) (TauCeti#27679). The third method, Sacks splitting, has not started beyond its length-of-agreement restraint and preservation lemmas.

Underneath sits a full foundation: a numbered [oracle-program type](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/SingleOracle/Code.html#TauCeti.Oracle.Single.Code) whose programs compute exactly the functions [recursive in the oracle](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/SingleOracle/Code.html#TauCeti.Oracle.Single.Code.exists_code), with bounded runs that record their use and satisfy the [use principle](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/SingleOracle/Evaln.html#TauCeti.Oracle.Single.Code.evaln_eq_some_of_eqOn). On top of it come the concrete [jump](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Jump/Basic.html#Set.jump), its iterates, and [low sets](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Jump/Low.html#Set.Low). Two named lemmas from Soare III.3 also landed: the [Modulus Lemma](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Approximation/CEOracle.html#TauCeti.Approximation.computableIn_iff_exists_isModulus_of_rePred) for c.e. oracles and the direction of the Limit Lemma that [computes a limit in the jump](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Approximation/Limit.html#TauCeti.Approximation.Converges.computableIn_jump). There is also a public calculus of [injury sets](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Priority/Injury/Basic.html#TauCeti.Priority.injurySet) shared by both constructions.

Smaller results include [Post's simple set](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Simple/Post.html#TauCeti.simple_postSimpleSet), a [computably inseparable pair](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Inseparable.html#TauCeti.exists_computablyInseparable_rePred) of c.e. sets, and the [split-and-join reduction](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Split.html#Disjoint.turingEquivalent_union_join). The arithmetical hierarchy is defined by quantifiers over oracle-computable matrices, but Post's theorem is proved only at [level one](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Computability/Hierarchy/One.html#TauCeti.ArithmeticalHierarchy.isSigma_one_iff_exists_recursiveIn_domain).
