# Roadmap: fukaya categories of compact Lagrangian branes

**Summary.** This roadmap constructs Lagrangian Floer cohomology and the filtered curved Fukaya category of compact Lagrangian branes in a finite-dimensional symplectic manifold, with its weak bounding cochains, potential components, continuation functors, geometric modules and split-closed derived categories. Layer 0 builds the completed graded coefficient rings, their localizations and shifted lattices, the curved `A∞` adapters, finite-loss functors and unit cells on MorseNovikov N1 and DGAInfinity; Layer 1 the branes, chords, labelled integral class groups and Hamiltonian flow adapters; Layer 2 the bordered domains, the Cauchy–Riemann section with its index theory, cluster domains and curvature margins; Layer 3 regularity, compactness, disc decomposition and smooth selection on HeegaardFloer's analytic substrate and sphere theory; Layer 4 the classical signed counts and the exact and weakly exact categories; Layer 5 the chain-level rational virtual chains of bordered moduli; Layer 6 the monotone fixed-potential categories; Layer 7 the general filtered operations and strict weakly unobstructed objects; Layer 8 the unit, sign and local-system laws with the zero-section, equator and surface computations; Layer 9 continuation, modules and derived categories. The end theorems are the filtered curved category with its curved `A∞` relations and strict weak bounding objects (FK7), the `Λ_loc`-linear quasi-equivalence of the localized fixed-potential categories under Hamiltonian isotopy together with the finite-loss positive-lattice comparisons and the retained positive-lattice torsion (FK9.2), the intersection bound `#(L∩φ_H(L))≥Σ_j dim_k H^j(L;k)` for a compact exact or weakly exact brane `L`, oriented and relatively spin, the spin lift being unused over `k` of characteristic 2 (FK9.0), the zero-section computation `HF(L,L)≅H*(L;End E)` with its cup product (FK8.0) and the torus torsion `H⁰≅Λ₀²⊕Λ₀/(T^A)` (FK8.1).

## Scope and ownership

The target is a finite-dimensional smooth Hausdorff second-countable symplectic manifold. The general virtual branch treats **closed** targets and compact boundaryless embedded oriented relatively spin Lagrangians, over a characteristic-zero ground field and its specified graded completed coefficient algebra. The classical branches treat compact weakly exact Lagrangians in closed targets, compact exact Lagrangians in completions of finite-type Liouville domains, and the suitable monotone class specified in FK6. Integral orientation-line counts belong to the classical branches. The general branch uses rationally weighted virtual chains; it asserts no integral virtual-count theorem. Objects in a weakly unobstructed category carry actual weak bounding data: not every compact Lagrangian is asserted to admit such data.

The geometric scope is discs and their genus-zero sphere and disc bubbles, with Hamiltonian perturbations, boundary markings, interior stabilizing markings and the relative families needed for operations and comparison functors. Closed Gromov–Witten classes, punctured contact/SFT curves, gauge field theories, wrapped categories and general quilt functors are separate theories. A fixed-width monotone quilt category is a comparison example of different analytic scope, not a proof of the polygon theory below.

This roadmap owns:

- the filtered, curved and even-cyclic specializations of DGAInfinity's `A∞` hierarchy: homogeneous pieces and localization of MorseNovikov N1's completed rings, shifted lattices, the energy-completed word coalgebra, nullary operations, bounding data and potentials, finite-loss functors and their equivalence notion, unit cells, the Γ-filtered obstruction theorem and the strict unit model (FK0);
- branes with relative spin lifts, gradings and local systems, chords and their dictionary with intersections, the labelled integral class groups `G_D` with their energy and Maslov homomorphisms, caps and coefficient transport, and the Hamiltonian flow adapters (FK1);
- ordered stable discs and associahedra, painted comparison domains, the unit-forgetful allowed-data carrier, the bordered Cauchy–Riemann section with its index and determinant theory, cluster domains with their Morse-edge steering theorem, and the flat end data with their curvature margins and weighted inequality (FK2);
- finite-order regularity, the bordered quantitative anchor windows for discs, smooth disc decomposition and reduction, the joint sphere–disc, sphere–Morse and node incidence problems with their frozen-node parameter dependence, selected self data, raw compactness in closed and Liouville targets, conditional gluing, openness and coherent smooth choices (FK3);
- classical signed chains, curved Stokes, geometric unit cells and the exact and weakly exact categories (FK4);
- the chain-level Kuranishi/multisection engine for bordered moduli: obstruction reductions, gluing, refinements, rational chains compatible with evaluation fibre products, and relative Stokes (FK5);
- monotone fixed-potential categories with the parallel `End(E)`-valued curvature, strict old units and the scalar weight lemma (FK6);
- the general filtered operations, strict weakly unobstructed objects and localized fixed-potential categories (FK7);
- unit, sign and local-system laws, the zero-section, equator and combinatorial surface computations (FK8);
- continuation and comparison counts, positive transport, finite-loss transport, Yoneda modules, split-closed derived categories, and the lune/chord, classical/virtual and implicit-atlas comparisons (FK9).

## Conventions

Write inputs as `(a_k,…,a_1)`, with `a_i:L_{i-1}→L_i`. Operations have cohomological degree `2−k`; `m₂(g,f)=g∘f` in the DG specialization. Use the [DGAInfinity](../DGAInfinity/README.md) tensor-map Koszul rule and Stasheff sign `(-1)^(r+st)`. FK0 extends that convention to arity zero. The suspension `s:A→A[1]` has degree −1; suspended structure maps have degree one. Transport all orientation signs through this suspension comparison. A count in a different convention must carry its explicit change-of-convention map.

For a Hamiltonian use `ι_{X_H}ω=−dH`, the perturbed equation `(du−X_K)^{0,1}=0`, and compare to the symplectic/Hamiltonian suppliers' convention through `f=−H`. The output is at the negative strip end `s→−∞`, inputs are at positive ends `s→+∞`, in cyclic boundary order. The strip equation is `∂_s u+J(∂_t u−X_H)=0`. The capped action `A_H(x,w)=−∫w*ω+∫H_t(x(t))dt` decreases with increasing `s`; the cochain differential runs from its positive-end input to its negative-end output. Distinguish the closed periodic-orbit and Lagrangian-chord index dictionaries. In the closed Hamiltonian test `CZ_HF(−H)+n=ind_Morse(H)`. For a self-chord in a Weinstein chart `ω=dq∧dp`, `H=h(q)`, the shear is `[[1,0],[t Hess h,1]]` and its chord Robbin–Salamon index is `μ_ch=½ sign(Hess h)=n/2−ind(h)`. Thus the cochain degree is `|x|=n/2−μ_ch=ind(h)`; the homogeneous boundary transform gives `graph(−dh)`. Prove the chord phase-lift/relative-degree, determinant and Morse-trajectory comparison with HeegaardFloer F1.4 and F3, including the positive/negative one-dimensional quadratic tests. General brane pairs use actual Maslov-cover lifts or relative/parity degrees, not a closed-orbit formula assigned to chords.

Use a grading group `B` with an integer shift and parity homomorphism `B→ℤ/2` taking the shift to one. The universal oriented geometric branch is parity graded. Integer gradings require an actual Maslov cover and each brane's phase lift. A `ℤ/(2N)` grading requires its actual even Maslov cover and parity map; it is not cyclic symmetry of operations. Give coefficient classes their actual Maslov/Chern degree. Curvature has degree two: in parity a degree-zero scalar can have that parity, whereas an integer-graded scalar potential must carry degree two. Keep different graded classes of equal energy distinct unless a proved grading-compatible coefficient quotient is chosen.

**Checks.**

- The sign `ι_(X_H)ω=−dH` on the torus `ℝ²/ℤ²` with `ω=dq∧dp` and `H(q)=−(4π)⁻¹cos(2πq)`: `X_H=(1/2)sin(2πq)∂_p`, so the time-one map is the shear `(q,p)↦(q,p+(1/2)sin(2πq))` of FK8.1; the opposite sign convention gives the inverse shear.
- The one-dimensional self-chord tests: `h=+x²/2` has `μ_ch=1/2=n/2−0` and cochain degree `|x|=ind(h)=0`; `h=−x²/2` has `μ_ch=−1/2` and `|x|=1`; the formula `n/2+μ_ch` would give `n−ind(h)`, the output cap codimension, not the degree.
- Degrees: `m₁` has degree `2−1=1`, `m₂` degree 0, so `|m₂(g,f)|=|g|+|f|`, and the curvature `m₀` has degree 2; in the parity grading a degree-zero scalar may carry curvature, while an integer-graded scalar potential has degree 2.

## How to read the build

The layers are in build order and each `*Needs:*` line names what a target's statement and route consume. Layer 0 delivers the coefficients, graded completions, unit cells, the direct strict weak-MC definition and the conditional old-model obstruction theorem, whose recursive specialization is used only after the actual local support proof. Layer 6 delivers the monotone categories, Layer 7 the full direct strict weak-MC categories with the full geometric old-model cell/residual theorem and its hypotheses, Layer 8 the unit, sign and local tests, and Layer 9 orders raw moving-end sections, pointwise energy, compactness, parameterized regularity/reductions, gluing and Stokes before completed functors; positive fixed-support self comparison and literal strict symplectic pushforward are distinct from finite-loss moving continuation. ### 2.4 Flat end data, curvature margins and the weighted inequality (FK2.4)

On a parameter interpolation of compatible cutoff profiles, compute the actual cross-Poisson and cutoff-derivative terms; do not infer the estimate from convexity alone. In pure-self sectors retain `K=0` and use their separate Morse/J incidence and simple-curve or obstruction-reduced theorem. Neither sector spends a nonexistent Hamiltonian margin. **API.**

- A symplectomorphism `φ` pushes forward the whole model with `F₁` only, `F₀=0`, exact area and degree preservation and strict weak-MC transport `B↦φ_*B`; in the Liouville branch the transported primitive is `(φ⁻¹)^*θ`, and when `φ^*θ=θ+dS` with `θ|L=df` the primitive on `φ(L)` in the original-`θ` model is `(f+S|L)∘φ⁻¹`.
- Every moving-end comparison is a finite-loss functor of FK0.3 into the shifted target lattices, with loss at most `C_H+C_extra` for a single family; its composite with its reverse is homotopic to the filtration inclusion with the summed losses; these comparisons induce `Λ_loc`-linear quasi-equivalences of the localized fixed-potential categories and equivalences of their split-closed derived categories, while fixed-end and fixed-support changes satisfying FK2.4's estimate have loss zero and give `Λ₀`-linear quasi-equivalences.
- The lune/chord comparison: FK8.1's `K=0` lune complex and the chord complex of FK1.2 for a small flat `H` are related by a finite-loss comparison of loss at most `C_H+C_extra`, the identity with loss zero when `H=0` and the pair is transverse, and FK8.1's combinatorial isotopy equivalences are the analytic continuation maps with the same losses.
- The classical/virtual comparison on shared closed characteristic-zero data through zero-obstruction charts, common coefficient maps and relative virtual chains, and the implicit-atlas comparison: FK5's rational chain pushed forward by evaluation equals ImplicitAtlases §4.3's class pushed forward by §5.4, and both equal the classical signed count on a regular stratum.

**Checks.**

- The torus pair of FK8.1: positive-lattice cohomology `Λ₀²` before and `Λ₀²⊕Λ₀/(T^A)` after the shear, localized cohomology of rank 2 in both cases; a `Λ₀`-linear quasi-equivalence between the two positive lattices would contradict the torsion.
- For a single moving-end family the comparison has loss `C_H+C_extra`, and the composite with its reverse is homotopic to `ι_(0,2(C_H+C_extra))`; in Fukaya–Oh–Ohta–Ono's displacement setting the forward map lowers the filtration level by `‖H‖` and the composite by `2‖H‖` (Remark 6.3, p. 20), not by 0.
- The lune/chord comparison on the torus pair of FK8.1 with `H=0`: the four intersection points are the four chords, the dictionary is the identity, and the comparison is the identity of the complex `d b=T^A(a−c)` with loss 0; for a small flat `H` the chord model has exactly one chord near each of the four points by FK1.2a, and the comparison has loss at most `C_H`, which is not asserted to be 0, so the two positive lattices are compared only up to that loss.
- A fixed-end mixed change whose comparison and homotopy families satisfy the nonnegative estimate of FK2.4 has loss 0 and gives a `Λ₀`-linear quasi-equivalence; the identity comparison of unchanged data is the first instance, with `F₁=id` and `F_k=0` for `k≠1`.
