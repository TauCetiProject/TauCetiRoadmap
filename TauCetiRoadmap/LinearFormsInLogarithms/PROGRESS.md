## LinearFormsInLogarithms: 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-11 (`0afe3c3` to `1c6a1a4`)

The first piece of this roadmap's analysis in several variables is target 0.2, sums of power series. The new theorem [`hasFPowerSeriesOnBall_tsum`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/InfiniteSum.html#TauCeti.hasFPowerSeriesOnBall_tsum) says that a family of functions with power series on a common ball can be summed term by term. The condition is that the coefficient norms, weighted by the radius to the degree, are summable over degree and index together. The sum then has the summed series as its power series on the same ball, so it is analytic at the centre. The index set is arbitrary and the domain need not be finite-dimensional. This is the interchange of summations that 0.3 needs to turn a normally convergent multi-index series into a power series. The multi-index coefficients themselves have not been started.

## LinearFormsInLogarithms: 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-19 (`1c6a1a4` to `b235000`)

Two named lemmas from Waldschmidt's chapter landed. The first is the Thue–Siegel lemma (target 3.4, his Lemmas 4.11 and 4.12). It says that when a pigeonhole count allows, there is a nonzero integer vector with coordinates at most `X` at which a family of linear forms is uniformly small. It is proved both for [real forms](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/ThueSiegel.html#TauCeti.ThueSiegel.exists_ne_zero_int_vec_abs_le_of_pow_lt) and for [complex forms](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/ThueSiegel.html#TauCeti.ThueSiegel.exists_ne_zero_int_vec_norm_le_of_pow_le), the latter with the exponential bounds that the auxiliary function will need. Examples show that the counting inequality is sharp and that neither hypothesis on the index types can be dropped. The second is the linear independence of exponential polynomials (2.3): if `∑ P_w(z) exp(w · z)` vanishes identically and the frequencies `w` are distinct, every polynomial `P_w` is zero ([`eq_zero_of_sum_eval_mul_cexp`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/ExpPolynomial/LinearIndependence.html#TauCeti.eq_zero_of_sum_eval_mul_cexp)). It is proved in one variable and in several, and it comes with the growth bound of 2.2 ([`norm_prod_pow_mul_cexp_le`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/ExpPolynomial/Growth.html#TauCeti.norm_prod_pow_mul_cexp_le)).

Layer 0's analysis in several variables is now in place except for its last step. The multi-index coefficient [`mvCoeff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/Coeff.html#FormalMultilinearSeries.mvCoeff) of a formal multilinear series comes with the identity theorem in coefficient form, the fact that an [analytic function is the sum of its multi-index series](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/Coeff.html#HasFPowerSeriesOnBall.hasSum_mvCoeff), and the converse, that [a normally convergent multi-index series has a power series](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/Coeff.html#TauCeti.exists_hasFPowerSeriesOnBall_tsum). The Taylor coefficient [`mvTaylorCoeff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/TaylorCoeff.html#TauCeti.mvTaylorCoeff) is built on it and has the whole API of 0.4. That includes agreement with iterated derivatives up to the factorial, the [product rule](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/TaylorCoeff.html#AnalyticAt.mvTaylorCoeff_smul), translation and locality, and the local expansion. The roadmap's normalisation checks are formalised as examples.

Three pieces are still missing: division by a coordinate (0.5), which the Schwarz lemma for Cartesian products needs; the Taylor coefficients of an exponential monomial (2.1); and the rest of Layer 3.
