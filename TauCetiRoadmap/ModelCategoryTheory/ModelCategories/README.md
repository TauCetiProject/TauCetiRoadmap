# Roadmap: lifting properties, presentable categories and combinatorial model categories

**Summary.** This roadmap develops the 1-categorical homotopical algebra on which the higher-categorical
roadmaps rest.

It builds on Mathlib's model categories, small object argument and locally presentable categories.

- **Weak saturation (Layers 1–2).** Weakly saturated classes of morphisms, and the identification of the class
  generated by a small set with a lifting class in a presentable category. In a κ-accessible category the
  retracts in that identification can be removed, using cell trees.
- **Homotopy in a model category (Layer 3).** Homotopy extension, the model structure on an under-category, left
  properness when all objects are cofibrant, and homotopy pushouts.
- **Quillen adjunctions (Layer 4).** Quillen adjunctions and Quillen equivalences, the latter characterized
  through derived functors.
- **Accessible and perfect classes (Layer 5).** Accessible and perfect classes of morphisms in presentable
  categories.
- **Combinatorial model categories (Layer 6).** The existence theorems for combinatorial model structures.
- **Diagram categories (Layer 7).** The projective and Reedy model structures on diagram categories.
- **Enrichment (Layer 8).** Closed monoidal categories, enrichment, tensors, cotensors and coends.

**Headline theorems.**
- The small object argument for a small set in a locally presentable category, and the equality of the weakly
  saturated class it generates with its lifting class (§1.4, §1.5).
- In a κ-accessible category, saturated maps are cell complexes of small cells, without retracts (§2.4).
- Homotopy extension (§3.2).
- The model structure on an under-category, with all three classes created by the forgetful functor (§3.3).
- A model category whose objects are all cofibrant is left proper (§3.4).
- The characterizations of Quillen adjunctions and of Quillen equivalences (§4.1, §4.2).
- The preimage of a perfect class under a functor preserving filtered colimits is perfect (§5.5).
- In a combinatorial model category, the fibrations and weak equivalences are accessible (§6.1).
- Smith's recognition theorem, which produces combinatorial model structures, and its perfect-class form,
  which produces left proper ones (§6.3).
- The transfer theorem along an adjunction (§6.4).
- Existence of the projective model structure on diagrams (§7.2).
- Existence of the Reedy model structure on diagrams indexed by a Reedy category (§7.7).

## Scope and ownership

Already in the library, cited and not planned: Mathlib's `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`
(HTT, Def. A.2.1.1, p. 828), its model structures on over-categories and opposite categories, its
homotopy relations, fundamental lemma and homotopy category of bifibrant
objects; Mathlib's small object argument for a class that permits it; Mathlib's locally presentable
and accessible 1-categories with the strong-generator characterization, presheaf categories and
reflective subcategories; the closed monoidal, lax monoidal and enriched vocabulary of Mathlib; and
Mathlib's `HomotopicalAlgebra.ReedyStructure` with `SimplexCategory.reedyStructure`. Only what lies
beyond these is planned here, each time with Checks of agreement.

Completeness of presentable categories is not used: the existence theorems for model structures on a
presentable category carry the finite-limit hypothesis explicitly.

Not in this roadmap: the Kan–Quillen model structure (ModelCategoryTheory, Part KanQuillen);
monoidal, simplicial and enriched model categories, the Bergner model structure, homotopy limits and
homotopy Kan extensions, the injective model structure, Reedy–injective comparisons, coends as left
Quillen bifunctors and realization of simplicial objects (ModelCategoryTheory, Part
SimplicialCategories); localization of enriched categories, left Bousfield localization and Dugger's
theorem (ModelCategoryTheory, Part EnrichedLocalization).

## Conventions

Locators such as (HTT, Prop. A.3.2.4, p. 876) refer to the editions listed under [References](#references).

Every statement to prove, define or construct carries a label `X.Y.Z` — its section, then its position within that
section — and a single labeled statement, cited as `§3.1.2` here and `Part ModelCategories §3.1.2` elsewhere, is
one target.

- **Universes.** A category is `C : Type u` with `Category.{v} C`. Presentability, smallness of
  sets of morphisms, transfinite compositions and weak saturation are measured in a fixed size
  universe w: presentable means Mathlib `IsLocallyPresentable.{w}`, a small set of morphisms is a
  `MorphismProperty` with `MorphismProperty.IsSmall.{w}`, and transfinite compositions are indexed
  by well-ordered types in `Type w`. Witness Check: for C the category of types in universe w, the
  set of the two maps from the empty type to a point and from two points to one point is w-small,
  and C is locally presentable; a set of morphisms indexed by all types in `Type w` is not w-small.
- **Model categories.** A model category is Mathlib's `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, whose
  definition asks for finite limits and colimits. Lurie's model categories (HTT, Def. A.2.1.1,
  p. 828) have all small limits and colimits; a statement that needs them carries
  `HasLimitsOfSize.{w, w}` and `HasColimitsOfSize.{w, w}` explicitly. The other axioms agree
  literally: two-out-of-three, retract stability of the three classes, the two lifting axioms and the
  two factorizations, which Mathlib phrases as two weak factorization systems
  (`ModelCategory.mk'`). Witness Check: the category of finite sets with the bijections as weak
  equivalences and all maps as cofibrations and fibrations is a model category in Mathlib's sense but
  not in Lurie's, since it lacks infinite coproducts.
- **Two model structures on sets used in Checks.** The *trivial structure* on sets has weak
  equivalences the bijections and all maps cofibrations and fibrations. The *injective structure* has
  all maps weak equivalences, cofibrations the injections and fibrations the surjections; a map of
  sets factors as X ⟶ X ⊔ Y ⟶ Y, an injection followed by a surjection, and injections lift against
  surjections, so this is a model structure with all small limits and colimits.
- **Several model structures on one category.** A model structure is a term `M : ModelCategory C`.
  An existence theorem says that some `M` has prescribed cofibrations, weak equivalences and
  fibrations, written `HasClasses M cof we fib`; this roadmap never installs a second global
  instance on a Mathlib type. Witness Check: on sets, the injective structure and the structure
  with all maps weak equivalences, all maps cofibrations and the bijections as fibrations are two
  different terms with the same weak equivalences; they differ on the map from two points to one, a cofibration only in the second.
- **Directions.** A map has the left lifting property against p when every square from it to p has
  a diagonal; Mathlib `MorphismProperty.llp` and `MorphismProperty.rlp`. Witness Check: in sets,
  the right lifting class of the inclusion of the empty set into a point is the class of surjections,
  while its left lifting class is the class of maps with nonempty source together with the identity
  of the empty set; exchanging the two directions changes the answer.
- **Cardinals.** κ, τ are regular cardinals in `Cardinal.{w}`, with `Fact κ.IsRegular`. A set is
  κ-small when its cardinality is below κ.
- **Shared declarations.** Part SimplicialCategories restates `IsWeaklySaturated`, `weakSaturation`,
  `IsLeftProper` and `IsCombinatorial` with bodies identical to the ones here, naming this Part as
  their owner.
- **Arrow categories.** A class of morphisms W of C is identified with the full subcategory of
  `Arrow C` on the arrows in W. Witness Check: the isomorphisms of C span a full subcategory of
  `Arrow C` equivalent to C itself.

## How to read the build

Layer 1 sets up compact objects, presentable categories and weakly saturated classes, and proves
that in a presentable category the class generated by a small set is the lifting class produced by
the small object argument. Layer 2 introduces cell trees and proves that, in a κ-accessible category,
saturated maps are honest transfinite composites of pushouts of small cells; Layer 6 uses this to
find small generating sets of trivial cofibrations. Layer 3 is homotopy theory inside one model
category: homotopy extension, model structures on objects over and under a fixed object, properness
and homotopy pushouts. Layer 4 relates two model categories
by Quillen adjunctions and equivalences. Layer 5 develops accessible and perfect classes of
morphisms, the input to Smith's theorem. Layer 6 defines combinatorial model categories and proves
Smith's recognition theorem, its perfect-class form and the transfer theorem. Layer 7 studies
diagram categories J ⥤ A: the injective and projective classes and the projective model structure,
then Reedy categories, latching and matching objects and the Reedy model structure, which needs no
combinatorial hypothesis. Layer 8 records the closed monoidal and enriched vocabulary, defines tensors
and cotensors, and fixes coends (§8.3), which no earlier layer uses; it depends on no other layer and
serves ModelCategoryTheory, Parts SimplicialCategories and EnrichedLocalization.

## Layer 1: compact objects, presentable categories and weak saturation

### 1.1 κ-compact objects

**1.1.1.** Define κ-compactness of an object X of a cocomplete category as Mathlib's `IsCardinalPresentable X κ`
(the corepresented functor preserves colimits over κ-filtered small categories), and prove that it
agrees with the poset form: the comparison map from the colimit of the hom-sets into a diagram to the
hom-set into its colimit is bijective for every diagram indexed by a κ-filtered partially ordered set
(HTT, Def. A.1.1.1, p. 806). An object is small when it is κ-compact for some regular κ, which is
Mathlib's `IsPresentable`; a small object is then κ'-compact for every regular κ' ≥ κ (Mathlib
`isCardinalPresentable_monotone`; HTT, Def. A.1.1.1, p. 806, states it for sufficiently large κ′).

- A partially ordered set is κ-filtered in Lurie's sense (every κ-small subset has an upper bound)
  exactly when it is `IsCardinalFiltered` as a category; one direction is Mathlib's
  `isCardinalFiltered_preorder`.
- X is `IsCardinalPresentable X κ` exactly when the corepresented functor preserves colimits indexed
  by every small κ-filtered partially ordered set.

*Proof route.* Every small κ-filtered category receives a cofinal functor from a κ-directed partially
ordered set (Mathlib `IsCardinalFiltered.exists_cardinal_directed`), and colimits along cofinal
functors agree.

**Checks.**

- For κ = ℵ₀ in the category of sets, a finite set is κ-compact and the set ℕ is not: ℕ is the union
  of its initial segments, a directed colimit, and the identity of ℕ factors through no finite segment.
  A definition using only colimits of chains of length ω would still classify ℕ correctly, but one
  using arbitrary small colimits would make the two-point set fail: maps from two points into a
  coproduct A ⊔ B need not land in one summand.
- The empty set is κ-compact for every κ: the colimit of a κ-filtered diagram of one-point hom-sets is
  a point. A definition requiring preservation of all colimits including empty ones would wrongly
  reject it as well, since a κ-filtered poset is nonempty but an arbitrary diagram is not.
- In the category of sets, a set of cardinality ℵ₁ is ℵ₂-compact but not ℵ₁-compact; this pins the
  strict inequality "cardinality < κ": a definition with "≤ κ" would make it ℵ₁-compact.

*Needs:* Mathlib `IsCardinalPresentable`, `IsPresentable`, `isCardinalFiltered_preorder`,
`IsCardinalFiltered.exists_cardinal_directed`, `isCardinalPresentable_monotone`.

### 1.2 Presentable categories

**1.2.1.** Define a presentable category as Mathlib's `IsLocallyPresentable.{w}`, and prove that it agrees with
Lurie's four conditions: small colimits exist; a small set of objects generates every object as the
colimit of a small diagram in it; every object is small; and hom-sets are small (HTT, Def. A.1.1.2,
p. 806).

- For `C : Type u` with `Category.{v} C`, C is `IsLocallyPresentable.{w}` exactly when C has
  colimits of size w, is locally w-small, and there is a w-small set P of presentable objects such
  that every object lies in the closure of P under colimits of some small shape.
- Under the generation condition, smallness of every object follows from smallness of the members of
  P (HTT, Def. A.1.1.2 (3), p. 806).

*Proof route.* From Lurie's form: a small set of small objects is κ-compact for one regular κ; a set
generating under colimits is a strong generator (Mathlib `ObjectProperty.IsStrongGenerator.mk_of_exists_colimitsOfShape`);
conclude with `IsCardinalLocallyPresentable.iff_exists_isStrongGenerator`. Conversely Mathlib's
instances give local smallness and presentability of every object.

**Checks.**

- The category of sets is presentable with generating set the one-point set; each set is the
  coproduct of its points. A definition requiring generation by a single object under filtered
  colimits alone would fail here, since a two-point set is not a filtered colimit of one-point sets.
- The category of sets in the universe w is not presentable relative to a smaller universe: its
  hom-sets are not small. This rules out dropping Lurie's condition (4).
- The opposite of the category of sets is cocomplete and locally small, and every object is the
  colimit of one small diagram of finite sets: an infinite set X is the limit, in sets, of the sets
  F ⊔ {∗} over the finite subsets F ⊆ X, with the maps collapsing the points outside the smaller
  subset to ∗, and that limit is X ⊔ {∗} ≅ X. It is not presentable, so condition (3) is not implied
  by (1), (2) and (4), and the comparison must use it. The two-point set alone would not do for (2):
  a limit in sets of a diagram with all values the two-point set is empty or a power of the two-point
  set, never a three-point set.

*Needs:* Mathlib `IsLocallyPresentable`, `IsCardinalLocallyPresentable.iff_exists_isStrongGenerator`,
`ObjectProperty.IsStrongGenerator.mk_of_exists_colimitsOfShape`; §1.1.1.

### 1.3 Weakly saturated classes

**1.3.1.** Define `IsWeaklySaturated` for a class S of morphisms: S is stable under cobase change, under
transfinite composition indexed by well-ordered types in `Type w`, and under retracts
(HTT, Def. A.1.2.2, p. 807). Lurie's transfinite compositions are indexed by ordinals; Mathlib's
`IsStableUnderTransfiniteComposition.{w}` is indexed by well-ordered types with a bottom, which agree
by reindexing along the order type. Every statement using the notion assumes the ambient category
cocomplete, as Lurie does.

- A weakly saturated class contains every isomorphism and is closed under composition
  (HTT, Prop. A.1.2.3, p. 808): composition is the case of a two-step composite, and isomorphisms the case of
  the composite of the empty sequence followed by an isomorphism of arrows.
- Any intersection of weakly saturated classes is weakly saturated; hence each class S generates a
  smallest weakly saturated class containing it, `weakSaturation` (HTT, Rem. A.1.2.4, p. 808).
- For every class T, the left lifting class `T.llp` is weakly saturated; hence `weakSaturation S` is
  contained in `S.rlp.llp` (HTT, Rem. A.1.2.4, p. 808).

*Proof route.* The lifting class is stable under cobase change and retracts (Mathlib
`MorphismProperty.llp` API) and under transfinite composition (Mathlib's lifting through transfinite
compositions in `SmallObject/TransfiniteCompositionLifting.lean`).

**Checks.**

- In the category of sets, the class of injections is weakly saturated, while the class of injections
  whose image has finite complement is not: the composite of the ω-sequence ∅ ⟶ {0} ⟶ {0, 1} ⟶ ⋯
  with colimit ℕ has infinite complement, though each step adds one point. That class is stable under
  pushout, finite composition and retracts, so a definition with finite composition only would accept
  it.
- The class of all isomorphisms is weakly saturated and is generated by the empty set: the empty
  family generates its smallest weakly saturated class, which must contain the isomorphisms by
  Prop. A.1.2.3. A definition omitting transfinite compositions of length 0 would make the empty
  class weakly saturated and give the wrong generated class.
- In the category of sets, the class of maps that are isomorphisms or have empty source is stable
  under transfinite composition and retracts but not under cobase change: pushing ∅ ⟶ ∗ out along
  ∅ ⟶ ∗ gives ∗ ⟶ ∗ ⊔ ∗, which is neither. A definition omitting cobase change would accept it.

*Needs:* Mathlib `IsStableUnderCobaseChange`, `IsStableUnderTransfiniteComposition`,
`IsStableUnderRetracts`, `MorphismProperty.llp`.

### 1.4 The small object argument

**1.4.1.** Prove `hasSmallObjectArgument_of_isLocallyPresentable`: in a locally presentable category every
small set A of morphisms permits the small object argument, so Mathlib's functorial factorization
(`SmallObject.functorialFactorizationData`) factors each map f as a map in the weakly saturated class
generated by A followed by a map with the right lifting property against A (HTT, Prop. A.1.2.5 (1),
pp. 808–809). Prove moreover that if every source and target of A is κ-compact, the factorization
functor on `Arrow C` built at that κ preserves κ-filtered colimits (HTT, Prop. A.1.2.5 (2), p. 809;
Lurie's construction depends on κ).

*Proof route.* For (1): choose κ for which every source of A is κ-presentable; the well-ordered type
of κ's initial ordinal is κ-filtered, which gives Mathlib's `IsCardinalForSmallObjectArgument`; the
first factor lies in `A.rlp.llp`, a weakly saturated class containing the transfinite composites of
pushouts of coproducts of members of A, hence in the generated class by §1.3 and Prop. A.1.2.3. For
(2): each step of the iteration is built from sets of squares from members of A, which commute with
κ-filtered colimits in `Arrow C` when sources and targets are κ-compact, and from coproducts and
pushouts; the iteration is a colimit of such steps.

*Needs:* for (1), §1.1.1, §1.2.1, §1.3.1; Mathlib `MorphismProperty.HasSmallObjectArgument`,
`SmallObject.functorialFactorizationData`, `IsCardinalForSmallObjectArgument`.

*Needs:* for (2), §1.1.1, §1.2.1; Mathlib `SmallObject.functorialFactorizationData`, `SmallObject.iteration`.

### 1.5 The generated class as a lifting class

**1.5.1.** Prove `llp_rlp_eq_weakSaturation`: in a presentable category, for a small set A, the left lifting
class of the right lifting class of A is the weakly saturated class generated by A
(HTT, Cor. A.1.2.7, p. 809).

*Proof route.* Lurie's retract argument: one inclusion is §1.3; for the other, factor f by §1.4; if f
lifts against the second factor, f is a retract of the first, which lies in the generated class.

*Needs:* §1.3.1, §1.4.1 (1).

**1.5.2.** Prove the cell presentation under the source: in a presentable category, for a small set S and f : X ⟶ Y in the class generated
by S, there is a transfinite sequence of objects under X, each successive map from the colimit of the
earlier stages a pushout of a single member of S, such that Y is a retract of the colimit in the
category of objects under X (HTT, proofs of Prop. A.1.2.5 and Cor. A.1.2.7, pp. 808–810; stated in
Rem. A.1.2.8). Mathlib retracts in `Arrow C` and attaches coproducts of cells at each step; the
stronger form fixes the source.

*Proof route.* Well-order each family of cells attached at one stage to attach them one at a time, and
run the retract argument of Cor. A.1.2.7, which is the identity on X.

*Needs:* §1.4.1 (1), §1.5.1 (Cor. A.1.2.7); Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.RelativeCellComplex`.

### Examples

- In the category of sets, with A the single map from the empty set to a point, the first step of
  the construction of §1.4 already factors a map f : X ⟶ Z as X ⟶ X ⊔ Z ⟶ Z, an injection followed by
  a surjection (each later step attaches a further copy of Z); the weakly saturated class generated
  by A is the class of injections.
- In the category of sets, the maps with the right lifting property against the fold map from two
  points to one are the injections, and the left lifting class of the injections is the class of
  surjections; so by §1.5 the fold map generates the surjections.

### Dependencies

Mathlib's presentable categories and small object argument; nothing else.

## Layer 2: cell trees and saturation without retracts

### 2.1 Cell trees and their partial colimits

**2.1.1.** Define, for a class S of morphisms in a presentable category C, an S-tree with root X: a well-founded
partially ordered set A, a functor from A to the category of objects under X, written α ↦ Y_α, and for
each α a pushout square exhibiting the map from the colimit of the Y_β with β < α to Y_α as a pushout
of a member C ⟶ D of S. For regular κ the tree is κ-good when every such C and D is κ-compact and each
set of predecessors of an element is κ-small (HTT, Def. A.1.5.1, pp. 817–818). For a downward-closed
B ⊆ A the restriction is an S-tree, and Y_B denotes its colimit under X; the empty set gives Y_∅ ≅ X
and the set of elements ≤ β gives Y_β (HTT, Not. A.1.5.2, p. 818). A transfinite sequence of pushouts
of members of S is the case of a well-ordered A.

**Checks.**

- A tree indexed by the empty poset has Y_A = X, so X ⟶ Y_A is the identity. A definition taking the
  colimits in C rather than under X would give the initial object instead.
- In sets, a tree with root a one-point set X, indexed by the discrete two-element poset, with both
  cells the inclusion of the empty set into a point, has Y_α = X ⊔ ∗ for each α and Y_A the pushout of
  the two over X, a three-point set. The colimit taken in C would have four points, two copies of the
  root.
- In sets, the chain ℕ ∪ {ω} with ω on top and every cell ∅ ⟶ ∗ is ℵ₁-good but not ℵ₀-good, since ω
  has ℵ₀ predecessors, although every cell is ℵ₀-compact. This rules out reading κ-good as a
  condition on cells alone.

*Needs:* §1.1.1, §1.2.1.

### 2.2 Tree inclusions lie in the generated class

**2.2.1.** Prove that for downward-closed A″ ⊆ A′ ⊆ A in an S-tree, the map Y(A″) ⟶ Y(A′) lies in the weakly
saturated class generated by S; in particular so does X ⟶ Y_A (HTT, Lemma A.1.5.6, p. 818).

*Proof route.* Lurie's. Two operations on trees reduce the claim to A″ = ∅ and A′ = A. Pushing an
S-tree with root X along a map X ⟶ X′ gives an S-tree with root X′ and values the pushouts of the Y_α
along X ⟶ X′ (HTT, Rem. A.1.5.3, p. 818). For a downward-closed B, the objects Y(B ∪ {β : β ≤ α}) for
α ∉ B form an S-tree on A ∖ B with root Y_B, the difference tree (HTT, Rem. A.1.5.5, p. 818). Then
exhaust the well-founded A by a transfinite sequence of downward-closed subsets, adding one minimal
element at a time; each step is a pushout of a member of S.

*Needs:* §2.1.1; §1.3.1.

### 2.3 Descending small trees

**2.3.1.** Prove that if C is κ-accessible, every source of S is κ-compact and Y is an S-tree with root X on a
κ-small A, then there are a map X′ ⟶ X with X′ κ-compact and an S-tree Y′ with root X′ whose pushout
along X′ ⟶ X (§2.2) is isomorphic to Y (HTT, Lemma A.1.5.11, p. 824).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: present X as a κ-filtered colimit of κ-compact objects and descend the
attaching maps one element at a time along an exhaustion of A, choosing upper bounds at limit stages,
possible since A is κ-small.

*Needs:* §2.1.1, §2.2.1 (pushout of a tree along its root); §1.1.1, §1.2.1; Mathlib
`IsCardinalAccessibleCategory`.

### 2.4 Saturated maps as cell complexes of small cells

**2.4.1.** Prove `le_transfiniteCompositions_pushouts`: let κ be an uncountable regular cardinal, C presentable
and κ-accessible, S̄ a weakly saturated class and S ⊆ S̄ its members between κ-compact objects; if S
generates S̄ as a weakly saturated class, then every f : X ⟶ Y in S̄ is a transfinite composition of
pushouts of members of S, with the colimit identified with Y under X (HTT, Prop. A.1.5.12, p. 826).
Equivalently in tree form, every such f is X ⟶ Y_A for a κ-good S-tree with root X, with Y_A ≅ Y under
X (HTT, Cor. A.1.5.13, p. 827). Mathlib's `transfiniteCompositions` of `MorphismProperty.pushouts`
records exactly a transfinite sequence starting at X with colimit Y.

*Proof route.* Lurie's. The cell presentation under the source (§1.5) gives a transfinite sequence
whose colimit retracts onto Y under X; view it as a tree and make it κ-good, and let T be an
idempotent of its colimit with image Y. Build a transfinite chain of downward-closed subsets B(γ)
carrying compatible idempotents, each stage adding a κ-small downward-closed set around a minimal
element not yet covered; the difference trees (§2.2) are κ-small, so by §2.3 each stage map is a
pushout of a member of S, and the images of the idempotents form the required sequence. This uses the
descent lemmas (HTT, proof of Prop. A.1.5.12, Lemmas A.1.5.8–A.1.5.10, pp. 820–823):
- for κ uncountable, an idempotent of Y_A under X restricts to a compatible idempotent of Y_B for a
  κ-small downward-closed B containing any given κ-small subset, found by enlarging B along a
  countable chain, since Y_B is κ-compact under X (Lemma A.1.5.8, p. 820);
- the same relative to a given downward-closed B with compatible idempotents, alternating countably
  often between B and A (Lemma A.1.5.9, p. 821);
- in a κ-accessible C, a retract in `Arrow C` of a pushout of a map f between κ-compact objects is a
  pushout of a map between κ-compact objects lying in the weakly saturated class generated by f,
  obtained by descending the idempotent to a stage of a κ-filtered presentation of the source
  (Lemma A.1.5.10, p. 823).
Making a tree κ-good is Lurie's refinement (HTT, Lemma A.1.5.7, p. 819): when every source and target
of S is κ-compact, an S-tree on (A, ≤) with root X admits a coarser partial order ≼ on A (α ≼ β implies
α ≤ β), a κ-good S-tree Y′ on (A, ≼) with the same root, and compatible maps Y′_α ⟶ Y_α inducing
isomorphisms Y′_B ≅ Y_B for every ≤-downward-closed B. It is built by transfinite induction along an
exhaustion of A by downward-closed subsets adding one minimal element at a time; at each step the
attaching map out of the κ-compact source factors through the colimit over a κ-small
downward-closed subset, which becomes the new set of ≼-predecessors. The tree form follows by viewing
the sequence as a tree and making it κ-good in the same way.

*Needs:* §1.1.1, §1.2.1, §1.3.1, §1.5.2; §2.1.1, §2.2.1, §2.3.1; Mathlib `IsCardinalAccessibleCategory`,
`transfiniteCompositions`, `MorphismProperty.pushouts`.

### Examples

- In the category of sets with S̄ the injections and κ = ℵ₁: S is the injections between countable
  sets, and an injection X ⟶ Y is the transfinite composite attaching the points of Y ∖ X one at a
  time, each a pushout of the inclusion of the empty set into a point; no retract is needed.

## Layer 3: homotopy, properness and homotopy pushouts in a model category

### 3.1 Homotopy relations and the homotopy category

**3.1.1.** Prove that for X cofibrant, Y fibrant and f, g : X ⟶ Y the following are equivalent: f and g are left
homotopic through every cylinder object for X; through some cylinder object; f and g are right
homotopic through every path object for Y; through some path object (HTT, Prop. A.2.2.1, p. 829).
Lurie's cylinder objects (the map from X ⊔ X a cofibration, the map to X a weak equivalence) are
Mathlib's good cylinders (`Cylinder` with `Cylinder.IsGood`), and his path objects are Mathlib's good
path objects (`PathObject` with `PathObject.IsGood`).

*Proof route.* Mathlib's `leftHomotopyRel_iff_rightHomotopyRel` gives the some-to-some equivalence,
and `LeftHomotopyRel.leftHomotopy` and `RightHomotopyRel.rightHomotopy` give the homotopy through any
good cylinder or path object.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Cylinder.IsGood`, `PathObject.IsGood`, `leftHomotopyRel_iff_rightHomotopyRel`,
`LeftHomotopyRel.leftHomotopy`, `RightHomotopyRel.rightHomotopy`.

**3.1.2.** Prove that the bifibrant objects modulo homotopy form a category hC, and that the evident functor from
hC to the localization of C at the weak equivalences is an equivalence; in particular the
localization is locally small when C is (HTT, §A.2.2, after Prop. A.2.2.1, and Rem. A.2.2.2, p. 830).

*Proof route.* Mathlib's `BifibrantObject.HoCat` is the quotient category, `BifibrantObject.toHoCat`
a localization of the bifibrant objects, and composing with the inclusion of bifibrant objects is a
localization of C; uniqueness of localizations gives the equivalence.

*Needs:* §3.1.1 (Prop. A.2.2.1); Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`,
`BifibrantObject.HoCat`, `BifibrantObject.toHoCat`, `Functor.IsLocalization`.

### 3.2 Homotopy extension

**3.2.1.** Prove `exists_extension_of_cofibration`: let A and B be cofibrant, X fibrant, i : A ⟶ B a cofibration
and f : A ⟶ X. For a localization L of C at the weak equivalences, if a map g : L(B) ⟶ L(X) satisfies
L(i) ≫ g = L(f), then g = L(g′) for a map g′ : B ⟶ X with i ≫ g′ = f (HTT, Prop. A.2.3.1, p. 830).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: represent g by some g₀ (Mathlib `map_surjective_of_isLocalization`); i ≫ g₀ is
homotopic to f (Mathlib `LeftHomotopyRel.iff_map_eq`); choose a good cylinder for A, and factor the
induced map from the pushout of B ⊔ B with that cylinder into a cofibration followed by a trivial
fibration over B, which is a good cylinder for B; the inclusion of the cylinder of A glued with B into
it is a trivial cofibration, so the homotopy extends, and its other end is g′.

*Needs:* §3.1.1; Mathlib `map_surjective_of_isLocalization`, `LeftHomotopyRel.iff_map_eq`, `Cylinder`.

### 3.3 Model structures on objects over and under a fixed object

**3.3.1.** Let S be an object of a model category C. Mathlib's `ModelCategory.over` makes the over-category
`Over S` a model category in which a map of objects over S is a cofibration, a fibration or a weak
equivalence exactly when its underlying map in C is one (Hirschhorn, Thm. 7.6.5; May–Ponto, §15.3).
This section supplies the dual.

Define `modelCategoryUnder S`, the model structure on the under-category `Under S`: a map of objects
under S is a cofibration, a fibration or a weak equivalence exactly when its underlying map in C is
one, so the forgetful functor from `Under S` to C creates all three classes (Hirschhorn, Thm. 7.6.5;
May–Ponto, §15.3).

- Construction: `Under S` is equivalent to the opposite of the over-category of S regarded as an
  object of Cᵒᵖ (Mathlib `Over.opEquivOpUnder`). Mathlib's model structure on Cᵒᵖ, then
  `ModelCategory.over`, then the opposite model structure again give a model structure on that
  category, and `ModelCategory.transport` carries it back along the equivalence. The three classes
  on `Under S` are `MorphismProperty.under` of the classes of C, and the inverse-image hypotheses of
  the transport hold by unfolding the definitions.
- The transported structure has cofibrations, weak equivalences and fibrations the classes of maps
  whose underlying map in C is a cofibration, a weak equivalence or a fibration, by construction. In
  particular the trivial cofibrations and trivial fibrations are also created by
  the forgetful functor.
- An object S ⟶ X under S is cofibrant exactly when S ⟶ X is a cofibration of C, and fibrant exactly
  when X is fibrant in C. Dually, an object X ⟶ S over S is fibrant exactly when X ⟶ S is a
  fibration, and cofibrant exactly when X is cofibrant.
- For a map f : S ⟶ T, pushout along f, from objects under S to objects under T, is left adjoint to
  precomposition with f; precomposition does not change underlying maps, so it preserves fibrations
  and trivial fibrations; pushout along f therefore preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations,
  each being the left lifting class of its partner, since lifting problems transfer across the
  adjunction (Mathlib `Adjunction.hasLiftingProperty_iff`).
- Taking S terminal gives pointed objects: the model structure on objects under the terminal object
  is the pointed model structure, with all classes detected after forgetting the base point.

**Checks.**

- In Mathlib's bounded-below cochain complexes of abelian groups every object is cofibrant, but
  under Sⁿ the object Sⁿ ⟶ 0 is not cofibrant, since Sⁿ ⟶ 0 is not a monomorphism. A definition
  calling an object under S cofibrant when its underlying object is cofibrant would accept it; the
  created classes reject it.
- In the injective structure on sets (Conventions), under a point ∗, the map from the two-point set
  pointed at one point to ∗ is surjective and not injective: a fibration that is not a cofibration.
  A transport through the opposite category that exchanged the two classes only once would call it
  a cofibration and not a fibration.
- Under an initial object ∅ of C, the forgetful functor from `Under ∅` to C is an equivalence, and the
  structure agrees with that of C. A definition that also required the structure maps S ⟶ X to be
  cofibrations of C would remove from `Under ∅` the objects that are not cofibrant in C, so it would
  not be equivalent to C as soon as C has an object that is not cofibrant.
- Dually, `Over S` for S terminal is equivalent to C with the same classes, the witness that Mathlib's
  `ModelCategory.over` uses the underlying map rather than the structure map X ⟶ S.

*Needs:* Mathlib `ModelCategory.over`, `ModelCategory.transport`, `Over.opEquivOpUnder`,
`MorphismProperty.under`; Mathlib's model structure on the opposite category
(`AlgebraicTopology/ModelCategory/Opposite.lean`); for pushout along f, Mathlib `Under.pushout`,
`Under.mapPushoutAdj` and `Adjunction.hasLiftingProperty_iff`.

### 3.4 Proper model categories

**3.4.1.** Define `IsLeftProper`: in every pushout square whose top map i is a cofibration and whose left map j
is a weak equivalence, the opposite map j′ is a weak equivalence. Dually, right proper asks that
weak equivalences pull back along fibrations, that is, the opposite model category is left proper
(HTT, Def. A.2.4.1, p. 832).

**Checks.**

- In every model category weak equivalences are preserved by pushout along trivial cofibrations
  (two-out-of-three in the square), so a definition testing only trivial cofibrations would make
  every model category left proper and Prop. A.2.4.2 empty.
- Mathlib's model structure on bounded-below cochain complexes of abelian groups (cofibrations the
  monomorphisms, weak equivalences the quasi-isomorphisms) has every object cofibrant, so it is left
  proper by Prop. A.2.4.2 (§3.4.2). Let D be the acyclic complex ℤ ⟶ ℤ (identity) in degrees n and n + 1
  and Sⁿ the complex ℤ in degree n; the projection D ⟶ Sⁿ is a chain map, not a monomorphism, and the
  pushout of the quasi-isomorphism D ⟶ 0 along it is Sⁿ ⟶ 0, not a quasi-isomorphism. A definition
  asking all pushouts of weak equivalences to be weak equivalences would make this model category
  fail.
- In the same model category, push the cofibration 0 ⟶ Sⁿ out along the quasi-isomorphism 0 ⟶ D:
  the map opposite to the weak equivalence is Sⁿ ⟶ D ⊕ Sⁿ, a quasi-isomorphism, while the map
  opposite to the cofibration is D ⟶ D ⊕ Sⁿ, which is not. A definition asking the latter to be a
  weak equivalence is false even here, which pins which map of the square is tested.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, `IsPushout`.

**3.4.2.** Prove that for a pushout square with top map i a cofibration, left map j : A ⟶ A′ a weak equivalence,
and both its source A and its target A′ cofibrant, the opposite map j′ is a weak equivalence
(HTT, Lemma A.2.4.3, p. 832). Consequently `isLeftProper_of_isCofibrant`: a model category in which
every object is cofibrant is left proper (HTT, Prop. A.2.4.2, p. 832).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: j′ is an isomorphism in the homotopy category because precomposition with it
is bijective on homotopy classes into every fibrant object; surjectivity by §3.2, injectivity by
applying surjectivity in the model category over Z × Z (Mathlib `ModelCategory.over`) to a path
object. When every object is cofibrant the lemma applies to every pushout square of Def. A.2.4.1.

*Needs:* §3.1.1, §3.1.2, §3.2.1; Mathlib `ModelCategory.over`, `PathObject`.

### 3.5 Homotopy pushout squares

**3.5.1.** Define a cofibrant resolution of a span A₀ ⟵ A ⟶ A₁: a span A′₀ ⟵ A′ ⟶ A′₁ with A′ cofibrant and both
maps cofibrations, with a map of spans to the given one that is a weak equivalence at each object. A
commutative square with corner M under the span is a homotopy pushout square when, for a cofibrant
resolution, the composite A′₀ ⊔ A′₁ (amalgamated over A′) ⟶ A₀ ⊔_A A₁ ⟶ M is a weak equivalence
(HTT, §A.2.4, after Lemma A.2.4.3, p. 833).

- Cofibrant resolutions exist, by factoring A's initial map and then the two legs.
- The condition does not depend on the resolution, so "for some" and "for every" agree.

*Proof route.* Independence is the cube lemma: a map of cofibrant spans with one leg a cofibration
that is a weak equivalence at each object induces a weak equivalence of pushouts. It follows from
Lemma A.2.4.3 and Ken Brown's factorization (Mathlib `CofibrantBrownFactorization`).

**Checks.**

- In Mathlib's bounded-below cochain complexes of abelian groups, the span 0 ⟵ Sⁿ ⟶ 0 has strict
  pushout 0, but its homotopy pushout is Sⁿ⁻¹ (resolve by Sⁿ ⟶ D′ with D′ acyclic, ℤ in degrees n − 1
  and n): so the square with corner 0 is not a homotopy pushout. A definition using the strict pushout
  would accept it.
- A square whose two maps out of the corner A are isomorphisms and whose map from the pushout to M is
  a weak equivalence is a homotopy pushout, the degenerate case. A definition requiring the given span
  itself to be cofibrant would reject such squares when A is not cofibrant.
- The square of the first Check is a pushout along the map Sⁿ ⟶ 0, which is not a cofibration; the
  pushout of the same span after replacing one leg by the cofibration Sⁿ ⟶ D′ is D′/Sⁿ ≅ Sⁿ⁻¹, a
  homotopy pushout as Prop. A.2.4.4 (§3.5.2) predicts. This pins the cofibration hypothesis.
- In the same model category every object is cofibrant, so a definition of cofibrant resolution
  asking only that A′ be cofibrant would let the span 0 ⟵ Sⁿ ⟶ 0 resolve itself and make the square
  of the first Check a homotopy pushout; requiring the legs to be cofibrations rules this out.

*Needs:* §3.4.2 (Lemma A.2.4.3); Mathlib `CofibrantBrownFactorization`.

**3.5.2.** Prove that a pushout square on a span A₀ ⟵ A ⟶ A₁ whose leg A ⟶ A₁ is a cofibration is a homotopy
pushout square if either A and A₀ are cofibrant, or C is left proper (HTT, Prop. A.2.4.4, p. 834).

*Proof route.* Lurie omits the proof. Case (i) is the cube lemma of §3.5.1. For case (ii), with the
cofibration i : A ⟶ A₁ and g : A ⟶ A₀, take a cofibrant resolution A′₀ ⟵ A′ ⟶ A′₁ and let R be the
pushout of A′ ⟶ A′₁ along A′ ⟶ A. By left properness A′₁ ⟶ R is a weak equivalence, so R ⟶ A₁ is one
by two-out-of-three. The comparison from the pushout of the resolution to A₀ ⊔ A₁ (amalgamated over
A) factors through the pushout of A₀ ⟵ A ⟶ R. The first factor is a pushout of the weak equivalence
A′₀ ⟶ A₀ along a cofibration, hence a weak equivalence by left properness. The second is the image
of the weak equivalence R ⟶ A₁ between cofibrant objects under A under pushout along g. That
functor, from the model category under A to the one under A₀ (§3.3), preserves cofibrations and
trivial cofibrations (§3.3), so Ken Brown's
lemma makes the image a weak equivalence.

*Needs:* §3.3.1 (objects under A and A₀, pushout along g); Def. A.2.4.1 (§3.4.1), Lemma A.2.4.3 (§3.4.2);
§3.5.1; Mathlib `CofibrantBrownFactorization`.

**3.5.3.** Define homotopy pullback squares dually, through fibrant resolutions of cospans, and prove the dual of
Prop. A.2.4.4: a pullback square along a fibration is a homotopy pullback square if the objects in the
cospan are fibrant, or C is right proper (dual of HTT, Prop. A.2.4.4, p. 834; stated in
Rem. A.2.4.5).

*Proof route.* Apply the pushout statements in the opposite model category.

**Checks.**

- In the opposite of Mathlib's bounded-below cochain complexes, the cospan 0 ⟶ Sⁿ ⟵ 0 has strict
  pullback 0 while its homotopy pullback is Sⁿ⁻¹, dual to the first Check on homotopy pushouts; so the
  square with corner 0 is not a homotopy pullback square, and a definition by strict pullback would
  accept it.
- In the trivial structure on sets every object is fibrant and every map a fibration, so a cospan is
  its own fibrant resolution and a square is a homotopy pullback exactly when its corner maps
  bijectively to the pullback. For the two different points of ∗ ⊔ ∗ the pullback is empty, so the
  square with corner ∅ is a homotopy pullback; a definition comparing the corner with the product
  would reject it.
- In the injective structure on sets every map is a weak equivalence, so every commutative square is
  a homotopy pullback, although the empty set is not fibrant (∅ ⟶ ∗ is not surjective). A definition
  requiring the given cospan itself to be fibrant would reject the pullback of ∅ ⟶ ∗ ⟵ ∅.

*Needs:* §3.4.1 (Def. A.2.4.1), §3.5.2 (Prop. A.2.4.4); Mathlib's model structure on the opposite category.

### Examples

- In Mathlib's bounded-below cochain complexes of abelian groups every object is cofibrant, so the

- Under the terminal object of a model category, §3.3 gives the pointed model structure, in which a
  map of pointed objects is a weak equivalence, cofibration or fibration when it is one after
  forgetting the base points.

## Layer 4: Quillen adjunctions and Quillen equivalences

### 4.1 Quillen adjunctions and derived functors

**4.1.1.** Define `IsQuillenAdjunction` for an adjunction F ⊣ G between model categories, F : C ⥤ D: F preserves
cofibrations and trivial cofibrations (HTT, §A.2.5, before Prop. A.2.5.1, p. 835).

- Prove `isQuillenAdjunction_tfae`: the following are equivalent: F preserves cofibrations and
  trivial cofibrations; G preserves fibrations and trivial fibrations; F preserves cofibrations and G
  preserves fibrations; F preserves trivial cofibrations and G preserves trivial fibrations (HTT,
  §A.2.5, p. 835). *Proof route.* Lifting properties transfer across the adjunction (Mathlib
  `Adjunction.hasLiftingProperty_iff`), and each class is the lifting class of its partner.

  *Needs:* Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, `Adjunction.hasLiftingProperty_iff`.
- F preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant objects and G preserves weak equivalences between
  fibrant objects (HTT, §A.2.5, p. 835). *Proof route.* Ken Brown's lemma (Mathlib
  `CofibrantBrownFactorization`).

  *Needs:* Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, `CofibrantBrownFactorization`; §3.1.
- Prove `hasLeftDerivedFunctor_of_isQuillenAdjunction`: F composed with the localization of D has a
  total left derived functor along the localization of C; dually G has a total right derived functor,
  and the two derived functors are adjoint (HTT, §A.2.5, p. 835). *Proof route.* The cofibrant
  objects form a left derivability structure (Mathlib `CofibrantObject.localizerMorphism`), and F
  inverts weak equivalences between them; the adjunction of derived functors is Mathlib's
  `Adjunction.derived`.

  *Needs:* the preservation of weak equivalences above; §3.1.2; Mathlib `CofibrantObject.localizerMorphism`,
  `Functor.HasLeftDerivedFunctor`, `Adjunction.derived`.

**Checks.**

- For any model category C, the functor from the terminal category (with its unique model
  structure) picking the initial object, left adjoint to the functor to the terminal category, is a
  Quillen adjunction: it carries the only map, an identity, to an identity. In sets with the
  injective structure the initial object ∅ is not fibrant, since ∅ ⟶ ∗ is not surjective, so a
  definition asking F also to preserve fibrant objects would reject it.
- On sets, the identity functor from the injective structure to the trivial structure, left adjoint to
  the identity, preserves cofibrations but not trivial cofibrations: ∅ ⟶ ∗ is a trivial cofibration of
  the injective structure but not a bijection. A definition asking only that F preserve cofibrations
  would accept it. The other conditions of the TFAE fail as well: the right adjoint, the identity from
  the trivial to the injective structure, carries the fibration ∅ ⟶ ∗ of the trivial structure to a
  map that is not a surjection.
- On sets, the identity from the trivial structure to the injective structure is not a left Quillen
  functor, since the fold map ∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗ is a cofibration of the first and not an injection, although
  it preserves trivial cofibrations (bijections), so a definition asking only for trivial cofibrations
  to be preserved would accept it; with the
  right adjoint the identity from the injective to the trivial structure, the surjection ∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗ is a
  trivial fibration of the injective structure whose image is not a bijection, so the second condition
  of the TFAE fails too, as it must.

*Needs:* for the definition and the Checks, Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`.

### 4.2 Quillen equivalences

**4.2.1.** Define `IsQuillenEquivalence`: a Quillen adjunction such that for every cofibrant X in C and fibrant
Y in D, a map X ⟶ G(Y) is a weak equivalence exactly when its adjoint F(X) ⟶ Y is (HTT, Prop. A.2.5.1,
condition (3), p. 835). Prove `isQuillenEquivalence_iff_isEquivalence`: for a Quillen adjunction and
any total left derived functor LF of F between localizations at the weak equivalences, the adjunction
is a Quillen equivalence exactly when LF is an equivalence; equivalently, when the total right
derived functor of G is (HTT, Prop. A.2.5.1, p. 835).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: the derived functors are adjoint, so one is an equivalence exactly when the
other is, exactly when the derived unit and counit are isomorphisms; evaluated on a cofibrant X with a
fibrant replacement of F(X), and on a fibrant Y with a cofibrant replacement of G(Y), these say
precisely condition (3) in each direction.

*Needs:* §4.1.1; Mathlib `Functor.IsLeftDerivedFunctor`, `Functor.IsLocalization`.

**Checks.**

- On sets, the identity from the trivial structure to the structure with all maps weak
  equivalences, all maps cofibrations and the bijections as fibrations (Conventions) is a Quillen
  adjunction but not a Quillen equivalence: the only fibrant object of the target is the point, and
  for X empty the map ∅ ⟶ ∗ is not a bijection while its adjoint, the same map in the target, is a
  weak equivalence. A definition counting every adjoint isomorphism of categories that is a Quillen
  adjunction as a Quillen equivalence would accept it.
- Let F from the terminal category (with its unique model structure) to sets with the injective
  structure pick the empty set, left adjoint to the functor to the terminal category. It is a Quillen
  equivalence: the fibrant sets are the nonempty ones, and for such Y both the map ∗ ⟶ ∗ and the map
  ∅ ⟶ Y are weak equivalences. F is not an equivalence of categories, ruling out that reading.
- On sets with the trivial structure, X ↦ X ⊔ X, left adjoint to Y ↦ Y × Y, is a Quillen adjunction but
  not a Quillen equivalence: for X a point and Y a point, the map ∗ ⟶ ∗ × ∗ is a bijection and its
  adjoint ∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗ is not. A definition testing condition (3) only for X the empty set would accept
  it, since then both maps are bijections exactly when Y is empty.

### Examples

- The identity functors between two model structures on one category with the same weak
  equivalences and with cofibrations of the first contained in those of the second form a Quillen
  equivalence.

## Layer 5: accessible and perfect classes of morphisms

### 5.1 Accessible subcategories

**5.1.1.** Define `IsCardinalAccessibleSubcategory`: for C presentable and κ regular, a full subcategory is
κ-accessible when it is closed in C under κ-filtered colimits and a small set of its objects
generates it under κ-filtered colimits; define `IsAccessibleSubcategory` as κ-accessible for some
regular κ (HTT, Def. A.2.6.2, p. 836). Lurie indexes by κ-filtered posets, which agrees by §1.1. A
class of morphisms is accessible when the full subcategory of `Arrow C` it spans is.

**Checks.**

- In sets, the full subcategory of nonempty sets is ℵ₀-accessible: a filtered colimit of nonempty
  sets is nonempty, and every nonempty set is the directed union of its nonempty finite subsets, so the
  nonempty finite sets (a small set up to isomorphism) generate it. The one-point set alone does not
  generate it: a filtered colimit of one-point sets is a point.
- In sets, the full subcategory of sets with at most one element is ℵ₀-accessible, generated by the
  empty set and the point, although it is not closed under coproducts. A definition asking for
  closure under all small colimits would reject it.
- The empty full subcategory is accessible, generated by the empty set of objects. A definition
  requiring a nonempty generating set would reject it.

*Needs:* §1.1.1, §1.2.1; Mathlib `ObjectProperty.IsClosedUnderColimitsOfShape`,
`ObjectProperty.colimitsOfShape`, `ObjectProperty.Small`, `IsCardinalFiltered`.

### 5.2 Sharply larger cardinals

**5.2.1.** Define, for regular κ and τ, the relation κ ≪ τ: τ′ to the power κ′ is below τ for every τ′ < τ and
κ′ < κ (HTT, Def. A.2.6.3, p. 837). The relation itself does not include regularity; every statement
using it assumes κ and τ regular, as HTT does. It differs from Mathlib's `Cardinal.SharplyLT`
(Adámek–Rosický's sharply less than), which is irreflexive; Mathlib's `Cardinal.SharplyLT.of_pow_lt`
proves that κ < τ and κ ≪ τ give `Cardinal.SharplyLT κ τ`.

- Every regular τ₀ is exceeded by a regular τ with κ ≪ τ, namely the successor of τ₀ to the power κ
  (HTT, Def. A.2.6.3, p. 837).

**Checks.**

- ℵ₀ ≪ τ holds for every regular uncountable τ and for τ = ℵ₀: finite powers of cardinals below τ stay
  below τ. A definition requiring κ < τ would reject ℵ₀ ≪ ℵ₀.
- ℵ₁ ≪ ℵ₁ fails: ℵ₀ to the power ℵ₀ is at least ℵ₁. This separates ≪ from ≤.
- Under the continuum hypothesis, ℵ₁ ≪ ℵ₂ holds; without it, ℵ₁ ≪ τ still holds for τ the successor
  of the continuum, since τ′ to the power ℵ₀ is at most the continuum for every τ′ < τ. A definition
  with "at most τ" in place of "below τ" would, under the continuum hypothesis, accept ℵ₁ ≪ ℵ₁, since
  ℵ₀ to the power ℵ₀ is then ℵ₁.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT`, `Cardinal.SharplyLT.of_pow_lt`.

### 5.3 The accessibility criterion

**5.3.1.** Prove: let C be presentable and C₀ a full subcategory closed under κ-filtered colimits. Then C₀ is
generated under κ-filtered colimits by a small set exactly when, for all sufficiently large regular
τ with κ ≪ τ, the following holds: for every τ-filtered poset A and diagram of τ-compact objects with
colimit in C₀, every τ-small subset of A lies in a τ-small κ-filtered subset whose partial colimit is
in C₀ (HTT, Prop. A.2.6.5, p. 837).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: if the condition holds, the τ-compact objects of C₀ generate it under
τ-filtered colimits, since each object of C₀ is the τ-filtered colimit of its good partial colimits;
conversely, given a generating set of τ-compact objects, compare the two presentations of an object
of C₀ by matching colimit presentations (HTT, Lemma A.2.6.6, p. 837): for κ ≪ τ with τ > κ regular, D
presentable, and two families of τ-compact objects indexed by τ-filtered posets A and B with
isomorphic colimits, any τ-small A₀ ⊆ A and B₀ ⊆ B lie in τ-small κ-filtered A′ ⊇ A₀ and B′ ⊇ B₀
whose colimits are isomorphic. That is the classical back-and-forth argument (Adámek–Rosický,
*Locally presentable and accessible categories*, ch. 2): a chain of length κ alternately enlarging
the subsets on each side, using τ-compactness of τ-small κ-filtered colimits of τ-compact objects,
and the fact that every τ-small subset of a κ-directed poset lies in a τ-small κ-directed one
(Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT` API); unions along a chain of length κ stay κ-filtered by regularity
and τ-small since κ < τ. This stays in ordinary categories; HTT deduces the lemma from an
∞-categorical statement instead.

*Needs:* §1.1.1, §1.2.1, §5.1.1, §5.2.1; Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT`.

### 5.4 Preimages and intersections

**5.4.1.** Prove that for a functor f : C ⥤ D between presentable categories preserving κ-filtered colimits and a
κ-accessible full subcategory D₀ of D, the preimage of D₀ is a κ-accessible subcategory of C
(HTT, Cor. A.2.6.7, p. 838).

*Proof route.* Lurie gives none. Closure under κ-filtered colimits is immediate. For generation use §5.3 at one
cardinal: Mathlib's `IsCardinalAccessibleCategory.uniformization` gives a single κ₀ ≥ κ at which f preserves
κ₀-compact objects, and `uniformization_aux` then gives every τ sharply above κ₀ — for instance the successor of `2 ^
ν` with ν ≥ κ₀, which also satisfies κ ≪ τ — so the criterion for D₀ gives the criterion for its preimage.

*Needs:* §5.1.1, §5.3.1 (Prop. A.2.6.5); Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT`, `IsCardinalAccessibleCategory.uniformization`,
`Functor.PreservesCardinalPresentable`.

**5.4.2.** Prove that the intersection of a small family of accessible subcategories of a presentable category
is accessible. This is the ordinary-categorical case of the ∞-categorical HTT, Prop. 5.4.7.10 (PDF
p. 470), which HTT invokes in the proof of Cor. A.2.6.8 (p. 838); classically it is in Adámek–Rosický,
ch. 2.

*Proof route.* Through §5.3: choose a regular κ for which every member is κ-accessible and which
exceeds the size of the family (Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT.exists_of_small`), and τ ≫ κ for which
every member satisfies the criterion; build a chain of τ-small κ-filtered subsets of length κ that
visits every member cofinally often; its union is κ-filtered and τ-small, and its partial colimit lies
in every member, being a κ-filtered colimit of objects of that member.

*Needs:* §5.1.1, §5.2.1, §5.3.1; Mathlib `Cardinal.SharplyLT.exists_of_small`.

### 5.5 Perfect classes of morphisms

**5.5.1.** Define `IsPerfect` for a class W of morphisms in a presentable category A: W contains the isomorphisms,
has two-out-of-three, is stable under filtered colimits, and some small W₀ ⊆ W has every member of W a
filtered colimit, in `Arrow A`, of members of W₀ (HTT, Def. A.2.6.12, p. 843). Mathlib's stability under
filtered colimits indexed by small categories agrees with Lurie's poset form by §1.1; with the last
two conditions it says that W spans an ℵ₀-accessible full subcategory of `Arrow A`.

- A perfect class is accessible in the sense of §5.1: its last two conditions say that it is
  ℵ₀-accessible (used in HTT, proof of Prop. A.2.6.15, p. 844, to apply Prop. A.2.6.10). *Needs:* §5.1.1,
  the definition above.
- The isomorphisms of a presentable category form a perfect class (HTT, Ex. A.2.6.13, p. 843).
- Prove `isPerfect_inverseImage`: for a functor between presentable categories preserving filtered
  colimits, the preimage of a perfect class is perfect (HTT, Cor. A.2.6.14, p. 843). *Proof route.*
  §5.4 with κ = ℵ₀, applied to the induced functor on arrow categories; the first three conditions
  pull back directly. *Needs:* §5.4.1, the definition above.

**Checks.**

- In sets, the isomorphisms together with all maps between finite sets contain the isomorphisms,
  have two-out-of-three and are filtered colimits of maps between finite sets, but they are not
  stable under filtered colimits: the inclusions of {0, …, n} into {0, …, n, ∗} have colimit the
  inclusion of ℕ into ℕ ⊔ {∗}, which is not a bijection. A definition omitting stability under
  filtered colimits would accept this class.
- In sets, the class of all maps is perfect, with W₀ the maps between finite sets. A definition
  requiring W₀ to consist of isomorphisms would reject it.
- In sets, the class of injections is not perfect, failing two-out-of-three (a non-injective g with
  g ∘ f injective). A definition that only asked for closure under composition would accept it.

*Needs:* §5.1.1, §5.4.1; Mathlib `HasTwoOutOfThreeProperty`, `IsStableUnderFilteredColimits`,
`MorphismProperty.IsSmall`.

### Examples

- For a ring R, the quasi-isomorphisms of chain complexes of R-modules are the preimage of the
  isomorphisms under the homology functor to graded modules, which preserves filtered colimits; so by
  §5.5 they form a perfect class.

Layer 1; Mathlib's cardinal and accessibility theory.

## Layer 6: combinatorial model categories

### 6.1 Combinatorial model categories

**6.1.1.** Define `IsCombinatorial` for a model category: it is presentable, and there are small sets I and J
with the cofibrations equal to the weakly saturated class generated by I and the trivial
cofibrations equal to the one generated by J (HTT, Def. A.2.6.1, p. 836; Smith's notion). By §1.5
these classes are `I.rlp.llp` and `J.rlp.llp`, so the fibrations are `J.rlp` and the trivial
fibrations are `I.rlp`.

**Checks.**

- The trivial model structure on sets (weak equivalences the bijections, all maps cofibrations and
  fibrations) is combinatorial with I = {∅ ⟶ ∗, ∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗} and J empty. A definition requiring J to
  generate the cofibrations would fail here.
- The trivial structure on sets is not generated by I = {∅ ⟶ ∗}: that set generates only the
  injections, strictly fewer than all maps. The definition asks for equality with the cofibrations; a
  definition asking only that the generated class be contained in the cofibrations would accept this
  I.
- The model structure on topological spaces is not combinatorial: the category of spaces is not
  presentable. A definition with only the generating-set conditions (cofibrantly generated) would
  accept it.

*Needs:* §1.2.1, §1.3.1, §1.5.1; Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, `MorphismProperty.IsSmall`.

**6.1.2.** Prove `isAccessibleSubcategory_of_isCombinatorial`: in a combinatorial model category, the fibrations,
the weak equivalences and the trivial fibrations span accessible full subcategories of `Arrow C`
(HTT, Cor. A.2.6.8, p. 838; Smith).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: for each map i : A ⟶ B the functor from `Arrow C` to arrows of sets sending f
to the map from Hom(B, X) to the set of squares from i to f preserves κ-filtered colimits for κ with A
and B κ-compact; the preimage of the surjections is accessible (§5.4); fibrations and trivial
fibrations are the intersections over J and I (§5.4.2), since the right lifting class of
a set is that of the class it generates (§1.3); the weak equivalences are the preimage of the trivial
fibrations under the second factor of the small object argument for J, which preserves κ-filtered
colimits for large κ (§1.4 (2)).

*Needs:* §1.3.1, §1.4.1, §5.4.1, §5.4.2, §6.1.1.

### 6.2 Generating trivial cofibrations

**6.2.1.** Fix a presentable A and classes C and W such that C is weakly saturated and generated by a small
subset, C ∩ W is weakly saturated, W is accessible and W has two-out-of-three. Fix, as in Lurie's
proof, κ with W κ-accessible and τ ≫ κ with τ > κ, W satisfying the criterion of §5.3 at τ, A
τ-accessible and C generated by its members between τ-compact objects, and let C₀ be those members.
For a τ-good C₀-tree with root X (§2.1), call a downward-closed subset B good when X ⟶ Y_B lies in W.

- The union of an increasing transfinite family of good subsets is good; every τ-small subset of a
  good B lies in a τ-small good subset of B; if B, B′ and B ∩ B′ are good, so is B ∪ B′ (HTT, proof of
  Lemma A.2.6.9, p. 840). *Proof route.* The first by weak saturation of C ∩ W; the second by the
  criterion of §5.3; the third through the pushout square of Y(B∩B′), Y_B, Y(B′), Y(B∪B′), whose
  maps lie in C by §2.2: Y(B∩B′) ⟶ Y(B′) lies in W by two-out-of-three, hence in C ∩ W; so does its
  pushout Y_B ⟶ Y(B∪B′), since the weakly saturated class C ∩ W is stable under pushout; and
  two-out-of-three again puts X ⟶ Y(B∪B′) in W. *Needs:* §2.1.1, §2.2.1, §5.1.1, §5.3.1; §1.3.1.
- If the whole index set is good: for a good A′ and a τ-small B₀ there is a τ-small good B ⊇ B₀ with
  B ∩ A′ good; hence there is a transfinite sequence of good subsets A(γ) exhausting A in which each
  stage A(γ) is the union of the earlier stages with a new τ-small good subset (limit stages included),
  so that each difference from the union of the earlier stages is τ-small (HTT, proof of Lemma A.2.6.9,
  pp. 840–841). *Proof route.* For the first, an alternating countable chain built with the second
  property above; for the second, a transfinite recursion which at each stage adds a τ-small good
  subset containing a minimal element not yet covered, using well-foundedness. *Needs:* the previous
  item; §2.1.1.
- For good downward-closed A′ ⊆ A″ with A″ ∖ A′ τ-small, the map Y(A′) ⟶ Y(A″) lies in the weakly
  saturated class generated by the members of C ∩ W between τ-compact objects (HTT, proof of Lemma
  A.2.6.9, p. 841). *Proof route.* The difference tree (§2.2.1) is a τ-small tree rooted at Y(A′),
  so by §2.3.1 (HTT, Lemma A.1.5.11) the map is a pushout of a member M ⟶ N of C₀; write Y(A′) as a
  τ-filtered colimit of τ-compact objects through which M ⟶ Y(A′) factors and use the criterion of §5.3.1 to find
  a τ-small κ-filtered subfamily whose pushout lies in W, using two-out-of-three on the two good
  stages. *Needs:* the first item; §2.1.1, §2.2.1, §2.3.1, §5.3.1; §1.1.1, §1.3.1.

**6.2.2.** Prove that C ∩ W is generated, as a weakly saturated class, by a small subset, namely its members
between τ-compact objects (HTT, Lemma A.2.6.9, p. 839).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: write a map of C ∩ W as X ⟶ Y_A for a τ-good C₀-tree (§2.4), so that A is
good; exhaust A by the sequence of the second item of §6.2.1 and conclude by its third item and weak saturation of
the generated class.

*Needs:* §6.2.1; §2.4.1 (Cor. A.1.5.13); §5.3.1 (Prop. A.2.6.5).

### 6.3 Smith's recognition theorem

**6.3.1.** Prove `exists_modelCategory_of_smith`: let A be presentable with finite limits, and C, W classes of
morphisms with C generated as a weakly saturated class by a small set, C ∩ W weakly saturated, W
accessible, W with two-out-of-three, and every map with the right lifting property against C in W.
Then there is a combinatorial model structure on A with cofibrations C, weak equivalences W and
fibrations the maps with the right lifting property against C ∩ W (HTT, Prop. A.2.6.10, p. 841).

*Proof route.* Lurie's for the factorizations and lifting: the small object argument (§1.4) for a
generating set of C, and for the small generating set of C ∩ W from §6.2, with §1.5. Build the
structure with Mathlib's `ModelCategory.mk'` from the weak factorization systems (C, C.rlp) and
(C ∩ W, (C ∩ W).rlp), after proving that C.rlp is the intersection of (C ∩ W).rlp with W as Lurie
does; `ModelCategory.mk'` derives retract stability of W from the two weak factorization systems, so
no separate argument from accessibility is needed (HTT's appeal at this point is to a statement about
idempotent completeness).

*Needs:* §1.4.1 (1), §1.5.1, §5.1.1, §6.1.1, §6.2.2; Mathlib `ModelCategory.mk'`,
`MorphismProperty.IsWeakFactorizationSystem`.

**6.3.2.** Prove `exists_modelCategory_of_isPerfect`: let A be presentable with finite limits, W a class of
morphisms and C₀ a small set such that W is perfect; in any vertical composite of two pushout squares
whose top map lies in C₀ and whose lower left vertical map lies in W, the lower right vertical map
lies in W; and every map with the right lifting property against C₀ lies in W. Then A has a left
proper combinatorial model structure whose cofibrations are the weakly saturated class generated by
C₀, whose weak equivalences are W, and whose fibrations are the maps with the right lifting property
against cofibrations in W (HTT, Prop. A.2.6.15, p. 843).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: the class of maps along whose pushouts weak equivalences are preserved, in
the sense of the second hypothesis, is weakly saturated and contains C₀, hence contains all
cofibrations; this gives left properness and that C ∩ W is weakly saturated; W is accessible because
it is perfect (§5.5); conclude by Smith's theorem (§6.3.1), with the right lifting class against C equal
to that against C₀ (§1.3).

*Needs:* §1.3.1, §1.4.1 (1), §3.4.1 (Def. A.2.4.1), §5.5.1, §6.1.1; §6.3.1 (Prop. A.2.6.10).

### 6.4 Transferred model structures

**6.4.1.** Prove `exists_transferred`: let A be a model category whose cofibrations and trivial cofibrations are generated by
small sets I and J, B a
presentable category with finite limits, and F ⊣ G an adjunction. If G carries every map in the weakly saturated class generated by F(J)
to a weak equivalence, then B has a combinatorial model structure with generating sets F(I) and F(J),
whose weak equivalences and fibrations are the maps that G carries to weak equivalences and
fibrations (Berger–Moerdijk, §2.5; Crans).

*Proof route.* The small object argument on F(I) and F(J) (§1.4) gives both factorizations; lifting
against G-fibrations comes by adjunction; the acyclicity hypothesis identifies the class generated by
F(J) with the trivial cofibrations, and the retract argument (§1.5) finishes; Mathlib's
`ModelCategory.mk'` assembles the structure.

*Needs:* §1.4.1 (1), §1.5.1, §6.1.1; Mathlib `ModelCategory.mk'`, `MorphismProperty.map`.

**6.4.2.** Prove the path-object criterion: if B has a fibrant replacement functor and functorial path objects
for fibrant objects, both in the sense created by G, then G carries every map in the class generated
by F(J) to a weak equivalence (Berger–Moerdijk, §2.6; Quillen's path-object argument).

*Proof route.* Quillen's argument: a map in the class generated by F(J) has the left lifting
property against the maps that G sends to fibrations; for such a map j : X ⟶ Y, lift in the square
against the fibrant replacement of X to get a retraction up to the path object of the replacement, and
conclude by two-out-of-three.

*Needs:* §1.3.1, §4.1.1, §6.4.1.

**6.4.3.** Prove that the transferred structure of §6.4.1 exists whenever B has a fibrant
replacement functor and functorial path objects for fibrant objects created by G (Berger–Moerdijk,
§§2.5–2.6).

*Proof route.* Combine §6.4.1 and §6.4.2.

*Needs:* §6.4.1, §6.4.2; §1.4.1 (1), §4.1.1, §6.1.1.

## Layer 7: model structures on diagram categories

### 7.1 Injective and projective classes

**7.1.1.** Define, for a small category J and a model category A, the classes of maps of diagrams J ⥤ A: a map is
an injective cofibration, a projective fibration or a weak equivalence when it is levelwise a
cofibration, a fibration or a weak equivalence (Mathlib `MorphismProperty.functorCategory`); an
injective fibration when it has the right lifting property against the injective cofibrations that
are weak equivalences; and a projective cofibration when it has the left lifting property against the
projective fibrations that are weak equivalences (HTT, Def. A.2.8.1, pp. 847–848).

**Checks.**

- For J the walking arrow 0 ⟶ 1 and A sets with the injective structure, the map from the empty
  diagram to the diagram X = (∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗) is levelwise a cofibration but not a projective cofibration:
  it does not lift against the levelwise surjection from E = (∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗ ⊔ ∗, the identity) to X, since
  a section would need a map ∗ ⟶ ∗ ⊔ ∗ whose composite with the fold map is the identity of ∗ ⊔ ∗. A
  definition making projective cofibrations levelwise would accept it.
- For the same J and A, the map from E = (∗ ⟶ ∗ ⊔ ∗, the first point) to B = (∗ ⟶ ∗) is levelwise a
  surjection but not an injective fibration: it does not lift against the levelwise injection from
  (∅ ⟶ ∗) to (∗ ⟶ ∗) for the square picking the second point of E at 1, since any lift at 0 lands at
  1 on the first point. A definition making injective fibrations levelwise would accept it.
- For J the walking arrow and A sets with the trivial structure, the map from (∅ ⟶ ∗) to (∗ ⟶ ∗) is
  not a weak equivalence, failing at 0, although the induced map of colimits is the bijection
  ∗ ⟶ ∗. A definition testing weak equivalences on colimits would accept it.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`, `MorphismProperty.functorCategory`,
`MorphismProperty.llp`, `MorphismProperty.rlp`.

### 7.2 The projective model structure

**7.2.1.** Prove `exists_projective`: for A combinatorial and J small, J ⥤ A carries a combinatorial model
structure with the levelwise weak equivalences, the projective fibrations and the projective
cofibrations, generated by the maps from the free diagram on a generating cofibration (or trivial
cofibration) of A at an object of J (HTT, Prop. A.2.8.2, projective structure, pp. 848–850).

*Proof route.* Lurie's, directly: for an object j and an object X of A, the free diagram at j sends k
to a coproduct of copies of X indexed by maps j ⟶ k; a map is a projective (trivial) fibration exactly
when it has the right lifting property against the free maps on the generating (trivial)
cofibrations; the small object argument (§1.4) gives the factorizations, the free maps on generating
trivial cofibrations are levelwise trivial cofibrations and so are their cell complexes (colimits in
J ⥤ A are levelwise), and the retract argument (§1.5) gives the rest. The same structure is a special
case of the transfer theorem (§6.4).

*Needs:* §1.4.1 (1), §1.5.1, §6.1.1, §7.1.1; Mathlib `ModelCategory.mk'`.

### 7.3 Restriction along discrete opfibrations

**7.3.1.** Prove that if g : C′ ⥤ C exhibits C′ as cofibered in sets over C (every map out of g(c′) has a unique
lift with source c′), then restriction along g carries injective fibrations to injective fibrations,
for A a combinatorial model category (HTT, Lemma A.2.8.10, p. 852).

*Proof route.* Lurie's: the left adjoint g_! is computed fiberwise as coproducts, (g_! F)(c) the
coproduct of F(c′) over the fiber of g at c; coproducts of trivial cofibrations are trivial
cofibrations, so g_! preserves injective trivial cofibrations, and by adjunction g* preserves the right
lifting class. The argument uses only small coproducts in A.

*Needs:* §6.1.1, §7.1.1; Mathlib `Functor.lan`, `MorphismProperty.llp_isStableUnderCoproductsOfShape`.

### 7.4 Reedy categories and induction on objects

Use Mathlib's `HomotopicalAlgebra.ReedyStructure W₁ W₂ α` as the definition of a Reedy category:
multiplicative classes W₁ (Lurie's J^L) and W₂ (Lurie's J^R) and a degree in a well-ordered α, with
non-identity W₁-maps lowering and non-identity W₂-maps raising degree, and every morphism factoring
uniquely as a W₁-map followed by a W₂-map. HTT's Reedy categories are small; statements take
`SmallCategory J` where they need it. The simplex category, with W₁ the surjections, W₂ the
injections and degree the length, is Mathlib's `SimplexCategory.reedyStructure` (HTT, Ex. A.2.9.5,
p. 853), and the opposite of a Reedy category is Reedy with the classes exchanged, Mathlib's
`ReedyStructure.op` (HTT, Ex. A.2.9.6, p. 853).

**7.4.1.** Prove the comparison with Lurie's definition and the induction principle it provides: J admits a
Reedy structure for some well-ordered α exactly when every morphism factors uniquely as a W₁-map
followed by a W₂-map, no non-identity endomorphism lies in W₁ or W₂, and the relation X ≺₀ Y (a W₂-map
X ⟶ Y or a W₁-map Y ⟶ X, with X ≠ Y) has no infinite descending chains (HTT, Def. A.2.9.1, p. 853).
Lurie's definition does not mention endomorphisms: the monoid ℕ as a one-object category, with W₁ the
identity and W₂ everything, satisfies it, and the characterization excludes it. In that situation:
- the transitive closure ≺ of ≺₀ is a well-founded strict partial order on objects (HTT, Rem. A.2.9.2,
  p. 853);
- a good filtration of J is an exhaustive transfinite sequence of full subcategories J_β, each obtained
  from the union J(<β) of the earlier ones by adjoining one object j_β all of whose ≺-predecessors lie
  in J(<β) (HTT, Not. A.2.9.11, p. 855); good filtrations exist, any well-ordering of the objects
  refining the degree being one, and their existence is equivalent to the absence of infinite
  descending ≺₀-chains (HTT, Rem. A.2.9.12, p. 855);
- each stage J_β of a good filtration is closed under factorizations: if a morphism of J_β factors as
  a W₁-map followed by a W₂-map, the middle object lies in J_β, so the classes restrict to a
  factorization system on J_β (HTT, Rem. A.2.9.13, p. 855).

*Proof route.* Given the conditions, define the degree by well-founded recursion on ≺; conversely the
degree witnesses well-foundedness. A good filtration is a well-ordering of the objects in which every
object comes after its ≺-predecessors, which exists exactly when ≺ is well-founded. For the closure of
J_β under factorizations of `f : I ⟶ K` through J: either the W₂-map `J ⟶ K` is an identity, so J = K,
or J ≺ K, and then J was adjoined before K.

**Checks.**

- The simplex category: the map [1] ⟶ [0] is in W₁ and lowers degree from 1 to 0; the map [0] ⟶ [1]
  picking 0 is in W₂. A definition with the classes exchanged would have W₁-maps raising degree and
  fails the axiom on this map.
- The cyclic group of order 2 as a one-object category is not Reedy: its non-identity automorphism
  would have to lie in W₁ or W₂ and change the degree of the single object. A definition allowing
  isomorphisms in either class (generalized Reedy) would accept it.
- A discrete category with any degree function is Reedy with W₁ = W₂ = identities; with α = ℕ and
  infinitely many objects of degree 0 this is fine, ruling out a definition requiring finitely many
  objects per degree. Every well-ordering of its objects is a good filtration.
- For the simplex category, the filtration adding [0], [1], [2], … in order is good, and one adding
  [1] before [0] is not, because [0] ≺ [1]. For the opposite of the simplex category the same order is
  good, since ≺ is the same relation with the classes exchanged; a definition of ≺ ignoring the
  direction of the W₁-maps would reverse it.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ReedyStructure`, `ReedyStructure.op`,
`SimplexCategory.reedyStructure`.

### 7.5 Latching and matching objects and the Reedy classes

**7.5.1.** Define, for a small Reedy category J, a category A with small limits and colimits, a functor X : J ⥤ A
and an object j: the latching category `latchingIndex` at j, the full subcategory of the
objects over j on the non-identity W₂-maps into j; the matching category `matchingIndex`, the
non-identity W₁-maps out of j; the latching object L_j X, the colimit of X over the latching
category, with `latchingMap` to X(j); and the matching object M_j X, the limit over the matching
category, with `matchingMap` from X(j) (HTT, Not. A.2.9.7, p. 854). Every object of the latching and
matching categories at j precedes j under ≺, so L_j X and M_j X are defined for functors on any full
subcategory containing the ≺-predecessors of j, as §7.6 needs.

**7.5.2.** Define, for f : X ⟶ Y in J ⥤ A, the relative latching map `relativeLatchingMap` from the pushout
X(j) ⊔ L_j Y (amalgamated over L_j X) to Y(j) and the relative matching map `relativeMatchingMap`
from X(j) to the pullback Y(j) × M_j X (fibered over M_j Y). Define the Reedy cofibrations
`reedyCofibrations` (all relative latching maps cofibrations, condition (C)), the Reedy fibrations
`reedyFibrations` (all relative matching maps fibrations, (F)), the levelwise weak equivalences (W),
and the classes (WC) and (WF) of maps whose relative latching maps are trivial cofibrations,
respectively whose relative matching maps are trivial fibrations (HTT, Prop. A.2.9.19, conditions
(C), (F), (W), (WC), (WF), p. 859).

- For a simplicial set X, with the Reedy structure on the opposite of the simplex category, the
  latching map at [n] is injective with image the degenerate n-simplices (Mathlib `SSet.degenerate`),
  and a map of simplicial sets is a monomorphism exactly when all its relative latching maps are
  injective (HTT, Ex. A.2.9.8, p. 854). *Proof route.* Lurie's: a nondegenerate simplex whose image is
  degenerate would be fixed by a non-identity operator after applying the monomorphism. *Needs:* the
  definitions above; Mathlib `SSet.degenerate`.

**Checks.**

- For X : Δᵒᵖ ⥤ Set the simplicial set Δ[0], the latching object at [1] is a point (the degenerate
  1-simplex) and the latching map is a bijection; at [0] the latching category is empty and L₀ X is
  the empty set. A definition including identities in the latching category would give L_j X = X(j)
  always.
- For the cosimplicial object Δ[•] in simplicial sets (the Yoneda embedding), the latching object at
  [n] is the boundary ∂Δ[n] and the latching map is its inclusion into Δ[n]; a definition including
  identities would give Δ[n] itself, and one using the surjections instead would have latching map at
  [1] with image all of Δ[1] rather than ∂Δ[1].
- For J discrete, latching objects are initial and matching objects terminal, so (C) and (F) are
  levelwise cofibrations and fibrations. A definition by absolute latching maps (L_j X ⟶ X(j)) would
  make a map between non-cofibrant diagrams never a Reedy cofibration even when it is an isomorphism.
- For J = Δᵒᵖ and A sets with the injective structure, the map from the empty simplicial set to the
  point is a Reedy cofibration: at [0] its relative latching map is ∅ ⟶ ∗, and at [n] with n > 0 it is
  the identity of a point. The map from the constant simplicial set on two points to the point is not:
  at [0] its relative latching map is the fold map ∗ ⊔ ∗ ⟶ ∗, which is not injective.
- For J the poset {0 < 1} with W₂ all maps and A sets with the injective structure, the map from
  (∅ ⟶ ∗) to (∗ ⟶ ∗) is levelwise injective but not a Reedy cofibration: at 1 its relative latching
  map is the fold map from ∗ ⊔ ∗ (amalgamated over ∅) to ∗. A definition making Reedy cofibrations
  levelwise would accept it.

*Needs:* §7.4, §7.4.1; Mathlib `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`.

### 7.6 One stage of a good filtration

**7.6.1.** Prove: let J_β add the object j to J(<β) in a good filtration, A have small limits and colimits, and
X : J(<β) ⥤ A. Extensions of X to J_β correspond to factorizations L_j X ⟶ c ⟶ M_j X of the canonical
map, the extension going to c = X(j); maps between two extensions that restrict to the identity of X
correspond to maps of the factorizations (HTT, Cor. A.2.9.15, pp. 855–856, the second assertion by
the same argument).

*Proof route.* An ordinary-categorical argument, unlike HTT's, which passes through a homotopy pushout
in the Joyal model structure: every morphism between j and an object of J(<β), and every non-identity
endomorphism of j, factors through the latching or matching category by the Reedy factorization, so a
functor on J_β is its restriction together with X(j) and maps L_j X ⟶ X(j) ⟶ M_j X (Hovey, *Model
Categories*, §5.1). The colimit over the objects of J(<β) over j may be replaced by the latching object:
for any full subcategory S of the objects over j containing every non-identity W₂-map into j and with
no member whose source is ≽ j, the colimit of X over S is L_j X, since the W₂-part of the Reedy
factorization of a member of S is not an identity, which makes the inclusion of the latching
category cofinal (HTT, Rem. A.2.9.10, pp. 854–855); dually for matching objects.

*Needs:* §7.4, §7.4.1, §7.5.1.

**7.6.2.** Prove the chain filtration of the nerve of a stage: for J_β = J(<β) ∪ {j} and a set S′ of chains
j ⟶ j ⟶ ⋯ ⟶ j of non-identity maps of positive length, let X(S′) be the simplicial subset (Mathlib
`SSet.Subcomplex`) of the nerve of J_β consisting of the simplices all of whose nondegenerate
positive-dimensional faces with every vertex at j lie in S′. Then X(S) is the whole nerve for S the
set of all chains; X(∅) is the pushout of the join of the nerves of the categories of objects of
J(<β) over j and under j, with j inserted (Tau Ceti `SSet.join`), with the nerve of J(<β), along the
join without j; and for a well-ordering of the chains in which shorter chains come first, the subsets
X(S_γ) of the initial segments S_γ increase and are continuous at limits (HTT, proof of
Prop. A.2.9.14, pp. 857–858).

*Proof route.* Direct from the definitions: a simplex of the nerve of J_β is determined by its chain
of morphisms, and the faces with all vertices at j are subchains.

*Needs:* §7.4.1; Mathlib `nerve`, `SSet.Subcomplex`; Tau Ceti `SSet.join`.

### 7.7 The Reedy model structure

**7.7.1.** Prove `exists_reedy`: for a small Reedy category J and a model category A with small limits and
colimits, not necessarily combinatorial, J ⥤ A carries a model structure with the Reedy cofibrations,
the levelwise weak equivalences and the Reedy fibrations, and its trivial cofibrations and trivial
fibrations are the classes (WC) and (WF) (HTT, Prop. A.2.9.19 and its proof, pp. 859–862). Its three ingredients are
results in their own right, each proved along a good filtration (§7.4) with §7.6 at each new object:
- the Reedy factorizations: every map factors as a (WC)-map followed by an (F)-map, and as a (C)-map
  followed by a (WF)-map (HTT, proof of Prop. A.2.9.19, pp. 860–861). *Proof route.* At a new object j
  factor in A the map from the relative latching object to the relative matching object, and extend
  by §7.6. *Needs:* §7.4.1, §7.5.2, §7.6.1;
- Reedy lifting: (C)-maps have the left lifting property against (WF)-maps, and (WC)-maps against
  (F)-maps (HTT, proof of Prop. A.2.9.19, p. 862). *Proof route.* At each new object the lifting
  problem becomes one in A between the relative latching map and the relative matching map (§7.6).
  *Needs:* §7.4.1, §7.5.2, §7.6.1;
- `reedyCofibrations_inf_eq` and `reedyFibrations_inf_eq`: a map satisfies (WC) exactly when it
  satisfies (C) and (W), and (WF) exactly when it satisfies (F) and (W) (HTT, proof of Prop. A.2.9.19,
  pp. 861–862). *Proof route.* Lurie's. For a map satisfying (C) or (WC) at the sources of all members
  of a sieve I of W₂-maps into j, and a smaller sieve I′, the map from colim(F|I) ⊔ colim(G|I′)
  (amalgamated over colim(F|I′)) to colim(G|I) is a cofibration, respectively a trivial cofibration,
  by transfinite induction adding one object of I at a time, each step a pushout of a relative
  latching map (HTT, Lemma A.2.9.20, pp. 859–860). This with two-out-of-three gives one direction;
  the other is induction along a good filtration with two-out-of-three in A at each object.
  *Needs:* §7.4, §7.4.1, §7.5.2.

*Proof route.* Assemble the factorizations, lifting and the identification of trivial classes with
Mathlib's `ModelCategory.mk'`; limits and colimits in J ⥤ A are levelwise.

*Needs:* the three ingredients; §7.5.2; Mathlib `ModelCategory.mk'`, `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory`.

### Examples

- For a small category J, the projective model structure of §7.2 is the transfer (§6.4) along the
  left adjoint of evaluation at the objects of J from the product model structure.
- For J discrete, the projective model structure on J ⥤ A is the product model structure, with
  generating cofibrations the generating cofibrations of A placed at one object and the initial object
  elsewhere.
- For A sets with the injective structure and J = Δᵒᵖ, the Reedy cofibrations of simplicial sets are
  exactly the monomorphisms, by the simplicial-set statement of §7.5.2.
- For J the poset {0 < 1} with W₂ all maps and W₁ the identities, Reedy cofibrations are the maps
  X ⟶ Y with X(0) ⟶ Y(0) and X(1) ⊔ Y(0) (amalgamated over X(0)) ⟶ Y(1) cofibrations, and Reedy
  fibrations are levelwise fibrations: the Reedy structure agrees with the projective one.

### Dependencies

Layers 1 and 6 (for §7.2 and §7.3); Mathlib's model categories and Reedy structures; Tau Ceti's
joins (§7.6 only). Coends, used with Reedy structures downstream, are in §8.3: no statement of this
layer uses them.

## Layer 8: closed monoidal categories, enrichment, tensors, cotensors and coends

### 8.1 Monoidal structures and lax monoidal functors

Use Mathlib's Cartesian monoidal structure on a category with finite products, with the final object
as unit (HTT, Ex. A.1.3.1, p. 811; Mathlib `CartesianMonoidalCategory.ofHasFiniteProducts`). A
monoidal category is left-closed (every functor A ⊗ − has a right adjoint) when it is Mathlib's
`MonoidalClosed C`, right-closed (every − ⊗ A has one) when its monoidal opposite is (`MonoidalOpposite`),
and closed when both (HTT, Def. A.1.3.4, p. 812; supplied, no new definition). Take
right-lax, left-lax and strong monoidal functors and monoidal transformations to be Mathlib's
`Functor.LaxMonoidal`, `Functor.OplaxMonoidal`, `Functor.Monoidal` and `NatTrans.IsMonoidal`
(HTT, Def. A.1.3.5, pp. 812–813). HTT's monoidal functor, a right-lax monoidal functor whose structure maps
are isomorphisms, is Mathlib's `Functor.Monoidal` through `Functor.CoreMonoidal.ofLaxMonoidal`
(HTT, Def. A.1.3.5, closing paragraph, p. 813).

- For a braided monoidal category, left-closed and right-closed are equivalent, the braiding
  identifying A ⊗ − with − ⊗ A. *Needs:* Mathlib `MonoidalClosed`, `MonoidalOpposite`,
  `BraidedCategory`.

**Checks.**

- The category of sets with Cartesian product is closed, with internal hom the set of functions; the
  unit is the one-point set. A definition using coproduct would have unit the empty set and no right
  adjoint to A ⊔ −, since that functor does not preserve the initial object.
- The category of finite sets with Cartesian product is closed, while the category of finite sets with
  disjoint union is not left-closed. This distinguishes closedness from mere existence of finite
  limits.
- The forgetful functor from abelian groups with tensor product to sets with Cartesian product is
  right-lax monoidal, with structure maps the bilinear map from A × B to A ⊗ B and the map picking
  1 ∈ ℤ, but it has no left-lax structure: the unit axiom forces the composite U(A) ≅ U(A ⊗ ℤ) ⟶
  U(A) × U(ℤ) ⟶ U(A) to be the identity, while the only natural maps U(A ⊗ B) ⟶ U(A) are constant at 0.
  This fixes the direction of the structure maps.

*Needs:* Mathlib `CartesianMonoidalCategory.ofHasFiniteProducts`, `MonoidalClosed`, `MonoidalOpposite`,
`Functor.LaxMonoidal`, `Functor.OplaxMonoidal`, `Functor.Monoidal`, `NatTrans.IsMonoidal`.

### 8.2 Enrichment, change of base, tensors and cotensors

**8.2.1.** A right-closed monoidal category is enriched over itself with mapping objects the internal homs
(HTT, Ex. A.1.4.1, p. 814; Mathlib `MonoidalClosed.enrichedCategorySelf`, in Mathlib's orientation, where the
hypothesis is `MonoidalClosed`). A right-lax monoidal functor G turns a category enriched over its
source into one enriched over its target, with mapping objects G applied to the old ones; applied to
the functor of points Hom(1, −) this gives the underlying ordinary category (HTT, Rem. A.1.4.3,
p. 815; Mathlib `TransportEnrichment`, `ForgetEnrichment`).

**8.2.2.** Define, for a monoidal category V (right-closed for tensors, left-closed for cotensors, in the sense
of §8.1) and a V-enriched category D, a tensor of v in V and X in D: an object
T with a map v ⟶ Map(X, T) such that for every Y the induced map from Map(T, Y) to the internal hom from
v into Map(X, Y) is an isomorphism; and a cotensor: an object P with a map v ⟶ Map(P, X) inducing
isomorphisms from Map(Y, P) to the internal hom (on the other side) from v into Map(Y, X). D is
tensored or cotensored over V when all tensors or cotensors exist (HTT, §A.1.4, pp. 816–817).

- A right-closed monoidal category enriched over itself is tensored over itself, with tensor v ⊗ X,
  and it is cotensored over itself exactly when it is also left-closed (HTT, Ex. A.1.4.5, p. 817).

*Proof route.* The tensor is given by the monoidal product and the associativity isomorphism. For the
converse, a cotensor of v and X represents Y ↦ Hom(Y ⊗ v, X) on underlying categories, so every
− ⊗ v acquires a right adjoint.

**Checks.**

- In sets enriched over themselves, the tensor of a set v with X is v × X and the cotensor is the set
  of functions from v to X. A definition of tensor by representing maps into X would produce the
  cotensor instead, and fail for v empty: the tensor is empty, the cotensor a point.
- In abelian groups enriched over themselves, the tensor of ℤ/2 with ℤ is ℤ/2 ⊗ ℤ ≅ ℤ/2, since maps
  out of it are Hom(ℤ/2, Hom(ℤ, Y)); the copower of ℤ by the underlying set of ℤ/2 is ℤ ⊕ ℤ. A
  definition by copowers of underlying sets would give ℤ ⊕ ℤ.
- In pointed sets with the smash product, the tensor of the two-point set S⁰ with X is X and the
  tensor of the one-point set with X is the one-point set. A definition using the Cartesian product of
  the underlying sets would give X × S⁰, with twice as many points.

*Needs:* §8.1; Mathlib `EnrichedCategory`, `MonoidalClosed.enrichedCategorySelf`,
`TransportEnrichment`, `ForgetEnrichment`.

### 8.3 Coends

Use Mathlib's coend for the coend of F : J ⥤ A and G : Jᵒᵖ ⥤ B along a bifunctor ⊗ : A ⥤ B ⥤ C into a
cocomplete category: the coequalizer of the two maps from the coproduct over maps j ⟶ j′ of
F(j) ⊗ G(j′) to the coproduct over j of F(j) ⊗ G(j) (HTT, §A.2.9, after Cor. A.2.9.25, p. 865;
Mathlib `Limits.coend` of the functor (j, j′) ↦ F(j′) ⊗ G(j)).

**Checks.**

- For J the group of order 2 as a one-object category, in sets with ⊗ the product and F, G both the
  group acting on itself by translation, the coend is the set of the two classes of F(∗) × G(∗) under
  (a, b + g) ∼ (a + g, b), recorded by a + b. A definition imposing the identifications only along
  morphisms between distinct objects would leave all four points.
- For J discrete with two objects, the coend is the coproduct of the two values, while the end would
  be the product. In sets with Cartesian product and one-point values, the coend has two points and
  the end one.
- For J the walking arrow 0 ⟶ 1, in sets with ⊗ the product, the coend of F and G is the quotient of
  F(0) × G(0) ⊔ F(1) × G(1) identifying (a, G(u)(b)) with (F(u)(a), b); with F constant at a point and
  G the representable at 1 it is a point, ruling out a definition without the identifications (which
  would have two points).

*Needs:* Mathlib `Limits.coend`.

### Examples

- Simplicial sets with Cartesian product are closed; enriched over themselves, they are tensored and
  cotensored, with tensor K × X and cotensor the internal hom from K to X.

### Dependencies

Mathlib's monoidal and enriched category theory, and Mathlib's coends.
