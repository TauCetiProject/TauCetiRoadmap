# Roadmap: the Kan–Quillen model structure

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the homotopy theory of simplicial sets from horns and simplices, with no
topological input, and ends with the combinatorial Kan–Quillen model structure on `SSet`: cofibrations the
monomorphisms, fibrations the Kan fibrations, and weak equivalences the weak homotopy equivalences of Kerodon (a
map inducing a bijection on `π₀ Fun(−, Z)` for every Kan complex `Z`).

It builds on Part ModelCategories and on the anodyne maps, factorizations and homotopy groups already in Mathlib
and Tau Ceti.

- **Kan complexes (Layer 1).** Higher homotopy groups are abelian; change of basepoint; the long exact sequence
  of a Kan fibration; Whitehead's theorem in the form of the Kan-group criterion.
- **Ex^∞ (Layer 2).** Kan's functor `Ex^∞` as a functorial Kan replacement.
- **The model structure (Layer 3).** Weak homotopy equivalences, the Kan–Quillen model structure, its
  combinatorial generation, properness, and simplexwise weakly contractible maps.
- **Connectivity (Layer 4).** The lifting characterization of `n`-connective Kan fibrations and the stability of
  `n`-connective maps under homotopy pushout.
- **Postnikov towers and Eilenberg–MacLane complexes (Layer 5).** Moore's Postnikov sections and the convergence
  of Postnikov towers; Eilenberg–MacLane complexes with their homotopy groups, their uniqueness and their pointed
  mapping spaces.

**Headline theorems.**
- The trivial fibrations are the Kan fibrations that are weak homotopy equivalences, and the anodyne maps are the
  monomorphisms that are weak homotopy equivalences.
- Hence the Kan–Quillen model structure, which is combinatorial, generated by the boundary and horn inclusions,
  left and right proper, and has weak equivalences stable under filtered colimits.
- A map whose pullback along every simplex is weakly contractible is a weak homotopy equivalence.

## Scope and ownership

- **Weak equivalences.** The weak equivalences of the model structure are Tau Ceti's Kerodon class
  `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`. The model structure is a definition over a
  `CategoryWithWeakEquivalences` structure carrying that class, not an instance, so it never
  competes with Tau Ceti's realization-based scoped instance on `SSet` in
  `TauCeti/AlgebraicTopology/SimplicialSet/WeakEquivalence.lean`. This roadmap neither redefines
  nor uses that instance, and no statement here involves geometric realization. The two
  classes agree: GeneralizedCohomology §G2.1 defines the realization weak equivalences (a bijection
  on components and isomorphisms on all based homotopy groups of the realization) and proves that
  they are characterized by the Kan-group criterion after fibrant replacement, and §3.1 here proves
  the same criterion, with `Ex^∞` as the fibrant replacement, for `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`.
  The criteria single out the same maps whatever Kan replacement `j_X : X → R X` is used, provided
  `j_X` lies in Kerodon's class `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`. This holds for `Ex^∞` (§3.1) and for
  an anodyne replacement, a relative cell complex of horn inclusions such as the small object
  argument on the horn inclusions produces (Tau Ceti `SSet.anodyneExtensions.isWeakHomotopyEquivalence`).
  It is a requirement on the replacement used in GeneralizedCohomology §G2.1, whose text says only
  "after fibrant replacement": the replacement there must be anodyne, or `Ex^∞`. A replacement known
  only to be a realization weak equivalence does not suffice, since it lies in Kerodon's class only
  once the two classes are known to agree, which is what is being shown. For `f : X → Y` choose `R f`
  with `R f ∘ j_X = j_Y ∘ f`; it exists when `j_X` is anodyne, by lifting against the Kan complex
  `R Y`, and as `Ex^∞ f` for `Ex^∞`. Then naturality of `ρ^∞` gives `Ex^∞(R f) ∘ ρ^∞_(R X) = ρ^∞_(R Y) ∘ R f`, and
  functoriality gives `Ex^∞(R f) ∘ Ex^∞ j_X = Ex^∞ j_Y ∘ Ex^∞ f`. The maps `ρ^∞_(R X)`, `ρ^∞_(R Y)`
  (§2.2, between Kan complexes) and `Ex^∞ j_X`, `Ex^∞ j_Y` (§3.1) are π-isomorphisms, so by two out
  of three (§1.3) `R f` is a π-isomorphism exactly when `Ex^∞ f` is.
  The comparison of `π n X x` with the homotopy groups of `|X|` is
  AlgebraicTopology §8.2's realization-unit theorem (HTT §A.2.7, PDF p. 847, defines weak
  equivalences through realization).
- **Already in the library.** Mathlib has Kan complexes and Kan fibrations (the scoped fibrations of
  `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen`), anodyne maps (`SSet.anodyneExtensions`) with the pushout-product
  theorem, strong anodyne maps and Moss's regular pairings, the subdivision adjunction
  `SSet.sdExAdjunction`, the generating sets `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.I` and
  `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.J`, `π₀`, the fundamental groupoid, coskeleta and the Dold–Kan
  correspondence. Tau Ceti has the two weak factorization systems (monomorphisms and trivial
  fibrations, anodyne maps and Kan fibrations), the homotopy groups `SSet.KanComplex.π` with their
  group structure in positive degrees, Kerodon's weak homotopy equivalences with two out of three
  and their characterization between Kan complexes as homotopy equivalences, weak contractibility,
  the theorem that anodyne maps are weak homotopy equivalences, the shrinking criterion for trivial
  fibrations, fibers of Kan fibrations, Moore's theorem that simplicial groups are Kan complexes and
  the Moore-complex computation of the homotopy groups of a simplicial abelian group, with its
Dold–Kan form (the homotopy groups of `Γ(C)` at the zero vertex are the homology of `C`). These are
  cited under Exact supplier contracts. None of them is a target here.
- **Eilenberg–MacLane spaces.** DGFloer (Layer 1, item 4, Eilenberg–MacLane spaces) owns the
  topological `K(A, n)` as the realization of the same simplicial abelian group `Γ(A[n])` that
  §5.4 uses for `n ≥ 2`, and of the nerve of `A`, compared there with `Γ(A[1])`, for `n = 1`. This roadmap owns only the combinatorial homotopy theory of that simplicial set.
- **Not here.** The Joyal model structure and every quasicategorical statement belong to the
  InfinityCategoryTheory family. The simplicial and monoidal model structure, diagonals of
  bisimplicial sets and hypercovers belong to Part SimplicialCategories. Model categories in
  general, combinatorial and proper model categories, homotopy pushouts in a model category and
  Reedy model structures belong to ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories (HTT §§A.2.1–A.2.9,
  except §A.2.7 and Lemma A.2.9.17, which is §3.6 here), which this roadmap consumes.

## Conventions

Locators such as (HTT, Prop. A.3.2.4, p. 876) refer to the editions listed under [References](#references).

Every statement to prove, define or construct carries a label `X.Y.Z` — its section, then its position within that
section — and a single labeled statement, cited as `§3.5.2` here and `Part KanQuillen §3.5.2` elsewhere, is one
target.

Simplicial sets are Mathlib's `SSet.{u}`, with Mathlib's notations `Δ[n]`, `∂Δ[n]`, `Λ[n, i]` and
`X _⦋n⦌` for the `n`-simplices. Morphisms `X ⟶ Y` of simplicial sets are written `f : X → Y`.
`Fun(K, X)` is Mathlib's internal hom `(ihom K).obj X`. A homotopy between `f, g : X → Y` is
Mathlib's `SSet.Homotopy f g`, a map `X ⊗ Δ[1] → Y` restricting to `f` at the vertex 0 and to `g` at
the vertex 1. The rows below fix every choice this roadmap makes. Each comes with a Check that pins
it.

- **Cofibrations, fibrations and anodyne maps.** The cofibrations are the monomorphisms and the
  fibrations are the Kan fibrations: the maps with the right lifting property against every horn
  inclusion `Λ[n, i] ⊆ Δ[n]`, `n ≥ 1`, `0 ≤ i ≤ n` (HTT §A.2.7, PDF p. 847; Kerodon, Definition
  3.1.1.1). These are Mathlib's scoped classes in `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen`. A Kan complex is
  Mathlib's `SSet.KanComplex`, the fibrant objects. The anodyne maps are Mathlib's
  `SSet.anodyneExtensions`, the left lifting class of the Kan fibrations, which is Kerodon's weakly
  saturated class generated by the horn inclusions (Kerodon, Definition 3.1.2.1 and Corollary
  3.1.8.5).
  - *Check.* `∂Δ[1] ⊆ Δ[1]` is a monomorphism with discrete fibers but not a Kan fibration: the
    vertex 0 of `∂Δ[1]` lies over the source of the edge of `Δ[1]` and has no edge above it. It
    lifts against every horn `Λ[n, i]` with `n ≥ 2`, which is connected and contains every vertex,
    so a definition omitting the one-dimensional horns, or calling every map with discrete fibers a
    fibration, would accept it.
  - *Check.* `Δ[1]` is a quasicategory but not a Kan complex: the map `Λ[2, 0] → Δ[1]` sending the
    vertices 0, 1, 2 to 0, 1, 0 has no filler, since a filler would need an edge from 1 to 0. A
    definition testing only inner horns would accept `Δ[1]`. The nerve of the group `ℤ` is Kan
    with non-unique fillers for `Λ[1, 0]`, so a definition requiring unique fillers would reject it.
  - *Check.* `{0} ⊆ Δ[1]` is anodyne (it is the horn `Λ[1, 0]`), but `∂Δ[1] ⊆ Δ[1]` is not, since
    it is not a weak homotopy equivalence (next row). A class generated by all boundary inclusions
    would contain it.
- **Weak homotopy equivalences.** A map `f : X → Y` is a weak homotopy equivalence when, for every
  Kan complex `Z`, precomposition with `f` is a bijection `π₀ Fun(Y, Z) → π₀ Fun(X, Z)` (Kerodon,
  Definition 3.1.7.12). This is Tau Ceti's `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`. As a class of
  morphisms it is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `weakHomotopyEquivalences`, a one-line body. A
  simplicial set is weakly contractible when its map to `Δ[0]` is a weak homotopy equivalence
  (Kerodon, Definition 3.2.4.16). This is equivalent to Tau Ceti's `SSet.IsWeaklyContractible` by
  `SSet.isWeaklyContractible_iff_isWeakHomotopyEquivalence`.
  - *Check.* `∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]` is not a weak homotopy equivalence. Take `Z = Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0]`: `Fun(Δ[0], Z)`
    has two components and `Fun(∂Δ[1], Z)` has four. A class defined by bijectivity on vertices
    modulo edges alone would also reject it, but would accept `∂Δ[2] → Δ[0]`, which the Kan-complex
    test rejects (§3.1).
- **Homotopy groups.** For a vertex `x` of `X`, `π n X x` is Tau Ceti's `SSet.KanComplex.π n X x`:
  homotopy classes relative to the boundary of maps `Δ[n] → X` sending `∂Δ[n]` to `x` (Kerodon,
  Construction 3.2.2.4). It is a group for `n ≥ 1` when `X` is Kan. The product `[p] · [q]` is the
  class of the face `d_n` of an `(n + 1)`-simplex whose faces `d_(n−1)`, `d_(n+1)` are `q`, `p` and
  whose other faces are constant. For `n = 1` this means `[p] · [q]` is "first `p`, then `q`".
  - *Check.* In the nerve of a group `G`, Mathlib composes the edges `g` then `h` to `h · g`. The
    2-simplex `(g, h)` therefore has `d₂ = g`, `d₀ = h`, `d₁ = hg`, so `[g] · [h] = [hg]` in `π 1`,
    and `g ↦ [g]` is an anti-isomorphism `G ≅ π 1`. For `G = S₃`, `g = (12)` and `h = (23)` the
    product is the class of `(132)`. The opposite convention would give `(123)`.
- **Fibers.** The fiber of `p : E → B` over a vertex `b` is Tau Ceti's subcomplex `SSet.fiber p b`
  of the simplices of `E` sent to degeneracies of `b`. It is a Kan complex when `p` is a Kan
  fibration.
  - *Check.* The fiber of `Δ[1] → Δ[0]` over the unique vertex is all of `Δ[1]`. The fiber of the
    inclusion of the vertex 0 into `Δ[1]` over the vertex 1 is empty. A convention taking fibers
    over all of `π₀ B` would instead give `Δ[0]` there.
- **Connectivity and truncation indices** (Kerodon's, not the classical "connected" indexing). A Kan
  complex is `n`-connective when it is nonempty and `π m X x = 0` for all `m < n` and all `x`
  (Kerodon, Definition 3.5.1.1). It is `n`-truncated when `π m X x = 0` for all `m > n` (Kerodon,
  Definition 3.5.7.1 with Prop. 3.5.7.9). A Kan complex is 0-connective exactly when it is
  nonempty. Both notions are stated for Kan complexes only.
  - *Check.* `K(A, n)` for `A ≠ 0` is `n`-connective and `n`-truncated but not
    `(n + 1)`-connective. In the classical indexing it is `(n − 1)`-connected. A roadmap mixing the
    two would call `K(ℤ, 2)` 2-connected, which is false.
  - *Check.* `∂Δ[2]` is not Kan, and its naive `π 1` (loops modulo 2-simplices) is trivial because its only loops are
    degenerate. Applying the definition to it without fibrant replacement would make it 2-connective, whereas it is
    only 1-connective (Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.2.6): `π 1` of `Ex^∞ ∂Δ[2]` is ℤ (§2.2); this is why the definitions
    require a Kan complex.
  - *Check.* Discrete simplicial sets are 0-truncated, the nerve of `S₃` is 1-truncated but not
    0-truncated (its `π 1` is `S₃`), and `K(A, n)` is `n`-truncated. Requiring triviality for
    `m ≥ n` instead of `m > n` would reject `K(ℤ, 1) = N(ℤ)` as 1-truncated, and testing only at
    one vertex would accept `Δ[0] ⊔ N(ℤ)` as 0-truncated.
- **The last-vertex map.** `Ex X` has `n`-simplices the maps `sd Δ[n] → X`, with Mathlib's `SSet.sd`
  and `SSet.ex`. The comparison `ρ_X : X → Ex X` is induced by the last-vertex map
  `sd Δ[n] → Δ[n]`, sending a nonempty subset of `[n]` to its largest element (Kerodon,
  Construction 3.3.4.3).
  - *Check.* The last-vertex map `sd Δ[1] → Δ[1]` sends the barycentre to the vertex 1, so the
    comparison `ρ` at `Δ[1]` sends the nondegenerate edge to the zigzag `0 → 1 ← 1`, which is not
    degenerate. The largest element is the only natural monotone choice, since the smallest element
    reverses inclusions.

## How to read the build

Layer 1 develops Kan complexes on their own, on top of the library's Kan fibrations and anodyne
maps: the homotopy groups with their abelian, basepoint and groupoid structure, the long exact
sequence of a Kan fibration, and Whitehead's theorem, which turns weak homotopy equivalences between
Kan complexes into isomorphisms on homotopy groups. Layer 2 constructs subdivision's right adjoint
`Ex`, its comparison map and the colimit `Ex^∞`, a functorial anodyne Kan replacement that preserves
finite limits and Kan fibrations. Layer 3 uses `Ex^∞` to detect weak homotopy equivalences on Kan
complexes and proves the two class identities, which together with the library's weak
factorization systems are the Kan–Quillen model structure; its properties and the simplexwise
criterion follow. Layer 4 treats connectivity of maps, and Layer 5 truncation, Postnikov towers and
Eilenberg–MacLane complexes. HTT §A.2.7 (PDF pp. 846–847) states the model structure and treats
the rest as background. The individual statements are located in Kerodon, in Moss's paper and in
the classical references.

## Layer 1: Kan complexes and their homotopy groups

### 1.1 Homotopy groups of Kan complexes

Use Tau Ceti's `SSet.KanComplex.π n X x` (Kerodon, Construction 3.2.2.4), a group for `n ≥ 1` with
the product of the Conventions (Kerodon, Theorem 3.2.2.10, whose product is the opposite group,
Warning 3.2.2.14) and functorial in pointed maps (Kerodon, Remark 3.2.2.16). This subsection
completes its basic theory: for a Kan complex `X`, `π n X x` agrees with `π₀` in degree 0, is
homotopy invariant, is abelian from degree 2, carries the change-of-basepoint isomorphisms with the
action of `π 1`, and has `π 1` given by the fundamental groupoid (Kerodon, §3.2.2).

**1.1.1. π₀ and homotopy invariance.** Prove that, for a Kan complex `X`, `π 0 X x` is in canonical
bijection with Mathlib's `π₀ X`, carrying the class of `x` to the component of `x` (Kerodon, Example
3.2.2.6). Prove that pointed homotopic maps induce the same maps on every `π n`, and that a pointed
homotopy equivalence induces bijections on all of them (Kerodon, Prop. 3.2.1.13). Prove that
`π n Δ[0]` is trivial for every `n`. Unpointed homotopy equivalences are treated with change of
basepoint (§1.1.3).

*Proof route.* For `π₀`, two vertices of a Kan complex are joined by an edge in each direction when
they are joined by a zigzag (Kan condition on `Λ[2, 0]` and `Λ[2, 2]`). For invariance, a pointed
homotopy `X ⊗ Δ[1] → Y` applied to a representative gives a homotopy relative to the boundary.

**Checks.**

- `π n Δ[0]` is trivial for every `n`, and so is `π n` of the nerve of the indiscrete groupoid on
  `m + 1` objects, which is contractible.
- For a group `G`, the nerve of `G` has `π 0` a point, `π 1 ≅ G` (as an anti-isomorphism) and
  `π n = 0` for `n ≥ 2` (Kerodon, Example 3.2.2.13).
- For the discrete simplicial set on `S`, `π 0` is `S` and every higher group vanishes. A model of
  `π 0` as the set of vertices would agree here but not on the nerve of the indiscrete groupoid on
  two objects, whose two vertices form one component.

*Needs:* for the bijection with `π₀`, Tau Ceti `SSet.KanComplex.π`; Mathlib `SSet.π₀`,
`SSet.KanComplex.hornFilling`.

**1.1.3. Change of basepoint.** Define, for an edge `e : x → y` of a Kan complex `X`, the isomorphism
`e_♯ : π n X x ≅ π n X y`, the composite of the isomorphisms
`π n X x ← π n Fun(Δ[1], X) e → π n X y` induced by evaluation at the two endpoints, which are
trivial fibrations and hence bijective on every `π n` at every vertex, by lifting representatives
and their homotopies against boundary inclusions (Kerodon, Example 3.2.2.18). It is a group
isomorphism for `n ≥ 1`.

- `e_♯` depends only on the class of `e` in the fundamental groupoid (Kerodon, Warning 3.2.2.19).
- `(e · e')_♯ = e'_♯ ∘ e_♯` for composable edges, and the constant edge gives the identity.
- For a loop `γ` at `x`, `γ_♯` defines a left action of `π 1 X x` on `π n X x` (after inversion, in
  the product order of the Conventions), by group automorphisms for `n ≥ 1`.
- A pointed map `f` satisfies `f_* ∘ e_♯ = (f e)_♯ ∘ f_*`.
- For a homotopy `h` from `f` to `g`, with `e` the edge traced by `x`, `g_* = e_♯ ∘ f_*` on
  `π n X x`. Hence a homotopy equivalence of Kan complexes induces bijections on every `π n` at every
  vertex (Kerodon, Remark 3.2.2.17).

**Checks.**

- For `e = (12)` and `e′ = (23)` in the nerve of `S₃`, `(e · e′)_♯` sends the class of `(12)` to
  the class of `(13)`, matching `e′_♯ ∘ e_♯`. Composing in the other order would give `(23)`.
- For `n = 1` and a loop `γ` at `x`, `γ_♯(a) = [γ]⁻¹ · a · [γ]`. In the nerve of `S₃` with
  `γ = (12)` it moves the class of `(13)` to the class of `(23)`.
- In the nerve of `S₃`, the loop `γ = (123)` sends the class of `(12)` to the class of `(23)`, not
  to itself. Together with the previous Check this excludes the definition giving the identity for
  every loop and the opposite conjugation.

**1.1.4. π₁ and the fundamental groupoid.** Prove that, for a Kan complex `X` and a vertex `x`, the

**Checks.**

### 1.2 The long exact sequence of a Kan fibration

**1.2.1.** Prove that, for a Kan fibration `p : E → B` between Kan complexes, a vertex `e` of `E`, `b = p(e)`
and `F = SSet.fiber p b`, there is a long exact sequence
`⋯ → π (n + 1) B b → π n F e → π n E e → π n B b → ⋯ → π 0 E e → π 0 B b`, exact at every term, as
groups down to `π 1` and as pointed sets below, together with an action of `π 1 B b` on `π 0 F`
whose orbits are the fibers of `π 0 F → π 0 E` (Kerodon, Theorem 3.2.6.1 and §§3.2.5–3.2.6). It
consists of:

- **the connecting map** `∂ : π (n + 1) B b → π n F e` for `n ≥ 0` (Kerodon, Definition 3.2.5.1 and
  Prop. 3.2.5.2): for a representative `τ : Δ[n + 1] → B`, lift it to `τ̃ : Δ[n + 1] → E` with
  `d_i τ̃` constant at `e` for `1 ≤ i ≤ n + 1`, using the Kan condition for `Λ[n + 1, 0]`, and set
  `∂[τ] = [d₀ τ̃]`. It depends only on `[τ]`, is a group homomorphism for `n ≥ 1` (Kerodon,
  Prop. 3.2.5.4) and is natural in maps of Kan fibrations;
- **exactness** at every term. In particular the image of `π 0 F` in `π 0 E` is the preimage of the
  component of `b` (Kerodon, Corollary 3.2.6.3). The exactness at the total space is
  the `Suggested.lean` declaration `range_π_map_fiber_ι`;
- **the action** of `π 1 B b` on `π 0 F` (Kerodon, Variant 3.2.5.5): two components of `F` have the
  same image in `π 0 E` exactly when they lie in one orbit (Kerodon, Corollary 3.2.6.5), and the
  stabilizer of the component of `e` is the image of `π 1 E e` (Kerodon, Corollary 3.2.6.7).

*Proof route.* The connecting map is well defined by lifting a homotopy of representatives against
the anodyne map of a horn times `Δ[1]`. Exactness at the fiber, total space and base are three
lifting arguments on representatives with pointed homotopies (Kerodon, Props. 3.2.6.2,
3.2.6.4 and 3.2.6.6); the action and its orbits come out of the same lifts of edges of `B`.

**Checks.**

- For the projection `X × Y → Y` with `X`, `Y` Kan, every `τ` lifts to `(e, τ)` and `∂` is trivial.
  A definition taking `d₀` of an arbitrary lift, without constant other faces, would not land in
  `F`.
- For the path fibration `P_x X = Fun(Δ[1], X) ×_X {x} → X` (evaluation at 1), whose total space is
  contractible, `∂` is an isomorphism `π (n + 1) X x ≅ π n (Ω X) x`, with `Ω X` the fiber.
- For the universal cover `N(ℤ ⋉ ℤ) → N(ℤ)` (the nerve of the action groupoid of `ℤ` on itself),
  with discrete fiber `ℤ` and contractible total space, `∂ : π 1 N(ℤ) → π 0 ℤ` is a bijection,
  which excludes the trivial map. The action of `π 1 = ℤ` on the fiber is by translation, with one
  orbit, matching the single component of the total space; a statement claiming `π 0 F → π 0 E`
  injective in general would fail here.
- For `p : Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0] → Δ[0]`, `F` has two points and `π 1 B` is trivial, so the action has two
  orbits and `π 0 E` has two elements, as exactness requires.

*Needs:*
- §1.1.1, π₀ and homotopy invariance; §1.1.3, change of basepoint (for the action).
- Mathlib `SSet.KanComplex.hornFilling`, `SSet.anodyneExtensions_pushoutObjObjι`, and the Kan
  fibrations `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.J.rlp` (for lifting `τ` and its homotopies).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.fiber` and its Kan instance; `SSet.KanComplex.π.mul_mk_eq_iff` (`∂` is a
  homomorphism).

### 1.3 Weak equivalences of Kan complexes and Whitehead's theorem

This subsection identifies, for Kan complexes, the π-isomorphisms, the trivial Kan fibrations and the
homotopy equivalences (Kerodon, §3.2.7).

**1.3.1. π-isomorphisms.** Define a map `f : X → Y` of Kan complexes to be a π-isomorphism when `π₀ f` is
a bijection and `π n f : π n X x → π n Y (f x)` is a bijection for every `n ≥ 1` and every vertex
`x` (Kerodon, Theorem 3.2.7.1, conditions (a) and (b)). Prove:

- π-isomorphisms satisfy two out of three and are closed under retracts;
- every homotopy equivalence is a π-isomorphism (§1.1, change of basepoint);
- by change of basepoint, it suffices to test `π n f` at one vertex of each component.

**Checks.**

- The inclusion of either object into the nerve of the groupoid with two uniquely isomorphic
  objects is a π-isomorphism. A definition asking for a bijection on vertices would reject it.
- `Δ[0] → N(ℤ)` (the nerve of the group `ℤ`) is a monomorphism of Kan complexes, bijective on `π₀`,
  but not a π-isomorphism, since `π 1` is `0` and `ℤ`.
- `Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0] → Δ[0]` is bijective on every `π n` at every vertex for `n ≥ 1` (all trivial) but not
  on `π₀`. A definition omitting `π₀` would accept it.

*Needs:*
- §1.1.1, π₀ and homotopy invariance; §1.1.3, change of basepoint.

**1.3.2. Trivial Kan fibrations.** Prove that a Kan fibration between Kan complexes has the right lifting
property against all monomorphisms exactly when it is a π-isomorphism, and exactly when it is a
homotopy equivalence (Kerodon, Prop. 3.2.7.2).

*Proof route.* By the long exact sequence, every fiber of a π-isomorphic Kan fibration has trivial
homotopy groups and is nonempty, so it is contractible (Kerodon, Theorem 3.2.4.3). Fill the
boundary inclusions `∂Δ[m] ⊆ Δ[m]` by induction on `m`, or produce a shrinking and apply Tau Ceti
`SSet.rlp_monomorphisms_iff`.

*Needs:*
- §1.3.1, π-isomorphisms.
- §1.2.1.
- Mathlib `SSet.rlp_monomorphisms` (it suffices to lift against `∂Δ[m] ⊆ Δ[m]`).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.Shrinking`, `SSet.rlp_monomorphisms_iff`.

**1.3.3. Whitehead's theorem.** Prove that a map of Kan complexes is a homotopy equivalence if and only if
it is a π-isomorphism (Kerodon, Theorem 3.2.7.1). Together with Tau Ceti's
`SSet.isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_iff_exists_homotopy_inverse`, this gives the Kan-group criterion: a
map of Kan complexes is a weak homotopy equivalence exactly when it is a π-isomorphism. This is the
`Suggested.lean` declaration `isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_iff_bijective_π`.

*Proof route.* Factor `f` through the mapping path object `X → X ×_Y Fun(Δ[1], Y) → Y` (Kerodon,
Example 3.1.8.10, tag 0101). The first map is a homotopy equivalence and the second a Kan fibration.
By two out of three the second map is a π-isomorphism, hence a trivial fibration, hence a homotopy
equivalence.

**Checks.**

- Every trivial fibration between Kan complexes, for instance evaluation at a vertex
  `Fun(Δ[n], X) → X`, is a π-isomorphism and a homotopy equivalence.
- `∂Δ[2]` is not Kan, so the criterion says nothing about `∂Δ[2] → Δ[0]` directly. Its naive `π 1`
  is trivial, while the weak homotopy type has `π 1 = ℤ` (§2.2). This is why Layer 2 is needed.
- For the nerve of the groupoid with two uniquely isomorphic objects, the inclusion of an object is
  a homotopy equivalence; a homotopy inverse must collapse both objects to one.

*Needs:*
- §1.3.2, trivial Kan fibrations.
- Mathlib `SSet.fibration_pullbackObjObjπ` (`Fun(Δ[1], Y) → Y × Y` is a Kan fibration).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_iff_exists_homotopy_inverse`.

### Examples

- The nerve of `S₃` is a Kan complex with `π 1 ≅ S₃` (anti-isomorphically, by the product order of
  the Conventions), `π n = 0` for `n ≥ 2`. Its loop at `(12)` acts on `π 1` by conjugation.
- For a Kan complex `X`, the path fibration `P_x X → X` has contractible total space, and its long
  exact sequence identifies `π n` of the loop space with `π (n + 1) X x`.
- `Fun(Δ[1], X) → X × X` is a Kan fibration for Kan `X`, with fiber over `(x, y)` the space of paths
  from `x` to `y`.

## Layer 2: Subdivision and Kan's Ex^∞

### 2.1 Subdivision, Ex and the comparison map

**2.1.1.** Use Mathlib's adjunction `SSet.sdExAdjunction : sd ⊣ ex` (Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.3.4), where
`sd Δ[n]` is the nerve of the poset of nonempty subsets of `[n]` and `Ex X` has `n`-simplices the
maps `sd Δ[n] → X`. Define the last-vertex map `λ_X : sd X → X` (`Suggested.lean` declaration `lastVertex`), natural in `X`,
induced by the map of posets sending a nonempty subset of `[n]` to its largest element. Define its
transpose `ρ_X : X → Ex X` (`Suggested.lean` declaration `exComparison`; Kerodon, Construction 3.3.4.3). Prove:

- `sd` preserves monomorphisms and anodyne maps, and preserves strong anodyne maps (Kerodon,
  Prop. 3.3.5.3; Moss, Prop. 2.19);
- `Ex` preserves Kan fibrations and Kan complexes (Kerodon, Corollaries 3.3.5.4 and 3.3.5.5);
- `ρ_X` is a monomorphism, bijective on vertices, and is a strong anodyne map (Moss, Theorem 3.3);
  in particular it is a weak homotopy equivalence (Kerodon, Theorem 3.3.5.1);
- `Ex` preserves finite limits and filtered colimits, being a right adjoint whose left adjoint
  preserves finite presentability of simplices.

**Checks.**

- `sd Δ[0] = Δ[0]`, so `Ex X` and `X` have the same vertices. A subdivision adding a barycentre to
  every simplex, vertices included, would break this.
- `sd Δ[1]` has three vertices and two nondegenerate edges, both ending at the barycentre. Hence an
  edge of `Ex X` is a zigzag `x → z ← y`, and `Ex Δ[1]` has an edge from 1 to 0, namely `1 → 1 ← 0`,
  although `Δ[1]` has none. So `ρ` at `Δ[1]` is not an isomorphism.
- `sd Δ[2]` has 7 vertices, 12 nondegenerate edges and 6 nondegenerate 2-simplices (the chains of
  nonempty subsets of `{0, 1, 2}`), and `sd ∂Δ[2]` is the hexagon. A subdivision through all subsets,
  the empty one included, would have a cone point and be contractible.

*Needs:*
- Mathlib `SSet.sd`, `SSet.ex`, `SSet.sdExAdjunction`.
- Mathlib `SSet.Subcomplex.Pairing.strongAnodyneExtensions`, `SSet.anodyneExtensions_eq_llp_rlp`.
- Tau Ceti `SSet.anodyneExtensions.isWeakHomotopyEquivalence`.

### 2.2 Ex^∞ is a functorial Kan replacement

**2.2.1.** Define `Ex^∞ : SSet ⥤ SSet` as the colimit of `X → Ex X → Ex² X → ⋯`, the `k`-th map being
`ρ_(Ex^k X)`, with its natural map `ρ^∞_X : X → Ex^∞ X` (`Suggested.lean` declarations `exInfty`, `toExInfty`; Kerodon,
Construction 3.3.6.1). Prove that, for every simplicial set `X`, `ρ^∞_X` is a strong anodyne
monomorphism, bijective on vertices, into a Kan complex, hence a weak homotopy equivalence (Kerodon,
Props. 3.3.6.7 and 3.3.6.9; Moss, Prop. 3.2 and Corollary 3.11;
`Suggested.lean` declarations `kanComplex_exInfty_obj`, `strongAnodyneExtensions_toExInfty_app`); that `Ex^∞` preserves finite limits, filtered colimits, Kan
fibrations, trivial fibrations, monomorphisms, homotopies and homotopy equivalences (Kerodon,
Props. 3.3.6.4 and 3.3.6.6; Moss, Prop. 3.12); and that for a Kan complex `X`, `ρ^∞_X`
is a homotopy equivalence and so a π-isomorphism (Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.6.10).

*Proof route.* `ρ^∞_X` is a monomorphism and a bijection on vertices because each `ρ` is (Kerodon,
Prop. 3.3.6.2). Finite limits and filtered colimits commute with the sequential colimit, and
`Ex` preserves them; in particular the fiber of `Ex^∞ f` over a vertex is `Ex^∞` of the fiber
(Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.6.5). A horn is finite, so a horn in `Ex^∞ X` comes from
some `Ex^k X` with `k ≥ 1`, and a horn in `Ex^k X` with `k ≥ 1` has a filler in `Ex^(k+1) X`: for
`k = 1` the filler is a map `sd sd Δ[n] → X`, built in the universal case `X = sd Λ[n, i]` from an
explicit map of posets (Kerodon, proof of Prop. 3.3.6.9). A horn in `X` itself has a filler in
`Ex² X` (Moss, the remark after Theorem 3.3), but in general none in `Ex X` (Checks below). For
the anodyne statement,
compose the regular pairings of §2.1 for each `ρ_(Ex^k X)` along the sequence (Moss, Theorem 3.3
and Corollary 3.11). For Kan `X`, an anodyne map between Kan complexes is a homotopy equivalence: a
retraction comes from lifting the identity of `X` against `ρ^∞_X`, and the homotopy from lifting
against the anodyne pushout-product of `ρ^∞_X` with `∂Δ[1] ⊆ Δ[1]` (Moss, Lemma 4.6). Then apply
Whitehead's theorem (§1.3).

**Checks.**

- `Ex^∞ Δ[0] = Δ[0]` and `Ex^∞ ∅ = ∅`. A Kan replacement through the small object argument would
  also fix `Δ[0]` up to homotopy, but not on the nose, and would not preserve products.
- `Ex^∞ (Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0])` is again two points. A functor adding paths between components would fail
  to preserve the coproduct.
- `π 1 (Ex^∞ ∂Δ[2])` is infinite cyclic, while the naive `π 1` of `∂Δ[2]` (§1.3) is trivial. A
  definition of the homotopy groups of a general simplicial set without fibrant replacement would
  give the wrong answer.
- `Ex^∞ Λ[2, 0]` has trivial homotopy groups at every vertex, because `Λ[2, 0] ⊆ Δ[2]` is anodyne
  and `Δ[2]` is contractible. For the nerve of the poset `[1]`, `Ex^∞` has trivial `π 1`.

*Needs:*
- §2.1.1.
- §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem.
- Mathlib `SSet.Subcomplex.Pairing.strongAnodyneExtensions` (a regular pairing gives a strong
  anodyne map); composing the pairings of the sequence is part of this target's proof (Moss,
  Corollary 3.11). Mathlib `SSet.anodyneExtensions_pushoutObjObjι`.

### Examples

- `Ex^∞ ∂Δ[2]` is a Kan model of the circle: `π 0` is a point, `π 1 ≅ ℤ`, `π n = 0` for `n ≥ 2`.
- `Ex^∞` of the nerve of the poset `0 < 1 > 2` (a zigzag) is contractible, although the nerve is not
  a Kan complex.
- A horn in `X` need not fill in `Ex X`. For `X = Λ[2, 0]` and the horn `ρ_X ∘ id`, a filler is a
  map `sd Δ[2] → X` sending `{0, 1} ↦ 1` and `{0, 2} ↦ 2` (the last-vertex map on the two given
  edges). The vertex `{1, 2}` of `sd Δ[2]` receives edges from `{1}` and `{2}`, so its image needs
  edges into it from 1 and from 2 in `X`, and `Λ[2, 0]` has none.

§2.2.

## Layer 3: Weak homotopy equivalences and the Kan–Quillen model structure

### 3.1 Weak homotopy equivalences

**3.1.1.** Use the weak homotopy equivalences of the Conventions, Tau Ceti's `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`
(Kerodon, Definition 3.1.7.12), and the class `weakHomotopyEquivalences` (`Suggested.lean` declaration). Prove:

- `f` is a weak homotopy equivalence if and only if `Ex^∞ f` is (Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.6.8);
- `f` is a weak homotopy equivalence if and only if `Ex^∞ f` is a homotopy equivalence, if and only
  if `Ex^∞ f` is a π-isomorphism. The last form is the Kan-group criterion for arbitrary simplicial
  sets;
- the class is closed under retracts (from the `Ex^∞` form and §1.3).

*Proof route.* `ρ^∞` is anodyne and so a weak homotopy equivalence, so two out of three in the
naturality square of `ρ^∞` reduces every statement to Kan complexes. There weak homotopy
equivalences are homotopy equivalences (Tau Ceti), hence π-isomorphisms (§1.3).

**Checks.**

- `∂Δ[2] → Δ[0]` is not a weak homotopy equivalence: maps into the Kan complex `N(ℤ)` up to homotopy
  are `ℤ` on the left (the degree around the triangle) and a point on the right. A definition
  through `π₀` and the naive `π 1` would accept it.
- Between Kan complexes the class agrees with the π-isomorphisms of §1.3 and with homotopy
  equivalences. A definition that only asked for bijections on `π₀ Fun(−, Z)` for `Z` discrete
  would accept every map between connected simplicial sets, such as `∂Δ[2] → Δ[0]`.

  `SSet.anodyneExtensions.isWeakHomotopyEquivalence`.

### 3.2 The Kan–Quillen model structure

**3.2.1.** Prove that the monomorphisms, the Kan fibrations and the weak homotopy equivalences form a model
structure on `SSet` (HTT §A.2.7, PDF pp. 846–847; Quillen, Ch. II, §3; Goerss–Jardine, Theorem
I.11.3; Moss, Theorem 4.9). The content beyond the library is the identification of the two
trivial classes:

- **trivial fibrations.** A map has the right lifting property against all monomorphisms if and only
  if it is a Kan fibration and a weak homotopy equivalence (Kerodon, Prop. 3.3.7.6). This is the
  `Suggested.lean` declaration `rlp_monomorphisms_eq`. Equivalently, a Kan fibration is a weak homotopy equivalence
  exactly when each of its fibers is contractible.
- **trivial cofibrations.** A map is anodyne if and only if it is a monomorphism and a weak homotopy
  equivalence (Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.7.7). This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `anodyneExtensions_eq`.

With Tau Ceti's weak factorization systems (monomorphisms, trivial fibrations) and (anodyne maps,
Kan fibrations), and two out of three for weak homotopy equivalences, these identifications are the
model axioms in the form of Mathlib's `HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory.mk'`. Construct the
`HomotopicalAlgebra.ModelCategory SSet` structure this way, as a definition rather than an instance,
over the `CategoryWithWeakEquivalences` structure `categoryWithWeakEquivalences` (`Suggested.lean` declaration) whose class is the
weak homotopy equivalences, so that it never competes with the scoped realization-based instance of
Tau Ceti. This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `modelCategory`. Then:

- every simplicial set is cofibrant, and the fibrant objects are the Kan complexes;
- the homotopy category is equivalent to Kan complexes modulo simplicial homotopy (ModelCategoryTheory, Part
  ModelCategories §3.1 (HTT, §A.2.2 after Prop. A.2.2.1)), with Mathlib's bifibrant-object homotopy
  category.

**Checks.**

- `Λ[2, 0] ⊆ Δ[2]` is a trivial cofibration (it is anodyne), but not a categorical equivalence:
  the homotopy category of `Λ[2, 0]` has no morphism from 1 to 2. A structure with the same
  cofibrations and Joyal's weak equivalences would reject it.
- `Δ[1]` is cofibrant but not fibrant, since it fails the horn `Λ[2, 0]` (Conventions), although it
  is fibrant for the Joyal structure.
- `∅ ⊆ Δ[0]` is a cofibration and not a trivial one, since `π₀` changes; `Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0] → Δ[0]` is a
  fibration and not a trivial one, since its fiber has two points.

*Proof route.* Follow Moss's combinatorial route, avoiding minimal fibrations and topological
spaces. For trivial fibrations, take a Kan fibration `p` that is a weak homotopy equivalence. Then
`Ex^∞ p` is a Kan fibration between Kan complexes and a π-isomorphism, hence a trivial fibration
(§1.3), and `Ex^∞` reflects trivial fibrations among Kan fibrations (Moss, Prop. 3.13): a
lifting problem against `∂Δ[n] ⊆ Δ[n]` is solved in `Ex^∞` and transported back along the strong
anodyne `ρ^∞` by Moss's argument. Conversely, a trivial fibration has a shrinking and so is a
homotopy equivalence (Tau Ceti `SSet.rlp_monomorphisms_iff`). For trivial cofibrations, factor a

anodyne factor.

### 3.3 Combinatorial generation and filtered colimits

**3.3.1.** Prove that the Kan–Quillen model structure is combinatorial in the sense of ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §6.1 (HTT, Def. A.2.6.1, PDF p. 836), with generating cofibrations the boundary inclusions
`∂Δ[n] ⊆ Δ[n]`, `n ≥ 0` (HTT §A.2.7, PDF p. 847), and generating trivial cofibrations the horn
inclusions `Λ[n, i] ⊆ Δ[n]`, `n ≥ 1` (Kerodon, Definition 3.1.2.1 and Corollary 3.3.7.7); the generation by the horn inclusions follows from `anodyneExtensions_eq`. Prove that weak homotopy equivalences are stable under filtered
colimits (Kerodon, Prop. 3.2.8.3); this is the `Suggested.lean` declaration
`isStableUnderFilteredColimits_weakHomotopyEquivalences`.

*Proof route.* `SSet` is locally presentable (Mathlib's instance for presheaf categories). The
cofibrations are the weak saturation of the boundary inclusions (Tau Ceti
`SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.llp_rlp_I`). The trivial cofibrations are the anodyne maps (§3.2), which
are the weak saturation of the horns (Mathlib `SSet.anodyneExtensions_eq_llp_rlp`). For filtered
colimits, `Ex^∞` preserves them (§2.2), and on Kan complexes the homotopy groups of a filtered
colimit are the colimit of the homotopy groups, since `Δ[n]` and `∂Δ[n]` are finite; then apply the
Kan-group criterion (§3.1).

**Checks.**

- The generating cofibrations include `∅ ⊆ Δ[0]` (the case `n = 0`). Without it, `∅ → Δ[0]` would
  lift against every remaining generator and count as a trivial fibration, although it is not a
  weak homotopy equivalence.
- The generating trivial cofibrations include the one-dimensional horns. Without them,
  `∂Δ[1] ⊆ Δ[1]` would be a fibration (Conventions).
- Filtered colimits cannot be replaced by arbitrary colimits. The map of spans from
  `Δ[0] ← ∂Δ[1] → Δ[1]` to `Δ[0] ← ∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]` is a weak homotopy equivalence at each of the three
  objects, but the induced map of pushouts `Δ[1]/∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]` is not one: `Δ[1]/∂Δ[1]` has
  `π 1 = ℤ` after `Ex^∞`.

### 3.4 Properness

**3.4.1.** Prove that the Kan–Quillen model structure is left proper: a pushout of a weak homotopy equivalence
along a monomorphism is a weak homotopy equivalence. This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration
`isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_of_isPushout` (ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §3.4 (HTT, Prop. A.2.4.2, PDF p. 832),
every simplicial set being cofibrant). Prove that it is right proper: a pullback of a weak homotopy
equivalence along a Kan fibration is a weak homotopy equivalence. This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration
`isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_of_isPullback` (Kerodon, Corollary 3.3.7.4; HTT §A.2.7, PDF p. 846).

*Proof route.* Left properness is ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §3.4 (HTT, Prop. A.2.4.2) applied to §3.2. For
right properness, `Ex^∞` preserves pullbacks and Kan fibrations, so it suffices to treat Kan
fibrations between Kan complexes. There the long exact sequences of the two fibrations, which have
the same fibers, and the five lemma give the π-isomorphism (Kerodon, Prop. 3.3.7.1). Moss
gives a combinatorial alternative: the pullback of a horn inclusion along a Kan fibration is strong
anodyne (Moss, Theorem 5.1).

**Checks.**

- The Kan fibration hypothesis is needed. Pull back the weak homotopy equivalence `{1} ⊆ Δ[1]` along
  the monomorphism `∂Δ[1] ⊆ Δ[1]`, which is not a Kan fibration: the result is `{1} ⊆ ∂Δ[1]`,
  which is not a weak homotopy equivalence.
- Pushing out the anodyne `Λ[2, 1] ⊆ Δ[2]` along any monomorphism gives an anodyne map, as left
  properness and §3.2 both predict.
- Pulling back the weak homotopy equivalence `Δ[1] → Δ[0]` along the Kan fibration
  `Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0] → Δ[0]` gives `Δ[1] ⊔ Δ[1] → Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0]`, a weak homotopy equivalence.

*Needs:* for left properness, §3.2.1; ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §3.4.2 (HTT, Prop. A.2.4.2).

*Needs:* for right properness:
- §2.2.1 (`Ex^∞` preserves pullbacks and Kan fibrations).
- §3.1.1 (reduction to Kan complexes through `Ex^∞`); §1.2.1; §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem.

### 3.5 Simplexwise weakly contractible maps

**3.5.1.** Prove that if `p : X → S` is a map such that, for every simplex `σ : Δ[k] → S`, the pullback
`Δ[k] ×_S X` is weakly contractible, then `p` is a weak homotopy equivalence (Kerodon, Corollary
3.4.2.17). This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_of_isWeaklyContractible_pullback`. The more
general form compares two maps over `S` simplex by simplex (Kerodon, Prop. 3.4.2.16).

**3.5.2.** Prove first the colimit lemma: for a model category `M` and a Reedy category `J` whose matching
categories are all empty or connected, the constant-diagram functor from `M` to the Reedy model
structure on `J ⥤ M` is right Quillen, so its left adjoint, the colimit, is left Quillen (Hirschhorn,
*Model Categories and Their Localizations*, §15.10). The route: for a fibration `Y ⟶ Y'` of `M`, the
matching object of a constant diagram at `j` is a power of `Y` indexed by the components of the
matching category, so it is the terminal object when that category is empty and `Y` when it is
connected. The relative matching map at `j` is then `Y ⟶ Y'` or an isomorphism, a fibration in both
cases, and the same holds for trivial fibrations.

*Needs:* ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.5.1 (HTT, Notation A.2.9.7), §7.7.1 (HTT, Prop.
A.2.9.19) and §4.1.1 (HTT, §A.2.5, Quillen adjunctions).

*Proof route.* Make the category of simplices `el(S)` a Reedy category in the sense of Part
ModelCategories §7.4: the degree of `σ : Δ[k] → S` is `k`, and the two classes and the unique
factorizations are lifted along the projection `el(S) → SimplexCategory` from Mathlib's
`SimplexCategory.reedyStructure`. `S` is the colimit over `el(S)` of `σ ↦ Δ[k]`, and since
colimits in `SSet` are universal, `X` is the colimit of `σ ↦ Δ[k] ×_S X`. The map of diagrams is a
levelwise weak homotopy equivalence. Both diagrams are Reedy cofibrant, their latching maps being
`∂Δ[k] ⊆ Δ[k]` and its pullback. The matching category at `σ` is empty when `σ` is nondegenerate,
and connected when `σ` is degenerate, since the Eilenberg–Zilber lemma gives `σ` a unique
nondegenerate root through which every degeneracy of `σ` factors. So the colimit functor is left
Quillen for the Reedy model structure (the colimit lemma, §3.5.2), and Ken Brown's lemma concludes.
Kerodon's route by induction on the skeleta of `S` and homotopy pushouts is an alternative.

**Checks.**

- For `S = Δ[0]` the hypothesis says that `X` is weakly contractible, and the conclusion is the
  same statement.
- `∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]` fails the hypothesis (its pullback along the vertex is `∂Δ[1]`) and is not a weak
  homotopy equivalence, as required.
- The converse fails: `Λ[2, 1] ⊆ Δ[2]` is a weak homotopy equivalence, but its pullback along the
  edge `{0, 2}` is two points, not weakly contractible. A statement asserting an equivalence would
  be false.

*Needs:*
- the colimit lemma, §3.5.2; §3.2.1, §3.1.1.
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.4.1 (HTT, Def. A.2.9.1)
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.5.1 (HTT, Notation A.2.9.7)
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.7.1 (HTT, Prop. A.2.9.19)
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §4.1.1 (HTT, §A.2.5, Quillen adjunctions) for left Quillen functors and Ken Brown's lemma
- Mathlib `SimplexCategory.reedyStructure`, `SSet.Subcomplex` and nondegenerate simplices
  (Eilenberg–Zilber).

### 3.6 Weakly contractible categories of factorizations

**3.6.1.** Prove that natural transformations of functors give homotopies of nerves (Kerodon, Example 3.1.5.7, tag 00ZX). A
natural transformation `α : F ⇒ G` of functors `A ⟶ B` between categories is a functor `A × [1] ⟶ B`, and so
gives a simplicial homotopy `N(A) × Δ[1] ≅ N(A × [1]) ⟶ N(B)` from `N(F)` to `N(G)`. Hence:

1. functors joined by a zigzag of natural transformations induce the same map on homotopy classes of maps into
   every Kan complex;
2. a functor with a left or a right adjoint induces a weak homotopy equivalence of nerves (Kerodon,
   Prop. 3.1.7.9, tag 00U8, which gives the stronger conclusion of a homotopy equivalence);
3. a category with a zigzag of natural transformations from the identity to a constant functor has weakly
   contractible nerve.

*Proof route.* The nerve preserves products, being a right adjoint, which gives the homotopy. A homotopy
`N(A) × Δ[1] → Z` is an edge of `Fun(N(A), Z)`, so maps joined by a zigzag of homotopies lie in one
component; composing the homotopies of nerves with a map `N(B) → Z` gives (1). For (2), the unit and
counit join the two composites to the identities, so by (1) the nerve of the functor induces bijections on
homotopy classes of maps into every Kan complex, which is a weak homotopy equivalence (Conventions; §3.1).
For (3), let `p` be the map to `Δ[0]` and `i` the inclusion of the constant value. Then `p ∘ i` is the
identity of `Δ[0]` and, by (1), `i ∘ p` acts as the identity on `π₀ Fun(N(A), Z)` for every Kan complex
`Z`.

**Checks.**

- The inclusion `{0} ⟶ [1]` of the initial object has the right adjoint `[1] ⟶ {0}`, and its nerve `Δ[0] ⟶ Δ[1]` is a weak homotopy
  equivalence, as (2) says; it is not an isomorphism, so (2) cannot be sharpened to isomorphisms of nerves.
- A category with a terminal object t has the natural transformation `id ⇒ const_t`, so its nerve is weakly
  contractible by (3). The discrete category on two objects has no such zigzag, and its nerve `∂Δ[1]` is not
  weakly contractible.

*Needs:*
- Mathlib `CategoryTheory.nerve`, `CategoryTheory.nerveAdjunction` (the nerve is a right adjoint);
- §3.1.1 (weak homotopy equivalences, weak contractibility); Tau Ceti `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence`,
  `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence.of_homotopy`, `SSet.IsWeaklyContractible`.

**3.6.2.** Prove that factorizations through earlier stages are weakly contractible (HTT, Lemma A.2.9.17, PDF p. 856).
Take a good filtration of a Reedy category (Part ModelCategories §7.4.2), the object `j` it adjoins at some stage, and
an endomorphism `f ≠ id` of `j`. The factorizations `j → i → j` of `f` whose middle object `i` was adjoined at an
earlier stage, with maps of middle objects compatible with both legs as morphisms, form a category whose nerve is
weakly contractible.

*Proof route.* Following HTT's proof, pass to the full subcategory `I₁` of factorizations whose
second map lies in the class raising degree, and then to the subcategory `I₂` where in addition the
first map lies in the class lowering degree. Both inclusions `I₂ ⊆ I₁ ⊆ I₀` admit an adjoint, since
the Reedy factorization of a map of `𝒥_β` factors through `𝒥_β` (Part ModelCategories §7.4; HTT Remark A.2.9.13, PDF p. 855).
A functor with an adjoint induces a weak homotopy equivalence of nerves (§3.6.1, part (2)).
`I₂` has a single object and no nontrivial endomorphisms, so its nerve is `Δ[0]`.

*Needs:*
- §3.1.1 (weak contractibility); §3.6.1, part (2); Tau Ceti
  `SSet.IsWeakHomotopyEquivalence.of_homotopy` (homotopy equivalences are weak homotopy equivalences).
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.4.1 (HTT, Def. A.2.9.1)
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §7.4.1 (HTT, Notation A.2.9.11; Remark A.2.9.13)
- Mathlib `CategoryTheory.nerve`.

### Examples

- `Ex^∞` is a fibrant replacement functor for the Kan–Quillen model structure, and `Fun(K, X)` for
  `X` Kan is a derived mapping space.
- The nerve of a category with a terminal object is weakly contractible. Applied to slices, §3.5
  shows that the nerve of a functor between categories whose comma categories are contractible is a
  weak homotopy equivalence (the simplicial form of Quillen's Theorem A).
- The localization of `SSet` at the weak homotopy equivalences is equivalent to Kan complexes modulo
  simplicial homotopy.

## Layer 4: Connectivity

### 4.1 Connective maps

**4.1.1.** Define a Kan complex to be `n`-connective as in the Conventions (Kerodon, Definition 3.5.1.1); this
is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `IsConnective`, which takes the Kan complex structure as an argument. Define a map `f : X → Y` of simplicial sets to be `n`-connective when,
after factoring `Ex^∞ f` as an anodyne map followed by a Kan fibration, every fiber of the Kan
fibration is `n`-connective (Kerodon, Definition 3.5.1.13, Variant 3.5.1.15 and Prop.
3.5.1.22). Every map is `n`-connective for `n < 0`. Prove:

- the notion is independent of the factorization, and invariant under weak homotopy equivalence of
  arrows;
- a composite of `n`-connective maps is `n`-connective, and if `g ∘ f` is `n`-connective and `g` is
  `(n + 1)`-connective then `f` is `n`-connective (Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.1.28);
- a map is a weak homotopy equivalence exactly when it is `n`-connective for every `n` (Kerodon,
  Remark 3.5.1.19).

**Checks.**

- A map is 0-connective exactly when it is surjective on `π₀` (Kerodon, Example 3.5.1.17). The
  inclusion `{0} ⊆ Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0]` is not 0-connective.
- `∂Δ[n]` is `(n − 1)`-connective (Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.2.6) but not `n`-connective: it is empty
  for `n = 0`, has two components for `n = 1`, and `π (n − 1)` of `Ex^∞ ∂Δ[n]` is `ℤ` for `n ≥ 2`. For `n = 2`,
  `∂Δ[2]` is connected with `π 1 = ℤ` after `Ex^∞`. The classical indexing would call it
  0-connected.
- `Δ[0] → K(A, n)` for `n ≥ 1` and `A ≠ 0` is `(n − 1)`-connective and not `n`-connective: its
  homotopy fiber is the loop space `K(A, n − 1)`, and `π n` gives `0 → A`, which is not surjective.
  A definition through the cofiber would call it `n`-connective.

*Needs:*
- §1.2.1.
- §1.3.1, π-isomorphisms; §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem.
- §2.2.1 (fibrant replacement); §3.1.1 (invariance under weak homotopy equivalence).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.isWeakFactorizationSystem_anodyneExtensions` (the
  factorization), `SSet.fiber`.

### 4.2 The lifting characterization

**4.2.1.** Prove that, for a Kan fibration `p : X → Y` and `n ≥ 0`, the following are equivalent (Kerodon,
Prop. 3.5.2.1): `p` is `n`-connective; every fiber `SSet.fiber p y` is `n`-connective; `p` has
the right lifting property against `A ⊆ B` for every simplicial set `B` of dimension `≤ n`; `p` has
the right lifting property against `∂Δ[m] ⊆ Δ[m]` for every `0 ≤ m ≤ n`. The equivalence of the
second and fourth conditions is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `isConnective_fiber_iff`.

*Proof route.* For fibers, an extension problem for `∂Δ[m] → X_y` is a null-homotopy of an
`(m − 1)`-sphere when `m ≥ 1`, and nonemptiness when `m = 0`. For a Kan fibration the lifting
problem against `∂Δ[m] ⊆ Δ[m]` reduces to a fiber by lifting the base simplex first. Skeletal
induction passes from boundary inclusions to all relative inclusions of dimension `≤ n`.

*Needs:*
- §4.1.1.
- §1.2.1.

### 4.3 Homotopy pushouts of connective maps

**4.3.1.** Prove that `n`-connective maps are stable under homotopy pushout: in a homotopy pushout square of
simplicial sets whose left vertical map `f` is `n`-connective, the right vertical map is
`n`-connective (Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.2.7). Homotopy pushout squares are those of ModelCategoryTheory, Part
ModelCategories §3.5 (HTT, §A.2.4, homotopy pushout squares, PDF p. 833) in the Kan–Quillen model structure,
equivalently Kerodon's (Kerodon, Definition 3.4.2.1).

*Proof route.* Factor `f` as an anodyne map followed by a Kan fibration and replace the square by
an ordinary pushout along a monomorphism, using the homotopy invariance of homotopy pushouts. By
the lifting criterion (§4.2, third condition) the monomorphism can be built, up to anodyne maps, by
attaching simplices of dimension `> n`. Such attachments are preserved by pushout, and they yield
`n`-connective maps by §4.2.

*Needs:*
- §4.2.1.
- §3.2.1 (the model structure, for homotopy pushouts).
- ModelCategoryTheory, Part ModelCategories §3.5.1 (HTT, §A.2.4, homotopy pushout squares).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.modelCategoryQuillen.isWeakFactorizationSystem_anodyneExtensions`.

Sections 4.1–4.3 together give the connectivity of maps of simplicial sets:
`n`-connective maps, their closure under composition, their lifting characterization for Kan
fibrations and their stability under homotopy pushout (Kerodon, §§3.5.1–3.5.2).

**Checks.**

- The homotopy pushout of `Δ[0] ← ∂Δ[n] → Δ[0]` is the suspension of `∂Δ[n]`, a model of the
  `n`-sphere. Since `∂Δ[n] → Δ[0]` is `(n − 1)`-connective, the theorem makes `Δ[0] → S^n`
  `(n − 1)`-connective, consistent with `S^n` being `n`-connective.
- Strict pushouts that are not homotopy pushouts are excluded: in the square with corners `∂Δ[1]`,
  `Δ[0]`, `Δ[0]` and the strict pushout `Δ[0]`, the map `∂Δ[1] → Δ[0]` is 0-connective, but this
  square is not a homotopy pushout (the homotopy pushout is the circle `Δ[1]/∂Δ[1]`), so the
  theorem says nothing about it.
- For `n = 0`, stability says that homotopy pushouts preserve surjectivity on `π₀`.

### Examples

- `S^n = Δ[n]/∂Δ[n]` is `n`-connective and not `(n + 1)`-connective.
- A relative cell complex with cells of dimension `> n` only is an `n`-connective monomorphism.

## Layer 5: Truncation, Postnikov towers and Eilenberg–MacLane complexes

### 5.1 Truncated Kan complexes and Postnikov sections

**5.1.1.** Define a Kan complex to be `n`-truncated as in the Conventions (Kerodon, Definition 3.5.7.1 and
Prop. 3.5.7.9); this is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `IsTruncated`. Define Moore's `n`-th Postnikov section
`X(n)` of a simplicial set `X` as the image of `X` in its `n`-coskeleton: two `m`-simplices are
identified when their restrictions to the `n`-skeleton of `Δ[m]` agree (May, *Simplicial objects*,
§8; Goerss–Jardine, §VI.2). These are the `Suggested.lean` declarations `postnikovSection` and `toPostnikovSection`. Prove
for a Kan complex `X` (May, §8; Kerodon, Definition 3.5.7.20):

- `X(n)` is a Kan complex (`Suggested.lean` declaration `kanComplex_postnikovSection`);
- `X(n)` is `n`-truncated (`Suggested.lean` declaration `isTruncated_postnikovSection`);
- `X → X(n)` is bijective on `π m` for `m ≤ n` at every vertex
  (`Suggested.lean` declaration `bijective_π_toPostnikovSection`), so it exhibits `X(n)` as an `n`-truncation of `X`.

For all simplicial sets `X` and `Y`, the sections commute with products: `(X × Y)(n) = X(n) × Y(n)`,
compatibly with the projections (May, §8), since the coskeleton is a right adjoint and the image of a
product map is levelwise the product of the images.

**Checks.**

- `X(n)` agrees with `X` in dimensions `≤ n`, and an `(n + 1)`-simplex of `X(n)` is determined by
  its faces. For `n = 0`, `X(0)` has as `m`-simplices the `(m + 1)`-tuples of vertices of simplices
  of `X`. For Kan `X` each component of `X(0)` is the codiscrete simplicial set on its vertices, so
  `X(0)` is homotopy equivalent to `π₀ X`.
- For a group `G`, the nerve of `G` is 1-truncated, and its first section is the nerve itself,
  since a 2-simplex of the nerve is determined by its edges. A section identifying simplices with
  equal `(n − 1)`-skeleta would collapse it to a point.
- For `X = Δ[0]` every section is `Δ[0]`. For the Kan complex `Ex^∞ ∂Δ[2]`, `X(0)` is
  contractible and `X(1)` has `π 1 = ℤ`.

*Needs:*
- §1.3.1, π-isomorphisms.
- Mathlib `SSet.KanComplex.hornFilling` (for the Kan property of `X(n)`).
- Mathlib `SSet.coskAdj`, `SSet.Subcomplex.range`, `SSet.Subcomplex.toRange`.

### 5.2 The Postnikov tower

**5.2.1.** Prove that, for a Kan complex `X`, the induced maps `X(n + 1) → X(n)` are Kan fibrations whose
fibers have homotopy groups concentrated in degree `n + 1`, equal there to `π (n + 1) X x` (May,
§8; compare Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.8.9).

*Proof route.* Lift a horn of `X(n)` to `X`, fill it there, and use that `X(n + 1)` and `X(n)` agree
up to dimension `n`. The homotopy groups of the fiber follow from the long exact sequence and §5.1.

*Needs:*
- §5.1.1.
- §1.2.1.

### 5.3 Convergence of towers

**5.3.1.** Prove that the map from `X` to the limit of its Moore tower is an isomorphism. More generally, prove that,
for a tower of Kan fibrations `⋯ → X₂ → X₁ → X₀` with each `X_(n+1) → X_n` bijective on `π k` for `k ≤ n`, the limit
`X` has `X → X_n` bijective on `π k` for `k ≤ n` (Goerss–Jardine, §VI.2; compare Kerodon, Definition 3.5.8.1, for
Postnikov towers). This is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `bijective_π_limit_π`. Consequently, if a Kan complex `Y`
maps compatibly to such a tower with each `Y → X_n` bijective on `π k` for `k ≤ n`, then `Y → lim X_n` is a weak
homotopy equivalence (Goerss–Jardine, §VI.2).

*Proof route.* For the Moore tower, an `m`-simplex is determined by its restriction to the
`m`-skeleton, so the stage `m` already sees every `m`-simplex. For a general tower, compare the long
exact sequences of the fibrations `lim X → X_n`. Milnor's `lim¹` terms vanish because the groups
stabilize.

*Needs:*
- §5.2.1.
- §1.2.1; §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem.

Sections 5.1–5.3 together give the Postnikov towers of Kan complexes (May, §8; Kerodon,
§§3.5.7–3.5.8). The fibers of the tower are identified with Eilenberg–MacLane complexes at the end
of §5.5.

**Checks.**

- `X → lim X(n)` is an isomorphism for the Moore tower, not only a weak homotopy equivalence. For a
  discrete `X` every `X(n)` is `X`.
- For `X = K(A, 2)` the tower is constant `Δ[0]` in degrees 0 and 1 and equal to `X` from degree 2.
- For the nerve of `G`, `X(n) = X` for `n ≥ 1` and `X(0) = Δ[0]`.

### 5.4 Eilenberg–MacLane complexes

**5.4.1.** Define, for an abelian group `A` and `n ≥ 0`, the Eilenberg–MacLane complex `K(A, n)` as the
underlying simplicial set of the simplicial `ℤ`-module that Mathlib's Dold–Kan functor
`Γ` assigns to the chain complex `A[n]` with `A` in degree `n`; this is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `eilenbergMacLane`,
over `ModuleCat ℤ` so that Tau Ceti's Dold–Kan homotopy groups apply to it, with base vertex
`eilenbergMacLane.basepoint` (`Suggested.lean` declaration), the zero vertex. For a group `G`, `K(G, 1)` is the nerve of `G`, and
for a set `S`, `K(S, 0)` is the constant simplicial set (Kerodon, Construction 2.5.6.9). `K(A, n)` is
a Kan complex by Tau Ceti's instance for simplicial modules. Tau Ceti identifies the homotopy groups
of `Γ(C)` at the zero vertex with the homology of `C`. What remains is to prove that
`π n K(A, n) = A` at the base vertex and that every other homotopy group is trivial at every vertex
(Kerodon, Exercise 3.2.2.22). These are the `Suggested.lean` declarations `nonempty_mulEquiv_π_eilenbergMacLane` (for `n ≥ 1`,
as groups) and `subsingleton_π_eilenbergMacLane`.

*Proof route.* At the zero vertex, compose Tau Ceti's `ChainComplex.doldKanPiAddEquivHomology`
(degrees `≥ 1`) and `ChainComplex.doldKanPiZeroEquivHomology` (degree 0) with the homology of the
single complex `A[n]`, which is `A` in degree `n` and `0` elsewhere. At another vertex `v`, translation
by `v` in the simplicial group is an automorphism of `K(A, n)` carrying `0` to `v`, so it moves the
computation there.

**Checks.**

- `K(A, n)` has a single `m`-simplex for `m < n`, `A` as its set of `n`-simplices, and `A^(n+1)` as
  its set of `(n + 1)`-simplices, one copy of `A` for each surjection `[n + 1] → [n]`. A model with
  `A^n` there would have the wrong `π (n + 1)`.
- `K(ℤ/2, 1)` is the nerve of `ℤ/2`, with `π 1 = ℤ/2`. The Dold–Kan construction gives the same
  simplicial set as the nerve for `n = 1` and `A` abelian.
- `K(A, 0)` is the discrete simplicial set on `A`, with `π 0 = A`.

*Needs:*
- §1.1.
- Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.DoldKan.Γ`, `HomologicalComplex.single`.
- Tau Ceti `CategoryTheory.SimplicialObject.kanComplex_comp_forget_moduleCat`,
  `ChainComplex.doldKanPiAddEquivHomology`, `ChainComplex.doldKanPiZeroEquivHomology`.

### 5.5 Uniqueness of Eilenberg–MacLane complexes

**5.5.1.** Prove the reduction lemma: for `n ≥ 1`, an `n`-connective Kan complex `X` and a vertex `x`, the simplicial
subset of `X` of simplices all of whose faces of dimension `< n` are degenerate at `x` is an `(n − 1)`-reduced Kan
complex (a single simplex in each dimension `< n`), and its inclusion into `X` is a weak homotopy equivalence (May,
§8, the Eilenberg subcomplex; compare Kerodon, Prop. 3.5.2.9 and Remark 3.5.2.10, which give a weak homotopy
equivalence from `X` to an `(n − 1)`-reduced Kan complex). **Checks.** For the nerve of a group and `n = 1` the subset
is the whole nerve. For the nerve of the groupoid with two uniquely isomorphic objects and `n = 1` it is `Δ[0]` at
either object, while a definition constraining only the faces of dimension `< n − 1` would return the whole nerve,
which is not 0-reduced. For `n = 1` the hypothesis is connectedness: for `X = Δ[0] ⊔ Δ[0]`, which is only
0-connective, the subset at `x` is `{x}`, and `{x} ⊆ X` is not bijective on `π₀`. The route: by connectivity and
homotopy extension, a simplex of `X` whose boundary already lies in the subset is homotopic relative to its boundary
to a simplex of the subset, by induction over the skeleta; applied to horns this gives the Kan property, and applied
to representatives of homotopy classes it gives the bijection on `π_k` for all `k` and base points.

*Needs:* §1.1 (homotopy groups of Kan complexes); §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem; §4.1.1 (connectivity).

**5.5.2.** Prove that a Kan complex is homotopy equivalent to `K(G, n)` exactly when it is `n`-connective and
`n`-truncated, with `G` a nonempty set for `n = 0`, a group for `n = 1` and an abelian group for
`n ≥ 2` (Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.7.19). The connectivity hypothesis includes nonemptiness, and
connectedness for `n ≥ 1`; `K(∅, 0) = ∅` is excluded because it is not 0-connective. The
form for `n ≥ 1` is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `exists_isWeakHomotopyEquivalence_of_mulEquiv`: two such Kan
complexes with isomorphic `π n` are related by a weak homotopy equivalence.

*Proof route.* By `n`-connectivity, replace `X` by a weakly equivalent `(n − 1)`-reduced
model, with a single simplex in each dimension `< n` (the reduction lemma, §5.5.1). Then pass to
its `n`-th Postnikov section (§5.1) and map it to `K(G, n)` by the fundamental class, the
tautological `n`-cocycle.

*Needs:*
- the reduction lemma, §5.5.1; §5.4.1, §5.1.1.
- §4.1.1 (connectivity).
- §1.3.3, Whitehead's theorem.
- Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Abelian.DoldKan.equivalence` (maps into `K(G, n)` as cocycles).

**5.5.3.** Consequently, for a Kan complex X and a vertex x, the fiber of `X(n + 1) → X(n)` over the image of x in the
Postnikov tower of §5.2 is (n + 1)-connective and (n + 1)-truncated, hence an Eilenberg–MacLane complex
`K(π (n + 1) X x, n + 1)` (May, §8; Kerodon, Corollary 3.5.8.9 for the canonical tower).

*Needs:* §5.2.1; §5.5.2; §1.2.1 (the action of `π 1` on `π 0` of the fiber).

### 5.6 Maps between Eilenberg–MacLane complexes

**5.6.1.** Prove that, for `n ≥ 1` and groups `A`, `B`, abelian when `n ≥ 2`, the pointed mapping complex
`Map_*(K(A, n), K(B, n))`, the fiber of `Fun(K(A, n), K(B, n)) → K(B, n)` over the base vertex, is
a Kan complex with `π 0 = Hom(A, B)` and contractible components (May, *Simplicial objects*, §23).

*Proof route.* Pointed maps `X → K(B, n)` correspond to normalized `n`-cocycles on `X` with values in
`B`, by the Dold–Kan adjunction (May, §23). Homotopy classes are cohomology classes, and the higher
homotopy groups of the mapping complex are lower cohomology groups of `K(A, n)` with coefficients in
`B`, which vanish in positive degrees below `n`. For `n = 1`, pointed maps `N(A) → N(B)` are
functors `A → B`, i.e. homomorphisms, and pointed homotopies are natural transformations with
identity component at the base point, hence trivial; so `Map_*` is the discrete set `Hom(A, B)`.

The pointed mapping complex is the `Suggested.lean` declaration `pointedMap`, the fiber of restriction
`Fun(K, Y) → Fun(Δ[0], Y) ≅ Y` along the base vertex, and the abelian case is the
`Suggested.lean` declarations `exists_mapHom_eq_and_homotopyClass_eq` (taking a pointed map to the homomorphism it induces on
`π n` is a bijection from pointed homotopy classes onto homomorphisms, which with §5.4 are
`A →+ B`) and `subsingleton_π_pointedMap` (trivial higher homotopy groups at every vertex).

*Needs:*
- §5.4.1 (`K(A, n)`, its base vertex and its `π n`).
- Mathlib `SSet.fibration_pullbackObjObjπ` (the mapping complex is Kan: restriction to the base
  vertex is a Kan fibration).
- §1.1.1.
- Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Abelian.DoldKan.equivalence`; `SSet.RelativeMorphism.ofSimplex₀` and
  `SSet.RelativeMorphism.homotopyClass` (pointed maps and their pointed homotopy classes).
- Tau Ceti `SSet.KanComplex.π.mapHom` (the homomorphism induced on `π n`), `SSet.fiber` and its
  Kan instance (the pointed mapping complex).

**Checks.**

- `Map_*(K(ℤ, 2), K(ℤ, 2))` has `π 0 = ℤ`, the degree. An unpointed mapping space would have the
  same `π 0` here but a component equal to `K(ℤ, 2)`, not contractible.
- For `n = 1` and `A = B = S₃`, pointed maps up to homotopy are the ten elements of `Hom(S₃, S₃)`,
  and the six automorphisms are six distinct classes. Unpointed homotopy classes identify conjugate
  homomorphisms and would merge the six into one.
- `K(A, n)` for `n ≥ 2` with `A ≠ 0` is not homotopy equivalent to `K(A, n + 1)`, since the two have
  `π n` equal to `A` and `0`.

### Examples

- The Postnikov tower of `Ex^∞ ∂Δ[2]` is constant from degree 1, `X(1) ≃ K(ℤ, 1)`.
- The fiber of `X(2) → X(1)` for a simply connected Kan complex `X` is `K(π 2 X, 2)`.
