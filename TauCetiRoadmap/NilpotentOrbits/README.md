# Roadmap: nilpotent orbits, Springer resolution and subregular geometry

**Summary.** Let `G` be a connected semisimple complex algebraic group, adjoint unless said
otherwise, with Lie algebra `𝔤` and rank `r`. An element `x ∈ 𝔤` is nilpotent when `ad x` is
nilpotent. The nilpotent elements form the nilpotent cone `𝒩`, the zero fibre of the adjoint
quotient `χ : 𝔤 → 𝔤//G`, taken as a scheme. This roadmap builds the geometry of `𝒩` and its
`G`-orbits, the unipotent variety, the Springer resolution and the slice surfaces at subregular
elements.

The first headline theorem is finiteness, with the structure of the regular orbit. There are
finitely many nilpotent orbits. `𝒩` is irreducible of dimension `dim 𝔤 − r`, and the regular
nilpotent orbit is dense in it. If `𝔤` is simple and nonzero, the complement of the regular orbit
is the closure of a single subregular orbit, of codimension two in `𝒩`.

The second is Kostant's theorem with the Springer resolution. The ideal of polynomial functions on
`𝔤` generated by the invariants of positive degree is prime, generated by a regular sequence of
`r` homogeneous invariants, and its quotient ring is integrally closed. So `𝒩` is a normal complete
intersection. The map `T*ℬ → 𝒩` from the cotangent bundle of the flag variety is a resolution of
singularities, and it is an isomorphism over the regular orbit.

The third is the exponential. For `G` connected reductive, the truncated exponential and logarithm
are mutually inverse `G`-equivariant isomorphisms of schemes between `𝒩` and the unipotent
variety of `G`.

The fourth is the theorem of Brieskorn and Slodowy. Let `𝔤` be simple and `e` subregular, with
`S = e + ker(ad f)` the slice of an `sl₂`-triple `(h, e, f)`. Then `S ∩ 𝒩` is a normal surface
whose only singular point is `e`. Its singularity has the ADE type of `𝔤` when `𝔤` is simply laced,
and types `A₂ₙ₋₁`, `Dₙ₊₁`, `E₆`, `D₄` for `Bₙ`, `Cₙ`, `F₄`, `G₂`. The Springer resolution restricts
to its minimal resolution, whose exceptional curves are projective lines of self-intersection
`−2`. Their intersection matrix is the negative of the Cartan matrix of that type.

ReductiveGroups supplies the group `G`, its Lie algebra, Borel subgroups, the adjoint quotient and
the folding of root data with which the last layer compares the exceptional lattices of the
subregular surfaces.
FormalConnectionsOpers supplies the principal `sl₂`-triple, the Kostant slice and the theorem that
the regular nilpotent elements form one orbit, each contained in a unique Borel subalgebra. The
Tau Ceti library already proves much of the linear algebra used here. Downstream,
SpringerResolutionAndCharacteristicCycles carries the nilpotent scheme and the Springer resolution
to other fields of characteristic zero and to split reductive groups, and
McKaySkewGroup compares the slice surfaces with quotient singularities.

Layers 0–2 study elements and orbits: Jordan decomposition, centralizers, `sl₂`-triples, weighted
Dynkin diagrams and the finite orbit set. Layer 3 treats the subregular stratum and Layer 4 type
`A`. Layer 5 proves reducedness of `𝒩` and the exponential isomorphism. Layer 6 builds the slices,
and Layer 7 the Springer resolution and normality. Layers 8 and 9 identify the subregular surfaces
and the action of the centralizer on their exceptional curves.

## How to read the build

The lower floors are Mathlib, the Tau Ceti library and ReductiveGroups: the algebraic group, its
Lie algebra and adjoint representation, orbits, Jordan decomposition, unipotent groups, central
isogenies, Borel subgroups and the adjoint quotient. Layer 0 (internal Jordan decomposition,
centralizers, orbits, regular elements) uses only these floors and the comparison of the adjoint
group with the inner automorphisms of LieHighestWeightPartII §0.2. Layer 1 builds the nilpotent
scheme from the adjoint quotient of ReductiveGroups 11C, compared with FormalConnectionsOpers §4.3's
invariant adapter; it builds the incidence variety and proves irreducibility of `𝒩` without the
later birational resolution or a dense regular orbit. Layer 2 constructs triples, weighted
diagrams and finiteness; only after FormalConnectionsOpers §4.3's finite principal and regular
exports does §2.3 compare them with Layer 0's regular elements. FormalConnectionsOpers builds
those exports from its own lower analytic dictionary, LieGroups Layer 7's compact form and
CharacteristicClasses §5.1. Apart from Layer 0, which is independent of FormalConnectionsOpers
and which that roadmap may use for shared Jordan and centralizer calculations, no output of this
roadmap feeds back into that chain. No
later theorem of FormalConnectionsOpers (affine, oper and quantum-centre constructions), of
LieGroups (exceptional groups, `G₂`, flag manifolds), of CharacteristicClasses (classifying
spaces, transgression) or of the cameral fundamental groups of ReductiveGroups Layer 11 is an
input here.

Layer 3 follows Layer 2, and Layer 4 follows Layer 3, whose subregular orbit it compares with
type `A`. The build then runs §5.0 (reducedness and complete intersection of the zero fibre),
§5.1 (the unipotent scheme), §5.2 (the exponential and its inverse), Layer 6 (slices), Layer 7
(resolution, normality, local rings), Layer 8 (exceptional curves) and Layer 9 (component actions
and the folding comparison). That is the order of the build, not of every dependency: §5.1 needs
only Layer 0, and Layer 6 needs §5.0 but not §§5.1–5.2. Each section's *Needs:* line lists what
its statement and its route use, so that a target proved by another route is not blocked behind
an unrelated section.

## Conventions

1. **The group.** `G` is a smooth connected affine algebraic group over `ℂ`, through the Hopf
   algebra, scheme and points dictionaries of ReductiveGroups Layer 0 (Mathlib's `CommHopfAlgCat`,
   closed subgroups as Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.HopfIdeal`). Smoothness, connectedness,
   affineness, finite type, adjointness and semisimplicity are separate hypotheses, written on the
   statement that uses them; no new class bundling them is introduced. The principal setting is
   `G` adjoint semisimple. The Lie algebra `𝔤 = Lie G` is ReductiveGroups Layer 2's (the counit
   derivations, Tau Ceti's `Module.Dual ℂ (TauCeti.Bialgebra.CotangentSpace ℂ H)` for `G = Spec H`), with
   its bracket, differentials and adjoint representation `Ad`.
2. **Isogenies and reductive groups.** For a central isogeny `π : G̃ → G` of semisimple groups,
   the differential `dπ` is an isomorphism, and the nilpotent cones, orbit sets, orbit closures and
   dimensions correspond under it (§§0.1, 2.4). Centralizers and their component groups are
   compared through explicit maps; they are not asserted to be invariant under isogeny. Layer 5
   also treats connected reductive `G`. There the nilpotent cone is the zero fibre of the adjoint
   quotient, so its central coordinate is zero, and unipotence means unipotence in a faithful
   representation. Two tests are not substitutes: in a reductive Lie algebra `ad x` is nilpotent
   for every central `x`, and in a nonadjoint group `Ad g` is unipotent for every central `g`.
3. **Lie vocabulary.** Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.ad`, `LieDerivation`, `IsNilpotent`,
   `Module.End.IsSemisimple`, `IsSl2Triple` and `LieAlgebra.IsKilling` (nondegenerate Killing
   form); Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.intLieAlgebra K L` (the group of inner automorphisms, generated by
   the `exp (ad y)` with `ad y` nilpotent) and `LieAlgebra.IsCentralizerRegular` (centralizer of
   minimal dimension). A statement about a Lie algebra alone is stated for a finite-dimensional
   complex Lie algebra `L` with nondegenerate Killing form, of which `𝔤` is an instance.
   Statements involving `TauCeti.intLieAlgebra` also carry a `LieAlgebra ℚ L` structure, which is
   unique on such an `L`.
4. **Triples and slices.** A triple is ordered `(h, e, f)` with `[h, e] = 2e`, `[h, f] = −2f` and
   `[e, f] = h`. Mathlib's `IsSl2Triple h e f` requires `h ≠ 0`, so existence statements using it
   assume `e ≠ 0`; the element `0` has the degenerate bracket data `(0, 0, 0)`, treated directly.
   The Slodowy slice of a triple is `S = e + ker(ad f)`, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.slodowySlice ℂ e f`;
   for `e = 0` take `f = 0`, so that `S = 𝔤` (`TauCeti.slodowySlice_zero_right`).
5. **Borel subgroups.** Fix a maximal torus `T` in a Borel subgroup `B`, with Lie algebras
   `𝔥 ⊆ 𝔟`, nilradical `𝔫 = Lie Rᵤ(B)`, and positive roots those occurring in `𝔫`. The Killing
   form identifies `(𝔤/𝔟)*` with `𝔫`, since the Killing-orthogonal of `𝔟` is `𝔫`; this fixes the
   identification `T*(G/B) ≅ G ×ᴮ 𝔫`. Every map below respects these choices and carries
   change-of-Borel and change-of-triple comparisons.
6. **Coordinates.** The coordinate ring of `𝔤` is `O(𝔤) = Sym(𝔤*)`, in Lean
   `SymmetricAlgebra K (Module.Dual K L)`; a point `x` evaluates `p` as
   `SymmetricAlgebra.lift (Module.Dual.eval K L x) p`. The invariant polynomial functions are Tau
   Ceti's `TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.lieInvariants K L (Module.Dual K L)`, and those of positive
   degree are the invariants `p` with `SymmetricAlgebra.algebraMapInv p = 0`. For connected `G`
   they are the `G`-invariants (ReductiveGroups 11C).
7. **Schemes and points.** The nilpotent scheme `𝒩` is the zero fibre of the adjoint quotient
   with its scheme structure (§1.1). For `G` semisimple its complex points are the nilpotent
   elements; for `G` connected reductive they are the nilpotent elements with zero central
   coordinate, that is, the nilpotent elements of `[𝔤, 𝔤]`. An equality of complex points is
   neither an equality of schemes nor a statement about points with values in a nonreduced ring:
   a nilpotent matrix over such a ring can have characteristic coefficients that are not zero
   (§1.1, Checks). Every functor-of-points statement
   carries the defining invariant equations. The unipotent variety `𝒰_G` is a reduced closed
   subscheme of `G` over `ℂ`, defined without a choice of representation (§5.1). Every statement
   over a complex algebra `A` base-changes that fixed scheme; reducing again after base change
   would change the functor.
8. **Rank and dimension.** `r = rank 𝔤` is Mathlib's `LieAlgebra.rank`, the dimension of a Cartan
   subalgebra. Dimensions of affine schemes are Krull dimensions of their coordinate rings. The
   zero Lie algebra is allowed throughout: its cone is a point and its only orbit is `{0}`.
9. **Names and homes.** Declarations of the acceptance file `Suggested.lean` live in
   `TauCetiRoadmap.NilpotentOrbits` and are the only names of this roadmap in code font; other
   targets are named in prose. Implementations go to `TauCeti/Algebra/Lie/Nilpotent/` for the
   linear-algebraic statements and to `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Nilpotent/` for the schemes,
   actions and resolutions.

## Layer 0: internal Jordan decomposition and adjoint orbits

This layer uses no output of FormalConnectionsOpers; its regular elements are compared with the
regular nilpotent exports there in §2.3.

### 0.1 The Lie algebra of G, its Killing form and faithful representations

Compare `𝔤 = Lie G` with a finite-dimensional Lie algebra with nondegenerate Killing form. For `G`
semisimple prove `LieAlgebra.IsKilling ℂ 𝔤` and that the centre of `𝔤` is zero; for `G`
connected reductive prove `𝔤 = 𝔷(𝔤) ⊕ [𝔤, 𝔤]` with `[𝔤, 𝔤]` Killing. For every faithful closed
representation `ρ : G → GL(V)` used below (one exists, ReductiveGroups Layer 1, through Tau
Ceti's `TauCeti.AffineGroupSchemeCat.exists_isClosedImmersion_generalLinear`), prove that
`dρ : 𝔤 → 𝔤𝔩(V)` is an injective Lie algebra homomorphism with
`dρ(Ad(g) x) = ρ(g) dρ(x) ρ(g)⁻¹`. Record the tangent, bracket, adjoint and differential
equations and their compatibility with central isogenies, products and a change of faithful
representation.

Call `x ∈ 𝔤` nilpotent when `ad x` is nilpotent and semisimple when `ad x` is semisimple. For `G`
semisimple these agree with nilpotence and semisimplicity of `dρ(x)` for every faithful `ρ`: Tau
Ceti's `LieHom.isNilpotent_apply_iff_isNilpotent_ad` and
`LieHom.isSemisimple_apply_iff_isSemisimple_ad`, applied to `dρ`. For `G` connected semisimple,
the image of `Ad : G(ℂ) → Aut(𝔤)` is the group `TauCeti.intLieAlgebra ℂ 𝔤` of inner automorphisms
(LieHighestWeightPartII §0.2). Hence the `G`-orbits on `𝔤` are the `Int(𝔤)`-orbits.
*Needs:* ReductiveGroups Layers 1, 2 and 6; LieHighestWeightPartII §0.2; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.AffineGroupSchemeCat.exists_isClosedImmersion_generalLinear`,
`LieHom.isNilpotent_apply_iff_isNilpotent_ad`, `LieHom.isSemisimple_apply_iff_isSemisimple_ad`,
`TauCeti.intLieAlgebra`, `TauCeti.intLieAlgebraLieEquiv`; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.IsKilling`.

**Checks.**
- In `𝔤𝔩₂` with `ρ` the identity, `x = I` has `ad x = 0`, nilpotent, while `ρ(x) = I` is not
  nilpotent: outside the semisimple case "nilpotent through `ad`" and "nilpotent in a faithful
  representation" differ, which is why the reductive case of Layer 5 uses the zero-fibre
  definition.

### 0.2 Internal Jordan decomposition

The internal Jordan decomposition is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.jordanDecomposition`. For a
finite-dimensional Lie algebra `L` with nondegenerate Killing form over an algebraically closed
field, it writes `x = xₙ + xₛ` with `ad xₙ` nilpotent, `ad xₛ` semisimple and `[xₙ, xₛ] = 0`, and
the decomposition is unique (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.eq_jordanDecomposition_iff`). Its proof runs as
follows. The Jordan parts of a Lie derivation `D` are again derivations
(`LieDerivation.exists_isNilpotent_isSemisimple`), because brackets of generalized `λ`- and
`μ`-eigenvectors are generalized `λ + μ`-eigenvectors (`LieDerivation.lie_mem_maxGenEigenspace`)
and the semisimple part acts by `λ` on the generalized `λ`-eigenspace. Apply this to `D = ad x`,
then use innerness of derivations (`LieDerivation.IsKilling.exists_eq_ad`) and injectivity of `ad`
(`LieDerivation.injective_ad_of_center_eq_bot`) to return to `L`. A polynomial expression in `D` is
not presumed to be a derivation merely because it is polynomial in `D`. The parts are preserved by
Lie algebra isomorphisms (`LieEquiv.map_nilpotentPart`, `LieEquiv.map_semisimplePart`) and computed
factorwise on products (`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.jordanDecomposition_prod`). In characteristic zero a
faithful representation carries them to the Jordan parts of the image
(`LieHom.apply_nilpotentPart_eq`, `LieHom.apply_semisimplePart_eq`).

Prove the group forms. For `g ∈ G(ℂ)`, `(Ad(g) x)ₛ = Ad(g)(xₛ)` and `(Ad(g) x)ₙ = Ad(g)(xₙ)`.
For a faithful closed representation `ρ`, `dρ(xₛ)` and `dρ(xₙ)` are the semisimple and nilpotent
parts of `dρ(x)`. For a central isogeny `π`, `dπ` carries Jordan parts to Jordan parts.
*Needs:* §0.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.LieAlgebra.jordanDecomposition`,
`TauCeti.LieAlgebra.eq_jordanDecomposition_iff`, `LieEquiv.map_nilpotentPart`,
`LieEquiv.map_semisimplePart`, `LieHom.apply_nilpotentPart_eq`, `LieHom.apply_semisimplePart_eq`.

**Checks.**
- In `sl₂`, `x = [[1, 1], [0, −1]]` has distinct eigenvalues `±1`, so it is semisimple: `xₛ = x`
  and `xₙ = 0`. Splitting it into its diagonal part `h` and strictly upper part `e` does not give
  the Jordan decomposition, since `[h, e] = 2e ≠ 0`.
- For `D = ad h` on `sl₂`, the polynomial `D²` is not a derivation: `D²[e, f] = D² h = 0`, while
  `[D² e, f] + [e, D² f] = [4e, f] + [e, 4f] = 8h`.
- In `𝔤𝔩ₙ`, whose centre is not zero, `x = I` has the two decompositions `(I, 0)` and `(0, I)`:
  the Killing hypothesis is what makes the internal decomposition unique.

### 0.3 Centralizers and adjoint orbits

For `x ∈ 𝔤`, the centralizer `C_G(x)` is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.centralizerHopfIdeal`, the
stabilizer of `x` under `Ad`. The adjoint orbit `Oₓ` represents the homogeneous quotient
`G/C_G(x)` (`TauCeti.fppfHomogeneousQuotientIsoAdjointOrbitPoints`). It satisfies
`dim Oₓ + dim C_G(x) = dim G`
(`TauCeti.topologicalKrullDim_range_projectiveOrbitMap_add_ringKrullDim_centralizerHopfIdeal`) and,
in characteristic zero, `dim Oₓ = dim [𝔤, x]`
(`TauCeti.topologicalKrullDim_range_projectiveOrbitMap_mk_one_eq_finrank_range_ad`). It is smooth
of relative dimension `dim [𝔤, x]` (`TauCeti.smoothOfRelativeDimension_adjointOrbit`), and it is
locally closed with boundary a union of orbits of smaller dimension (ReductiveGroups Layer 3A).

Prove the rest. In characteristic zero `C_G(x)` is smooth, by the group-scheme dictionary of
ReductiveGroups, and its Lie algebra is `ker(ad x)`; prove the hypotheses under which that
dictionary applies. The tangent space of `Oₓ` at `x` is `[𝔤, x]`, the image of the differential
of the orbit map `g ↦ Ad(g) x`, whose kernel is `Lie C_G(x)`. Supply orbit maps, equivariant
transport `O_{Ad(g)x} = Oₓ`, products of groups, inclusions of closures, and the fact that
conjugacy is an equivalence relation whose classes are the orbits.
*Needs:* §0.1; ReductiveGroups Layers 2, 3 and 3A; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.centralizerHopfIdeal`,
`TauCeti.fppfHomogeneousQuotientIsoAdjointOrbitPoints`,
`TauCeti.topologicalKrullDim_range_projectiveOrbitMap_add_ringKrullDim_centralizerHopfIdeal`,
`TauCeti.topologicalKrullDim_range_projectiveOrbitMap_mk_one_eq_finrank_range_ad`,
`TauCeti.smoothOfRelativeDimension_adjointOrbit`.

**Checks.**
- In `sl₂` with `e = [[0, 1], [0, 0]]`, `ker(ad e) = ℂe` and `[𝔤, e] = span(e, h)`, so the orbit
  of `e` has dimension `2 = 3 − 1`: it is the cone `𝒩` minus its vertex.

### 0.4 Component groups and regular elements

Distinguish the centralizer `C_G(x)` as a group, its Lie algebra, its identity component
`C_G(x)°`, and its finite component group `A(x) = C_G(x)/C_G(x)°` (identity components and
quotients from ReductiveGroups Layer 3). Their actions on the slices, Springer fibres and
exceptional curves attached to `x` are constructed with those objects, in §9.1. Export the inclusion and conjugation maps `A(x) → A(Ad(g) x)` and the maps
induced by a central isogeny; these are maps, and they are not asserted to be isomorphisms.

A regular element is one whose centralizer has minimal dimension, Tau Ceti's
`LieAlgebra.IsCentralizerRegular` on the Lie algebra and
`Module.Dual.isCentralizerRegular_iff_forall_ringKrullDim_le` for the group centralizer. Regular
elements exist (`LieAlgebra.exists_isCentralizerRegular`) and are Zariski dense
(`Module.Basis.vanishingIdeal_setOfPred_isCentralizerRegular_eq_bot`). Prove that the minimal
centralizer dimension is `r`. The comparison with the regular nilpotent orbit of
FormalConnectionsOpers is made in §2.3, not used here.
*Needs:* §0.3; ReductiveGroups Layer 3; Tau Ceti `LieAlgebra.IsCentralizerRegular`,
`LieAlgebra.exists_isCentralizerRegular`,
`Module.Basis.vanishingIdeal_setOfPred_isCentralizerRegular_eq_bot`,
`Module.Dual.isCentralizerRegular_iff_forall_ringKrullDim_le`.

**Checks.**
- For `e = [[0, 1], [0, 0]]` the centralizer in `SL₂` is `{±[[1, t], [0, 1]]}`, with component
  group of order two, while in `PGL₂` it is `{[[1, t], [0, 1]]}`, connected. Component groups
  change under the central isogeny `SL₂ → PGL₂`, which is why only maps are exported.
- In `sl₃`, `diag(1, 1, −2)` has a centralizer of dimension `4 > 2 = r`, so it is not regular
  although it is semisimple and nonzero; `diag(1, 0, −1)` has centralizer the diagonal matrices,
  of dimension `2`, and is regular.

### Examples

In `sl₂` every nonzero element is regular; the nilpotent orbits are `{0}` and `𝒩 ∖ {0}`. In
`sl₃` the Jordan decomposition of `[[a, 1, 0], [0, a, 0], [0, 0, −2a]]` has semisimple part
`diag(a, a, −2a)` and nilpotent part `E₁₂`.

## Layer 1: the nilpotent cone, the adjoint quotient and the incidence variety

### 1.1 The adjoint quotient and the nilpotent scheme

The adjoint quotient `χ : 𝔤 → 𝔤//G = Spec O(𝔤)^G` is ReductiveGroups 11C's. Prove that
`O(𝔤)^G` is `TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.lieInvariants ℂ 𝔤 (Module.Dual ℂ 𝔤)` (Convention 6).
Compare the pullback of `χ` to `𝔥` with the Cartan quotient `𝔥 → 𝔥/W`. Tau Ceti's
`LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso` is stated on `S(𝔤)`, as
`lieInvariants K L L ≃ₐ[K] linearInvariants H`; transport it to `S(𝔤*) = O(𝔤)` through the
Killing form, by LieHighestWeight Layer 7's restriction square, and use
ReflectionInvariantTheory §3 for the `W`-invariants. Compare it also with the finite invariant maps of
FormalConnectionsOpers §4.3.

Define the nilpotent scheme `𝒩 = χ⁻¹(0)` as the closed subscheme of `𝔤` cut out by
`nilpotentConeIdeal`. For a Lie algebra `L` over `K`, this is the ideal of
`SymmetricAlgebra K (Module.Dual K L)` generated by the invariants with
`SymmetricAlgebra.algebraMapInv p = 0`. Retain this ideal, not its radical, together with the
augmentation at the origin.
- `nilpotentConeIdeal` is homogeneous and stable under the coadjoint action, so `𝒩` is closed,
  `G`-stable and stable under scalars.
- For `L` with nondegenerate Killing form over a field of characteristic zero, the common zeros
  of `nilpotentConeIdeal` in `L` are exactly the `x` with `ad x` nilpotent (Tau Ceti
  `TauCeti.isNilpotent_ad_iff_forall_lift_eval_eq_zero`). Over `ℂ` the complex points of `𝒩` are
  therefore the nilpotent elements.
- Under an extension of fields the ideal of the base-changed Lie algebra is generated by the image
  of `nilpotentConeIdeal`, because invariants commute with flat base change. Record the
  base-change equations of `𝒩`.
- For `L = 0` the ideal is zero and `𝒩` is a point.

Reducedness and the complete-intersection structure are §5.0; irreducibility is §1.3.
*Needs:* ReductiveGroups 11C; FormalConnectionsOpers §4.3; LieHighestWeight Layer 7;
ReflectionInvariantTheory §3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.lieInvariants`,
`TauCeti.isNilpotent_ad_iff_forall_lift_eval_eq_zero`,
`LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso`; Mathlib `SymmetricAlgebra`,
`SymmetricAlgebra.algebraMapInv`.

**Checks.**
- In `sl₂` with `x = [[a, b], [c, −a]]` the invariants are generated by `a² + bc`, so `𝒩` is the
  quadric cone `a² + bc = 0`, of dimension `2 = 3 − 1`.
- Over `A = ℂ[ε]/(ε²)`, the matrix `X = [[0, 1], [ε, 0]] ∈ sl₂(A)` is nilpotent (`X² = εI`,
  `X⁴ = 0`), but `det X = −ε ≠ 0`, so `X` is not an `A`-point of `𝒩`. Nilpotence is not the
  functor of points of `𝒩`.
- In `sl₃` the ideal generated by the entries of `X³` has no element of degree two, while
  `tr(X²)` vanishes at every nilpotent matrix. So the scheme `X³ = 0` has the same complex points
  as `𝒩` but is not reduced; the ideal of `𝒩`, generated by `tr(X²)` and `det X`, contains
  `tr(X²)`.

### 1.2 The flag variety and the incidence variety

Construct the flag variety `ℬ = G/B` as the quotient of ReductiveGroups Layer 3 (Tau Ceti's
`CommHopfAlgCat.fppfHomogeneousQuotient`, represented by a projective orbit scheme, locally closed in
a projective space, by `TauCeti.HopfIdeal.exists_fppfHomogeneousQuotient_iso_projectiveOrbitPoints`),
parametrizing the Borel subgroups of `G` and the Borel subalgebras of `𝔤`. Construct its universal
subbundle of Borel subalgebras and its universal nilradical subbundle. The representing scheme is
smooth over `ℂ`, as is every projective orbit scheme of a reduced affine group of finite type over
an algebraically closed field (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Comodule.smooth_projectiveOrbitToSpec`). Prove
that `ℬ` is proper, by ReductiveGroups Layer 7's completeness of `G/B`. Prove the precise point
dictionaries of the quotient and of the subbundles.

Construct the closed incidence scheme `𝒩̃ = {(x, B′) : x ∈ Lie Rᵤ(B′)} ⊆ 𝔤 × ℬ` and its
isomorphism with the associated bundle `G ×ᴮ 𝔫`, which is the total space of the vector bundle
on `ℬ` associated with `𝔫` (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.totalSpaceScheme`). Prove that
`𝒩̃` is smooth and irreducible and that its projection to `𝔤` is proper with image in `𝒩`.
Properness comes from the closed embedding of `𝒩̃` in `𝔤 × ℬ` over the proper `ℬ`. It does not
come from the vector-bundle projection `𝒩̃ → ℬ`, which is not proper. This roadmap owns the
flag-incidence and associated-bundle constructions.
*Needs:* ReductiveGroups Layers 3 and 7; AlgebraicVectorBundles (the total spaces of the supplier
contracts); Tau Ceti `CommHopfAlgCat.fppfHomogeneousQuotient`,
`TauCeti.HopfIdeal.exists_fppfHomogeneousQuotient_iso_projectiveOrbitPoints`,
`TauCeti.Comodule.smooth_projectiveOrbitToSpec`, `TauCeti.HopfIdeal.IsBorel`,

### 5.1 The unipotent scheme and its defining ideal

For a faithful closed representation `ρ : G → GL(V)`, let `J_ρ ⊆ O(G)` be generated by the

points are exactly the unipotent elements (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.HopfAlgebra.IsUnipotentPoint`).

dictionary and the Nullstellensatz (Mathlib `MvPolynomial.vanishingIdeal_zeroLocus_eq_radical`),
after a finite polynomial presentation of `O(G)`; construct the quotient and point-evaluation
adapters for that presentation. So `𝒰_G` is the reduced closed subscheme supported on the
unipotent elements, with its embedding into `G`. Construct conjugation and inversion on it, and
the maps for products of groups, central isogenies and changes of representation, with their
equations on coordinate rings. The reduced-variety convention is the one of BMIY22, §2.1, p. 5

- For a complex algebra `A`, the `A`-points are those of the base change `(𝒰_G)_A`, and their

representations of the first Check below: the two ideals `J_ρ`, their common radical, and the

### 5.2 Polynomial exponential and logarithm, factorization and inverse laws

Choose a positive uniform bound `N` for a faithful representation `ρ`; enlarge a
zero-dimensional representation by a trivial line when a positive bound is needed, keeping the
rank-zero group. Derive the bound on the universal nilpotent element from its characteristic
coefficients. They are invariant functions of positive degree, so they lie in `nilpotentConeIdeal`,
and Cayley–Hamilton (Mathlib `Matrix.aeval_self_charpoly`) gives `dρ(X)^N = 0` in the coordinate
ring of `𝒩`. On `𝒰_G` the characteristic equations lie in `I_U`, and in the same way
`(ρ(g) − I)^N = 0`. The truncated exponential `exp_N(X) = Σ_{k<N} Xᵏ/k!` and logarithm
`log_N(U) = Σ_{1≤k<N} (−1)^(k+1) (U − I)ᵏ/k`, over every rational algebra, are Mathlib's
`IsNilpotent.exp` and Tau Ceti's `IsNilpotent.log` under the vanishing-power hypothesis
(`IsNilpotent.exp_eq_sum`, `IsNilpotent.log_eq_sum`). Their inverse laws are pinned:
`IsNilpotent.log_exp` and `IsNilpotent.exp_log`, with `IsNilpotent.pow_exp_sub_one_eq_zero` and
`IsNilpotent.pow_log_eq_zero` for the bounds. So are naturality under ring maps
(`IsNilpotent.map_exp`, `IsNilpotent.map_log`), unipotence (`IsNilpotent.isNilpotent_exp_sub_one`),
the commuting law `IsNilpotent.exp_add_of_commute` and the divided-power form
`TauCeti.exp_smul_eq_sum_smul_dividedPower`. Prove independence of the bound. Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.baseChangeExp` is used only with its supplied stable lattice, preservation of divided
powers and parameter-ring hypotheses, when comparing with the integral root-subgroup models of
ReductiveGroups; it is not a general constructor of unipotent schemes.

**Complex points.** For a closed subgroup `H ⊆ GLₙ` over a rational domain and a nilpotent
`X ∈ Lie H`, `exp X ∈ H` (`TauCeti.GeneralLinear.nilpotentExpUnit_mem_hopfIdealPointsSubgroup_iff`).
The curve `exp(tX)` lies in `H` over every rational algebra
(`TauCeti.GeneralLinear.expSMulHom_mem_hopfIdealPointsSubgroup`), and exponential and logarithm
are inverse bijections between the nilpotent elements of `Lie H` and the unipotent points of `H`
(`TauCeti.GeneralLinear.expEquivHopfIdealPoints`). Applied to `ρ(G)` this puts `exp_N(dρ(X))` in
`G(ℂ)` as a unipotent element for every `X ∈ 𝒩(ℂ)`. Compare it with the Lie-group exponential.
Let `E_G(X) = exp_{G(ℂ)}(θ_G X)`, the exponential of LieGroups Layer 0 for the complex Lie group
`G(ℂ)`, where `θ_G` identifies the algebraic Lie algebra with the analytic one. Use the lower
dictionary of FormalConnectionsOpers §4.3 on its fixed Hopf-point carrier, with its complex atlas
(§2.4 there), and the realification and global holomorphic exponential of LieGroups Layer 7, under
their finite-dimensional, smooth, Hausdorff and boundaryless hypotheses. Prove that `ρ(ℂ)` is a
holomorphic homomorphism with analytic differential `dρ`. By the naturality of LieGroups Layer 3,
`ρ(ℂ)(E_G(X)) = exp(dρ(X))`, the matrix exponential, and this is `exp_N(dρ(X))` by
`IsNilpotent.normedSpace_exp_eq_exp` and `NormedSpace.exp_eq_tsum_rat`. Keep the point, this
equation, the tangent and naturality squares, the change-of-representation comparisons and the
conjugation equations as outputs. No exponential isomorphism for a noncommutative unipotent
radical is assumed for this pointwise statement.

**The binomial curve.** For a unipotent `u ∈ G(ℂ)` define
`c_u(t) = Σ_{j<N} (t choose j) (u − I)ʲ`. Prove that `c_u(m) = uᵐ` for every natural number `m`,
that `det c_u(t) = 1`, and that `c_u(t + s) = c_u(t) c_u(s)`. The natural numbers are Zariski dense
in `𝔸¹` and `G` is closed in `GL(V)`, so `c_u` is a homomorphism `𝔾_a → G`. Its derivative at `0`
is `log_N(u)`. So `log_N(u) ∈ Lie G`, and by the Jordan and representation dictionary it lies in
the cone with central coordinate zero. Keep the maps and the differential equations, not only the
existence of a one-parameter subgroup.

**Scheme morphisms.** Since `𝒩` is reduced (§5.0) and `I_U` is a radical ideal, the pointwise
statements give pullback containments of ideals for both maps. The exponential sends the ideal of
`𝒰_G` into `nilpotentConeIdeal`, and the logarithm sends `nilpotentConeIdeal` into `I_U`. Prove

**Isogenies, centres and subgroups.** For a central isogeny `π : G̃ → G`, prove that `π` maps

## Layer 6: Slodowy slices and transverse geometry

### 6.1 The slice, its transversality and the contracting action

The linear algebra of the slice is Tau Ceti's. For a triple `(h, e, f)`, the slice

`dim ker(ad f) = dim ker(ad e)` (`IsSl2Triple.finrank_ker_ad_e_eq_finrank_ker_ad_f`). At every
`x ∈ S` the tangent space `[𝔤, x]` of the orbit and `ker(ad f)` span `𝔤`
(`IsSl2Triple.codisjoint_range_ad_ker_ad_f`), and no point of `S` has a larger centralizer than
`e` (`IsSl2Triple.finrank_ker_ad_le_finrank_ker_ad_e`). The contracting action
`IsSl2Triple.contractingAction`, `c ↦ c² · Ad(λ(c⁻¹))` on the level of the Lie algebra, has weight
`2 − j` on `𝔤(j)` (`IsSl2Triple.contractingAction_apply_of_mem`) and preserves `S`
(`IsSl2Triple.contractingAction_apply_mem_slodowySlice`). Its orbit maps on `S` are polynomial with
limit `e` at `0` (`IsSl2Triple.exists_contractingAction_eq`); its weights on `ker(ad f)` are at
least `2`. An invariant of degree `d` scales by `c^(2d)`
(`IsSl2Triple.lift_eval_contractingAction_apply`). The transverse setup is the one of LNS10, §2,
pp. 3–4 (LNS10 lines 99–119).

Prove the geometric statements. `S` is a closed affine subscheme of `𝔤`, with its embedding. The
action map `G × S → 𝔤` is smooth, its differential being surjective at every point of `G × S`
(the codisjointness above with the orbit dictionary of §0.3). Prove the change-of-triple
equivariances. The contracting action on `S` is `t · (e + v) = t² Ad(λ(t⁻¹))(e + v)` for the
cocharacter `λ` of §2.1; it extends to `t = 0` with limit `e`. For `e = 0`, `S = 𝔤`. Construct the
restriction `χ|S` and its zero fibre `S ∩ 𝒩` as schemes. For `e, f` in a Lie algebra `L` over a
field `K`, the coordinate ring of `S ∩ 𝒩` is `sliceConeRing`: the polynomial functions on
`ker(ad f)` modulo the ideal generated by the image of `nilpotentConeIdeal` under `restrictAffine`,
the algebra map, defined over every commutative ring, that sends a polynomial function `p` on `L` to
`v ↦ p(e + v)`. This is the scheme-theoretic intersection, not its reduction. The point `e` is the
ideal `basePointIdeal`, the image of the functions vanishing at `v = 0`. From the graded maps and
the quotient, prove that `χ|S` is flat and that `S ∩ 𝒩` has dimension `dim ker(ad e) − r`.

`LinearMap.dualMap`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.slodowySlice`, `TauCeti.direction_slodowySlice`,

`IsSl2Triple.finrank_ker_ad_e_eq_finrank_ker_ad_f`, `IsSl2Triple.codisjoint_range_ad_ker_ad_f`,
`IsSl2Triple.finrank_ker_ad_le_finrank_ker_ad_e`, `IsSl2Triple.contractingAction`,

- `x² + y²z + z^(m−1) = 0` for `Dₘ` (`m ≥ 4`);
- `x² + y³ + z⁴ = 0` for `E₆`, `x² + y³ + yz³ = 0` for `E₇` and `x² + y³ + z⁵ = 0` for `E₈`.

`subregularEquation` attaches to each Dynkin type these equations, and to `Bₙ`, `Cₙ`, `F₄`, `G₂`
those of their surface types (§8.3). For a simple complex Lie algebra whose root system, for a
Cartan subalgebra and a base, has the Cartan matrix of the Dynkin type `T` (`TauCeti.HasCartanType`),
with `T` in the rank ranges of `TauCeti.DynkinType.Valid`, and for a triple with `e` subregular as in
§6.2, `nonempty_algEquiv_adicCompletion_sliceConeRing` states that the completion of
`sliceConeRing` at `basePointIdeal` is isomorphic as a `ℂ`-algebra to `ℂ⟦x, y, z⟧` modulo
`subregularEquation T` (LNS10, introduction, pp. 2–3, LNS10 lines 51–54 and 78–81). The valid
ranges matter: `C₁` has the Cartan matrix of `A₁`, but the `Cₙ` equation at `n = 1`,
`x² + y²z + z`, is smooth at the origin.

The isomorphisms are of complete local `ℂ`-algebras at `e` and at the origin, respecting the
maximal ideals, with continuous inverses. Lift them to isomorphisms of the completed resolved
neighbourhoods over these rings. Compare their special fibres and first infinitesimal
neighbourhoods, so as to transfer the exceptional curves and the degrees of their normal bundles
to the algebraic restricted resolution of §7.3. Construct the completion and comparison lemmas
here. A germ calculation does not give a global polynomial isomorphism. Treat `D₃` as `A₃`
through an explicit reindexing.
*Needs:* §§6.2, 7.3; Mathlib `AdicCompletion`, `MvPowerSeries`,
`MvPolynomial.coeToMvPowerSeries.ringHom`, `LieAlgebra.IsKilling.rootSystem`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.DynkinType`, `TauCeti.DynkinType.Valid`, `TauCeti.HasCartanType`.

**Checks.**
- The `A₂` surface of §6.2 is `xy + z³ = 0` exactly, after `x = c`, `y = d`, `z = −2a`.
- The `D₃` equation `x² + y²z + z² = 0` becomes `x² + u² − y⁴/4 = 0` with `u = z + y²/2`, an `A₃`
  equation. A treatment that used the star-shaped `D` pattern at `m = 3` would get the wrong
  diagram.

### 8.2 Explicit resolutions and the exceptional lattice

Resolve these surfaces by explicit charts and proper maps, and identify the result with the
restricted Springer resolution. Construct all exceptional irreducible curves. Show that each is
isomorphic to `ℙ¹`, that they meet transversally with the incidence of the Dynkin diagram, and
that each has self-intersection `−2` (Yun16, §1.3.8, pp. 7–8, Yun16 lines 275–286, and lines
297–300 for simply laced `𝔤`; Slo80, §6, for the other types).
Compute the full intersection pairing on the free abelian group they generate. Prove that the
negative of the intersection matrix is the Cartan matrix of the type, through an explicit
bijection of components with nodes. Mathlib's `CartanMatrix.A`,
`CartanMatrix.D` and `CartanMatrix.E` give these matrices, and `CartanMatrix.E_det` gives
`det E_n = 9 − n`. Include the rank and determinant. Construct the root and coroot pairing of the
lattice and its change-of-label maps. The divisor, normal-bundle degree and local
intersection-multiplicity lemmas needed for these surfaces are part of this section, with their
full basic API.
*Needs:* §8.1; Mathlib `CartanMatrix.A`, `CartanMatrix.D`, `CartanMatrix.E`,
`CartanMatrix.E_det`; RootSystems (Cartan matrices of bases).

**Checks.**
- For `A₁` there is one exceptional curve, with intersection matrix `(−2)`, the negative of the
  Cartan matrix `(2)`.
- The determinants of the Cartan matrices are `m + 1` for `Aₘ`, `4` for `Dₘ`, and `3`, `2`, `1`
  for `E₆`, `E₇`, `E₈`. The intersection matrix of `Aₘ` has determinant `(−1)ᵐ(m + 1)`: the sign
  convention matters.

### 8.3 Nonsimply-laced Lie types

For simply laced `𝔤` the exceptional configuration has the type of `𝔤`. For the other types the
subregular surface types are `Bₙ → A₂ₙ₋₁`, `Cₙ → Dₙ₊₁`, `F₄ → E₆` and `G₂ → D₄`. Prove these with
the explicit small-rank identifications and their rank restrictions; for the completed local
rings these are the cases `Bₙ`, `Cₙ`, `F₄`, `G₂` of `nonempty_algEquiv_adicCompletion_sliceConeRing`.
These are types of surfaces, not the output of folding a Lie algebra by a diagram automorphism,
which is compared with them in Layer 9. The dictionary is in LNS10, introduction, p. 3 (LNS10 lines 78–81), and Yun16, §1.3.8, p. 7 (Yun16
lines 279–286).
*Needs:* §§8.1–8.2; RootSystems.

**Checks.**
- `B₂ = C₂` gives `A₃` from `B₂` and `D₃` from `C₂`, and `D₃ = A₃`: the two formulas agree.
- `C₁ = A₁` is excluded from `Cₙ → Dₙ₊₁`: it would give `D₂`, the disconnected diagram
  `A₁ × A₁`, while the subregular surface of `sl₂` is `A₁`.

### Examples

For `𝔤` of type `E₈` the subregular surface is `x² + y³ + z⁵ = 0`, resolved by eight `(−2)`-curves
with unimodular intersection matrix.

## Layer 9: component actions and the oriented folding comparison

### 9.1 Centralizer and component-group actions

Construct the action of the triple centralizer `C_G(e, h, f)` on `S`, on its nilpotent fibre and on
the restricted resolution. Independently, construct the action of the full `C_G(e)` on the
Springer fibre. Prove that `C_G(e)` is the semidirect product of `C_G(e, h, f)` with a unipotent
normal subgroup, in characteristic zero (BMIY22, §3.1, Proposition 3.3, pp. 9–10, BMIY22 lines
456–462).
Deduce the isomorphism of their component groups. Compare the two actions on the exceptional
components. Prove that the identity component acts trivially on
the finite set of components, and construct the effective finite diagram action, with its kernel,
orbit maps and central-isogeny and change-of-triple comparisons.
*Needs:* §§0.4, 2.1, 6.2, 7.1, 7.3, 8.2; ReductiveGroups Layer 6A.

**Checks.**
- For `sl₂` and `e = 0`, `C_G(0, 0, 0) = G` acts on the single exceptional curve `ℙ¹`, and its
  effective action on the one-element set of components is trivial.

### 9.2 The effective diagram action

Identify the full effective action, rather than a generator chosen from a picture. For `G₂` the
subregular surface has type `D₄`. Distinguish the full action of `S₃` on its components from the
action of a subgroup of order three generated by triality, and construct both with their
permutations. The same curve of type `D₄` occurs for `C₃`, with an action of `S₂`, and for `D₄`
itself, with trivial effective action. Every comparison keeps the input Lie type and its action.
The component groups `S₂` and `S₃` and the coincidence of the `G₂`, `C₃` and `D₄` curves are in
GHR06, §2.2, Table 1 and Example 2.1, pp. 4–5 (GHR06 lines 149–187); the action and its comparison
maps are targets here.
*Needs:* §9.1; RootSystems §5.2 (oriented diagram automorphisms).

**Checks.**
- The folded Cartan matrix cannot tell `S₃` from its subgroup of order three on `D₄`: both have
  the same orbits, the central node and the three outer nodes. This is why the action itself, not
  only its orbits, is recorded.

### 9.3 Invariant lattices and the folding comparison

From the exceptional intersection lattice of §8.2 construct the invariant and coinvariant
lattices, the index maps, the length factors and the root and coroot pairing. Compare them with
the pinned fixed-Lie construction of ReductiveGroups 9A.1b, respecting the group action and the
oriented Cartan entries. The fixed-Lie folding of `A₂ₙ₋₁` gives `Cₙ`, whereas the subregular
input above was `Bₙ`. The folding of `Dₙ₊₁` gives `Bₙ`, whereas that input was `Cₙ`. Prove the
dual comparison, rather than identifying these root data. `F₄` and `G₂` also need the explicit
transport of long and short coroots, although their type names are self-dual. Export the full
diagram, lattice and Lie comparison squares. Tau Ceti's
`Matrix.foldedCartanMatrix` and `Matrix.ShellLieAlgebra.isSerreSystem_folded` give the folded
Cartan matrices. No geometric McKay equivalence, universal deformation theorem, perverse sheaf or
Springer representation is a premise of this section.
*Needs:* §§8.2–8.3, 9.2; ReductiveGroups 9A.1b; RootSystems §5.2; Tau Ceti
`Matrix.foldedCartanMatrix`, `Matrix.ShellLieAlgebra.isSerreSystem_folded`.

**Checks.**
- For `B₃` the subregular surface is `A₅` with an action of `S₂`, and the fixed-Lie folding of `A₅`
  is `C₃`, not `B₃`. A comparison identifying the root data without dualizing would attach `C₃` to
  `B₃`.

### Examples

For `G₂` the input of the comparison is the surface of type `D₄` with its `S₃`-action, and for
`F₄` the surface of type `E₆` with its `S₂`-action. In both cases the comparison of §9.3
transports long and short coroots explicitly. The orbits are `{central node, outer nodes}` on
`D₄` and four orbits, two fixed nodes and two swapped pairs, on `E₆`.

orbits (Layer 0); the nilpotent scheme, the flag variety with its universal subbundles, the
