# Roadmap: revealed preference

**Summary.** Revealed preference asks what observed choices say about the preferences behind
them. An observer sees, for a family of budgets, which alternatives were chosen, and asks whether
some preference (a total and transitive ranking) chooses exactly so, each choice set being the set
of best alternatives of its budget. This roadmap proves the three classical answers: one for
arbitrary choice data, one for finitely many purchases at given prices, and one for a demand
function of prices and income.

The headline theorems are these.

- *Richter's theorem.* A choice that picks a nonempty subset of every budget is the set of best
  elements under some total preorder if and only if it satisfies the congruence axiom: whenever
  `y` is chosen from a budget containing `x`, and `x` is revealed at least as good as `y` through
  a chain of observed choices, `x` is chosen from that budget too. No structure is assumed on the
  family of budgets. When every choice set is a single point, the congruence axiom is equivalent
  to the strong axiom of revealed preference, and this is Houthakker's theorem in Richter's form.
- *Afriat's theorem.* For finitely many observations of positive prices `pᵗ` and nonnegative
  bundles `xᵗ` of at least one good, four conditions are equivalent: some locally nonsatiated
  utility on the orthant is maximized by each `xᵗ` over the bundles it could afford at `pᵗ`; the
  data satisfy the generalized axiom of revealed preference (GARP); the Afriat inequalities
  `uₜ ≤ uₛ + λₛ pˢ·(xᵗ − xˢ)` have a solution with every `λₛ > 0`; and some continuous, concave,
  strictly increasing utility rationalizes the data.
- *Samuelson's theorem.* If a demand function on positive prices and positive incomes exhausts
  every budget and satisfies the weak axiom of revealed preference, then at every such
  price–income pair its Slutsky matrix `S` has `wᵀ S w ≤ 0` for every vector `w`, and `S p = 0` and
  `pᵀ S = 0`. Nothing is asserted about the symmetry of `S`.

The preferences are the total preorders of UtilityRepresentationTheorems, taken as plain
relations. The budget sets, local nonsatiation, Marshallian demand and the Slutsky matrix are
those of ConsumerTheoryDuality, whose theorem that the Slutsky matrix of a single-valued
demand generated by a continuous utility is symmetric and negative semidefinite is the duality
counterpart of Samuelson's theorem. Mathlib supplies transitive closures of relations and
Szpilrajn's extension of a partial order to a linear order, which is the engine of Richter's
theorem.

Layer 1 below treats choice structures, the revealed preference relations, Richter's theorem and
Houthakker's theorem. Layer 2 treats finite price–quantity data and Afriat's theorem. Layer 3
treats the weak axiom for demand functions and Samuelson's theorem. The three layers do not
depend on one another.

## Conventions

- **Choice structures.** Alternatives form an arbitrary type `α`. A choice structure is a family
  of budgets `B : ι → Set α` indexed by an arbitrary type `ι`, with a choice `c : ι → Set α`. The
  index lets one budget appear several times with different choices, as repeated observations
  can; the family need not contain all finite sets, nor be closed under unions or intersections,
  and the empty family is allowed. A choice is decisive when every `c i` is nonempty and univalent
  when every `c i` is a singleton. The standing hypothesis `c i ⊆ B i` is stated theorem by
  theorem.
- **Preferences.** A preference on `α` is a relation `R : α → α → Prop` read "`R x y`: `x` is at
  least as good as `y`", that is total (Lean's `Std.Total R`) and transitive (Mathlib's
  `IsTrans α R`): a total preorder, unbundled, as in the conventions of
  UtilityRepresentationTheorems. Indirect revelation is Mathlib's transitive closure
  `Relation.TransGen`.
- **Commodities, prices and data.** There are `ℓ` goods, bundles and prices are vectors
  `Fin ℓ → ℝ` with the pointwise order, and the cost of `x` at `p` is `p ⬝ᵥ x`. "Positive prices"
  means `∀ i, 0 < p i`, written `p ≫ 0`. Finite data are families `p x : ι → Fin ℓ → ℝ` over a
  finite index type `ι`, which may be empty; prices and bundles may repeat across observations.
- **Demand functions.** A demand function is `x : (Fin ℓ → ℝ) → ℝ → Fin ℓ → ℝ`, of which only
  the values at `p ≫ 0` and `m > 0` enter the axioms; the Slutsky matrix uses Mathlib's `fderiv`
  of `Function.uncurry x`, which is `0` where `x` is not differentiable, so that the matrix is `0`
  there.
- **Copied definitions.** `budgetSet`, `LocallyNonsatiated`, `marshallianDemand` and
  `slutskyMatrix` are ConsumerTheoryDuality's (its 1.1, 1.2 and 5.1), copied verbatim into
  `Suggested.lean`. In particular the budget set contains only nonnegative bundles, and local
  nonsatiation asks for an improving nonnegative bundle within sup-norm distance `ε`.

## Layer 1: choice structures and Richter's theorem

### 1.1 Revealed preference relations and the axioms

Define, for a choice structure `(B, c)`:

- `revealedPreferred B c x y`: some `i` has `x ∈ c i` and `y ∈ B i` (Border, *Preference and
  Rational Choice*, Definition 2, p. 6, the relation `V`);
- `strictlyRevealedPreferred B c x y`: some `i` has `x ∈ c i`, `y ∈ B i` and `y ∉ c i` (Border,
  p. 7, the relation `S` defined after Definition 2);
- `RationalizedBy B c R`: every `c i` equals the set of `x ∈ B i` with `R x y` for all `y ∈ B i`
  (Border, Definition 3, p. 7);
- `SatisfiesCongruence B c`: for every `i`, every `x ∈ B i` and every `y ∈ c i`, if `x` is related
  to `y` by the transitive closure of `revealedPreferred B c`, then `x ∈ c i` (Border, the
  Congruence Axiom 18, p. 11, with the transitive closure `W` of Definition 15, p. 10);
- `SatisfiesSARP B c`: the transitive closure of `strictlyRevealedPreferred B c` is asymmetric
  (Border, the Strong Axiom 32, p. 15).

If `R` rationalizes `c`, then `revealedPreferred B c x y` gives `R x y`: `x` is a best element of
a budget containing `y` (Border, Lemma 6, p. 8). With transitivity, so does the transitive
closure.

**Checks.**
- Three alternatives `x`, `y`, `z` and the three budgets `{x, y}`, `{y, z}`, `{x, z}` with the
  choices `{x}`, `{y}`, `{z}`: `x` is revealed preferred to `y` and `y` to `z`, so `x` is
  indirectly revealed preferred to `z`, which is chosen from `{x, z}` while `x` is not; the
  congruence axiom fails. The weak axiom (no `x` revealed preferred to `y` while `y` is strictly
  revealed preferred to `x`; Border, Definition 21, p. 12) holds: each pair of alternatives lies
  in one budget only, from which one of the two is chosen. So when the family of budgets is not
  closed under unions, the weak axiom does not imply congruence. The relation "`x` over `y`
  over `z` over `x`", made reflexive, rationalizes this choice and is total but not transitive.
- A budget listed twice, `B₁ = B₂ = {a, b}`, with `c₁ = {a}` and `c₂ = {b}`: `b` is revealed
  preferred to `a` through the second copy, `b ∈ B₁` and `b ∉ c₁`, so congruence fails, as it
  must, since no relation makes both choices the sets of best elements of one set.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Relation.TransGen`. (Proved here.)

### 1.2 Richter's theorem

Prove `exists_totalPreorder_rationalizedBy_iff_congruence`: if `c i ⊆ B i` and `c i` is nonempty
for every `i`, then some total preorder rationalizes `c` if and only if `c` satisfies the
congruence axiom (Border, *Preference and Rational Choice*, Theorem 17, p. 10, for the W-axiom,
and Proposition 19, p. 11, for its equivalence with the congruence axiom for decisive choices;
both are stated there without proof and attributed to Richter, for a family of budget sets; the
form here, for an indexed family in which a budget may repeat with different choices, and its
proof are given here).

Route. Necessity: if `R` rationalizes `c`, the transitive closure `W` of `revealedPreferred` lies
in `R` (1.1); for `y ∈ c i`, `x ∈ B i` and `W x y`, every `z ∈ B i` has `R y z`, hence `R x z`,
so `x` is a best element of `B i` and lies in `c i`. Decisiveness is not used here. Sufficiency:
the reflexive closure of `W` is a preorder, whose strict part is that of `W`. Pass to its quotient
by mutual relatedness, Mathlib's `Antisymmetrization`, which is a partial order; extend it to a
linear order by Mathlib's `extend_partialOrder` (Szpilrajn's theorem); and pull the linear order
back to a total preorder `R` on `α`. Then `W ⊆ R`, and `W y x ∧ ¬W x y` gives `¬R x y`, since the
classes of `y` and `x` are distinct and the linear order is antisymmetric (Border, Szpilrajn's
Theorem 54, p. 26, proves this extension for preorders directly by Zorn's lemma). Every `x ∈ c i`
is `R`-best in `B i`, since `x` is revealed preferred to every element of `B i`. Conversely, let
`x ∈ B i` be `R`-best in `B i` and pick `y ∈ c i`, by decisiveness. Then `W y x`; if `¬W x y`,
then `¬R x y`, against the choice of `x`; so `W x y`, and congruence gives `x ∈ c i`.

**Checks.**
- Decisiveness is needed: with one budget `{a, b}` and `c = ∅`, the congruence axiom holds
  vacuously, while every total preorder on a finite nonempty set has a best element there.
  Decisiveness is not needed for rationality itself: on `ℕ` with the single budget `ℕ` and
  `c = ∅`, the order `≥` rationalizes, having no greatest element.
- `c i ⊆ B i` is needed: with the single budget `{a}` and `c = {a, b}`, congruence holds (the only
  element of the budget is chosen) and no relation rationalizes, since best elements lie in the
  budget.
- The 3-cycle of 1.1 fails congruence and has no rationalizing total preorder.
- An exhaustive search over the alternatives `{1, 2, 3, 4}` and every multiset of at most four
  pairs `(B, c)` with `c` nonempty and `c ⊆ B` (864,501 choice structures, repeated budgets with
  different choices included) finds that a rationalizing total preorder exists exactly when
  congruence holds.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `Relation.TransGen`, `Antisymmetrization`, `toAntisymmetrization`,
`extend_partialOrder`, `Std.Total`, `IsTrans`.

### 1.3 Houthakker's theorem in Richter's form

Prove `exists_totalPreorder_rationalizedBy_iff_sarp`: if `c i ⊆ B i` and every `c i` is a
singleton, then some total preorder rationalizes `c` if and only if `c` satisfies the strong axiom
of revealed preference (Border, *Preference and Rational Choice*, Proposition 33, p. 15, that for
univalent choices the W-axiom is equivalent to the strong axiom, attributed to Richter and stated
without proof, with Theorem 17, p. 10; Varian, *Revealed Preference*, Definition 3, p. 3, for
Houthakker's axiom on competitive budgets; the form for indexed families and the proof are given
here).

Route: by 1.2 it suffices to show that congruence and the strong axiom are equivalent for a
univalent choice, `c i = {aᵢ}`. A step `u` revealed preferred to `v` is then either `u = v` or a
strict revelation, since `u = aᵢ` and `v ∈ B i`. If the strong axiom holds, `y = aᵢ`, `x ∈ B i`,
`x ≠ y` and `x` is indirectly revealed preferred to `y`, then dropping the trivial steps gives a
chain of strict revelations from `x` to `y`, while `y` is strictly revealed preferred to `x`
through `B i`; this is a strict cycle, against the strong axiom, so `x = y ∈ c i`. Conversely, a
strict cycle through `x` begins with `x = aᵢ`, `u ∈ B i`, `u ≠ x`, and the rest of the cycle
reveals `u` preferred to `x`, so congruence gives `u ∈ c i = {x}`, a contradiction.

**Checks.**
- The weak axiom is not enough. For three goods take the observations `p¹ = (1, 1, 2)`,
  `x¹ = (0, 2, 1)`, `p² = (2, 1, 1)`, `x² = (1, 0, 2)`, `p³ = (1, 2, 1)`, `x³ = (2, 1, 0)`, with
  the budgets `Bᵗ = budgetSet pᵗ (pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ)` and `c(Bᵗ) = {xᵗ}`. The costs `pˢ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ` are
  `4, 5, 3` for `s = 1`, `3, 4, 5` for `s = 2` and `5, 3, 4` for `s = 3`. Each `xˢ` costs `4` at
  its own prices, so `x¹` is strictly revealed preferred to `x³`, `x³` to `x²` and `x²` to `x¹`:
  the strong axiom fails. For each pair only one of the two bundles is affordable at the other's
  prices (the other costs `5`), so the weak axiom holds.
- Univalence is needed: with two copies of the budget `{a, b}` and the choices `{a, b}` and `{a}`,
  the only strict revelation is `a` over `b`, so the strong axiom holds; but `b` is revealed
  preferred to `a` through the first copy and is not chosen from the second, so congruence fails
  and, by 1.2, no total preorder rationalizes.
- An exhaustive search over the alternatives `{1, 2, 3, 4}` and every multiset of at most five
  pairs `(B, {a})` with `a ∈ B` (435,897 univalent choice structures) finds that a rationalizing
  total preorder exists exactly when the strong axiom holds.

*Needs:* 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `Relation.TransGen`.

### Examples

The 3-cycle on three alternatives; a repeated budget with two different choices; the cyclic
three-good data of 1.3; competitive budgets `budgetSet p m` with a demand function, which form a
univalent choice structure indexed by the pairs `(p, m)` with `p ≫ 0` and `m > 0`.

### Dependencies

Mathlib `Relation.TransGen`, `Antisymmetrization`, `toAntisymmetrization`, `extend_partialOrder`,
`Std.Total`, `IsTrans`. No other layer.

## Layer 2: finite data and Afriat's theorem

### 2.1 Price–quantity data

Copy `budgetSet`, `LocallyNonsatiated` and `marshallianDemand` from ConsumerTheoryDuality 1.1 and
1.2. For finite data `p x : ι → Fin ℓ → ℝ` define:

- `directlyRevealedPreferred p x s t`: `pˢ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ ≤ pˢ ⬝ᵥ xˢ`, that is, `xᵗ` was affordable when
  `xˢ` was bought (Varian, *Revealed Preference*, Definition 1, p. 2);
- `SatisfiesGARP p x`: if `s` is related to `t` by the transitive closure of
  `directlyRevealedPreferred p x`, then `pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ ≤ pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xˢ` (Varian, Definition 4, p. 7;
  Fostel, Scarf and Todd, Definition 1, p. 2, in the equivalent form that a cycle of weak
  revelations consists of equalities);
- `SatisfiesAfriatInequalities p x φ lam`: every `lam t > 0`, and
  `φ t ≤ φ s + lam s * (pˢ ⬝ᵥ (xᵗ − xˢ))` for all `s`, `t` (Fostel, Scarf and Todd, the Afriat
  inequalities, p. 3; Varian, Theorem 1, condition 3, p. 7);
- `Rationalizes u p x`: for every `t`, `xᵗ` lies in `marshallianDemand u pᵗ (pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ)`, so it is
  nonnegative and maximizes `u` over the nonnegative bundles costing at most `pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ` (Fostel,
  Scarf and Todd, Section 1, p. 1).

**Checks.**
- GARP compares a weak chain with a strict reversal. With repeated prices
  `p¹ = p² = (1, 1)` and `x¹ = (1, 0)`, `x² = (0, 1)`, each bundle is affordable when the other is
  bought, at equal cost, and GARP holds; the utility `y₁ + y₂` rationalizes, and `φ = (0, 0)`,
  `lam = (1, 1)` solve the Afriat inequalities. The strong axiom, which asks that a bundle
  revealed preferred to a distinct bundle cost strictly more than that bundle at that bundle's
  prices, fails here.
- A GARP violation: `p¹ = (1, 2)`, `x¹ = (3, 0)`, `p² = (1, 4)`, `x² = (0, 1)`. At `p¹` the bundles
  cost `3` and `2`, at `p²` they cost `3` and `4`, so each bundle was strictly cheaper than the
  other at the other's prices.
- The cyclic three-good data of 1.3 violate GARP through the cycle `1, 3, 2, 1` of strict
  revelations, while every pair of observations satisfies the weak axiom.
- A single observation always satisfies GARP: the only chains go from it to itself.

*Needs:* ConsumerTheoryDuality 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `Relation.TransGen`, `dotProduct`. (Proved
here.)

### 2.2 Afriat's theorem

Prove `afriat_tfae`: for a finite index type `ι`, at least one good, prices with positive
coordinates and nonnegative bundles, the following are equivalent (Mathlib's `List.TFAE`):

1. some `u` with `LocallyNonsatiated u` rationalizes the data;
2. `SatisfiesGARP p x`;
3. the Afriat inequalities have a solution `φ`, `lam`;
4. some `u` that is continuous, concave on all of `Fin ℓ → ℝ` and strictly increasing
   (`StrictMono u` for the pointwise order) rationalizes the data.

(Fostel, Scarf and Todd, Theorem 1, p. 3, that GARP gives a continuous, concave, strictly
monotone and piecewise linear utility generating the data, with the necessity of GARP on p. 2;
their technical report version states the four conditions as Theorem 1 (a)–(d), p. 3; Varian,
Theorem 1, p. 7, stated without proof.)

Route, as in Fostel, Scarf and Todd. (1 ⇒ 2), p. 2: if `xᵗ` is affordable at `pˢ` when `xˢ` is
bought, `u xˢ ≥ u xᵗ`; if it is strictly cheaper, a nonnegative bundle near `xᵗ` is still
affordable and better than `xᵗ`, so `u xˢ > u xᵗ`; along a chain from `s` to `t` this gives
`u xˢ ≥ u xᵗ`, and `pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xˢ < pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ` would give `u xᵗ > u xˢ`. (2 ⇒ 3), Sections 2
and 3, pp. 4–6: induction on the number of observations through the matrix `aₛₜ = pˢ ⬝ᵥ (xᵗ − xˢ)`.
GARP yields a row with no negative entry (Claim 1, p. 4); remove that observation `n`, replace
`aᵢⱼ` by `min(aᵢⱼ, aᵢₙ)` wherever `aₙⱼ = 0`, check that the smaller matrix again has no cycle of
nonpositive entries with a negative one (Claim 2, p. 5), solve it by induction, and set
`φₙ = minᵢ (φᵢ + λᵢ aᵢₙ)` and `λₙ = max(1, max (φⱼ − φₙ)/aₙⱼ)` over the `j` with `aₙⱼ > 0`
(p. 6). The induction uses only the matrix `a` with zero diagonal, not the prices. (3 ⇒ 4),
p. 3: for nonempty `ι` take `u y = minₜ (φ t + lam t * (pᵗ ⬝ᵥ (y − xᵗ)))`, a minimum of
finitely many affine functions, so continuous, concave and piecewise linear, and strictly
increasing because every `lam t * pᵗ` has positive coordinates; the inequalities give
`u xᵗ = φ t`, and `pᵗ ⬝ᵥ y ≤ pᵗ ⬝ᵥ xᵗ` gives `u y ≤ φ t`. For empty `ι` take `u y = ∑ i, y i`.
(4 ⇒ 1): a strictly increasing `u` is locally nonsatiated on the orthant, since
`y + (ε/2) • 1` is nonnegative, within distance `ε` and strictly larger; this uses `ℓ ≥ 1`.

**Checks.**
- The utility in condition 1 must be locally nonsatiated: a constant utility rationalizes every
  data set, including the GARP violation of 2.1.
- The multipliers must be positive: with `lam = 0` and `φ` constant the inequalities hold for every
  data set, including that violation. For it no solution with positive `lam` exists: there
  `a₁₂ = a₂₁ = −1`, and adding `φ 2 ≤ φ 1 − lam 1` to `φ 1 ≤ φ 2 − lam 2` gives
  `lam 1 + lam 2 ≤ 0`.
- Positive prices are needed for 2 ⇒ 1: one observation with `p¹ = 0` and `x¹ = (1, 1)`
  satisfies GARP and the Afriat inequalities (`φ = 0`, `lam = 1`), but a rationalizing utility
  would have its maximum over the whole orthant at `x¹`, which local nonsatiation forbids.
- Positive prices are needed for 1 ⇒ 4: one observation with `p¹ = (1, 0)` and `x¹ = (1, 0)` is
  rationalized by the locally nonsatiated `y₁`, but a strictly increasing `u` would have
  `u (1, 1) > u (1, 0)` with `(1, 1)` affordable.
- Nonnegative bundles are needed: one observation with `ℓ = 1`, `p¹ = 1` and `x¹ = −1` satisfies
  GARP, and no utility rationalizes it, since `−1` lies in no budget set.
- At least one good is needed: for `ℓ = 0` the only bundle is `0`, every data set satisfies
  conditions 2, 3 and 4 (with a constant `u`, strictly increasing vacuously), and no `u` is
  locally nonsatiated.
- With no observations all four conditions hold, with `u y = ∑ i, y i` for 1 and 4.
- Every data set of two observations of three goods with price coordinates in `{1, 2}` and bundle
  coordinates in `{0, 1, 2}` (46,656 data sets, 42,798 of them satisfying GARP), and every data
  set of four observations of two goods with price coordinates in `{1, 2}` and bundle
  coordinates in `{0, 1}` (65,536 data sets, 54,066 satisfying GARP): on each one satisfying
  GARP, the induction of the route, in exact arithmetic, finds a row with no negative entry at
  every step and returns `φ` and `lam ≥ 1` solving the Afriat inequalities, and the utility of
  the route takes the value `φ t` at `xᵗ`.

*Needs:* 2.1; ConsumerTheoryDuality 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `List.TFAE`, `Relation.TransGen`,
`ConcaveOn`, `ConcaveOn.inf`, `Continuous.min`, `StrictMono`, `Finset.inf'`, `dotProduct`.

### Examples

The tie of two bundles at repeated prices, the two-observation GARP violation, the cyclic
three-good data, data with a zero price or a price with a zero coordinate, and data with no
observations.

### Dependencies

ConsumerTheoryDuality 1.1, 1.2 (copied definitions). Mathlib `List.TFAE`, `Relation.TransGen`,
`ConcaveOn`, `ConcaveOn.inf`, `Continuous.min`, `StrictMono`, `Finset.inf'`, `dotProduct`. No
other layer.

## Layer 3: the weak axiom and the Slutsky matrix

### 3.1 The weak axiom for demand functions

Copy `slutskyMatrix` from ConsumerTheoryDuality 5.1: the entry `(i, j)` at `(p, m)` is
`∂xᵢ/∂pⱼ + xⱼ ∂xᵢ/∂m`, from the Fréchet derivative of `Function.uncurry x` at `(p, m)`. Define
`SatisfiesWARP x`: for `p, q ≫ 0` and `m, n > 0`, if `x q n ≠ x p m` and
`p ⬝ᵥ x q n ≤ m`, then `n < q ⬝ᵥ x p m` (Border, *WARP and the Slutsky matrix*, Definition 1,
p. 1, as the asymmetry of strict revealed preference among demanded bundles, and the form
following it under budget exhaustion; Varian, *Revealed Preference*, Definition 2, p. 2).

**Checks.**
- The clause `x q n ≠ x p m` is needed. Without it, taking `(q, n) = (p, m)` for a demand that
  exhausts the budget gives `m < m`; so no budget-exhausting demand satisfies the axiom as
  Varian's Definition 2 writes it.
- A demand generated by a utility with a unique maximizer on each budget satisfies the axiom:
  if `x q n` is affordable at `(p, m)` and differs from `x p m`, it is strictly worse, and then
  `x p m` cannot be affordable at `(q, n)`. This holds for the Cobb–Douglas demand
  `(m/(3p₁), 2m/(3p₂))` of `x₁^(1/3) x₂^(2/3)`.
- The demand `x(p, m) = m p/(p ⬝ᵥ p)` exhausts every budget and fails the axiom: at
  `(p, m) = ((1, 1), 2)` it is `(1, 1)`, at `(q, n) = ((1, 2), 3)` it is `(3/5, 6/5)`, which costs
  `9/5 ≤ 2` at `p`, while `(1, 1)` costs exactly `3` at `q`.

*Needs:* ConsumerTheoryDuality 5.1; Mathlib `fderiv`, `Function.uncurry`, `dotProduct`. (Proved
here.)

### 3.2 Samuelson's theorem

Prove `slutskyMatrix_negSemidef_of_warp`: if `x` satisfies the weak axiom and
`q ⬝ᵥ x q n = n` for all `q ≫ 0` and `n > 0`, then for every `p ≫ 0` and `m > 0` the Slutsky
matrix `S` at `(p, m)` satisfies `w ⬝ᵥ (S *ᵥ w) ≤ 0` for every `w`, `S *ᵥ p = 0` and
`p ᵥ* S = 0` (Border, *WARP and the Slutsky matrix*, Lemma 2 and Theorem 3, p. 2, with the proof
on p. 3, for negative semidefiniteness and a demand differentiable everywhere, attributing the
argument to Kihlstrom, Mas-Colell and Sonnenschein and the lemma to Samuelson; the form here,
with no differentiability hypothesis and no sign condition on the demand, and the two kernel
statements are proved here).

Route. If `Function.uncurry x` is not differentiable at `(p, m)`, then `S = 0` and all three
statements hold. Otherwise:

- *Compensated law of demand* (Border, Lemma 2): for `x⁰ = x p⁰ w⁰` and `x¹ = x p¹ (p¹ ⬝ᵥ x⁰)`,
  with `p⁰, p¹ ≫ 0`, `w⁰ > 0` and `p¹ ⬝ᵥ x⁰ > 0`, `(p¹ − p⁰) ⬝ᵥ (x¹ − x⁰) ≤ 0`. If `x¹ ≠ x⁰`,
  the weak axiom at `(p¹, p¹ ⬝ᵥ x⁰)` and `(p⁰, w⁰)` gives `p⁰ ⬝ᵥ x¹ > w⁰ = p⁰ ⬝ᵥ x⁰`, while budget
  exhaustion gives `p¹ ⬝ᵥ x¹ = p¹ ⬝ᵥ x⁰`.
- *Negative semidefiniteness* (Border, proof of Theorem 3): for `w` and small `t`, let
  `g t = x (p + t • w) ((p + t • w) ⬝ᵥ x p m)`. Then `p + t • w ≫ 0` and the income is near
  `m > 0`, so the lemma gives `t * (w ⬝ᵥ (g t − g 0)) ≤ 0`, hence `w ⬝ᵥ (g t − g 0)/t ≤ 0` for
  `t ≠ 0`. By the chain rule (Mathlib's `HasFDerivAt.comp`), `g` has at `0` the derivative
  `S *ᵥ w`, and the limit (Mathlib's `le_of_tendsto`) gives `w ⬝ᵥ (S *ᵥ w) ≤ 0`. Only
  differentiability at `(p, m)` is used.
- *`S *ᵥ p = 0`*: the weak axiom with budget exhaustion makes `x` homogeneous of degree zero, since
  `x (t • p) (t * m)` and `x p m` are affordable in the same budget and each exhausts it, so they
  are equal or the axiom fails. Mathlib's Euler identity
  `HasFDerivAt.apply_self_eq_smul_of_eventuallyEq`, with the constant scaling function `1`, gives
  `∑ⱼ (∂xᵢ/∂pⱼ) pⱼ + (∂xᵢ/∂m) m = 0`, and `p ⬝ᵥ x p m = m` turns this into `(S *ᵥ p) i = 0`.
- *`p ᵥ* S = 0`*: the function `(q, n) ↦ q ⬝ᵥ x q n − n` vanishes near `(p, m)`, so its
  derivative there is `0` (Mathlib's `HasFDerivAt.congr_of_eventuallyEq` and
  `HasFDerivAt.unique`); by the product rule (`HasFDerivAt.mul`, `HasFDerivAt.sum`) this says
  `xⱼ + ∑ᵢ pᵢ ∂xᵢ/∂pⱼ = 0` and `∑ᵢ pᵢ ∂xᵢ/∂m = 1`, so `(p ᵥ* S) j = −xⱼ + xⱼ = 0`.

The quadratic form `w ⬝ᵥ (S *ᵥ w)` depends only on the symmetric part of `S`, and the theorem
says nothing about the antisymmetric part.

**Checks.**
- Cobb–Douglas with weights `1/3, 2/3` at `p = (2, 3)`, `m = 5`: `S` is
  `[[−5/18, 5/27], [5/27, −10/81]]`, the quadratic form at `w = (1, 0)` is `−5/18`, and
  `S *ᵥ p = 0`, `p ᵥ* S = 0`.
- The weak axiom is needed: the demand `m p/(p ⬝ᵥ p)` of 3.1 exhausts every budget and is
  homogeneous of degree zero, so `S *ᵥ p = 0` and `p ᵥ* S = 0` hold for it, but at
  `((1, 1), 2)` its Slutsky matrix is `[[1/2, −1/2], [−1/2, 1/2]]`, with quadratic form `2` at
  `w = (1, −1)`.
- Budget exhaustion is needed: for one good, `x(p, m) = 2m/p` satisfies the weak axiom (if
  `2n/q ≠ 2m/p` and `2pn/q ≤ m`, then `n ≤ mq/(2p) < 2mq/p`), and its Slutsky term is
  `−2m/p² + (2m/p)(2/p) = 2m/p² > 0`.
- One good: budget exhaustion forces `x(p, m) = m/p`, and `S = −m/p² + (m/p)(1/p) = 0`.
- On the grid of prices with coordinates in `{1/2, 1, 2, 3}`, the compensated law of demand
  holds for the Cobb–Douglas demand, with largest value `0` of `(p¹ − p⁰) ⬝ᵥ (x¹ − x⁰)`,
  attained exactly when `p¹` is a positive multiple of `p⁰`, where `x¹ = x⁰` by homogeneity.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the revealed preference relations of choice structures, the congruence axiom
and the strong axiom, Richter's theorem and Houthakker's theorem in Richter's form (Layer 1);
the generalized axiom, the Afriat inequalities, rationalization of finite data and Afriat's
theorem (Layer 2); the weak axiom for demand functions and Samuelson's theorem that it gives a
negative semidefinite Slutsky matrix with `p` in both kernels (Layer 3).

Already in the libraries and therefore not targets: transitive closures of relations
(`Relation.TransGen`), the antisymmetrization of a preorder (`Antisymmetrization`) and
Szpilrajn's extension of a partial order to a linear order (`extend_partialOrder`), which 1.2
consumes; the chain rule, Euler's identity for homogeneous functions and uniqueness of
derivatives, which 3.2 consumes.

Neighbouring roadmaps:

- ConsumerTheoryDuality owns budget sets, local nonsatiation, Marshallian demand and the Slutsky
  matrix; its definitions in its 1.1, 1.2 and 5.1 are copied verbatim above and are its, not this
  roadmap's. Its 5.2 proves, for a continuous utility with a single-valued demand, that the
  Slutsky matrix at `(p, e(p, ū))` is symmetric and negative semidefinite with `p` in its kernel,
  by the concavity of the expenditure function. Samuelson's theorem of 3.2 has different
  hypotheses (the weak axiom and budget exhaustion, with no utility), concludes no symmetry, and
  adds the left kernel; nothing of that 5.2 is restated or used.
- UtilityRepresentationTheorems owns preferences as unbundled total preorders and their utility
  representations; this roadmap uses its convention for preferences, and its rationalizing
  relations in Layer 1 are such total preorders. This roadmap states no utility representation,
  and nothing of that roadmap is used as an input. Whether the total preorder of 1.2 has a
  utility is the question that roadmap answers. SocialChoice 1.1 bundles the same total preorders as
  `WeakPref`; a statement here about a total preorder `R` applies to `R.rel` for `R : WeakPref α`.
- Rationalization by a quasilinear utility, for which the data must be cyclically monotone, leads
  to Rockafellar's characterization of cyclically monotone sets: Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.isCyclicallyMonotone_pairingCost_iff_exists_fenchelConjugate`
  (`TauCeti/MeasureTheory/OptimalTransport/CTransform/Rockafellar.lean`) and
  ConvexAnalysisAndDuality 4.4. No statement about quasilinear utilities is made here.

Outside the scope: rationalization of finite data by strictly concave utilities with unique
maximizers (Matzkin and Richter), Richter's representation of choices by utility functions,
revealed preference with infinitely many observations, homothetic and separable rationalization,
goodness-of-fit indices, the converse direction from a negative semidefinite Slutsky matrix to the
weak axiom, and the integrability problem.
