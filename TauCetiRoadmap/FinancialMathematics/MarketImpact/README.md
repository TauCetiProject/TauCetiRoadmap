# Roadmap: market impact from endogenous dynamic hedging


**Summary.** Build endogenous clearing and feedback pricing on the one-factor financial foundation, from consistent reference demand through finite-claim coupling and smooth expiry to local nonlinear existence, first variations, noncompact comparison and least-squares volatility fits. The endpoint theorems distinguish full-horizon replication markets, fixed-cutoff price slices and the unsmoothed first-order boundary layer.

## Conventions



Fix a finite horizon `[0,T]`, a constant short rate `r`, a positive non-dividend-paying asset `X`, a riskless account `β`, one Brownian factor `W`, and a filtered probability space satisfying the conditions required by M0 and StochasticCalculus. Reference income initially follows

`dY_t = μ₁ Y_t dt + η₁ Y_t dW_t`, with `Y₀>0`, `η₁>0`. Here `μ₁` is the source model’s specialization of M0’s income-drift symbol `μ_Y`; it is not the asset drift `μ_S`.

The same Brownian factor drives income and the asset generated by market clearing. A market has reference demand `D(x,y,t)` and program-trader demand `𝓗(x,t)`. Normalize total asset supply to one and impose

`D(X_t,Y_t,t) + 𝓗(X_t,t) = 1`.

For a claim price `C(x,t)`, the single-claim demand is `𝓗=ρ C_x`, with `ρ≥0`. For finitely many claims it is `𝓗(x,t)=Σ_i q_i(t) C_{i,x}(x,t)`, where each deterministic weight is specified and regular. Prices and all derivatives are spot-first in formulas, `C(x,t)`. The native-function abbreviation `Price` uses the same spot-first argument order as M0; write time-to-maturity prices as `f(s,τ)=C(s,T−τ)`. `μ₁` always denotes income drift; asset drift is `μ_S` when using M0, or `b_X` for the clearing-induced coefficient. Use `q_i` only for program-trader supply weights and `ρ` only for the feedback scale. M0 names the market price of risk `κ` and holdings `(a,b)`; retain that convention.

Use `𝓗` exclusively for program-trader demand, `𝓧ᵥ` for the normalized-price Banach space, `𝒱` for Black–Scholes vega, `𝓡^ρ` and `C_{rem}` for the perturbation remainder and its bound, `ℓᵢ` for the discounted cash trace, `d_Γ` for the gamma-direction denominator, with the asset X, bond holding b, demand D and strike K distinct.

This chapter uses the M0.2 self-financing replication route in the declared complete one-factor filtration. The pricing equation contains the short rate and feedback volatility, not the physical asset drift. In the primary delta-only model, the source income drift affects the clearing-induced asset drift and cancels from its replicated pricing PDE. With `σ=γη₁` fixed, `μ₁` and `η₁` are not independent inputs to that scalar pricing equation. For the enlarged observable state in §1.3, additional-state exposures introduce the risk-adjusted drift `b_Ξ−κν_Ξ`; this may depend on income drift, and its observable closure is an explicit hypothesis. A risk-neutral expectation may be used as an expectation identity only after deriving it from M0.2 and the actual feedback coefficient; it is not a second unexplained pricing convention.

For the feedback asset, verify M0.2's variable-coefficient hypotheses on the actual solution region: bounded percentage volatility and reciprocal, bounded market price of risk, true density, the original P-Brownian natural filtration and predictable representation transferred to Q. Own those coefficient bounds from the clearing map, the positive margins, and the higher derivatives proved in §3.2; the baseline bounded parabolic Hölder space of order 2+α,1+α/2 estimate alone does not supply Cₓₓₓ or Cₓₜ. A stopped verification alone does not imply full-horizon completeness or an arbitrage-free market; prove the nonexplosion, density and integrability passage required by M0.2. Deterministic-shift equality of Brownian natural filtrations in the constant-coefficient example is not a proof for this feedback market.

For any jointly C¹ equilibrium map with continuous spatial Hessian `X=ψ(Y,t)`, Itô gives the actual asset coefficients

`b_X(y,t) = ψ_t(y,t) + μ₁ y ψ_y(y,t) + (η₁² y²/2) ψ_{yy}(y,t)`,

`ν_X(y,t) = η₁ y ψ_y(y,t)`.

These are drift and diffusion in `dX_t=b_X(Y_t,t)dt+ν_X(Y_t,t)dW_t`; if expressing percentage coefficients, divide each by `ψ`. The map, strict monotonicity, range, nonexplosion, and regularity needed for this representation are proved, not inferred from a formal implicit derivative.

## How to read the build

Layer 1 specifies clearing and feedback pricing. Layer 2 defines finite claim families, smoothing and expiry data. Layer 3 constructs their local nonlinear solutions and first variations, with weighted comparison and full-market sufficiency kept separate. Layer 4 derives local fits and the fixed-cutoff limit. Layer 5 transports the model to log and residual-cash coordinates and establishes its system estimates and exact positive paths. A price-slice theorem is never a substitute for the full-horizon market theorem.

## Layer 1: Equilibrium map, asset dynamics and pricing

### 1.1 Clearing and implicit solution


For `G(x,y,t)=D(x,y,t)+𝓗(x,t)`, the local clearing theorem starts at a positive root `G(x_*,y_*,t_*)=1` with `G_x(x_*,y_*,t_*)≠0`. Require joint C¹ regularity in `(x,y,t)` on an open neighborhood and a spatial Hessian `D²_(x,y)G` continuous jointly in `(x,y,t)`. The implicit-function theorem gives a unique local branch `x=ψ(y,t)`, its state/time neighborhood, and `ψ_y=−G_y/G_x`, `ψ_t=−G_t/G_x`. Derive the continuous second spatial derivatives needed for Itô. Global uniqueness on the positive price domain is a separate target.

For a global clearing theorem, prove endpoint crossing and strict monotonicity for each state/time. One sufficient case has `G_x<0` throughout the positive-price domain and `lim_{x↓0}G>1>lim_{x→∞}G`. A more flexible sufficient case gives a positive multiplier `m(x,y,t)` with the same joint regularity as G such that `Φ=m(G−1)` is strictly monotone with opposite endpoint limits. Prove `Φ_x≠0` at its root for differentiability; strict monotonicity alone does not imply a simple root. At a root, `Φ_x=mG_x`. A global theorem may instead assume a unique positive simple root for every state/time and patch the local branches. State separately the diffusion existence, preservation of the state domain and nonexplosion hypotheses. These clearing contracts also apply with fixed supply `s>0` replacing one.

In the linear-consistency single-claim model `D(x,y)=d_0 y^γ/x`, `d_0,γ>0`, clearing becomes

`X_t(1−ρ C_x(X_t,t)) = d_0 Y_t^γ`.

For multiple claims replace `ρ C_x` by `𝓗`. Sufficient global clearing conditions on the relevant state domain are `1−𝓗>0`, `1−𝓗−x𝓗_x≥δ>0`, and limits of `x(1−𝓗)` equal to 0 and `+∞` at the two x endpoints. The middle expression is the derivative of `x(1−𝓗)`; these conditions prove existence and uniqueness of the positive equilibrium instead of assuming it. Local versions are allowed only when the theorem explicitly restricts to the range of the clearing map.

Define `clearingMap` for fixed demand H by x(1−H(x)); `global_clearing` asserts its unique positive inverse value for each y>0 under differentiability, a uniform strictly positive derivative margin, limit zero at 0 and limit infinity at infinity. The more general simple-root and multiplier formulations above remain targets (SP97 §2.3, (2.7)–(2.11), pp.7–8, with explicit simple-root regularity).

- The map has source (0,∞) and target (0,∞) under the crossing and monotonicity hypotheses, and its inverse preserves order.
- Local branches agree on overlaps containing the same simple root; the price partial derivative controls inverse derivatives.
- For the linear demand, the root equation agrees with income clearing, and the lower derivative margin gives an inverse Lipschitz bound of 1/δ on its range.

**Checks.**

- D=y/x,H=0 gives root x=y and clearing map x; at y=2 the root is 2 and Gₓ=−1/2.
- D=y/x,H=1 gives G=1+y/x>1 for y>0 and no root; its clearing map is zero.
- For x(1−H)=1+(x−1)³ the endpoint limits are 0 and infinity and the map is strictly increasing, but its derivative at x=1 is zero; it cannot justify the differentiable inverse claim.
- A multiplier m=2 gives Φₓ=2Gₓ at a root; omitting the multiplier changes the derivative but not the zero set.

*Needs:* Mathlib implicit-function APIs in the supplier contract; the intermediate value theorem and derivative monotonicity; StochasticCalculus §5.1 for the subsequent Itô adapter.

### 1.2 Feedback volatility and the pricing equation

Under the positive quadrant and consistency assumptions, derive the market-clearing diffusion from `ψ` and prove the linear-demand feedback volatility

`σ_fb(x,t)=σ x (1−𝓗(x,t))/(1−𝓗(x,t)−x𝓗_x(x,t))`,

where `σ=γη₁` is the no-feedback asset volatility. For a single claim `𝓗=ρ C_x`, this is the source's (2.24). The effective variance in the pricing PDE is `σ_fb²`, so the single-claim equation is

`C_t + (σ²x²/2) ((1−ρ C_x)/(1−ρ C_x−ρxC_{xx}))² C_{xx} + r(xC_x−C)=0`.

Prove the `ρ=0` reduction by substitution. For general consistent demand `D=U(y^γ/x)`, require `U′>0` on `(0,∞)` and the regularity used in the clearing/Itô derivatives. Its image J is an interval; define `V:J→(0,∞)` as its inverse and prove its derivative identities. Require `1−𝓗(x,t)∈J` throughout the admitted price-time domain. Clearing is `y^γ=xV(1−𝓗)`. Set `J_t(x)=xV(1−𝓗(x,t))` and prove

`J_t′(x)=[V(1−𝓗)U′(V(1−𝓗))−x𝓗_x]/U′(V(1−𝓗))`.

For the global positive branch require this derivative positive and endpoint limits `J_t(0+)=0`, `J_t(∞)=∞`; state quantitative lower bounds on the regions where estimates are claimed. Derive the unspecialized feedback coefficient and prove that its clearing denominator has this derivative sign. The inverse-profile range and denominator conditions are part of the theorem. For `U(z)=d₀z`, recover the linear-demand formula explicitly.

The parabolic domain is part of each theorem. For linear demand use, on the solution and closed time-space region under consideration,

`1−𝓗 ≥ δ`, `1−𝓗−x𝓗_x ≥ δ`, and `1−𝓗+x𝓗_x ≥ δ` for some `δ>0`.

The first inequality keeps positive income/clearing; the second keeps the equilibrium derivative away from zero; the third is the sign of the derivative of the nonlinear diffusion with respect to gamma. State additional smoothness and bounds needed for uniform parabolic estimates. A nonzero denominator alone is inadequate: a negative or vanishing coefficient can reverse parabolicity or clearing monotonicity.

Use `Price`, `timeDeriv`, `priceDeriv` and `gamma` for real spot-first functions and their calendar-time, first-spot and second-spot derivatives. Define `blackScholesResidual` as the baseline backward operator, `linearFeedbackVolatility` and `linearFeedbackResidual` by the displayed single-claim formulas, and `feedbackParabolicMargins` by δ>0, x>0 and lower bounds δ on the three displayed factors. These simple formulas state the nonlinear existence and first-variation headlines (SP97 (2.23)–(2.24), p.12).

- Price derivatives are linear on differentiable prices; mixed derivatives commute only with the stated smoothness.
- The feedback residual at ρ=0 equals the baseline residual. The volatility agrees with the clearing-induced diffusion under §1.1's hypotheses.
- Each margin is continuous in the relevant normalized derivatives and stays positive on a sufficiently small neighborhood. The profile inverse V has domain U((0,∞)), not all real numbers.

**Checks.**

- For C(x,t)=x²+t at (1,0), time derivative is 1, delta is 2 and gamma is 2; at r=0,σ=1 the baseline residual is 2. For C=x and C=0 the time derivative and gamma are zero, while deltas are 1 and 0.
- For σ=x=1,ρ=1/10,C=x², volatility is 4/3 and the diffusion term is 16/9; the three margins are 4/5,3/5,1, so δ=1/2 succeeds and δ=7/10 fails.
- For ρ=0 the volatility is σx and all three factors equal 1; a zero denominator at ρ=1/4,C=x²,x=1 is excluded even though real division would return zero.
- For U(z)=2z, V(h)=h/2 and J′=(1−H−xHₓ)/2; for U(z)=1−exp(−z), its image is (0,1), and h=2 has no admissible inverse value.
- For affine C=x and ρ=1, both numerator and denominator vanish; a nonzero σ does not restore parabolicity.

*Needs:* §1.1 clearing and derivatives; FinancialMathematics M0.2–M0.3; StochasticCalculus §5.1.

### 1.3 Consistency of reference demand

The analytic characterization is this: for jointly C¹ demand with Dₓ<0 on the connected quadrant `x,y>0`, and fixed `γ>0`, the identity

`D_y/D_x = −γx/y`

for every `(x,y)` is equivalent to `D(x,y)=U(y^γ/x)` for a C¹ profile with U′>0. The corresponding profile satisfies `U'(z)>0` for every `z>0`; conversely `U'>0` gives `D_x<0,D_y>0`. Prove both directions with `U(z)=D(1,z^(1/γ))` and the scaling orbits of `γx∂_x+y∂_y`. A statement of strict monotonicity alone is insufficient for the derivative conclusion.

This global analytic identity is stronger than the source's market proposition as worded. With `ρ=0`, the market only visits the clearing level set `D=1`; matching diffusion on that one level does not determine D away from it. Consistency on every supply level `c` in an interval proves the ratio/profile identity only on `D^{-1}(I)`; it gives a profile on the corresponding invariant-coordinate interval, not automatically on the full quadrant. A global model “if and only if” needs consistency on all attainable levels whose preimages cover the entire claimed domain (or a separate analytic continuation hypothesis). Interpret SP97 Proposition 1, p.11, as a global coefficient condition; its single-level interpretation is insufficient. Do not present the analytic characterization as proved from one observed equilibrium curve or from an interval whose preimage is only a strip.

For the time/state-dependent extension, work only on domains where `η(y,t)` and `λ(x,t)` are nonzero and the primitives

`Q(y,t)=∫^y dv/η(v,t)`, `L(x,t)=∫^x dv/λ(v,t)`

are defined. Prove the fixed-time compatibility `D=Ũ(Q−L,t)` by differentiating and integrating the first-order relation. Because Q and L depend on time, separately compute the `t`-derivatives in the clearing-induced Itô drift; do not claim a drift or pricing identity that drops them. State connectedness, normalization constants, inverse-profile range and regularity. In a generalized state-dependent no-feedback model, eliminate income y using the actual clearing inverse only when `G_y≠0`; then prove the adapter from the source coefficient `−η(y,t)D_y/D_x` to M0’s spot diffusion coefficient `σ_S(x,t)x`. If y cannot be eliminated or affects future coefficients independently of x, enlarge the state and do not assert a one-dimensional Markov PDE.


#### General Itô state, observability and hedge-loading adapter

Let the complete state `Z=(Y,Ξ)∈ℝ^m` have specified dynamics `dZ=b(t,Z)dt+Σ(t,Z)dW`, driven by `d` Brownian coordinates relative to the working filtration. `Y` may be latent; `Ξ` denotes additional state observed by program traders. Their demand is `𝓗(x,ξ,t)` and obeys this observability restriction. State each stochastic weight's dynamics, covariance, filtration and measurability. A pathwise Itô identity also admits progressive coefficients under its local integrability hypotheses; a Markov pricing equation requires a specified complete Markov state. Deterministic weights may be adjoined as ODE coordinates. Random weights without a state/dynamics contract give no closed generator.

For `G(x,z,t)=D(x,z,t)+𝓗(x,ξ,t)` and supply `s>0`, apply §1.1 at a positive simple root; use a unique positive simple root at every state/time, or prove a multiplier monotonicity/crossing criterion, for a global branch. Require joint C¹ regularity in `(x,z,t)` and full spatial Hessian `D²_(x,z)G` continuous jointly in `(x,z,t)`. Prove

`ψ_t=−G_t/G_x`, `D_zψ=−D_zG/G_x`,

`D²_zzψ=−(D²_zzG+G_xz⊗D_zψ+D_zψ⊗G_xz+G_xx D_zψ⊗D_zψ)/G_x`.

A quantitative lower bound for `|G_x|` is needed on the regions used by the estimate, rather than on the entire positive quadrant. Itô gives stock drift `b_X=ψ_t+Dψ b+½ tr(ΣΣᵀD²ψ)` and Brownian loading `ν_X=DψΣ`. Include the full covariance contraction for all state coordinates.

For an observable claim price `C_i(x,ξ,t)`, write `ν_Ξ` for the observed state's Brownian loading. Its exposure is `k_i=C_{i,x}ν_X+D_ξC_i ν_Ξ`. A stock holding `a_i` replicates this exposure exactly when `k_i=a_iν_X`. For one risk and nonzero `ν_X`, this gives `a_i=C_{i,x}+(D_ξC_i ν_Ξ)/ν_X`; with several risks it requires vector parallelism. Define program demand by `𝓗=Σ_i q_i a_i`, with each weight observable. The source's delta-only rule is recovered when the extra exposure vanishes.

Own the coupled clearing/loading/hedge system: `G(x,z,t)=s`, `x=ψ(z,t)`, `ν_X=DψΣ`, and the exposure equations for the `a_i`. For each claimed regular solution, derive the full pricing generator and self-financing drift equation `C_{i,t}+b_X C_{i,x}+b_Ξ·D_ξC_i+½ tr(A D²_{(x,ξ)}C_i)=rC_i+a_i(b_X−rx)`, where A is the covariance of `(X,Ξ)`. State the solution class, local uniqueness/continuation hypotheses, positivity and risk-span conditions. Prove the conditional regular-solution-to-market theorem with nonexplosion, a true equivalent density, martingale representation when completeness is claimed, moment bounds and admissible holdings. Prove the converse from an admissible regular equilibrium to these equations. Existence for a particular coupled system is a separate stated target under its specified coefficient/margin assumptions; arbitrary progressive coefficients do not imply PDE solvability.

Require observable descent on the clearing set. In the one-risk model define `κ(z,t)=(b_X(z,t)−rx)/ν_X(z,t)`, with `x=ψ(z,t)`. For a coefficient-level observable Markov theorem supply functions `R_obs(x,ξ,t)`, `b_obs^Q(x,ξ,t)` and `A_obs(x,ξ,t)` such that, on every admitted clearing state,

`ν_Ξ/ν_X=R_obs`, `b_Ξ−κν_Ξ=b_obs^Q`, and `A=A_obs`.

Weights and the resulting holdings must descend as well: `a_i=C_{i,x}+D_ξC_i R_obs` and `𝓗=Σ_iq_i a_i` are functions of `(x,ξ,t)`. A sufficient scalar-latent-state case proves `G_y≠0` and a unique inverse `y=y(x,ξ,t)` on the admitted domain; substitute this inverse into every coefficient and prove the required joint regularity. If `ν_Ξ=0`, only the remaining drift/covariance coefficients need observable closure. With several risks require the corresponding observable exposure-in-span factorization and a specified risk-premium vector.

Under these closure hypotheses the one-risk observable pricing equation is

`C_{i,t}+b_obs^Q·D_ξC_i+½tr(A_obs D²_(x,ξ)C_i)+r(xC_{i,x}−C_i)=0`.

The drift of Ξ is risk adjusted and need not lose its income-drift dependence. If coefficient-level closure fails, retain the full-state equations and require any price-specific holdings and generator expressions to descend before asserting an observable demand/price. Some particular claims can descend without all coefficients doing so; no general impossibility theorem is inferred from failure of the sufficient closure conditions. A partial-observation/filtering extension must specify and construct its actual observed state and filtration before claiming a closed pricing theorem.

M0.2 and §3.4 give the complete-market conclusion only under their one-risk filtration, true-density, representation and admissibility hypotheses. With `d>1` one stock has rank at most one; replicated claims must have exposure in its traded span. A complete multi-risk extension specifies further traded assets and full-rank volatility/representation. A jump extension must separately supply jump-Itô terms, state preservation and admissibility. State elimination or closure is required for every scalar PDE. The random-weight remark of SP97 §2.6, p.13, does not supply these hypotheses; the state/observability/hedge system here is the precise conditional target.

Use `Demand` and `Profile` for real-function carriers, `classicalConsistentDemand` for U(y^γ/x), and `demandRatioCondition` for the positive-quadrant derivative ratio. The two directions are `ratio_condition_characterizes_profile` and `profile_implies_ratio_condition`; the first assumes joint C¹ regularity and Dₓ<0, the second assumes C¹ profile with U′>0 (SP97 Proposition 1, p.11, formulated as a global analytic condition).

- The invariant coordinate is constant on (x,y)↦(a^γ x,ay) for a>0; recover U(z)=D(1,z^(1/γ)).
- Strictly positive U′ gives Dₓ<0 and Dᵧ>0; the ratio is well-defined because Dₓ≠0.
- For generalized Q−L, primitive constants change the profile argument, not the physical demand; time derivatives are retained.
- For an observed state, exposure is a Brownian vector; replication is its equality with a stock multiple, and observable descent is equality on every admitted clearing state.

**Checks.**

- For γ=1,U(z)=z, at (x,y)=(2,3), D=3/2, Dₓ=−3/4 and Dᵧ=1/2, giving ratio −2/3 in both directions.
- U=0 gives both derivatives zero and totalized ratio zero, which is not −γx/y; U(z)=(z−1)³ is increasing but has derivative zero at 1 and fails the strict derivative hypothesis there.
- D=y/x+(y/x−1)² sin x agrees with y/x and its ratio on the level y=x, but at (1,2) the extra derivatives are nonzero; one clearing level does not imply global consistency.
- With η=λ=1, Q=y and L=x, D=exp(y−x) has Dᵧ/Dₓ=−1; adding t to Q requires an additional Dₜ term exp(y−x+t).
- For νₓ=(1,0), extra exposure (0,1) cannot be replicated by stock; exposure (2,0) is replicated by holding 2. In one risk with Cₓ=1 and extra exposure 2νₓ the holding is 3, not 1.

## Layer 2: Multiple claims, smoothing and expiry

### 3.1 Exact critical smoothing thresholds

For a call with strike K, maturity T, constant `σ>0,r`, and `τ=T−t>0`, let `s_σ=σ√τ` and

- For c=x=1, q=(1/10,1/10), Γ=(1,1), d=4/5, A=25/16 and the second eigenvalue is 75/32; treating the system as diagonal misses it.
- For Γ=0, J=A I; for one claim, the formula is c²(c+xqΓ)/d³. Positive d alone does not imply c+xqΓ>0: c=1,xqΓ=−2 gives d=3 and a negative scalar eigenvalue −1/27.

*Needs:* §1.2 feedback margins, §2.1 aggregate demand, §3.2 local solution; PDE Lane F F27.2–F27.5 for scalar inverses.

### 5.2 Residual cash coordinates and weight normalization

For common maturity set Bᵢ(t)=Kᵢexp(−r(T−t)) and Pᵢ=Cᵢ−x+Bᵢ. Derive Cᵢ,ₓ=Pᵢ,ₓ+1, Cᵢ,ₓₓ=Pᵢ,ₓₓ and Cᵢ,ₜ=Pᵢ,ₜ−rBᵢ. For arbitrary fixed weights qᵢ the transformed numerator and denominator are 1−Σqᵢ−ΣqᵢPᵢ,ₓ and 1−Σqᵢ−Σqᵢ(Pᵢ,ₓ+xPᵢ,ₓₓ). Prove each transformed PDE with the same common coefficient and r(xPᵢ,ₓ−Pᵢ), its cutoff trace, Pᵢ(0,t)=Bᵢ(t), and its zero far-field limit for call data (SP97 (5.1)–(5.4), pp.26–30, with total-weight correction to (5.4)).

- Sum the transformed equations for constant weights; time-varying weights introduce their differentiated terms.
- For qᵢ=ρ/n and n>0 the baseline is 1−ρ; for qᵢ=ρ it is 1−nρ.
- This coordinate change preserves residual equations, not the nonlinear call-to-put pricing operator.

**Checks.**

- With r=0,K=1,C=x, P=1 and both residuals vanish: the affine cash shift contributes zero.
- With n=2,ρ=1/10 and qᵢ=ρ/n, the baseline is 9/10; the alternative per-claim weights give 4/5, while 1−ρ/n is 19/20 and agrees with neither.
- With an empty family the numerator and denominator are both 1; no division by its cardinality is defined.

*Needs:* §1.2 pricing equation; §2.1 finite sums and constant-weight aggregation.

### 5.3 Exact positive asset identity

In the static linear-demand single-claim case let gₜ(x)=x(1−ρCₓ(x,t)). Under the global clearing conditions of §1.1, prove on the entire admitted horizon

    X_t = inverse(g_t)(Z_t),
    Z_t = X_0*(1-rho*C_x(X_0,0))*exp(sigma*(mu_1/eta_1-eta_1/2)*t + sigma*W_t).

Here σ=γη₁, η₁>0, and X₀(1−ρCₓ(X₀,0))=d₀Y₀^γ>0. This identifies the initial income and the actual positive path from geometric income; prove the identity by the power of the geometric-income solution, with the inverse regularity and stochastic integrability hypotheses from §§1.1 and 3.4. The corresponding log drift of X is bₓ/X−νₓ²/(2X²) and its log diffusion is νₓ/X (SP97 §5.1, pp.27–28, derived directly from §2.3).

- The inverse has domain (0,∞) and range (0,∞); every realized Z lies in that domain.
- Recover the initial value at t=0 and the no-feedback geometric asset at ρ=0.
- Relate the Itô coefficients of the inverse to §1.1 and require Cₓₓₓ and Cₓₜ when differentiating the price-defined clearing map.

**Checks.**

- With γ=η₁=σ=1 and μ₁=1/2, Zₜ=Z₀exp(Wₜ); its log drift is zero, not 1/2.
- With C=ax, gₜ(x)=(1−ρa)x, so the inverse is z/(1−ρa); at ρa=1 it has no positive-domain inverse.
- For C=x³, Cₓₓₓ=6; a second-order price norm by itself does not provide the third derivative used in the drift bounds.

*Needs:* §1.1 clearing inverse, §3.2 higher regularity, §3.4 full-horizon hypotheses; FinancialMathematics M0.1 and StochasticCalculus §9.4 geometric income.
