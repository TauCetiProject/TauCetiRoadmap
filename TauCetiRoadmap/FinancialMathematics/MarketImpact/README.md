# Market impact from endogenous dynamic hedging

This chapter develops the one-factor feedback market and its consequences. It consumes the reusable market, replication and Black–Scholes contracts in [FinancialMathematics](../README.md) M0.1–M0.4. It owns the endogenous clearing map, model-specific asset dynamics and pricing system, multi-claim coupling, perturbation and fitting theorems, and numerical/statistical methods. Each theorem states its hypotheses and proof obligations; source formulas and numerical observations remain distinct from the mathematical targets.

## Scope and conventions

Fix a finite horizon `[0,T]`, a constant short rate `r`, a positive non-dividend-paying asset `X`, a riskless account `β`, one Brownian factor `W`, and a filtered probability space satisfying the conditions required by M0 and StochasticCalculus. Reference income initially follows

`dY_t = μ₁ Y_t dt + η₁ Y_t dW_t`, with `Y₀>0`, `η₁>0`. Here `μ₁` is the source model’s specialization of M0’s income-drift symbol `μ_Y`; it is not the asset drift `μ_S`.

The same Brownian factor drives income and the asset generated by market clearing. A market has reference demand `D(x,y,t)` and program-trader demand `\mathcal H(x,t)`. Normalize total asset supply to one and impose

`D(X_t,Y_t,t) + \mathcal H(X_t,t) = 1`.

For a claim price `C(x,t)`, the single-claim demand is `\mathcal H=ρ C_x`, with `ρ≥0`. For finitely many claims it is `\mathcal H(x,t)=Σ_i q_i(t) C_{i,x}(x,t)`, where each deterministic weight is specified and regular. Prices and all derivatives are spot-first in formulas, `C(x,t)`. The M0 Lean alias `Price s t` has the same spot-first argument order; write time-to-maturity prices as `f(s,τ)=C(s,T−τ)`. `μ₁` always denotes income drift; asset drift is `μ_S` when using M0, or `b_X` for the clearing-induced coefficient. Use `q_i` only for program-trader supply weights and `ρ` only for the feedback scale. M0 names the market price of risk `κ` and holdings `(a,b)`; retain that convention.

Use `\mathcal H` exclusively for program-trader demand, `\mathcal X_V` for the normalized-price Banach space, `\mathcal V` for Black–Scholes vega, `\mathcal R^ρ` and `C_{rem}` for the perturbation remainder and its bound, `\ell_i` for the discounted cash trace, `d_Γ` for the gamma-direction denominator, and `\mathfrak a_j,\mathfrak b_j` for the finite-difference coefficients. This keeps the asset `X`, bond holding `b`, demand `D`, strike `K`, and finite-difference operators `D₀,D₂` distinct.

This chapter uses the M0.2 self-financing replication route in the declared complete one-factor filtration. The pricing equation contains the short rate and feedback volatility, not the physical asset drift. In the primary delta-only model, the source income drift affects the clearing-induced asset drift and cancels from its replicated pricing PDE. With `σ=γη₁` fixed, `μ₁` and `η₁` are not independent inputs to that scalar pricing equation. For the enlarged observable state in M1.3, additional-state exposures introduce the risk-adjusted drift `b_Ξ−κν_Ξ`; this may depend on income drift, and its observable closure is an explicit hypothesis. A risk-neutral expectation may be used as a computational identity only after deriving it from M0.2 and the actual feedback coefficient; it is not a second unexplained pricing convention.

For the feedback asset, verify M0.2's variable-coefficient hypotheses on the actual solution region: bounded percentage volatility and reciprocal, bounded market price of risk, true density, the original P-Brownian natural filtration and predictable representation transferred to Q. Own those coefficient bounds from the clearing map, the positive margins, and the higher derivatives proved in M3.2; the baseline C_b^{2+α,1+α/2} estimate alone does not supply C_{xxx} or C_{xt}. A stopped verification alone does not imply full-horizon completeness or an arbitrage-free market; prove the nonexplosion, density and integrability passage required by M0.2. Deterministic-shift equality of Brownian natural filtrations in the constant-coefficient example is not a proof for this feedback market.

For any differentiable equilibrium map `X=ψ(Y,t)`, Itô gives the actual asset coefficients

`b_X(y,t) = ψ_t(y,t) + μ₁ y ψ_y(y,t) + (η₁² y²/2) ψ_{yy}(y,t)`,

`ν_X(y,t) = η₁ y ψ_y(y,t)`.

These are drift and diffusion in `dX_t=b_X(Y_t,t)dt+ν_X(Y_t,t)dW_t`; if expressing percentage coefficients, divide each by `ψ`. The map, strict monotonicity, range, nonexplosion, and regularity needed for this representation are proved, not inferred from a formal implicit derivative.

## M1. Equilibrium map, asset dynamics and pricing

### M1.1 Clearing and implicit solution


For `G(x,y,t)=D(x,y,t)+\mathcal H(x,t)`, the local clearing theorem starts at a positive root `G(x_*,y_*,t_*)=1` with `G_x(x_*,y_*,t_*)≠0`. Require joint C¹ regularity in `(x,y,t)` on an open neighborhood and a spatial Hessian `D²_(x,y)G` continuous jointly in `(x,y,t)`. The implicit-function theorem gives a unique local branch `x=ψ(y,t)`, its state/time neighborhood, and `ψ_y=−G_y/G_x`, `ψ_t=−G_t/G_x`. Derive the continuous second spatial derivatives needed for Itô. Global uniqueness on the positive price domain is a separate target.

For a global clearing theorem, prove endpoint crossing and strict monotonicity for each state/time. One sufficient case has `G_x<0` throughout the positive-price domain and `lim_{x↓0}G>1>lim_{x→∞}G`. A more flexible sufficient case gives a positive multiplier `m(x,y,t)` with the same joint regularity as G such that `Φ=m(G−1)` is strictly monotone with opposite endpoint limits. Prove `Φ_x≠0` at its root for differentiability; strict monotonicity alone does not imply a simple root. At a root, `Φ_x=mG_x`. A global theorem may instead assume a unique positive simple root for every state/time and patch the local branches. State separately the diffusion existence, preservation of the state domain and nonexplosion hypotheses. These clearing contracts also apply with fixed supply `s>0` replacing one.

In the linear-consistency single-claim model `D(x,y)=d_0 y^γ/x`, `d_0,γ>0`, clearing becomes

`X_t(1−ρ C_x(X_t,t)) = d_0 Y_t^γ`.

For multiple claims replace `ρ C_x` by `\mathcal H`. Sufficient global clearing conditions on the relevant state domain are `1−\mathcal H>0`, `1−\mathcal H−x\mathcal H_x≥δ>0`, and limits of `x(1−\mathcal H)` equal to 0 and `+∞` at the two x endpoints. The middle expression is the derivative of `x(1−\mathcal H)`; these conditions prove existence and uniqueness of the positive equilibrium instead of assuming it. Local versions are allowed only when the theorem explicitly restricts to the range of the clearing map.

### M1.2 Feedback volatility and the pricing equation

Under the positive quadrant and consistency assumptions, derive the market-clearing diffusion from `ψ` and prove the linear-demand feedback volatility

`σ_fb(x,t)=σ x (1−\mathcal H(x,t))/(1−\mathcal H(x,t)−x\mathcal H_x(x,t))`,

where `σ=γη₁` is the no-feedback asset volatility. For a single claim `\mathcal H=ρ C_x`, this is the source's (2.24). The effective variance in the pricing PDE is `σ_fb²`, so the single-claim equation is

`C_t + (σ²x²/2) ((1−ρ C_x)/(1−ρ C_x−ρxC_{xx}))² C_{xx} + r(xC_x−C)=0`.

Prove the `ρ=0` reduction by substitution. For general consistent demand `D=U(y^γ/x)`, require `U′>0` on `(0,∞)` and the regularity used in the clearing/Itô derivatives. Its image J is an interval; define `V:J→(0,∞)` as its inverse and prove its derivative identities. Require `1−\mathcal H(x,t)∈J` throughout the admitted price-time domain. Clearing is `y^γ=xV(1−\mathcal H)`. Set `J_t(x)=xV(1−\mathcal H(x,t))` and prove

`J_t′(x)=[V(1−\mathcal H)U′(V(1−\mathcal H))−x\mathcal H_x]/U′(V(1−\mathcal H))`.

For the global positive branch require this derivative positive and endpoint limits `J_t(0+)=0`, `J_t(∞)=∞`; state quantitative lower bounds on the regions where estimates are claimed. Derive the unspecialized feedback coefficient and prove that its clearing denominator has this derivative sign. The inverse-profile range and denominator conditions are part of the theorem. For `U(z)=d₀z`, recover the linear-demand formula explicitly.

The parabolic domain is part of each theorem. For linear demand use, on the solution and closed time-space region under consideration,

`1−\mathcal H ≥ δ`, `1−\mathcal H−x\mathcal H_x ≥ δ`, and `1−\mathcal H+x\mathcal H_x ≥ δ` for some `δ>0`.

The first inequality keeps positive income/clearing; the second keeps the equilibrium derivative away from zero; the third is the sign of the derivative of the nonlinear diffusion with respect to gamma. State additional smoothness and bounds needed for uniform parabolic estimates. A nonzero denominator alone is inadequate: a negative or vanishing coefficient can reverse parabolicity or clearing monotonicity.

### M1.3 Consistency of reference demand

The analytic characterization is this: on the connected quadrant `x,y>0`, for fixed `γ>0`, the identity

`D_y/D_x = −γx/y`

for every `(x,y)` is equivalent to `D(x,y)=U(y^γ/x)`. If `D_x<0`, the corresponding profile satisfies `U'(z)>0` for every `z>0`; conversely `U'>0` gives `D_x<0,D_y>0`. Prove both directions with `U(z)=D(1,z^(1/γ))` and the scaling orbits of `γx∂_x+y∂_y`. A statement of strict monotonicity alone is insufficient for the derivative conclusion.

This global analytic identity is stronger than the source's market proposition as worded. With `ρ=0`, the market only visits the clearing level set `D=1`; matching diffusion on that one level does not determine D away from it. Consistency on every supply level `c` in an interval proves the ratio/profile identity only on `D^{-1}(I)`; it gives a profile on the corresponding invariant-coordinate interval, not automatically on the full quadrant. A global model “if and only if” needs consistency on all attainable levels whose preimages cover the entire claimed domain (or a separate analytic continuation hypothesis). Record the original p32 “iff” without that coverage as a source overclaim. Do not present the analytic characterization as proved from one observed equilibrium curve or from an interval whose preimage is only a strip.

For the time/state-dependent extension, work only on domains where `η(y,t)` and `λ(x,t)` are nonzero and the primitives

`Q(y,t)=∫^y dv/η(v,t)`, `L(x,t)=∫^x dv/λ(v,t)`

are defined. Prove the fixed-time compatibility `D=Ũ(Q−L,t)` by differentiating and integrating the first-order relation. Because Q and L depend on time, separately compute the `t`-derivatives in the clearing-induced Itô drift; do not claim a drift or pricing identity that drops them. State connectedness, normalization constants, inverse-profile range and regularity. In a generalized state-dependent no-feedback model, eliminate income y using the actual clearing inverse only when `G_y≠0`; then prove the adapter from the source coefficient `−η(y,t)D_y/D_x` to M0’s spot diffusion coefficient `σ_S(x,t)x`. If y cannot be eliminated or affects future coefficients independently of x, enlarge the state and do not assert a one-dimensional Markov PDE.


#### General Itô state, observability and hedge-loading adapter

Let the complete state `Z=(Y,Ξ)∈ℝ^m` have specified dynamics `dZ=b(t,Z)dt+Σ(t,Z)dW`, driven by `d` Brownian coordinates relative to the working filtration. `Y` may be latent; `Ξ` denotes additional state observed by program traders. Their demand is `\mathcal H(x,ξ,t)` and obeys this observability restriction. State each stochastic weight's dynamics, covariance, filtration and measurability. A pathwise Itô identity also admits progressive coefficients under its local integrability hypotheses; a Markov pricing equation requires a specified complete Markov state. Deterministic weights may be adjoined as ODE coordinates. Random weights without a state/dynamics contract give no closed generator.

For `G(x,z,t)=D(x,z,t)+\mathcal H(x,ξ,t)` and supply `s>0`, apply M1.1 at a positive simple root; use a unique positive simple root at every state/time, or prove a multiplier monotonicity/crossing criterion, for a global branch. Require joint C¹ regularity in `(x,z,t)` and full spatial Hessian `D²_(x,z)G` continuous jointly in `(x,z,t)`. Prove

`ψ_t=−G_t/G_x`, `D_zψ=−D_zG/G_x`,

`D²_zzψ=−(D²_zzG+G_xz⊗D_zψ+D_zψ⊗G_xz+G_xx D_zψ⊗D_zψ)/G_x`.

A quantitative lower bound for `|G_x|` is needed on the regions used by the estimate, rather than on the entire positive quadrant. Itô gives stock drift `b_X=ψ_t+Dψ b+½ tr(ΣΣᵀD²ψ)` and Brownian loading `ν_X=DψΣ`. Include the full covariance contraction for all state coordinates.

For an observable claim price `C_i(x,ξ,t)`, write `ν_Ξ` for the observed state's Brownian loading. Its exposure is `k_i=C_{i,x}ν_X+D_ξC_i ν_Ξ`. A stock holding `a_i` replicates this exposure exactly when `k_i=a_iν_X`. For one risk and nonzero `ν_X`, this gives `a_i=C_{i,x}+(D_ξC_i ν_Ξ)/ν_X`; with several risks it requires vector parallelism. Define program demand by `\mathcal H=Σ_i q_i a_i`, with each weight observable. The source's delta-only rule is recovered when the extra exposure vanishes.

Own the coupled clearing/loading/hedge system: `G(x,z,t)=s`, `x=ψ(z,t)`, `ν_X=DψΣ`, and the exposure equations for the `a_i`. For each claimed regular solution, derive the full pricing generator and self-financing drift equation `C_{i,t}+b_X C_{i,x}+b_Ξ·D_ξC_i+½ tr(A D²_{(x,ξ)}C_i)=rC_i+a_i(b_X−rx)`, where A is the covariance of `(X,Ξ)`. State the solution class, local uniqueness/continuation hypotheses, positivity and risk-span conditions. Prove the conditional regular-solution-to-market theorem with nonexplosion, a true equivalent density, martingale representation when completeness is claimed, moment bounds and admissible holdings. Prove the converse from an admissible regular equilibrium to these equations. Existence for a particular coupled system is a separate stated target under its specified coefficient/margin assumptions; arbitrary progressive coefficients do not imply PDE solvability.

Require observable descent on the clearing set. In the one-risk model define `κ(z,t)=(b_X(z,t)−rx)/ν_X(z,t)`, with `x=ψ(z,t)`. For a coefficient-level observable Markov theorem supply functions `R_obs(x,ξ,t)`, `b_obs^Q(x,ξ,t)` and `A_obs(x,ξ,t)` such that, on every admitted clearing state,

`ν_Ξ/ν_X=R_obs`, `b_Ξ−κν_Ξ=b_obs^Q`, and `A=A_obs`.

Weights and the resulting holdings must descend as well: `a_i=C_{i,x}+D_ξC_i R_obs` and `\mathcal H=Σ_iq_i a_i` are functions of `(x,ξ,t)`. A sufficient scalar-latent-state case proves `G_y≠0` and a unique inverse `y=y(x,ξ,t)` on the admitted domain; substitute this inverse into every coefficient and prove the required joint regularity. If `ν_Ξ=0`, only the remaining drift/covariance coefficients need observable closure. With several risks require the corresponding observable exposure-in-span factorization and a specified risk-premium vector.

Under these closure hypotheses the one-risk observable pricing equation is

`C_{i,t}+b_obs^Q·D_ξC_i+½tr(A_obs D²_(x,ξ)C_i)+r(xC_{i,x}−C_i)=0`.

The drift of Ξ is risk adjusted and need not lose its income-drift dependence. If coefficient-level closure fails, retain the full-state equations and require any price-specific holdings and generator expressions to descend before asserting an observable demand/price. Some particular claims can descend without all coefficients doing so; no general impossibility theorem is inferred from failure of the sufficient closure conditions. A partial-observation/filtering extension must specify and construct its actual observed state and filtration before claiming a closed pricing theorem.

M0.2 and M3.4 give the complete-market conclusion only under their one-risk filtration, true-density, representation and admissibility hypotheses. With `d>1` one stock has rank at most one; replicated claims must have exposure in its traded span. A complete multi-risk extension specifies further traded assets and full-rank volatility/representation. A jump extension must separately supply jump-Itô terms, state preservation and admissibility. State elimination or closure is required for every scalar PDE. Specify existence, local uniqueness and stopping tolerance for the solution branch near the smooth PDE solution.
