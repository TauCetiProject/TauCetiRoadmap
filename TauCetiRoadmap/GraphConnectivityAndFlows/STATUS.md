### Named results

- **The max-flow/min-cut theorem**: for nonnegative capacities over any linearly ordered additive group, some flow's value equals some cut's capacity, with integrality in any subgroup containing the capacities ([`TauCeti.Flow.exists_val_eq_arrowSum`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Network/ShortestAugmentation.html#TauCeti.Flow.exists_val_eq_arrowSum)). It now also holds for simple graphs through the doubled quiver ([`TauCeti.DoubledQuiver.exists_flow_val_eq_pairCutCapacity_mem`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/DoubledQuiver.html#TauCeti.DoubledQuiver.exists_flow_val_eq_pairCutCapacity_mem)).
- **Menger's theorem**: the largest number of internally disjoint paths between two nonadjacent vertices equals the least size of a separating vertex set ([`SimpleGraph.exists_paths_vertexSeparator_card_eq`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/Menger.html#SimpleGraph.exists_paths_vertexSeparator_card_eq)). It is also proved in edge, set-to-set, capacitated and directed forms, and globally, as with `k`-arc-strong arrow families ([`TauCeti.isArcStrong_iff_exists_paths`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Network/Connectivity/Basic.html#TauCeti.isArcStrong_iff_exists_paths)).
- **The Gomory–Hu theorem**: every symmetric submodular function on a nonempty finite type has a cut tree whose path minima give all pairwise minimum cut values ([`TauCeti.TerminalCut.exists_isCutTree`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Network/MinimumCut/GomoryHu.html#TauCeti.TerminalCut.exists_isCutTree)). For a finite simple graph with unit capacities, the least tree-edge weight is the edge connectivity ([`TauCeti.TerminalCut.IsFlowEquivalentTree.exists_edge_simpleGraph_edgeConnectivity_eq_weight`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/CutTree.html#TauCeti.TerminalCut.IsFlowEquivalentTree.exists_edge_simpleGraph_edgeConnectivity_eq_weight)).
- **Robbins' theorem**: a multigraph with finitely many edges has a strongly connected orientation exactly when it is 2-edge-connected ([`Graph.exists_stronglyConnected_orientation_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Orientation/StronglyConnected.html#Graph.exists_stronglyConnected_orientation_iff)). The classical bridgeless form for simple graphs is also proved ([`SimpleGraph.Preconnected.exists_stronglyConnected_orientation_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Orientation/StronglyConnected.html#SimpleGraph.Preconnected.exists_stronglyConnected_orientation_iff)).
- **Kőnig's edge-colouring theorem**: a finite bipartite multigraph is `k`-edge-colourable exactly when no vertex meets more than `k` edges ([`Graph.IsBipartition.nonempty_edgeColoring_iff_forall_incidenceCount_le`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Matching/EdgeColoring.html#Graph.IsBipartition.nonempty_edgeColoring_iff_forall_incidenceCount_le)). It rests on the capacitated Kőnig and Hall theorems.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Residual updates** ([`TauCeti.residualUpdate`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Network/Residual.html#TauCeti.residualUpdate)): one operation that updates any feasible assignment along residual arrows. Augmentation, the minimum-cut theory and the circulation adjustments are all special cases of it.
- **Ear decompositions as data** ([`Graph.EarDecomposition`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Graph/Connectivity/Ear/Basic.html#Graph.EarDecomposition), [`TauCeti.Network.EarDecomposition`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/Network/Ear/Basic.html#TauCeti.Network.EarDecomposition)): ears keep their prefixes and edge identities. They now transport along isomorphisms as well as between multigraphs, simple graphs and `Digraph`.
- **The doubled-quiver network** ([`TauCeti.DoubledQuiver.network`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Connectivity/DoubledQuiver.html#TauCeti.DoubledQuiver.network)): it identifies a simple graph's bidirected network with the doubled quiver. Flows, walks, cut capacities, and arc and vertex connectivity then pass between simple graphs and the directed theory.
