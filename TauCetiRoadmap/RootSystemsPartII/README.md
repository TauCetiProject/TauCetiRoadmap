# Roadmap: root systems, integral dominance and finite diagram folding, part II

**Summary.** This continuation of RootSystems builds integral dominance and finite diagram folding on its positive roots, Weyl groups, chambers and Cartan–Killing classification. It proves the Stembridge positive-root step, constructs the orbit-sum fold of every finite reduced crystallographic based root pairing, compares its integral cone and dominant chamber with the invariant original ones, and obtains an existential positive-coroot orbit step in the actual cocharacter lattice, with central directions and torsion retained. The fold includes orthogonal simple orbits, the adjacent reversal of even A type and arbitrary cycles of components. The combinatorial endpoint supplies the root choice needed in Kisin–Zhou, Proposition 5.2.3; constructing the geometric curve and relative root-subgroup maps belongs to the geometric consumer (KZ25, author preprint §5.2, pp. 49–52, KZ25 line 2179).

## Scope and ownership

RootSystems, Layers 1–6, stops at finite root combinatorics, Coxeter theory, chambers, classification and numbered integral root data. This Part II extends it in the direction of integral dominance and finite orbit-sum folding. The positive monoid, chambers, finite dominant intervals, classification, numbered Cartan matrices, diagram permutations, type-A datum and real base change are already in the library; their exact declarations are in the supplier contracts. They are inputs, not construction targets here. In particular, the numbered B₂, F₄ and G₂ rigidity cases are supplied by the library; §3.1 extends them to arbitrary irreducible pairings and the Bₙ/Cₙ families rather than reconstructing those three finite cases. LieHighestWeight, Layers 2 and 6, owns the general weight-lattice, fundamental-weight and highest-weight dictionary. We use an explicitly specified integral subgroup and simple-coroot values rather than construct a second dictionary.

ReductiveGroups §9A.1b, *Pinned fixed Lie algebras and signed ADE folding*, owns signed ADE fixed Lie algebras, their normalized folded presentations, distinct-sum versus projection/full-sum maps, the invariant simple-root lattice basis, and component-return transport. Their ADE constructions are imported by that section. Layers 2–3 below extend these interfaces to arbitrary finite real `RootPairing` carriers and arbitrary crystallographic component types, identifying the **unsigned distinct orbit-sum** root set and its positive-root lifts. They do not construct signed Lie fixed lattices, a second ADE presentation, or a second component-return inverse. In particular, an orbit sum and a root of a normalized fixed Lie algebra need different root/coroot comparison maps.

ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.1.4, *Minuscule coweights and the dominance order*, and §RG2.4.3, *Dominant inertia-coinvariant cocharacters*, state coweight dominance through the positive monoid of the flipped base. Layer 1 gives that same relation its translation and coordinate API; there is one order, with an explicit compatibility statement. ReductiveGroupsPartII owns the actual reductive-group, échelonnage and inertia-coinvariant realizations. It supplies their hypotheses before the abstract export of §4.4 is applied; the export does not identify absolute and relative coroot lattices without a specified realization.

Dominant subtraction, affine Bruhat/Demazure theory, twisted Newton reduction and the affine action, as well as integral representation bounds and minuscule detection, are not part of this roadmap: they extend RootSystems in other directions, and none of them supplies an ingredient here. This roadmap owns the ordinary and invariant Stembridge steps, the irreducible diagram case theorem, fixed Weyl group comparison for the orthogonal fold, the general orbit-sum assembly, its chamber/cone comparisons, and the ambient lattice export.

## Conventions

Unless a subsection says otherwise, P is a finite reduced crystallographic real root pairing in perfectly paired real vector spaces E and E∨, its roots and coroots span, and b is a base. The root-index type and the two vector spaces may lie in independent universes; no countability assumption is made. Rank zero is allowed except in the irreducible case theorem, which explicitly assumes a nonempty root set. Write Δ = {αᵢ}, Q for the subgroup generated by all roots, and Q⁺ for `TauCeti.posRootCone P b`. A subgroup L ⊆ E containing Q only records where increments land; no integrality of its elements or σ-stability is assumed. Where integral simple-coroot values are assumed (§4.1), integrality at simple coroots is enough, since every coroot is an integral combination of the simple coroots. Dominant means the **closed** chamber `RootPairing.dominantChamber P b`, including its walls. Strict integral comparison means comparison plus inequality of endpoints.

Cartan entries have root rows and coroot columns: aᵢⱼ = ⟨αᵢ, αⱼ∨⟩. A based automorphism is `RootPairing.Aut` whose index permutation preserves b's support in both directions. Its weight map sends roots forwards. Its coweight map is contravariant; the forward geometric action on coroots is its inverse. For coroot dominance replace P and b by `RootPairing.flip` and `RootPairing.Base.flip`, throughout; the forward weight map on the dual pairing is the inverse of the original coweight map, and its contravariant map is the inverse of the original weight map. A witness for the row convention is A₃ reversal: outer sum followed by middle root gives off-diagonal entries −2 and −1. A witness for direction is D₄ triality: the root action cycles 1 → 3 → 4 → 1; its coweight map carries the coroot at 3 back to that at 1 (Mathlib `RootPairing.Hom`, `Hom.lean`, fields of that structure; Stem08, equation (2), p. 1, Stem08 line 30).

On the root span use `RootPairing.RootForm`, the sum of products of coroot evaluations. It is positive definite there and invariant under a based automorphism by reindexing the finite sum. It need not be positive definite on an ambient central summand. For displayed simply-laced length calculations rescale positively on each irreducible component to give every original root squared length 2; the scale is constant on a cycle of isomorphic components. This changes no coroot pairing. The fixed space E^σ is `LinearMap.fixedSubmodule` of the weight map, so its vectors satisfy σx = x and its inclusion into E is the native submodule inclusion. Root orbits are finite sets of distinct roots. Sum each element once. For a positive period m, the full m-term sum is m divided by the orbit size times the distinct sum; this integer quotient is used only when the orbit size divides m. There is no period-zero formula. For example, six triality iterates of an outer D₄ root give twice the three-member orbit sum (Stem08, p. 1, Stem08 line 24; §2.1).

## How to read the build

Layer 1 wraps the integral positive monoid and proves the ordinary step. Layer 2 specializes the distinct-orbit maps to native real pairings, transports the ADE orthogonal fold and identifies its fixed Weyl group. Layer 3 proves the diagram case theorem, handles adjacent even A and nonsimply-laced component cycles, then assembles the fold for every based automorphism. Layer 4 compares chambers and exact integral cones, applies Layer 1 in that fold, and exports the result to ambient and torsion-bearing cocharacter lattices. The fixed Weyl-group theorem is an independent deep output of Layer 2; the proof of the orbit-step endpoint only needs the root-system fold and the chamber/cone comparisons.

## Layer 1: integral dominance and the Stembridge step

### 1.1 Integral root dominance

Define integral root dominance x ≤ y on P and b by y − x ∈ Q⁺, stated literally as membership of the difference in `TauCeti.posRootCone P b`. On L this is integral root dominance; use the flipped pairing and base for coweights. It is an explicit relation, without an order instance on the ambient vector space. The construction specializes the shared coweight-order convention, and its coordinate API follows from natural simple-root coordinates and independence (Rap00, §1, pp. 153–154, Rap00 line 64; §2, p. 157, Rap00 line 172; `TauCeti.mem_posRootCone`).

- For any x,y, comparison holds exactly when y − x is a sum Σᵢ nᵢαᵢ with natural nᵢ over the simple support.
- The relation is reflexive, transitive and antisymmetric. Zero, addition and pointedness of Q⁺ give these laws, including rank zero.
- For any ambient vector z, comparing x + z and y + z is equivalent to comparing x and y.
- Every based root-pairing equivalence preserves and reflects comparison, with both endpoints mapped by its weight map.
- If x and y have integral simple-root coordinates aᵢ,bᵢ, comparison is equivalent to aᵢ ≤ bᵢ for every simple index.
- Integral comparison implies comparison in the rational positive cone. The converse requires the difference to have integral simple-root coordinates, even if both endpoints lie in L.
- For the flipped base, comparison means that the difference is a natural sum of simple coroots, exactly the coweight convention of ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.1.4 and §RG2.4.

**Checks.**

- In the PGL₂ cocharacter lattice ℤ, with positive coroot 2, 0 is below 2 and is not below 1. The rational positive ray contains 1; saturation would give the wrong answer.
- With empty simple support, Q⁺ = {0}, so comparison is equality even with independent central directions. A chamber-only definition would incorrectly compare all central vectors.
- In A₂ root coordinates, (0,1) is below (1,1), with difference (1,0); (1,0) and (0,1) are incomparable since their differences have a negative coordinate.
- For every x,y the test is literally membership in the native positive monoid; for x = y the difference is 0. The real conical hull of the simple roots admits half a simple root and is therefore larger than Q⁺; in A₁, topological closure alone leaves Q⁺ = ℕα unchanged.

*Needs:* `TauCeti.posRootCone`, `TauCeti.mem_posRootCone`, `TauCeti.isPointed_posRootCone`; `RootPairing.Base` and its independence; `RootPairing.Base.flip`; the order convention of ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.1.4.

### 1.2 The ordinary Stembridge step

Prove `stembridge_step`: for a finite reduced crystallographic real root pairing, a base b, and distinct dominant real λ,μ with λ ≤ μ in §1.1, there is a positive root β such that λ + β is dominant and λ < λ + β ≤ μ. This statement does not require the ambient modules to be spanned by roots, and no integrality of the endpoints is needed: the lattice statement of the source is the special case λ,μ ∈ L. If λ ∈ L and Q ⊆ L, then λ + β ∈ L (Rap00, Lemma 2.3, equations (2.10)–(2.13), pp. 157–158, Rap00 line 191).

One proof route writes μ − λ = Σ nᵢαᵢ and chooses a maximal positive root β = Σ mᵢαᵢ with mᵢ ≤ nᵢ. Such roots form a nonempty finite set: a nonzero coefficient supplies an eligible simple root. At a simple index i, maximality says either β + αᵢ is not a root or mᵢ = nᵢ. In the first case positivity excludes opposite roots, so the negative-pairing root-sum theorem gives ⟨β,αᵢ∨⟩ ≥ 0. In the second case express λ + β as μ minus the unused coefficients at the other simple roots; dominance of μ and the nonpositive off-diagonal Cartan entries give the same inequality. The remaining natural coefficients give the upper comparison. Positivity and pointedness give strictness. This argument uses only simple-coroot sign tests, never integrality of λ or μ, and therefore keeps central directions (Rap00 line 195 through Rap00 line 214).

**Checks.**

- In A₂, λ = 0 and μ = α₁ + α₂ have pairings (0,0) and (1,1). Either simple increment has pairings (2,−1) or (−1,2), whereas the highest-root increment has (1,1) and remaining budget 0.
- In the coroot version for PGL₂, the step from 0 to 2 is 2; the candidate 1 has remaining integral budget 1, outside 2ℕ.
- For equal endpoints the budget is 0 and no positive increment can fit. In rank zero the distinct-comparable hypotheses cannot hold.
- In A₁ the non-integral endpoints λ = α/10 and μ = 11α/10 have simple-coroot values 1/5 and 11/5, are dominant and differ by α; the step α lands on μ with budget 0. A hypothesis of integral endpoints would exclude this valid instance. By contrast 0 and α/2 are not comparable, since α/2 is not a natural sum of simple roots; an order through the rational cone would wrongly demand an increment.

*Needs:* §1.1; `TauCeti.exists_root_eq_sum_nat_of_mem_posRoots`; finiteness of the root set; `RootPairing.root_add_root_mem_of_pairingIn_neg` with its nonopposite hypothesis; `RootPairing.Base.cartanMatrix_le_zero_of_ne`; `TauCeti.exists_intCast_eq_coroot'_of_mem_posRootCone` for the lattice compatibility; `RootPairing.Base.flip` for the coroot version.

### Examples

For the A₁ root lattice ℤα with ⟨α,α∨⟩ = 2, the endpoints 0 and 2α admit the step α with remaining budget α. In the PGL₂ cocharacter coordinates the same coroot vector is 2, not 1. The difference between these coordinate presentations is a lattice normalization, not a different order (Rap00, §2, pp. 156–158, Rap00 line 172 and Rap00 line 191).

### Dependencies

This layer uses the Mathlib base and Cartan-sign/root-sum declarations and Tau Ceti positive-monoid and chamber declarations in the supplier contracts; RootSystems Layers 1 and 4 supply their root-combinatorial and chamber setting. ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.1.4 fixes the dual convention. No folding, affine theory or representation theory is used in the ordinary step.

## Layer 2: orthogonal diagram folding

### 2.1 Distinct root-orbit sums

Specialize the distinct-orbit sum of ReductiveGroups §9A.1b to a real root pairing. Define `orbitIndices` at P, σ and i to be the finite set of distinct forward iterates of i under σ's index permutation, and `orbitSum` at the same arguments to be the sum of their root vectors. Thus its source is a root index and its target is E; for a based σ its value lies in the fixed subspace E^σ. No exponent or orbit length is additional input. On the root span σ has finite order because it permutes a finite spanning set. For a simple orbit O write βₒ = Σᵢ∈O αᵢ (Stem08, p. 1, Stem08 line 24; ReductiveGroups §9A.1b, distinct-sum comparison).

- An orbit set contains its starting index, is stable under σ and is unchanged by moving to another member of the orbit; its cardinality is positive.
- Every orbit sum is σ-fixed and independent of its representative. For a fixed root it equals that root.
- If σᵐi = i and m > 0, the orbit size d divides m and the m-term sum is (m/d) times the distinct sum. Equality with the distinct sum requires m = d, unless additional cancellation makes the vector equality hold.
- For a positive root in a based orbit, the sum is a nonzero member of Q⁺: every summand is positive and the simple coefficients cannot all cancel.
- A based equivalence intertwining σ and τ sends each orbit set bijectively to the corresponding orbit set and its sum to the corresponding sum.
- The map from root orbits to vectors need not be injective; define `foldedIndices` as its image set in E, used as a subtype for the injectively indexed folded roots. Its membership means being an actual orbit sum, and two different orbits with the same vector give one index.

**Checks.**

- For σ = id the orbit set is {i} and its sum is αᵢ. Summing an arbitrary supplied period would incorrectly multiply it.
- A₂ reversal has the simple orbit {α₁,α₂} with sum α₁ + α₂. The average is half that vector and has the wrong integral budget.
- D₄ triality has a three-member outer orbit. A full period-six sum is 2(α₁ + α₃ + α₄), whereas the distinct sum is α₁ + α₃ + α₄.
- In A₂ the fixed highest-root orbit and the two-member simple-root orbit both sum to α₁ + α₂. Its six roots have four orbits but only two distinct image vectors, ±(α₁ + α₂). An orbit-indexed root embedding without taking the image would fail injectivity.
- For the identity on A₂, all six singleton-orbit sums are distinct; the image set has six elements, and zero is absent. For the empty system the image set is empty, rather than containing the empty sum: its source has no root index.
- For A₄ reversal, the twenty roots have twelve orbits but eight distinct image vectors. In particular f₁ = α₁ + α₂ + α₃ + α₄ has both a singleton and a two-member lift, and 2f₁ is absent; the image is neither the orbit quotient nor the monoid generated by the sums.

*Needs:* `RootPairing.Aut`; finite root indices; `RootPairing.Base`; `TauCeti.exists_root_eq_sum_nat_of_mem_posRoots`; `TauCeti.isPointed_posRootCone`; ReductiveGroups §9A.1b's distinct-sum map, for agreement with its ADE specialization. L need not be chosen to define these maps; if Q ⊆ L, all values belong to L, since an orbit sum is a sum of roots.

### 2.2 The orthogonal fold on a native real pairing

Assume every irreducible component is simply laced and the distinct simple roots within each σ-orbit are pairwise orthogonal. Transport the ADE distinct-sum root-pairing and root/coroot comparison of ReductiveGroups §9A.1b to E^σ. Construct its based realization Ψ with root set exactly {Σ(σ,α) : α ∈ Φ}, taking the **image** to remove duplicates, and simple base {βₒ : O a simple orbit}. The form is the restriction of the invariant positive form, with the component normalization of the conventions. Positivity is precisely the image of positive original roots (Stem08, condition (1), equation (2), Claims 1 and 4, pp. 1–3, Stem08 line 21, Stem08 line 30, Stem08 line 41 and Stem08 line 106).

A realization `OrbitSumRealization` bundles a native root pairing on the fixed space E^σ (`LinearMap.fixedSubmodule` of the weight map) and its real linear dual, with root-index type `foldedIndices`, a native base, spanning/reduced/crystallographic conditions, the evaluation pairing, and literal root-vector and simple-support equations. This is a specification of the realization, with no assumed chamber, cone or Weyl comparison. Its root inclusion carries each index to that index’s ambient vector; its base support consists exactly of indices arising from simple-root orbit sums.

For original squared lengths 2, prove (βₒ,βₒ) = 2|O| and, for distinct simple orbits O,T, ⟨βₒ,βₜ∨⟩ = −Nₒₜ/|T|, where Nₒₜ counts edges joining the blocks. This is an integer because each vertex of T has the same number of neighbors in O. The denominator is nonzero since T is an orbit. The base vectors are independent and span E^σ, as fixed simple coordinates are constant on orbits (Stem08, equations (2)–(3), p. 1, Stem08 line 29).

- The transported root set is exactly the distinct-sum image, and every positive folded root has a positive original-root lift.
- Its simple roots are the βₒ, indexed by simple orbits; its signs and integral expansions agree with this base.

- Its norms and oriented Cartan entries are those above; no interchange of the denominator's block is permitted.
- Reflection in βₒ restricts the commuting product of original simple reflections indexed by O to E^σ. Each factor fixes the other simple roots in O.
- The identity automorphism gives the original based system, including the empty system on zero space.
- Based isometries intertwining automorphisms induce based equivalences of the realizations, carrying each orbit-sum vector to its image.

The realization equations determine its coroot functionals and reflections, so the comparisons apply to any bundled realization with these root vectors and base. This is consumed, not re-derived: for two realizations the evaluation fields identify their perfect pairings and the root-vector fields identify their root embeddings, so `RootPairing.IsRootSystem.ext` identifies the two root pairings (the folded index type is finite, ℝ is a characteristic-zero domain, and both are root systems by their fields). The simple-support equations then identify the supports, and a base is determined by its support since its remaining fields are propositions. Coroot-by-coroot, the same uniqueness is `Module.Dual.eq_of_preReflection_mapsTo` applied to the finite spanning folded root set.

For the unsigned-root characterization, a proof route uses positive height descent under the block reflections. A positive nonzero orbit sum has positive squared norm. A block with positive pairing lowers its height; if the corresponding positive orbit becomes negative, it was the simple orbit of that block. The block action permutes root orbits, so every orbit sum is generated from a simple block sum. Conversely the generated roots are orbit sums. Under the finite hypotheses, the same descent shows every original root orbit is orthogonal, not just the simple ones. This finite positivity step is essential; it cannot be imported for an affine system (Stem08, proof of Claim 1 and Claim 4, p. 3, Stem08 line 87 through Stem08 line 107).

### 2.3 The fixed Weyl group

Define `fixedWeylGroup` as the inverse image in the native Weyl group of the centralizer of σ in the root-pairing automorphism group. Membership is exactly commutation with σ; its inclusion is the native subgroup inclusion, and products and inverses retain that commutation equation. For any `OrbitSumRealization` under the orthogonal-fold hypotheses, prove `fixed_weyl_group`: restriction W(Φ)^σ → W(Ψ) is an isomorphism whose inverse sends reflection in βₒ to the product of the commuting simple reflections in O. Here W(Φ)^σ means the elements fixed by **conjugation** by σ, equivalently commuting with σ; it does not mean the pointwise stabilizer of E^σ (Stem08, Claim 3, pp. 2–3, Stem08 line 57).

- Membership of a vector in the fixed space is exactly the equation σx = x; its inclusion into E preserves addition and scalar multiplication.
- Sums and scalar multiples of fixed vectors are fixed, including zero, so the carrier is a submodule rather than an invariant quotient.
- The fixed-space inclusion is injective and identifies its image with the fixed-point set; for identity it is the whole space.
- Membership in the fixed Weyl subgroup means wσ = σw in the original automorphism group, equivalently σwσ⁻¹ = w.
- Its inclusion preserves identity, products and inverses, with the original Weyl action on E.
- The product or inverse of commuting-with-σ Weyl elements again commutes with σ. Identity σ gives the entire native Weyl group; restriction to the fixed submodule is a group action.

For surjectivity, the block reflection calculation supplies every folded simple generator. For exhaustion of the fixed subgroup, choose a simple inversion of a nonidentity fixed element. Every simple in its σ-orbit is an inversion. Right multiplication by each reflection in that block drops the inversion cardinality by one; the commuting factors fix the other simple roots in the block, so the total drop is exactly |O|. Induction gives generation by block products without a new parabolic normal-form theorem. For injectivity, choose a strictly dominant vector by the nonsingular simple-coroot matrix and sum its finite σ-orbit. This produces a strictly dominant fixed vector. An element trivial on E^σ fixes that vector and hence is identity by the wall-stabilizer theorem (Stem08, proof of Claim 3, Stem08 line 71; the specified inversion and stabilizer supplier declarations).

**Checks.**

- For A₃ reversal the fixed group has order 8 and acts faithfully on its two-dimensional fixed space. The block product s₁s₃ is an involution restricting to reflection in α₁ + α₃.
- For D₄ triality the fixed group has order 12. The product of the three commuting outer simple reflections has one inversion removed per factor, a total drop of 3 in the indicated descent.
- For identity on A₂ the group is W(A₂) ≃ S₃ of order 6, a nonabelian example: conjugation-fixed means every Weyl element, whereas the pointwise stabilizer of the whole root space is {1}.
- In rank zero both groups are trivial, of order 1, and the fixed root span has dimension 0. For identity on A₁, the fixed space is the whole one-dimensional root span and the fixed Weyl group has order 2. No nonexistent strictly dominant root index is selected.
- A₂ reversal has fixed vectors (t,t) in simple coordinates, a one-dimensional submodule; the anti-invariant vector (1,−1) maps to (−1,1) and is excluded. For σ = id the inclusion of the fixed space is identity. These test the defining fixedness equation rather than an arbitrary invariant quotient.

*Needs:* §2.2's reflection comparison; `RootPairing.finite_weylGroup`; `RootPairing.weylGroupToPerm_injective` on the spanning space; `TauCeti.exists_mem_support_mem_inversions_of_ne_one`; `TauCeti.ncard_inversions_mul_ofIdx_of_mem`; `TauCeti.stabilizer_eq_closure_wallReflections`; `TauCeti.wallReflections_eq_empty_of_mem_openDominantChamber`; nonsingularity of the simple-coroot matrix. ## Layer 3: all finite based diagram automorphisms

### 3.1 The irreducible case theorem

For a nonempty irreducible finite reduced crystallographic spanning real root system and a based automorphism τ, prove two statements. If the Cartan matrix is not simply laced, τ fixes every simple root and hence every root index; this is `indexEquiv_eq_self_of_multiple_edge`. If the Cartan matrix is simply laced, either every simple orbit is orthogonal, or the system has type A₂ₙ with n ≥ 1 and τ reverses its numbered simple roots; this is `simpleOrbit_pairing_eq_zero_or_typeA_even`. In the exceptional case the adjacent middle pair {αₙ,αₙ₊₁} is the only nonorthogonal simple orbit. The second statement supplies a simultaneous Cartan numbering and its reversal equation, not merely the name A₂ₙ (derived from the numbered matrices and tree supplier contracts; Stem08, condition (1), p. 1, Stem08 line 12 through Stem08 line 22, specifies the orthogonality condition being classified).

A proof route uses the finite diagram tree and the classification. The centre of a finite tree is one vertex or two adjacent vertices, and every automorphism preserves it; so a tree automorphism either fixes some vertex of the centre (this includes a two-vertex centre fixed pointwise, as for the D₅ fork exchange) or the centre has two vertices and it exchanges them. In the first case it preserves distance from the fixed vertex, and adjacent vertices of a tree have distances from any vertex differing by exactly one, so adjacent vertices cannot be in the same orbit. In the second case, inspecting the valid finite types leaves the even A path and its reversal: types D and E have a unique branch vertex, while the nonsimply-laced paths have a unique oriented multiple edge. For numbered B₂, F₄ and G₂ use the three supplied rigidity theorems; on the remaining Bₙ/Cₙ paths even a graph reversal fails Cartan preservation. A numbering transports τ into the numbered symmetry group by its entrywise criterion; reversal is independent of a change of A-type numbering since the path's two symmetries commute. The classification and diagram carriers themselves are inputs, not conclusions of this case analysis (the exact pinned tree, symmetry and type declarations in the supplier contracts).

**Checks.**

- A₃ reversal fixes its middle vertex. Its paired simple roots have pairing 0; the central singleton contributes no distinct pair.
- A₄ reversal exchanges the central edge. The middle orbit has pairing −1, the outer orbit pairing 0; this selects n = 2 in the exceptional alternative.
- D₄ triality, the Dₙ fork exchange and E₆ reversal fix the branch vertex. In D₄ the three outer pairings are all 0, though the permutation has order 3.
- In B₂ and G₂ the attempted node exchange changes off-diagonal entries (−2,−1) to (−1,−2), and (−3,−1) to (−1,−3). It is a graph permutation but not an allowed based automorphism.

### 3.3 Component cycles and their return maps

### 3.4 The general finite orbit-sum fold

- Its simple base is indexed by simple σ-orbits with vectors βₒ.

## Layer 4: invariant dominance and coroot orbit steps

### 4.1 Integral weights and the folded chamber

Prove `invariant_weight_transport` for any `OrbitSumRealization` of the general fold of §3.4. Let λ ∈ E^σ. If every original simple-coroot value is an integer, prove the same for every folded simple-coroot value. More precisely, original values are constant, say dₒ, on each simple orbit O. The folded value is dₒ for an orthogonal orbit and 2dₒ for the transported adjacent-middle even-A orbit. Thus λ is original-dominant if and only if it is folded-dominant. Chamber equivalence holds for all fixed real λ; only the integrality assertion needs integral dₒ. There is no converse assertion that every folded integral weight is original-integral (derived from §§2.2, 3.2–3.4; Stem08 equation (2), p. 1, Stem08 line 29; KZ25, proof of Proposition 5.2.3, pp. 49–50, KZ25 line 2197, is the application requiring this integral comparison).

For an orthogonal orbit of common original squared length l², compute (βₒ,βₒ) = |O|l² and 2(λ,βₒ) = |O|l²dₒ; their ratio is dₒ. In the adjacent pair the denominator is half this orthogonal value, giving 2dₒ. Diagonal transport multiplies numerator and denominator by the same positive integer c. Every denominator is positive, since a simple-orbit sum is nonzero and the root-span form is positive definite. These multipliers are positive integers 1 or 2, giving forward integrality and equality of signs without dividing an integral value by 2.

**Checks.**

- A₃ outer-orbit value d and middle value e become d,e. In a two-component cycle both bilinear numerator and denominator double, so these values stay d,e.
- Original values (−1,−1) in A₂ give folded value −2. Both chambers reject them, testing the reverse implication as well as the forward one.

The coefficients are unique. The same statement with integer coefficients identifies Q^σ with the embedded folded root lattice. Consequently integral dominance of any two invariant endpoints agrees with folded integral dominance; no integrality of the endpoints is needed for this equivalence, since it concerns only their difference (derived from the invariant simple-root basis of ReductiveGroups §9A.1b and §3.3's extension to every crystallographic component; KZ25, §5.2, KZ25 line 2197 through KZ25 line 2206, motivates retaining the integer budget).

### 4.3 The existential orbit-sum step

Prove `invariant_step`. Let P be finite, reduced, crystallographic and spanning, b a base, σ a based automorphism, and λ,μ distinct σ-fixed dominant real weights with λ ≤ μ in the integral order; no integrality of the endpoints is assumed. Then there is a positive original root α whose distinct orbit sum β is nonzero, λ + β is fixed and dominant, and λ < λ + β ≤ μ. If λ ∈ L and Q ⊆ L, then β and λ + β belong to L; no σ-stability of L is needed. The conclusion selects an orbit; it makes no assertion about the sum of an arbitrarily selected individually eligible orbit (Rap00, Lemma 2.3, pp. 157–158, Rap00 line 191; Stem08, Claim 4, p. 3, Stem08 line 106; the finite extension of §3.4; KZ25, proof of Proposition 5.2.3, pp. 49–50, KZ25 line 2197 through KZ25 line 2206).

A proof route puts the endpoints into the finite fold by the chamber equivalence of §4.1, which holds for all fixed real vectors, and the cone equivalence of §4.2, and applies §1.2 there. Lift the resulting positive folded root to a positive original root using §3.4's root-image characterization. A based action preserves signs, so a negative original orbit cannot give a positive folded root. The orbit sum lies in Q ⊆ L, is fixed and retains both the dominant inequalities and the residual integral cone budget. Nonzero positivity gives strictness. Apply this to the **coroot** pairing for the cocharacter step; original roots then test dominance. Relative root-subgroup maps and the selection of a geometric short restricted-root lift are consumer interfaces, not supplied by a bare finite root choice.

**Checks.**

- A₂ reversal with endpoints 0,f, f = α₁ + α₂, admits the two-simple-root orbit sum f, leaving budget 0. The fixed highest-root orbit is another lift of the same valid increment.
- In D₄ with central node 2 and triality on 1,3,4, take λ = 2ρ and μ = λ + θ, where θ = (1,2,1,1) in simple-root coordinates. Endpoint simple-coroot values are (2,2,2,2) and (2,3,2,2). Their sum (1,3,1,1) leaves budget (0,−1,0,0), which fails. The fixed root θ gives a valid alternative, with remaining budget 0.
- In rank one with identity automorphism, an orbit has one member, so the theorem gives exactly the ordinary root step. With no roots, distinct comparable endpoints do not exist.

*Needs:* §1.2; §2.1; §3.4's folded root system and positive-root lift; §4.1's chamber equivalence (its integrality assertion is not used); §4.2's exact cone equivalence; `TauCeti.isPointed_posRootCone`. The fixed-Weyl isomorphism, affine theory and geometric curve construction are not required by this route.

### 4.4 Ambient cocharacters, central directions and torsion

Prove `ambient_coroot_step` for a finite reduced crystallographic real root pairing P in finite-dimensional perfectly paired spaces V,V∨, without the spanning mixin. Let σ preserve the base, let L ⊆ V be an additive subgroup containing every root, and let distinct λ,μ ∈ L be fixed and dominant, with μ − λ ∈ Q⁺. There is a positive-root orbit sum β ≠ 0 with λ + β ∈ L fixed and dominant, λ < λ + β ≤ μ, and the same central component as λ and μ. Neither σ-stability of L nor integrality of L or of the endpoints is assumed: λ + β ∈ L because β is a sum of roots. No finite-order condition is imposed on σ on the center. Apply P equal to a flipped cocharacter root pairing to obtain the positive-**coroot** step in the actual cocharacter space (derived from §4.3 and root-span positivity; Rap00, Lemma 2.3, Rap00 line 191; KZ25, §5.2, KZ25 line 2197).

Put E = spanℝ Φ and Z equal to the common kernel of the simple-coroot functionals. Nonsingularity of their evaluation matrix on a simple basis gives V = E ⊕ Z and projection p : V → E preserving every simple-coroot value. Both summands are σ-stable (σ permutes the simple roots, and its contravariant coweight map permutes the simple coroots), so pλ,pμ are fixed dominant vectors of E. Their difference is μ − λ, which is nonzero and has its original integral budget. Apply §4.3 to the root-span restriction P_E below, with σ restricted to E; the projected endpoints need not lie in L. Its orbit increment lies in Q ⊆ L. Add it to the **original** λ. This keeps its Z component, gives the same simple-coroot values and leaves the original budget below μ. The positive form is used on E, not asserted positive definite on V.

The root-span restriction. Let P_E be `RootPairing.restrictScalars` of P with both scalar fields ℝ: a root system on E and the coroot span E′ ⊆ V∨, with the same root index type, roots, coroots and pairings. Prove that P_E is reduced and crystallographic, that b's support with b's fields restricted is a base of P_E, that σ restricts to a based automorphism of P_E with the same index permutation (its weight map is σ on E, its coweight map the coweight map on E′), and that for x ∈ E the simple-coroot values, membership in the dominant chamber and membership in Q⁺ computed in P_E agree with those computed in P; consequently the orbit sums of P_E are those of P. Each statement transfers through the inclusions E → V and E′ → V∨, the pairing equalities of `RootPairing.restrictScalars_pairing` and linear independence in a submodule (the root-span supplier contract; `RootPairing.Base`).

- Root and coroot embeddings of P_E are the original ones viewed in the spans, and its pairing on E × E′ is the restriction of the perfect pairing of P.
- Reducedness, crystallographicity, the base with support b's support and the restricted automorphism are as stated; the Cartan matrix of that base equals b's.
- For x ∈ E the dominant chamber and the positive monoid of P_E consist of the same vectors as those of P intersected with E.

**Checks.**

- For GL₂ with roots and coroots ±(1,−1) in ℝ² under the dot product, E and E′ are the lines through (1,−1) and P_E is A₁; the vector (1,−1) has simple-coroot value 2 in both P and P_E, and (−1,1) has value −2 in both, so it lies in neither chamber. Pairing E with all of V∨ instead of E′ would not be perfect, since the central functional (1,1) vanishes on E.
- For a torus E = 0 and P_E is the empty root system on zero space; Q⁺ = {0} on both sides.
- For a spanning P, E = V and P_E agrees with P root by root and pairing by pairing; its base has the same support. A restriction that rescaled the pairing would change the Cartan entry 2 on the diagonal.

Prove `torsion_lattice_coroot_step` on an abelian group A, allowing torsion, with specified root lifts r : ι → A, an additive real realization f : A → V, and an additive automorphism τ of A. Assume f(rᵢ) = αᵢ; f is injective on the subgroup generated by all rᵢ; f(τx) = σ(fx) for every x; and τ(rᵢ) = r at the index σ(i). Let λ,μ ∈ A be distinct and **actually τ-fixed**, let their realized vectors be dominant, and require μ − λ to be in the additive monoid generated by the lifted simple roots in A. Then some positive-root orbit gives β = Σⱼ∈O rⱼ ≠ 0, with λ + β actually fixed, dominant after realization, and both β and μ − (λ + β) in that same integral monoid in A. These are the precise hypotheses for applying the export to X_*(T)_I; a torsion class is never recovered uniquely from its real image (ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.4.3, *Dominant inertia-coinvariant cocharacters*; the lift is derived from the ambient theorem and injectivity on the root subgroup).

For this lift, realize the exact coefficient budget. If fμ = fλ, injectivity on the root subgroup would force μ − λ = 0, contradicting distinctness; thus the ambient step applies. Lift its actual orbit sum, not an arbitrary real representative. Equivariance on the root lifts gives exact fixedness, and injectivity on their generated subgroup identifies the remaining natural simple-root combination with μ − λ − β in A. All torsion in the original endpoint is retained. No splitting of the whole abelian group or passage to a saturated root lattice is used.

**Checks.**

- For a torus Q⁺ = {0}, so comparable endpoints are equal, even when σ acts nontrivially on the center. With σ acting by multiplication by 2 on a real central line, its order is infinite, but the root-step implication still has no distinct comparable endpoints.
- For GL₂, λ = (c,c), μ = (c+1,c−1) differ by the coroot (1,−1). Both have coordinate sum 2c, and the selected increment has sum 0; the central coordinate is preserved.
- For PGL₂ the positive coroot is 2 in ℤ. The comparison 0 ≤ 1 fails; tensoring with ℝ does not authorize a half-coroot in the original lattice.
- In A = ℤ ⊕ ℤ/2 with root lift r = (2,0), f(n,t) = n and identity action, λ = (0,1), μ = (2,1) have step (2,0), landing at (2,1). Their common torsion coordinate 1 survives. Endpoints (0,0),(0,1) are distinct with equal real images but have no positive-root difference in A, so the theorem does not apply.
- The map τ(n,t) = (n,t+n mod 2) has identity real action and fixes the root lift (2,0), yet sends (1,0) to (1,1). Real fixedness alone does not satisfy the endpoint-fixedness hypothesis.

*Needs:* §4.3 applied to the root-span restriction; `RootPairing.restrictScalars`, its root, coroot and pairing equations and its root-system instance, with balancedness from the instances of `Finite/Nondegenerate.lean` under `Fintype.ofFinite`; `RootPairing.Base`; the simple-coroot evaluation matrix and its nonsingularity; `RootPairing.posRootForm_rootFormIn_posDef`; `RootPairing.Base.flip`; the actual additive maps, root lifts and injectivity for the torsion form. ReductiveGroupsPartII §RG2.4 supplies the group-theoretic realization when A is its coinvariant cocharacter lattice. It must verify these hypotheses rather than infer them from an unrelated absolute lattice.

### Examples

In A₂ reversal the invariant original weight lattice lands in the even part of the folded A₁ weight lattice, yet the positive integral root cones agree exactly. These are distinct statements: a folded weight of pairing 1 is not an original integral invariant weight, whereas the simple folded root f is an original integral invariant root sum. In D₄, the bad orbit (1,3,1,1) and the good fixed root θ = (1,2,1,1) have different central coefficients despite each member of the bad orbit fitting separately. The ambient GL₂ and torsion examples show that the exported increment belongs to the original group, with its original central and torsion data (§§4.1–4.4; KZ25 line 2197 through KZ25 line 2206).

### Dependencies

The weight comparison uses the root/base/form outputs of §§2.2 and 3.2–3.4. The cone comparison uses simple-coordinate independence and the shared native monoid. The invariant step uses these two comparisons and the ordinary Stembridge step. The ambient export adds the root-span/central-kernel splitting, and its exact torsion form adds an injective, equivariant realization of the root subgroup. No later consumer supplies a prerequisite for these conclusions.

## Downstream consumers

Reductive root geometry uses the coroot export to select an invariant dominant increment before choosing a relative short root and constructing root-subgroup curves. Kisin–Zhou, Proposition 5.2.3 and Corollary 5.2.7, require this kind of finite combinatorial input but also geometric and descent results outside its scope (KZ25, pp. 49–53, KZ25 line 2179 and KZ25 line 2332). Continuations of RootSystems in the directions of dominant subtraction and Demazure reduction, and of minuscule bounds, can consume the integral order of §1.1; they supply no ingredient to the finite fold.

## Worked examples

**A₂ and the middle factor.** Normalize (α₁,α₁) = (α₂,α₂) = 2 and (α₁,α₂) = −1. Then f = α₁ + α₂ has norm squared 2, and a fixed λ with original values (d,d) has folded value 2d. In particular λ = f/2 has folded value 1 and original values (1/2,1/2). For endpoints 0 and f, the folded step f has a fixed highest-root lift and a two-member simple-root lift. This distinguishes forward weight integrality, exact cone equality and noninjectivity of the orbit map (§§2.1, 3.2, 4.1–4.3).

**D₄ and the orbit budget.** With outer nodes 1,3,4 and center 2, the outer sum has norm 6, the center norm 2 and inner product −3, hence entries −3,−1. A period-six sum doubles the outer sum. For the endpoints of §4.3 the three translates of α₁ + α₂ fit individually but consume central budget 3 rather than the available 2 when summed. The fixed highest root consumes exactly (1,2,1,1), giving residual 0. Thus the finite combinatorial inference from individual translate bounds to a sum bound is false. The theorem provides the stronger **existential choice**, without asserting a counterexample to the geometric proposition (KZ25, author preprint proof of Proposition 5.2.3, pp. 49–50, KZ25 line 2197 through KZ25 line 2206; §§2.2, 4.3).

**Central coordinates and parity.** In PGL₂ the root budget is 2ℕ inside ℤ. In GL₂ a coroot step preserves the sum of coordinates. In a torus comparison is equality. In ℤ ⊕ ℤ/2 with root (2,0), an integral step retains the original torsion coordinate. These four computations prevent replacing the actual cocharacter group by its rationalized or semisimple image (Rap00, p. 157, Rap00 line 172; §4.4).
