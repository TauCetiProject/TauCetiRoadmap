# Roadmap: the lovász local lemma

**Summary.** This roadmap develops the Lovász local lemma: a finite family of events in a
probability space, each of which is independent of all but a few of the others, can be avoided
simultaneously with positive probability, with an explicit lower bound for that probability. It
states the lemma in its general, symmetric and lopsided forms, shows that the natural dependency
structure of events determined by independent random variables satisfies its hypothesis, and
derives the classical applications to two-colourings of hypergraphs, to satisfiability of
formulas in conjunctive normal form and to lower bounds for diagonal Ramsey numbers. Events are
measurable sets of a probability space, indexed by a finite set, and independence is mutual
independence of an event from a σ-algebra. No procedure for finding a good point is part of the
roadmap.

The headline theorems are these.

- *The general local lemma.* Let `A₁, …, Aₙ` be events and let `Γ(i)` be sets of indices such
  that each `Aᵢ` is independent of the σ-algebra generated by the events outside `Γ(i) ∪ {i}`. If
  numbers `xᵢ ≤ 1` satisfy `P(Aᵢ) ≤ xᵢ ∏_{j ∈ Γ(i)} (1 − xⱼ)` for every `i`, then the probability
  that no `Aᵢ` occurs is at least `∏ᵢ (1 − xᵢ)`. The same conclusion holds under the weaker,
  lopsided hypothesis that bounds `P(Aᵢ ∩ B)` by `xᵢ ∏_{j ∈ Γ(i)} (1 − xⱼ) P(B)` for every event
  `B` that is an intersection of complements of events outside `Γ(i) ∪ {i}`.
- *The symmetric local lemma.* If every `Γ(i)` has at most `d` elements, `P(Aᵢ ∩ B) ≤ p P(B)`
  for every `i` and every such intersection `B`, and `e · p · (d + 1) ≤ 1`, then with positive
  probability no event occurs. For a dependency digraph the hypothesis on `B` reduces to
  `P(Aᵢ) ≤ p`.
- *Applications.* A finite hypergraph whose edges have at least `k` vertices and each meet at most
  `d` other edges, with `2e(d + 1) ≤ 2^k`, has a two-colouring with no monochromatic edge. A
  formula in conjunctive normal form whose clauses have at least `k` variables and each share a
  variable with at most `d` other clauses, with `e(d + 1) ≤ 2^k`, is satisfiable. If
  `e · (binom(k, 2) · binom(n, k − 2) + 1) · 2 ≤ 2^binom(k, 2)`, then the diagonal Ramsey number
  `R(k, k)` exceeds `n` (Spencer's criterion).

The roadmap is built on Mathlib's independence of σ-algebras and of random variables and on its
product measures. It consumes the clauses and
satisfiability of PropositionalSatisfiability, whose scope leaves the local lemma outside, and the
Ramsey numbers and the finite Ramsey theorem of RamseyNumbers, whose Erdős counting bound is the
union-bound counterpart of the criterion here.

Layer 1 below proves the local lemma in its three forms. Layer 2 treats the variable model and
depends on Layer 1 only for the notion of a dependency digraph. Layers 3, 4 and 5 apply Layers 1
and 2 to hypergraph colourings, to satisfiability and to Ramsey numbers, and do not depend on one
another.

## Conventions

- **Events and probabilities.** `Ω` carries a σ-algebra and a probability measure `μ`
  (`MeasureTheory.IsProbabilityMeasure μ`). Events are measurable sets `A : ι → Set Ω` indexed by
  a finite type `ι` with decidable equality; probabilities are real numbers, `μ.real s`, Mathlib's
  `MeasureTheory.Measure.real`. The event that no `Aᵢ` occurs is `⋂ i, (A i)ᶜ`; over the empty
  index type it is all of `Ω`.
- **Independence.** Independence of two σ-algebras is Mathlib's `ProbabilityTheory.Indep`:
  `μ (s ∩ t) = μ s * μ t` for every `s` measurable in the first and `t` in the second. An event is
  independent of a family of events when the σ-algebra it generates, `generateFrom {A i}`, is
  independent of the σ-algebra generated by the family. This is mutual independence from the
  family, which is stronger than independence from each member of it (Check of 1.2).
- **Dependency digraphs.** A digraph on `ι` is a map `Γ : ι → Finset ι`; `Γ i` may contain `i`.
  This does not change whether `Γ` is a dependency digraph, since `i` is excluded from the events
  `A i` must be independent of; in 1.1 to 1.3 a loop at `i` adds the factor `1 − x i` to the bound
  and counts in `#(Γ i)`, so it only strengthens the hypotheses. Undirected dependency graphs are
  the symmetric case.
- **Numbers.** The weights `x : ι → ℝ` are only assumed to satisfy `x i ≤ 1`. The source states
  the lemma for weights in `[0, 1)`; a weight `1` makes the conclusion trivial, and a negative
  weight makes the hypothesis at `i` force `x j = 1` for some `j ∈ Γ i` (Check of 1.1), so the
  general statement costs nothing. `e` is `Real.exp 1`. Conditions with `2^(1 − k)` are written
  without negative exponents, as `… · 2 ≤ 2 ^ k`.
- **Variables, colourings, clauses.** A two-colouring of a type `V` is a map `V → Bool`; an edge
  `e` of a hypergraph `H : Finset (Finset V)` is monochromatic under `c` when `c` is constant on
  `e`, so an edge with at most one vertex is monochromatic under every colouring. Literals, clauses,
  formulas, assignments and satisfiability are those of PropositionalSatisfiability 1.1: a literal
  is `(v, b) : V × Bool`, a clause a finite set of literals, a formula a finite set of clauses,
  and `τ : V → Bool` satisfies a clause when it gives some literal `(v, b)` the value `τ v = b`.

## Layer 1: the local lemma

### 1.1 The lopsided local lemma

Prove `prod_one_sub_le_measureReal_iInter_compl_of_lopsided`: let the events `A i` be measurable,
`Γ : ι → Finset ι`, and `x : ι → ℝ` with `x i ≤ 1` for all `i`. Suppose that for every `i` and
every finite set `S` of indices with `i ∉ S` and `S` disjoint from `Γ i`,

    μ.real (A i ∩ ⋂ j ∈ S, (A j)ᶜ) ≤ x i * (∏ j ∈ Γ i, (1 - x j)) * μ.real (⋂ j ∈ S, (A j)ᶜ).

Then `∏ i, (1 - x i) ≤ μ.real (⋂ i, (A i)ᶜ)`. The case `S = ∅` of the hypothesis is the bound
`P(Aᵢ) ≤ xᵢ ∏_{Γ(i)} (1 − xⱼ)`. (Zhao, §6.5, Theorem 6.5.1, p. 93, where it is the form used by
Erdős and Spencer, 1991; Zhao assumes `P(Aᵢ | B) ≤ P(Aᵢ)` for these intersections `B` together
with that bound, and weights in `[0, 1)`. The hypothesis here is the one inequality that the
proof uses, Zhao, Remark 6.1.11, p. 83, and §6.5, p. 92, and it follows from Zhao's two by
multiplying, also when `P(B) = 0`.)

Route (Zhao, proof of Theorem 6.1.9, pp. 82–83). If some `x i = 1` the left side is `0`; if some
`x i < 0`, the case `S = ∅` at `i` gives `μ (A i) ≤ x i · ∏_{Γ(i)} (1 − x j)`, whose right side is
negative unless some `x j = 1`, so again the left side is `0`. Otherwise `0 ≤ x i < 1`, and one
proves by induction on `#S` that for `i ∉ S`, `μ (⋂_{j ∈ S} (A j)ᶜ) ≥ ∏_{j ∈ S} (1 − x j) > 0` and
`μ (A i ∩ ⋂_{j ∈ S} (A j)ᶜ) ≤ x i · μ (⋂_{j ∈ S} (A j)ᶜ)`. For the second, split `S` into
`S₁ = S ∩ Γ i` and `S₂ = S \ Γ i`: the numerator is at most `μ (A i ∩ ⋂_{S₂})`, bounded by the
hypothesis with `S₂`; the denominator `μ (⋂_{S})` is `μ (⋂_{S₂})` times a product over the
elements of `S₁`, taken one at a time, of factors `1 − μ (A j | …)`, each at least `1 − x j` by
the induction hypothesis; since `S₁ ⊆ Γ i` and every factor `1 − x j` lies in `(0, 1]`, the
quotient is at most `x i`. The conclusion follows by the chain rule over an enumeration of `ι`,
using `MeasureTheory.measure_inter_add_sdiff` for each conditional step.

**Checks.**
- For mutually independent events with `Γ i = ∅` and `x i = μ.real (A i)`, the hypothesis holds
  with equality and the two sides of the conclusion are equal, both `∏ (1 − P(Aᵢ))`, so the
  bound is attained.
- The bound `x i ≤ 1` is needed: on four equally likely points, two independent events of
  probability `1/2` with `Γ = ∅` and `x = 2` satisfy the hypothesis for every `S`, and the
  conclusion would read `1 ≤ 1/4`.
- A negative weight is harmless, as the route shows: on two points of mass `1/2`, take the null
  event with `x = −1` and the sure event with `x = 1/2`, each the other's only neighbour. The
  bound at the sure event holds (`1 ≤ (1/2) · 2`) and the conclusion would read `1 ≤ 0`, but the
  case `S = ∅` at the null event fails (`0 ≤ −1 · (1/2)` is false).
- The hypothesis for `S ≠ ∅` cannot be dropped: an event `E` of probability `1/2` and its
  complement, with `Γ = ∅` and `x = 1/2`, satisfy the case `S = ∅` (`1/2 ≤ 1/2`), while no point
  avoids both; the case `S = {2}` at `i = 1` fails (`1/2 ≤ 1/4` is false).
- Exhaustive test in exact rational arithmetic over all pairs of events on a three-point space
  whose masses are multiples of `1/4`, all sixteen digraphs on two indices (loops included) and
  all weights in `{−1/2, 0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1}`: of the `552960` instances, `62145` satisfy the
  hypothesis, and the conclusion holds in all of them.

*Needs:* Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Measure.real`, `MeasureTheory.measure_inter_add_sdiff`,
`MeasureTheory.IsProbabilityMeasure`. (Proved here, following the cited route.)

### 1.2 Dependency digraphs and the general local lemma

Define `IsDependencyDigraph μ A Γ`: for every `i`, `ProbabilityTheory.Indep` holds between
`generateFrom {A i}` and `generateFrom (A '' {j | j ≠ i ∧ j ∉ Γ i})` under `μ` (Zhao,
Definition 6.1.2, p. 80, with Definition 6.1.1 for independence from a set of events; Moser and
Tardos, Theorem 1.1, p. 1). The complete digraph, `Γ i = univ`, is a dependency digraph for every
family, since the second σ-algebra is then trivial; a digraph obtained by adding arcs to a
dependency digraph is again one.

Prove `prod_one_sub_le_measureReal_iInter_compl`: if the events are measurable, `Γ` is a
dependency digraph, `x i ≤ 1` and `μ.real (A i) ≤ x i * ∏ j ∈ Γ i, (1 - x j)` for every `i`, then
`∏ i, (1 - x i) ≤ μ.real (⋂ i, (A i)ᶜ)` (Erdős and Lovász, 1975; Zhao, Theorem 6.1.9, p. 81;
Moser and Tardos, Theorem 1.1, p. 1; both state it for weights in `(0, 1)` or `[0, 1)`).

Route: for `S` disjoint from `Γ i ∪ {i}` the set `⋂_{j ∈ S} (A j)ᶜ` is measurable for the second
σ-algebra (`MeasurableSpace.measurableSet_generateFrom` and closure under complements and finite
intersections), so independence gives
`μ (A i ∩ ⋂_{S}) = μ (A i) · μ (⋂_{S})`, by `ProbabilityTheory.Indep_iff`, and the hypothesis of
1.1 follows from the bound on `μ (A i)`.

**Checks.**
- Pairwise independence is not enough. With `X`, `Y` independent fair bits, the events
  `{X = 1}`, `{Y = 1}` and `{X + Y even}` are pairwise independent, but the third is not
  independent of the σ-algebra of the first two (`P(A₃ ∩ A₁ᶜ ∩ A₂ᶜ) = 1/4` against
  `P(A₃) P(A₁ᶜ ∩ A₂ᶜ) = 1/8`), so the empty digraph is not a dependency digraph; with `x = 1/2` the
  conclusion would give `1/8`, while every point lies in one of the three events.
- For `ι` empty both sides are `1`.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `ProbabilityTheory.Indep`, `ProbabilityTheory.Indep_iff`,
`MeasurableSpace.generateFrom`, `MeasurableSpace.measurableSet_generateFrom`. (Proved here,
following the cited route.)

### 1.3 The symmetric local lemma

Prove `measure_iInter_compl_pos`, in the lopsided form: if the events are measurable,
`#(Γ i) ≤ d` for every `i`, `e · p · (d + 1) ≤ 1`, and for every `i` and every finite set `S` of
indices with `i ∉ S` and `S` disjoint from `Γ i`,

    μ.real (A i ∩ ⋂ j ∈ S, (A j)ᶜ) ≤ p * μ.real (⋂ j ∈ S, (A j)ᶜ),

then `0 < μ (⋂ i, (A i)ᶜ)` (Zhao, Corollary 6.5.2, p. 93, which assumes `P(Aᵢ | B) ≤ P(Aᵢ)` and
`P(Aᵢ) ≤ p`, whose product is the hypothesis here; the constant `e` cannot be lowered, Shearer,
1985, as Zhao, Remark 6.1.8, p. 81, states). In particular, the usual symmetric lemma (Zhao,
Theorem 6.1.7, p. 81) holds: if `Γ` is a dependency digraph with `#(Γ i) ≤ d`, `μ.real (A i) ≤ p`
for every `i` and `e · p · (d + 1) ≤ 1`, then `0 < μ (⋂ i, (A i)ᶜ)`, since independence gives
`μ (A i ∩ ⋂_{S}) = μ (A i) · μ (⋂_{S})` exactly as in the route of 1.2. This is the form that
Layers 3 to 5 use.

Route: for `d ≥ 1` apply 1.1 with `x i = 1/(d + 1)`; then
`x i ∏_{Γ(i)} (1 − x j) ≥ (1/(d + 1)) (d/(d + 1))^d ≥ 1/(e (d + 1)) ≥ p`, where
`(1 + 1/d)^d ≤ e` follows from `Real.add_one_le_exp`, so the hypothesis of 1.1 follows from the
one here (`μ (⋂_{S}) ≥ 0`), and the lower bound `(d/(d + 1))^#ι` is positive. For `d = 0` the
choice `x i = 1/(d + 1) = 1` of Zhao's derivation (p. 81) gives only the bound `0`; there every
`Γ i` is empty, and 1.1 with `x i = max p 0`, which is at most `1/e < 1`, gives the lower bound
`(1 − x)^#ι > 0`.

**Checks.**
- Some constant factor is needed, since `p (d + 1) ≤ 1` alone does not suffice: an event of
  probability `1/2` and its complement, each the other's only neighbour, have `p = 1/2`, `d = 1`
  and `p (d + 1) = 1`, and no point avoids both; here `e · p · (d + 1) = e > 1`. That the constant
  `e` itself cannot be lowered is Shearer's theorem, cited above, and is not a target.
- `(d/(d + 1))^d ≥ 1/e` holds for every `1 ≤ d < 2000`, as computed; it is the inequality the
  route uses.
- For a dependency digraph with `d = 0` and `p = 1/e` the events are independent, and the
  probability that none occurs is `∏ (1 − P(Aᵢ)) ≥ (1 − 1/e)^#ι > 0`.
- Exhaustive test in exact arithmetic on the family of the last Check of 1.1 (`15360` choices of
  space, events and digraph): with `p` the least value allowed by the hypothesis on `B`, the
  condition `e · p · (d + 1) ≤ 1` holds in `1488` of them, and in each some point avoids both
  events.

*Needs:* 1.1; for the dependency-digraph form, 1.2 (the definition) and Mathlib
`ProbabilityTheory.Indep_iff`, `MeasurableSpace.measurableSet_generateFrom`; Mathlib
`Real.add_one_le_exp`, `Real.exp`. (Proved here, following the cited route.)

### Examples

Independent events with the empty digraph, where the general lemma is an equality; the three
pairwise independent events of the Check of 1.2; an event and its complement, which no form of
the lemma can avoid.

### Dependencies

Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Measure.real`, `MeasureTheory.measure_inter_add_sdiff`,
`MeasureTheory.IsProbabilityMeasure`, `ProbabilityTheory.Indep`, `ProbabilityTheory.Indep_iff`,
`MeasurableSpace.generateFrom`, `MeasurableSpace.measurableSet_generateFrom`,
`Real.add_one_le_exp`, `Real.exp`. No other layer.

## Layer 2: the variable model

### 2.1 Events determined by independent variables

Let `X : ∀ v, Ω → β v` be a family of random variables indexed by a type `V`, with no finiteness
assumed, and let each event `A i` be determined by the variables in a finite set `vbl i`: it is
measurable for the σ-algebra `⨆ v ∈ vbl i, MeasurableSpace.comap (X v) _`. Define
`dependencyNbhd vbl i` as the set of indices `j ≠ i` with `vbl i` and `vbl j` not disjoint.

Prove `isDependencyDigraph_dependencyNbhd`: if each `X v` is measurable and the family is
independent (`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun X μ`), then `dependencyNbhd vbl` is a dependency
digraph for `A` (Moser and Tardos, §1, p. 2, the paragraph defining the dependency graph; Zhao,
Setup 6.1.5, p. 80).

Route: by `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_iff_iIndep` the σ-algebras `comap (X v)` are independent,
and `ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_disjoint`, which needs each of them below the ambient
σ-algebra (measurability of `X v`), makes the suprema over the disjoint sets `vbl i` and
`⋃ {vbl j | j ≠ i, j ∉ dependencyNbhd vbl i}` independent. The σ-algebra generated by `A i` lies
below the first and the one generated by the other events below the second
(`MeasurableSpace.generateFrom_le`), and `ProbabilityTheory.indep_of_indep_of_le_left` with its
right-hand analogue, through `ProbabilityTheory.Indep.symm`, passes independence down.

**Checks.**
- Independence of the variables is needed: with `X₁ = X₂` a fair bit, the events `{X₁ = 1}` and
  `{X₂ = 1}` have disjoint variable sets, so the variable-sharing digraph is empty, but
  `P(A₁ ∩ A₂) = 1/2 ≠ 1/4`.
- The digraph need not be the least dependency digraph: with independent fair bits `X₁`, `X₂`,
  the events `{X₁ = 1}` and `{X₁ ≠ X₂}` share the variable `X₁` and are adjacent, yet they are
  independent.
- An event determined by no variable is measurable for the trivial σ-algebra, so it is `∅` or
  `Ω`; it has no neighbour and is independent of everything.

*Needs:* 1.2 (the definition); Mathlib `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun`,
`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_iff_iIndep`, `ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_disjoint`,
`ProbabilityTheory.indep_of_indep_of_le_left`, `ProbabilityTheory.Indep.symm`,
`MeasurableSpace.comap`, `MeasurableSpace.generateFrom_le`. (Proved here, following the cited
route.)

### Examples

Independent fair bits indexed by the vertices of a hypergraph, by the variables of a formula and
by the edges of a complete graph: the three models of Layers 3 to 5, in each of which an event is
determined by the bits of one edge, one clause or one set of vertices.

### Dependencies

Layer 1 (1.2). Mathlib `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun`, `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_iff_iIndep`,
`ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_disjoint`, `ProbabilityTheory.indep_of_indep_of_le_left`,
`ProbabilityTheory.Indep.symm`, `MeasurableSpace.comap`, `MeasurableSpace.generateFrom_le`.

## Layer 3: two-colourings of hypergraphs

The probability space of this layer is the finite product of fair coins on the vertices that lie
in some edge, `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi` of `ProbabilityTheory.uniformOn Set.univ` on `Bool`,
whose coordinates are independent by `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`; a colouring is extended
by `false` to the other vertices. The event of an edge `e` is that `e` is monochromatic, and it is
determined by the coordinates in `e`.

### 3.1 Property B under a local condition

Prove `exists_two_colouring_of_card_nbhd_le`: if every edge of a finite hypergraph `H` has at
least `k` vertices, every edge meets at most `d` other edges, and `e · (d + 1) · 2 ≤ 2 ^ k`, then
some `c : V → Bool` takes both values on every edge (Zhao, Theorem 6.2.1, p. 83, for
`k`-uniform hypergraphs; Moser and Tardos, §1, p. 2, recall the existence statement with about
`2^k/e` neighbours).

Route: an edge with `m ≥ 1` vertices is monochromatic with probability `2^(1 − m) ≤ 2^(1 − k)`;
apply 2.1 with `vbl e = e`, then 1.3 with `p = 2^(1 − k)`.

**Checks.**
- The hypothesis forces `k ≥ 3` (it fails for `k ≤ 2`, since `2e > 4`), so every edge has at
  least three vertices; for `k = 3` it allows only `d = 0`, for `k = 4` `d ≤ 1`, for `k = 5`
  `d ≤ 4`.
- The Fano plane, seven 3-element edges any two of which meet, is not two-colourable; its `d = 6`
  gives `14e ≈ 38.1 > 8`, consistent with the theorem.

*Needs:* 1.3, 2.1; Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi`, `ProbabilityTheory.uniformOn`,
`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`. (Proved here, following the cited route.)

### 3.2 Regular hypergraphs

Prove `exists_two_colouring_of_card_edges_le`: if every edge of `H` has exactly `k ≥ 9` vertices
and every vertex lies in at most `k` edges, then some `c : V → Bool` takes both values on every
edge (Zhao, Corollary 6.2.2, p. 83, for `k`-regular `k`-uniform hypergraphs).

Route: an edge meets at most `k (k − 1)` other edges, one count of at most `k − 1` through each of
its vertices, and `e · (k (k − 1) + 1) · 2 ≤ 2 ^ k` for `k ≥ 9`, since it holds at `k = 9` and the
ratio of consecutive right sides, `2`, exceeds that of the left sides, at most `91/73`, for
`k ≥ 9`; apply 3.1 with `d = k (k − 1)`.

**Checks.**
- `k = 9`: `e · 73 · 2 ≈ 396.9 ≤ 512`. `k = 8`: `e · 57 · 2 ≈ 309.9 > 256`, so the route stops at
  `k = 9`.
- The count `k (k − 1)` of neighbours is attained: in the Fano plane (`k = 3`, every point on
  three lines) every line meets the six others.

*Needs:* 3.1. (Proved here, following the cited route.)

### Examples

The Fano plane; the disjoint union of edges, where `d = 0`; for `N > k`, the `N` intervals of `k`
consecutive residues in `ℤ/N`, which form a `k`-uniform hypergraph with every vertex in exactly
`k` edges, so that 3.2 applies to it for `k ≥ 9`.

### Dependencies

Layers 1 (1.3) and 2 (2.1). Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi`, `ProbabilityTheory.uniformOn`,
`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`.

## Layer 4: satisfiability of formulas with sparse clause overlaps

The clause sets, models and variable sets are those of PropositionalSatisfiability 1.1, copied
verbatim into `Suggested.lean`: `Clause`, `CNF`, `Satisfies`, `Models`, `Satisfiable` and
`Clause.vars`. The probability space is the product of fair coins on the variables of the formula,
as in Layer 3, and the event of a clause is that the assignment falsifies it.

### 4.1 Clauses that share few variables

Prove `satisfiable_of_card_nbhd_le`: if every clause `C` of `F : CNF V` has at least `k`
variables (`k ≤ (Clause.vars C).card`), shares a variable with at most `d` other clauses of `F`,
and `e · (d + 1) ≤ 2 ^ k`, then `F` is satisfiable (Zhao, remark after Theorem 6.6.6, p. 100, for
clauses of exactly `k` literals on distinct variables).

Route: a clause that contains a literal and its negation is never falsified; any other clause
with `m` variables has one literal per variable and is falsified with probability `2^(−m)`. So
each event has probability at most `2^(−k)`; apply 2.1 with `vbl C = Clause.vars C`, then 1.3, and
extend a good point by `false` outside the variables of `F`.

**Checks.**
- For `k = 3` the hypothesis allows `d ≤ 1`, for `k = 4` `d ≤ 4`, for `k = 5` `d ≤ 10`, the values
  `⌊2^k/e − 1⌋`.
- The bound is within the factor `e` of the truth: the `2^k` clauses on the same `k` variables,
  one for each choice of signs, form an unsatisfiable formula in which each clause has `2^k − 1`
  neighbours.
- `F = {∅}` is unsatisfiable; its clause has no variable, so `k = 0`, and `e · (d + 1) ≤ 1` fails.

*Needs:* 1.3, 2.1; PropositionalSatisfiability 1.1; Mathlib `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi`,
`ProbabilityTheory.uniformOn`, `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`. (Proved here, following the
cited route.)

### 4.2 Variables that occur in few clauses

Prove `satisfiable_of_card_occurrences_le`: if `k ≥ 1`, every clause of `F` has exactly `k`
variables, every variable lies in the variable sets of at most `t` clauses, and
`e · k · t ≤ 2 ^ k`, then `F` is satisfiable.

Route: a clause shares a variable with at most `k (t − 1)` other clauses, and
`k (t − 1) + 1 ≤ k t` because `k ≥ 1`; apply 4.1 with `d = k (t − 1)`. If `F` is empty there is
nothing to prove, and otherwise `t ≥ 1`. (Proved here from 4.1.)

**Checks.**
- `k ≥ 1` is needed: `F = {∅}` has every clause with exactly `0` variables, no variable at all, and
  `e · 0 · t = 0 ≤ 1`, and it is unsatisfiable.
- The hypothesis allows `t ≤ 1` for `k = 4`, `t ≤ 2` for `k = 5`, `t ≤ 6` for `k = 7`, and only
  `t = 0`, the empty formula, for `k ≤ 3`.

*Needs:* 4.1; PropositionalSatisfiability 1.1.

### Examples

The unsatisfiable formula of all `2^k` sign patterns on `k` variables; formulas whose clauses
have pairwise disjoint variables, where `d = 0` and the hypothesis `e ≤ 2 ^ k` of 4.1 holds
exactly when `k ≥ 2`.

### Dependencies

## Layer 5: Spencer's lower bound for diagonal Ramsey numbers

The Ramsey property `RamseyProp` and the Ramsey number `ramseyNumber` are those of RamseyNumbers
1.1, copied verbatim into `Suggested.lean`; `R(k, k)` is `ramseyNumber ![k, k]`.

### 5.1 Spencer's criterion

Prove `lt_ramseyNumber_of_exp_mul_le`: for all `n` and `k`, if
`e · (binom(k, 2) · binom(n, k − 2) + 1) · 2 ≤ 2 ^ binom(k, 2)`, then `n < R(k, k)` (Spencer,
1977; Zhao, Theorem 1.1.9 and its proof, p. 6, with strict inequality, and the random variable
form of the lemma, Theorem 1.1.8, p. 6).

Route: colour the edges of the complete graph on `Fin n` by independent fair coins, one for each
edge. For a `k`-element set `S` of vertices let `E_S` be the event that all edges inside `S` have
one colour; it is determined by the edges inside `S` and has probability `2^(1 − binom(k, 2))`
for `k ≥ 2`. Two such sets share an edge exactly when they share at least two vertices, and at
most `binom(k, 2) · binom(n, k − 2)` sets `S' ≠ S` do. By 2.1 and 1.3 some colouring has no
monochromatic `k`-set, so `RamseyProp ![k, k] n` fails, and RamseyNumbers 1.2 gives
`n < R(k, k)`. The hypothesis fails for `k ≤ 2`, so the truncated subtraction `k − 2` plays no
part: for `k ≤ 1` the left side is `2e` against `1`, and for `k = 2` it is `4e` against `2`.

**Checks.**
- For `k = 3` the hypothesis holds only for `n = 0`, and for `k = 4` exactly for `n ≤ 2`, both
  far below `R(3, 3) = 6` and `R(4, 4) = 18`.
- The criterion is stronger than Erdős's counting criterion of RamseyNumbers 5.1 only for larger
  `k`: the largest admissible `n` is `96` against `100` for `k = 10`, `156` against `152` for
  `k = 11`, and `7739` against `5817` for `k = 20`, computed exactly.
- The number of `k`-sets `S' ≠ S` sharing at least two vertices with `S` is
  `∑_{i = 2}^{k − 1} binom(k, i) · binom(n − k, k − i)`, which is at most
  `binom(k, 2) · binom(n, k − 2)`, since each such `S'` is a pair of vertices of `S ∩ S'` together
  with `k − 2` further vertices; both were confirmed by enumeration for `n ≤ 12`, `2 ≤ k ≤ 6`.

*Needs:* 1.3, 2.1; RamseyNumbers 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `SimpleGraph.TopEdgeLabeling`,
`SimpleGraph.EdgeLabeling.labelGraph`, `SimpleGraph.IsNClique`, `MeasureTheory.Measure.pi`,
`ProbabilityTheory.uniformOn`, `ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`. (Proved here, following the
cited route.)

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the lopsided, general and symmetric forms of the Lovász local lemma and the
notion of a dependency digraph (Layer 1), the dependency digraph of the variable model (Layer 2),
and the three applications above: two-colourings of hypergraphs with few overlapping edges,
satisfiability of formulas with few overlapping clauses or few occurrences per variable, and
Spencer's criterion for `R(k, k)`.

Already in the libraries and therefore not targets: Mathlib's independence of σ-algebras, of
families of σ-algebras and of families of random variables, with the independence of suprema over
disjoint index sets (`ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_disjoint`), and the independence of the
coordinates of a product measure (`ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun_pi`). The pinned Mathlib and
Tau Ceti contain no form of the local lemma.

Taken from other roadmaps:

- PropositionalSatisfiability 1.1 owns clauses, formulas, assignments, models and variable sets;
  Layer 4 copies its definitions verbatim and states only the two satisfiability criteria, which
  that roadmap places outside its scope together with random formulas.
- RamseyNumbers 1.1 owns the Ramsey property and the Ramsey number, and its 1.2 proves the finite
  Ramsey theorem in the form Layer 5 uses. Its 5.1 is Erdős's union-bound criterion
  `binom(n, k) · 2 < 2^binom(k, 2)`; 5.1 here is a different criterion, whose hypothesis neither
  implies nor is implied by that one (Check of 5.1). RamseyNumbers places the local lemma and
  Spencer's asymptotic constant outside its scope.

Outside the scope: the asymptotic consequences of Spencer's criterion, such as
`R(k, k) ≥ (√2/e + o(1)) k 2^(k/2)`; Shearer's exact region and the optimality of `e`; the
random injection model and the application to Latin transversals; local lemmas for infinite
families and their compactness consequences; the lopsided criterion of Moser and Tardos in terms
of evaluations of variables; the algorithmic local lemma of Moser and Tardos, its running time and
every other procedure for finding an assignment or colouring; the off-diagonal Ramsey numbers
`R(3, k)`; and the other applications in Zhao's chapter (van der Waerden-type colourings,
acyclic edge colourings, coverings of the plane).
