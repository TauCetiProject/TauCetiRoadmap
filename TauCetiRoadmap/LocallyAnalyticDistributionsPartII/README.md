# Roadmap: locally analytic distributions, part II: noncommutative distribution algebras and microlocalisation

**Summary.** This roadmap continues *Locally analytic distributions, growth and character spaces* (LocallyAnalyticDistributions), which stops at the commutative theory: distributions on ℤ_p and on compact abelian groups, their Amice and Mellin transforms, and the locally convex calculus that relates function spaces, their Banach stages and their strong duals. Here the group G is a compact p-adic Lie group that need not be commutative. The distribution algebra D(G, K), its Fréchet–Stein structure, its coadmissible modules, its faithful flatness over the rational Iwasawa algebra and the Schneider–Teitelbaum duality between Iwasawa modules and admissible Banach representations are taken from PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1; the uniform groups, p-valuations, ordered coordinates, Iwasawa algebras and Lazard's Lie algebra of a compact p-adic analytic group are taken from NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa Layer 0. On these carriers the roadmap builds the structure that relates Iwasawa algebras to enveloping algebras: the lattice-level structure of Lazard's Lie algebra of a uniform group and its normalisation g_R = (1/p) R L_G, the embedding U(g_K) ⊂ D(G, K) and the centrality of Z(g); the coadmissible-module theory of an arbitrary Fréchet–Stein algebra and the Banach radius algebras D_r(G, K) of a p-valued group in ordered coordinates; the p-adically completed enveloping algebras Û(h)_K, Frommer's closures U_r(g, K) and the crossed-product decomposition of the Banach steps `D_{r_n}(G, K)`; the Arens–Michael envelope 𝒰(g); the Ardakov–Wadsley microlocalisation of the Iwasawa algebra K[[G]], which identifies its completion at the powers of p with Û(g_R)_K, together with Lie modules and the comparison of isotypic quotients; central specialisation along infinitesimal characters; and the algebraic Peter–Weyl theorem on the first congruence subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p). The end theorem is Fu's injectivity theorem: for p odd, G a finite product of m first congruence subgroups of SL₂(ℤ_p) and every λ ∈ ℤ_p^m, the composite ℚ_p[[G]] → Û(g) → Û(g)_λ of the microlocalisation and the central specialisation Δ_i ↦ λ_i is injective (Fu, Theorem 1.5). It is proved through the faithfulness of affinoid Verma modules (Ardakov–Wadsley 2014), and Fu's direct route through distributions is laid out alongside it. Two further headline theorems are the semisimplicity of finite-dimensional modules over the rational Iwasawa algebra of a congruence kernel (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, Theorem 11.1) and the algebraic Peter–Weyl theorem K[[G]]/Ann(W_k) ≅ End_K(W_k) for G a product of first congruence subgroups (Fu, §6, (32)). These are the algebraic inputs of Fu's sharp multiplicity bounds for cohomological cusp forms over fields that are not totally real.

The targets below are specifications for formalisation. Every definition comes with the facts it must satisfy and with checks that a plausible wrong definition fails; every theorem carries its hypotheses, its source and what it rests on. The companion [Suggested.lean](Suggested.lean) is the acceptance test: it states the headline theorems that are statable against the pinned Mathlib and Tau Ceti, and names in its docstring those that are not, with the carrier each one lacks. It is not the roadmap; this document is definitive.

## Conventions

1. **Fields.** p is a prime and ℚ_p ⊆ L ⊆ K ⊆ ℂ_p with L/ℚ_p finite and K complete and discretely valued; |p| = `p^{−1}`. R denotes the ring of integers of K, π a uniformiser and k the residue field. Where a statement needs K finite over ℚ_p it says so. §1.4 alone works over any field complete for a nontrivial nonarchimedean absolute value. In Layer 3, R is unramified over ℤ_p (π = p) until coefficients are extended by base change in §3.6.
2. **Groups.** For p odd a uniform group G of dimension d carries the p-valuation ω_U of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.7, which takes the value n + 1 on `G^{p^n}` ∖ `G^{p^{n+1}}`; a minimal topological generating set g₁, …, g_d is an ordered basis with ω(g_i) = 1, and b_i = g_i − 1. G₁ = ker(SL₂(ℤ_p) → SL₂(𝔽_p)); h = diag(1, −1), e = E₁₂ and f = E₂₁, so [h, e] = 2e, [h, f] = −2f and [e, f] = h.
3. **Distributions.** D(G, K) is the strong dual of the locally analytic functions with the convolution of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1, which pairs the first factor with the left variable: (λ ∗ μ)(f) = λ(g ↦ μ(h ↦ f(gh))), so δ_g ∗ δ_h = `δ_{gh}`. An element x ∈ g gives the distribution x̃(f) = (d/dt) `f(exp(tx))|_{t=0}`; with this sign x̃ ∗ ỹ − ỹ ∗ x̃ = [x, y]~, and an ordered monomial x^α pairs with the coordinate monomial t^β of the second kind to `α!·δ_{αβ}`. Schneider–Teitelbaum (2002, Lemma 2.4) differentiate along exp(−tx) and obtain an extra factor `(−1)^{|α|}`.
4. **Banach steps.** For an ordered basis, τ_α = Σ α_i ω(h_i) and ‖Σ d_α b^α‖_r = sup |d_α| `r^{τ_α}`; D_r(G, K), for 1/p ≤ r < 1, is the completion, consisting of the series with |d_α| `r^{τ_α}` → 0, and is distinct from the space `D_{<r}(G, K)` of series with bounded |d_α| `r^{τ_α}`. These are the radius algebras of §1.5, the Banach stages of the Fréchet–Stein algebra D(G, K) of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1. The radii r_n = `p^{−1/p^n}`, n ≥ 1, are the ones at which D_r is a crossed product.
5. **Enveloping algebras.** For a free R-Lie lattice h, Û(h) = lim_a U(h)/π^a U(h) and Û(h)_K = K ⊗_R Û(h); `Û(h)_{n,K}` = Û(π^n h)_K. U_r(g, K) is the closure of U(g_K) in D_r(G, K). U(g)_r is the completion of U(g) for sup |a_n| `r^{|n|}` in a PBW basis of an integral lattice, and 𝒰(g) = `lim_{r ≥ 1}` U(g)_r is the Arens–Michael envelope. Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni write Û(g) for 𝒰(g); here Û(g)_K always denotes the single Banach algebra of the lattice. For p odd and G uniform with the lattice g_R = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G of §0.1, Û(g_K) abbreviates Û(g_R)_K, as in Fu, §4, p. 12, and Û(g) abbreviates `Û(g_{ℤ_p})_{ℚ_p}`; both depend on G, not only on g (§2.1, the lattice-dependence Check).
6. **Casimir and central characters.** In U(sl₂), Δ = ½h² − h + 2ef = ½h² + ef + fe = ½h² + h + 2fe. It is central, acts on Sym^k(K²) by ½k(k + 2) and on a Verma module of highest weight a by ½a(a + 2). It is four times the Killing-form Casimir `TauCeti.casimirElement`: the Killing form has κ(h, h) = 8 and κ(e, f) = 4, the Killing-dual basis of (h, e, f) is (h/8, f/4, e/4), and h²/8 + (ef + fe)/4 = Δ/4. Z(g) is the centre of U(g) for the ℚ_p-Lie algebra g, and Z_K := Z(U(g_K)) = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` Z(g) is the centre of U(g_K). A K-valued character of the centre is a K-algebra homomorphism λ : Z_K → K, equivalently a ℚ_p-algebra homomorphism Z(g) → K extended K-linearly by λ(a ⊗ z) = aλ(z); ker λ always denotes its kernel in Z_K, every central specialisation of a K-algebra is formed over Z_K, and the antipode S acts on Z_K by its K-linear extension. For K = ℚ_p, Z_K = Z(g). For G₁^m, Z(g) = ℚ_p[Δ₁, …, Δ_m], and λ = (λ₁, …, λ_m) ∈ K^m stands for the character Δ_i ↦ λ_i of Z_K = K[Δ₁, …, Δ_m]. Fu writes the kernel through p²Δ + λ₀, so λ₀ = −p²λ, and λ ∈ ℤ_p iff v_p(λ₀) ≥ 2.
7. **Modules.** Modules are left modules. A coherent sheaf for a Fréchet–Stein algebra A with Banach steps A_n has transition isomorphisms A_n `⊗_{A_{n+1}}` `M_{n+1}` ≅ M_n, the Banach algebra on the left. For a homomorphism f : A → B of rings that need not be commutative, B is flat as a right A-module when extension of scalars P ↦ B ⊗_A P along f (`TauCeti.BalancedExtension`, functorial through `TauCeti.BalancedExtension.map`) carries every injective A-linear map of left A-modules to an injective map, and flat as a left A-module when the same holds for the opposite homomorphism Aᵐᵒᵖ → Bᵐᵒᵖ (`RingHom.op`), whose extension of scalars is P ↦ P ⊗_A B on right A-modules. A ring map is flat when it is flat on both sides, unless a side is named. Mathlib's `Module.FaithfullyFlat`, like `Module.Flat`, needs a commutative ring of scalars; it is used only for the coefficient extension K/ℚ_p of §4.14, where the two notions of flatness agree.
8. **Microlocalisation.** S₀ = ⋃_a (p^a + `m^{a+1}`) ⊆ R[[G]] is formed only for R unramified, with finite or infinite residue field; over a ramified K the microlocalisation K[[G]] → Û(g_R)_K is the base change of the one over an unramified subfield K′ ⊆ K with K/K′ finite, K′ = ℚ_p when K is finite over ℚ_p (§3.6).
9. **Witnesses.** Convolution order: for G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) and the non-commuting elements exp(pe), exp(pf) ∈ G₁, `δ_{exp(pe)}` ∗ `δ_{exp(pf)}` = `δ_{exp(pe)exp(pf)}` ≠ `δ_{exp(pf)exp(pe)}`; the opposite rule λ(g ↦ μ(h ↦ f(hg))) would give `δ_{exp(pf)exp(pe)}` and would make x ↦ x̃ an anti-homomorphism. Lie sign: for G = ℤ_p and x = 1, x̃(f) = f′(0), which is 3 for f(z) = z² + 3z, and its Amice transform is log(1 + T). Casimir: Δ acts on the highest weight vector x^k of Sym^k(K²) by ½k² − k + 2k = ½k(k + 2), which is 4 for k = 2. Induced parameter: χ(diag(t, t⁻¹)) = t^c gives λ(Δ) = ½c² + c and 2λ + 1 = (c + 1)².
10. **Names.** New declarations live under `TauCetiRoadmap.LocallyAnalyticDistributionsPartII` in the companion file; their names follow Mathlib's conventions.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 proves that the explicit valuation of a uniform group is saturated, takes Lazard's Lie algebra of a uniform group from NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a, records the lattice-level facts that fix the integral Lie algebra g_R = (1/p) R L_G used by every completion that follows, and computes it for the first congruence subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p). Layer 1 puts U(g_K) inside the distribution algebra of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 with a fixed sign, decomposes D(G, K) over open normal subgroups and proves that Z(g) is central; it also states the coadmissible-module theory of an arbitrary Fréchet–Stein algebra and describes the Banach stages D_r(G, K) in ordered coordinates, with the norms and graded rings on which the later computations rest, identifying them with the stages of the Fréchet–Stein algebra of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1. Layer 2 builds the two completions of U(g) that the sources use and must not be confused: the p-adic completion Û(h)_K, a single noetherian Banach algebra, and the Arens–Michael envelope 𝒰(g), a Fréchet–Stein algebra; between them sit Frommer's closures U_r(g, K) inside the Banach steps, over which `D_{r_n}` is a crossed product. Layer 3 microlocalises the Iwasawa algebra and identifies the result with Û(g_R)_K and with `D_{1/p}(G, K)`; it gives Lie modules, the semisimplicity theorem and the comparison of isotypic quotients. Layer 4 specialises along infinitesimal characters and proves Fu's injectivity theorem through affinoid Verma modules; Fu's direct route through eigenspaces of locally analytic functions and the Banach steps is a second line of targets in the same layer. Layer 5 identifies the maximal W_k-quotient of K[[G]] through algebraic matrix coefficients.

## Layer 0: Lazard lattices and first congruence subgroups

Schneider–Teitelbaum's norms, Frommer's closures and the Ardakov–Wadsley microlocalisation all use the same integral Lie algebra of a uniform group: Lazard's lattice L_G, rescaled to g_R = (1/p) R L_G. The Lie algebra itself is NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a's; this layer records the lattice-level facts it needs on the uniform groups of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.1, fixes the normalisation, and computes the lattice for the first congruence subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p), whose rescaled lattice is sl₂(ℤ_p). Throughout the layer p is odd; at p = 2 the first congruence subgroup contains −1 and is not uniform, and the level-four subgroup takes its place in the sources.

### 0.1 The Lazard Lie lattice of a uniform group

Let p be odd, G a uniform pro-p group of dimension d (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.1) and R a complete discrete valuation ring with fraction field K ⊇ ℚ_p. With the valuation ω_U of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.7, (G, ω_U) is p-saturated for odd p (first bullet below), so NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a, item 1, provides Lazard's ℤ_p-Lie algebra Log G on the underlying set of G. Its operations are λ·x = x^λ for λ ∈ ℤ_p (the p-adic powers of the pro-p group), x + y = `lim_{i→∞} (x^{p^i} y^{p^i})^{p^{−i}}` and [x, y] = `lim_{i→∞} (x^{−p^i} y^{−p^i} x^{p^i} y^{p^i})^{p^{−2i}}`, where `z^{p^{−i}}` is the unique p^i-th root of z ∈ `G^{p^i}` in G, roots existing and being unique by saturation; these are the formulas of Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.2, p. 68, and Fu, §4, equations (10)–(12), p. 12, who cite Dixon–du Sautoy–Mann–Segal, Theorem 4.30. Write L_G := Log G, the Lazard lattice, and import from §0.13a: that ℚ_p ⊗ L_U = ℚ_p ⊗ L_G for every open uniform subgroup U ⊆ G, the common ℚ_p-Lie algebra being denoted g and called the Lie algebra of G; that a continuous homomorphism of uniform groups induces a homomorphism of Lazard lattices (a Lie algebra map, since it is the same map on underlying sets); and that for a closed subgroup G ⊆ GL_n(ℤ_p) the Lie algebra g is the ℚ_p-span of log(G ∩ (1 + p^k M_n(ℤ_p))) in gl_n(ℚ_p), the matrix logarithm being Lazard's Log on the congruence subgroup. Define the R-Lie algebra g_R := R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G and g_K := K ⊗_R g_R (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.2, the Definition, p. 68; Fu, §4, the definition of g_R, p. 12). Prove the lattice-level facts that §0.13a does not state:

- (G, ω_U) is saturated, and every ordered minimal generating set g₁, …, g_d satisfies Schneider–Teitelbaum's condition (HYP), used in §1.5: the valued group is p-saturated and ω_U(g_i) + ω_U(g_j) > p/(p − 1) for i ≠ j. An x with ω_U(x) > p/(p − 1) has ω_U(x) = n + 1 with n ≥ 1, since p/(p − 1) ≤ 3/2 for odd p, so x ∈ `G^{p^n}` ∖ `G^{p^{n+1}}`; the p-th power map is a bijection from `G^{p^{n−1}}` onto `G^{p^n}` for uniform G, so x = y^p with y ∈ `G^{p^{n−1}}` ∖ `G^{p^n}`, that is ω_U(y) = ω_U(x) − 1; and ω_U(g_i) + ω_U(g_j) = 2 > p/(p − 1) (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §4, the Remark following the condition (HYP), p. 20, citing Dixon–du Sautoy–Mann–Segal, Lemma 4.10).
- L_G is free of rank d over ℤ_p, with basis any minimal topological generating set of G (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.2, p. 68; Dixon–du Sautoy–Mann–Segal, Theorem 4.30).
- L_G is powerful: [L_G, L_G] ⊆ p L_G. Consequently (1/p) L_G is closed under the bracket, and g_R is an R-Lie algebra, free of rank d over R (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.2, pp. 68–69).
- For n ≥ 0 the subgroup `G^{p^n}` is uniform and `L_{G^{p^n}}` = p^n L_G inside g: the limits computed in `G^{p^n}` and in G agree, and `G^{p^n}` is the image of the p^n-th power map, which is p^n· in L_G.
- The one-parameter subgroup of x ∈ L_G in the analytic structure of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.2 is t ↦ x^t, t ∈ ℤ_p, so that g is the Lie algebra of the analytic group and the ordered coordinates of §0.6 are coordinates of the second kind (Schneider, Corollary 31.7, p. 229, and Theorem 32.1, p. 232).

*Needs:* NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.1 (uniform groups and the lower p-series), §0.7 (the valuation ω_U and the notion of saturation, with unique p-th roots in a saturated group), §0.13a (Log G, independence of the open uniform subgroup, functoriality, the logarithmic description for closed subgroups of GL_n(ℤ_p)), §0.2 (the analytic structure, for the one-parameter subgroups only), §0.6 (ordered coordinates, for freeness); Mathlib `LieAlgebra` and `PadicInt`.

**Checks.**

- For G = 1 + pℤ_p ⊂ ℤ_p^× the Lazard addition is the group multiplication, (1 + p)^a + (1 + p)^b = `(1 + p)^{a+b}`, and the bracket is zero; the logarithm identifies L_G with pℤ_p ⊂ ℚ_p and `g_{ℤ_p}` with ℤ_p·log(1 + p)/p. This is the n = 1 case of the GL₁ check of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a (1 + pℤ_p has Lie algebra ℚ_p, since log(1 + pℤ_p) = pℤ_p) together with the normalisation by 1/p. A definition that took the group commutator itself as the bracket would agree here and is separated by the next check.
- For the trivial group, L_G = 0 and g_R = 0.
- Saturation is a property of the valuation, not of the group: on G = ℤ_p (p odd), ω_U = 1 + v_p is saturated, an x with v_p(x) ≥ 1 being p·(x/p) with v_p(x/p) = v_p(x) − 1, while the valuation 2 + v_p on the same group gives the unit 1 the value 2 > p/(p − 1) although 1 ∉ pℤ_p, so it is not saturated. A definition of L_G through an arbitrary p-valuation, rather than ω_U, would have to pass to the saturation first.
- For the first congruence subgroup G₁ of SL₂(ℤ_p), the logarithm carries `L_{G₁}` onto p·sl₂(ℤ_p) and the Lazard bracket onto the matrix bracket: [pe, pf] = p²h, and ph lies in `L_{G₁}` but not in `pL_{G₁}`, so [`L_{G₁}`, `L_{G₁}`] ⊆ `pL_{G₁}` while [`L_{G₁}`, `L_{G₁}`] ⊄ `p²L_{G₁}`. The normalisation g_R = R ⊗ L_G, without the factor 1/p, would give `g_{ℤ_p}` = p·sl₂(ℤ_p) instead of sl₂(ℤ_p).
- For G₁, `g_{ℤ_p}` is `LieAlgebra.SpecialLinear.sl` (Fin 2) ℤ_p, with exp(px) ∈ G₁ corresponding to x ∈ `g_{ℤ_p}`.

### 0.2 First congruence subgroups of SL₂(ℤ_p)

Define `firstCongruenceSL2` p, the first congruence subgroup G₁ of SL₂(ℤ_p), as the kernel of the homomorphism SL₂(ℤ_p) → SL₂(ℤ/p) induced by `PadicInt.toZMod` through `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.map`; equivalently G₁ = SL₂(ℤ_p) ∩ (I₂ + pM₂(ℤ_p)). For m ≥ 0 define `firstCongruenceProduct` p m as the subgroup G = G₁^m of SL₂(ℤ_p)^m (functions Fin m → SL₂(ℤ_p)) whose every coordinate lies in G₁, via `Subgroup.pi` (Fu, §1, before Theorem 1.4, p. 3, and §5, before Lemma 5.6, p. 19).

- A matrix g ∈ SL₂(ℤ_p) lies in G₁ iff every entry of g − I₂ lies in pℤ_p.
- G₁ is a normal subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p); it is open, since it is the preimage of the identity under a continuous map to a discrete group, and of index |SL₂(𝔽_p)| = p(p² − 1), since reduction SL₂(ℤ_p) → SL₂(𝔽_p) is surjective (elementary matrices lift).
- G₁^m is an open normal subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p)^m of index (p(p² − 1))^m, and the product of the subspace topologies is its topology as a subgroup of the product group.
- With the subspace topology, G₁ and G₁^m are compact topological groups, so `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra` ℤ_p G is defined and is the Iwasawa algebra ℤ_p[[G]].

*Needs:* Mathlib `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup`, `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.map`, `PadicInt.toZMod`, `MonoidHom.ker`, `Subgroup.pi`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`.

**Checks.**

- I₂ ∈ G₁, and for m = 0 the product G₁^0 is the trivial group, whose Iwasawa algebra is ℤ_p.
- The matrix with rows (1, p) and (0, 1) lies in G₁, while the matrix with rows (1, 1) and (0, 1) lies in SL₂(ℤ_p) but not in G₁: membership is a condition on all four entries of g − I₂, not on the determinant.
- For p odd, −I₂ ∉ G₁, since −1 − 1 = −2 is a unit. For p = 2, −I₂ ≡ I₂ mod 2 lies in G₁, which therefore has an element of order 2 and is not uniform; this is why the odd-prime hypothesis of §0.3 cannot be dropped.
- The index of G₁ in SL₂(ℤ_p) is 24 for p = 3 and 120 for p = 5, the orders of SL₂(𝔽₃) and SL₂(𝔽₅).

### 0.3 The Lie lattice of the first congruence subgroup

Prove: (i) if p is odd and G is a compact open uniform subgroup of SL_n(ℚ_p), then g = ℚ_p ⊗ L_G is isomorphic to sl_n(ℚ_p) as a ℚ_p-Lie algebra, through the matrix logarithm on an open subgroup of G contained in 1 + pM_n(ℤ_p) after a conjugation (Fu, Lemma 4.1, p. 12, citing Dixon–du Sautoy–Mann–Segal, Chapter 9, Exercise 9; NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a, item 1); (ii) let p be odd. Then G₁ is uniform of dimension 3; the elements exp(ph), exp(pe), exp(pf) form a minimal topological generating set; and (1/p) `L_{G₁}` = `g_{ℤ_p}` = ℤ_p h ⊕ ℤ_p e ⊕ ℤ_p f = sl₂(ℤ_p), with [h, e] = 2e, [h, f] = −2f and [e, f] = h, so (h, e, f) is an sl₂-triple in the sense of `IsSl2Triple`. For G = G₁^m the same holds componentwise, with `g_{ℤ_p}` = sl₂(ℤ_p)^m (Fu, Lemma 5.6 and its proof, p. 19; Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.4, p. 16, where the congruence kernel with n = 0 satisfies p^n g = (1/p) R L_G).

*Proof sketch.* (ii): for x ∈ pM₂(ℤ_p) and p odd the exponential and logarithm series converge and are mutually inverse between pM₂(ℤ_p) and 1 + pM₂(ℤ_p), and det(exp x) = exp(tr x) = 1 iff tr x = 0 for x ∈ pM₂(ℤ_p); so log(G₁) = p·sl₂(ℤ_p), and by the functoriality of Log along G₁ ⊆ 1 + pM₂(ℤ_p) (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.13a, where Log of the congruence subgroup is the matrix logarithm) the limits of §0.1 become the sum and the matrix bracket, so `L_{G₁}` = p·sl₂(ℤ_p). ## Layer 1: The enveloping algebra, the centre and the Banach stages of D(G, K)

The distribution algebra D(G, K) of a compact locally ℚ_p-analytic group, with its convolution, Dirac distributions, open-subgroup comparisons and Fréchet–Stein property, is the one of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1. This layer adds the structures on it that the later layers use: the embedding of U(g_K) with the sign fixed in Conventions, item 3; the crossed-product form over an open normal subgroup; the centrality of Z(g) for open subgroups of connected algebraic groups; the coadmissible-module theory of an arbitrary Fréchet–Stein algebra, which Layers 2 and 4 apply to algebras other than D(G, K); and the Banach stages D_r(G, K) of a p-valued group in ordered coordinates, on which every norm computation of Layers 2–4 rests. Groups are locally ℚ_p-analytic throughout the roadmap, and g = ℚ_p ⊗ L_U for an open uniform subgroup U (§0.1).

### 1.1 The enveloping algebra inside D(G, K)

Let G be a compact locally ℚ_p-analytic group, U an open normal uniform subgroup (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.2), g = ℚ_p ⊗ L_U its Lie algebra (§0.1) and K a complete discretely valued extension of ℚ_p. For x ∈ g write x = `p^{−n}` y with y ∈ L_U and put exp(tx) = `y^{t p^{−n}}` for t ∈ p^n ℤ_p. Define the distribution x̃ ∈ D(G, K) by x̃(f) = (d/dt) `f(exp(tx))|_{t=0}`; the derivative exists because t ↦ f(exp(tx)) is locally analytic, the ordered coordinates of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.6 being analytic charts. Prove that x ↦ x̃ is ℚ_p-linear and satisfies x̃ ∗ ỹ − ỹ ∗ x̃ = [x, y]~, so that by `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift` it extends to a K-algebra homomorphism U(g) `⊗_{ℚ_p}` K = U(g_K) → D(G, K), and that this homomorphism is injective (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, §2, p. 12; Fu, §5, equations (22)–(23), p. 15).

- x̃ is independent of the choice of U and of the representation x = `p^{−n}` y.
- For λ ∈ D(G, K), x̃ ∗ λ = `lim_{t→0}` `t^{−1}(δ_{exp(tx)} − δ₁)` ∗ λ in D(G, K) (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, §2, the limit formula, p. 12).
- For g ∈ G, δ_g ∗ x̃ ∗ `δ_{g⁻¹}` = (Ad(g)x)~, where Ad(g) is the automorphism of g induced by conjugation by g on an open uniform subgroup normalised by g (Fu, proof of Proposition 5.3, p. 17).
- Under the action of D(G, K) on the locally analytic functions by the contragredient of left translation, x̃ acts by the derivation f ↦ (d/dt) `f(exp(−tx)·)|_{t=0}`.
- For a locally analytic homomorphism φ : G → H, the pushforward of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 sends x̃ to (dφ(x))~, where dφ is the induced map of Lazard lattices of §0.1.
- The homomorphism U(g_K) → D(G, K) is injective: pairing with polynomial functions in the ordered coordinates computes PBW coefficients, the ordered monomial x^α pairing with the coordinate monomial of the second kind t^β to `α!·δ_{αβ}` (Conventions, item 3; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Lemmas 2.4–2.5, pp. 10–11), and the ordered monomials are a basis of U(g_K) (Tau Ceti `Module.Basis.pbwBasis`).
- The K-span of the Dirac distributions δ_g, g ∈ G, is dense in D(G, K): a continuous linear form on D(G, K) vanishing on every δ_g is, by strong biduality (PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1), evaluation against a locally analytic function f with f(g) = 0 for all g, hence zero, and the Hahn–Banach theorem over the spherically complete K (LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.2) then gives density (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Lemma 3.1, p. 13).

*Proof sketch.* The commutator relation follows from the limit formula and the expansion exp(tx)exp(sy)exp(−tx)exp(−sy) = exp(ts[x, y] + higher order), which in the Lazard lattice is the definition of the bracket of §0.1.

*Needs:* §0.1; PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 (D(G, K), `distributionConvolution`, Dirac distributions, pushforward, strong biduality); NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §§0.2, 0.6; LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.2 (Hahn–Banach, for the density of the Dirac span) and §0.3 (analytic charts and their independence); Tau Ceti `Module.Basis.pbwBasis` (PBW basis, for injectivity); Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.lift`.

**Checks.**

- For G = ℤ_p and x = 1, x̃(f) = f′(0); on f(z) = z² + 3z it takes the value 3. The opposite sign convention, differentiating along exp(−tx), gives −3.
- 0̃ = 0, and for a finite group, where g = 0, the homomorphism is the inclusion K → D(G, K) of scalar multiples of δ₁.
- For G = G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) and p odd, ẽ ∗ f̃ − f̃ ∗ ẽ = h̃ and h̃ ∗ ẽ − ẽ ∗ h̃ = 2ẽ in D(G₁, ℚ_p). With the opposite convolution λ(g ↦ μ(h ↦ f(hg))) the first commutator would be −h̃, and x ↦ x̃ would be an anti-homomorphism.
- For G = ℤ_p the distribution 1̃ is neither in the span of Dirac distributions nor a bounded measure: its Amice transform is log(1 + T), whose coefficients `(−1)^{n+1}/n` are unbounded.

### 1.2 Crossed products over open normal subgroups

Let G be a compact locally ℚ_p-analytic group and H ⊆ G an open subgroup with representatives g₁ = 1, …, g_l of the left cosets gH. The open-subgroup comparison of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 makes D(G, K) a free right D(H, K)-module on `δ_{g₁}`, …, `δ_{g_l}`, D(G, K) = ⊕_i `δ_{g_i}` ∗ D(H, K) (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, proof of Lemma 3.1, p. 13), and, since the inverses g_i⁻¹ represent the right cosets Hg, a free left D(H, K)-module on `δ_{g₁⁻¹}`, …, `δ_{g_l⁻¹}`; topologically D(G, K) ≅ D(H, K)^l on either side. For H not normal one set of representatives need not serve on both sides: for the finite group G = S₃ (a compact ℚ_p-analytic group of dimension 0, with D(G, K) = K[S₃]), H = ⟨(12)⟩ and the left-coset representatives 1, (23), (123), one has H(23) = H(123) = {(23), (123)}, so the left D(H, K)-span of δ₁, `δ_{(23)}`, `δ_{(123)}` has dimension 4 < 6, while the inverses 1, (23), (132) lie in the three distinct right cosets and give a left basis. Prove that if H is normal, D(G, K) is the crossed product D(H, K) ∗ (G/H): conjugation by δ_g preserves D(H, K), and for any choice of representatives ḡ = `δ_{g_i}` of the cosets, ḡ D(H, K) = D(H, K) ḡ and ḡ h̄ ∈ D(H, K)^× · (gh)‾, with twisting `δ_{g_i}` ∗ `δ_{g_j}` = `δ_{h_{ij}}` ∗ `δ_{g_k}` for `h_{ij}` ∈ H and g_i g_j ∈ g_k H (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, proof of Lemma 3.1, p. 13; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, proof of Theorem 5.1, Step 1, pp. 29–30; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.6, the definition of a crossed product, p. 72). The crossed product is that of McConnell–Robson §1.5.8; the twisting is nontrivial whenever G/H does not lift to a subgroup of G.

*Needs:* PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 (open-subgroup comparison, Dirac distributions); NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.3 (the same statement for Iwasawa algebras, compatible with the map K[[G]] → D(G, K)).

### 1.3 The centre of U(g) is central in D(G, K)

Let 𝔾 be a connected linear algebraic group over ℚ_p with Lie algebra g, and G an open compact subgroup of 𝔾(ℚ_p). Prove that the image of Z_K = Z(g) ⊗ K (Conventions, item 6) under U(g_K) → D(G, K) (§1.1) lies in the centre of D(G, K). Consequently every z ∈ Z(g) commutes with every δ_g, and acts on the locally analytic functions on G by G-equivariant continuous endomorphisms for both the left and the right translation action (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, §3, Proposition 3.7 and its proof, p. 16; Fu, §5, proof of Theorem 5.2, p. 16).

*Proof sketch.* By density of the span of Dirac distributions (§1.1, last bullet) it suffices that δ_g ∗ z ∗ `δ_{g⁻¹}` = z, that is, that the adjoint action of 𝔾(ℚ_p) on U(g) fixes Z(g); on generators this is the conjugation formula of §1.1, and for connected 𝔾 the adjoint action fixes Z(g) (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Proposition 3.7, citing Bourbaki and Demazure–Gabriel II §6.1.5). Connectedness is essential: for 𝔾 = O(2), U(so(2)) = Z(so(2)) = K[x], and an element s of determinant −1 has Ad(s)x = −x, so δ_s ∗ x̃ ∗ `δ_{s⁻¹}` = −x̃ ≠ x̃ and x is not central in D(G, K) for G = O(2)(ℤ_p), or for any open subgroup of O(2)(ℚ_p) containing an element of determinant −1. The hypothesis cannot be read as "G open in a disconnected group": SO(2)(ℤ_p) is an open subgroup of O(2)(ℚ_p), being open in the index-two subgroup SO(2)(ℚ_p), and on it x is central by the connected case.

*Needs:* §1.1 (the conjugation formula and the density of the Dirac span); Mathlib `Subalgebra.center` (the centre of U(g)).

### 1.4 Fréchet–Stein algebras and coadmissible modules

Let K be a field complete for a nontrivial nonarchimedean absolute value, not necessarily discretely valued (the standing field of Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, p. 4), and A a K-Fréchet algebra, not necessarily commutative. A continuous seminorm q on A is an algebra seminorm if q(ab) ≤ c·q(a)q(b) for some c > 0 and all a, b; its completion A_q is then a K-Banach algebra and A → A_q is an algebra homomorphism. Define A to be a K-Fréchet–Stein algebra if there is an increasing sequence q₁ ≤ q₂ ≤ ⋯ of continuous algebra seminorms defining the topology of A such that every A_n := `A_{q_n}` is left noetherian and A_n is flat as a right `A_{n+1}`-module along the transition map (Convention 7), and two-sided Fréchet–Stein if moreover every A_n is right noetherian and flat as a left `A_{n+1}`-module. A coherent sheaf for (A, (q_n)) is a family of finitely generated left A_n-modules M_n with A_n-linear isomorphisms A_n `⊗_{A_{n+1}}` `M_{n+1}` ≅ M_n, and a left A-module is coadmissible if it is isomorphic to Γ(M_n) := lim_n M_n for some coherent sheaf (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §3, the definitions of an algebra seminorm, of a Fréchet–Stein algebra, of a coherent sheaf and of a coadmissible module, pp. 7–8). PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 uses this notion for A = D(H, L); it is stated here for every A because §2.5 applies it to the Arens–Michael envelope and §§4.1 and 4.6 to central quotients of D(G, K). Prove:

- The full subcategory C_A of coadmissible left A-modules does not depend on the sequence: any two sequences with these properties that define the topology of A, in particular a sequence and its cofinal subsequences, give the same coadmissible modules (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §3, p. 8).
- Theorems A and B: for a coherent sheaf (M_n) and M = Γ(M_n), the map M → M_n has dense image for the canonical Banach topology of the finitely generated A_n-module M_n, and `lim^{(i)}` M_n = 0 for i ≥ 1; consequently Γ is exact (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §3, the Theorem, p. 9).
- A_n `⊗_A` M ≅ M_n for every n; every A_n is flat as a right A-module; and Γ is an equivalence from coherent sheaves to C_A (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Corollary 3.1, Remark 3.2 and Corollary 3.3, pp. 9–10).
- C_A is an abelian subcategory of the category of left A-modules: it contains finite direct sums, the kernel, cokernel and image of every A-linear map between coadmissible modules and the sum of two coadmissible submodules, and every finitely generated submodule of a coadmissible module and every finitely presented module is coadmissible (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Corollaries 3.4 and 3.5, pp. 10–11).
- A coadmissible M = Γ(M_n), with the projective-limit topology of the canonical topologies of the M_n, is a K-Fréchet space on which A acts continuously; a submodule N ⊆ M is coadmissible iff M/N is coadmissible iff N is closed; every A-linear map between coadmissible modules is continuous and strict; and every finitely generated left ideal of A is closed (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Lemma 3.6 and the two paragraphs following it, p. 11).
- For a closed two-sided ideal I of a K-Fréchet–Stein algebra A, the quotient A/I with the quotient seminorms is a K-Fréchet–Stein algebra, A_nI is the closure of the image of I in A_n, and A/I ≅ lim_n A_n/A_nI as Fréchet algebras; the same holds for two-sided Fréchet–Stein algebras, by the same argument for the opposite algebras (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Proposition 3.7 and its proof, pp. 11–12).
- For a continuous unital homomorphism A → B of K-Fréchet–Stein algebras such that B is coadmissible as a left A-module, a left B-module is coadmissible over B iff it is coadmissible over A (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Lemma 3.8, p. 12).

*Needs:* LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.0 (locally convex limits and strict maps) and §0.2 (Fréchet open mapping, strict countable inverse limits); Mathlib `IsNoetherianRing`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.BalancedExtension` and `TauCeti.BalancedExtension.map` (the one-sided extensions of scalars of Convention 7).

**Checks.**

- For A = K with q_n = |·| for every n, every A_n is K, a coherent sheaf is a constant family of finite-dimensional spaces, and the coadmissible modules are exactly the finite-dimensional K-vector spaces. The infinite direct sum of countably many copies of K is not coadmissible, since it is not finitely generated over A₁ = K; a definition that allowed arbitrary Banach A_n-modules in a coherent sheaf would admit it.
- Flatness is a condition on the sequence. On A = K[ε]/(ε²), finite-dimensional, so that every norm defines its topology, put q₁(a + bε) = |a| and q_n(a + bε) = max(|a|, |b|) for n ≥ 2; both are algebra seminorms, since (a + bε)(a′ + b′ε) = aa′ + (ab′ + a′b)ε. So (q_n) is not a Fréchet–Stein sequence, although A is Fréchet–Stein for the constant sequence q₂, A being noetherian.

### 1.5 The Banach radius algebras D_r(H, K) in ordered coordinates

Let K be a complete discretely valued extension of ℚ_p (Conventions, item 1) and H a compact locally ℚ_p-analytic group with a p-valuation ω of finite rank (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.7) and an ordered basis h₁, …, h_d satisfying Schneider–Teitelbaum's condition (HYP): (H, ω) is p-saturated, and ω(h_i) + ω(h_j) > p/(p − 1) for i ≠ j. Put b_i = h_i − 1 ∈ ℤ_p[H] ⊆ D(H, K), b^α = `b₁^{α₁}` ⋯ `b_d^{α_d}` and τ_α = Σ_i α_i ω(h_i). For 1/p ≤ r < 1 define ‖Σ_α d_α b^α‖_r := sup_α |d_α| `r^{τ_α}`, the Banach radius algebra D_r(H, K) as the completion of D(H, K) for ‖·‖_r, which consists of the series Σ d_α b^α with |d_α| `r^{τ_α}` → 0, and the larger space `D_{<r}(H, K)` of the series with bounded |d_α| `r^{τ_α}` (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §4, pp. 16–19). For p odd, a uniform G with the valuation ω_U and any ordered minimal generating set satisfies (HYP), with τ_α = |α| (§0.1). Prove:

- Every λ ∈ D(H, K) has a unique convergent expansion λ = Σ_α d_α b^α with d_α ∈ K and {|d_α| `s^{|α|}`} bounded for every 0 < s < 1, every such series converges in D(H, K), and λ lies in the image of ℤ_p[[H]] iff every d_α ∈ ℤ_p. The proof identifies b^α(f) with the α-th Mahler coefficient of the pullback of f along the ordered coordinates ℤ_p^d → H, and uses Amice's theorem in d variables: a continuous f : ℤ_p^d → K is locally analytic iff its Mahler coefficients satisfy |c_α| `s^{|α|}` → 0 for some s > 1, which is part of this target, the case d = 1 being PadicFunctionalAnalysis §4.4 (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §4, pp. 15–17, citing Lazard III.1.3.9 for Amice's theorem).
- Each ‖·‖_r, 1/p ≤ r < 1, is submultiplicative on D(H, K) (Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2, pp. 17–18) and does not depend on the ordered basis for the same ω (the discussion following Theorem 4.10, p. 25); D_r(H, K) and `D_{<r}(H, K)` are K-Banach algebras, D_r ⊆ `D_{<r}` ⊆ `D_{r′}` for 1/p ≤ r′ < r, and D(H, K) = lim_r D_r(H, K) = lim_r `D_{<r}(H, K)` (p. 19).
- gr_r D_r(H, K) ≅ gr K `⊗_{𝔽_p[ε]}` gr_r ℤ_p[[H]], with gr K ≅ k[ε₀, ε₀⁻¹] for the residue field k of K and the symbol ε₀ of a uniformiser (Lemma 4.3, p. 20); for 1/p < r < 1 it is the polynomial ring over gr K in the symbols σ_r(b₁), …, σ_r(b_d), so the norm ‖·‖_r is multiplicative and D_r(H, K) is an integral domain (Theorem 4.5 (i), p. 22); `D_{1/p}(H, K)` is a left and right noetherian integral domain with multiplicative norm `‖·‖_{1/p}` (Remark 4.6, p. 22).
- The Fréchet–Stein property is imported: by PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1, D(H, K) is a K-Fréchet–Stein algebra with noetherian Banach radius algebras and flat transitions (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Theorems 4.9 and 4.10, pp. 23–24). Prove the comparison: for H satisfying (HYP), the radius algebras of §0.1 at r ∈ p^ℚ ∩ (1/p, 1) are the completions D_r(H, K) for the norms ‖·‖_r, so the coadmissible D(H, K)-modules of §1.4 for these norms are those of §0.1. Prove also the opposite-side statements, which make the presentation two-sided in the sense of §1.4: for r′ ≤ r in p^ℚ ∩ (1/p, 1), D_r(H, K) is right noetherian and `D_{r′}(H, K)` is flat as a left D_r(H, K)-module along the transition map (Theorem 4.5 (ii), p. 22, and Theorem 4.9, pp. 23–24, where flatness of a ring map is flatness on both sides by the convention of p. 22).

*Proof sketch.* The structure constants of b^βb^γ = Σ_α `c_{βγ,α}` b^α satisfy v_p(`c_{βγ,α}`) ≥ τ_β + τ_γ − τ_α, because `‖·‖_{1/p}` is multiplicative on ℤ_p[[H]] (Lazard's computation `gr_{1/p} ℤ_p[[H]]` ≅ U(gr H), as recalled by Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003 on p. 17); this gives submultiplicativity. Under (HYP) the commutator h_i⁻¹h_j⁻¹h_ih_j is a p-th power g^p with ω(g) ≥ ω(h_i) + ω(h_j) − 1, so ‖b_ib_j − b_jb_i‖_r < ‖b_ib_j‖_r for r > 1/p (Lemma 4.4, p. 21) and the symbols commute. The noetherianity of `D_{1/p}(H, K)` passes from its graded ring (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.5 for the lifting through a complete filtration). The opposite-side statements follow from the left-sided ones: inversion h ↦ h⁻¹ induces an anti-automorphism ι of D(H, K) with ι(δ_h) = `δ_{h⁻¹}`, which carries b^α to an ordered monomial of the same weight τ_α in the reversed ordered basis h_d⁻¹, …, h₁⁻¹ for the same ω (ω(h⁻¹) = ω(h)); by the independence of ‖·‖_r from the ordered basis (second bullet), ι is an isometry for every ‖·‖_r, so it gives ring isomorphisms D_r(H, K)ᵐᵒᵖ ≅ D_r(H, K) compatible with the transitions, which exchange left and right noetherianity and left and right flatness.

*Needs:* §§0.1 (the uniform case of (HYP)), 1.4; PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 (D(H, K), the map ℤ_p[[H]] → D(H, K), the Fréchet–Stein property with noetherian radius algebras and flat transitions); NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §§0.5, 0.6, 0.7; PadicFunctionalAnalysis §4.4 (Amice's analytic basis, for the Mahler coefficients of locally analytic functions); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`.

**Checks.**

- For H = {1}, d = 0 and D_r(H, K) = K for every r.
- For H = ℤ_p, p odd, ω = 1 + v_p and h₁ = 1, b₁ = δ₁ − 1 is the variable T of the Amice transform, and D_r(ℤ_p, K) is the algebra of series Σ d_n T^n with |d_n| r^n → 0, the functions on the closed disc |T| ≤ r; the norm of T^p/p is r^p·p, which at r = `p^{−1/p}` equals 1 while ‖T‖_r = `p^{−1/p}` (§2.2).
- The completion consists of null series, not bounded ones: for H = ℤ_p and r = `p^{−1/p}`, the series with `d_{pj}` = `p^{−j}` and all other coefficients zero has `|d_{pj}|` `r^{pj}` = `p^{j}·p^{−j}` = 1 for every j, so it lies in `D_{<r}(ℤ_p, K)` but not in D_r(ℤ_p, K) (Source corrections, item 1).
- A group without a p-valuation has no radius algebras of this kind: SL₂(ℤ₃) contains −I of order 2, and ω((−I)³) = ω(−I) + 1 would contradict (−I)³ = −I (§4.1, Checks). For the first congruence subgroup G₁ (p odd) with ω_U, ‖exp(px) − 1‖_r = r for x ∈ {h, e, f} and τ_α = |α|.

- For G = G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) (p odd), the Casimir Δ = ½h² − h + 2ef is central in D(G₁, ℚ_p) by §1.3; ẽ and f̃ are not, by the commutator check of §1.1.
- For G = ℤ_p and H = pℤ_p, D(ℤ_p, K) = `⊕_{a=0}^{p−1}` δ_a ∗ D(pℤ_p, K) (§1.2); under the Amice transform this is the decomposition of an open-disc series by residue classes of the exponent of 1 + T.
- For a finite group G, D(G, K) is the group algebra K[G] via δ_g ↦ g, and §1.2 with H = {1} is its decomposition on the group basis; for G = {1}, D(G, K) = K.
- In D(G₁, ℚ_p), `δ_{exp(pe)}` ∗ `δ_{exp(pf)}` ≠ `δ_{exp(pf)}` ∗ `δ_{exp(pe)}`, since the two group elements do not commute and g ↦ δ_g is injective; D(G, K) is commutative exactly when G is.
- For G₁ (p odd) with ω_U and 1/p < r < 1, gr_r D_r(G₁, ℚ_p) ≅ 𝔽_p[ε₀, ε₀⁻¹][F, H, E], with F, H, E the symbols of exp(pf) − 1, exp(ph) − 1 and exp(pe) − 1 (§1.5): the symbols commute although the elements do not, their commutators having strictly smaller norm.
- The Fréchet–Stein algebra D(ℤ_p, K) of §1.5 has stages the closed-disc algebras D_r(ℤ_p, K), and the coadmissible module D(ℤ_p, K)/(T − a), for a ∈ K with |a| < 1, has stage K at every r ≥ |a| and stage 0 at every r < |a| (§1.4).

## Layer 2: Completed enveloping algebras, Frommer's theorem and the Arens–Michael envelope

Two completions of U(g) appear in the sources. The p-adic completion Û(h)_K of an integral lattice is a single noetherian Banach algebra; Layer 3 identifies it with `D_{1/p}(G, K)` and with the microlocalisation of the Iwasawa algebra. The Arens–Michael envelope 𝒰(g) is the limit of the weighted completions U(g)_r over all radii r ≥ 1, the algebra of distributions supported at the identity. Between them sit Frommer's closures U_r(g, K) inside the Banach steps D_r(G, K), over which D_r is free of finite rank; at r_n = `p^{−1/p^n}` this is a crossed product by `G/G^{p^n}`. The Banach steps themselves, with their coordinate norms, multiplicativity and graded rings, are the radius algebras of §1.5.

### 2.1 The p-adically completed enveloping algebra Û(h)_K

Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformiser π and fraction field K, and h an R-Lie algebra that is free of finite rank as an R-module, with basis u₁, …, u_d. Define Û(h) := lim_a U(h)/π^a U(h) (π is central, so π^a U(h) is a two-sided ideal and each quotient is a ring), Û(h)_K := K ⊗_R Û(h) with the norm whose unit ball is Û(h), and for n ≥ 0 the n-th deformation `Û(h)_{n,K}` := Û(π^n h)_K (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §3.4 Example (c), §3.5, §3.7 Definition, Lemma and Corollary, pp. 14–16, and §10.4, the display defining Û(h)_K, p. 70; Fu, §4, the definitions of Û(g_R) and Û(g_K), p. 12).

- The ordered monomials u^α form an R-basis of U(h), and gr U(h) ≅ Sym(h) for the PBW filtration: both are in Tau Ceti for a Lie algebra that is free over an arbitrary commutative ring, as `Module.Basis.pbwBasis`, whose vector at α is the increasing product of the powers of the generators (`Module.Basis.pbwBasis_eq_prod_pow`), and `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwAssociatedGradedEquiv`. Hence gr U(h) is flat over R and U(h) is a deformable R-algebra (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §3.4 Example (c)).
- Every element of Û(h)_K is uniquely a convergent sum Σ_α c_α u^α with c_α ∈ K and c_α → 0, and ‖Σ c_α u^α‖ = sup_α |c_α|.
- U(h_K) → Û(h)_K is injective with dense image, and the inclusions U(h_K) ⊂ `Û(h)_{n+1,K}` ⊂ `Û(h)_{n,K}` are dense.
- Each `Û(h)_{n,K}` is a left and right noetherian K-Banach algebra, an almost commutative affinoid algebra, with `Gr(Û(h)_{n,K})` ≅ Sym(h ⊗_R k) (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §3.7 Corollary and §3.8 Proposition, pp. 16–17).
- A Lie algebra map φ : h → B into an R-algebra B that is π-adically complete, separated and π-torsion-free extends uniquely to a continuous R-algebra map Û(h) → B.
- For a finite extension K′/K with ring of integers R′ and ramification index e, K′ ⊗_K `Û(h)_{n,K}` ≅ `Û(R′ ⊗_R h)_{en,K′}`, compatibly with the maps from U(h); in particular K′ ⊗_K Û(h)_K ≅ `Û(R′ ⊗_R h)_{K′}` (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §3.9 Lemma (c), p. 17). Finiteness of K′/K is needed.

*Needs:* Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwFiltration`, `Module.Basis.pbwBasis`, `Module.Basis.pbwBasis_eq_prod_pow`, `TauCeti.UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra.pbwAssociatedGradedEquiv`; NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.5 (noetherian lifting through a complete filtration).

**Checks.**

- For h = R·u abelian of rank one, Û(h)_K is the Tate algebra K⟨u⟩: the element Σ_n π^n u^n lies in it and Σ_n u^n does not; Û(π^n h)_K = K⟨π^n u⟩ is the Tate algebra of the disc of radius `|π|^{−n}`.
- For h = 0, Û(h)_K = K.
- For h = sl₂(ℤ_p), p odd, the Casimir Δ = ½h² − h + 2ef has norm 1 in `Û(h)_{ℚ_p}` (its PBW coefficients ½, −1, 2 are units), and `Û(h)_{ℚ_p}` is noncommutative: ef − fe = h ≠ 0.
- Û(h)_K depends on the lattice and not only on h_K: for h ≠ 0 with a basis vector u (for instance h = R·u), Û(πh)_K = `Û(h)_{1,K}` is a proper subalgebra of Û(h)_K, since Σ_n π^n u^n = Σ_n (πu)^n lies in Û(h)_K, its coefficients π^n in the basis u^n tending to zero, but not in Û(πh)_K, where its coefficients in the basis (πu)^n are all 1. For h = 0 the two coincide, Û(π·0)_K = Û(0)_K = K, so nonzero rank is needed.

### 2.2 The closure U_r(g, K) of U(g) in D_r(G, K)

Let p be odd, G uniform with minimal generators g₁, …, g_d, b_i = g_i − 1, and x_i := log(1 + b_i) = `Σ_{n≥1}` `(−1)^{n+1}` b_i^n/n, the image in D(G, K) of log(g_i) ∈ g under §1.1. For 1/p ≤ r < 1, define U_r(g, K) as the closure in the Banach step D_r(G, K) of §1.5 of the image of U(g_K) under §1.1 (Kohlhaase, §1.4, definition of U_r(g, K), p. 15; Fu, §5, the definition of U_r(g), p. 17).

- U_r(g, K) is a closed subalgebra of D_r(G, K), the topological closure in the sense of `Subalgebra.topologicalClosure`.
- x_i lies in U_r(g, K) and is the image of log(g_i): the logarithm series converges in D_r(G, K) for r < 1 and computes the derivative at t = 0 of t ↦ `δ_{g_i^t}`.
- δ_g U_r(g, K) `δ_{g⁻¹}` = U_r(g, K) for g ∈ G, by the conjugation formula of §1.1 and continuity of conjugation (Fu, proof of Proposition 5.3, p. 17).
- For r′ ≤ r the transition D_r → `D_{r′}` maps U_r(g, K) into `U_{r′}(g, K)`.
- The map U(g_K) → U_r(g, K) is injective with dense image; on generators it is x ↦ x̃ of §1.1.

*Needs:* §§0.1, 1.1, 1.5; Mathlib `Subalgebra.topologicalClosure`.

**Checks.**

- For G = {1}, U_r = D_r = K.
- For G = ℤ_p (p odd) and r₁ = `p^{−1/p}`, `‖log(1 + T)‖_{r₁}` = max_k `r₁^{p^k}/|p^k|` = max_k `p^{k − p^{k−1}}` = 1, attained only at k = 1, by the term T^p/p; the term T has norm `p^{−1/p}` < 1. A norm computed from the linear term alone would give `p^{−1/p}`.
- For G = ℤ_p and r = r₁, U_r ≠ D_r: δ₁ = 1 + T is not in the closure of K[log(1 + T)] at that radius, while δ_p = exp(p log(1 + T)) is (Fu, proof of Proposition 5.3); `D_{r₁}` is free of rank p over `U_{r₁}` (§2.3).
- For G = ℤ_p and `p^{−1}` < r < `p^{−1/(p−1)}`, ‖log(1 + T) − T‖_r < ‖T‖_r, so log(1 + T) has the same norm r as T and U_r = D_r: the closure is proper only at the larger radii.

### 2.3 Frommer's theorem

Let K be discretely valued, G a uniform pro-p group (p odd) with τ_i = ω(g_i) = 1, more generally a p-valued group with an ordered basis as in Kohlhaase §1.4 with τ_i = ω(h_i), let r ∈ p^ℚ with 1/p < r < 1 and x_i = log(1 + b_i). Prove: (i) U_r(g, K) = {Σ_α d_α x^α : d_α ∈ K, |d_α| ‖x^α‖_r → 0}, with ‖Σ d_α x^α‖_r = sup_α |d_α| ‖x^α‖_r; (ii) U_r(g, K) is a noetherian subalgebra of D_r(G, K); (iii) with ℓ_i = max{m ≥ 1 : `sup_{n ≥ 1}` |1/n| `r^{nτ_i}` = |1/m| `r^{mτ_i}`}, D_r(G, K) is a free right U_r(g, K)-module with basis the b^α, 0 ≤ α_i < ℓ_i. Let H be a closed uniform subgroup compatible with G, that is, with a minimal topological generating set that extends to one of G; choose the generators of G so that g₁, …, g_j topologically generate H. Then the same holds for D(H, K) with the restricted norms; and if D(G, K)_H ⊆ D(G, K) denotes the closed subspace of distributions supported in H (those vanishing on every locally analytic function whose support misses H), its closure D_r(G, K)_H in D_r(G, K) is a free right U_r(g, K)-module with basis the b^α with 0 ≤ α_i < ℓ_i for i ≤ j and α_i = 0 for i > j, a basis contained in K[H], so of rank ℓ₁⋯ℓ_j; D_r(G, K)_H is also the closure of D(H, K)·U(g_K) in D_r(G, K), since the right U(g_K)-module generated by the δ_h, h ∈ H, is dense in D(G, K)_H (Kohlhaase, §1.2, the definition of D(G, K)_C and Lemma 1.2.10, pp. 5–8, and §1.4, Theorem (Frommer) and Corollary 1.4.1 with its proof, pp. 15–16; Fu, §5, equation (27) and the proof of Proposition 5.3, p. 17). The supported subspace is not the product D(G, K)·D(H, K), which is all of D(G, K) since 1 ∈ D(H, K). The theorem is stated for r ∈ p^ℚ ∩ (1/p, 1) only.

*Proof sketch.* The monomials x^α are orthogonal for ‖·‖_r (Frommer's orthogonality lemma, as summarised by Kohlhaase §1.4). The integer ℓ_i records where the maximum in ‖log(1 + b_i)‖_r = max_n |1/n| `r^{nτ_i}` is attained, and the b^α with α_i < ℓ_i fill the remaining coordinates (Kohlhaase, proof of Corollary 1.4.1). Noetherianity follows from the graded ring of U_r being a polynomial ring, as for D_r.

*Needs:* §§1.5 (coordinate norms, multiplicativity and graded rings of D_r), 2.2; NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.5.

### 2.4 `D_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` as a crossed product of `U_{r_n}(g)` by `G/G^{p^n}`

Let p be odd, G uniform with minimal generators g_i, ω(g_i) = 1, and r_n = `p^{−1/p^n}` for n ≥ 1. Prove: (i) `‖log(1 + b_i)‖_{r_n}` = `p^{n−1}`; (ii) for every g ∈ G, `g^{p^n}` ∈ `U_{r_n}(g)`, since `g^{p^n}` = exp(p^n log g) and the exponential series converges in `U_{r_n}(g)`; (iii) `D_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` is the crossed product `U_{r_n}(g)` ∗ (`G/G^{p^n}`): it is a free left and right `U_{r_n}(g)`-module on δ_γ for any set of coset representatives γ of `G/G^{p^n}`, with δ_γ `U_{r_n}(g)` = `U_{r_n}(g)` δ_γ and twisting in `U_{r_n}(g)^×` ∩ `δ_{G^{p^n}}`; (iv) if G is open in the ℚ_p-points of a connected split reductive group, then for every character λ of Z(g), `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` ≅ `U^λ_{r_n}(g)` ∗ (`G/G^{p^n}`), with the central quotients of §4.1 (Fu, Proposition 5.3 and its proof, pp. 17–18; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.6, Proposition and Remark, pp. 71–72).

*Proof sketch.* (i): ‖log(1 + b_i)‖_r = max_k `r^{p^k}/|p^k|` = max_k `p^{k − p^{k−n}}`, which is `p^{n−1}` at k = n and strictly smaller for every other k ≥ 0, because k − `p^{k−n}` < n − 1 for k ≠ n when p ≥ 3; terms T^m with m not a power of p are dominated by the term with m replaced by `p^{v_p(m)}`. (ii): `‖x_i^k‖_{r_n}` = `p^{(n−1)k}` by multiplicativity, and `|p^{nk}/k!| p^{(n−1)k}` = `p^{−k + v_p(k!)}` ≤ `p^{−k(p−2)/(p−1)}` → 0, so exp(p^n x_i) converges and equals `g_i^{p^n}`; products of these give all of `G^{p^n}` by ordered coordinates. (iii): §2.3 with ℓ_i = p^n; the b^α with α_i < p^n span the same right `U_{r_n}`-module as the δ_γ, and `U_{r_n}` is stable under conjugation (§2.2). (iv): Z(g) is central (§1.3) and contained in `U_{r_n}(g)`, so the crossed-product structure passes to the quotient by the central ideal.

*Needs:* §§1.2, 1.3, 2.2, 2.3; §1.5 (multiplicativity of ‖·‖_r); NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.6 (ordered coordinates).

### 2.5 The Arens–Michael envelope 𝒰(g) and its coadmissible modules

Let E/ℚ_p be finite and g the Lie algebra of a linear algebraic group over o_E, with a chosen o_E-basis x₁, …, x_d of the integral Lie lattice g₀ (closed under the bracket). For r > 0 put |Σ_n a_n x^n|_r := sup_n |a_n| `r^{|n|}` on U(g) in PBW coordinates and let U(g)_r be the completion; for r ≥ 1 these are Banach algebras. Define the Arens–Michael envelope 𝒰(g) := `lim_{r ≥ 1}` U(g)_r (Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, §2.2.29, Remark 2.2.30, Definition 2.2.31, Definition 2.2.33, §2.2.34 and Proposition 2.2.35, pp. 23–24; Schmidt, §3.2, Proposition 3.2.1, Remark 3.2.2 and Proposition 3.2.3, pp. 5–6, and Theorem 4.3.3, p. 16; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, §2, Lemmas 2.4 and 2.5, pp. 10–11).

- The norms |·|_r, r > 1, are submultiplicative and cofinal among the submultiplicative seminorms on U(g), so their completion is the Arens–Michael envelope of U(g) (Schmidt, Proposition 3.2.1).
- Let π_E be a uniformiser of E and r_j := `|π_E|^{−j}` for j = 0, 1, 2, …; these radii are cofinal among the r ≥ 1. The unit ball of `|·|_{r_j}` is the closure of the o_E-span of the monomials (π_E^j x)^n, so `U(g)_{r_j}` = Û(π_E^j g₀)_E = `Û(g₀)_{j,E}` is the left and right noetherian Banach algebra of §2.1, with R = o_E and h = g₀. 𝒰(g) is a Fréchet–Stein algebra for the sequence `U(g)_{r_j}`, an integral domain and a nuclear Fréchet space (Schmidt, Proposition 3.2.3, whose noetherian Banach algebras are the completions for single norms). For every r ∈ p^ℚ with r ≥ 1, U(g)_r is a left and right noetherian Banach algebra, flat over 𝒰(g): after a finite extension E′/E in which r is a value of the absolute value, E′ `⊗_E` U(g)_r is a Banach algebra of §2.1 for the lattice `o_{E′} ⊗_{o_E} g₀`, and both properties descend along the central extension E′/E (Schmidt, the remark following Proposition 3.2.3 and the proof of Theorem 4.3.3, p. 16, at radii in p^ℚ; Source corrections, item 13). Outside p^ℚ neither property is proved.
- 𝒰(g) is the strong dual of the space `𝒪_{G,e}` of germs at e of analytic functions, a space of compact type: the germs are the power series Σ a_n T^n in coordinates of the second kind with |a_n| `s^{|n|}` → 0 for some s > 0, their dual consists of the Σ b_n Z^n with sup |b_n| `s^{−|n|}` < ∞ for all s > 0, and x^n pairs with T^n to give n!·δ (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Lemmas 2.4–2.5).
- U(g) → 𝒰(g) is injective with dense image, x ↦ (f ↦ (d/dt) f(exp(tx))|₀), compatibly with §1.1.
- For the small-group distribution algebras D(G_r) of the congruence polydisc subgroups G_r there are cofinal comparisons: for every r there are r′ and r″ with `U(g)_{r′}` → D(G_r) → `U(g)_{r″}`, so 𝒰(g) = lim_r U(g)_r = lim_r D(G_r) (Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Remark 2.2.30).
- A left 𝒰(g)-module is coadmissible in the sense of §1.4 for the sequence `U(g)_{r_j}`, with transition isomorphisms `U(g)_{r_j}` `⊗_{U(g)_{r_{j+1}}}` `M_{j+1}` ≅ M_j. U(g) → U(g)_r is flat for every r ∈ p^ℚ with r ≥ 1, and U(g) → 𝒰(g) is flat (Schmidt, Theorem 4.3.3 and its proof, which deduces the second from the first), so M ↦ 𝒰(g) `⊗_{U(g)}` M is an exact functor from finitely generated U(g)-modules to coadmissible 𝒰(g)-modules (Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Proposition 2.2.35).
- An admissible 𝒰(g)-module is the continuous dual of a coadmissible one, a space of compact type with the contragredient action (Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Definition 2.2.33).

𝒰(g) is not Û(g_R)_K: the latter is the single Banach step for the norm of a lattice, while 𝒰(g) is the limit over all radii r ≥ 1.

*Needs:* §1.1; §2.1 (the noetherian Banach algebras `Û(g₀)_{j,E}`); §1.4 (coadmissible modules over a Fréchet–Stein algebra); LocallyAnalyticDistributions §§0.2, 0.3 (compact-type duality and strong duality for analytic compact-type spaces); Tau Ceti `Module.Basis.pbwBasis` (PBW coordinates); Mathlib `UniversalEnvelopingAlgebra`.

**Checks.**

- For g = E·x abelian, U(g)_r is the Tate algebra of the disc |x| ≤ r and 𝒰(g) = {Σ a_n x^n : |a_n| r^n → 0 for all r}, the entire series; exp(x) = Σ x^n/n! lies in U(g)_r only for r < `p^{−1/(p−1)}` < 1, so exp(x) ∉ 𝒰(g). The finitely generated module E[x]/(x − a) completes to the coadmissible module 𝒪(𝔸¹)/(x − a) ≅ E for every a ∈ E.
- For g = 0, 𝒰(g) = E.
- With the convention of §1.1, the ordered monomial `x₁^{α₁}` ⋯ `x_d^{α_d}` pairs with the coordinate monomial t^β of the second kind to `α!·δ_{αβ}`; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Lemma 2.4, obtain `(−1)^{|α|}` α! because they differentiate along exp(−tx).
- For a left module the transition `U(g)_{r_j}` `⊗_{U(g)_{r_{j+1}}}` `M_{j+1}` ≅ M_j is the one that is defined; the reversed `M_{j+1}` `⊗_{U(g)_{r_{j+1}}}` `U(g)_{r_j}`, the form of Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Definition 2.2.31, would need `M_{j+1}` to be a right module.
- The radii of the Fréchet–Stein presentation lie in p^ℚ. For E = ℚ₃ and j = 1, r₁ = 3 and the unit ball of |·|₃ is {|a_n| ≤ `3^{−|n|}`}, the closure of ⊕ ℤ₃·(3x)^n, so U(g)₃ = `Û(g₀)_{1,ℚ₃}`. The integer radius r = 2 is not in 3^ℚ, since log₃ 2 is irrational, and is not of the form `|π_E|^{−j}` for any finite E/ℚ₃ (it would be `3^{j/e}`); a presentation along r = 1, 2, 3, … would rest on noetherianity of U(g)₂, which no cited proof covers.

## Layer 3: Microlocalisation of Iwasawa algebras and Lie modules

Ardakov–Wadsley's microlocalisation inverts, in the Iwasawa algebra of a uniform group over an unramified coefficient ring, the elements whose principal symbol is a power of p, and completes. Lazard's computation of the graded ring makes this possible, and the result is Û(g_R)_K, which is also the Banach step `D_{1/p}(G, K)`. Finite-dimensional modules on which these elements act invertibly are unchanged by microlocalisation; this gives the semisimplicity of finite-dimensional K[[G]]-modules and Fu's comparison of W-isotypic quotients. Here K[[G]] = K ⊗_R R[[G]] with R[[G]] = `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra` R G, and p is odd.

### 3.1 Algebraic microlocalisation of complete filtered rings

Let A be a ring with an exhaustive separated ℤ-filtration F_•A (F_iA·F_jA ⊆ `F_{i+j}A`, 1 ∈ F₀A, in the sense of `IsRingFiltration`) which is Zariskian, for example complete with noetherian associated graded ring (NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.5), and let T ⊆ gr A be a left and right Ore set of homogeneous elements. Define S := {s ∈ A : gr s ∈ T}, the Ore localisation A_S (`OreLocalization`) with its induced filtration, and the microlocalisation Q_T(A) as the completion of A_S for that filtration; for a finitely generated A-module M with a good filtration, Q_T(M) is the completion of M_S for the induced filtration (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §§2.1–2.4, the Definition of Q_T(A) and Q_T(M), the Lemma and the Corollary, pp. 7–9, citing Li–Van Oystaeyen). A filtered map φ : A → B (φ(F_nA) ⊆ F_nB for all n) is strict if φ(F_nA) = φ(A) ∩ F_nB for every n, and degree-preserving if gr φ : gr A → gr B is injective, that is, φ carries F_nA ∖ `F_{n−1}A` into F_nB ∖ `F_{n−1}B`; for separated filtrations a degree-preserving map is injective and strict.

- S is an Ore set of A (`OreLocalization.OreSet`), the induced filtration on A_S is Zariskian with gr(A_S) ≅ (gr A)_T, and good filtrations localise to good filtrations.
- The map A → Q_T(A) is a filtered ring map, and gr Q_T(A) ≅ (gr A)_T, the associated graded map being the localisation map gr A → (gr A)_T (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §2.4, Lemma (b)). It need not be strict, nor injective (Checks).
- If every element of T is regular in gr A, the localisation map gr A → (gr A)_T is injective, so A → Q_T(A) is degree-preserving, hence injective and strict: F_nA is the preimage of F_nQ_T(A).
- Q_T(A) is flat as a right A-module: for every injective A-linear map P → P′ of left A-modules, the induced map Q_T(A) ⊗_A P → Q_T(A) ⊗_A P′ of extensions of scalars along A → Q_T(A) (`TauCeti.BalancedExtension.map`) is injective. Q_T(A) is flat as a left A-module: the same holds for P ⊗_A Q_T(A) → P′ ⊗_A Q_T(A) on right A-modules, the extension of scalars along the opposite map Aᵐᵒᵖ → Q_T(A)ᵐᵒᵖ (`RingHom.op`; Convention 7).
- Q_T(A) ⊗_A M ≅ Q_T(M) for every finitely generated M with a good filtration.
- Q_T(M) = 0 iff every element of M is killed by some s ∈ S.
- A filtered ring map φ : A → B into a complete separated filtered ring such that gr φ carries T into the units of gr B extends uniquely to a filtered ring map Q_T(A) → B; if φ is degree-preserving, so is the extension, whose graded map is the localisation (gr A)_T → gr B of gr φ.

*Needs:* Mathlib `OreLocalization`, `OreLocalization.OreSet`, `IsRingFiltration`, `RingHom.op`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.BalancedExtension`, `TauCeti.BalancedExtension.map` (the one-sided tensor products of the flatness statements); NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.5 (Zariskian filtrations).

**Checks.**

- For T = {1}, S = 1 + `F_{−1}A` is the set of elements with symbol 1; these are units, because the Zariskian condition puts `F_{−1}A` in the Jacobson radical of F₀A, so A_S = A and Q_T(A) = Â is the completion of A for its filtration, which is A exactly when the filtration is complete. For A = ℤ_(3), the local ring at 3 with its 3-adic filtration (Zariskian, not complete), Q_T(A) = ℤ₃ ≠ A; for A = ℤ₃, Q_T(A) = A.
- For commutative A with the I-adic filtration and T the powers of the symbol of a central regular x, Q_T(A) is the completion of A[1/x] for the induced filtration.
- For A = ℤ_p[[X]] with the (p, X)-adic filtration and T the powers of gr p, the microlocalisation is ℚ_p⟨X/p⟩ (power series in X/p with coefficients tending to zero): p + X² ∈ S becomes a unit, and X ∉ S is not inverted. Here gr A = 𝔽_p[P, X] is a domain, so A → Q_T(A) is degree-preserving: with `F_{−n}A` = (p, X)^n, both p and X = p·(X/p) lie in `F_{−1}` ∖ `F_{−2}` of A and of Q_T(A), X/p having degree 0.
- Strictness fails without regularity. Let A = ℚ₃[x, x⁻¹] with F_nA the span of the x^i with |i| ≤ n for n ≥ 0 and F_nA = 0 for n < 0. The filtration is exhaustive, separated and complete (`F_{−1}A` = 0), its Rees ring is ℚ₃[t, u, v]/(uv − t²), noetherian, so it is Zariskian, and gr A = ℚ₃[X, Y]/(XY) with X, Y the degree-one symbols of x and x⁻¹. For T the powers of X, Y is killed in (gr A)_T = ℚ₃[X, X⁻¹], and Q_T(A) is the field ℚ₃((x⁻¹)) of Laurent series in x⁻¹ filtered by the degree in x. The element x⁻¹ has degree 1 in A and degree −1 in Q_T(A): it lies in `F_{−1}Q_T(A)` although `F_{−1}A` = 0, so A → Q_T(A) is injective and filtered but not strict. A statement asserting strictness for every homogeneous Ore set would fail here.
- Injectivity fails without regularity. For A = ℚ_p[[x, y]]/(xy) with its (x, y)-adic filtration, gr A = ℚ_p[X, Y]/(XY), and T the powers of X, the element y ≠ 0 of A is killed by x ∈ S, so it maps to 0 in A_S and in Q_T(A).
- For A = ℤ_p[[X]] with its (p, X)-adic filtration and T the powers of gr p, the nonzero module M = ℤ_p[[X]]/(p) = 𝔽_p[[X]] is S-torsion (p ∈ S and pM = 0), so its microlocalisation is 0: Q_T(A) ⊗_A M = Q_T(A)/Q_T(A)p = 0 because p is a unit of Q_T(A) = ℚ_p⟨X/p⟩. Microlocalisation is flat but not faithfully flat.
- Flatness is a statement about one-sided tensor products over a ring that need not be commutative. For A = M₂(ℚ₃) with the discrete filtration (F_nA = A for n ≥ 0 and F_nA = 0 for n < 0, so gr A = A in degree 0 and the Rees ring A[t] is noetherian) and T = {1}, one has S = {1} and Q_T(A) = A, which is not commutative (E₁₂E₂₁ = E₁₁ ≠ E₂₂ = E₂₁E₁₂); both flatness statements hold because A ⊗_A P ≅ P and P ⊗_A A ≅ P carry an injection to the same injection. A formulation of flatness through modules over a commutative ring of scalars does not apply to this A.

### 3.2 The m-adic filtration of the Iwasawa algebra of a uniform group

Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformiser p (unramified over ℤ_p) and residue field k, which may be finite or infinite (for example R = W(𝔽̄_p)), G uniform of dimension d with minimal generators g_i, b_i = g_i − 1, R[[G]] = `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra` R G = lim_N R[G/N], and m = ker(R[[G]] → k) the maximal ideal. Prove that every element of R[[G]] is uniquely a convergent ordered series Σ_α λ_α b^α with λ_α ∈ R, so that R[[G]] ≅ ∏_α R·b^α as R-modules, and that its m-adic degree is deg(Σ λ_α b^α) = min_α (v(λ_α) + |α|). Consequently the m-adic filtration is complete, it is the weighted filtration of NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.8 for the valuation ω_U when R is finite over ℤ_p, and gr_m R[[G]] ≅ k[P] `⊗_{𝔽_p[P]}` gr_m ℤ_p[[G]] ≅ U(gr G `⊗_{𝔽_p}` k) as graded k[t]-algebras (t = P the symbol of p), where gr G ⊗ k ≅ t·h_k[t] with h_k = h/ph and h = (1/p) R L_G; in particular gr_m R[[G]] is a noetherian domain and the m-adic filtration is Zariskian (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.1, where no assumption on the residue field is made, §10.2 Lemma, §10.3 Theorem and Corollary, pp. 67–69; Schneider, Theorem 28.3, p. 199). The degree formula and the description of gr need p to be a uniformiser of R. For R finite over ℤ_p the ordered series and the graded ring are NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.8, whose coefficient rings are finite free over ℤ_p; for a residue field infinite over 𝔽_p this roadmap proves them from the case R = ℤ_p, as below.

Prove also, for every open normal subgroup N of G, that R[[G]] is free as a left and as a right R[[N]]-module on any set of representatives of the cosets of N, and is the crossed product R[[N]] ∗ (G/N) in the sense of §1.2: for every open normal subgroup U of G contained in N, R[G/U] is free on either side over R[N/U] on the images of the representatives; these decompositions are compatible as U shrinks, so the inverse limit, a limit of free modules of the fixed finite rank [G : N] with compatible bases, is free on the same representatives; conjugation by a representative preserves R[[N]], and the product of two representatives is δ_n times a third for some n ∈ N, δ_n being a unit of R[[N]] (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.6, proof of the Proposition, (b) and (c), p. 72, where §10.1 makes no assumption on the residue field). For R finite over ℤ_p this is NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.3; §3.8 uses it with N = `G^{p^n}` for every unramified R.

*Proof sketch.* For R = ℤ_p the ordered-series description and the graded ring for ω_U are NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §0.8, and the m-adic topology of ℤ_p[[G]] is its compact topology. For general R, R[G/N] = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` ℤ_p[G/N] with ℤ_p[G/N] finite free and R p-adically complete, so R[[G]] is the limit of the R ⊗ ℤ_p[[G]]/V over the open ideals V of ℤ_p[[G]]; computed along the cofinal family of the powers V = `m_{ℤ_p}^n` of the maximal ideal of ℤ_p[[G]], for which ℤ_p[[G]]/`m_{ℤ_p}^n` = `⊕_{|α|<n} (ℤ/p^{n−|α|})·b^α`, this limit is ∏_α R·b^α. The structure constants of the ordered basis lie in ℤ_p and satisfy the degree inequality of the case R = ℤ_p, so the degree formula defines a ring filtration of R[[G]]; its n-th piece is m^n, because m = R[[G]]p + Σ_i R[[G]]b_i (group each series by the last factor of its monomials) and a series of degree ≥ n is a finite sum of R-multiples of the p^a b^α with a + |α| = n, |α| < n, and of series y·b^β with |β| = n. The multiplication map k[P] `⊗_{𝔽_p[P]}` gr ℤ_p[[G]] → gr R[[G]] is a homomorphism because R is central, and bijective because both sides are free over k on the symbols P^a b̄^α (PBW for U(gr G) over 𝔽_p[P]). The comparison gr G ⊗ k ≅ t·h_k[t] uses that commutators agree modulo `G^{p²}` with the Lazard bracket of §0.1 (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, Lemma 10.2).

*Needs:* §0.1; NoncommutativeAndEquivariantIwasawa §§0.5, 0.7, 0.8 (with ℤ_p coefficients for the extension to an infinite residue field) and §0.3 (the crossed product for R finite over ℤ_p); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`, `Module.Basis.pbwBasis` (PBW basis of U(t·h_k[t]) over k[t]).

**Checks.**

- For G = ℤ₃ with b = γ − 1, R = ℤ₃ gives gr_m R[[G]] = 𝔽₃[P, X] and R = W(𝔽̄₃) gives 𝔽̄₃[P, X], with P = gr 3 and X = gr b in degree 1; in degree n each has dimension n + 1 over its residue field, so the second is infinite-dimensional over 𝔽₃. A description through gr_m ℤ₃[[G]] alone, without the factor k[P] `⊗_{𝔽₃[P]}` −, would give 𝔽₃[P, X] in both cases.
- For G = {1}, R[[G]] = R, m = pR, deg λ = v(λ) and gr_m R = k[P]; the formula min_α (v(λ_α) + |α|) has the single term α = 0.
- The finite-free coefficient extension does not produce R[[G]] when k is infinite: for G = ℤ_p, R = W(𝔽̄_p) and Teichmüller lifts [x_n] of elements x_n ∈ 𝔽̄_p linearly independent over 𝔽_p, the series Σ_n [x_n] b^n lies in R[[G]], but it is not in the image of R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` ℤ_p[[G]], whose elements have all their coefficients in one finitely generated ℤ_p-submodule of R, with finite-dimensional image in k. For R finite over ℤ_p the two agree.

### 3.3 The microlocal Ore set S₀

Under the hypotheses of §3.2, the powers of gr p form a multiplicatively closed set of homogeneous central regular elements of the noetherian domain gr_m R[[G]], hence an Ore set T. Define S₀ ⊆ R[[G]] as the associated set of §3.1, S₀ = {x ∈ R[[G]] : gr x = (gr p)^a for some a ≥ 0} = `⋃_{a ≥ 0}` (p^a + `m^{a+1}`), and for n ≥ 0 the analogous set S_n ⊆ `R[[G^{p^n}]]` (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.4, the definition of S, p. 70, and §10.6, the sets S_n, p. 71; Fu, §4, equation (17), p. 14).

- S₀ is an Ore set of R[[G]] not containing 0, and x ∈ S₀ iff x ∈ p^a + `m^{a+1}` for some a ≥ 0, iff gr x = (gr p)^a.
- gr p is central and regular in gr_m R[[G]]: it is t in degree 1 of k[t] ⊆ U(t·h_k[t]).
- S_n is stable under conjugation by G, since conjugation preserves the m_n-adic filtration of `R[[G^{p^n}]]` and fixes p.
- The set is formed only for R unramified; over a ramified K the microlocalisation is obtained by base change (§3.6).

*Needs:* §§3.1, 3.2.

**Checks.**

- p ∈ S₀ (a = 1) and 1 ∈ S₀ (a = 0).
- For G = {1}, R[[G]] = R, m = pR and S₀ = ⋃_a p^a(1 + pR), the elements x whose unit part `p^{−v(x)}x` has residue 1: for R = ℤ₃, 1, 3 ∈ S₀ but the nonzero unit 2 ∉ S₀ (its residue is 2). S₀ is not R ∖ {0}, only a subset whose saturation is R ∖ {0}; its localisation `R_{S₀}` = R[1/p] = K is still passage to K, since 1 + pR consists of units.
- b_i ∉ S₀: its symbol has degree 1 but is not gr p. For G = ℤ_p and R = ℤ_p, S₀ contains p + b² but not b + p²: its elements are those whose leading form is a power of p.
- For R = ℤ_p[√p] (uniformiser ϖ = √p, so p = ϖ² ∈ m²) the set ⋃_a (p^a + `m^{a+1}`) contains 0 = p − p ∈ p + m², so it is not an Ore set of nonzerodivisors over a ramified coefficient ring.

### 3.4 The microlocalisation of R[[G]] is the completed enveloping algebra

Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring with uniformiser p and fraction field K, its residue field finite or infinite (§3.2), G a uniform pro-p group with minimal generators g_i, and h = (1/p) R L_G with R-basis u_i corresponding to the g_i. Prove: the exponential exp(u) converges in A := Û(h)_K (§2.1) for u ∈ L_G ⊆ p h, and g ↦ exp(log g) is an injective group homomorphism G → A^×; it extends to a degree-preserving ring homomorphism ψ : R[[G]] → A (§3.1) with ψ(b_i) = exp(p u_i) − 1 ≡ p u_i, sending gr p to the invertible symbol s and gr b_i to s u_i; and the induced map from the microlocalisation is an isomorphism of complete ℤ-filtered rings `Q_{gr p}(R[[G]])` ≅ Û(h)_K. Consequently K[[G]] → Û(h)_K is flat on both sides and, for a finitely generated K[[G]]-module M, Û(h)_K `⊗_{K[[G]]}` M = 0 iff M is S₀-torsion. For n ≥ 0, `Q_{gr p}(R[[G^{p^n}]])` ≅ `Û(h)_{n,K}` (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.4, Theorem and its proof, pp. 70–71; §10.5, Remarks (a)–(d), p. 71; §10.6, Proposition (a), p. 72; Fu, §4, equation (18), p. 14).

*Proof sketch.* The Campbell–Hausdorff formula shows log(exp u exp v) ∈ L_G, so u ↦ exp u identifies G with exp(L_G) ⊂ A^×. Then ψ(b^α) ≡ `p^{|α|}` u^α modulo `p^{|α|+1}` F₀A, so with the degree formula of §3.2 and the PBW basis of gr₀ A ≅ U(h_k), deg ψ(x) = deg x on R[G], and ψ extends by density to a degree-preserving map. Since gr A ≅ U(h_k)[s, `s^{−1}`], ψ extends to the microlocalisation by the universal property of §3.1, and the extension is again degree-preserving, hence injective; its graded map is also surjective, so it is bijective, both filtrations being complete. Because gr_m R[[G]] is a domain (§3.2), the canonical map R[[G]] → `Q_{gr p}(R[[G]])` is itself degree-preserving (§3.1); strictness here comes from this computation, not from the general theory. The microlocalisation already inverts p, so the target is the rational algebra Û(h)_K, not Û(h).

*Needs:* §§0.1, 2.1, 3.1, 3.2, 3.3; Mathlib `NormedSpace.exp` (for the series in the Banach algebra Û(h)_K).

### 3.5 The Banach step `D_{1/p}(G, K)` is the completed enveloping algebra

Let p be odd, K a complete discretely valued extension of ℚ_p with ring of integers R, and G a uniform group with g = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G. Prove that there is a natural isomorphism of K-Banach algebras `D_{1/p}(G, K)` ≅ Û(g)_K extending g ↦ exp(log g) on G ⊂ D(G, K), with the norm `‖·‖_{1/p}` on the left and the norm of the lattice Û(g) on the right; `D_{1/p}(G, K)` consists of the series Σ d_α b^α with |d_α| `p^{−|α|}` → 0 (Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.1 and the Lemma of §5.2 with its proof, p. 14; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.5, Remark (c), p. 71; Fu, §4, p. 12, and the proof of Theorem 5.1, p. 21). The completion consists of null sequences, not of bounded ones (Source corrections, item 7).

*Proof sketch.* K contains a closed subfield K′, unramified over ℚ_p, with K/K′ finite and with the residue field of K, which may be infinite: the fraction field of a strict Cohen ring of that residue field mapped into R, as in §3.6. Over K′, §3.4 identifies the microlocalisation with `Û(g′)_{K′}`, and both sides are the completion of K′[G] for `‖·‖_{1/p}`. Base change to K uses §2.1 on the right and the coefficient description of `D_{1/p}` on the left.

*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.4, 3.6 (the coefficient subfield K′ only); DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.10 (a strict Cohen ring of the residue field and its coefficient map into R); §1.5 (the coefficient description of `D_{1/p}(G, K)`).

### 3.6 Microlocalisation over a ramified coefficient field

Let K be a complete discretely valued extension of ℚ_p with ring of integers R (possibly ramified, with finite or infinite residue field), G uniform, and K′ ⊆ K a closed subfield, unramified over ℚ_p (its ring of integers R′ has uniformiser p), with K/K′ finite. Such a K′ exists: DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.10 gives a strict Cohen ring R′ of the residue field k of R and a local ring map R′ → R inducing the identity of k; prove that this map is injective, that R is free over R′ on 1, π, …, `π^{e−1}` with e = v_π(p), and hence that K′ = R′[1/p] is a closed subfield of K, unramified over ℚ_p, with residue field k and [K : K′] = e. For K finite over ℚ_p one may also take K′ = ℚ_p (Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.2, proof of the Lemma, p. 14). Put `g_{R′}` = R′ `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G and g_R = R `⊗_{R′}` `g_{R′}` = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G. Define the microlocalisation of K[[G]] = K ⊗_R R[[G]] as the base change K `⊗_{K′}` (K′[[G]] → `Û(g_{R′})_{K′}`) of the map of §3.4 over R′. Prove: K[[G]] = K `⊗_{K′}` K′[[G]], because R is finite free over R′, so R[G/N] = R `⊗_{R′}` R′[G/N] at every finite level and R `⊗_{R′}` − commutes with the inverse limit; K `⊗_{K′}` `Û(g_{R′})_{K′}` ≅ Û(g_R)_K (§2.1, for the finite extension K/K′); the resulting map K[[G]] → Û(g_R)_K is flat on both sides and is the unique K-linear extension of g ↦ exp(log g) that is continuous on R[[G]] for its m-adic topology, so it does not depend on the choice of K′; and for every K[[G]]-module M, Û(g_R)_K `⊗_{K[[G]]}` M ≅ `Û(g_{R′})_{K′}` `⊗_{K′[[G]]}` M, where M is regarded as a K′[[G]]-module by restriction (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §3.9, Lemma, p. 17; Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.2, proof of the Lemma, p. 14; Fu, §4, equations (17)–(18), p. 14). For K finite over ℚ_p and K′ = ℚ_p this is the base change K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` (ℚ_p[[G]] → `Û(g_{ℤ_p})_{ℚ_p}`), the form used in §§3.7, 3.10 and 4.14. This replaces the direct use of the set (17) over a ramified R (Source corrections, item 3).

*Proof sketch.* The coefficient subfield: the kernel of R′ → R is a prime ideal of the discrete valuation ring R′ not containing p, since R has characteristic zero, so it is 0. Each quotient π^iR/`π^{i+1}`R, 0 ≤ i < e, is spanned over k = R′/p by the class of π^i, so 1, π, …, `π^{e−1}` span R/pR = R/π^eR over R′/p; as R′ and R are p-adically complete and separated, successive approximation writes every element of R as `Σ_{i<e} c_i π^i` with c_i ∈ R′, and R, finitely generated and torsion-free over R′, is free on these e elements. Since 1 is a basis vector, `p^n`R ∩ R′ = `p^n`R′, so the complete ring R′ is closed in R and K′ is closed in K. The microlocalisation: base change of completed enveloping algebras is §2.1; two such maps agree on G, hence on its R-linear span R[G], which is m-adically dense in R[[G]] = R `⊗_{R′}` R′[[G]] by the ordered series of §3.2, hence on R[[G]] and on K[[G]] (G itself is not dense: the augmentation is continuous, sends every g to 1 and 0 to 0). With B = K `⊗_{K′}` Û′, A = K `⊗_{K′}` K′[[G]] and Λ = K′[[G]], B ≅ Û′ ⊗_Λ A as a right A-module, K being central in each (`Algebra.TensorProduct.rid` for the identification K `⊗_{K′}` K′ = K), so B ⊗_A M ≅ Û′ ⊗_Λ (A ⊗_A M) ≅ Û′ ⊗_Λ M by associativity of one-sided tensor products (`TauCeti.BalancedTensorProduct.assoc`, `TauCeti.BalancedExtension.lid`). Flatness on the right follows: an injective map of A-modules is an injective map of Λ-modules, and Û′ is flat over Λ by §3.4; flatness on the left is the same argument for the opposite rings (Convention 7).

*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.2 (ordered series, for the density of R[G]), 3.4 (over the unramified R′, whose residue field may be infinite); DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra §1.10 (strict Cohen ring of k and the coefficient map R′ → R); Mathlib `Algebra.TensorProduct.rid`, `RingHom.op`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`, `TauCeti.BalancedExtension`, `TauCeti.BalancedExtension.lid`, `TauCeti.BalancedTensorProduct.assoc`.

**Checks.**

- For G = {1} the microlocalisation is the identity K → K, both for K = ℚ₃ and for K the completion of the maximal unramified extension of ℚ₃, with R = W(𝔽̄₃) not finite over ℤ₃ (K′ = K in the second case). A definition through K `⊗_{ℚ₃}` (ℚ₃[[G]] → `Û(g_{ℤ₃})_{ℚ₃}`) agrees for G = {1} but has the wrong source for that K as soon as G ≠ {1}: K `⊗_{ℚ₃}` ℚ₃[[ℤ₃]] is strictly smaller than K[[ℤ₃]] (§3.2, last check).
- The deformation index of the target grows with ramification: for K = ℚ₃(√3), π = √3, e = 2 and G the level-nine congruence kernel of SL₂(ℤ₃), g_R = 3·sl₂(R) = π²·sl₂(R), so Û(g_R)_K = `Û(sl₂(R))_{2,K}`; it does not contain Σ_j (3h)^j, whose coefficients in the basis (3h)^j are all 1, while `Û(sl₂(R))_{1,K}` = Û(π·sl₂(R))_K does, the coefficients there being π^j.
- For G = ℤ₃ with b = γ − 1 and K = ℚ₃(√3), the microlocalisation is K[[b]] → K⟨u⟩, b ↦ exp(3u) − 1 = 3u·(unit), the Tate algebra of the disc |b| ≤ 1/3, in which b/3 has norm 1. Microlocalising R[[b]] directly at the powers of gr π for its (π, b)-adic filtration, which is an Ore set over the ramified R, gives instead K⟨b/π⟩, the disc |b| ≤ `3^{−1/2}`, in which b/3 = (b/π)·π⁻¹ has norm `3^{1/2}`: a series Σ c_n (b/3)^n with |c_n| = `3^{−⌈n/4⌉}` converges in the first ring and not in the second. Base change from an unramified K′ is what produces Û(g_R)_K.
- The coefficient subfield. For R = W(𝔽̄₃)[π] with π² = 3 (e = 2, residue field 𝔽̄₃), the strict Cohen ring is R′ = W(𝔽̄₃), the coefficient map is the inclusion, R = R′ ⊕ R′π and [K : K′] = 2, although K is infinite over ℚ₃: the choice K′ = ℚ₃ would not make K/K′ finite. For R = ℤ₃, R′ = ℤ₃ and the rank is 1. In characteristic p the coefficient map is not injective (the map ℤ_p → 𝔽_p[[t]] kills p): injectivity uses that K has characteristic zero.

### 3.7 Microlocalisation of finitely generated Iwasawa modules

For K finite over ℚ_p, G uniform and M a finitely generated K[[G]]-module, define its microlocalisation M̂ := Û(g_K) `⊗_{K[[G]]}` M, with the natural K[[G]]-linear map η_M : M → M̂, m ↦ 1 ⊗ m (Fu, §4, the paragraph after equation (18), p. 14; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.5, Remark (d), p. 71, and §10.6, Proposition (e), p. 72).

- M ↦ M̂ is an exact functor on finitely generated K[[G]]-modules, natural in M, with values in finitely generated Û(g_K)-modules.
- K[[G]]^ = Û(g_K).
- For K = ℚ_p, M̂ = 0 iff M is S₀-torsion; for general K the criterion holds after restriction to ℚ_p[[G]] (§3.6).
- Microlocalisation is not faithful: S₀-torsion modules are killed. Faithful flatness of K[[G]] → D(G, K) (PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1) is a different statement.

*Needs:* §§3.1, 3.4, 3.6.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial K[[G]]-module K, K̂ ≅ K and η_K is an isomorphism: the augmentation of every s ∈ S₀ lies in p^a(1 + pℤ_p) and is nonzero, so S₀ acts invertibly.
- 0̂ = 0.
- For G = 1 + pℤ_p ≅ ℤ_p with b = (1 + p) − 1, the module M = ℚ_p[[b]]/((1 + b)^p − 1) of functions on G/G^p has M̂ ≅ ℚ_p: under b ↦ exp(pu) − 1, (1 + b)^p − 1 = exp(p²u) − 1 = p²u·v with v = Σ_k (p²u)^k/(k + 1)! a unit of ℚ_p⟨u⟩ (each term with k ≥ 1 has norm `p^{−2k + v_p((k+1)!)}` < 1), so only the trivial character survives and the p − 1 nontrivial characters, defined over ℚ_p(ζ_p), are killed.
- For the same M, D(G, ℚ_p) `⊗_{ℚ_p[[G]]}` M ≅ M is p-dimensional (faithful flatness, PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1), while M̂ is one-dimensional: microlocalisation and passage to distributions differ.

### 3.8 Lie modules and the semisimplicity theorem

Let R be unramified with uniformiser p, K = Frac R, and G a uniform group satisfying the hypotheses of Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.12 without the condition on p: there are a connected split semisimple group scheme 𝔾 over R with Lie algebra 𝔤 and an integer a ≥ 0 (the congruence depth) with `p^{a+1}` 𝔤 = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` L_G; the congruence kernel `ker(𝔾(ℤ_p) → 𝔾(ℤ/p^{a+1}))` is the example. Ardakov–Wadsley also assume 𝔾 simply connected (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §6.7, the standing assumption from there on, p. 45); here that assumption is made only where it is used, in the second half of (b) and in (c). The central isogeny from the simply connected cover 𝔾_sc → 𝔾 identifies Lie(𝔾_sc) with an R-Lie subalgebra 𝔤_sc ⊆ 𝔤 of finite index (injective because R is torsion-free, of finite index because the fibres over K agree); fix c ≥ 0 with `p^c` 𝔤 ⊆ 𝔤_sc, so c = 0 when 𝔾 is simply connected. Put U_n = `Q_{gr p}(R[[G^{p^n}]])` ≅ `Û(𝔤)_{n+a,K}` (§3.4) and let U_n ∗ H_n, H_n = `G/G^{p^n}`, be the crossed products of Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.6. Let ℳ be the category of K[[G]]-modules that are finite-dimensional over K, and define the Lie modules as the objects of ℳ obtained from finite-dimensional U₀-modules by restriction along K[[G]] → U₀, and the Artin modules as those on which an open subgroup of G acts trivially. Prove:

- (P) If M ∈ ℳ is S_n-torsion-free, η_M : M → (U_n ∗ H_n) `⊗_{K[[G]]}` M is an isomorphism; every M ∈ ℳ is S_n-torsion-free for all n ≥ n₀, for some n₀ depending on M (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §11.1 Proposition, p. 79, and Corollary 10.11, pp. 76–77).
- (a) ℳ is semisimple: every submodule of an object of ℳ has a K[[G]]-stable complement (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §11.1, Theorem (a), p. 79, for 𝔾 simply connected; for general 𝔾 by the same proof at a deeper level n, below).
- (b) U₀ `⊗_{K[[G]]}` − is an equivalence between the Lie modules and the finite-dimensional U(𝔤_K)-modules that admit a `p^a`𝔤-stable R-lattice, equivalently whose action extends to U₀ ≅ Û(`p^a`𝔤)_K, and a simple Lie module V has `End_{K[[G]]}(V)` = K. If 𝔾 is simply connected, every finite-dimensional U(𝔤_K)-module admits such a lattice (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §9.2, Proposition and its proof, p. 61), so the Lie modules are equivalent to all finite-dimensional U(𝔤_K)-modules (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §11.1, Theorem (b) and Corollary, p. 80).
- (c) If 𝔾 is simply connected, every simple M ∈ ℳ is V ⊗ ρ with V a simple Lie module and ρ a simple Artin module, and the factors are unique up to isomorphism (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §11.2 Lemma and Theorem, pp. 80–81, and §11.3 Theorem, p. 81).

Simple connectivity cannot be dropped from the second half of (b). For p = 3, 𝔾 = PGL₃ over ℤ₃ and a = 0, 𝔤 is the image 𝔞 of gl₃(ℤ₃) in sl₃(ℚ₃) under X ↦ X − tr(X)·I/3, which contains q = diag(1, 1, −2)/3, with [q, E₁₃] = E₁₃, [q, E₂₃] = E₂₃ and [q, E₁₂] = 0; G is the first congruence kernel (1 + 3M₃(ℤ₃))/(1 + 3ℤ₃) of PGL₃(ℤ₃), with L_G = 3𝔞 through the matrix logarithm. On the standard representation ℚ₃³ of sl₃(ℚ₃), q has eigenvalues 1/3, 1/3, −2/3 (det q = −2/27, of valuation −3), so no lattice is q-stable; correspondingly 1 − 3q is a unit of Û(𝔞), with inverse Σ_n (3q)^n, but kills e₁ on ℚ₃³, so ℚ₃³ is not U₀ ⊗ V for any Lie module V. Here 𝔤_sc = sl₃(ℤ₃) and c = 1: the deeper lattice 3𝔞 lies in sl₃(ℤ₃) and stabilises ℤ₃³.

The headline instance is `isSemisimpleModule_of_finiteDimensional`: for p odd, m ≥ 0 and G = G₁^m (`firstCongruenceProduct` p m), every module M over ℚ_p `⊗_{ℤ_p}` ℤ_p[[G]] that is finite-dimensional over ℚ_p, the scalars acting through the algebra, is semisimple (`IsSemisimpleModule`). Here 𝔾 = SL₂^m over ℤ_p, which is simply connected, the congruence depth is a = 0 and the congruence level a + 1 = 1 by §0.3, whatever the number m of factors, and the modules are abstract modules over the rational Iwasawa algebra, with no continuity hypothesis. For m = 0 the algebra is ℚ_p and the statement is semisimplicity of vector spaces.

*Proof sketch.* (P) for n = 0: a finite-dimensional S₀-torsion-free module is unchanged by localisation at S₀, since each s acts injectively and hence bijectively, and its R-lattice is already p-adically complete; general n by restriction to `G^{p^n}` and the crossed-product identity (U_n ∗ H_n) ⊗ M ≅ U_n `⊗_{K[[G^{p^n}]]}` M. (a): by (P), V is S_n-torsion-free for every n ≥ n₀; take such an n with n + a ≥ c and identify V with a module over U_n ∗ H_n. Then `p^{n+a}`𝔤 ⊆ 𝔤_sc, and every finite-dimensional U(𝔤_K)-module has a 𝔤_sc-stable, hence `p^{n+a}`𝔤-stable, R-lattice (highest-weight theory for the simply connected 𝔾_sc: Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §9.2, proof of the Proposition), so the argument of Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §9.2, Proposition and Corollary, shows that restriction identifies finite-dimensional U_n-modules with finite-dimensional U(𝔤_K)-modules, which are semisimple by Weyl's theorem; averaging a U_n-linear projection over the finite group H_n in characteristic zero gives a K[[G]]-linear one. (b): restriction and base change are adjoint, and (P) with n = 0 shows that unit and counit are isomorphisms on Lie modules, so the Lie modules are equivalent to the finite-dimensional U₀-modules; restriction along U(𝔤_K) ⊆ U₀ is fully faithful on these (finite-codimensional ideals of the noetherian Banach algebra U₀ are closed, so its finite-dimensional modules are continuous, and U(𝔤_K) is dense), with essential image the modules with a `p^a`𝔤-stable lattice (such a lattice is finitely generated, hence p-adically complete, hence a Û(`p^a`𝔤)-module; conversely the unit ball of U₀ acts through a bounded set of operators, which carries any lattice into a stable one).

*Needs:* §§0.3, 2.1, 3.2 (the crossed product R[[G]] = `R[[G^{p^n}]]` ∗ H_n, for every unramified R), 3.3, 3.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_isCompl_of_isKilling` (Weyl's complete reducibility theorem for 𝔤_K); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`; Mathlib `IsSemisimpleModule`, `TensorProduct`.

**Checks.**

- The trivial module K is a Lie module, the restriction of the U₀-module K on which 𝔤_K acts by 0 (each g ∈ G acts by exp(0) = 1), and an Artin module; a module that is both is trivial. If an open subgroup V acts trivially on a Lie module and u ∈ L_V acts on it by the matrix A, then exp(tu), t ∈ ℤ_p, acts by exp(tA) = 1 for all t, so A = 0; as L_V spans 𝔤_K, 𝔤_K acts by 0, U₀ acts through its augmentation and G acts trivially.
- For G = G₁ (𝔾 = SL₂, R = ℤ_p, a = 0), Sym²(ℚ_p²) with basis x², xy, y² (x = e₁, y = e₂) is a simple Lie module (§3.9) and not an Artin module: exp(pe) fixes x and sends y to y + px, hence y² to y² + 2p·xy + p²·x², and its p^n-th power exp(`p^{n+1}`e) sends y² to y² + 2`p^{n+1}`·xy + `p^{2n+2}`·x² ≠ y²; every open subgroup of G₁ contains `G₁^{p^n}`, the kernel of reduction modulo `p^{n+1}`, for some n, and with it exp(`p^{n+1}`e).
- For G = G₁ and a surjection G₁ → ℤ/p through G₁/G₁^p ≅ 𝔽_p³ (§0.3), the field ℚ_p(ζ_p), with a generator of ℤ/p acting by multiplication by ζ_p, is a simple Artin module of dimension p − 1 over K = ℚ_p (its invariant subspaces are ℚ_p(ζ_p)-subspaces, the p-th cyclotomic polynomial being irreducible over ℚ_p) and is not a Lie module, by the first check, since G₁ acts nontrivially. K = ℚ_p is unramified, as §3.8 requires; ζ_p itself generates a ramified extension.
- Semisimplicity needs 𝔤 semisimple. For G = ℤ_p (p odd; uniform, with abelian Lie algebra, so outside the hypotheses) with topological generator γ, the K[[G]]-module K[[G]]/((γ − 1)²) has basis 1, γ − 1, on which γ acts by the matrix with rows (1, 0) and (1, 1); its only γ-stable line is K·(γ − 1), so that submodule has no stable complement.

### 3.9 The Lie module attached to a finite-dimensional representation

Let K be finite over ℚ_p with ring of integers R, G uniform with g_R = (1/p) R L_G and g_K split semisimple, W a finite-dimensional U(g_K)-module and W₀ ⊆ W a g_R-stable R-lattice. Define 𝓛(W) as W with the K[[G]]-module structure obtained as follows: the U(g_R)-action on W₀ extends continuously to Û(g_R), since W₀ is finite free and hence p-adically complete; so the U(g_K)-action on W extends to Û(g_K); pull it back along K[[G]] → Û(g_K) of §3.6 (Fu, §4, the paragraphs following equation (18), p. 14; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §9.2, Proposition and its proof, p. 61).

- If g_R is contained in a Chevalley lattice of g_K (the Lie algebra over R of a simply connected split semisimple group scheme whose Lie algebra over K is g_K; for instance g_R = sl₂(R)^m for G = G₁^m), a g_R-stable lattice exists for every finite-dimensional W, by highest-weight theory (Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §9.2, proof of the Proposition, p. 61). Without that hypothesis it need not exist, and then W is not of the form 𝓛(−) (Checks; §3.8). When W₀ exists, 𝓛(W) does not depend on its choice: two lattices are commensurable, and the extended actions agree on the dense subalgebra U(g_K).
- An element g ∈ G acts on 𝓛(W) by exp(log g), and the differentiated action of 𝓛(W) is the given g_K-action (`LieModule`).
- A U(g_K)-linear map W → W′ is K[[G]]-linear 𝓛(W) → 𝓛(W′).
- If W is irreducible, 𝓛(W) is an irreducible Lie module with `End_{K[[G]]}(𝓛(W))` = K (§3.8 (b)).
- For G = G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) and W = Sym^k(K²) with the lattice W₀ = Sym^k(ℤ_p²), on which G₁ acts continuously through the algebraic representation, 𝓛(W) is K ⊗ W₀ with the ℤ_p[[G₁]]-module structure of `TauCeti.IsCompactModule.completedGroupAlgebraModule` on the compact ℤ_p-module W₀: both module structures are continuous, agree on the group elements (g acts by exp(log g) = g, by `TauCeti.IsCompactModule.completedSMul_of`), and ℤ_p[G₁] is dense in ℤ_p[[G₁]]. This is the module structure that §5.2 (i) uses.

*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.6, 3.8; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_isCompl_of_isKilling` (complete reducibility, for the highest-weight lattices of the proof of §3.8 (b)); Mathlib `LieModule`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCompactModule.completedGroupAlgebraModule`, `TauCeti.IsCompactModule.completedSMul_of`.

**Checks.**

- For W = K the trivial g_K-module, 𝓛(W) is the trivial K[[G]]-module, on which K[[G]] acts through the augmentation.
- For G = G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) and W = ℚ_p² the standard representation, exp(pe) ∈ G₁ acts by the matrix with rows (1, p) and (0, 1), the exponential of p·e.
- For W = Sym^k(ℚ_p²) with lattice Sym^k(ℤ_p²), 𝓛(W) is the restriction to G₁ of the algebraic representation Sym^k of SL₂(ℤ_p).
- For p = 3 and G the first congruence kernel of PGL₃(ℤ₃), g_R is the image 𝔞 of gl₃(ℤ₃) in sl₃(ℚ₃) (§3.8), which contains q = diag(1, 1, −2)/3; the standard representation W = ℚ₃³ has no g_R-stable lattice, since q has the nonintegral eigenvalue 1/3 (det q = −2/27). A definition that took a stable lattice to exist for every W would assign 𝓛(W) to this W; but no Û(𝔞)_K-module structure on W extends the sl₃(ℚ₃)-action, since the unit 1 − 3q of Û(𝔞) would have to act by the singular matrix diag(0, 0, 3).
- Over K containing ζ_p, a nontrivial character of G₁ trivial on G₁^p is an Artin module but not a Lie module: a one-dimensional Lie module comes from a one-dimensional sl₂(K)-module, which is trivial because sl₂ is perfect, so G₁ would act trivially.

### 3.10 Microlocalisation preserves W-isotypic quotients

Let K be finite over ℚ_p and G uniform with g_K split semisimple, satisfying the hypotheses of §3.8; let W be an absolutely irreducible finite-dimensional g_K-module with a stable lattice, viewed as a Lie module (§3.9). Write Ann(W) ⊆ U(g_K), Ânn(W) ⊆ Û(g_K) and Ãnn(W) ⊆ K[[G]] for the annihilators, and for a finitely generated K[[G]]-module M put M_W = M/Ãnn(W)M and M̂_W = M̂/Ânn(W)M̂. Prove that U(g_K)/Ann(W) ≅ Û(g_K)/Ânn(W) ≅ End_K(W), and that for every finitely generated K[[G]]-module M the natural map M_W → M̂_W is an isomorphism, naturally in M; in particular K[[G]]/Ãnn(W) ≅ Û(g_K)/Ânn(W) ≅ End_K(W) (Fu, §4, displays (19)–(20) and Theorem 4.3 with its proof, pp. 14–15). Surjectivity of K[[G]]/Ãnn(W) → End_K(W) is part of the conclusion.

*Proof sketch.* U(g_K)/Ann(W) ≅ End_K(W) is the density theorem for the absolutely irreducible W (Fu (19), citing Bourbaki VIII §6.2), and U(g_K) is dense in Û(g_K), which acts through the same finite-dimensional quotient. Apply the exact functor of §3.7 to 0 → Ãnn(W)M → M → M_W → 0: M_W is a finite sum of copies of W, hence S₀-torsion-free with S₀ acting invertibly, so M_W ≅ Û ⊗ M_W by §3.8 (P) with n = 0; for ramified K, (P) is applied over ℚ_p through §3.6. Maximality of M̂_W gives Ânn(W)M̂ ⊆ Û ⊗ Ãnn(W)M, and Û·Ãnn(W) ⊆ Ânn(W) gives the reverse inclusion.

*Needs:* §§3.6, 3.7, 3.8, 3.9; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_isCompl_of_isKilling` (M_W is a finite sum of copies of W).

## Layer 4: Infinitesimal characters and injectivity of central specialisation

The endpoint of the roadmap. Ardakov–Wadsley's faithfulness of affinoid Verma modules gives Fu's injectivity theorem for every λ ∈ ℤ_p^m after a finite extension of coefficients, followed by descent (§§4.12–4.14). Fu's own route, for induced λ, passes through distributions: density of eigenfunctions in C(G, ℚ_p), the projective limit over the Banach steps, a domain property of central quotients at the radii r_n and a crossed-product grade argument (§§4.3–4.11). The domain property at the radii r_n rests on a separatedness condition that the sources do not establish (§4.7 (iii)); the targets of the direct route carry it as an explicit hypothesis, and the end theorem does not use it.

### 4.1 Central specialisations of distribution and enveloping algebras

Let 𝔾 be a connected split reductive group over ℚ_p with Lie algebra g, G a compact open subgroup of 𝔾(ℚ_p), K/ℚ_p finite, Z(g) the centre of U(g), Z_K = Z(U(g_K)) = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` Z(g) the centre of U(g_K), and λ : Z_K → K a K-algebra character, equivalently a ℚ_p-algebra homomorphism Z(g) → K extended K-linearly (Conventions, item 6). Z(g) is a finitely generated commutative ℚ_p-algebra (second bullet below). For a finite set z₁, …, z_n generating Z(g) as a ℚ_p-algebra, whose images 1 ⊗ z_i generate Z_K as a K-algebra, ker λ ⊆ Z_K is generated by z₁ − λ(z₁), …, z_n − λ(z_n): modulo these elements every z_i is congruent to the scalar λ(z_i), so the quotient of Z_K by the ideal they generate is spanned by 1, and λ maps it onto K. Define D(G, K)_λ := D(G, K) `⊗_{Z_K,λ}` K = D(G, K)/Σ_i D(G, K)(z_i − λ(z_i)), and the eigenspace C^la(G, K)[λ] of locally analytic functions f with zf = λ(z)f for all z ∈ Z_K (equivalently, for all z ∈ Z(g)), Z_K acting K-linearly through §1.3. When moreover p is odd and G is uniform, so that the Banach steps D_r(G, K), 1/p ≤ r < 1, of §1.5 (for the valuation ω_U, §0.1), the closures U_r(g, K) of §2.2 and the lattice g_R = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G of §0.1 are defined, define D^λ_r(G, K) := D_r(G, K)/D_r(G, K)·ker λ, U^λ_r(g, K) := U_r(g, K)/U_r(g, K)·ker λ and `Û(g_R)_{K,λ}` := Û(g_R)_K/Û(g_R)_K·ker λ, written U^λ_r(g) and Û(g)_λ when K = ℚ_p and R = ℤ_p (Fu, §5, the definitions before Theorem 5.2, p. 16, and of U^λ_r(g), D^λ_r(G, ℚ_p), p. 17, G being uniform from §4, p. 12, on; §3, the Casimirs Δ_i and the quotients, pp. 7–8).

- Z(U(g_K)) = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` Z(g) inside U(g_K) = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` U(g): K ⊗ 1 is central and generates U(g_K) together with 1 ⊗ U(g), so the centre of U(g_K) is the centraliser of 1 ⊗ U(g), which is the image of K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` Z(g) because K is free over ℚ_p (Mathlib `Subalgebra.centralizer_range_includeRight_eq_center_tensorProduct`); that image is K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` Z(g) itself, K being flat over ℚ_p. Restriction to Z(g) is a bijection from K-algebra characters of Z_K to ℚ_p-algebra homomorphisms Z(g) → K, with inverse a ⊗ z ↦ aλ(z).
- g is a reductive Lie algebra and Z(g) is finitely generated. ### 4.2 Induced infinitesimal characters

In the setting of §4.1, fix a Borel pair (𝔹, 𝕋) of 𝔾, let ℕ be the unipotent radical of 𝔹, and put T = 𝕋(ℚ_p) ∩ G, N = ℕ(ℚ_p) ∩ G and B = 𝔹(ℚ_p) ∩ G. Let g = n̄ ⊕ t ⊕ n be the triangular decomposition with t = Lie 𝕋, n = Lie ℕ and n̄ the Lie algebra of the unipotent radical ℕ̄ of the Borel subgroup opposite to 𝔹 with respect to 𝕋; no integral lattice is needed here (Fu, who takes G uniform, decomposes the lattice g₀ = `g_{ℤ_p}` as well). The Cartan subalgebra t = Lie 𝕋 contains the centre z of g, and t′ = t ∩ g′ is a Cartan subalgebra of g′ = [g, g] with t = z ⊕ t′; ad(t′) acts on g′ diagonalisably, with eigenvalues the differentials of the roots of the split torus 𝕋, which lie in ℚ_p, so `LieModule.IsTriangularizable` holds for t′ acting on g′; and the roots of t′ in n are the positive roots for a base b. Let HC : Z(g) → U(t) = S(t) be the Harish-Chandra homomorphism for this decomposition: the restriction to Z(g) of the projection of U(g) = U(t) ⊕ (n̄U(g) + U(g)n) onto U(t) along the second summand. It is a ℚ_p-algebra homomorphism, and under Z(g) ≅ S(z) ⊗ Z(g′) and S(t) = S(z) ⊗ S(t′) it is the identity of S(z) tensored with `TauCeti.hcProjection` b of g′ (Fu, §5, (24), p. 16, citing Bourbaki VIII §6.4; LieHighestWeight §9.2 for Z(g) ≅ S(z) ⊗ Z(g′)). A locally analytic character χ : T → 1 + pℤ_p has a derivative dχ : U(t) → ℚ_p. Let S be the antipode of U(g), the anti-automorphism with S(x) = −x on g; it preserves Z(g). Define a character λ : Z(g) → ℚ_p (the case K = ℚ_p of §4.1, where Z_K = Z(g)) to be induced if λ = dχ ∘ HC ∘ S for some such χ; this is the infinitesimal character by which Z(g) acts on the principal series Ind(χ) of §4.4 under the action of §1.1 (Fu, §5, the Harish-Chandra homomorphism (24) and the definition of induced characters, p. 16, where the character is written dχ ∘ HC; Source corrections, item 10). For 𝔾 = SL₂^m the antipode fixes each Casimir Δ_i, so induced means λ = dχ ∘ HC, as in Fu.

- For 𝔾 = SL₂ and G = G₁, T ∩ G₁ = {diag(t, t⁻¹) : t ∈ 1 + pℤ_p} ≅ ℤ_p, every continuous character to 1 + pℤ_p is t ↦ t^c with c ∈ ℤ_p, and dχ(h) = c.

With Δ = ½h² + h + 2fe, HC(Δ) = ½h² + h (½h² − h for the opposite Borel), so the character t ↦ t^c gives λ(Δ) = ½c² ± c and 2λ(Δ) + 1 = (c ± 1)²: λ is induced iff 2λ(Δ) + 1 is the square of an element of ℤ_p. The criterion does not depend on the choice of n versus n̄.

### 4.3 Strong duality after infinitesimal specialisation

In the setting of §4.1, with C^la(G, K)[ν] the eigenspace of a K-algebra character ν : Z_K → K of the centre of U(g_K) (§4.1) for the action of §1.1 (the contragredient of left translation) and S the antipode of U(g_K), the K-linear extension of the antipode of U(g) (§4.2), prove that the pairing between D(G, K) and the locally analytic functions induces a topological isomorphism of Fréchet spaces D(G, K)_λ ≅ C^la(G, K)[λ ∘ S]′_b; equivalently, for every character ν the annihilator of C^la(G, K)[ν] in D(G, K) is the closed ideal D(G, K)·ker(ν ∘ S) (Fu, Theorem 5.2, first assertion, and its proof, equations (25)–(26), p. 16; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2002, Proposition 3.7, p. 16; Source corrections, item 10). S restricts to an involutive K-algebra automorphism of the commutative algebra Z_K, so S(ker ν) = ker(ν ∘ S) and the two forms are equivalent. For 𝔾 = SL₂^m, S fixes every Δ_i (§4.2), so λ ∘ S = λ and D(G, K)_λ ≅ C^la(G, K)[λ]′_b. Without the antipode the statement fails: for the torus G = 1 + 3ℤ₃ ⊂ G_m(ℚ₃) = ℚ₃^×, x = 1 ∈ g = ℚ₃ (so exp(tx) = exp(t) for t ∈ 3ℤ₃) and f(g) = g, the action of §1.1 gives (x·f)(g) = (d/dt) exp(−t)g at t = 0, that is x·f = −f, so f ∈ C^la[ν] with ν(x) = −1; the element x̃ + δ₁ of D·ker ν pairs with f to x̃(f) + f(1) = 1 + 1 = 2 ≠ 0, while x̃ − δ₁, which generates D·ker(ν ∘ S), pairs to 1 − 1 = 0.

*Proof sketch.* The z_i − λ(z_i) act by continuous G-equivariant endomorphisms (§1.3), giving the strict left exact sequence 0 → C^la(G, K)[λ] → C^la(G, K) → C^la(G, K)^n, f ↦ ((z_i − λ(z_i))f)_i. For spaces of compact type the strong dual is exact on strict sequences, and the closed subspace has dual the quotient of D(G, K) by its annihilator (LocallyAnalyticDistributions §§0.2–0.3, with Hahn–Banach over the spherically complete K). The annihilator is the closure of the image of the transposed map. To compute it, write ρ(μ)f(h) = μ(g ↦ f(hg)) for the right regular action, so that ⟨μ ∗ μ′, f⟩ = ⟨μ, ρ(μ′)f⟩ by Conventions, item 3; the inversion ι(μ)(f) = μ(f ∘ inv) is an anti-automorphism of D(G, K) with ι(x̃) = −x̃, so ι = S on U(g); and for central ζ, δ_h ∗ ζ = ζ ∗ δ_h gives ζ(g ↦ f(gh)) = ζ(g ↦ f(hg)). Hence a central z acts by the action of §1.1 as ρ(S z), the transposed map is D(G, K)^n → D(G, K), (μ_i) ↦ Σ μ_i ∗ (S z_i − λ(z_i)), and its image is the finitely generated ideal D(G, K)·ker(λ ∘ S), ker(λ ∘ S) ⊆ Z_K being generated by the S z_i − λ(z_i) by §4.1 applied to the character λ ∘ S and the generating set S z₁, …, S z_n of Z_K (S is a K-algebra automorphism of Z_K), since (λ ∘ S)(S z_i) = λ(S²z_i) = λ(z_i); the ideal is closed (§4.1). ### 4.4 The locally analytic principal series and a nowhere-vanishing eigenfunction

In the setting of §4.2, for a locally analytic character χ : T → 1 + pℤ_p define Ind(χ) := {f ∈ C^la(G, ℚ_p) : f(gtn) = χ(t)f(g) for g ∈ G, t ∈ T, n ∈ N}, a closed G-stable subspace (G acting by left translation) (Fu, §5, the definition of Ind(χ), p. 16, and the proof of Theorem 5.2, second part, p. 17).

The functions in Ind(χ) are determined by their values along a section of the projection to the quotient manifold X := G/TN, which is G/B, Fu's quotient space, when B = TN (§4.2). Let ℕ̄ be the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup opposite to 𝔹 with respect to 𝕋 (§4.2), N̄ := ℕ̄(ℚ_p) ∩ G, and π : G → X the projection, X carrying the quotient topology. Prove:

- (i) The multiplication map μ : ℕ̄(ℚ_p) × 𝕋(ℚ_p) × ℕ(ℚ_p) → 𝔾(ℚ_p), (n̄, t, n) ↦ n̄tn, is injective with open image Ω(ℚ_p), and μ and its inverse Ω(ℚ_p) → ℕ̄(ℚ_p) × 𝕋(ℚ_p) × ℕ(ℚ_p) are locally analytic.
- (ii) For g ∈ G, the map φ_g : N̄ → X, n̄ ↦ gn̄TN, is a homeomorphism of the compact open subgroup N̄ of ℕ̄(ℚ_p) onto the compact open subset `U_g := π(gN̄TN)` of X, and for g, h ∈ G the transition `φ_h^{−1} ∘ φ_g` on `φ_g^{−1}(U_g ∩ U_h)` sends n̄ to the ℕ̄-component of `μ^{−1}(h^{−1}gn̄)`, a locally analytic map. These charts make X a compact ℚ_p-analytic manifold of dimension dim ℕ̄ = |Φ⁺|, the number of positive roots, for which π and the action G × X → X by left translation are locally analytic.
- (iii) There are g₁ = 1, g₂, …, g_k ∈ G such that the sets `V_i := U_{g_i} ∖ (U_{g_1} ∪ … ∪ U_{g_{i−1}})` form a finite cover of X by disjoint compact open subsets, and s(x) := `g_i·φ_{g_i}^{−1}(x)` for x ∈ V_i is a locally analytic section of π with s(π(1)) = 1.
- (iv) Every g ∈ G is uniquely s(π(g))·t·n with t ∈ T and n ∈ N; τ(g) := t defines a locally analytic map τ : G → T with τ(gt′n′) = τ(g)t′ for t′ ∈ T and n′ ∈ N, and τ ∘ s = 1; and restriction along s, f ↦ f ∘ s, and F ↦ (g ↦ χ(τ(g))F(π(g))) are mutually inverse topological isomorphisms Ind(χ) ≅ C^la(X, ℚ_p).

(Fu, §5, proof of Theorem 5.2, second part, p. 17, asserts the splitting X ↪ G ↠ X and the isomorphism Ind(χ) ≅ C^la(X, ℚ_p) for X = G/B without a construction; the construction from the big cell is this roadmap's.)

*Proof sketch.* (i): ReductiveGroups §9B.1 constructs, for the pinned split reductive group over ℤ of a root datum, the open immersion U⁻ × T × U⁺ → G onto the big cell Ω together with its inverse, compatibly with base change; the isomorphism theorem of ReductiveGroups Layer 9D identifies (𝔾, 𝕋) with the base change to ℚ_p of the pinned group of its root datum, the base being that of the positive roots of 𝔹, so that ℕ̄, 𝕋 and ℕ become U⁻, T and U⁺. An open immersion is smooth, so its image Ω(ℚ_p) is open in 𝔾(ℚ_p) (ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.0.2, *smooth-morphism-open-map*); μ and its inverse are given by regular functions, locally quotients of regular functions by nonvanishing ones, and these are locally analytic in the charts of ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.0.2, *smooth-group-analytic-charts*, which are built from regular local parameters by the analytic inverse function theorem. (ii): if gn̄TN = gn̄′TN then n̄′ = n̄tn with t ∈ T and n ∈ N, so n̄′ = n̄ by injectivity of μ. Since G is open, N̄, T and N are open in ℕ̄(ℚ_p), 𝕋(ℚ_p) and ℕ(ℚ_p), so N̄TN = μ(N̄ × T × N) is open in 𝔾(ℚ_p) and contained in G, and π is open; N̄ is compact, being closed in G, and X is Hausdorff because TN is compact, so φ_g is a homeomorphism onto its image. If gn̄ ∈ hN̄TN then h⁻¹gn̄ ∈ N̄TN ⊆ Ω(ℚ_p), which gives the transition formula. In these charts π on gN̄TN is the ℕ̄-component of `μ^{−1}(g^{−1}·)`, and near a point (h₀, φ_g(n̄₀)) the action is (h, n̄) ↦ the ℕ̄-component of `μ^{−1}((h₀g)^{−1}hgn̄)` in the chart `φ_{h₀g}`, locally analytic by (i). (iii): X = π(G) is compact and covered by the open sets U_g, each compact and hence closed; a finite subcover listed with U₁ first gives the V_i, which are open and closed in X. (iv): on π⁻¹(V_i), g = g_i n̄tn with (n̄, t, n) = `μ^{−1}(g_i^{−1}g)` ∈ N̄ × T × N, so τ(g) is the 𝕋-component of `μ^{−1}(g_i^{−1}g)`, locally analytic by (i), and uniqueness is the injectivity of μ. From gt′n′ = s(π(g))·(tt′)·(t′⁻¹nt′n′) follow the equivariance of τ and that F ↦ χ(τ)·(F ∘ π) lands in Ind(χ); every f ∈ Ind(χ) equals χ(τ)·(f ∘ s ∘ π), and τ ∘ s = 1, so the two maps are mutually inverse. Both are continuous, being analytic pullbacks followed by multiplication by the locally analytic function χ ∘ τ (LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.3, global analytic pullback and multiplication; PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1, restriction, pullback and gluing of locally analytic functions).

Properties of Ind(χ) and of the eigenfunction f₀:

- Z(g) acts on Ind(χ) by the induced character λ = dχ ∘ HC ∘ S, so Ind(χ) ⊆ C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ]: under the action of §1.1 (the contragredient of left translation) a monomial x₁⋯x_k acts by the composite of the right-invariant operators f ↦ (d/dt) f(exp(−tx_i)·), which is the left-invariant operator of Ad(g⁻¹)S(x₁⋯x_k) at the point g, so a central z acts by the left-invariant operator of S(z); in the triangular PBW decomposition S(z) = HC(S(z)) + (terms ending in n), right N-invariance kills the second part and right T-equivariance makes U(t) act through dχ. For 𝔾 = SL₂^m, where S fixes each Δ_i, this is dχ ∘ HC.
- The function f₀ := χ ∘ τ, the image of the constant function 1 under (iv), lies in Ind(χ), takes values in χ(T) ⊆ 1 + pℤ_p, equals 1 on s(X) and χ(t) at t ∈ T.
- For every locally constant φ on G, f₀·φ ∈ C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ], because Z(g) acts by differential operators, which commute with multiplication by φ on each open set where φ is constant.

- For G = G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) one chart suffices. An element g ∈ G₁ with rows (a, b) and (c, d) has a ∈ 1 + pℤ_p, and g is the lower unipotent matrix with entry c/a times the matrix with rows (a, b) and (0, a⁻¹), the lower-right entry of the product being (bc + 1)/a = d. ### 4.5 Density of eigenfunctions and injectivity into D(G, ℚ_p)_λ

Let λ be an induced infinitesimal character (§4.2) for G as in §4.1. Prove that C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ] and C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ ∘ S] are dense in C(G, ℚ_p) for the supremum norm, and consequently that the composite ℚ_p[[G]] → D(G, ℚ_p) → D(G, ℚ_p)_λ is injective (Fu, Theorem 5.2, second assertion, and its proof, pp. 16–17). For non-induced integral λ injectivity is §4.14.

*Proof sketch.* f₀·C^sm(G, ℚ_p) ⊆ C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ] (§4.4); f₀ is a unit of C(G, ℚ_p) and the locally constant functions are dense, so f₀·C^sm is dense. Inversion f ↦ f ∘ inv is an isometry of C(G, ℚ_p) that carries C^la[ν] onto C^la[ν ∘ S] for every ν: it intertwines the action of §1.1 with the right regular action ρ, through which a central z acts as the action of §1.1 of S z (§4.3). So C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ ∘ S] is dense as well. For 𝔾 = SL₂^m the two eigenspaces coincide.

### 4.6 Central specialisation commutes with the Banach steps

In the setting of §4.1 with G = G₁^m (p odd) and r_n = `p^{−1/p^n}`, prove that the natural map D(G, ℚ_p)_λ → lim_n `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` is an isomorphism of Fréchet algebras (Fu, Proposition 5.5 and its proof, with the estimate preceding (29), pp. 18–19; Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Proposition 3.7 and its proof, pp. 11–12). The isomorphism is not formal: tensor products do not commute with inverse limits in general.

*Proof sketch.* I = D(G, ℚ_p)·ker λ is a closed two-sided ideal (§4.1), so the quotient theorem for Fréchet–Stein algebras gives D/I ≅ lim_n `D_{r_n}/D_{r_n}I`, and `D_{r_n}I` = `D_{r_n}·ker` λ because ker λ is generated by central elements; the r_n are cofinal in p^ℚ ∩ (1/p, 1). Fu's proof dualises instead: D_λ ≅ C^la[λ ∘ S]′_b (§4.3; λ ∘ S = λ for G₁^m) is the limit of the duals of the Banach spaces `C^{n,an}(G, ℚ_p)[λ ∘ S]`, and the translation estimate ‖g − 1‖ ≤ 1/p on `C^{n,an}(G, ℚ_p)` for g ∈ `G^{p^{n+1}}` gives maps `D_{r_{n+1}}` → `C^{n,an}(G, ℚ_p)′_b`; both routes give the same map on Dirac distributions.

### 4.7 Central quotients D^λ_r(G₁^m, ℚ_p) are domains for integral λ

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m, λ ∈ ℤ_p^m and 1/p < r < 1. Put Δ′_i = p²Δ_i, written in the coordinates F_i, H_i, E_i of §0.3 through pf_i = log(1 + F_i), ph_i = log(1 + H_i), pe_i = log(1 + E_i). Prove:

- (i) For 1/p < r′ < `p^{−1/2}`, the principal symbol of Δ′_i − p²λ_i for `‖·‖_{r′}` is ½H_i² + 2E_iF_i, and `gr_{r′}` `D_{r′}(G, ℚ_p)/(Δ′_i − p²λ_i)_i` ≅ 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][F_i, H_i, E_i]/(½H_i² + 2E_iF_i)_i, an integral domain.
- (ii) D^λ_r(G, ℚ_p) is an integral domain for every real r with 1/p < r < `p^{−1/2}`: for r ∈ p^ℚ this follows from (i), and for every real r in the range it is §4.8 (ii), whose range [1/p, `p^{−1/(p−1)}`) contains (1/p, `p^{−1/2}`) because 1/2 ≥ 1/(p − 1) for p ≥ 3.
- (iii) For `p^{−1/2}` ≤ r < 1, D^λ_r(G, ℚ_p) is an integral domain provided the filtration induced by `‖·‖_{r′}` on D_r/D_r·ker λ is separated for some r′ ∈ (1/p, `p^{−1/2}`), that is, D_r ∩ `D_{r′}·ker` λ = D_r·ker λ inside `D_{r′}`. For p ≥ 5 the radii `p^{−1/2}` ≤ r < `p^{−1/(p−1)}` are covered unconditionally by §4.8.
- (iv) Integrality of λ is necessary: for r = `p^{−2/3}` and λ(Δ) = `p^{−1}`, D^λ_r(G₁, ℚ_p) = 0 (§4.1, the non-integral-parameter Check).

(Fu, Theorem 5.7 and its proof, p. 20, with Lemma 2.2, p. 6, and Lemma 3.3, p. 9; Source corrections, items 4 and 5.)

*Proof sketch.* `gr_{r′}` `D_{r′}(G₁, ℚ_p)` ≅ 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][F, H, E] (§1.5), and for r′ < `p^{−1/2}` ≤ `p^{−1/(p−1)}` the logarithms log(1 + X) have principal symbol X, since |1/k| r′^k < r′ for every k ≥ 2. Then p²Δ − p²λ = ½(log(1 + H))² − p log(1 + H) + 2 log(1 + E) log(1 + F) − p²λ has symbol ½H² + 2EF: the degree-two terms have norm r′², while ‖p log(1 + H)‖ = `p^{−1}r′` < r′² and |p²λ| ≤ `p^{−2}` < r′² because r′ > 1/p. ½H² + 2EF is a nondegenerate ternary quadratic form over 𝔽_p (p odd), hence irreducible over the algebraic closure, so the quotient of the polynomial ring is a domain, also for products (Fu, Lemma 3.3, third part). Lemma 2.2 of Fu transfers the domain property from the graded ring to D^λ_r when the induced filtration is separated; for r′ = r < `p^{−1/2}` with r ∈ p^ℚ this holds because the ideal is closed, being finitely generated in the noetherian Banach algebra D_r (Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Theorem 4.5(ii), which is proved for r ∈ p^ℚ only; Source corrections, item 2). For every real r in (1/p, `p^{−1/2}`), including the irrational radii such as r = `p^{−1/√2}` where noetherianity of D_r is not available, the closedness of the ideal and the domain property are §4.8 (ii), by bounded division in logarithmic coordinates. At the radius r_n itself the direct graded argument fails: log(1 + X) has principal symbol `p^{−n}X^{p^n}`, so the symbol of p²Δ − p²λ for `‖·‖_{r_n}` is `p^{−2n}(½H² + 2EF)^{p^n}` (Frobenius is additive and fixes 𝔽_p), and gr `D^λ_{r_n}` has nilpotents; this is why the weaker norm `‖·‖_{r′}` is used, and why separatedness is needed.

### 4.8 Small radii: logarithmic coordinates and central quotients

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m and `p^{−1}` ≤ r < `p^{−1/(p−1)}`, any real radius, and put ρ = pr. Prove: (i) the assignment e_i, f_i, h_i ↦ `p^{−1}` log(1 + E_i), `p^{−1}` log(1 + F_i), `p^{−1}` log(1 + H_i) extends to an isometric isomorphism of Banach algebras from the completion B_ρ of U(sl₂(ℤ_p)^m) ⊗ ℚ_p for the weighted PBW norm |Σ c_α x^α|_ρ = sup |c_α| `ρ^{|α|}` onto D_r(G, ℚ_p), with inverse given on group coordinates by b_i ↦ exp(p x_i) − 1; (ii) for λ ∈ ℤ_p^m the ideal D_r·ker λ is closed and D^λ_r(G, ℚ_p) is a nonzero integral domain with a normal form: every element is uniquely Σ_α d_α Π_i `e_i^{a_i}` `f_i^{b_i}` `h_i^{c_i}` with c_i ∈ {0, 1} and |d_α| `ρ^{|α|}` → 0, and the quotient norm is attained on normal forms. ### 4.9 Ext and grade over a finite crossed product

Let T = R ∗ H be a crossed product of a ring R by a finite group H and M a right T-module. Prove that Ext^i_T(M, T) ≅ Ext^i_R(M, R) as left R-modules for all i ≥ 0, with the left-module version by passing to opposite rings. Consequently, if R and T are noetherian, the grade j_T(M) = inf{i : Ext^i_T(M, T) ≠ 0} equals j_R(M), and the injective dimensions of R and T agree (Ardakov–Brown, §5.4, Lemma and its proof, and the Corollary, pp. 16–17; Fu, §5, proof of Theorem 5.1, p. 21).

*Proof sketch.* T is free of finite rank over R, so projective T-resolutions restrict to projective R-resolutions, and it suffices that T ≅ Hom_R(T_R, R_R) as right T-modules, T acting on the right of Hom_R(T_R, R_R) by (f·t)(x) = f(tx). Normalise the basis so that 1̄ = 1, write ḡh̄ = τ(g, h)·(gh)‾ with τ(g, h) ∈ R^×, and let pr₁(Σ_g r_g ḡ) = r₁ be the identity coefficient, which is left R-linear and satisfies pr₁(yr) = pr₁(y)r. The isomorphism is α(f) = `Σ_{g∈H}` f(ḡ) ḡ⁻¹, with inverse β(t)(x) = pr₁(tx), the Frobenius pairing: β(t) is right R-linear, β(ts) = β(t)·s, β(α(f))(h̄) = pr₁(Σ_g f(ḡ) ḡ⁻¹h̄) = f(h̄) because ḡ⁻¹h̄ ∈ R^×·(g⁻¹h)‾, and α(β(t)) = t because τ(g⁻¹, g)·ḡ⁻¹ = (g⁻¹)‾. On a basis element, β(Σ r_g ḡ)(h̄) = `r_{h⁻¹}`·τ(h⁻¹, h), so the cocycle cannot be omitted (Source corrections, item 11): for R = ℚ, H = C₂ and T = ℚ[u]/(u² − 2) with ū = u, the coefficient f of u (f(1) = 0, f(u) = 1) has α(f) = u⁻¹ = u/2, and β(u/2)(u) = pr₁(u²/2) = 1 = f(u), β(u/2)(1) = pr₁(u/2) = 0 = f(1), whereas the cocycle-free formula would give 1/2 at u.

### 4.10 Normal form of `U^λ_{r_n}(g)` and its injection into Û(g)_λ

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m, λ ∈ ℤ_p^m and r = r_n with n ≥ 1. Prove: `U_{r_n}(g)` is the weighted completion of U(sl₂(ℤ_p)^m) ⊗ ℚ_p for the PBW weight p^n on each of e_i, f_i, h_i; every element of `U^λ_{r_n}(g)` is uniquely a convergent sum Σ_α d_α Π_i `e_i^{a_i}` `f_i^{b_i}` `h_i^{c_i}` with c_i ∈ {0, 1} and |d_α| `p^{n|α|}` → 0; `U^λ_{r_n}(g)` is an integral domain; and the natural map `U^λ_{r_n}(g)` → Û(g)_λ is injective, Û(g)_λ having the same normal forms with weight 1 (Fu, proof of Theorem 5.1, the displayed description of U^λ_r(g) and the injection into Û(g) `⊗_{Z(g),λ}` ℚ_p, p. 21; Lemma 3.3, pp. 9–10). Separatedness of the quotient topology and the injection are proved together from the normal form.

*Proof sketch.* Frommer's description (§2.3) with x_i = log(g_i) ∈ {ph_i, pe_i, pf_i} and `‖x_i‖_{r_n}` = `p^{n−1}` (§2.4) gives the weighted completion with weight p^n. Bounded division by Δ_i − λ_i, replacing h_i² by 2(Δ_i − λ_i) + 2λ_i + 2h_i − 4e_if_i, does not increase the weighted norm when ρ = p^n ≥ 1 and |λ_i| ≤ 1, so normal forms are unique with remainders bounded by the norm and the ideal is closed. The graded ring for the weighted norm is 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][e_i, f_i, h_i]/(½h_i² + 2e_if_i), a domain. The inclusion of the weight-p^n completion into the weight-1 completion Û(g) sends normal forms to normal forms with the same coefficients.

*Needs:* §§2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 4.1.

### 4.11 Fu's direct route for induced characters

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m and λ an induced infinitesimal character. Prove: (i) if 0 ≠ δ ∈ ℚ_p[[G]], n ≥ 1, the image of δ in `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` is nonzero, and `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` is an integral domain, then there is δ′ ∈ `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` with 0 ≠ δδ′ ∈ `U^λ_{r_n}(g)`; (ii) if `D^λ_{r_n}(G, ℚ_p)` is an integral domain for every n ≥ 1 (for instance under the separatedness hypothesis of §4.7 (iii)), the composite ℚ_p[[G]] → `Û(g_{ℚ_p})` → Û(g)_λ of the microlocalisation (§3.4) and the central specialisation is injective (Fu, Theorem 5.1 and its proof, pp. 16 and 21; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §2.5, Definitions and Proposition, pp. 9–10). The conclusion of (ii) holds without the domain hypothesis by §4.14; (ii) records that the direct route reaches it once the domain property at the radii r_n is known.

*Proof sketch.* (i): `D^λ_{r_n}` is noetherian and Auslander–Gorenstein (its graded ring for `‖·‖_{r_n}` is 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][F_i, H_i, E_i]_i modulo the symbols `(½H_i² + 2E_iF_i)^{p^n}` of p²Δ_i − p²λ_i, i = 1, …, m (§4.7, proof); each symbol is monic in its own variable H_i, so they form a regular sequence and the graded ring is a commutative complete intersection, a hypersurface only for m = 1, hence Gorenstein; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, Proposition 2.5(b)), so the torsion module `D^λ_{r_n}/δD^λ_{r_n}` has positive grade (Proposition 2.5(a)). `D^λ_{r_n}` = `U^λ_{r_n}` ∗ (`G/G^{p^n}`) (§2.4), so its grade over `U^λ_{r_n}` is the same (§4.9); if `U^λ_{r_n}` ∩ `δD^λ_{r_n}` were 0, `U^λ_{r_n}` would embed in `D^λ_{r_n}/δD^λ_{r_n}`, contradicting `j(U^λ_{r_n})` = 0. (ii): the image of δ in D(G, ℚ_p)_λ is nonzero (§4.5), hence nonzero in some `D^λ_{r_n}` (§4.6); apply (i); `U^λ_{r_n}` → Û(g)_λ is injective (§4.10) and Û(g) = `D_{1/p}(G, ℚ_p)` (§3.5), so the image of δδ′, hence of δ, in Û(g)_λ is nonzero.

*Needs:* §§2.4, 3.4, 3.5, 4.5, 4.6, 4.9, 4.10; §4.7 (iii) for an instance of the hypothesis of (ii).

### 4.12 Integral Casimir parameters come from integral highest weights after a finite extension

Let p be odd and λ = (λ₁, …, λ_m) ∈ ℤ_p^m. Let K = ℚ_p(√(1 + 2λ₁), …, √(1 + 2λ_m)), a finite extension of degree at most 2^m, with ring of integers R. Prove that a_i := −1 + √(1 + 2λ_i) ∈ R satisfies a_i(a_i + 2)/2 = λ_i, so that the ℤ_p-linear map a : t₀ = ⊕ ℤ_p h_i → R, h_i ↦ a_i, extended R-linearly to the R-linear character a : R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` t₀ = ⊕ R h_i → R of §4.13 (with n = 0), has central character χ_a(Δ_i) = λ_i on the affinoid Verma module of highest weight a. For p = 3, λ = 1 and R = ℤ₃[√3], a(h) = −1 + √3 and a(√3·h) = √3(−1 + √3); the vector √3·h lies in R ⊗ t₀ but not in t₀ = ℤ₃h, which is why the domain is R ⊗ t₀. The extension K is in general ramified or nontrivial even when λ_i ∈ ℤ_p; λ is induced over ℚ_p (§4.2) exactly when every √(1 + 2λ_i) lies in ℤ_p (Fu, Theorem 1.5 and the sentence following it, p. 4; Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §4.5, the R-linear character and the affinoid Verma module, and §4.6, the central character χ_λ, pp. 10–11).

*Proof sketch.* a_i is a root of the monic X² + 2X − 2λ_i ∈ ℤ_p[X], hence integral over ℤ_p, and a_i(a_i + 2)/2 = ((a_i + 1)² − 1)/2 = λ_i. On a highest weight vector v (e_iv = 0, h_iv = a_iv), Δ_iv = (½a_i² − a_i + 2e_if_i)v = (½a_i² − a_i + 2a_i)v = ½a_i(a_i + 2)v, using e_if_i = f_ie_i + h_i.

*Needs:* §§4.1, 4.2; Mathlib `PadicInt`.

### 4.13 Affinoid Verma modules are faithful over Iwasawa algebras

Let p be odd, K a complete discretely valued extension of ℚ_p with ring of integers R, uniformiser π and ramification index e = v_π(p), 𝔾 a connected simply connected split semisimple group scheme over ℤ_p, 𝔤 := R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` Lie(𝔾) the Lie algebra of its base change to R, with Cartan subalgebra t = R ⊗ t₀ for t₀ the Lie algebra over ℤ_p of a split maximal torus of 𝔾, nilradical n⁺, Borel subalgebra b⁺ = t ⊕ n⁺ and opposite nilradical n⁻ (so 𝔤 = n⁻ ⊕ t ⊕ n⁺), p a very good prime for 𝔾, and G a uniform group with p^n 𝔤 = R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G, for example the congruence kernel `ker(𝔾(ℤ_p) → 𝔾(ℤ/p^{n+1}))`. Since p^n is a unit times `π^{en}`, the lattices p^n 𝔤 and p^n b⁺ give the affinoid enveloping algebras Û(p^n 𝔤)_K = `Û(𝔤)_{en,K}` and Û(p^n b⁺)_K = `Û(b⁺)_{en,K}` of §2.1: the deformation index is en, which differs from n as soon as K is ramified and n ≥ 1. For an R-linear character λ : p^n t → R, pulled back to p^n b⁺ along the projection with kernel p^n n⁺, let K_λ be the one-dimensional Û(p^n b⁺)_K-module on which it acts, and V̂^λ = Û(p^n 𝔤)_K `⊗_{Û(p^n b⁺)_K}` K_λ the affinoid Verma module. Prove: (i) its annihilator in Û(p^n 𝔤)_K is the two-sided ideal generated by ker(χ_λ : Z(𝔤_K) → K); (ii) V̂^λ is faithful as a K[[G]]-module through the microlocalisation K[[G]] → Û(R `⊗_{ℤ_p}` (1/p) L_G)_K = Û(p^n 𝔤)_K of §3.6 (of §3.4 when K is unramified). Hence K[[G]] → Û(p^n 𝔤)_K/Û(p^n 𝔤)_K·ker χ_λ is injective. For 𝔾 = SL₂^m over ℤ_p and G = G₁^m one has n = 0 (§0.3), so en = 0 for every K, and every odd p is very good for type A₁ (Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, Theorem 4.4, Lemma 4.5, Lemma and Theorem 4.6, pp. 10–11, applied at deformation index en; §§5.1–5.4, Theorem 5.4 and its proof, pp. 13–16; Source corrections, item 12).

*Proof sketch.* (i): the characteristic variety of V̂^λ is (b⁺_k)^⊥; the graded ideal of ker χ_λ·Û is `S(𝔤_k)^{G_k}_+` · S(𝔤_k), prime, with zero set the nilpotent cone; the annihilator is G(R)-stable, so its characteristic variety contains G·(b⁺_k)^⊥, which is dense in the nilpotent cone, and primality forces equality of the graded ideals. (ii): V̂^λ is free of rank one over Û(p^n n⁻)_K, since p^n 𝔤 = p^n n⁻ ⊕ p^n b⁺; the action of R[[T]] on U(n⁻_K)v is faithful (Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, Proposition 4.7), so V̂^λ is faithful over R[[B]] by the general faithfulness theorem 5.3, then over R[[B⁺]] by the Cartan involution (Proposition 4.8), and over K[[G]] by Theorem 5.3 again.

*Needs:* §0.3; §2.1 (the affinoid enveloping algebras `Û(𝔤)_{en,K}` and their base change); §3.4 (the microlocalisation over an unramified R) and §3.6 (the microlocalisation K[[G]] → Û(p^n 𝔤)_K for every complete discretely valued K, through an unramified subfield K′ with K/K′ finite); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.VermaModule`, `TauCeti.vermaCentralCharacter` and `TauCeti.hcProjection` (Verma modules over K, their central characters and the Harish-Chandra homomorphism).

**Checks.**

- For 𝔾 = SL₂, G = G₁, n = 0 and λ(h) = a ∈ R, V̂^a = {Σ_j c_j f^j v : c_j ∈ K, c_j → 0} is free of rank one over Û(n⁻)_K = K⟨f⟩, with h·f^j v = (a − 2j) f^j v and e·f^j v = j(a − j + 1) `f^{j−1}` v; Δ = ½h² + h + 2fe acts on fv by ½(a − 2)² + (a − 2) + 2a = ½a(a + 2), the scalar by which it acts on v, so Δ − ½a(a + 2), which generates ker χ_λ ⊆ K[Δ], kills V̂^a, as (i) requires.

V̂^k is neither that quotient, which is not faithful over K[[G₁]], nor the algebraic Verma module ⊕_j K f^j v: the element Σ_j p^j f^j v lies in V̂^k and not in the algebraic one.
- The deformation index is en, not n. For p = 3, R = ℤ₃[π] with π² = 3 (e = 2), 𝔾 = SL₂, n = 1 (G the level-nine congruence kernel, (1/3) R L_G = 3·sl₂(R)) and the character λ(3h) = 1 of 3t, one has λ(πh) = π⁻¹ ∉ R. In Û(πb⁺)_K = `Û(b⁺)_{1,K}` the element 3h = π·(πh) has norm |π| < 1, so 1 − 3h is a unit with inverse Σ_j (3h)^j; a module on which 3h acts by λ(3h) = 1 would have 1 − 3h acting by 0, so no K_λ exists over the index-n algebra. In Û(3b⁺)_K = `Û(b⁺)_{2,K}`, 3h is a basis vector of norm 1, 1 − 3h is not a unit (its image in the Tate algebra Û(3t)_K = K⟨3h⟩ vanishes at 3h = 1), and K_λ is defined.

### 4.14 Fu's injectivity theorem

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m = Π (I₂ + pM₂(ℤ_p)) ∩ SL₂(ℤ_p) and g = sl₂(ℚ_p)^m with Casimirs Δ₁, …, Δ_m. Prove that for every λ ∈ ℤ_p^m the composite ℚ_p[[G]] → `Û(g_{ℤ_p})_{ℚ_p}` → Û(g)_λ = `Û(g_{ℤ_p})_{ℚ_p}/(Δ_i − λ_i)_i` of the microlocalisation (§3.4) and the central specialisation (§4.1) is injective. Equivalently, the image in Û(g) of every nonzero element of ℚ_p[[G]] is generic: it stays nonzero in Û(g)_λ for all λ ∈ ℤ_p^m (Fu, Theorem 1.5 and the following paragraph, pp. 3–4; Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, Theorems 4.6 and 5.4, pp. 11 and 16). λ is arbitrary in ℤ_p^m, including parameters that are not induced over ℚ_p.

*Proof sketch.* Choose K and integral a_i with a_i(a_i + 2)/2 = λ_i (§4.12). By §4.13 with n = 0, K[[G]] → Û(g_R)_K/(Δ_i − λ_i) is injective. Descent: K[[G]] = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` ℚ_p[[G]], Û(g_R)_K = K `⊗_{ℚ_p}` `Û(g_{ℤ_p})_{ℚ_p}` (§2.1), the quotient by the central ideal commutes with K ⊗ − (over K the ideal is generated by the same Δ_i − λ_i, which generate the kernel in Z_K of the K-linear extension of λ, §4.1), and the square formed by the two microlocalisation maps commutes (§3.6); K is faithfully flat over ℚ_p (`Module.FaithfullyFlat`), so the kernel of the ℚ_p-map is zero once its base change to K is. For induced λ, §4.11 gives a second proof once its domain hypothesis is available.

*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.4, 3.6, 4.1, 4.12, 4.13; Mathlib `Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

## Layer 5: Algebraic functions and the algebraic Peter–Weyl theorem

The last layer turns Iwasawa-module statements into multiplicities of algebraic representations. Schikhof duality (PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1) identifies finitely generated K[[G]]-modules with admissible Banach representations, so W-quotients of an Iwasawa module are dual to W-isotypic vectors. For open subgroups of SL₂(ℤ_p) the algebraic functions give these explicitly: the degree-k graded piece is W_k ⊗ W_k^*, and K[[G]]/Ann(W_k) ≅ End_K(W_k).

### 5.1 Algebraic functions on an open subgroup of SL₂(ℤ_p)

Let G ⊆ SL₂(ℤ_p) be an open subgroup (for example G₁) and K/ℚ_p finite. Define the algebraic functions C^alg(G, K) as the image of the coordinate ring K[SL₂] = K[x, y, z, w]/(xw − yz − 1) (`MvPolynomial` modulo the determinant relation) under restriction to G, a matrix g with rows (x, y) and (z, w) being evaluated at its entries; define the degree filtration `C^alg_{≤n}` as the image of the polynomials of total degree ≤ n; and let G × G act by (g₁, g₂)·f(g) = f(g₁⁻¹ g g₂). For G = G₁^m use the tensor product of the factors (Fu, §6, the definition of C^alg(G, K) and its degree filtration, pp. 21–22).

- Restriction K[SL₂] → C(G, K) is injective, so C^alg(G, K) ≅ K[SL₂]: the kernel of evaluation on G of K[x, y, z, w] is (xw − yz − 1), because an open subgroup of SL₂(ℚ_p) is Zariski dense (it contains a neighbourhood of 1, on which an analytic chart shows that a regular function vanishing there is zero) and SL₂ is irreducible.
- C^alg(G, K) ⊆ C^la(G, K) ⊆ C(G, K), and each `C^alg_{≤n}` is stable under the two-sided action of G × G.
- dim_K `C^alg_{≤n}/C^alg_{≤n−1}` = C(n + 3, 3) − C(n + 1, 3) = (n + 1)²: the associated graded of the degree filtration is K[x, y, z, w]/(xw − yz), whose degree-n piece is the degree-n polynomials modulo (xw − yz) times the degree-(n − 2) polynomials.
- The identity C(n + 3, 3) − C(n + 1, 3) = (n + 1)² holds for every n ≥ 0, with C(n + 1, 3) = 0 for n ≤ 1.

*Needs:* §0.2; Mathlib `MvPolynomial`, `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup`, `ContinuousMap`.

**Checks.**

- dim `C^alg_{≤0}` = 1: the constants.
- dim `C^alg_{≤1}/C^alg_{≤0}` = 4, spanned by the classes of x, y, z, w; no linear relation holds, since the relation has degree 2.
- dim `C^alg_{≤2}/C^alg_{≤1}` = 9 = C(5, 3) − C(3, 3) = 10 − 1, the relation xw − yz = 1 removing one quadric; a count that ignored the relation would give 10.
- For the finite subgroup {±I₂} ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p), restriction of K[SL₂] is not injective (x − w vanishes on it); openness of G is needed.

### 5.2 The algebraic Peter–Weyl theorem on the first congruence subgroup

Let p be odd, G = G₁^m, k ∈ ℕ^m, W_k = ⊠_i `Sym^{k_i}(K²)` and K/ℚ_p finite. Prove: (i) W_k, restricted to any open subgroup of G, is absolutely irreducible and self-dual (W_k^* ≅ W_k), its differentiated action is the algebraic sl₂^m-action, and it is the Lie module 𝓛(W_k) of §3.9; (ii) for m = 1, `C^alg_{≤k}/C^alg_{≤k−1}` ≅ W_k ⊗ W_k^* as G × G-representations, and the W_k-isotypic algebraic vectors in C(G, K), for either the left or the right action, form a closed subspace isomorphic to W_k ⊗ W_k^*; for m ≥ 1 the same holds for the tensor products; (iii) through the Schikhof duality of PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1, the maximal W_k-quotient of K[[G]] is `K[[G]]/Ann_{K[[G]]}(W_k)` ≅ End_K(W_k), so dim_K `Hom_{K[[G]]}(K[[G]], W_k)` = dim W_k = Π (k_i + 1) (Fu, §6, pp. 21–22, including (32); Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §§11.2–11.3, Theorem (factorisation), pp. 80–81).

The headline form of (iii) is `exists_surjective_algHom_end_symPower`: for p odd, m ≥ 0, k ∈ ℕ^m and K/ℚ_p finite, with W_k realised as the tensor product `⨂[K] i, Sym[K]^(k i) (Fin 2 → K)` (Mathlib `PiTensorProduct` and `SymmetricPower`) on which g ∈ G = G₁^m (`firstCongruenceProduct` p m) acts by `PiTensorProduct.map` of the maps `TauCeti.symPowerRep` K 2 k_i applied to the image of g_i in GL₂(K), there is a K-algebra map Φ : K `⊗_{ℤ_p}` ℤ_p[[G]] → End_K(W_k) through which every group element acts by this representation, and Φ is surjective. Its kernel is the annihilator of W_k for the module structure through Φ, so K[[G]]/Ann(W_k) ≅ End_K(W_k), of dimension (Π (k_i + 1))². The existence of Φ is the extension of §3.9 (the continuous action of ℤ_p[[G]] on the compact lattice ⊗_i `Sym^{k_i}(ℤ_p²)`, base changed to K); surjectivity holds for every such Φ, since the K-span of the images of the group elements is already End_K(W_k) by (i) and Burnside's theorem. For m = 0 the statement is K ≅ End_K(K).

*Proof sketch.* (i): an open subgroup is Zariski dense in SL₂^m, so its invariant subspaces in the algebraic representation W_k are sl₂^m-stable, and W_k is irreducible under sl₂^m (it has the primitive vector x^k of weight k and dimension k + 1, so it is the irreducible module of `TauCeti.exists_isIrreducible_hasPrimitiveVectorWith` by `TauCeti.finrank_eq_of_hasPrimitiveVectorWith` and `TauCeti.lieModuleEquivOfHasPrimitiveVectorWith`, and the tensor product of absolutely irreducible modules of the factors is irreducible over sl₂^m); self-duality is the invariant pairing on Sym^k; W_k is a Lie module because it is the restriction of an algebraic representation (§3.9; Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §11.2 Theorem with trivial Artin factor). (ii): x^k, as a function of g with rows (x, y) and (z, w), is invariant under left translation by the lower unipotent subgroup (the first row of the lower unipotent matrix with entry a is (1, 0)) and under right translation by the upper unipotent subgroup (right multiplication by the upper unipotent matrix with entry a does not change the first column), with highest weight k on both sides, and generates a copy of W_k ⊗ W_k^* in degree k; the dimension count (k + 1)² of §5.1 gives equality, complete reducibility identifies the isotypic algebraic vectors, and finite-dimensional subspaces are closed. (iii): duality identifies the maximal W_k-quotient of K[[G]] with the dual of the W_k-isotypic part of C(G, K), which is W_k ⊗ W_k^*; alternatively (32) follows from §3.10 with M = K[[G]].

*Needs:* §§0.2, 3.9, 3.10, 5.1; PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §0.1 (Schikhof duality; C(G, K) as the dual of K[[G]] through PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §1.7); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.exists_isIrreducible_hasPrimitiveVectorWith`, `TauCeti.finrank_eq_of_hasPrimitiveVectorWith`, `TauCeti.lieModuleEquivOfHasPrimitiveVectorWith`, `TauCeti.exists_isCompl_of_isKilling`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra`, `TauCeti.symPowerRep`; Mathlib `SymmetricPower`, `PiTensorProduct.map`, `Matrix.SpecialLinearGroup.toGL`.

### Examples

- k = 0: the maximal trivial quotient of K[[G]] is K, through the augmentation, and End(W₀) = K.
- k = 1, m = 1: K[[G₁]]/Ann(K²) ≅ M₂(K), of dimension 4 = (1 + 1)², matching dim `C^alg_{≤1}/C^alg_{≤0}` = 4.
- For G = ℤ_p and E = C(ℤ_p, K) with the left regular action, the dual is K[[ℤ_p]], free of rank one; for G₁, the dual of K[[G₁]] as a module over itself is C(G₁, K), and W_k^* ≅ W_k is a finite-dimensional K[[G₁]]-module (Fu, §6, opening paragraphs, p. 21).

## Worked examples

- **The one-dimensional case.** For G = 1 + pℤ_p ≅ ℤ_p (p odd) with b = (1 + p) − 1: L_G = ℤ_p·(1 + p) and `g_{ℤ_p}` = ℤ_p·u, u = log(1 + p)/p (§0.1); D_r(ℤ_p, K) is the ring of series in b convergent on |b| ≤ r; x = log(1 + b) has `‖x‖_{r_n}` = `p^{n−1}` (§2.4); U_r is all of D_r for r < `p^{−1/(p−1)}` and has index p at r₁ (§2.2); the microlocalisation of ℤ_p[[b]] is ℚ_p⟨u⟩ with b ↦ exp(pu) − 1, which is `D_{1/p}` (§§3.4–3.5); the module of functions on G/G^p microlocalises to ℚ_p (§3.7).
- **The first congruence subgroup.** For G₁ ⊂ SL₂(ℤ_p) (p odd): `(1/p)L_{G₁}` = sl₂(ℤ_p) (§0.3); Δ = ½h² − h + 2ef = 4·`TauCeti.casimirElement` is central in D(G₁, ℚ_p) (§1.3) and has norm 1 in `Û(sl₂(ℤ_p))_{ℚ_p}` (§2.1); gr_r D_r(G₁, ℚ_p) ≅ 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][F, H, E] for 1/p < r < 1 (§1.5); for r < `p^{−1/2}` and λ ∈ ℤ_p the central quotient has graded ring 𝔽_p[ε, ε⁻¹][F, H, E]/(½H² + 2EF) (§4.7), while at r = `p^{−2/3}` and λ = `p^{−1}` it is zero (§4.1); finite-dimensional ℚ_p[[G₁]]-modules are semisimple (§3.8); K[[G₁]]/Ann(Sym^k) ≅ `M_{k+1}(K)` (§5.2); and ℚ_p[[G₁]] → Û(sl₂)_λ is injective for every λ ∈ ℤ_p (§4.14), at p = 3 and λ = 1 through ℚ₃(√3).

## Source corrections

Each correction is stated at its locator; the targets above use the corrected statements.

1. Fu, §4, equation (16), p. 13 (arXiv v2). D_r(G, K), defined as the completion for ‖·‖_r, is described by the bounded condition sup_α |λ_α| `r^{|α|}` < ∞. The completion consists of the series with |λ_α| `r^{|α|}` → 0; the bounded condition describes the larger `D_{<r}(G, K)` of Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Lemma 4.8. For G = ℤ_p and r = `p^{−1/p}`, `λ_{pj}` = `p^{−j}` (zero otherwise) gives `|λ_{pj}|` `r^{pj}` = 1 for all j: bounded, not tending to zero. Nothing downstream uses the bounded description.
2. Fu, Theorem 4.2, p. 14. D_r(G, K) is stated to be noetherian for every 1/p ≤ r < 1. Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Theorem 4.5(ii) and Remark 4.6, prove noetherianity for r ∈ p^ℚ and for r = 1/p; multiplicativity and the domain property hold for every 1/p < r < 1. The statement lacks a proof at irrational radii, and nothing here needs them.
3. Fu, §4, equation (17), p. 14. The set ⋃_a (p^a + `m^{a+1}`) is formed in R[[G]] with R the integers of an arbitrary finite extension of ℚ_p. For ramified R, p ∈ m², so 0 = p − p ∈ p + m² and the set is not an Ore set of nonzerodivisors (§3.3, last check). Use R unramified, as in Ardakov–Wadsley 2013, §10.1, and extend coefficients by base change (§3.6).
4. Fu, Theorem 5.7, p. 20. D^λ_r(G, ℚ_p) is stated to be an integral domain for every λ and every 1/p < r < 1. For r = `p^{−2/3}` and λ(Δ) = `p^{−1}`, ‖Δ‖_r ≤ `p^{2/3}` < |λ| and D^λ_r = 0; the proof's own first branch, where the symbol is a unit, reaches this zero quotient. The integrality hypothesis v_p(λ₀) ≥ 2, that is λ(Δ) ∈ ℤ_p, is needed (§4.7).
5. Fu, proof of Theorem 5.7, p. 20, as used in the proof of Theorem 5.1, p. 21. The graded ring of D^λ_r for the weaker norm `‖·‖_{r′}` is identified through Lemma 2.2 with the quotient of the graded ring by the symbol of p²Δ + λ₀. This needs the filtration induced by `‖·‖_{r′}` on D_r/D_r(p²Δ + λ₀) to be separated, which is equivalent to D_r ∩ `D_{r′}(p²Δ + λ₀)` = D_r(p²Δ + λ₀); it holds for r < `p^{−1/2}` but is not established at the radii r_n of Theorem 5.1. D_r → `D_{r′}` is flat but not faithfully flat (for G = ℤ_p, T − t with r′ < |t| ≤ r becomes a unit in `D_{r′}`), so the condition does not follow formally. §4.7 (iii) and §4.11 carry it as a hypothesis; Theorem 1.5 itself follows from Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, as the paper notes (§4.14).
6. Fu, Remark 5.8, second bullet, pp. 20–21. λ is said to be induced iff v_p(λ₀) ≥ 2. Only the forward implication holds: λ is induced iff 2λ(Δ) + 1 is a square in ℤ_p (§4.2). At p = 3, λ(Δ) = 1 has v₃(λ₀) = 2 but 2λ + 1 = 3 is not a square in ℚ₃.
7. Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.2, proof of the Lemma, p. 14 (arXiv v1; the published text prints the same condition). `D_{1/p}(G, K)` is described by finiteness of sup_α |λ_α| `p^{−|α|}`. As the completion for `‖·‖_{1/p}`, `D_{1/p}(G, K)` consists of the series with |λ_α| `p^{−|α|}` → 0, which is what matches the null sequences of Û(g)_K: under b_i ↦ exp(pu_i) − 1 ≡ pu_i, the bounded series with λ_α = `p^{−|α|}` at α = (n, 0, …, 0) would correspond to Σ u₁^n plus higher-order terms, which is not in Û(g)_K. The proof is unaffected because the comparison maps are isometric.
8. Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Definition 2.2.31, p. 24 (arXiv v1). For a left 𝒰(g)-module the transition is printed as `M_{r+1}` `⊗_{U(g)_{r+1}}` U(g)_r ≅ M_r, which needs a right module; for left modules it is U(g)_r `⊗_{U(g)_{r+1}}` `M_{r+1}` ≅ M_r, the Banach algebra on the left, as in Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, §3, p. 8; §2.5 writes it along the radii r_j of item 13.
9. Fu, §6, the paragraph identifying the degree-k graded piece with W_k ⊗ W_k^*, p. 22. The function x^k is said to be right invariant under a "lower upper triangular" nilpotent subgroup. It is right invariant under the upper triangular unipotent subgroup: for n with rows (1, a) and (0, 1), the (1, 1) entry of gn is g₁₁, while for n with rows (1, 0) and (a, 1) it is g₁₁ + a g₁₂ (§5.2).
10. Fu, §5, the sentence before Theorem 5.2, p. 16, for a general connected split reductive 𝔾. The principal series Ind(χ), on which G acts by left translation, is said to have the infinitesimal character dχ ∘ HC. With the derived action (22) of left translation a central z acts on Ind(χ) by the left-invariant operator of S(z), S the antipode, hence by dχ(HC(S(z))) (§4.4). For 𝔾 = SL₂^m the antipode fixes the Casimirs and the two agree, so Theorem 5.1 and its proof are unaffected; for sl₃ they differ (§4.2, the SL₃ antipode Check). The roadmap defines induced characters through dχ ∘ HC ∘ S. With the same reading of (22), the first assertion of Theorem 5.2 identifies D(G, ℚ_p) `⊗_{Z(g),λ}` ℚ_p with the strong dual of C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ ∘ S], not of C^la(G, ℚ_p)[λ]: the transpose of f ↦ z·f is right multiplication by S(z) (§4.3, with the torus witness there). The second assertion is unaffected, because inversion carries C^la[λ] onto C^la[λ ∘ S] (§4.5).
11. Ardakov–Brown, §5.4, proof of the Lemma, p. 17. The inverse of α(f) = Σ f(ḡ)ḡ⁻¹ is written β(Σ r_g ḡ)(h̄) = `r_{h⁻¹}`, which omits the twisting of the crossed product. The inverse is β(t)(x) = pr₁(tx), the identity coefficient of tx, whose value at h̄ is `r_{h⁻¹}`·τ(h⁻¹, h) with (h⁻¹)‾h̄ = τ(h⁻¹, h) ∈ R^×. For ℚ[u]/(u² − 2) as a crossed product of ℚ by C₂ the printed formula sends α(f) = u/2 back to a map with value 1/2 at u instead of 1 (§4.9). The Lemma and its Corollary are unaffected.
12. Ardakov–Wadsley 2014, §5.4, Theorem and its proof, p. 16, with §4.5, the Definition, p. 10. The affinoid Verma module of §4.5 is formed over `Û(𝔤)_{n,K}` = Û(π^n 𝔤)_K from an R-linear character of π^n t, while the Theorem of §5.4 takes R-linear characters of p^n t and its proof works over Û(p^n 𝔤)_K and Û(p^n n⁻)_K. Over a ramified K the two indices differ, and the Theorem holds for the affinoid Verma module of §4.5 at deformation index en, e = v_π(p). Read at index n, the character need not define a module: for π² = 3, n = 1 and λ(3h) = 1, the unit 1 − 3h of Û(πb⁺)_K would act by 0 (§4.13). For K unramified, and for n = 0, the two readings agree, so the end theorem (§4.14), which uses n = 0, is unaffected.
13. Schmidt, the remark following Proposition 3.2.3, p. 6 (arXiv v2), and Boxer–Calegari–Gee–Pilloni, Definition 2.2.31, p. 24 (arXiv v1). The completion U_r(g) for a single norm is said to be a noetherian Banach algebra, flat over 𝒰(g), for every r > 1, and the coadmissible modules of 𝒰(g) are presented along the radii r and r + 1. The arguments given (Schmidt, proof of Theorem 4.3.3, p. 16, which passes to a finite extension of E in which r is a value of the absolute value, and, for the analogous algebras D_r, Schneider–Teitelbaum 2003, Theorem 4.5) treat radii in p^ℚ only; for p = 3 the radius 2 is not in 3^ℚ. The statement lacks a proof outside p^ℚ, and §2.5 uses the cofinal radii r_j = `|π_E|^{−j}`, at which `U(g)_{r_j}` is the noetherian algebra `Û(g₀)_{j,E}` of §2.1.
