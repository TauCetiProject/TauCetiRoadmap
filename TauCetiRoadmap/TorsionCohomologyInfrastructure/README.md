# Roadmap: reusable infrastructure for torsion in arithmetic cohomology

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the integral machinery that attaches Galois determinants to Hecke eigensystems in the torsion cohomology of locally symmetric spaces for `GL_n` over a totally real or CM field, following Scholze's *On torsion in the cohomology of locally symmetric varieties* (Annals 182 (2015)). It has three strands. The geometric strand compares the compactly supported completed cohomology of a Hodge-type Shimura tower with the almost integral cohomology of the boundary ideal sheaf on its perfectoid minimal compactification, and uses integral formal models and Plücker sections to show that the tame Hecke algebra acts on torsion cohomology through the topology defined by classical cusp forms. The algebraic strand separates a Levi factor from a determinant by twisting with characters of opposite weights and extracts its degree-`n` determinant as a homogeneous polynomial law. The arithmetic strand realises these steps on the Siegel boundary of `Sp_2n` and `U(n,n)` Shimura varieties and assembles them, with uniform nilpotent errors, into determinants on every finite Hecke image `T_{F,S}(K,ξ,i,m)`. We want it because almost every modern result on Galois representations attached to torsion classes, and every modularity-lifting argument over CM fields that uses them, consumes these comparisons with their quantified error ideals. Formalising them once, on the objects their suppliers construct, keeps those downstream proofs honest about which errors are almost, which are nilpotent and which come from a characteristic-zero input.

The end theorems are the finite torsion determinant theorem of Layer 4 (Scholze, Theorem 5.4.1): there is `N = N([F:ℚ],n)` such that every finite Hecke image `T_{F,S}(K,ξ,i,m)` has an ideal `I` with `I^N = 0` and a continuous `n`-dimensional determinant of `G_{F,S}` over the quotient with characteristic polynomial `P_v` at every `v ∉ S`; its integral version over the closed simultaneous action ring; its residual and local specialisations (Corollaries 5.4.3–5.4.4); and the four-route compatibility theorem, which identifies the geometric and boundary descriptions of the degree-`h` ambient polynomial on common quotients and, after auxiliary-character weighted extraction, identifies their degree-`n` Levi polynomial with the spherical normalisation. The milestones on the way are the perfectoid compact-support comparison (Theorem 4.2.1) with its vanishing above the middle degree (Corollary 4.2.2), continuity of the Hecke action for the classical topology (Theorem 4.3.1), and the Levi interior determinant (Theorem 5.3.1).

Every comparison keeps its error category. An almost isomorphism over `O_C` is not an isomorphism; a nilpotent ideal of a Hecke image is a separate error with its own exponent; a characteristic-zero input is used only through the exact theorem that supplies it. Where the published text needs a correction, the corrected statement is the target and the correction is recorded in *Corrections to the published sources*. The companion [Suggested.lean](Suggested.lean), in the namespace `TauCetiRoadmap.TorsionCohomologyInfrastructure`, states the algebraic milestones of Layer 3a: twisted-product separation, uniqueness and polynomiality of finite-weight factorisations, weighted determinant extraction, and the Laurent coefficient-ideal nilpotence bound. Weighted extraction is stated on Mathlib’s `PolynomialLaw`, with unitality, multiplicativity and homogeneity explicit after every scalar extension. The geometric and arithmetic headline theorems remain in this README: their formal models, perfectoid compactifications, arithmetic cohomology carriers, cohomological Hecke images and Satake actions are built by the roadmaps named under *Scope and ownership*.

**Endoscopic classification input.** Scholze's Theorem 5.1.2 (Arthur for `Sp_2n` over a totally real field, Mok for quasi-split `U(n,n)` over a CM field) describes the transfer of a cuspidal representation of `G_0` with discrete-series archimedean components of weight `k` as an isobaric sum of conjugate-self-dual cuspidal representations of `GL_{n_i}` in segments of length `ℓ_i`. This roadmap neither proves nor plans that classification. The theorems of Layers 3b and 4 come in two forms. The **unconditional CM form** assumes that `F` is CM and contains an imaginary quadratic field and that `S` is the set of places over a finite set `S_ℚ` of rational places containing `p` and every place where `F/ℚ` ramifies (Scholze, Remark 5.4.6, p.1061); it uses unitary similitude Shimura varieties of Hodge type and Shin's cohomological base change for unitary similitude groups ([Shin, *On the cohomological base change for unitary similitude groups*, appendix to Goldring, Theorem 1.1, pp.1–2](https://math.berkeley.edu/~swshin/BCofU.pdf)), which §3b.2 builds as `unitarySimilitudeBaseChange`. The GU branch uses the distinct group, multiplier and Hecke interfaces of `unitarySimilitudeBoundaryAdapter` in §3b.1a, including its restricted classical topology and integral boundary action. The **general form**, for any totally real or CM `F`, carries the rank-specific hypothesis **(EC)_n**: for every cuspidal `π` of `G_0` whose archimedean components are the discrete series `π_k` of Proposition 5.1.1, the conclusion of Theorem 5.1.2 holds, with the unramified local compatibility of its display on p.1033 and the regularity of `Π_i|·|^{(h−ℓ_i)/2}` at the archimedean places. For the general rank-`n` conclusions of Layer 4, **`(EC)_{≤n}`** means this transfer theorem for every rank `1 ≤ r ≤ n`, with `G_{0,r} = Sp_{2r}` or `U(r,r)` and every allowed classical weight; `(EC)_{≤0}` is empty. Layer 3b at rank `n` uses only `(EC)_n`. These hypotheses appear explicitly in the respective general-form statements; the algebraic constructions of Layer 3a and the geometric comparisons of Layers 0–2 do not use these hypotheses. The status of the stabilisation that these hypotheses rest on is surveyed in Atobe–Gan–Ichino–Kaletha–Mínguez–Shin, arXiv:2410.13504v3, §§0.3–0.4. The symplectic group and its similitude group are not interchangeable: each statement names the group whose Shimura variety realises it.

**Outside this roadmap.** The construction of perfectoid Shimura varieties, of the Hodge–Tate period map and of anticanonical towers belongs to PerfectoidShimuraVarieties; completed cohomology in general to CompletedCohomologyPartII; arithmetic quotients, Borel–Serre compactifications and Satake maps to ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces; determinants, reconstruction and interpolation to IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants; Galois representations for characteristic-zero automorphic forms to AutomorphicGaloisRepresentationsPartII. This roadmap does not construct Galois representations for reductive groups other than `GL_n`, and it gives no explicit value of `N([F:ℚ],n)` beyond the bounds its proofs produce.

## Conventions

- **Primes and coefficients.** `p` is a fixed prime. `O` is the ring of integers of a finite extension of `ℚ_p`, with uniformiser `ϖ`. `C` is a complete algebraically closed extension of `ℚ_p` with ring of integers `O_C` and maximal ideal `𝔪_C`. In Layer 0 the base is any complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field `K` with a fixed `0 ≠ ϖ ∈ 𝔪_K`; when `K = C` we take `ϖ = p`. The geometric comparisons use `ℤ/p^m` at a fixed `m ≥ 1`. An `O/ϖ^m` version passes through the coefficient-extension comparison of CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.3–2.9; its exponent is never identified with a `p`-power exponent.
- **Three error categories.** Almost mathematics is relative to `(O_C, 𝔪_C)`, with `𝔪_C² = 𝔪_C`. `M^a` is the almost module of `M`; an almost-zero module need not be zero, and an almost isomorphism is not an isomorphism of modules. A **nilpotent error** is an ideal `I` of a Hecke image with `I^N = 0` for a recorded `N`. A **characteristic-zero input** is one of the named theorems of AG2.3, AG2.7, Shin's base change for unitary similitude groups (§3b.2, `unitarySimilitudeBaseChange`) or `(EC)_n` at the relevant rank. Maps between these categories are named, and no statement identifies two of them.
- **Completed cohomology.** For a tame level `K^p` and `m ≥ 1`, `H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m) = colim_{K_p} H^i_c(X_{K_pK^p}, ℤ/p^m)`, the colimit along pullback (Scholze §4.2, p.1020; CompletedCohomologyPartII §8.6). The integral object `H̃^i_c(ℤ_p)` is the inverse limit over `m` of these colimits; the derived coefficient limit keeps its Milnor `lim¹` term (CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.3–2.9) until its vanishing hypotheses are verified.
- **Compactifications.** At a finite Hodge-type level `K`, `X*_K` is the genuine minimal compactification and `X̄_K` the scheme-theoretic image compactification inside the Siegel minimal compactification. The finite map `X*_K → X̄_K` of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties Layer 2 is an isomorphism over the interior and sends boundary to boundary; it is not assumed to be an isomorphism, and `X̄_K` is not assumed normal. The perfectoid charts live on the image tower `X̄_{K^p} ~ lim_{K_p} X̄_{K_pK^p}`. Integral formal models with their boundary ideals are built on `X*_K`, through the preimages of the image charts (Scholze, p.1030, corrected; see *Corrections*).
- **Boundary ideals and cusp forms.** `I ⊂ O_{X*_K}` is the ideal sheaf of the reduced boundary; on a perfectoid space, `I⁺ = ker(O⁺ → i_*O⁺_Z)` for the reduced strongly Zariski closed boundary `Z`. Classical cusp forms of weight `k` are `H⁰(X*_K, ω^k ⊗ I)`; integral cusp sections keep the integral boundary lattice at every reduction.
- **Hecke algebras.** For the Shimura data, `T = ℤ_p[G(𝔸_f^p)//K^p]` with `vol(K^p) = 1`, or its unramified part `T_{S⁺} = ⊗_{v ∉ S⁺} ℤ_p[G_0(F⁺_v)//K_v]`. For `GL_n/F`, `T_{F,S} = ⊗_{v ∉ S} T_v` with `T_v = ℤ_p[GL_n(F_v)//GL_n(O_{F_v})]`, and `U_{i,v}` is the double coset of `diag(ϖ_v,…,ϖ_v,1,…,1)` with `i` entries `ϖ_v`. Under the normalised Satake isomorphism, `U_{i,v} = q_v^{i(n−i)/2} e_{i,v}` with `e_{i,v}` the `i`-th elementary symmetric function of the Satake parameters.
- **Characteristic polynomials.** Scholze's polynomial is `P_v(X) = Σ_{i=0}^n (−1)^i q_v^{i(n+1)/2} e_{i,v} X^i = Σ_{i=0}^n (−1)^i q_v^{i(i+1)/2} U_{i,v} X^i ∈ T_v[X]` (p.1057), with constant term `1`; the second form has integral coefficients and no square root of `q_v`. IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 3a uses the monic polynomial `P^std_v(Y) = Σ_{i=0}^n (−1)^i q_v^{i(i−1)/2} U_{i,v} Y^{n−i}`. They are related by `P_v(X) = (X^n P^std_v(1/X))|_{X ↦ q_vX}`: the reciprocal of the standard polynomial evaluated at `q_vX`. Determinants follow Scholze's constant-one form `D(1 − X·g)`; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants uses the monic form `D(X − g)`, converted by `P(X) ↦ X^d P(1/X)` in the specified degree `d`.
- **Frobenius.** `Frob_v` is the geometric Frobenius, and `ε(Frob_v) = q_v^{−1}` for the `p`-adic cyclotomic character `ε`. Tau Ceti's ClassFieldTheory normalises Artin maps by arithmetic Frobenius; the geometric normalisation used here composes `globalArtinMap` with inversion. Passing from `P^std_v` to `P_v` adds the twist `ε^{−1}`; passing between geometric and arithmetic Frobenius inverts the group element. These are two different operations. **Witness:** for `n = 1` and the trivial character (`U_{1,v}` acting by `1`), `P_v(X) = 1 − q_vX`, so the attached character takes the value `q_v` at geometric Frobenius: it is `ε^{−1}`, and in arithmetic normalisation its polynomial is `1 − q_v^{−1}X`.
- **Dimensions and exponents.** In Layers 3b–4, `d` is the complex dimension of the ambient Shimura variety: `d = [F:ℚ]·n(n+1)/2` for `Sp_2n` over totally real `F`, and `d = [F⁺:ℚ]·n²` for `U(n,n)` over CM `F`. The ambient rank is `h = 2n+1` in the real case and `h = 2n` in the CM case. Error exponents are `d+1` for one descent filtration, `2(d+1)` for the ambient interior error and `4(d+1)` after Levi extraction.
- **Weight steps.** The classical weights are `ak` for `k ≥ 1` with a fixed step `a`; determinant interpolation needs `a > n` for `Sp_2n` and `a ≥ n` for `U(n,n)` (Proposition 5.1.1; see *Corrections*).
- **Library pins.** The vocabulary is that of Mathlib `6b7abb3c7686292736be2955bd3eb9ebf63b456a` and Tau Ceti `a91d3aafa8cd3e6bc33dfde0d7677ed0f1625039`. A prerequisite that names a roadmap layer refers to that layer's stated development; a Mathlib or Tau Ceti prerequisite refers only to the named declaration.

## How to read the build

The construction proceeds in the order Layer 0, Layer 2a, Layer 1, Layer 2, Layer 3a, Layer 3b, Layer 4. Layer 0 constructs integral models and boundary ideals on affinoid covers and the almost boundary sequence on perfectoid charts. Layer 2a proves the ordinary colimit description of the perfectoid boundary ideal modulo `p^m`, which Layer 1 needs before it can approximate sections. Layer 1 transports integral lattices along the period map and turns finitely many bounded sections into classical cusp forms. Layer 2 compares completed compact-support cohomology with almost Čech cohomology of the boundary ideal and proves continuity of the Hecke action. Layer 3a is pure algebra, uses only Mathlib and IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants, and can be built first; consumers of the factor-extraction argument import it without importing any arithmetic. Layer 3b realises the algebra on the Siegel boundary and extracts the Levi interior determinant. Layer 4 assembles the finite torsion theorem, passes to integral coefficients, and validates the normalisations. Within each layer the targets are ordered by their dependencies, and each *Needs* annotation names the earlier target or the supplier item it uses.

## Layer 0: integral sheaves, formal models and boundary traces

This layer works on finite affinoid covers and on perfectoid charts before anything is glued along a tower. It distinguishes the power-bounded lattice from every other lattice with the same generic fibre, keeps the almost symbol on every support comparison, and records the error bound of every normalised trace. Throughout, `K` is a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field with `0 ≠ ϖ ∈ 𝔪_K`, a formal `O_K`-scheme is locally topologically of finite presentation in the sense of AdicSpacesPartIII R2.2, and for a formal scheme `𝔛` over `Spf O_K` we write `X_m = 𝔛 ×_{Spf O_K} Spec(O_K/ϖ^m)`, an ordinary scheme with the same underlying space as `𝔛`.

### 0.1 Ampleness from the special fibre

**Ample line bundles on formal models.** An invertible sheaf `𝔏` on a quasi-compact formal `O_K`-scheme `𝔛` is *ample* if `𝔏/ϖ` is ample on the scheme `X_1`, and `𝔛` is *projective* if it is proper over `Spf O_K` and carries an ample invertible sheaf. Prove `FormalScheme.ample_of_affine_cover`: if `s̄_1, …, s̄_N ∈ H⁰(X_1, 𝔏/ϖ)` have affine non-vanishing loci `D(s̄_i)` that cover `X_1`, then `𝔏` is ample, and for every `m ≥ 1` the reduction `𝔏/ϖ^m` is ample on `X_m`. The first assertion is the definition of ampleness (Stacks, Tag 01PS); the second holds because `X_1 → X_m` is a closed immersion that is a homeomorphism (Stacks, Tag 09MW). No noetherian hypothesis on `O_K` is used: ampleness is a property of the schemes `X_m`, and `O_K/ϖ^m` is not noetherian when the value group is dense. Source: the last paragraph of the proof of Lemma 2.1.1 in [Scholze, p.954](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), which reduces ampleness to the special fibre and its affine cover. *Needs:* AdicSpacesPartIII F0.3 (formal schemes and their reductions), R2.2; Stacks Tags 01PS, 09MW.

**Checks.**

- For `𝔛 = P̂¹_{O_K}` with `𝔏 = O(1)` and the coordinate sections `x̄_0, x̄_1`, the loci `D(x̄_0), D(x̄_1)` are the two standard affine lines and cover `X_1`, so `O(1)` is ample on every `X_m`; `O(−1)` has no nonzero sections on `X_1` and fails the hypothesis.
- For `𝔛 = Spf O_K⟨T⟩` and `𝔏 = O`, the single section `1` has affine non-vanishing locus `X_1`, so `O` is ample; but `𝔛` is not proper, so it is not projective. Ampleness and properness are separate conditions.
- A single section `s̄` whose locus `D(s̄)` is affine but does not cover `X_1` (for `ℙ^1` and `s̄ = x̄_0`, missing the point at infinity) does not meet the covering condition: the target is not an assertion about one chart.

### 0.2 The integral section model of a framed cover

**Integral section model.** Let `X` be a reduced proper rigid space over `K`, viewed as an adic space, `L` a line bundle on `X`, and `X = ⋃_{i∈I} U_i` a finite cover by affinoids `U_i = Spa(R_i, R_i°)` with power-bounded plus rings, whose intersections `U_J = ⋂_{i∈J} U_i = Spa(R_J, R_J°)` are affinoid for nonempty `J ⊆ I`. All intersections and assertions involving `R_J` in §§0.2–0.3 are indexed by nonempty finite subsets; every localisation uses `∅ ≠ J ⊆ J′ ⊆ I`, so `0 < |J| ≤ |J′|`. Suppose sections `s_j^{(i)} ∈ H⁰(U_i, L)` for `i, j ∈ I` satisfy:

1. each `s_i^{(i)}` is a generator of `L|_{U_i}`, and every ratio `s_j^{(i)}/s_i^{(i)}` lies in `H⁰(U_i, O⁺)`;
2. inside `U_i`, the intersection `U_{ij}` is the locus `|s_j^{(i)}/s_i^{(i)}| = 1`;
3. on every `U_{i_1i_2}` and for every `j`, `|s_j^{(i_1)}/s_{i_1}^{(i_1)} − s_j^{(i_2)}/s_{i_1}^{(i_1)}| ≤ |ϖ|`.

Construct `framedFormalModel`, a formal `O_K`-scheme `𝔛` with an invertible sheaf `𝔏`, and prove: for `∅ ≠ J ⊆ J′ ⊆ I` the map `Spf R_{J′}° → Spf R_J°` is an open embedding, and `𝔛` is glued from the charts `𝔘_i = Spf R_i°` with `𝔘_J = Spf R_J°`; the generic fibre of `𝔛` is `X`; there is a unique invertible `𝔏` on `𝔛` with generic fibre `L` and `𝔏|_{𝔘_i} = s_i^{(i)}·O_{𝔘_i}`; there are unique `s̄_j ∈ H⁰(𝔛, 𝔏/ϖ)` with `s̄_j|_{𝔘_i} = s_j^{(i)} mod ϖ` for every `i`; and `𝔛` is projective with `𝔏` ample. The open embedding comes from writing `U_{J′} ⊂ U_J` as the locus `|f| = 1` for `f` the product of the ratios `s_j^{(i)}/s_i^{(i)}` over `j ∈ J′ ∖ J` (for a fixed `i ∈ J`), so that `R_{J′}°` is the `ϖ`-adic completion of `R_J°[f^{−1}]`; for `J′ = J` the map is the identity. The line bundle glues because, by condition 2 on `U_{ij}` and condition 3 with `(i_1, i_2) = (j, i)`, both `L_i` and `L_j` restrict to the free sheaf generated by `s_j^{(i)}`. On triple overlaps the cocycle condition holds automatically, because every `𝔏_i = s_i^{(i)}·O_{𝔘_i}` is a subsheaf of the one sheaf `L` and the identifications are equalities of subsheaves; likewise the ring restrictions are the restriction maps of the affinoids `U_J`. The reductions glue by condition 3 after dividing by `s_{i_1}^{(i_1)}`. Properness follows from properness of `X` and AdicSpacesPartIII R2.6; ampleness from §0.1, since the `D(s̄_i)` are the affine special fibres of the charts. The integral rings are the power-bounded rings `R_i°`, not an arbitrary choice of plus rings, and the construction needs the cover to be finite and `X` reduced.

API: `framedFormalModel.chartSections` (`Γ(𝔘_i, O) = R_i°` and `Γ(𝔘_i, 𝔏) = R_i°·s_i^{(i)}`); `framedFormalModel.reduction_restrict` (`s̄_j` restricts to `s_j^{(i)} mod ϖ` on every chart); `framedFormalModel.refinement` (a compatible refinement of the framed cover gives the same generic fibre and the same reduction maps after a common admissible refinement); `framedFormalModel.glue_hom` (morphisms from the glued formal scheme to a formal scheme `𝔜` are exactly compatible families of chart morphisms; descent is unique and preserves identities and composition; nothing is asserted about uniqueness among unrelated formal models).

Source: [Scholze, Lemma 2.1.1 and proof, pp.953–954](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf); the three numbered conditions are the lemma's hypotheses, and the identification of the localisation uses the explicit choice of the function `f` recorded under *Corrections*. *Needs:* AdicSpacesPartIII F0.3, R2.1, R2.3, R2.6; §0.1.

**Checks.**

- For `X = ℙ^1_K`, `L = O(1)`, the two charts `|x_1/x_0| ≤ 1` and `|x_0/x_1| ≤ 1` with sections `s^{(i)}_j = x_j` give `𝔛 = P̂¹_{O_K}`, `𝔏 = O(1)`, and `s̄_0, s̄_1` the coordinate sections modulo `ϖ`.
- The empty index set would give `U_∅ = ℙ¹_K` in the preceding example, which is not affinoid; it has no ring `R_∅` in this chart system.
- For nonempty `J′ = J` the localisation is the identity, and multiplying `f` by a ratio that is already a unit on `U_J` does not change the completed localisation: the construction does not depend on which units are included.
- Replacing one overlap section by `s_j^{(i_2)} + c·s_{i_1}^{(i_1)}` with `|c| > |ϖ|` violates condition 3, and then the reductions modulo `ϖ` need not glue: no global `s̄_j` is asserted without that condition.

### 0.3 The integral boundary ideal

**Integral boundary ideal.** In the situation of §0.2, let `I ⊂ O_X` be a coherent ideal sheaf. Construct `integralBoundaryIdeal`, a coherent ideal `𝔍 ⊂ O_𝔛`, by `Γ(𝔘_i, 𝔍) = I(U_i) ∩ R_i°`. Prove that each `J_i = I(U_i) ∩ R_i°` is a finitely generated `ϖ`-saturated ideal of `R_i°`, that `J_{J′}` is the `ϖ`-adic completion of `J_J[f^{−1}]` for `∅ ≠ J ⊆ J′ ⊆ I`, that the `J_i` glue to a coherent `𝔍`, and that the generic fibre of `𝔍` is `I` with its inclusion into `O_X`. For the reduced boundary of an arithmetic compactification, `I` is its actual ideal sheaf. On a chart, the analytic integral ideal `I⁺ = I ∩ O⁺` and `𝔍` have the same sections. Finite generation is AdicSpacesPartIII R2.1 for the saturated submodule `J_i` of `R_i°`, and coherence is R2.1; neither uses a noetherian hypothesis on `O_K`. For a finite level map, the extended ideal is compared with the saturation of the pullback of the generic ideal; equality is a hypothesis supplied by the boundary compatibility of the level map, not a consequence of the construction.

API: `integralBoundaryIdeal.sections` (`Γ(𝔘_i, 𝔍) = I(U_i) ∩ R_i°`); `integralBoundaryIdeal.genericFibre` (inverting `ϖ` recovers `I ⊂ O_X`); `integralBoundaryIdeal.reduction_restrict` (`𝔍/ϖ^m` restricts along the completed localisations, compatibly with refinements); `integralBoundaryIdeal.eq_sup` (`Γ(𝔘_i, 𝔍)` is the largest `R_i°`-submodule of `R_i` contained in both `I(U_i)` and `R_i°`; these properties determine `𝔍` and its inclusion uniquely).

Source: [Scholze, Lemma 2.1.2 and proof, pp.954–955](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §0.2; AdicSpacesPartIII §R2.1.

**Checks.**

- `I = O_X` gives `𝔍 = O_𝔛`, not the zero ideal.
- `I = 0` gives `𝔍 = 0`, and `𝔍/ϖ^m = 0` for every `m`.
- In `R = K⟨T⟩`, `I = (T)` gives `J = T·O_K⟨T⟩`; the ideal `(ϖT)` has the same generic fibre but is not `ϖ`-saturated (it does not contain `T`), so it is not the integral boundary ideal.

### 0.4 The bounded lattice extension across a good triple

**Bounded lattice extension.** Let `(X, Z, U)` be a good triple over `K` in the sense of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §1.5 and `0 ≠ t ∈ O_K` with `|p| ≤ |t| < 1`. Prove `goodTriple.lattice_extension`: for a finite free `O⁺_X`-module `ℰ⁺ = ⊕_{k=1}^r O⁺_X·e_k` with an explicit frame, restriction `H⁰(X, ℰ⁺/t)^a → H⁰(X ∖ Z, ℰ⁺/t)^a` is an isomorphism and `H⁰(X, ℰ⁺/t)^a → H⁰(U, ℰ⁺/t)^a` is injective; and bounded sections of `ℰ⁺[1/t] = ℰ⁺ ⊗_{O⁺_X} O_X` over `X ∖ Z` extend to `X`. In the arithmetic specialisation `t` is a nonzero power of `p`, so this agrees with inverting `p`. The statement is the scalar statement of the good triple applied to each coordinate; it is invariant under a change of frame whose matrix and inverse are integral. No assertion is made for an arbitrary vector bundle or an arbitrary divisor complement. Good triples, their normality and codimension conditions, and their stability (including the restricted form in genus one) are those of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§1.5–1.6; in the finite-cover stability statement the trace dual is self-dual and projectivity is not inferred. For the arithmetic chart intersections one uses common good refinements. Source: [Scholze, Definition 2.3.8 and the assertion after it, pp.966–967; Lemmas 2.3.9–2.3.11 and Corollary 2.3.12, pp.967–970](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§1.5–1.6.

**Checks.**

- Rank zero gives zero sections on both sides.
- For `K = Ĉ(𝔽_p((t)))`, `X = Spa(K, O_K)`, `Z = ∅`, `U = X` and the rank-one lattice, the generic module is `O_X ≠ 0`: inverting the nonzero scalar `t` gives the correct module, while inverting `p = 0` gives zero.
- An integral change of frame with integral inverse preserves coordinatewise boundedness and transports the extension map; the identity frame change fixes it.

An almost-zero module is not declared zero: the isomorphism is in the almost category.

### 0.5 Reduction of the closed-quotient sequence

**The boundary sequence modulo `p^m`.** Let `(R, R⁺)` be a perfectoid Tate pair over `C`, `I ⊂ R` a closed ideal, and `(R_I, R_I⁺)` the perfectoid closed quotient of PerfectoidSpaces §§P4.14–P4.15. Put `I⁺ = ker(R⁺ → R_I⁺)`. Prove `closedQuotient.reduction_almostExact`: for every `m ≥ 1` the sequence `0 → I⁺/p^m → R⁺/p^m → R_I⁺/p^m → 0` is almost exact, naturally in compatible pairs `(R, I) → (R′, I′)` and in `m`. The proof is short and is the whole content: `R_I⁺` is a subring of the `ℚ_p`-algebra `R_I`, so it has no `p`-torsion; `0 → I⁺ → R⁺ → R_I⁺ → 0` is almost exact because `R⁺ → R_I⁺` is almost surjective (PerfectoidSpaces §§P4.14–P4.15); reduction modulo `p^m` is exact on it because `Tor_1(R_I⁺, ℤ/p^m) = R_I⁺[p^m] = 0`. In particular `I⁺ ∩ p^mR⁺ = p^mI⁺` up to almost zero modules. Source: the strongly closed boundary sequence in [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.2.1, pp.1021–1022, and Lemma 2.2.9, pp.957–958](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the corrected closed quotient (see *Corrections*). *Needs:* PerfectoidSpaces §§P4.14–P4.15; PerfectoidSpaces Layer P0.

The almost symbol cannot be removed: `R⁺ → R_I⁺` is in general only almost surjective, since `R_I⁺` is the integral closure of the image.

### 0.6 The almost boundary support comparison

**Almost boundary support comparison.** Let `X̄_∞ = X̄_{K^p}` be the image-compactified perfectoid Hodge-type tower of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3, `Z_∞` its reduced boundary, which is strongly Zariski closed, `i : Z_∞ → X̄_∞` and `j : X_∞ → X̄_∞` the inclusions, and `I⁺_∞ = ker(O⁺_{X̄_∞} → i_*O⁺_{Z_∞})`. Prove `boundarySupport.almostIso`: for every `m ≥ 1`, the sequence `0 → I⁺_∞/p^m → O⁺_{X̄_∞}/p^m → i_*O⁺_{Z_∞}/p^m → 0` is almost exact, and the canonical map identifies `(j_!O⁺_{X_∞}/p^m)^a` with `(I⁺_∞/p^m)^a` on the étale site and on the analytic site. The maps are natural in `m` and for boundary-preserving level correspondences. The proof applies §0.5 on each affinoid perfectoid chart, checks the support map on interior and boundary stalks, and passes between sites by étale almost acyclicity. Almostification is relative to `𝔪_C`. The image and genuine compactifications are related only by the finite map of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§2.1, 2.8.

Source: [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.2.1, pp.1021–1023; Lemma 2.2.9, pp.957–958](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §0.5; PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§2.1, 2.3, 2.8; PerfectoidSpaces §§P2.7–P2.8, P3.13, P3.21–P3.22; PerfectoidSpaces §§P4.14–P4.15.

For `Z = ∅` the comparison gives `O⁺/p^m`; for `Z = X` it gives `0`.

### 0.7 Traces of integrally closed ideals

**Trace integrality.** Let `R` be a normal domain, `S` a finite `R`-algebra, and `f ∈ R` a nonzerodivisor with `S[f^{−1}]` finite étale over `R[f^{−1}]`. Prove `trace_le_of_integrallyClosed`: the trace `tr : S[f^{−1}] → R[f^{−1}]` sends `S` into `R`; and if `I ⊂ R` is an integrally closed ideal and `J ⊂ S` is the integral closure of `I` in `S` (the elements `x ∈ S` satisfying `x^n + a_1x^{n−1} + ⋯ + a_n = 0` with `a_k ∈ I^k`), then `tr(J) ⊂ I`. Membership in `R`, and in `I`, can be tested at valuations of `R`; after passing to the valuation ring of an algebraically closed field, the normalisation of `S` is a finite product of copies of that valuation ring, where both statements are clear. Normality of `R`, the generically étale hypothesis and integral closedness of `I` are all needed. Source: [Scholze, Lemma 4.3.2 and proof, pp.1025–1026](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* Mathlib `Algebra.trace`, `Algebra.isIntegral_trace`, `IsIntegrallyClosed`; AdicSpacesPartIII R3.8 (the trace of a finite locally free algebra).

**Checks.**

- For `R = ℤ_p`, `S = ℤ_p[√p]` and `f = p`, the trace sends `a + b√p` to `2a ∈ ℤ_p`. For `I = pℤ_p`, the integral closure of `I` in `S` is `pS`, not the radical `√p·S`: `√p` satisfies `x² − p = 0`, but `p ∉ I² = p²ℤ_p`. Then `tr(p(a + b√p)) = 2pa ∈ I`.
- Normality of `R` is needed: for the cusp `R = ℤ_p[[t², t³]]` and its normalisation `S = ℤ_p[[t]]`, with `f = t²`, one has `S[f^{−1}] = R[f^{−1}]`, the trace is the identity, and `tr(t) = t ∉ R`.
- Integral closedness of `I` is needed: for `R = S = ℤ_p[[x, y]]`, `f = 1` and `I = (x², y²)`, the element `xy` is integral over `I` (`(xy)² − x²y² = 0` with `x²y² ∈ I²`), and `tr = id` sends it to `xy ∉ I`.

**Reduced-boundary trace.** With `R`, `S` and `f` as in the trace-integrality target, let `I ⊂ R` be radical and put `J = √(IS)`. Prove `trace_radical_le`: `tr(J) ⊆ I`. This target is owned here. For a proof, extend the fraction field of `R` to an algebraic closure and let `B` be the integral closure of `R` there. If `x ∈ J`, some `x^N` lies in `IS`, so every generic conjugate of `x` lies in `√(IB)`. Their sum, counted with the multiplicities of the generic algebra, lies there too. Trace integrality puts this sum in `R`, and lying over gives `√(IB) ∩ R = √I = I`. This argument permits ramification over `V(I)` and does not require normality of `R/I` or flatness of `S`.

On every normal affinoid chart of a genuine minimal level map, prove that the inverse image of the boundary is the boundary as a closed set: level change preserves the interior and every boundary stratum. Thus the reduced source ideal is `√(IS)`. The corresponding plus ideal is `J⁺ = J ∩ S⁺`; trace integrality for the plus rings and `trace_radical_le` give `tr(J⁺) ⊆ I ∩ R⁺ = I⁺`. The same argument applies componentwise for normal charts with several components. This is separate from the ideal-theoretic integral closure in the preceding target.

Source: the boundary-trace application after [Scholze, Lemma 4.3.2, pp.1025–1026](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the radical-ideal argument above. *Needs:* §0.7 trace integrality; §0.3; AdicSpacesPartIII R3.8; Mathlib `Ideal.exists_ideal_over_prime_of_isIntegral_of_isDomain` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/GoingUp.lean`) and `Ideal.comap_radical` (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/Maps.lean`).

**Checks.**

- For `R = C⟨q⟩`, `S = C⟨u⟩`, `q = u^e`, `e > 1`, and `I = (q)`, the reduced source ideal is `(u)`, whereas the ideal-theoretic integral closure of `I` is `(u^e)`. For `k ≥ 1`, `tr(u^k) = 0` if `e ∤ k`, and `tr(u^k) = e q^{k/e}` otherwise; every such trace lies in `(q)`. Intersecting with the plus lattices preserves this conclusion.
- For a split cover `S = R × R`, `√(IS) = I × I` and the trace is addition, including at points of the boundary.
- Radicality is necessary: for `R = S = C⟨q⟩` and `I = (q²)`, the identity trace sends `q ∈ √I` outside `I`.

### 0.8 Boundary trace transport

**Boundary trace transport.** For a finite level map `π : X*_{K_1} → X*_{K_2}` of genuine minimal compactifications in the anticanonical tower that is finite étale over the interior, use the reduced-boundary identification and `trace_radical_le` of §0.7, together with trace integrality, chartwise to obtain traces `π_*O → O`, `π_*I → I`, `π_*O⁺ → O⁺` and `π_*I⁺ → I⁺` on the boundary ideals, and, after tensoring with `ω^k` of the lower level, `π_*(ω^k ⊗ I_{K_1}) → ω^k ⊗ I_{K_2}`. Prove `boundaryTrace.normalized_bound`: the normalised Tate traces of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §1.16 preserve the boundary condition after inverting `p` and send the source integral boundary lattice into `p^{−C_r}` times the target integral boundary lattice, with the error estimate of that section and constants `C_r = (g² + g + 1)·ε·Σ_{r′ ≥ r} p^{−r′}` tending to zero; choose an integer `b ≥ C_r`, so `p^b` times the normalised trace is integral, and form its reduction square modulo `p^m` only after this denominator has been absorbed; comparisons with the unscaled trace retain the loss `b` and are interpreted in the almost category; and a normalisation by a power of `p` is always accompanied by its bound. The boundary-preserving hypothesis is verified for each correspondence; it is not assumed for an arbitrary finite cover. A trace along a degree-`p` cover is never divided by `p` without the estimate.

Source: [Scholze, Lemma 4.3.2, pp.1025–1026, and Corollary 3.2.23, pp.994–995](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§0.3, 0.4, 0.7; PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §1.16.

**Checks.**

- For the unit boundary ideal the bound is exactly `tr̄_r(O⁺) ⊆ p^{−C_r}O⁺` of Corollary 3.2.23; the boundary condition gives no improvement in integrality.
- For the zero boundary ideal every trace is zero, including after scaling and reduction.
- The algebra `S = ℤ_p[T]/(T^p − T − 1)` is finite étale and the integral element `T^{p−1}` has trace `p−1`, so its normalised trace `(p−1)/p` is not integral. Thus even for the unit boundary ideal division by the degree needs a denominator bound.

## Layer 2a: the ordinary boundary-ideal colimit

This layer separates the ordinary colimit of quotients of bounded sections from the almost comparison with sections of the quotient sheaf. It precedes Layer 1 because classical section approximation descends bounded sections through the ordinary colimit.

### 2a.1 The boundary-ideal colimit

**Boundary-ideal colimit.** On each standard affinoid perfectoid chart `V_i` of the image-compactified tower, write `I⁺_∞(V_i)` for its bounded boundary-ideal sections, and similarly at finite image levels and on their genuine preimages `Ṽ_{i,K_p}`. For every `m ≥ 1`, prove `boundaryIdeal.colimit_iso`, the ordinary module isomorphisms

```text
colim_{K_p} (I⁺_{K_p}(V_{i,K_p})/p^m) ≅ I⁺_∞(V_i)/p^m,
colim_{K_p} (I⁺_{K_p}(Ṽ_{i,K_p})/p^m) ≅ I⁺_∞(V_i)/p^m.
```

The colimits use pullback of bounded sections and the genuine-level map constructed below; the isomorphisms are natural in `m`, restriction to chart intersections and level refinement. These are quotients of sections of the ideal sheaf. Injectivity at image levels follows from the colimit description `R⁺/p^m = colim_{K_p} R⁺_{K_p}/p^m` of the chart rings supplied with the tower (PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3) and `ϖ`-saturation of the ideals, `I⁺ ∩ p^mR⁺ = p^mI⁺`; at genuine levels one constructs the unique map to `I⁺` compatible with evaluation away from the boundary and deduces injectivity from valuations. Surjectivity approximates a bounded ideal section by finite-level sections, controls its boundary values, and applies the boundary-preserving normalised traces of §0.8 with `C_r → 0`, followed by two successive precision and divisibility corrections, to obtain an ordinary representative modulo `p^m`; the trace squares used inside the argument are almost squares formed after absorbing the denominators of §0.8. Completion of the ambient colimit of plus rings does not by itself identify its ideal modulo `p^m`, and ordinary surjectivity is not inferred from an almost-surjective map of rings.

Source: [Scholze, Lemma 4.3.3 and proof, pp.1026–1028](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the colimit description of Theorem 4.1.1(i) made explicit (see *Corrections*). *Needs:* §§0.6, 0.8; PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§2.1, 2.3, 2.8.

**Checks.**

- With empty boundary, the ordinary colimit is the plus-ring colimit modulo `p^m` of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3.
- With zero ideal, all quotients of sections and both colimits vanish.
- In the tower `R_r = C⟨T^{1/p^r}⟩` with perfectoid limit `R_∞ = C⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and boundary `T = 0`, the finite reduced ideals are `T^{1/p^r}R_r⁺`. Their quotients modulo `p^m` have colimit `ker(R_∞⁺ → O_C)/p^m`; the genuine and image versions coincide in this example.

**Almost section comparison.** Prove `boundaryIdeal.sections_almostIso`: on each chart intersection `V_J` at infinite level and for `m ≥ 1`, the canonical map `(I⁺_∞(V_J)/p^m)^a → H⁰(V_J, I⁺_∞/p^m)^a` is an isomorphism. It is natural in restriction and in `m`, and intertwines the pullback and boundary-preserving trace maps of the ordinary colimit after almostification and the denominator corrections of §0.8. The exact sequence

```text
0 → I⁺_∞(V_J)/p^m → H⁰(V_J, I⁺_∞/p^m) → H¹(V_J, I⁺_∞)[p^m] → 0
```

exhibits the obstruction to an ordinary isomorphism. The strongly closed boundary sequence and almost acyclicity of its two structure sheaves make `H¹(V_J, I⁺_∞)` almost zero. Consequently the almost Čech complex may use quotients of bounded sections termwise, then apply the ordinary colimit. No ordinary vanishing of the obstruction at a fixed exponent is required. For the genuine-level map in the colimit theorem, use the maps to `H⁰(V_i, I⁺_∞/p^m)` for all `m` and the ordinary identification after their inverse limit, as in Scholze's proof; a fixed-exponent almost isomorphism alone does not construct that map.

Source: [Scholze, proof of Lemma 4.3.3, p.1028](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§0.5–0.6; PerfectoidSpaces §§P2.7–P2.8, P3.21; PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3.

**Checks.**

- At `V = Spa(C,O_C)` with unit ideal the map is the identity on `O_C/p^m`.
- For zero ideal both sides vanish, as does the obstruction group.
- For a `p`-torsion-free ideal sheaf with nonzero `H¹(V,I⁺)[p^m]`, the displayed sequence makes the section map non-surjective as an ordinary map, even when that obstruction is almost zero. Almost acyclicity alone cannot remove it.

## Layer 1: period-map bundles and approximating sections

The period isomorphism first identifies rational bundles, with the Tate twist visible. Integral bounds, reduction transport and independence of approximating choices are proved afterwards. Existence of the finite-level formal models and approximating Plücker sections is PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.9; this layer transports them, localises at their sections, and lifts finitely many bounded sections to classical cusp forms. Nothing here lifts torsion cohomology classes to characteristic zero. In this layer the integral models of Layer 0 are used with `ϖ = p^m` at a fixed precision `m ≥ 1`, and `X_{(j)} = 𝔛 ×_{Spf O_C} Spec(O_C/p^j)`.

### 1.1 Period lattice transport

**Period lattice transport.** On the perfectoid Siegel tower of genus `g`, let `W` be the tautological rank-`g` bundle on the flag variety `Fl` of Lagrangian subspaces, `ω_Fl = det(W^∨)` its ample line bundle, `A` the universal abelian variety and `ω_A = det(Lie A)^∨` the Hodge line. Construct `periodLatticeTransport`. From the equivariant isomorphism `Lie A ⊗ O(1) ≅ π_HT^*W` of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.3, taking determinants gives `π_HT^*ω_Fl ≅ ω_A(−g)`, with the Tate twist `(−g)` recorded before any trivialisation is chosen; after choosing a compatible trivialisation of the Tate twist this identifies the underlying line bundles, and the `G(ℚ_p)`-action is transported together with the similitude character. On each of the `2^g` Lagrangian coordinate charts, those whose `g` coordinates select one of `e_i, e_{g+i}` for each `i`, choose the Plücker frame and compare the finite-level automorphic lattice with the bounded lattice in that frame: on each quasicompact chart there are integers `a, b ≥ 0` with `p^a·L_bdd ⊂ L_fin ⊂ p^{−b}·L_bdd`. For a Hodge-type datum, the pullback uses the Hodge–Levi torsor comparison of PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§3.6, 3.8 and keeps the weight-dependent Tate twist. This is a transport of the supplied bundles along the supplied period map, not a second period map. The rational comparison alone gives no equality of integral lattices. Correspondences away from `p` preserve the transported data; the action of `G(ℚ_p)` moves the flag chart as well as the lattice and carries its character.

API: `periodLatticeTransport.periodLine` (`π_HT^*ω_Fl ≅ ω_A(−g)` with the chosen Tate trivialisation labelled); `periodLatticeTransport.latticeBounds` (for each chosen quasicompact chart, integers `a, b` with `p^a L_bdd ⊂ L_fin ⊂ p^{−b} L_bdd`); `periodLatticeTransport.tame_comm` (integral restrictions and tame correspondences commute with the transported rational comparison and the recorded denominators); `periodLatticeTransport.frameChange` (changing a Plücker frame by a rational unit transports the lattice inclusions through its norm bounds; an integral unit gives equal transported lattices; the identity frame change and successive frame changes agree with direct transport).

Source: [Scholze, Theorem 3.3.18 and proof, pp.1012–1016; Theorem 3.1.2(ii), p.972; Theorem 4.1.1, pp.1018–1020](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.3, §§3.6, 3.8; §0.4.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 1` the line comparison is `π_HT^*ω_Fl ≅ ω_A(−1)`. The twist `O(1)` carries the similitude character of `GSp_2(ℚ_p)` through the Weil pairing, so a comparison that omitted it would not be `G(ℚ_p)`-equivariant.
- For `g = 2` the coordinate plane `span(e_1, e_3)` is not isotropic, but its Plücker chart `Fl_{13}` is not empty: `span((1,0,0,1), (0,1,1,0))` is Lagrangian for `⟨v,w⟩ = v_1w_3 + v_2w_4 − v_3w_1 − v_4w_2` and has `p_{13} = p_{12} = 1`. This chart is excluded from the `2^g` admissible charts because it is not one of them, not because it is empty.
- If `L_fin = L_bdd` has bounds `a = b = 0`, replacing `L_bdd` by `pL_bdd` allows `a = 0`, `b = 1`: `pL_bdd ⊂ L_fin ⊂ p^{−1}(pL_bdd)`. The lattices are unequal. In general the new bounds can be taken as `max(a − 1, 0)` and `b + 1`; an API asserting equality of all chart lattices would fail.

### 1.2 The approximation atlas

**Approximation atlas transport.** For each `m ≥ 1`, take the finite-level formal model and the approximating Plücker sections modulo `p^m` on the Lagrangian coordinate cover supplied by PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.9, at a finite level that depends on `m` and on the finite set of charts and sections. This is Scholze's version of that target, which holds for every `p`; the Pilloni–Stroh variant with a level uniform in `m`, which that roadmap records as conditional at `p = 2`, is not used. Construct `approximationAtlas`, which identifies, through §0.2 and §0.3, the reductions modulo `p^m` of the formal charts with the bounded period-line reductions of §1.1 and the boundary-ideal reductions. Prove that two sufficiently accurate choices of approximating sections agree modulo `p^m` after passing to a common finer `p`-level and a common formal refinement, and hence induce the same maps on localised cusp sections; and construct the transport for each tame Hecke correspondence at common source levels. This is independence of the approximation maps after refinement, not uniqueness of the formal model before refinement. The approximations are taken with precision exceeding `m` plus the recorded lattice losses; only integral bounds that the approximation actually supplies are used.

API: `approximationAtlas.reducedChartMap` (the transported chart map identifies the formal lattice modulo `p^m` with its bounded chart reduction); `approximationAtlas.commonRefinement` (two approximation choices give equal localisation maps after a common sufficiently fine level and refinement); `approximationAtlas.heckeSquare` (for each tame correspondence, the pullback and boundary-preserving trace squares commute at fixed `m`); `approximationAtlas.refinement_comp` (the reduction and cusp-localisation maps commute with the refinement maps; the identity refinement acts as the identity, and successive refinements agree with their composite).

Source: [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.3.1, pp.1029–1031](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §3.9; §§0.2, 0.3, 1.1.

**Checks.**

- Adding `p^m` times an integral section leaves the reduction modulo `p^m` unchanged.
- At `m ≥ 1` the integral sections `0` and `p^{m−1}` in a unit frame agree modulo `p^{m−1}` and differ modulo `p^m`: approximation to one fewer digit does not determine the reduction map at precision `m`.
- If `e′ = u e` for an integral unit `u`, the coordinates of a fixed section are multiplied by `u^{−1}`; transporting coordinates and frames together leaves the section unchanged. At `p = 3`, the section `e` has coordinates `1` in `e` and `1/2` in `2e`.

### 1.3 Sections over the non-vanishing locus of a section

**Sections over a non-vanishing locus.** Let `Y` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme, `ℒ` an invertible sheaf, `s ∈ Γ(Y, ℒ)` and `ℱ` a quasi-coherent `O_Y`-module. Prove `Scheme.sections_nonvanishingLocus`: restriction induces an isomorphism `colim_k Γ(Y, ℱ ⊗ ℒ^{⊗k}) ≅ Γ(Y_s, ℱ)`, where the transition maps are multiplication by `s` and `a ∈ Γ(Y, ℱ ⊗ ℒ^{⊗k})` goes to `a ⊗ s^{−k}`. Injectivity needs only quasi-compactness. Source: [Stacks Project, Tag 01PW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01PW). *Needs:* Mathlib schemes and quasi-coherent modules.

### 1.4 Localised cusp sections

**Localised cuspidal sections.** On the formal model `𝔛` of §1.2 with ample `𝔏` and integral boundary ideal `𝔍`, for a nonempty `J ⊂ {1, …, N}` put `s̄_J = ∏_{j∈J} s̄_j ∈ Γ(X_{(m)}, 𝔏^{⊗|J|}/p^m)` and `r = |J|`. Prove `localizedCuspSections`: restriction identifies `Γ(D(s̄_J), 𝔍/p^m)` with `colim_k Γ(𝔛, (𝔍 ⊗ 𝔏^{⊗kr})/p^m)`, the transition maps being multiplication by `s̄_J`; the same holds along any cofinal subsequence of `k`, in particular after imposing a positive divisibility condition on `k`; and the identification carries the boundary condition and agrees with bounded chart localisation. The notation on the right means the reduction of `𝔍 ⊗ 𝔏^{⊗kr}`. The proof is §1.3 on the scheme `X_{(m)}`, whose locus `D(s̄_J)` is the reduction of the chart intersection `𝔘_J`, followed by the chart identifications of §§0.3, 1.2. The transition multiplies by the product `s̄_J`, so the twist increases by `|J|`; dropping `𝔍` would replace cusp sections by all sections.

Source: [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.3.1, pp.1030–1031](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§1.2, 1.3; AdicSpacesPartIII R2.1.

### 1.5 Serre vanishing over an arbitrary base

**Serre vanishing for flat sheaves of finite presentation.** Let `A` be any commutative ring, `f : Y → Spec A` proper and of finite presentation, `ℒ` an ample invertible sheaf on `Y`, and `ℱ` a quasi-coherent `O_Y`-module of finite presentation that is flat over `A`. Prove `serreVanishing_of_flat`: there is `t_0` such that `H^q(Y, ℱ ⊗ ℒ^{⊗t}) = 0` for every `q > 0` and `t ≥ t_0`. Write `A` as the filtered union of its finitely generated `ℤ`-subalgebras `A_λ`. For large `λ`, `Y`, `ℒ` and `ℱ` descend to `Y_λ` proper over `A_λ` (Stacks Tags 01ZM, 081F), `ℒ_λ` ample (Tag 09MT) and `ℱ_λ` of finite presentation and flat over `A_λ` (Tags 01ZR, 05LY). Noetherian Serre vanishing (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.6; AdicSpacesPartIII §F0.9) gives `t_0` with `H^q(Y_λ, ℱ_λ ⊗ ℒ_λ^{⊗t}) = 0` for `q > 0` and `t ≥ t_0`. For such `t`, `RΓ(Y_λ, ℱ_λ ⊗ ℒ_λ^{⊗t})` is a perfect complex concentrated in degree zero whose formation commutes with arbitrary base change (Tag 0B91), so its base change to `A`, which is `RΓ(Y, ℱ ⊗ ℒ^{⊗t})`, is concentrated in degree zero. Flatness is used; without it the base change of the cohomology need not vanish. Source: [Stacks Project, Tags 01ZM, 01ZR, 05LY, 081F, 09MT, 0B91](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B91), and, for the noetherian case, [Tag 0B5U](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B5U). *Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.6; AdicSpacesPartIII §F0.9.

### 1.6 Integral cuspidal lifting

**Integral cuspidal lifting.** Let `𝔛` be the projective formal model over `O_C` of §1.2, built on the genuine minimal compactification `X*_K` through the preimages of the image charts, with ample `𝔏` (generic fibre `ω`) and integral boundary ideal `𝔍` (generic fibre the boundary ideal `I`). Put `ℱ_t = 𝔍 ⊗ 𝔏^{⊗t}`. Prove `integralCuspLifting`: there is `t_0` such that for all `t ≥ t_0` and all `m ≥ 1` the reduction map `Γ(𝔛, ℱ_t) → Γ(X_{(m)}, ℱ_t/p^m)` is surjective, `H^1(X_{(j)}, ℱ_t/p^j) = 0` for every `j ≥ 1`, hence `H^1(𝔛, ℱ_t) = 0`, and `Γ(𝔛, ℱ_t)[1/p] = H⁰(X*_K, ω^{⊗t} ⊗ I)` is the space of classical cusp forms of weight `t`. The argument: `𝔍` is `p`-torsion free and coherent (§0.3), hence flat over `O_C`, so `𝔍/p` is flat and of finite presentation over `O_C/p`; `𝔏/p` is ample on `X_{(1)}` (§0.1), so §1.5 gives `t_0` with `H^1(X_{(1)}, ℱ_t/p) = 0` for `t ≥ t_0`. Since `ℱ_t` is `p`-torsion free, `0 → ℱ_t/p → ℱ_t/p^{j+1} → ℱ_t/p^j → 0` (the first map multiplication by `p^j`) is exact for every `j`; induction on `j` gives `H^1(X_{(j)}, ℱ_t/p^j) = 0` and surjectivity of `Γ(ℱ_t/p^{j+1}) → Γ(ℱ_t/p^j)`. As `Γ(𝔛, ℱ_t) = lim_j Γ(X_{(j)}, ℱ_t/p^j)` with surjective transition maps, reduction to `p^m` is surjective; and `H^1(𝔛, ℱ_t)` sits in the Milnor sequence between `lim¹_j Γ(X_{(j)}, ℱ_t/p^j)`, which vanishes by that surjectivity, and `lim_j H^1(X_{(j)}, ℱ_t/p^j) = 0`. The generic-fibre identification is §0.3 for the ideal and rigid GAGA on the proper `X*_K` (AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7). Vanishing of `H^1` of one reduction `ℱ_t/p^m` alone would not give integral lifting; the argument uses the vanishing for `ℱ_t/p`, which propagates to every `p^j` because `ℱ_t` has no `p`-torsion. The formal model and its boundary ideal live on the genuine compactification, so no global isomorphism between the genuine and image compactifications is used.

Source: [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.3.1, pp.1030–1031](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the corrected compactification of p.1030 (see *Corrections*). *Needs:* §§0.1, 0.3, 1.4, 1.5; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7 (GAGA); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.cohomologyδ`.

### 1.7 Classical section approximation

**Classical section approximation.** Fix `m ≥ 1`, a positive weight step `a`, and a finite list of bounded cuspidal sections on chart intersections `V_J` of the image-compactified tower. Prove `classicalSectionApprox`: there are a common finer finite `p`-level and, in the prescribed cofinal sequence of weights `ak`, all sufficiently large weights, such that every section in the list is represented modulo `p^m`, after the recorded integral losses, by the localisation of the reduction of an integral classical cusp form of that weight; the representations are compatible with a prescribed finite set of tame pullback and trace correspondences. When the representations feed the determinant interpolation of Layer 3b, the step satisfies `a > n` for `Sp_2n` and `a ≥ n` for `U(n,n)`. The proof applies `boundaryIdeal.colimit_iso` to the bounded sections modulo `p^m`, uses `boundaryIdeal.sections_almostIso` when these sections represent terms of the almost Čech complex, localises on the approximation atlas with §1.4 along the cofinal weight sequence, lifts with §1.6, and repeats at common levels for the finite set of correspondences.

**Checks.**

- A bounded section already descending to a genuine chart uses the ordinary quotient of sections there; its image in the quotient sheaf agrees after the almost comparison of §2a.1.

## Layer 2: coherent, completed and torsion cohomology

The almost support comparison of Layer 0 joins the fixed-exponent completed carrier of CompletedCohomologyPartII to the Čech complex of the boundary ideal on perfectoid charts. Geometric dimension gives the amplitude and its Iwasawa consequences. Continuity of the Hecke action for the classical topology is pointwise on finite sets of classes. Descent and coefficient limits use the completed objects of CompletedCohomologyPartII with their derived-limit conditions. Throughout, `(G, D)` is a Shimura datum of Hodge type with a fixed embedding into `(GSp_2g, D_{GSp_2g})`, the tame level `K^p` lies in the principal level-`N` subgroup of `GSp_2g(𝔸_f^p)` for some `N ≥ 3` prime to `p`, and `d = dim_C X_K`.

### 2.1 The perfectoid compact-support comparison

**Constructible primitive comparison.** Prove `constructiblePrimitiveComparison`: for a proper morphism `f : X → Y` of finite-type `C`-schemes, constructible `𝔽_p`-sheaf `F` and `i ≥ 0`, the canonical map `(R^if_*F)^ad ⊗ O^{+a}/p → R^if^{ad}_*(F^ad ⊗ O^{+a}/p)` is an isomorphism. For proper, possibly singular `X`, open `j : U → X`, constructible `ℤ/p^m`-sheaf `F` on `U` and `m ≥ 1`, deduce `H^i_ét(X,j_!F) ⊗ O_C^a/p^m ≅ H^i_ét(X^ad,j_!(F^ad ⊗ O_U^{+a}/p^m))`, naturally for maps of pairs and coefficient change. Use PadicHodgeTheory §2.7's smooth, SNC and singular proper comparisons, constructible devissage and proper base change; prove trace compatibility for finite étale maps over `U`. Source: [Scholze, *Perfectoid spaces: a survey*, Theorem 3.13 and Lemmas 3.14–3.16, pp.17–19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.5948); [Scholze, proof of Theorem 4.2.1, pp.1021–1023](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* PadicHodgeTheory §2.7.

**Checks.**

- At a point the comparison is scalar extension and all positive-degree groups vanish.
- On `ℙ¹` with `U = 𝔸¹`, extension by zero gives `H⁰_c = 0`; replacing `j_!` by `j_*` would give a nonzero degree-zero group.
- For a proper nodal curve the singular comparison uses resolution and localization; its cohomological upper bound is still `2`, and the two preimages of the node in the normalization remain distinct.

**Perfectoid compact-support comparison.** For every `m ≥ 1` and `i ≥ 0`, prove `perfectoidComparison`: there is a natural isomorphism of almost `O_C`-modules

```text
H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m) ⊗_{ℤ/p^m} O_C^a/p^m  ≅  H^i(X̄_{K^p}, (I⁺_∞/p^m)^a),
```

where the right side is cohomology on the topological space `|X̄_{K^p}|` of the image-compactified perfectoid tower. It is computed by the alternating almost Čech complex of the sections of `I⁺_∞/p^m`, equivalently the almostified quotients `I⁺_∞(V_J)/p^m` by `boundaryIdeal.sections_almostIso`, on the finite cover of `X̄_{K^p}` by the `2^g` Lagrangian chart preimages, since all their intersections are affinoid perfectoid and almost acyclic. It is compatible with change of `m`, with pullback for `K_p^1 ⊂ K_p^2`, and with tame Hecke correspondences through both pullback and trace on common refinements. The proof applies, at each finite level, the constructible primitive comparison just stated to `j_!ℤ/p^m` on the genuine proper minimal compactification `X*_K`, which is singular in general; since the finite map `X*_K → X̄_K` is an isomorphism over the interior, the compactly supported cohomology is the same on both compactifications. It then passes to the filtered `p`-level system through the tilde-limit theorem of PerfectoidSpaces §P7.15, identifies `j_!O⁺/p^m` with the almost boundary ideal through §0.6, compares étale and analytic cohomology by étale almost acyclicity, uses almost purity over the interior to compare traces, and uses almost Čech acyclicity on all chart intersections. The compatibility of the primitive comparison with finite étale traces over the interior is proved here from the definition of the trace. The left side is the fixed-exponent carrier of CompletedCohomologyPartII §8.6.

Source: [Scholze, Theorem 4.2.1 and proof, pp.1021–1023](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), which uses Corollary 7.18 and Propositions 6.14 and 7.13 of *Perfectoid spaces*. *Needs:* §0.6; §2a.1 (`boundaryIdeal.sections_almostIso`); CompletedCohomologyPartII §8.6; PadicHodgeTheory §2.7; PerfectoidSpaces Layers P2, P3 and P7; PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §§2.1, 2.3, 2.8.

Every degree, support and coefficient exponent is recorded, and the tame correspondence square is checked for the trace as well as for the pullback.

**Checks.**

- For empty boundary the almost Čech terms are `(O⁺(V_J)/p^m)^a`, giving the proper comparison.
- For zero boundary ideal all Čech terms vanish.
- At a point with unit ideal the only Čech term is `(O_C/p^m)^a` in degree zero; the quotient of sections and the sections of the quotient agree even before almostification in this case.

### 2.2 Almost faithfulness of free modules over `O_C/p`

**Almost faithfulness.** Prove `almostZero_tensor_iff`: for an `𝔽_p`-vector space `V`, the `O_C/p`-module `V ⊗_{𝔽_p} O_C/p` is almost zero if and only if `V = 0`. The module is free over `O_C/p`, and `O_C/p` is not almost zero because `p^{1/2}·1 ≠ 0` in it; a nonzero free module therefore contains a copy of `O_C/p`. The statement is specific to free modules: `𝔪_C/p` is a nonzero `O_C/p`-module that is not almost zero, but the residue field `O_C/𝔪_C` is a nonzero almost-zero module. Source: [Scholze, proof of Corollary 4.2.2, p.1023](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* PerfectoidSpaces Layer P0.

### 2.3 Cohomological dimension of the perfectoid compactification

**Cohomological dimension.** For a spectral space `S` say that `S` has cohomological dimension `≤ d` if `H^{q}(S, ℱ) = 0` for every abelian sheaf `ℱ` and every `q > d`. Prove `cohomologicalDimension_perfectoidCompactification`: `|X̄_{K^p}|` has cohomological dimension `≤ d`. The proof has two parts. (a) If `S = lim S_j` is a cofiltered limit of spectral spaces of cohomological dimension `≤ d` along spectral maps, then `S` has cohomological dimension `≤ d`: every abelian sheaf on `S` is a filtered colimit of constructible sheaves, each constructible sheaf is pulled back from some `S_j`, and cohomology on `S` is the colimit of cohomology on the `S_j`. (b) Each finite-level `|X̄_K|` has cohomological dimension `≤ d`: it is the limit of the underlying spaces of its formal models, each the underlying space of a scheme of finite type and dimension `≤ d` over the residue field of `O_C`, which is a noetherian spectral space of dimension `≤ d`, so Grothendieck's vanishing theorem and (a) apply. The bound uses `|X̄_{K^p}| ≅ lim_{K_p} |X̄_{K_pK^p}|` from PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3. Source: [Scholze, proof of Corollary 4.2.2, pp.1023–1024](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), which cites Huber's Proposition 2.5.8 for (b) and gives the formal-model argument as an alternative. *Needs:* PerfectoidShimuraVarieties §2.3; AdicSpacesPartIII R2.3, R2.5 (formal models and Raynaud's theorem); Grothendieck's vanishing for noetherian spaces ([Stacks Project, Tag 02UZ](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02UZ)).

**Checks.**

- The empty spectral space has zero cohomology in every degree.
- A point has `H⁰` equal to the stalk and no higher cohomology.
- For the Zariski space `S = 𝔸¹_k`, `U = S ∖ {0,1}`, `j : U ↪ S` and the two closed-point inclusions `i₀`, `i₁`, the exact sequence `0 → j_!ℤ → ℤ → i₀,*ℤ ⊕ i₁,*ℤ → 0` gives `H¹(S, j_!ℤ) = ℤ`: the map on global sections is the diagonal `ℤ → ℤ²`, and the constant sheaf on the irreducible space `S` is flasque. Thus the bound `1` is sharp; affine quasi-coherent acyclicity cannot replace all-abelian-sheaf cohomological dimension.

### 2.4 Geometric completed amplitude

**Vanishing above the middle degree.** Prove `completedCohomology_vanishing`: for `i > d` and every `m ≥ 1`, `H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m) = 0`, and the ordinary inverse limit `H̃^i_c(ℤ_p) = lim_m H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m)` vanishes for `i > d`. For `m = 1`, §2.1 identifies `H̃^i_c(𝔽_p) ⊗ O_C^a/p` with almost cohomology of a sheaf on `|X̄_{K^p}|`, which vanishes for `i > d` by §2.3; §2.2 then gives `H̃^i_c(𝔽_p) = 0`. For general `m`, induct with the Bockstein sequences of `0 → ℤ/p^{m−1} → ℤ/p^m → ℤ/p → 0`. Vanishing in degrees `≤ d` is not claimed, and the derived coefficient limit keeps its own conditions (CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.3–2.9). Source: [Scholze, Corollary 4.2.2 and proof, pp.1023–1024; Theorem 1.0.7, p.950](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§2.1–2.3; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.3–2.9.

Every `m` is covered, not only `m = 1`.

### 2.5 Geometric Iwasawa bounds

**Iwasawa bounds.** Let `K_p` be a torsion-free pro-`p` level and `Λ = ℤ_p[[K_p]]`. Prove `completedHomology_bounds`: the completed Borel–Moore homology `H̃^{BM}_i` vanishes for `i > d`, `H̃^{BM}_d` is `p`-torsion free, and for `i < d` the completed homology `H̃_i` has codimension at least `d − i` over `Λ`, where the codimension of a finitely generated `Λ`-module `M` is its grade `j_Λ(M) = min{i : Ext^i_Λ(M, Λ) ≠ 0}` over the possibly noncommutative Auslander-regular ring `Λ`, as in CompletedCohomologyPartII §5.5. The first two statements follow from §2.4 through completed Poincaré duality (CompletedCohomologyPartII §7.6) and a Bockstein argument. For the third, suppose some `H̃_i` with `i < d` violates the bound and choose, among all violating modules, one of minimal codimension; the grade spectral sequence of CompletedCohomologyPartII §5.5 then gives a contradiction, because the terms of smaller codimension that could interfere are excluded by minimality. The relevant invariants of the group are `l_0 = 0` and `q_0 = d`. Ordinary and Borel–Moore supports are kept separate; an inequality for `i < d` is not a freeness statement. Source: [Scholze, Corollary 4.2.3 and proof, p.1024](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the corrections recorded under *Corrections*. *Needs:* §2.4; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§4.1–4.6, CompletedCohomologyPartII §5.5, CompletedCohomologyPartII §7.6.

### 2.6 The classical Hecke topology

**Classical cusp action over a finite extension.** At fixed sufficiently small Hodge-type level `K` and weight `t ≥ 1`, prove that `H⁰(X*_K, ω^t ⊗ I)` and all its tame Hecke operators descend simultaneously to a finite extension `E/ℚ_p`. Use the rational cusp space over a number field and field base change of AutomorphicBundles §B4.18, its geometric Hecke maps §§B5.10–B5.11, and the proper toroidal-to-minimal morphism and canonical models of ShimuraCompactifications §§C2.8–C2.9. This target also proves the characteristic-zero cusp-section comparison: normality gives `π_*O = O`, and the reduced inverse-image boundary maps onto the reduced minimal boundary, so `π_*I_D = I`; the projection formula identifies the Hodge-line cusp sections on both models. Complete the number field at the chosen `p`-adic place and extend to `C`. The same `E` works for every tame operator because the entire Hecke correspondence construction is defined over the rational model, rather than by descending each matrix separately. Source: [Scholze, Corollary 5.1.7 and proof, pp.1036–1037](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), the rational cusp space preceding it; [Stacks, Tag 02KH](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02KH) for flat field base change. *Needs:* AutomorphicBundles §B4.18, §§B5.10–B5.11; ShimuraCompactifications §§C2.8–C2.9; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7.

**Checks.**

- Scalar `ℤ_p[α]` acting on `C` preserves `O_C` for a transcendental unit `α`, but is not finite over `ℤ_p`; this action has no finite-extension form.
- On a one-dimensional `E`-form a bounded Hecke image is contained in `p^{−b}O_E` for some `b`, hence is a finite `ℤ_p`-module.
- A zero cusp space has zero image and descends over any such `E`; field extension preserves the zero space and the action kernel.

**Classical Hecke topology.** Let `T = ℤ_p[G(𝔸_f^p)//K^p]` with `vol(K^p) = 1`, and fix a positive weight step `a`. Define `T_cl` to be `T` with the weakest topology for which every action map

```text
T → End_C(H⁰(X*_K, ω^{⊗ak} ⊗ I)),   K = K_pK^p,  k ≥ 1,
```

is continuous, over all sufficiently small `K_p ⊂ G(ℚ_p)`, where `H⁰(X*_K, ω^{⊗ak} ⊗ I)` is the finite-dimensional `C`-vector space of classical cusp forms on the genuine minimal compactification and `End_C` carries its `p`-adic topology. In Lean this is the initial topology of a family of ring maps into topological rings, `⨅_i TopologicalSpace.induced (ρ_i)`. Let `T_{K,k}` be the image of `T` in that endomorphism ring. Use the preceding form over `E/ℚ_p`. Pullback and the integral trace of §0.8 preserve the integral cusp sections of §1.6, so the Hecke image is bounded in the finite-dimensional `E`-endomorphism space. It is therefore contained in a finite `ℤ_p`-lattice; as a `ℤ_p`-submodule of that lattice, `T_{K,k}` is finitely generated and torsion-free. Its subspace topology is its `p`-adic topology; finite intersections of the ideals `ker(T → T_{K,k}/p^r)` therefore form a basis of open ideals of `T_cl`. The topology need not be separated, and passing to the separated completion is a separate operation. The weight step used for determinant interpolation in Layer 3b satisfies `a > n` for `Sp_2n` and `a ≥ n` for `U(n,n)`, and the classical spaces are taken for every `k ≥ 1`, not only for large `k`. An integral lattice describes the neighbourhoods only when it is Hecke-stable; it is not part of the definition.

API: `heckeTopology.basis` (finite intersections of `ker(T → T_{K,k}/p^r)` over finitely many `(K_p, k)` and `r ≥ 1` form a basis of open ideals); `heckeTopology.lattice_basis` (for any Hecke-stable bounded `O_C`-lattice `Λ` in a classical cusp space, the kernels of `T → End(Λ/p^rΛ)` give the same neighbourhood filter as that space's `p`-adic topology; the integral cusp sections of §1.6 are such a lattice); `heckeTopology.continuous_action` (each defining action is continuous); `heckeTopology.cofinal_weights` (restricting to a cofinal sequence of weights changes the neighbourhoods only through the approximation maps of §1.7); `heckeTopology.continuous_iff` (a ring map `f : B → T_cl` from a topological ring is continuous if and only if every defining action composed with `f` is continuous; this criterion is preserved by identities and composition).

Source: [Scholze, Theorem 4.3.1 and footnote 18, p.1025; Corollaries 5.1.7 and 5.1.11, pp.1036–1037 and 1038](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). Theorem 4.3.1 defines the topology, its footnote notes that it may not be separated, Corollary 5.1.7 shows the image of `T` is flat over `ℤ_p` with finite étale generic fibre, and the proof of Corollary 5.1.11 uses the basis of open ideals and the weight range of Proposition 5.1.1. *Needs:* §§0.8, 1.6; the cusp-action form over a finite extension above; Mathlib `TopologicalSpace.induced`, `continuous_iInf_rng`; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 4 (uniform congruence witnesses).

**Checks.**

- For the two actions `ℤ → ℤ/2` and `ℤ → ℤ/3` with discrete targets, the open subgroups of the initial topology on `ℤ` are those containing `6ℤ`: `6ℤ` is open, `4ℤ` is not. The neighbourhoods come from the intersection of kernels, not their union.

### 2.7 Continuity of the Hecke action

**Hecke continuity.** Prove `heckeContinuity`: for every `m ≥ 1` and `i ≥ 0`, the action `T_cl → End(H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m))` is continuous when `H̃^i_c(ℤ/p^m)` is discrete and the target carries the pointwise topology; equivalently, the annihilator in `T` of any finite set of classes contains a basic open ideal of §2.6. The statement is compatible with tame correspondences. ### 2.8 Descent and coefficient limits

**Descent and coefficient limits.** Prove `finiteLevelDescent`: for a torsion-free pro-`p` level `K_p` and its finite-level quotients, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of CompletedCohomologyPartII §§6.1–6.4 for the actual tower descends `H̃^*_c(ℤ/p^m)` to `H^*_c(X_{K_pK^p}, ℤ/p^m)`; its `E_2` rows are nonzero only for tower degrees `0 ≤ j ≤ d` (§2.4), so each finite-level group has a Hecke-stable filtration of length at most `d + 1`. ## Layer 3a: weighted-determinant algebra

This layer is commutative algebra and polynomial-law algebra over arbitrary commutative rings, including nonreduced, disconnected and zero rings. It uses Mathlib polynomials, Laurent polynomials, power series and polynomial laws, and the determinants of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0; it has no geometric, automorphic or Galois prerequisite, so it can be built first and imported by any roadmap that separates factors of a determinant. Its main theorem extracts, from a determinant of `G × ℤ` whose characteristic polynomials factor by weight, a determinant of `G` for each weight. Weighted extraction is a theorem about an input determinant; it never assumes that an ambient representation splits.

### 3a.1 The twisted polynomial product

**Twisted polynomial product.** For a commutative ring `R`, polynomials `P, Q ∈ R[X]` and a unit `u ∈ Rˣ`, define

```text
weightedProduct P Q u = P(u⁻¹X) · Q(uX) = P.comp (C u⁻¹ * X) * Q.comp (C u * X).
```

It records the opposite character weights by which a Levi factor and its dual are separated. It is ordinary substitution through `Polynomial.comp`, and it creates no new polynomial carrier. Its basic theory identifies the value at `u = 1` with `P·Q` and the constant coefficient with `P(0)·Q(0)`, proves compatibility with coefficient change along every ring map `f : R → S`, and proves `weightedProduct P Q u = weightedProduct Q P u⁻¹` by exchanging the opposite substitutions.

Source: the character-twist computation in [Scholze, proof of Lemma 5.3.4, pp.1048–1049](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* Mathlib `Polynomial.comp`, `Polynomial.compRingHom`, `Polynomial.coeff_mul`. *Lean:* `weightedProduct`.

**Checks.**

- Constants: for `P = 2`, `Q = 3` over `ℤ` and `u = −1`, the result is the constant `6`.
- Opposite scaling: over `ℚ` with `P = 1 − 2X`, `Q = 1 − 3X`, `u = 2`, the result is `(1 − X)(1 − 6X) = 1 − 7X + 6X²`; scaling both factors by `u` would give `(1 − 4X)(1 − 6X) = 1 − 10X + 24X²`.
- Collision: at `u = 1`, interchanging `1 − 2X` and `1 − 3X` leaves the product unchanged, so separation needs the unit hypotheses of §3a.2 and is not built into the definition.

### 3a.2 Twisted-product separation

**Separation theorem.** Prove `twisted_product_separation`: let `R` be any commutative ring, `n ≥ 0`, `u ∈ Rˣ`, and `P, Q, P′, Q′ ∈ R[X]` of degree at most `n` with constant term `1`. If `P·Q = P′·Q′`, `weightedProduct P Q u = weightedProduct P′ Q′ u`, and `1 − u^{2j}` is a unit for every `1 ≤ j ≤ n`, then `P = P′` and `Q = Q′`. Proof by induction on the coefficient index: if the coefficients agree below `j`, comparing the coefficients of `X^j` gives `δP_j + δQ_j = 0` from the product and `u^{−j}δP_j + u^jδQ_j = 0` from the twisted product, so `(1 − u^{2j})δQ_j = 0`; the unit hypothesis gives `δQ_j = 0 = δP_j`, and coefficients above `n` vanish by the degree bound. There is no domain or characteristic hypothesis, and the statement commutes with ring maps that preserve the unit hypotheses. For `n = 0` all four polynomials are `1`. The hypothesis is invertibility of each difference, not `u^{2j} ≠ 1`.

Source: [Scholze, proof of Lemma 5.3.4, pp.1048–1049](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), abstracted from the first-differing-coefficient computation. *Needs:* §3a.1; Mathlib `Polynomial.coeff_mul`. *Lean:* `twisted_product_separation`.

**Checks.**

- Over `ℤ/9` take `u = 2`, `P = 1 + 3X`, `Q = 1`, `P′ = 1`, `Q′ = 1 + 3X`. Both products are `1 + 3X`, both twisted products are `1 + 6X` (as `2^{−1} = 5` and `3·5 = 6`), yet `P ≠ P′`: here `1 − u² = 6` is nonzero but not a unit.
- Over `ℚ` with `u = 2`, `n = 1`: `1 − u² = −3` is a unit, so `(1 − 2X)(1 − 3X)` with twisted product `1 − 7X + 6X²` determines `P = 1 − 2X`, `Q = 1 − 3X`; the swapped pair has twisted product `(1 − (3/2)X)(1 − 4X) = 1 − (11/2)X + 6X²`.
- For `n = 0` the theorem holds over the zero ring and every ring: degree-zero polynomials with constant term `1` are equal to `1`.

### 3a.3 Finite-weight factorisation of series

**Uniqueness and polynomiality of weight factorisations.** For `m ∈ ℤ` and `F ∈ S⟦X⟧` write `F(T^mX) ∈ S[T^{±1}]⟦X⟧` for `PowerSeries.rescale (T^m) (PowerSeries.map C F)`. Prove `finiteWeight_factorization_unique`: let `S` be a commutative ring and `M ⊂ ℤ` a finite set; if `Q_m, Q′_m ∈ S⟦X⟧` have constant term `1` for `m ∈ M` and `∏_{m∈M} Q_m(T^mX) = ∏_{m∈M} Q′_m(T^mX)`, then `Q_m = Q′_m` for every `m ∈ M`. Dividing (each factor is a unit) reduces to `∏ Q_m(T^mX) = 1`. Let `r ≥ 1` be the first order at which some `Q_m` differs from `1`; modulo `X^{r+1}` the product is `1 + X^r Σ_m q_{m,r}T^{mr}`, and since the exponents `mr` are distinct for distinct `m`, every `q_{m,r} = 0`, a contradiction. Prove `finiteWeight_factorization_polynomial`: if moreover `∏_{m∈M} Q_m(T^mX)` is a polynomial in `X` with Laurent coefficients, then every `Q_m` is a polynomial. For the least weight `m_0`, replacing `X` by `XT^{−m_0}` lowers every weight by `m_0`, so one may assume `m_0 = 0` and all weights `≥ 0`; the product then lies in `S[T][[X]] ∩ S[T^{±1}][X] = S[T, X]`, and setting `T = 0` shows `Q_0 ∈ S[X]`. Every monomial `X^aT^e` of the remaining product `Q′ = ∏_{m>0} Q_m(T^mX)` has `a ≤ e`, since the remaining weights are positive, so expanding in powers of `T` gives `Q′ = Σ_{e≥0} φ_e(X)T^e` with `φ_e ∈ S[X]` of degree at most `e`. The coefficient of `T^e` in `Q′·Q_0 = ∏_{m∈M} Q_m(T^mX)` is `φ_e·Q_0`, which vanishes once `e` exceeds the `T`-degree of the product; since `Q_0` has constant term `1` it is a nonzerodivisor of `S[X]`, so `φ_e = 0` for those `e` and `Q′ ∈ S[T, X]`. Induction on `|M|` finishes. Cancelling `Q_0` from a product that is only known to be a polynomial in `X` would not suffice, since `Q_0` is not a unit of `S[T, X]`; the expansion in `T` is what makes the cancellation coefficientwise. The statement is natural in ring maps `S → S′`. Finite weight support is essential, and a repeated weight must be grouped into one factor before uniqueness is asserted. Source: [Scholze, Lemma 5.3.10 and proof, pp.1053–1054](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), with the corrected first order and expansion variable (see *Corrections*). *Needs:* Mathlib `PowerSeries.rescale`, `PowerSeries.map`, `LaurentPolynomial`, `Polynomial.coeToPowerSeries.ringHom`. *Lean:* `weightSubst`, `finiteWeight_factorization_unique`, `finiteWeight_factorization_polynomial`.

The order-one case is the first nontrivial instance: `Q_0 = 1 + aX`, `Q_1 = 1 + bX` give `1 + (a + bT)X + abTX²`, from which `a` and `b` are read off as the coefficients of `T^0` and `T^1` in the `X`-coefficient.

**Checks.**

- Weight zero: `F(T^0X)` is `F` with its coefficients viewed as constant Laurent polynomials.
- Direction of the weight: `(1 − X)(T^2X) = 1 − T^2X`, not `1 − T^{−2}X` and not `1 − 2X`.
- Distinct weights are distinguished: `(1 + X)(T^1X) = 1 + TX` and `(1 + X)(T^2X) = 1 + T^2X` differ, while a repeated weight `m = m′` would merge two factors into `(1 + T^mX)^2`, which is why the weights form a set.

### 3a.4 Homogeneous decomposition of multiplicative group-algebra laws

**Homogeneous decomposition.** Let `R` be a commutative ring, `G` a group, and `D : R[G] → R` a multiplicative `R`-polynomial law in the sense of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 (`D_S(1) = 1` and `D_S(xy) = D_S(x)D_S(y)` after every scalar extension `S`). Prove `PolynomialLaw.exists_homogeneous_decomposition`: there are an integer `N` and a decomposition `R = R_0 × ⋯ × R_N` into direct factors (a complete family of orthogonal idempotents) such that for each `d`, the induced law `R_d[G] → R_d` is homogeneous of degree `d`, hence a `d`-dimensional determinant. Restricting `D` to scalars gives a multiplicative law `R → R`; evaluating it at `T ∈ R[T]` gives `f(T) = Σ_k a_kT^k` with `f(UT) = f(U)f(T)` in `R[U, T]`. If `a_d` is the lowest nonzero coefficient, comparing coefficients of `U^dT^d` gives `a_d² = a_d`; split `R` along this idempotent; on the factor where `a_d = 1`, comparing coefficients of `U^dT^{d+i}` gives `a_{d+i} = 0` for `i ≥ 1`, so `f(T) = T^d`, and multiplicativity gives `D(s·x) = D(s·1)D(x) = s^dD(x)`; on the complementary factor continue with the next coefficient. The process ends because `f` has finitely many coefficients and `f(1) = D(1) = 1`. Without the unit condition `D(1) = 1`, as in the source, a further factor appears on which the law is identically zero; under the convention of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants that factor is the zero ring. Degree zero and disconnected or nonreduced coefficient rings are included. Source: [Scholze, Lemma 5.3.13 and proof, pp.1056–1057](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 (multiplicative and homogeneous laws, determinants); Mathlib `PolynomialLaw`, `IsIdempotentElem`.

**Checks.**

- For `R = ℚ × ℚ` and `D(x) = (det_1(x), det_2(x))` with `det_1` the augmentation and `det_2` the square of the augmentation on `ℚ[G]`, the decomposition is `R_1 = ℚ × 0`, `R_2 = 0 × ℚ`; `D` is homogeneous of no single degree on `R`.
- The constant law `1` is homogeneous of degree `0`: `f(T) = 1`, `R = R_0`.
- On a connected ring (no nontrivial idempotents) the law is homogeneous of a single degree; over `ℤ` the law `x ↦ ε(x)²` (with `ε` the augmentation) has `f(T) = T²` and degree `2`.

### 3a.5 The weighted unit law

**Weighted unit law.** Let `G` be a group and `R` a commutative ring. For each `m ∈ ℤ` let `g ↦ P_g^{(m)}(X)` be a map `G → R[X]` with values of degree at most `n_m` and constant term `1`, with `n_m = 0` for all but finitely many `m`; put `h = Σ_m n_m`. Suppose `D̃` is an `h`-dimensional determinant of the group `G × ℤ` with coefficients in `R[T^{±1}]`, with `V` the generator of `ℤ`, such that for every `g ∈ G` and every `k ∈ ℤ`

```text
D̃(1 − X·V^k g) = ∏_{m∈ℤ} P_g^{(m)}(T^{km}X)   in R[T^{±1}][X].
```

Construct `weightedUnitLaw`: for every `R`-algebra `S` and every `m`, the unique multiplicative map `D_0^{(m)} : 1 + U·S[G]⟦U⟧ → 1 + U·S⟦U⟧` with `D̃(f(XV)) = ∏_m D_0^{(m)}(f(XT^m))` in `S[T^{±1}]⟦X⟧` for every `f`. It satisfies `D_0^{(m)}(1 − U^a x g) = P_g^{(m)}(U^a x)` for `a ≥ 1`, `x ∈ S`, `g ∈ G`, and it maps `1 + U·S[G][U]` into `1 + U·S[U]`. Uniqueness of each value is §3a.3; multiplicativity follows from uniqueness and multiplicativity of `D̃`; existence reduces, through `U`-adic closure and multiplicativity, to the generators `1 − U^a x g`, where the defining identity of `D̃` gives the decomposition. Then define the law `D^{(m)} : R[G] → R` by `D^{(m)}(x) = D_0^{(m)}(1 + U(x − 1))|_{U=1}`; polynomiality is proved before evaluation at `U = 1`. Prove that `D^{(m)}` is multiplicative: reduce to the universal case and then to nilpotent `x, y`, and show that every `f ∈ 1 + U·S[G][U]` with `f ≡ 1` modulo a nilpotent ideal and `f(1) = 1` is a product of quotients `(1 − U^{a+1}zg)/(1 − U^a zg)` with `z` nilpotent, on which `D_0^{(m)}(·)|_{U=1} = 1`. The kernel element is formed with the inverse `(1 − Uy)^{−1}(1 − Ux)^{−1}` of `(1 − Ux)(1 − Uy)`, in that order (see *Corrections*). The characteristic identities are required for every `g ∈ G` and every `k ∈ ℤ`, not only for Frobenius elements or `k = 1`. Continuity, when `G` is profinite, is treated separately in Layer 3b.

API: `weightedUnitLaw.charpoly` (`D_0^{(m)}(1 − U^a xg) = P_g^{(m)}(U^a x)` for `a ≥ 1` over every test algebra); `weightedUnitLaw.map_mul` (`D_0^{(m)}(fg) = D_0^{(m)}(f)D_0^{(m)}(g)`, including noncommuting group-algebra inputs); `weightedUnitLaw.evaluated` (`D^{(m)}(x) = D_0^{(m)}(1 + U(x − 1))|_{U=1}` is natural and multiplicative); `weightedUnitLaw.naturality` (for every `R`-algebra map `S → S′`, coefficient change commutes with the unit law and its evaluation; identity and composite maps obey the same law); `weightedUnitLaw.ext` (the values on all `1 − U^a xg` determine the unit law, by finite-order approximation and §3a.3).

Source: [Scholze, Lemma 5.3.8, Remark 5.3.9 and Lemmas 5.3.11–5.3.12 with proofs, pp.1052–1056](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §3a.3; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 (determinants, Amitsur's formula, multiplicative laws); Mathlib `PolynomialLaw`.

**Checks.**

- Rank one: for a character `χ : G → Rˣ` and `P_g = 1 − χ(g)X` in a single weight, the evaluated law sends `Σ a_g g` to `Σ a_gχ(g)`; the augmentation is the case `χ = 1`.
- Degree zero: if `n_m = 0`, then `P^{(m)} = 1`, and both the unit law and the evaluated law are identically `1`, including at `0 ∈ R[G]`.
- Nilpotent coefficients: over `S = k[ε]/(ε²)` the unit law sends `1 − Uεg` to `1 − Uεχ(g)`, keeping `ε`; reducing to `k` first would lose this coefficient.

### 3a.6 Weighted determinant factors

**Extraction of weighted factors.** Under the hypotheses of §3a.5, prove `weightedDeterminantFactors`: for each `m` there is a unique `n_m`-dimensional determinant `D^{(m)} : R[G] → R` with `D^{(m)}(1 − Xg) = P_g^{(m)}(X)` for every `g ∈ G`; it is compatible with every ring map `R → R′` and with nilpotent quotients. The law is the evaluated law of §3a.5. Its homogeneity: §3a.4 splits `R` into factors on which `D^{(m)}` has some degree `d_m`; on a nonzero factor, `D^{(m)}(1 − Xg)` has degree exactly `d_m` because `D^{(m)}(g)` is a unit, so `d_m ≤ n_m`; and `Σ_m d_m = h = Σ_m n_m` because the characteristic polynomial of `D̃` at `g` has degree exactly `h` with unit top coefficient. Hence `d_m = n_m` on every factor. Uniqueness follows from *Coefficient reconstruction from group words* in IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0. The law is a homogeneous multiplicative polynomial law, not a pointwise choice of polynomial roots; exact degrees are not inferred from the upper bounds alone. Source: [Scholze, Lemma 5.3.8 and Remark 5.3.9, pp.1052–1053, with the proof through p.1057](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§3a.4, 3a.5; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0.

Degree zero and disconnected or nonreduced coefficient rings are included; nothing from Layers 0–2 or from automorphic forms enters this theorem. In [Suggested.lean](Suggested.lean), `weightedDeterminantFactors` states the extraction on Mathlib’s `PolynomialLaw`, with the unital, multiplicative and homogeneous conditions written at every test algebra. The definition `lawCharpoly` evaluates a law at `1 − Xg`.

### 3a.7 The Laurent coefficient ideal

**Laurent coefficient-ideal bound.** For a commutative ring `R` and `a ∈ ℤ`, let `laurentSubst a : R[T^{±1}] → R[T^{±1}]` be the substitution `T ↦ T^a`, the ring map induced by multiplication by `a` on `ℤ`. For an ideal `I ⊂ R[T^{±1}]` put `I_a = laurentSubst(a)^{−1}(I)` and `I′ = ⋂_{a∈ℤ} I_a`, and let `J ⊂ R` be the ideal generated by all Laurent coefficients of elements of `I′`. Prove `laurentCoeffIdeal_pow_eq_bot`: if `N ≥ 1` and `I^N = 0`, then `J^N = 0`; and prove the containment `I′ ⊂ J·R[T^{±1}]`. Consequently `R[T^{±1}] → (R/J)[T^{±1}]` factors through `R[T^{±1}]/I′`, so characteristic identities realised modulo `I′` descend to `(R/J)[T^{±1}]` with the same exponent `N`. For the bound, take `f_1, …, f_N ∈ I′` with exponents in `[−B, B]` and choose `a_i = (2B + 1)^{i−1}`; distinct tuples `(k_1, …, k_N) ∈ [−B, B]^N` give distinct sums `Σ a_ik_i`, so the coefficient of `T^{Σ a_ik_i}` in `∏ f_i(T^{a_i}) ∈ I^N = 0` is the product of the coefficients of `T^{k_i}` in `f_i`; every product of `N` generators of `J` is therefore zero. In the determinant application, every transformed quotient is realised first and the law over the intersection quotient is obtained by coefficient-subring descent; the coefficient-ideal statement alone does not construct a determinant. Source: [Scholze, proof of Lemma 5.3.7, pp.1051–1052](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* Mathlib `LaurentPolynomial`, `AddMonoidAlgebra.mapDomainRingHom`, `Ideal.comap`, `Ideal.span`; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 5 (*Nilpotence through a quotient extension*). *Lean:* `laurentSubst`, `substStableIdeal`, `laurentCoeffIdeal`, `laurentCoeffIdeal_pow_eq_bot`.

**Checks.**

- Substitution direction: `laurentSubst 2 (3T^{−1} + T²) = 3T^{−2} + T⁴` over `ℤ`; multiplying coefficients by `2` or reversing exponents would fail.
- At `a = 0`, `T^k` maps to `1` for every `k`, so `3T^{−1} + T²` maps to `4`. At `a = 1` it remains unchanged, and at `a = −1` its exponents are reversed. These are ring substitutions, including the noninjective evaluation at `1`.
- Composition satisfies `laurentSubst a (laurentSubst b f) = laurentSubst (a*b) f`; for `a = 2`, `b = −3` and `f = T`, both sides are `T^{−6}`.
- If `I = 0` then `I′ = 0` and `J = 0`.
- For `R = ℤ/4` and `I = (2) ⊂ R[T^{±1}]`, `I² = 0`; substitution sends `I` into `I`, so `I ⊂ I_a` for every `a`, while `I_1 = I`. Thus `I′ = I`, `J = (2)` and `J² = 0`, with the exponent `2` kept. Individual preimages can be larger: `T − 1 ∈ I_0` but `T − 1 ∉ I`.
- Over `ℤ`, `I = (T − 1)` is substitution-stable and proper by evaluation at `T = 1`, but its coefficient ideal is the unit ideal. Thus `I′ ⊆ J·ℤ[T^{±1}]` can be strict.
- The intersection over all `a` matters: for `R = 𝔽_2[a, b]/(a², b²)` and `I = (a + bT)`, `I² = ((a + bT)²) = 0`, but the coefficients `a, b` of `a + bT` generate an ideal whose square contains `ab ≠ 0`. Indeed `a + bT ∉ I_2`: writing `g = g_0 + ag_a + bg_b + abg_{ab}`, the product `(a + bT)g` has `a`-part `g_0` and `b`-part `Tg_0`, which cannot equal `1` and `T²` simultaneously. So the coefficient ideal must be taken from `I′`, not from `I`.

## Layer 3b: arithmetic Levi realisation

### 3b.1 Boundary Satake polynomials

**Levi and ambient polynomials.** At a place `v ∉ S` of `F` with residue cardinality `q = q_v`, let `e_i = e_{i,v}` be the elementary symmetric functions of the normalised `GL_n` Satake parameters (`e_0 = 1`, `e_n` a unit), and `U_i = U_{i,v}` the standard double cosets, so `U_i = q^{i(n−i)/2}e_i`. Define the Levi polynomial and its dual

```text
P_v(X) = Σ_{i=0}^{n} (−1)^i q^{i(n+1)/2} e_i X^i = Σ_{i=0}^{n} (−1)^i q^{i(i+1)/2} U_i X^i,
P_v^∨(X) = Σ_{i=0}^{n} (−1)^i q^{−i(n+1)/2} (e_{n−i}/e_n) X^i,
```

and the ambient polynomial `P̃_v(X) = (1 − X)·P_v(X)·P_v^∨(X)` if `F` is totally real, `P̃_v(X) = P_v(X)·P_{v^c}^∨(q_vX)` if `F` is CM. The second form of `P_v` lies in `T_v[X]` with no square root of `q`; `P_v^∨` is the normalised reciprocal of `P_v`: if `c = (−1)^n q^{n(n+1)/2} e_n` is its unit top coefficient, then `P_v^∨(X) = c^{−1}·X^n P_v(X^{−1})`. Prove `boundarySatake.ambient_eq`: under the full unnormalised Satake map `T_{S⁺} → T_{K_M,S⁺}` of ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.4.2, the ambient Hecke polynomial `Σ_{i=0}^h (−1)^i q^{iδ/2} T_{i,v} X^i` of Lemma 5.1.6 maps to `P̃_v(X)`, at split and at inert places (with `v^c = v` and `q_v = q_{v⁺}²` in the inert case). The Levi root images are `q^{(n+1)/2}X_i` and their inverses for `Sp_2n`; `q^{n/2}X_i` and `q^{−n/2}Y_i` at a split place of a CM field; `q_v^{n/2}X_i` and inverses at an inert place. The coefficient of `X^{2n}` in the CM ambient polynomial is positive. Prove the standard-to-Levi conversion `P_v(X) = (X^n P^std_v(X^{−1}))|_{X ↦ qX}`, where `P^std_v(Y) = Σ (−1)^i q^{i(i−1)/2} U_i Y^{n−i}` is the monic polynomial of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 3a; this conversion adds the twist `ε^{−1}`, and is separate from the conversion between geometric and arithmetic Frobenius. The full unnormalised hyperspecial Satake transform is used, not an Iwahori or positive-part map; at split CM places both conjugate blocks and their duals are kept.

API: `leviPolynomial` (constant term `1`, coefficients `(−1)^i q^{i(i+1)/2} U_i` in the integral Hecke algebra); `dualPolynomial` (constant term `1`, roots reciprocal to those of `P_v`, with division by the unit `e_n`); `ambient_eq` (the restriction identity above, for `Sp_2n` and for `U(n,n)`); normalised reciprocity (for a polynomial of degree at most `n` with constant term `1` and unit coefficient `c` of `X^n`, the reciprocal is `c^{−1}·Polynomial.reflect n P`; it has constant term `1`, applying reciprocity twice with `c` then `c^{−1}` returns `P`, and it commutes with coefficient change).

- Normalised reciprocal: for `P = 1 − 6X`, `n = 1` and `c = −6` over `ℚ`, the dual is `1 − X/6`; its constant term is `1`, and dualising again returns `P`.

**Character twists on the Levi.** Let `χ : G_{F,S′} → W(𝔽̄_p)^×` be a continuous character of odd order prime to `p`, with `S′ ⊃ S` containing its ramification. In the unconditional CM branch require `S′` to be pulled back from rational places as in Scope and ownership, enlarging it to include every place above a rational prime where `χ` ramifies. Construct `auxiliaryCharacterTwist`. Through ClassFieldTheory Layer 11, in the geometric normalisation of `globalArtinMap`, `χ` gives a finite-order idele class character `ψ` with `ψ_v(ϖ_v) = χ(Frob_v)` for `v ∉ S′`; oddness makes `ψ` trivial at the archimedean places, and unramifiedness makes it trivial on local units outside `S′`. Choose a normal open `K′ ⊂ K` of index prime to `p` with `ψ` trivial on `det(K′)`. The component description of ALS.0.7 gives the unit-valued function `c_ψ([g]) = ψ(det g)^{−1}`; oddness makes this well defined across the archimedean components. Its line is free of rank one, with distinguished generator `c_ψ`, and pointwise multiplication satisfies `c_ψ c_{ψ^{−1}} = 1`. The inverse in this definition makes cup product carry the Hecke action to the inverse-character twist `f_ψ` of ALS.3.12. Put `d_K = [K:K′]` and `r_K = d_K^{−1} tr_{K′/K}`. Then `r_K π^* = id` integrally over `W(𝔽̄_p)` and modulo `p^m` (ALS.3.6). The map `x ↦ π^*x ∪ c_ψ` gives an injection `H^i_!(X_K, ℤ/p^m) ⊗ (W(𝔽̄_p)·c_ψ) → H^i_!(X_{K′}, W(𝔽̄_p)/p^m)` and has retraction `y ↦ r_K(y ∪ c_{ψ^{−1}})`, after identifying the source with its coefficient extension using `c_ψ`. Multiplication by the inverse component function first undoes the twist; the normalized level trace then retracts pullback. Writing this injection as `C_ψ`, the convention is `T(t) C_ψ = C_ψ T(f_ψ(t))`, where `f_ψ(U_{i,v}) = χ(Frob_v)^{−i} U_{i,v}` (ALS.3.9, ALS.3.12). The retraction carries the inverse twist. On the twisted image, the Levi polynomial becomes `P_{v,χ}(X) = P_v(X/χ(Frob_v))` and its dual `P^∨_{v,χ}(X) = P_v^∨(χ(Frob_v)X)`; in the CM term the conjugate place `v^c` is used. Applying §3b.4 to the twisted Hecke image gives a determinant `D̃_χ` over `A/I_χ` with `I_χ^{2(d+1)} = 0` and `D̃_χ(1 − X·Frob_v) = P̃_{v,χ}(X)`, where `P̃_{v,χ}` is formed from `P_{v,χ}` and `P^∨_{v,χ}` as in §3b.1. These are determinants at the twisted level, not formal twists of eigenvalues. The order must be prime to `p`: the splitting uses the prime-to-`p` index and does not average over a `p`-cover. Oddness is needed: an even prime-to-`p` character need not occur in degree-zero cohomology at the stated archimedean quotient.

API: `auxiliaryCharacterTwist.level` (the prime-to-`p` cover realising `ψ` at a common level); `auxiliaryCharacterTwist.function` (the inverse-character component function `c_ψ` and its multiplicative inverse); `auxiliaryCharacterTwist.levelTrace` (the normalized prime-to-`p` level trace retracts pullback); `auxiliaryCharacterTwist.charpoly` (cup product intertwines each tame Hecke action with `P_v(X/χ_v)` and the corresponding dual and conjugate twists); `auxiliaryCharacterTwist.retraction` (inverse-character cup product followed by the normalized level trace is a left inverse of twisted pullback and intertwines the inverse Hecke twist).

**Checks.**

### 3b.6 Residual-kernel factorisation of continuous determinants

**Factorisation through the pro-`p` residual kernel.** Let `G` be a profinite group, `k` a finite field of characteristic `p`, `ρ̄ : G → GL_d(k)` a continuous semisimple representation, `A` a commutative profinite local `W(k)`-algebra with residue field `k`, and `D : A[G] → A` a continuous `d`-dimensional determinant lifting `det ρ̄`. Let `H ⊂ ker ρ̄` be the smallest closed normal subgroup of `G` contained in `ker ρ̄` with `ker ρ̄/H` pro-`p`. Prove `Determinant.factors_through_proP_kernel`: `D` factors through `A[G] → A[G/H]`, i.e. `D(X − gh) = D(X − g)` for all `g ∈ G` and `h ∈ H`. Reduce to `A` finite and, by continuity, to `D` factoring through a finite quotient `G′` of `G`; in the finite ring `B = A[G′]/ker D`, the Jacobson radical is the kernel of the residual map to `M_d(k)` induced by `ρ̄` (IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 1), so `ker ρ̄` maps into the finite `p`-group `1 + rad(B)`, and its image factors through the maximal pro-`p` quotient (`TauCeti.maximalProPQuotient.lift`). State continuity, compatibility with coefficient change, and the application factorwise to a finite product of local rings. The field-only statement for the kernel of a residual representation and the group-only universal property of the maximal pro-`p` quotient do not by themselves give this factorisation of a determinant. Source: [Chenevier, Lemma 3.8 and proof, §3.1, p.43](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0415v2). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 (continuity and the open-kernel criterion) and Layer 1 (Cayley–Hamilton quotients, semisimple reconstruction); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.maximalProPQuotient.lift`.

**Checks.**

- If `ρ̄` is trivial of dimension `1` and `G = ℤ_ℓ` with `ℓ ≠ p`, then `ker ρ̄ = G` and `H = G` (as `ℤ_ℓ` has no nontrivial pro-`p` quotient), so every continuous `D` lifting the trivial character is trivial on `G`: a character `ℤ_ℓ → (W(k)/p^r)^×` lifting `1` takes values in `1 + pW(k)/p^r`, a `p`-group, hence is trivial.
- If `G = ℤ_p` and `ρ̄` is trivial, `H = 1` and the statement is empty: the character `1 ↦ 1 + p` of `ℤ_p` into `ℤ_p^×` does not factor through any proper quotient.
- The factorisation is through `G/H`, not through `G/ker ρ̄`: for `G = ℤ/ℓ × ℤ_p` with `ℓ ≠ p` and `ρ̄` trivial of dimension one, `ker ρ̄ = G` and `H = ℤ/ℓ`. A continuous character of `G` with values in `(W(k)/p^r)^×` lifting `1` lands in the `p`-group `1 + pW(k)/p^r`, so it is trivial on `ℤ/ℓ`, but it can be nontrivial on `ℤ_p`.

### 3b.7 Cyclotomic factor extension

**Finite-coefficient descent for the character pair.** For fixed `χ` in §3b.5, set `B = (A_0 ⊗_{ℤ_p} W(𝔽̄_p))/Ĩ_χ`, with the discrete topology and `Ĩ_χ = I_1 + I_χ`. Prove that the continuous determinants `D̃_1`, `D̃_χ` over `B` descend as polynomial laws to a common finite subring `B_χ ⊆ B`. Include in `B_χ` the image of `A_0` and the finitely many values of `χ` modulo `p^m`. By continuity each determinant factors through a finite quotient of `G_{F,S_ℓ}` (Chenevier Lemma 2.33); coefficient reconstruction on that quotient gives a finite coefficient ring (Lemma 3.10, discrete case). The subring generated by the two finite coefficient rings and the additional data is finite: it is a quotient of their tensor product over `ℤ/p^m`. Enlarge `B_χ` inside `B` by finitely many Witt scalars as needed to realize the semisimple residual representations over finite fields, decompose this finite ring into local factors, apply §3b.6 there, and extend scalars to `B`. This does not change the ideal `Ĩ_χ` or its exponent. Neither `B` nor its local factors are assumed profinite.

Source: [Chenevier, Lemmas 2.33 and 3.10, pp.38, 44–45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0415v2). *Needs:* §3b.6; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 (coefficient-subring descent and coefficient reconstruction) and Layer 1 (semisimple reconstruction over finite extensions of finite fields).

**Checks.**

- With `A_0 = 𝔽_p` and `Ĩ_χ = 0`, `B = 𝔽̄_p` is infinite and discrete, whereas the coefficient ring of a fixed pair is finite. The local-ring hypothesis is used on that finite ring.
- A fixed character of order prime to `p` has finitely many values in a finite unramified coefficient extension modulo `p^m`; no common finite ring for every `χ` is asserted.
- For `B_χ = 𝔽_p[ε]/(ε²)`, descent retains `ε` and its square-zero ideal; passing only to the residue field would lose the determinant's infinitesimal coefficients.

**Continuous Levi coefficients.** Prove `cyclotomicFactorExtension`: in the branch of §3b.2, there are an ideal `I_0 ⊂ A_0` with `I_0^{4(d+1)} = 0` and a unique continuous function `g ↦ P_g` from `G_{F,S}` to `(A_0/I_0)[X]` with `P_{Frob_v} = P_v` for all `v ∉ S`. 3. *Pro-`p` factorisation.* Descend the fixed pair `D̃_1`, `D̃_χ` to `B_χ` by the preceding finite-coefficient descent target. 4. *Separation.* For `g ∈ Gal(L^∞_ℓ/F) ∖ Gal(L^∞_ℓ/F^{cycl}_ℓ)`, choose `χ` with `Ĩ_χ ∩ A_0 = I_0` and `χ(g)^{2j} ≠ 1` for `1 ≤ j ≤ n`; only finitely many `χ` violate the second condition. Both factors have degree at most `n`, constant coefficient `1` and unit top coefficient (degree `n` over a nonzero coefficient ring). The coefficient induction of §3a.2 therefore makes the coefficients of `P_v` constant modulo `Ĩ_χ ∩ A_0 = I_0`.
5. *Two primes.* Doing this for `ℓ` and `ℓ′` and using that `F^{cycl}_{ℓ′}` is linearly disjoint from `L^∞_ℓ` covers the omitted kernels, glues the two continuous functions by uniqueness, and descends them to `G_{F,S}`, removing the ramification at `ℓ` and `ℓ′`.

The coefficients are independent of the auxiliary choices. ### 4.1 The integral boundary filtration

**Compact-support product comparison for Levi manifolds.** Construct `compactSupportProductComparison`: for neat, Hausdorff, second-countable finite-dimensional Levi manifolds `X` and `Y` which are interiors of the compact manifold-with-corners models of ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces, and `R = O/ϖ^m`, `m ≥ 1`, construct the natural product quasi-isomorphism


### 4.2 Integral boundary induction

### 4.3 The finite torsion determinant theorem

**Determinants on finite Hecke images.** Prove `finiteTorsionDeterminant`, Scholze's Theorem 5.4.1 in the unconditional CM branch of §3b.2 or under `(EC)_{≤n}` in the general form: there is `N = N([F:ℚ], n)` such that for every `K = K_SK^S`, every `ξ` and all `i ≥ 0`, `m ≥ 1`, the ring `A = T_{F,S}(K,ξ,i,m)` has an ideal `I` with `I^N = 0` and a continuous `n`-dimensional determinant `D` of `G_{F,S}` with values in `A/I` and `D(1 − X·Frob_v) = P_v(X)` for every `v ∉ S`. The proof is by induction on `n`. First reduce to a neat `K` and trivial `ξ` with the finite-cover Hochschild–Serre sequence for a neat normal `K′ ⊂ K` on which `M_ξ/p^m` is trivial (ALS.6.2; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§6.1–6.4 for the untwisting). For neat `K` put `H^i_! = im(H^i_c → H^i)` and `B_i = im(H^i → H^i_∂)`, so `0 → H^i_! → H^i → B_i → 0`, and let `A_!` and `A_B` be the faithful Hecke images on `H^i_!` and `B_i`; `A_B` is a quotient of the full boundary image, so `A → A_! × A_B` is defined. An element of its kernel kills `H^i_!` and maps `H^i` into `H^i_!`, so the kernel has square zero. §3b.9 gives `I_!` on `A_!`, §4.2 gives `I_∂` on `A_B` (after pushing the boundary determinant to its quotient `A_B`), and the kernel of `A → A_!/I_! × A_B/I_∂` has exponent at most `2·max(4(d+1), N_∂)` (IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 5, *Quantified composition of Hecke error ideals*); coefficient-subring descent on the joint quotient (*Determinant from a quantified Hecke comparison*) gives `D`. Uniformity is in `K`, `ξ`, `i` and `m` with `[F:ℚ]` and `n` fixed; the error ideals at different exponents are independent. Annihilation of `H^i` is not annihilation of all of `H^i_∂`: the boundary factor is the image `B_i`. The theorem is an assembly for this family of Hecke images; it states nothing for other reductive groups and gives no explicit `N` beyond the bounds of the proof. Source: [Scholze, Theorem 5.4.1 and proof, pp.1057–1059; Remark 5.4.6, p.1061](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf). *Needs:* §§3b.9, 4.2; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.0.9, ALS.4.1, ALS.6.2; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§6.1–6.4; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 5.

### 4.4 Integral and inverse-system transport

### 4.8 The arithmetic nonlifting check

**A torsion class with no integral lift.** Take `F = ℚ`, `n = 1`, `p = 2`, `K = ℤ̂^×` and the trivial coefficient lattice. With the stack convention, `X_K` is `[pt/{±1}]`: the symmetric space `ℝ^×/ℝ_{>0}K_∞`, with `K_∞ = O(1) = {±1}` the maximal compact subgroup, is a point, and `ℚ^×∖[(ℝ^×/ℝ_{>0}K_∞) × 𝔸_f^×/ℤ̂^×]` is a single point with stabiliser `ℚ^× ∩ ℤ̂^× = {±1}` (ALS.0.8). The maximal compact subgroup is part of the statement: on `(ℝ^×/ℝ_{>0}) × 𝔸_f^×/ℤ̂^×`, whose first factor has two points, `−1 ∈ ℚ^×` exchanges the two points and the stabiliser is trivial, so the class would disappear. Prove `nonlifting_example`: `H^1(X_K, ℤ_2) = H^1({±1}, ℤ_2) = Hom({±1}, ℤ_2) = 0`, while `H^1(X_K, 𝔽_2) = Hom({±1}, 𝔽_2) = 𝔽_2`, and the Bockstein maps `H^1(X_K, 𝔽_2)` isomorphically onto `H^2({±1}, ℤ_2)[2] = 𝔽_2`; so this torsion class has no integral `H^1` lift. At every odd prime `ℓ` the rank-one Hecke operator acts as the identity, and the extracted mod-`2` determinant is the trivial character, since `P_ℓ = 1 − ℓX ≡ 1 − X` modulo `2`. The universal coefficient diagram commutes, and the simultaneous finite-coefficient kernel lies in the integral kernel, as §4.4 requires. As a separate algebraic diagnostic, the two-term integral complex `ℤ →(2) ℤ` has a degree-zero class `1` modulo `2` with no integral degree-zero lift. Replacing the stack by its coarse quotient (a point) would lose the class: the stack convention is part of the statement. Source: the stack convention of [Scholze, §5.4, p.1057](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v182-n3-p03-p.pdf), the coefficient sequence in the proof of Corollary 5.4.2 (p.1060) and Remark 5.4.5 (p.1061); the computation of the group cohomology of `{±1}` is elementary and is not stated in the source. *Needs:* §§4.4, 4.7; ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpaces ALS.0.8, ALS.1.5; CompletedCohomologyPartII §§2.3–2.9; Mathlib `groupCohomology`, `groupCohomology.H1IsoOfIsTrivial`.

**Checks.**

- At the stated level and `p = 2`, the homomorphism `{±1} → 𝔽_2` sending `−1` to `1` represents the nonzero mod-`2` class. Any homomorphism `{±1} → ℤ_2` is zero since `ℤ_2` has no element of additive order `2`.
- For an odd coefficient prime `p`, the order of `{±1}` is invertible in `ℤ_p` and `𝔽_p`, so both first cohomology groups vanish; this source of nonlifting occurs at `p = 2`.
- The coarse quotient is a point with zero first cohomology, and the quotient using `K_∞^∘` has trivial stabiliser in this rank-one example. Neither carries the displayed torsion class.

## Corrections to the published sources

The targets above use the following corrected forms. Each item names the place in the source, says what is changed, and says why. Page numbers are those of the published Annals paper unless another source is named.

- **Lemma 2.1.1, proof, p.954.** The proof obtains a function `f` with `U_{J′} = {|f| = 1}` inside `U_J` from hypothesis (i) alone. One such `f` is the product, for a fixed `i ∈ J`, of the ratios `s_j^{(i)}/s_i^{(i)}` over `j ∈ J′ ∖ J`. That the ratios are bounded is hypothesis (i), and that their absolute-value-one locus is `U_{ij}` is hypothesis (ii); both are used. The later gluing of the reduced sections uses hypothesis (iii), as printed. The lemma has three numbered hypotheses.
- **Remark 2.2.4 and Definition 2.2.6, pp.956–957.** The remark denies surjectivity of `R → S` for the universal closed perfectoid quotient, and the definition of a strongly Zariski closed subset asks only that `R⁺ → S⁺` be almost surjective. In the corrected form, used through PerfectoidSpaces §§P4.14–P4.15, `R → R_I` is surjective for every Zariski closed subset ([Bhatt–Scholze, *Prisms and prismatic cohomology*, Theorem 7.4 and Remark 7.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.08229)) and the plus ring of the quotient is the integral closure of the image of `R⁺` ([Scholze, *Étale cohomology of diamonds*, Definition 5.7(ii) and Theorem 5.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07343)).
- **The chart-orbit assertion, p.1008; Theorem 3.3.18(i), pp.1012–1014; Theorem 4.1.1(i), pp.1019–1020; their use on p.1030.** Only the `2^g` Lagrangian coordinate charts, choosing one of `e_i, e_{g+i}` for each `i`, are one `GSp_2g(ℤ_p)`-orbit, and they cover the flag variety. For `g = 2` the chart `p_{13} ≠ 0` is not empty, and it is not in that orbit: writing a basis of its Lagrangians as `(1, a, 0, b), (0, c, 1, d)`, isotropy is `bc − ad = 1`, so its reduction has `p³ − p` points over `𝔽_p`, while an admissible graph cell has `p³` (`24` against `27` at `p = 3`, `120` against `125` at `p = 5`); an integral symplectic coordinate change would preserve the count.
- **Theorem 3.3.18(vi), proof, last paragraph, p.1015.** The set `|h| ≤ |p|^{C+1}` does not meet `U` minus the boundary, where `|h| ≥ |p|^C`; it is therefore an open subset contained in the boundary, hence empty. The printed text says it does not meet the boundary.
- **Theorem 4.1.1, proof, first displayed limit, p.1020.** The limit runs over levels `K^{p′} ⊂ G′(𝔸_f^p)` of the Siegel group; the spaces in the display are Siegel spaces.
- **Theorem 4.1.1(i) and the end of its proof, pp.1018–1020.** The identification of the plus ring of the perfectoid tower with the `p`-adic completion of the colimit of the finite-level plus rings, and the descent of the charts `V_J` to finite level, need an argument; it is supplied with the tower by PerfectoidShimuraVarieties Layer 2 and is used in the injectivity step of Layer 2a.
- **Theorem 4.3.1, proof, p.1030.** In both places the preimages `Ṽ_{JK_p}` of the charts in the genuine minimal compactification are meant: the formal model and its boundary ideal are built on the genuine compactification.
- **Corollary 4.2.3 and its proof, p.1024.** For the group here, `l_0 = rank G(ℝ) − rank K_∞ = 0` and `q_0 = (dim_ℝ X − l_0)/2 = d`; the printed values are exchanged. In the proof, choose the violating module of minimal codimension, so that terms of smaller codimension cannot interfere in the spectral sequence.
- **Theorem 5.1.2, p.1033.** The segment attached to `Π_i` runs from `(1 − ℓ_i)/2` to `(ℓ_i − 1)/2` in unit steps, so the last exponent is `(ℓ_i − 1)/2`; `Π_i` is a representation of `GL_{n_i}`.
- **Remark 5.1.5, p.1034.** From `(Π^∨)^c ≅ Π` one gets `(Π′^∨)^c ≅ Π′ ⊗ |·|^{n−1−k}` for `Π′ = Π|·|^{(k+1−n)/2}`, so `χ = |·|^{n−1−k}`.
- **Corollary 5.1.11, p.1038.** With all weights `ak`, `k ≥ 1`, the first weight must already lie in the range of Proposition 5.1.1, so the step is `a > n` for `Sp_2n` and `a ≥ n` for `U(n,n)`.
- **Lemma 5.2.2, p.1038.** The projection is `X^P_{K_P} → X^M_{K^M_P}`; the spaces are indexed by compact open subgroups.
- **The paragraph before Lemma 5.2.3, p.1041.** The embedding is `X^M_{K^M} → X^P_{K^P}`, a section of the projection onto the Levi space.
- **Lemma 5.2.5(ii)–(iii), pp.1042–1043.** The coefficient of `X^{2n}` in the unitary ambient polynomial is `+q_v^n T_{2n,v}`: the two degree-`n` top coefficients have product sign `(−1)^{2n} = 1`. The ambient polynomial is `P_v(X)·P^∨_{v^c}(q_vX)`.
- **Corollary 5.2.6, proof, p.1044.** For one auxiliary prime `ℓ` the argument gives an ideal `J_ℓ` with `J_ℓ^{d+1} = 0` only in the Hecke image away from `S⁺ ∪ {ℓ}`, and a determinant unramified outside `S ∪ {ℓ}`, because the Hochschild–Serre sequence of the level-`ℓ` tower is equivariant only for Hecke operators away from `ℓ`. Since the ambient Hecke image is finite, infinitely many `ℓ` give the same extended ideal; for two of them the determinants agree at all Frobenius elements outside `S ∪ {ℓ_1, ℓ_2}`, hence coincide, are unramified at `ℓ_1` and `ℓ_2`, and give the statement for `S` with exponent `d + 1`. Gluing two primes without this stabilisation gives only exponent `2d + 1`. The same proof in [arXiv:1306.2070v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.2070v2), Corollary V.2.6, p.90, has the same text.
- **Lemma 5.3.6, setup and proof, pp.1049–1051.** The auxiliary prime must satisfy `I_{0,ℓ} = I_0` as well as `ℓ ∤ [F̃:F]`; otherwise the character family has no common coefficient quotient. Twisted and untwisted determinants are compared modulo `Ĩ_χ = I_1 + I_χ`, and only characters with `Ĩ_χ ∩ A_0 = I_0` are used.
- **Lemma 5.3.10, proof, pp.1053–1054.** Let `r` be the first order at which some factor differs from `1` and compare modulo `X^{r+1}`; with the printed indexing the induction does not start at order one. For polynomiality the printed text writes `Q′ ∈ S[[X, XT]] ⊂ S[T][[X]]`, expands `Q′ = Σ_a Q′_a(T)X^a` and calls `Q_0` an element of `S[T]`; the roles of `X` and `T` are exchanged. Since every remaining weight is positive, `Q′` has only monomials `X^aT^e` with `a ≤ e`. It is expanded as `Σ_a Q′_a(X)T^a` with `Q′_a ∈ S[X]`. Then `Q′_aQ_0` is the `T^a`-coefficient of a polynomial and vanishes for large `a`, and `Q_0 ∈ S[X]`, with constant coefficient `1`, cancels. The shift that makes the weights nonnegative is `X ↦ XT^{−m_0}` for the least weight `m_0`.
- **Proof of Lemma 5.3.8, the display before Lemma 5.3.12, p.1055.** The inverse of `(1 − Ux)(1 − Uy)` is `(1 − Uy)^{−1}(1 − Ux)^{−1}`; with this order the kernel element specialises to `1` at `U = 1`, as Lemma 5.3.12 requires, while the printed order does not when `x` and `y` do not commute.
- **Proof of Corollary 5.4.2, p.1060.** The containment is the reverse of the printed one: the kernel of `T_{F,S} → ∏_m T_{F,S}(K,ξ,i,m)` is contained in the kernel of `T_{F,S} → T_{F,S}(K,ξ,i)`. The universal coefficient sequence injects `H/p^m` into `H^i(X_K, M_ξ/p^m)`; its `Tor` term in the next degree prevents the converse.
- **Corollary 5.4.4, p.1060.** The representation is `σ_𝔪`, and the right-hand side of its characteristic identity lies in the local Hecke quotient, not in the residue field.
- **§5.4, the setup paragraph, p.1057.** `K_S ⊂ ∏_{v∈S} GL_n(O_{F_v})` is any compact open subgroup and `K^S = ∏_{v∉S} GL_n(O_{F_v})`; the printed sentence repeats a phrase.
- **Remark 5.4.5 and the proof printed after it, p.1061.** The simultaneous rings are closed images in products over `m` (or over `(i, m)`), not in direct sums: the identity has infinitely many nonzero components. The proof printed after the remark is the proof of Corollary 5.4.4 (it uses the irreducibility of `σ_ψ` and produces `σ_𝔪` over the localised ring); the remark says only that the same proof gives the stronger statements. The arXiv version, Remark V.4.5, p.104, has the same layout.
- **Caraiani–Le Hung, author PDF, §3, Remark 3.1, p.8.** The cohomology local systems of the nilmanifold fibres are computed from the actual integral cochain complex with its Levi and Hecke action; their identification with `∧^b` of the dual of `Lie U` needs further hypotheses and is not a consequence of neatness. For `GL_3/ℚ`, the full upper unipotent `U`, principal level `3` and `p = 5`, the lattice has coordinates `a, b, c ∈ 3ℤ` with commutators in the central `9ℤ`; its abelianisation is `ℤ² ⊕ ℤ/3`, so `H^1(U-lattice, 𝔽_5)` has dimension `2`, while `∧^1(Lie U)^∨` has dimension `3`. This correction concerns the cited author version.
