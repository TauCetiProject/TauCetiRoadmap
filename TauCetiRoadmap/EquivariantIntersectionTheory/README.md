# Roadmap: equivariant intersection theory and Picard groups of quotient stacks

**Summary.** Build the Chow groups of quotient stacks `[X/G]` in the sense of Edidin and Graham,
and with them the Picard groups of quotient stacks, starting from graded rings. Layer 0 is
commutative algebra in Mathlib's vocabulary: a `𝔾_m`-action on `Spec A` is a `ℤ`-grading of `A`,
and the equivariant line bundles with trivial underlying bundle are classified by the degrees of
the homogeneous units. Layer 1 makes `G`-linearised line bundles and `Pic [X/G]` precise on the
quotient stacks of SchemeAndStackFoundations. Layer 2 constructs equivariant Chow groups by
Totaro's approximation, their operations and the localisation sequence, and proves
`Pic [X/G] ≅ A¹_G(X)` for `X` smooth. Layer 3 computes the classical examples: weighted affine
spaces minus an invariant divisor, `M_{1,1}`, the stacks of smooth binary forms of Arsie–Vistoli,
the hyperelliptic Weierstrass stacks of Edidin–Hu, and `[M_{0,n}/𝔖ₙ₋₁]` for every `n`.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns: the degree group of homogeneous units of a `ℤ`-graded ring and the grading of
a localisation at a homogeneous element; `G`-linearised line bundles on a scheme with a group
scheme action and the equivariant Picard group; equivariant Chow groups of schemes with an
action of a linear algebraic group, their operations, and the comparison with the Picard group of
the quotient stack; and the computations of Layer 3.

It does not own, and consumes:

- *Algebraic stacks and quotient stacks* `actionQuotient G X = [X/G]`, with
  `actionQuotient.torsorEquiv`: [SchemeAndStackFoundations](../SchemeAndStackFoundations/README.md)
  §1.21; quasi-coherent modules on stacks: §1.22; torsors and the multiplicative group: §1.15.
- *Chow groups of schemes*: SchemeAndStackFoundations §5.1 (`ChowGroup κ X k`), §5.2 (proper
  pushforward, flat pullback, localisation sequence), §5.3 (first Chern class), §5.4 (projective
  bundles, Chern classes, homotopy invariance for vector bundles), §5.6 (intersection products on
  smooth varieties); Picard groups of schemes: §3.4.
- *Group schemes over a base and their representations*: the group-scheme layer that
  SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.15 cites for `𝔾_m`, `GL_n` and `μ_n`.
- *Moduli stacks of curves* `M_{g,n}` and `M_{1,1}`: SchemeAndStackFoundations (the moduli stack of
  stable pointed curves). Layer 3 uses their presentations as quotient stacks, which it proves.

Out of scope: equivariant cohomology of topological spaces, equivariant K-theory, and Chow rings
of stacks that are not global quotients (Kresch's Chow groups of algebraic stacks).

## Conventions

1. `k` is a field. Schemes in Layers 1–3 are separated and of finite type over `k`; groups are
   linear algebraic groups over `k`, that is smooth affine group schemes of finite type.
2. A `𝔾_m`-action on `Spec A` is the same as a `ℤ`-grading `A = ⊕_d A_d`, where `t ∈ 𝔾_m` acts on
   `A_d` by `t^d`. Weights are integers; a weighted polynomial ring `k[x_i]` with weights `w_i` is
   Mathlib's `MvPolynomial` with `weightedHomogeneousSubmodule k w`.
3. `A(d)` denotes `A` with the grading shifted by `d`: the equivariant line bundle on `Spec A` on
   which a homogeneous element of degree `e` of the fibre has weight `e + d`.
4. Chow groups are graded by dimension, `A^G_i(X)`, and for `X` smooth of pure dimension `n` by
   codimension, `A^i_G(X) = A^G_{n−i}(X)`. The equivariant Chow group of a point is written
   `A^*_G = A^*_G(pt)`.

## Layer 0: graded rings and homogeneous units

### 0.1 Degrees of homogeneous units

For a `ℤ`-graded `R`-algebra `(A, 𝒜)` (Mathlib's `GradedAlgebra 𝒜`, `𝒜 : ℤ → Submodule R A`),
define `homogeneousUnitDegrees 𝒜 : AddSubgroup ℤ`, generated by the degrees of the units of `A`
lying in some `𝒜 d`, and `TrivialUnderlyingPic 𝒜 = ℤ ⧸ homogeneousUnitDegrees 𝒜`. Prove
`mem_homogeneousUnitDegrees_iff`: the inverse of a homogeneous unit is homogeneous of the opposite
degree, so a degree is in the group iff it is the degree of a homogeneous unit. Prove that a graded
`A`-module free of rank one is `A(d)` for some `d`, and that `A(d) ≅ A(e)` as graded modules iff
`d − e ∈ homogeneousUnitDegrees 𝒜`; hence `TrivialUnderlyingPic 𝒜` classifies graded free
modules of rank one up to graded isomorphism, with the group law of the tensor product.

**Checks.** For the trivial grading, the group is `⊤`. For the Laurent ring `k[t, t⁻¹]` with `t` of
degree `1`, it is `⊤`. For `k[t, t⁻¹]` with `t` of degree `2`, it is `2ℤ`.

### 0.2 Localisation at a homogeneous element

For `f ∈ 𝒜 e`, define `awayGrading 𝒜 hf : ℤ → Submodule R (Localization.Away f)`, with
`a / fⁿ` of degree `deg a − n e`, and prove `awayGrading_isInternal`, so that it is a grading.
Give the `GradedAlgebra` instance, the compatibility of `algebraMap A A_f` with the gradings, and
the identification of the degree-zero part with Mathlib's `HomogeneousLocalization.Away 𝒜 f` when
`𝒜` is `ℕ`-graded.

### 0.3 Weighted polynomial rings

Let `k` be a field and `w : σ → ℤ` positive weights on a finite type `σ`. Prove
`homogeneousUnitDegrees_weighted`: the units of `k[σ]` are the nonzero constants, so the group is
`⊥`. For `f` weighted homogeneous of weight `e > 0` and prime, prove
`homogeneousUnitDegrees_away_prime`: the units of `k[σ]_f` are the `c fᵐ`, `c ∈ kˣ`, `m ∈ ℤ`, so the
group is `eℤ` and `TrivialUnderlyingPic ≅ ℤ/e`. For `f` a product of distinct primes `f_j` of
weights `e_j`, the group is generated by the `e_j`.

### 0.4 The generic discriminant

Define `genericDepressed k m = Xᵐ + a₂ Xᵐ⁻² + ⋯ + aₘ` over `k[a₂, …, aₘ]`, the weights
`depressedWeight m` (`aⱼ` of weight `j`), and `depressedDiscr k m`, its discriminant in Mathlib's
`Polynomial.discr`. Prove `depressedDiscr_mem` (weight `m (m − 1)`), `depressedDiscr_prime` (prime
in characteristic `0`, for `m ≥ 2`), and `homogeneousUnitDegrees_depressed`: the group is
`m (m − 1) ℤ`.

**Checks.** `m = 2`: the discriminant is `−4 a₂`, of weight `2`. `m = 3`: it is
`−4 a₂³ − 27 a₃²`, of weight `6`, and the group is `6ℤ`.

## Layer 1: linearised line bundles and the Picard group of a quotient stack

### 1.1 Linearisations

For a group scheme `G` acting on a scheme `X` by `a : G × X → X`, define a `G`-linearisation of a
line bundle `L` on `X` as an isomorphism `φ : a^* L ≅ pr₂^* L` satisfying the cocycle condition on
`G × G × X`, and `Pic^G(X)`, the group of isomorphism classes of linearised line bundles under
tensor product. Prove that for `G = 𝔾_m` and `X = Spec A` a linearisation of the trivial bundle is
the same as a graded free rank-one module, so the kernel of `Pic^{𝔾_m}(Spec A) → Pic(A)` is
`TrivialUnderlyingPic 𝒜` (Layer 0.1).

### 1.2 The exact sequence

Prove the exact sequence `0 → H¹(G, k[X]^*) → Pic^G(X) → Pic(X)^G` for `G` finite, the first map
sending a cocycle of `G` in the units to the trivial bundle with that linearisation; and, for `G`
connected and `X` a normal variety with `k[X]^* = k^*`, the exact sequence
`0 → X^*(G) → Pic^G(X) → Pic(X)`, a character `χ` giving the trivial bundle twisted by `χ`
(Knop–Kraft–Luna–Vust).

### 1.3 Picard groups of quotient stacks

Prove `Pic [X/G] ≅ Pic^G(X)`, using `actionQuotient.torsorEquiv` and descent of line bundles along
the `G`-torsor `X → [X/G]` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.15, 1.21, 1.22). Prove the change of
group: for `H ⊆ G` closed and `X = G ×^H Y`, `Pic^G(X) ≅ Pic^H(Y)`, so that
`[G ×^H Y / G] ≅ [Y/H]`. Prove the slice reduction: for `G = U ⋊ T` with `U` unipotent acting
freely with a section `Y ⊆ X` stable under `T` and meeting each `U`-orbit once, `[X/G] ≅ [Y/T]`.

## Layer 2: equivariant Chow groups

### 2.1 Totaro's approximation

For `G` linear of dimension `g`, a representation `V` and an open `U ⊆ V` on which `G` acts freely
with complement of codimension `> n − i`, define `A^G_i(X) = A_{i + l − g}(X ×^G U)`
(`l = dim V`), and prove that it does not depend on `(V, U)` (Edidin–Graham). The mixed spaces `X ×^G U` are algebraic spaces in general and schemes under the
quasi-projectivity hypotheses of Edidin–Graham; state the definition for those.

### 2.2 Operations

Proper pushforward, flat pullback, the localisation sequence
`A^G_i(Z) → A^G_i(X) → A^G_i(X ∖ Z) → 0` for an invariant closed `Z`, equivariant Chern classes of
equivariant vector bundles, intersection products for `X` smooth, and homotopy invariance for
equivariant vector bundles, all deduced from SchemeAndStackFoundations §§5.2–5.6 on mixed spaces
(Edidin–Graham).

### 2.3 Classifying spaces

Prove `A^*_{𝔾_m} ≅ ℤ[t]`, `t = c₁` of the standard character, `A^*_{𝔾_m^r} ≅ ℤ[t₁, …, t_r]`,
`A^*_{GL_n} ≅ ℤ[c₁, …, c_n]`, and `A^*_{μ_n} ≅ ℤ[t]/(n t)` (Edidin–Graham; Totaro).

### 2.4 The Picard group

For `X` smooth, prove `Pic^G(X) ≅ A¹_G(X)`, compatible with `c₁` (Edidin–Graham).
With Layer 1.3, `Pic [X/G] ≅ A¹_G(X)`.

**Checks.** For `𝔾_m` acting on `𝔸ⁿ ∖ 0` with all weights `1`, `A^*_{𝔾_m}(𝔸ⁿ ∖ 0) ≅ ℤ[t]/(tⁿ)`, the
Chow ring of `ℙⁿ⁻¹`, so `Pic = ℤ` for `n ≥ 2`; for `μ_n ⊆ 𝔾_m`, `A¹_{μ_n} = ℤ/n`.

## Layer 3: computations

### 3.1 Weighted affine space minus an invariant divisor

For `𝔾_m` acting on `𝔸ⁿ` with positive weights and `D = {f = 0}` an integral invariant divisor of
weight `e`, prove `Pic [(𝔸ⁿ ∖ D)/𝔾_m] ≅ ℤ/e`, generated by the standard character, by the
localisation sequence of 2.2 and `A^1_{𝔾_m}(𝔸ⁿ) = ℤ t`; check it against Layer 0.3 through 1.1.

### 3.2 `M_{1,1}`

Prove `M_{1,1} ≅ [(𝔸² ∖ {Δ = 0})/𝔾_m]` with weights `4, 6` (`y² = x³ + a x + b`,
`Δ = −16(4a³ + 27b²)`) over `ℤ[1/6]`, and `Pic M_{1,1} ≅ ℤ/12`, generated by the Hodge bundle
(Mumford 1965; Fulton–Olsson 2010, who prove it over any reduced base).

### 3.3 Binary forms and hyperelliptic curves

Prove the presentation of the stack of smooth divisors of degree `n` in `ℙ¹`, and of the stack of
hyperelliptic curves of genus `g`, as quotients of open subsets of spaces of binary forms by
`GL₂` or `GL₂/μ_{g+1}`, and the Picard groups computed by Arsie–Vistoli. Prove Edidin–Hu,
Theorem 1.3: `A^*([M_{0,2g+2}/𝔖_{2g+1}]) ≅ ℤ[l]/(2g(2g+1) l)` for `g ≥ 2` over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic not dividing `2g + 2`.

### 3.4 `[M_{0,n}/𝔖ₙ₋₁]` for every `n`

Over a field of characteristic `0`, prove `[M_{0,n}/𝔖ₙ₋₁] ≅ [U/Aff]`, with `U` the monic squarefree
polynomials of degree `m = n − 1` and `Aff = 𝔾_a ⋊ 𝔾_m` the stabiliser of `∞` in `PGL₂`, by fixing
the `n`-th point at `∞`; then `[U/Aff] ≅ [U₀/𝔾_m]` by the slice reduction of 1.3 (roots of sum
zero, coefficients of weights `2, …, m`). With 3.1 and Layer 0.4, prove
`Pic [M_{0,n}/𝔖ₙ₋₁] ≅ ℤ/(n − 1)(n − 2)`, generated by the tangent line bundle at the `n`-th point,
whose weight is `∓1`; and that the tensor product of the tangent lines at all `n` points has order
`n − 1`. For `n = 2g + 2` this recovers the Picard group of 3.3.

**Checks.** `n = 4`: `ℤ/6`, compatible with `Pic M_{1,1} = ℤ/12` through the `μ₂`-gerbe
`M_{1,1} → [M_{0,4}/𝔖₃]`. `n = 5`: `ℤ/12`. `n = 6`: `ℤ/20`.
