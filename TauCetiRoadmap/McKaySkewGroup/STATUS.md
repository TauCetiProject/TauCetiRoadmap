### Named results

- **The binary tetrahedral group as the Hurwitz units** — the 24-element [binary tetrahedral subgroup](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryTetrahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryTetrahedralSubgroup) of SU(2) is [exactly the set of unit quaternions with half-integer coordinates](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryTetrahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.mem_binaryTetrahedralSubgroup_iff_exists_int), and is [generated by i, j and ω = (−1 + i + j + k)/2](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryTetrahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryTetrahedralSubgroup_eq_closure).
- **The binary octahedral group of order 48** — the [binary octahedral subgroup](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryOctahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryOctahedralSubgroup) consists of the unit quaternions whose coordinates lie all in ℤ/2 or all in ℤ/√2. It has [48 elements](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryOctahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.natCard_binaryOctahedralSubgroup) and [contains the tetrahedral group with index two](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryOctahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.relIndex_binaryTetrahedralSubgroup_binaryOctahedralSubgroup).
- **The binary dihedral group of order 4n** — r = diag(exp(πi/n), exp(−πi/n)) and s = !![0, 1; −1, 0] generate the [binary dihedral subgroup](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryDihedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryDihedralSubgroup), with normal forms r^j and s·r^j, [exactly 4n elements](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryDihedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.natCard_binaryDihedralSubgroup), and an [isomorphism with Mathlib's `QuaternionGroup n`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryDihedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryDihedralEquiv).
- **The cyclic model and finite subgroups of the torus** — the [cyclic subgroup](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/Cyclic.html#TauCeti.SU2.cyclicSubgroup) generated by diag(exp(2πi/n), exp(−2πi/n)) is [isomorphic to `Multiplicative (ZMod n)`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/Cyclic.html#TauCeti.SU2.cyclicEquiv), and [every finite subgroup of the diagonal torus is the cyclic model of its order](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/Cyclic.html#Subgroup.eq_SU2_cyclicSubgroup_of_le_torus). This is the uniqueness step the odd-order case of the classification relies on.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Unit quaternions as SU(2)** — the quaternion–SU(2) equivalence `Quaternion.unitaryEquivSpecialUnitaryGroup` is already in the library, and every model is now tied to quaternion coordinates. The binary dihedral generators of orders eight and sixteen are matched with quaternions inside the tetrahedral and octahedral groups, so later computations can use either the coordinate generators or the presentation generators.
- **Finite coordinate lists** — the two exceptional groups are parametrised by integer points of small four-dimensional spheres ([tetrahedral coordinates](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryTetrahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryTetrahedralCoordsEquiv), [octahedral coordinates](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/SU2/BinaryOctahedral.html#TauCeti.SU2.binaryOctahedralCoordsEquiv)). Distinctness and counting then reduce to `decide`, a pattern the icosahedral model can follow once it has coordinates in ℤ[φ].
- **The double cover SU(2) → SO(3)** — `TauCeti.SU2.toSO3`, with its kernel identified as the centre, comes from LieGroups. It is the map along which the classification in 0.2 lifts conjugacies from rotation groups.

## The frontier

- **The binary icosahedral model (0.1.5)** — the 120 vertices of the 600-cell: the 24 tetrahedral points plus the 96 even permutations of (0, ±1, ±φ, ±φ⁻¹)/2, with closure and order 120. This is the last missing concrete model. The coordinates live in ℤ[φ], so the integer-point counting used for orders 24 and 48 does not carry over directly.
- **Per-model packaging (0.1)** — for all five families, the inclusion into SL₂(ℂ), the faithful defining representation and the invariant determinant-one alternating form. Layer 1's McKay matrix takes these as input.
- **Pole sets and the rotation classification (0.2)** — the pole set of a finite subgroup of SO(3), the orbit equation Σ(1 − 1/mⱼ) = 2 − 2/|K|, and explicit conjugacies to the cyclic, dihedral, tetrahedral, octahedral and A₅ rotation models. This does not depend on the icosahedral model until the (2,3,5) comparison.
- **Klein's classification and conjugacy (0.2)** — `finite_subgroup_SL2_classification`, `finite_subgroup_SL2_conjugate_of_mulEquiv` and `card_eq_48_conjugate`, by lifting through the double cover and averaging a Hermitian form. The odd-order case can now reuse the torus uniqueness result. The theorems are blocked on the items above.
- **Skew-group algebras (3.1–3.2)** — the equivalence between modules over A ⋊ Γ and Γ-equivariant A-modules, and the actions on Sym(V), Λ(V) and T(V). They are stated for any finite group of invertible order, so they can start alongside Layer 0.
