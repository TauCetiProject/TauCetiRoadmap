# Roadmap: parameter stacks, invariant theory and spectral coefficients

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the stack of local Langlands parameters of a pinned split reductive dual group over `ℤ_ℓ`, `ℓ ≠ p`, together with the three structures that the spectral side of the local Langlands programme reads off it: its deformation theory and singularities, its coarse quotient and the algebra of excursion operators, and its categories of perfect complexes. It uses the finite-wild cocycle scheme and excursion algebra of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6, with the dual group and L-group of ReductiveGroupsPartII (layer RG2.5), and builds their condensed, derived and categorical refinements. It ends with three theorems of Fargues–Scholze, Chapter VIII: geometric points of the coarse quotient are semisimple parameters at every `ℓ ≠ p` (Proposition VIII.3.2 and VIII.3.8); every categorical action of Hecke data gives a map from the excursion algebra to the centre (Theorem VIII.4.1); and when `ℓ` does not divide the order of the torsion of `π₁(Ĝ)`, the category of perfect complexes on the parameter stack is generated by representations of the dual group and its Ind-completion is the category of modules over the cocycle algebra (Theorem VIII.5.1). We want it because the spectral action of Fargues–Scholze, the integral local Langlands correspondence in families of Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss and Zhu's coherent sheaves on the stack of parameters all take these three structures as their coefficients, and because its modular representation theory (good filtrations, fixed groups of finite automorphism groups, wild gerbes) is needed nowhere else in this form.

The roadmap has seven layers. LP0 fixes the coefficient convention and the Weil-group conventions, and defines the wild enhancements of Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens. LP1 identifies the supplied integral parameter schemes with condensed parameters, builds the derived parameter stacks and computes their deformation theory and singularities. LP2e (excursion presentation) builds the coarse quotient, the derived free-group presentation and the categorical excursion operators of an abstract categorical Hecke action, all without a restriction on `ℓ`. LP2c (semisimple characters) reconstructs semisimple parameters from pseudocharacters with a prescribed finite projection and proves the local continuity theorems. LP3 develops the modular representation theory: good filtrations, induced perfect complexes, fixed groups and wild gerbe elimination. LP2i (integral invariants) uses LP3 for the equivariant refinement of the supplied scalar excursion comparison at good primes. LP4 builds the universal representation bundles and proves generation and the module comparison.

`Suggested.lean` contains no headline statements at the pins. Their objects include the local Weil group, relatively discrete coefficient functor, parameter schemes, excursion algebra and stable ∞-categories of perfect complexes on quotient stacks; these are absent from the pinned libraries. The module docstring identifies the missing objects for each layer. The mathematical targets and their hypotheses are specified below.

## Conventions

Let `E` be a nonarchimedean local field with residue characteristic `p` and residue cardinality `q`, and fix a prime `ℓ ≠ p`. `Ĥ` is a pinned split reductive group scheme over `ℤ_ℓ` (or over `ℤ[1/p]` when the integral scheme is meant), `Q` a finite group acting on `Ĥ` by pinning-preserving automorphisms, and `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q`. A homomorphism `η : Γ → Q` is fixed whenever `Γ` replaces the Weil group: a parameter into `J` lifts this `η`, and changing `η` changes the problem.

Examples with `Ĥ = G_m` use `Q = 1` unless an action is explicitly specified.

A crossed cocycle for an action `α` of `Γ` on `Ĥ` satisfies `c(γδ) = c(γ) α(γ)(c(δ))`, and gauge by `h` is `(h·c)(γ) = h c(γ) α(γ)(h)⁻¹`. The section attached to `c` is `γ ↦ (c(γ), η(γ))` into `Ĥ ⋊ Q`, and gauge corresponds to conjugation of the section by `(h, 1)`. Cocycles are homomorphisms only for the trivial action. In section form, parameters are homomorphisms `Γ →* Ĥ ⋊[β] Q` whose composite with `SemidirectProduct.rightHom` is `η`; the cocycle is the first coordinate, and LP0.1's section equivalence identifies this form with `CrossedCocycle (β.comp η)`.

For a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ`, the coefficient object is the relatively discrete condensed algebra `Λ_disc ⊗_{ℤ_{ℓ,disc}} ℤ_ℓ`. Its sections over a profinite set `S` are the maps `S → Λ` that take values in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-submodule `M ⊂ Λ` and are continuous for the ℓ-adic topology of `M`. For an `𝔽_ℓ`-algebra this is the discrete condensed structure; for `Λ = ℚ_ℓ` it admits continuous characters of inertia with infinite image. A continuous map `S → Λ` for some topology on `Λ` does not by itself specify this condition.

Choose a geometric Frobenius `σ` and a tame generator `τ` with `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ^q`. The geometric degree sends `σ` to `1`; the Weil–Deligne scaling is `q^{-deg(w)}`, so `Ad(φ₀(σ))(σ·N) = q⁻¹ N`. ClassFieldTheory and Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss use the arithmetic Frobenius `Fr = σ⁻¹`, whose degree is the negative of ours; the conversion is `geometricDegree d = d⁻¹` in multiplicative notation, and the action, degree and Tate character are transported together.

The framed finite-presentation model over `ℤ[1/p]` is the scheme constructed in LP1.1. Comparisons between different dense discrete models are canonical only after base change to `ℤ_ℓ`, through continuous extension; a canonical comparison over `ℤ[1/p]` is not used. Framed schemes have relative dimension `dim Ĥ` and absolute dimension `dim Ĥ + 1` over the one-dimensional base; quotient stacks have expected relative dimension zero.

Cohomological indexing is used throughout: a module in degree zero shifted by `[-1]` sits in degree `1`. Tangent and cotangent complexes are derived objects, and continuous Weil cochains are not the cochains of the abstract Weil group with its topology forgotten. Cotangent duality uses `Lie(Ĥ)^*` with the Tate twist; no invariant pairing `Lie(Ĥ) ≅ Lie(Ĥ)^*` is assumed. Singularities are `H^{-1}(L ⊗ T)` with coordinate algebra `Sym H¹(L^∨)`.

The good-filtration t-structure lives on `Ind Perf(BG)` with connective part `D^{≤0}`. `Perf^ind(X/G)` denotes the stable subcategory closed under retracts generated by pullbacks from `Perf(BG)`; equality with `Perf(X/G)` is a generation theorem, never a definition. The centre of a stable ∞-category is `π₀ End(id)`, and a Schur condition on an object `X` includes a specified `L`-algebra isomorphism `π₀ End(X) ≅ L`. In LP3, following Fargues–Scholze §VIII.5, `G` denotes a reductive group over an algebraically closed field `L` of characteristic `ℓ` (playing the role of the dual group), and dominant weights are characters of its maximal torus. "Reductive" means connected; a group whose identity component is reductive is called generalized reductive when its component group is finite étale.

The three strengths of the invariant comparison are kept apart:

| Result | Coefficient hypothesis | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closed-orbit classification; excursion comparison a universal homeomorphism | every `ℓ ≠ p` | LP2e.6; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, used in LP2e.9 |
| Excursion comparison an isomorphism | after inverting `ℓ` | SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, used in LP2e.9 |
| Scalar integral isomorphism | `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` | SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3 |
| Isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, higher-cohomology vanishing, all coefficient base changes, generation of `Perf` | `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` | LP3, LP2i, LP4 |

The component idempotents of `Q` are integral even when `ℓ` divides `|Q|`, and the finite-`Q` reconstruction theorem has no prime-to-`|Q|` restriction. For bad primes, independence of the full excursion algebra from the dense discrete model is not known (Fargues–Scholze, remark after Proposition VIII.3.7, p.289); the ℓ-torsion-free quotient is independent, and only that quotient is used where independence matters.

## How to read the build

The layers are built in the order LP0, LP1, LP2e, LP2c, LP3, LP2i, LP4. LP0 extends SR.6.1's cocycle and discretization interfaces to condensed coefficients and prescribed projections. LP1 needs LP0 and the derived foundations. The two first parts of LP2 need LP1 and unconditional reductive invariant theory, and in particular never the good-prime results of LP3: closed orbits, the excursion presentation, reconstruction and continuity hold at every `ℓ ≠ p`. LP3 is the modular representation theory in characteristic `ℓ`. LP2i applies LP3 to the equivariant refinement of the integral excursion comparison, and LP4 applies LP3 to the categories of perfect complexes. Prerequisites are cited by layer and subsection number, as `LP2e.4`.

## Layer LP0: cocycles, coefficients and wild enhancements

LP0 uses SR.6.1's crossed cocycles and finite-wild discretizations, extends their restriction and section APIs to relatively discrete condensed coefficients, and constructs normal wild cutoffs and the geometric-degree normalisation. Its second half defines the wild enhancements of Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens using the intrinsic twisted centralizer, which differs from the component group of an ordinary centralizer.

### LP0.1 Restriction, sections and continuity of crossed cocycles

For a group `Γ` acting on `H` by `α : Γ →* MulAut H`, use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Crossed cocycles and gauge action*, for `CrossedCocycle α`, its unit and inverse identities, gauge action, equivariant coefficient change and section homomorphism. Extend that API by restriction: `CrossedCocycle.restrict c f = c ∘ f` for `f : Δ →* Γ` and action `α ∘ f`, with identity, composition, gauge and coefficient-change compatibility. Construct `CrossedCocycle.sectionEquiv` as the inverse equivalence to the supplied section map. For a fixed `η : Γ →* Q` and action `β`, identify the supplied cocycles for `β ∘ η` with lifts `φ : Γ →* H ⋊[β] Q` of `η`. The lifts form the subtype cut out by `SemidirectProduct.rightHom.comp φ = η`; the first coordinate recovers the cocycle, and conjugation by `SemidirectProduct.inl h` transports the supplied gauge action. Define `H¹(Γ, H)` as the gauge-orbit set with restriction. For topological groups with continuous action, extend the supplied cocycles to continuous cocycles and prove preservation of continuity by gauge, continuous restriction and continuous coefficient change (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1, p.278; Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §1.2, pp.4–6). *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Crossed cocycles and gauge action*; Mathlib `SemidirectProduct`, `MonoidHom`, `Subgroup`. SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1 supplies the comparison with Mathlib's distinct Čech cocycles `CategoryTheory.PresheafOfGroups.OneCocycle`.

**Checks.**

- For trivial `α`, crossed cocycles are exactly group homomorphisms `Γ →* H`; for `Γ = 1` there is exactly one cocycle.
- The gauge of the unit cocycle by `h` evaluates to `h α(γ)(h)⁻¹`; for `Γ = ℤ` acting on `ℚˣ` by inversion at the generator and `h = 2`, the value at the generator is `4`, which pins both the side and the inverse in the gauge formula.
- For `C₂` acting on `ℤ` by negation, `c(s) = 1` is a crossed cocycle (`c(s²) = 1 − 1 = 0`) that is not a homomorphism `C₂ → ℤ`: a definition requiring multiplicativity fails.
- Under `sectionEquiv`, the projection of the section is the identity and its first coordinate is `c`; gauge by `h` becomes conjugation by `inl h`, and the lift attached to the unit cocycle with `η` is `inr ∘ η`.
- Restriction along the trivial map `ℤ → 1` sends every cocycle on `1` to the unit cocycle on `ℤ`, and restriction along `id` is the identity.
- For negation on `ℤ`, `H¹(C₂, ℤ_neg) ≅ ℤ/2`: a cocycle is determined by any integer at the involution, and gauge changes that integer by an even integer.
- `H¹(1,H)` is a singleton. For trivial action, `H¹(ℤ,H)` is the set of conjugacy classes of elements of `H`, by evaluation at a generator.

### LP0.2 Condensed L-parameters

Fix the pinned split dual `Ĥ` over `ℤ_ℓ` with its standard action factoring through `Q` and `W_E → Q`. For a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ`, define `condensedCoefficients Λ`, the relatively discrete condensed algebra `Λ_disc ⊗_{ℤ_{ℓ,disc}} ℤ_ℓ`, and prove that its sections on a profinite set `S` are the maps `S → Λ` with values in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-submodule on which they are ℓ-adically continuous. Define an L-parameter `LParameter` as a condensed crossed cocycle `W_E → Ĥ(Λ)` for these coefficients; prove `LParameter.asSection` (cocycles are sections of `Ĥ(Λ) ⋊ W_E → W_E`) and `LParameter.asLift` (sections are lifts of `W_E → Q` to `Ĥ(Λ) ⋊ Q`), both natural in `Λ`. Prove `LParameter.ext` (equality on every test profinite set and section), `LParameter.gauge` (the action `h·φ(w) = h φ(w) (w·h)⁻¹` preserves the coefficient and continuity conditions, with identity and multiplication laws), `LParameter.restrictWild` (restriction to wild inertia with the same convention), and `LParameter.matrixCriterion`: for a closed embedding `Ĥ ⋊ Q → GL_N`, a cocycle is an L-parameter exactly when its matrix coefficients restricted to inertia are finite-type continuous (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1, p.278). The cyclotomic normalisation is compared with other conventions only after adjoining `√q`. *Needs:* LP0.1; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `Condensed`, `CondensedMod`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action, L-parameters are continuous characters `W_E → Λˣ` whose inertia values lie in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-module.
- For an `𝔽_ℓ`-algebra `Λ` the relatively discrete structure is discrete, so every parameter is locally constant on inertia. A continuous map from compact inertia then has finite image; an arbitrary abstract homomorphism with non-open kernel fails this condition.
- For `Λ = ℚ_ℓ`, take the tame ℓ-primary coordinate `t_ℓ : I_E → ℤ_ℓ`, with `t_ℓ(Fr i Fr⁻¹) = q t_ℓ(i)`. In split `GL₂`, set `ρ(i) = [[1,t_ℓ(i)],[0,1]]` and `ρ(Fr) = diag(q,1)`. This is finite-type continuous with infinite inertia image; imposing discrete coefficients would exclude it. The ℓ-adic cyclotomic character itself is unramified for `ℓ ≠ p`, so it cannot witness infinite inertia.
- The projection of `asSection φ (w)` is `w` and its first coordinate is `φ(w)`.

### LP0.3 Finite wild ramification

A parameter has finite wild ramification if its restriction to wild inertia `P_E` is trivial on a relatively open subgroup (`HasFiniteWildRamification`). For an open subgroup `P ⊂ P_E`, normal in `W_E` and contained in the kernel of `W_E → Q`, define the finite-wild piece `FiniteWildPiece P` as the parameters trivial on `P`, with `FiniteWildPiece.inflate : FiniteWildPiece P → FiniteWildPiece P′` for `P′ ⊂ P`, injective. Prove `LParameter.finiteWild`: every L-parameter for these coefficients has finite wild ramification, because a continuous image of the pro-`p` group `P_E` in an ℓ-adic congruence kernel is trivial, and reduction modulo `ℓ` makes the remaining image finite (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1 and Theorem VIII.1.3 with its proof, pp.278–280). *Needs:* LP0.2; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4.

Prove the descent statements on the quotient group: if `P` is normal and in the kernel of the action and `c` is trivial on `P`, then `c` descends uniquely to `W_E/P` (`CrossedCocycle.descend`, with `descend_mk` on representatives), giving `CrossedCocycle.quotientEquiv` between cocycles on `W_E/P` and cocycles trivial on `P`, inverse to inflation, and its continuous version `LParameter.quotientEquiv`; gauge commutes with descent. For a continuous homomorphism from a topological group with compact wild subgroup to a `T₁` topological group, prove `finiteWild_iff_finite_range`: an open kernel on the wild subgroup is equivalent to finite image there. Construct the normal cutoff explicitly: with `s_c(γ) = (c(γ), η(γ))` the homomorphism of LP0.1, put `U = P ∩ ker s_c` (`NormalWildCutoff.subgroup`); prove that `U` is normal, contained in `P` and in `ker η`, kills `c`, is relatively open when `c` has finite image on `P` and `η|P` is continuous into the discrete `Q`, is the largest subgroup of `P ∩ ker η` on which `c` is trivial (`le_subgroup_iff`), is unchanged by gauge (`subgroup_gauge`), and pulls back along any `f : Δ →* Γ` to the cutoff of the restricted data (`subgroup_restrict`). This is the group-theoretic step of Remark VIII.1.2 and the proof of Theorem VIII.1.3 (pp.278–279); the coefficient argument that every parameter has finite wild image is the separate input above.

**Checks.**

- An unramified parameter lies in the piece `P = P_E` when `Q` is unramified; for `P′ ⊂ P`, triviality on `P` implies triviality on `P′`, which fixes the direction of inflation.
- For coefficients in a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, the condition is finite image of wild inertia.
- For the identity cocycle of the discrete group `ℤ`, the kernel `{0}` is open but the image is infinite: compactness cannot be dropped from `finiteWild_iff_finite_range`. For the identity map of `C₂` with the indiscrete topology on both sides, the image is finite and the map continuous but the kernel is not open: the `T₁` hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- For `Γ = Q`, `η = id` and the unit cocycle, the cutoff is `{1}` even for trivial action, so replacing `ker η` by the kernel of the action is wrong; for trivial `Q` and the identity cocycle `Γ → Γ` the cutoff is `{1}`, which distinguishes it from `P ∩ ker η`; for trivial `H` and `Q` the cutoff is `P`.
- For `Γ = H = Q = S₃` with `η = id`, conjugation action and the unit cocycle, gauge by `(01)` changes the value at `(12)` to a nonidentity permutation, yet the cutoff stays `{1}`: the identity fibre of the cocycle alone is not gauge invariant.
- The unit cocycle of `C₂` acting on `ℤ` by negation kills the whole source but does not descend to the trivial quotient, because the action does not: the action-kernel hypothesis of `descend` cannot be dropped.

### LP0.4 Discrete Weil models and the geometric degree

For an open `W_E`-normal subgroup `P ⊂ P_E` killing the action, use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Finite-wild Weil discretization*, for the finitely presented dense discrete `W ⊂ W_E/P`, and *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization* for unique extension and canonical ℓ-adic comparisons. Identify its relatively ℓ-adic continuous cocycles with LP0.2's condensed cocycles, naturally in every `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra and compatibly with gauge and restriction. The identification sends every value at `w ∈ W` to that same value; locally finite-type coefficient continuity is proved using the tame matrix's finite unipotent binomial expansion. Thus the condensed extension is the supplied extension, with its identity and composition laws. No canonical independence over `ℤ[1/p]` is asserted (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, pp.279–280; Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §4.1, Corollary 4.2, pp.30–31).

Construct the geometric convention on this same model. For ClassFieldTheory's arithmetic degree `d : W_E →* Multiplicative ℤ`, define `geometricDegree d = d⁻¹` and prove `geometricDegree_apply`, `geometricDegree_geometricDegree`, `geometricDegree_ker` and compatibility with precomposition. For a unit `q_Λ ∈ Λˣ` and geometric degree `d_geo`, define `tateCharacter q_Λ d_geo` by `w ↦ q_Λ^{−d_geo(w)}`. It takes arithmetic Frobenius to `q_Λ` and geometric Frobenius to `q_Λ⁻¹`; prove `tateCharacter_geometricDegree` and compatibility with precomposition. The generator conversion is `σ = Fr⁻¹`, giving `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ^q`; transport the action and degree together, without inverting the Weil group. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Finite-wild Weil discretization*, *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization*; LP0.1–LP0.3; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; Mathlib `zpowersHom`.

**Checks.**

- For `d(Fr) = 1`, `geometricDegree d (Fr) = −1` and `geometricDegree d (Fr⁻¹) = 1`; inertia, of degree zero, has geometric degree zero, and the zero degree map is fixed by the conversion.
- With `q = 3` the Tate character takes the value `3` at arithmetic Frobenius and `1/3` at geometric Frobenius, and the value `1` on inertia: a convention that forgot to invert would give `3` at `σ`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m`, the tame relation forces `χ(τ)^{q−1} = 1`; replacing geometric Frobenius by the arithmetic one in `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ^q` gives `Fr τ Fr⁻¹ = τ^q`, the arithmetic relation.
- Changing the tame generator scales the normalised monodromy coordinate; the comparison of parameter functors is canonical, not an equality of unnormalised monodromy operators.

### LP0.5 Wild inertial parameters

For a group projection `π : L → W` and a homomorphism `i : P → W`, a wild inertial parameter is a homomorphism `ρ : P → L` for which some `φ : W → L` satisfies `π ∘ φ = id` and `φ ∘ i = ρ`; the extension is a condition, not chosen data (`IsWildInertialParameter`). For the classical definition, let `G` be quasi-split classical over a local field of odd residual characteristic, with complex dual group. First construct `ClassicalAdmissibleParameter`: continuous homomorphisms `φ : W_F × SL₂(ℂ) → ᴸG` with prescribed projection to `W_F`, finite inertia image after projection to `Ĝ ⋊ Q` for a finite quotient `Q` through which the action factors, Frobenius-semisimple Weil restriction and algebraic `SL₂` restriction. Define `S_φ = C_Ĝ(φ)/(C_Ĝ(φ)° Z(Ĝ)^{W_F})`, enhanced parameters by irreducible complex representations of `S_φ`, and `Lang(G)` as their `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes, with conjugation and restriction APIs. Define classical wild inertial parameters by existence of such an admissible extension, and take their `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes. The abstract predicate `IsWildInertialParameter` only expresses existence of a section; it imposes none of the classical admissibility conditions (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.20–1.21, pp.8–9). Define `WildInertialParameter` as this subtype and prove `projection` (`π ∘ ρ = i`), `ext` (the underlying homomorphism determines the parameter), `ofLanglands` (restriction of an admissible parameter, with `ofLanglands_val`), and the conjugation action of `ker π` with `conjugate_one`, `conjugate_mul`, `ofLanglands_conjugate` and, for `L = H ⋊ W`, `conjugate_left`: the `H`-coordinate transforms by `h ρ(p)_H α(i(p))(h)⁻¹`, the gauge action of LP0.1. No wild local Langlands correspondence is asserted. *Needs:* LP0.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4.

**Checks.**

- The parameter `p ↦ (1, p)` with trivial dual-group component is extended by the standard unramified admissible parameter.
- A finite-image Weil homomorphism with trivial `SL₂` part is admissible. In split `SL₂`, the homomorphism trivial on inertia with nonidentity unipotent Frobenius has the projection and continuity properties but fails Frobenius semisimplicity. An infinite-image unipotent inertia homomorphism fails finite inertia even if it is a condensed parameter.
- Restricting `hφh⁻¹` gives `hρh⁻¹`; two admissible extensions with the same restriction define the same wild inertial parameter.
- When `i` is injective and `P` nontrivial, the constant homomorphism `p ↦ 1` is not a wild inertial parameter, since `π ∘ ρ = i` fails: a definition asking only that some extension exist without the section equation would accept it.
- In `L = W = S₃` with `π = i = id`, conjugation by `(01)` sends `(12)` to `(02)`, changing the prescribed value; only elements of `ker π` may conjugate.

### LP0.6 Twisted wild centralizers

For a wild inertial parameter `ρ` with `P_F` normal in `W_F`, define `twistedWildCentralizer ρ` as the set of `(g, w) ∈ ᴸG` with `(g, w) ρ(w⁻¹ p w) (g, w)⁻¹ = ρ(p)` for all `p ∈ P_F`. Prove that it is a subgroup (`one_mem_twistedWildCentralizer`, `mul_mem_twistedWildCentralizer`, `inv_mem_twistedWildCentralizer`), that its projection to `W_F` is surjective with kernel the ordinary centralizer `C_Ĝ(ρ)` (`inl_mem_twistedWildCentralizer_iff`), that the values of an extending section lie in it (`section_mem_twistedWildCentralizer`), and that a chosen admissible extension `φ` gives `splitEquiv`, an isomorphism with `C_Ĝ(ρ) ⋊_{Ad φ} W_F` sending `(g, w)` to `(g φ(w)⁻¹, w)`; the subgroup is intrinsic and only the splitting depends on `φ` (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, p.8). This is a twisted centralizer, not the centralizer of `ρ(P_F)` in the L-group. *Needs:* LP0.5; Mathlib `Subgroup`, `MonoidHom`.

**Checks.**

- For `ρ(p) = (1, p)` and trivial `P_F`-action on `Ĝ`, the twisted centralizer is the whole L-group.
- The elements above `w = 1` are exactly the usual centralizer of `ρ` in `Ĝ`.
- With `H = 1`, `P = W = S₃` and `ρ = inr ∘ P.subtype`, the value `inr (01)` lies in the twisted centralizer but does not commute with `ρ((12))`. Thus the ordinary centralizer excludes a genuine section value.
- For every admissible extension `φ`, each `φ(w)` lies in the twisted centralizer.

### LP0.6a The centre of the local Weil group

For each nonarchimedean local field `F` of odd residual characteristic, prove `WeilGroup.center_eq_bot`. A central Weil element is central in the absolute Galois group by density. For each finite Galois `L/F`, it therefore acts trivially on the abelianization of `G_L`. In mixed characteristic, conjugation-equivariant local reciprocity and injectivity of the local Artin map force its restriction to fix `Lˣ`. In equal characteristic, construct the Artin–Schreier identification `H¹(G_L, 𝔽_p) = L/(x^p−x)` and prove that every nonidentity finite-order automorphism of `L` acts nontrivially on this quotient. For a wild automorphism `t ↦ t + c t^{r+1} + …`, use `t^{-n}` with `n > r` and `p ∤ n(n−r)`; its difference has a pole of order prime to `p`. Residue-field and tame automorphisms are detected by coefficients and prime-to-`p` pole orders. This proves triviality on every `L`, hence on the separable closure (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, equation (1.1), p.8; local reciprocity and conjugation as in ClassFieldTheory layers 3, 6–9; Artin–Schreier exact sequence, Stacks [0A3J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A3J)). The equal-characteristic Artin–Schreier identification and pole test are targets here; full Artin–Schreier–Witt reciprocity is not an input. *Needs:* ClassFieldTheory layers 3, 6–9 with their mixed-characteristic hypotheses; LocalFieldsRamification layers 1–4; LP0.1.

**Checks.**

- In a mixed-characteristic finite Galois extension, a nonidentity automorphism moves an element of `Lˣ`, and its Artin image detects the conjugation action.
- For `L = 𝔽_p((t))`, `p` odd, the involution `t ↦ −t` moves the class of `t^{-1}` in `L/(x^p−x)`.
- The tame quotient alone cannot prove centre-freeness of the full Weil group: every element of wild inertia has trivial image there; finite ramified extensions and their abelian characters are essential.

### LP0.7 Wild enhancement groups

Define `wildEnhancementGroup ρ = Z(C_{ᴸG}(ρ)) / Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}`, the centre of the twisted centralizer modulo the image of the invariant dual centre under `z ↦ (z, 1)`. Prove `centerIdentification`: a chosen extension `φ` identifies the numerator with `Z(C_Ĝ(ρ))^{φ(W_F)}`, carrying the embedded invariant dual centre to itself, using that `Z(W_F)` is trivial; prove that this centre lies in `C_Ĝ(φ(W_F × SL₂(ℂ)))`, so that inflation from `S_φ = C_Ĝ(φ) / (C_Ĝ(φ)° Z(Ĝ)^{W_F})` followed by restriction gives `restrictRep : Rep(S_φ) → Rep(S_ρ)`; and prove `conjugateEquiv`, the transport of `S_ρ` and its representations under conjugation of `ρ` by `h ∈ Ĝ` (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, equation (1.1), p.8). `S_ρ` is not `π₀ C_Ĝ(ρ)`. *Needs:* LP0.6; LP0.6a; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `Subgroup.center`, `Representation`.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial dual-group component `ρ(p) = (1, p)`, the numerator is `Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}` and `S_ρ` is trivial; the constant map `p ↦ 1`, which loses the projection, cannot serve as this test.
- For `G = Sp₂` and `Ĝ = SO₃`, a ramified cyclic cubic character of `W_{ℚ₃}` gives a nontrivial wild parameter `diag(θ, θ⁻¹, 1)`. Its centralizer is the diagonal torus `T`, the Weil action on `T` is trivial, and `S_ρ = T`; its full parameter centralizer is connected, so `S_φ = 1`.
- For the `S₃` quotient of `W_{ℚ₃}` from the splitting field of `X³−3`, use the orthogonal representation `std₂ ⊕ sign` into `SO₃`. Wild inertia maps onto `C₃`, its centralizer is `T`, and the tame involution inverts `T`. Hence `S_ρ = μ₂` and `S_φ = C₂`; both characters restrict nontrivially or trivially as expected.
- The restricted representation is trivial on `Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}`.
- Conjugating `φ` and `ρ` by `h` transports the restricted representation along `conjugateEquiv`.

### LP0.8 Extended wild inertial parameters

An extended wild parameter is a pair `(ρ, χ_ρ)` with `ρ` a wild inertial parameter and `χ_ρ` a representation of `S_ρ` obtained by restricting some irreducible enhancement `χ_φ` of an admissible extension `φ`. Prove from the classical isotypic decomposition that the centralizer has general linear, symplectic and orthogonal factors, with the special-orthogonal determinant condition where appropriate; deduce that `S_φ` is an elementary abelian 2-group, so every irreducible complex enhancement and its restriction is one-dimensional. Define `ExtendedWildParameter`, the set `WildParameterClasses` of their `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes, the map `restrictEnhancedParameter : Lang(G) → Wild(G)` sending `(φ, χ_φ)` to `(φ|_{P_F}, χ_φ|_{S_ρ})`, and `ExtendedWildParameter.forget` to the class of `ρ`; prove that `restrictEnhancedParameter` is well defined on conjugacy classes (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, pp.8–9). The scope is that of the source: quasi-split classical groups, odd residual characteristic, complex coefficients. *Needs:* LP0.5; LP0.7; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For trivial dual-group component `ρ(p) = (1,p)`, `S_ρ = 1` and every restricted enhancement is its one-dimensional trivial representation.
- For the ramified cyclic cubic parameter of LP0.7, `S_ρ = T` but `S_φ = 1`: the trivial character extends, while a nontrivial character of `T` does not arise from any admissible extension of this same `ρ`, since `C_Ĝ(φ) ⊂ T` remains connected.
- For the dihedral parameter of LP0.7, `S_ρ = S_φ = C₂`, and both the trivial and sign character give extended wild parameters.
- Conjugate enhanced Langlands parameters have the same image in `WildParameterClasses`.
- Forgetting `restrictEnhancedParameter (φ, χ_φ)` gives the conjugacy class of `φ|_{P_F}`.

## Layer LP1: integral parameter spaces and deformation theory

Finite wild cutoffs reduce the parameter space to finitely presented equations, whose union over all cutoffs is the full scheme. Flatness, the complete-intersection property and the dimension bound come first; the derived parameter stack and its classicality follow; the cotangent complex then gives deformation theory, the Weil–Deligne comparison and the singularity stack with its support theory.

### LP1.1 ℓ-adic representability of finite-wild parameters

Use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *One integral finite-wild cocycle scheme*, and write `IntegralCocycleScheme W Ĥ = Z¹(W, Ĥ)` for that supplied scheme over `ℤ[1/p]`, with its universal cocycle, point equivalence and gauge coaction. Identify its base change to `ℤ_ℓ` with the finite-wild condensed parameter functor of LP0.3 through LP0.4, naturally in coefficients and equivariantly for gauge. Transport the supplied coordinate presentation: for `C = O(Ĥ)`, the coproduct `T` indexed by finite generators of `W` is divided by the ideal `J` generated by `c(r)(a) − ε(a)` for relators `r` and algebra generators `a` of `C`. This is `T/J`, retaining nilpotents. Construct `IntegralCocycleScheme.presentationEquiv` by the supplied representing property; it fixes all universal evaluations, so different presentations identify the same scheme (Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §1, pp.4–7, and §4.1, Theorem 4.1, pp.29–30; Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, p.280). *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *One integral finite-wild cocycle scheme*, *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization*; LP0.1; LP0.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- For `W = F_n` the scheme is `Ĥⁿ` with twisted diagonal conjugation; for `W = 1` it is the base.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action the tame scheme is `G_m × μ_{q−1}`; in the fibre at `2` for `q = 3`, the universal element `t − 1` is nonzero with square zero, so the scheme is not its reduction.
- For the unramified action with arithmetic Frobenius inverting `G_m`, the tame scheme is `G_m × μ_{q+1}`: its tame coordinate `b` satisfies `b⁻¹ = b^q`. For `q = 3` in characteristic `2`, `(b − 1)⁴ = 0` but `(b − 1)² ≠ 0`.
- Its `ℤ_ℓ`-points on `ℤ_ℓ`-algebras agree with the finite-wild condensed parameters of LP0.3.

### LP1.2 Clopen finite-wild pieces

Prove that the parameter scheme `Z¹(W_E, Ĥ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` is the filtered union of the finite-wild pieces `Z¹(W_E/P, Ĥ)`, that for `P′ ⊂ P` the `P`-piece is open and closed in the `P′`-piece, and that the union is a disjoint union of affine schemes of finite type after separating the clopen wild-kernel strata: the condition `φ(γ) = 1` is clopen for `γ` of finite `p`-power order because `p` is invertible, finitely many such conditions cut out the `P`-piece, and every parameter has an open wild kernel (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, pp.279–280). Use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.2, *Finite wild strata and reductive centralizers*, for the fixed-depth wild strata after integral-closure base change, with their smooth restriction orbits and centralizers (Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, Theorem 3.1 and §4.1, equations (4.1)–(4.2)); distinguish these from varying the depth. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.2, *Finite wild strata and reductive centralizers*; LP0.3; LP1.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3.

**Checks.**

- The full union is not quasi-compact when infinitely many wild strata occur.
- For `P′ = P` the inclusion is the identity.
- For trivial `Ĥ` every piece is the base.

### LP1.3 Weil cohomology: amplitude and Euler characteristic

For a free `Λ`-module `M` of finite rank with a condensed action of `W_E` in the relatively discrete convention, prove that `RΓ(W_E, M)` is a perfect complex of `Λ`-modules with amplitude in `[0, 2]` and Euler characteristic zero; for field coefficients, `dim H⁰ − dim H¹ + dim H² = 0`. The proof kills an open wild kernel (exact invariants for a finite `p`-group), then resolves tame ℓ-primary inertia and the Frobenius `ℤ` each by a two-term complex; the total complex has amplitude `[0, 2]` and alternating rank zero, and the same calculation on a finite-wild dense `W` gives the derived deformation complex (Fargues–Scholze, the dimension argument in the proof of Theorem VIII.1.3 and Proposition VIII.2.2, pp.280–282). Cohomology is continuous (condensed), not abstract cohomology of `W_E` with its topology forgotten. *Needs:* LP0.4; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ContinuousCohomology.continuousCohomologyFunctor`; Mathlib `Module.Free`.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial rank-one `ℚ_ℓ`-representation, `H⁰` and `H¹` are one-dimensional and `H² = 0`.
- For `ℚ_ℓ(1)`, `H⁰ = 0`.
- The alternating sum is zero in both cases.

### LP1.4 Unipotent classes and the Frobenius–tame dimension bound

For a smooth affine finite-type group `H` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` with reductive identity component, first prove that it has finitely many unipotent conjugacy classes. The connected finiteness input and its use here are stated in the proof of Fargues–Scholze Lemma VIII.1.4, p.281, which credits Lusztig 1976. For the disconnected step, descend `H` to a finite subfield and use Fulman–Guralnick 2012, Corollary 2.6, pp.3028–3029; that corollary assumes a finite-field model and transfers finiteness from the identity component. Let `σ` act on `ℤ/ℓ^mℤ` by multiplication by `q`. Prove that the scheme `Hom(ℤ/ℓ^mℤ ⋊ σ^ℤ, H)` has dimension at most `dim H`: stratify by the unipotent class of the image of the generator; on a stratum the generator has `dim H − dim Z_H(x)` choices and the choices of `σ`, when nonempty, form a torsor under `Z_H(x)`. ### LP1.8 Classicality of the derived cocycle scheme

### LP1.9 Local Tate duality for Weil cochains

- For `M = ℚ_ℓ(1)` the roles of `H⁰` and `H²` are exchanged.

### LP1.10 Cotangent complexes of parameter stacks

### LP1.11 Weil–Deligne parameters

Over a `ℚ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ`, define a Weil–Deligne parameter `WeilDeligneParameter` as a pair `(φ₀, N)`: `φ₀ : W_E → Ĥ(Λ)` a crossed cocycle trivial on an open subgroup of inertia, `N ∈ Lie(Ĥ) ⊗ Λ` nilpotent, and `Ad(φ₀(w))(w·N) = q^{−deg(w)} N` for the geometric degree, so that arithmetic Frobenius scales `N` by `q`. Provide the projections `cocycle` and `monodromy`, `gauge` (sending `(φ₀, N)` to `(h·φ₀, Ad(h)N)`, preserving the conditions), `zeroMonodromy` (every discrete-inertia cocycle with `N = 0`), and `ext` (Fargues–Scholze, Definition VIII.2.4, p.282). Nilpotence is in `Lie(Ĥ)`, distinct from the dual nilpotent cone of LP1.13. For split `GL_n`, algebraic matrix nilpotence and the scaling identity alone do not impose discrete inertia or the actual geometric Weil degree. Over nonreduced rings algebraic matrix nilpotence is also weaker than membership in the scheme-theoretic nilpotent cone. *Needs:* LP0.1; LP0.4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ClassFieldTheory layer 9.

**Checks.**

- An unramified `φ₀` with `N = 0` is a Weil–Deligne parameter.
- For a torus the scheme-theoretic nilpotent cone is the zero section, so every Weil–Deligne parameter has `N = 0`. Over a reduced ring, a nilpotent `1 × 1` matrix is zero; over `ℚ[ε]/(ε²)` with the zero degree map, `N = ε` satisfies algebraic matrix nilpotence and scaling but is not in the torus nilpotent cone.
- Gauge of `zeroMonodromy φ₀` is `zeroMonodromy (h·φ₀)`.
- In split `GL₂` with `q = 3`, `N = E₁₂` and `φ₀(σ) = diag(1/3, 1)`, conjugation sends `N` to `N/3 = q⁻¹ N`, compatible with `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ³`; the reciprocal matrix `diag(3, 1)` gives `3N` and fails the condition.

### LP1.11a Quasi-unipotence and nilpotent logarithm/exponential

For a finite free module over a commutative `ℚ_ℓ`-algebra, construct the finite polynomial maps `log(1+X)` for nilpotent `X` and `exp(N)` for nilpotent `N`; prove they are inverse and commute with coefficient change and conjugation. For a faithful closed embedding `Ĥ → GL_N`, prove that they identify the scheme-theoretic nilpotent cone of `Lie(Ĥ)` with the unipotent variety inside `Ĥ`, compatibly with that embedding. For the universal finite-wild cocycle, prove that locally on the coefficient scheme a sufficiently divisible prime-to-`p` power of tame inertia is unipotent: the Frobenius conjugacy to the `q`-power bounds the root-of-unity eigenvalues, and Cayley–Hamilton bounds the nilpotence exponent. Deduce quasi-unipotence for its field-valued specializations and the continuous formula `φ(x) = exp(t_ℓ(x)N)` on an open inertia subgroup. Prove uniqueness of `N` after the tame coordinate is fixed, and prove that it lies in the scheme-theoretic nilpotent cone: after taking a power `m` of Frobenius killing the finite pinned action, scaling makes every adjoint invariant of positive degree `d` vanish, since `q^{md}−1` is invertible over `ℚ_ℓ` (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, pp.279–280, and construction before Proposition VIII.2.5, p.282; Zhu, §3.1, Lemma 3.10, pp.35–36). *Needs:* LP0.4; LP1.1; LP1.11; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib matrix polynomials and Cayley–Hamilton.

**Checks.**

- For `N = E₁₂`, `exp(xN) = 1+xN` and `log(1+xN) = xN`.
- For `U = 1+E₁₂+E₂₃` over `ℚ₂`, `log U = E₁₂+E₂₃−E₁₃/2`; an integral statement would fail.
- For a finite-order tame matrix, a power killing its semisimple part has logarithm zero; changing that power to a multiple leaves the normalized logarithm unchanged.

### LP1.12 Monodromy and the Weil–Deligne comparison

For a chosen tame coordinate and Frobenius, prove that there is an `Ĥ`-equivariant isomorphism `Z¹(W_E, Ĥ)_{ℚ_ℓ} ≅ Par_WD` onto the scheme of Weil–Deligne parameters, constructed as follows: on a small enough ℓ-primary tame inertia subgroup `φ(x) = exp(xN)`; a unipotent power of `τ` gives `N = m⁻¹ log φ(τ^m)`; and `φ₀ = φ · exp(−t_ℓ(·)N)` has finite inertia image. This gives the algebraic monodromy morphism to the nilpotent cone of `Lie(Ĥ)`. Prove independence of the sufficiently divisible `m`, equivariance under gauge, and that changing the tame coordinate scales `N` accordingly (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.2.5 and the construction before it, p.282; Zhu, §3.1, Lemma 3.10, pp.35–36, numbered Lemma 3.1.8 in the earlier version cited by Fargues–Scholze). Quasi-unipotence and the logarithm and exponential are supplied by LP1.11a after a faithful embedding of `Ĥ ⋊ Q`. The comparison is not integral: `log` and `exp` need `ℚ_ℓ`. *Needs:* LP1.11; LP0.4; LP1.11a; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- The torus case has `N = 0`.
- The special representation `Sp(2)` of `GL₂` has `N ≠ 0` with `Ad(φ₀(σ))(N) = q⁻¹N`.
- There is no general integral logarithm: for `ℓ = 2`, the unipotent `GL₃` matrix `U = 1 + E₁₂ + E₂₃` has `log U = E₁₂ + E₂₃ − E₁₃/2`, outside `M₃(ℤ₂)`.

### LP1.13 Singularities of parameter stacks

For `X = [Z¹(W_E, Ĥ)/Ĥ]` define `ParameterSingularities`, the relative singularity stack of the syntomic stack `X` over `ℤ_ℓ`: on an affine syntomic chart `Spec B → X`, its coordinate ring is `Sym_B H¹(L_{B/ℤ_ℓ}^∨)`, representing `T ↦ H^{−1}(L_{B/ℤ_ℓ} ⊗_B T)`, and these charts glue along smooth pullback (`smoothPullback`). Prove `fiber` (the fibre at `φ` is `H⁰(W_E, Lie(Ĥ)^*_{Ad φ}(1))`) and `embed` (a closed embedding into the dual Lie bundle `[Lie(Ĥ)^*/Ĥ] ×_{BĤ} X`). Define `dualNilpotentCone`, the closed locus of covectors whose `Ĥ`-orbit closure contains `0`, and `NilpotentSingularSupport` for a coherent complex on `X`: support in the pullback of `dualNilpotentCone` (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.2.2, Definitions VIII.2.6 and VIII.2.8, pp.282–285). **Checks.**

- On the smooth locus of a syntomic chart the singularity fibre is zero.
- At the trivial torus parameter over `ℚ_ℓ`, `H⁰(W_E, ℚ_ℓ(1)) = 0` and the fibre is zero.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `ℓ | q − 1`, the trivial `𝔽_ℓ`-parameter has a one-dimensional singularity fibre although the dual nilpotent cone of the torus is zero: nilpotent singular support is a real condition.
- With a specified invariant perfect Lie pairing, the inclusion transports to the usual nilpotent cone; without one no identification is made.
- Every perfect complex on `X` has nilpotent singular support, since its singular support lies in the zero section.
- On the split tame torus component with `q = 3` and `ℓ = 2`, the residue sheaf at the trivial parameter, pushed forward from its residual gerbe, has the full singularity line as support. This line contains nonzero covectors, while the dual nilpotent cone is zero, so the sheaf does not have nilpotent singular support.
- For split `GL₂` over a characteristic-zero `ℤ_ℓ`-field, at the parameter with trivial inertia and arithmetic Frobenius `diag(q,1)`, the structure sheaf of the residual gerbe, pushed forward to `X`, is nonperfect but has nilpotent singular support. Its singular support is the full obstruction line, contained in the dual nilpotent cone; in a transverse formal chart the singularity is `uv = 0`.

### LP1.14 Hochschild operators and singular support of parameter complexes

On an affine syntomic chart `B` over the regular noetherian base `ℤ_ℓ`, construct `cotangentToHochschild : H¹(L_{B/ℤ_ℓ}^∨) → HH²(B/ℤ_ℓ)` from commutative to associative square-zero extensions and `hochschildSelfExtAction : HH^*(B/ℤ_ℓ) → Ext^*_B(N, N)` by tensoring bimodule endomorphisms with `N`. Prove that for bounded coherent `N` the resulting graded `Sym_B H¹(L^∨)`-module `Ext^*_B(N, N)` is finitely generated (Gulliksen's theorem for complete intersections), that its conical support descends to the parameter stack, that `N` is perfect exactly when this support lies in the zero section, and that the projection of the nonzero support is the complement of the largest open on which `N` is perfect (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.2.7, Theorem VIII.2.9 and Corollary VIII.2.10, pp.283–284, citing Gulliksen, Theorem 3.1, and Arinkin–Gaitsgory, Theorem 4.2.6 and Appendix D). **Checks.**

- A vector bundle has support in the zero section.
- Over the singular hypersurface `k[x, y]/(xy)` the residue field at the origin has nonzero singular support and is not perfect.
- Not all of `HH²` comes from `H¹(L^∨)` (for a smooth polynomial ring in two variables the map is not surjective onto `HH²`).

### LP1.15 Nilpotence of singularity fibres

At `φ` over a `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L`, prove that the fibre of `ParameterSingularities` is contained in the dual nilpotent cone if either `L` has characteristic zero, or `ℓ ∤ q^{en} − 1` for every degree `e` of a homogeneous generator of the Chevalley invariants, where `n` is the residue degree of the extension cutting out the action. A covector fixed by the Tate-twisted adjoint action has every homogeneous invariant scaled by `q^{en}`, so all positive-degree invariants vanish when `q^{en} − 1` is invertible (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.2.11 with Remarks VIII.2.12–VIII.2.13, pp.284–285). **Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `ℓ ∤ q − 1` both the fibre and the cone are zero.
- At a smooth point of the `GL₂` parameter stack the fibre is zero while the nilpotent cone is not, so equality fails.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `ℓ | q − 1` the hypothesis fails and the fibre is not in the cone (LP1.13).

## Layer LP2e: coarse quotients, the excursion presentation and excursion operators

This part of LP2 builds the coarse quotient and the free-group presentation of the cocycle algebra with no restriction on `ℓ`, and the excursion operators of an abstract categorical Hecke action. Taking integral invariants of the derived presentation in `Ind Perf(BĤ)` is the later, stronger theorem LP2i.1. The connected integral quotient package and connected complete-reducibility and closed-tuple criteria come from ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11; their extensions and parameter applications are targets here. None of them uses LP3.

### LP2e.1 Coarse parameter quotients

For each finite-wild affine piece `X = Z¹(W, Ĥ)` with coordinate algebra `A` and gauge coaction `δ : A → O(Ĥ) ⊗ A`, use the coaction invariant subalgebra `A^Ĥ` supplied by Mathlib as specified under Exact supplier contracts, and define `ParameterCoarseQuotient = Spec A^Ĥ` with `quotientMap : X → X//Ĥ` induced by the inclusion. Prove the scheme-theoretic factorization statement (an invariant morphism to an affine scheme factors uniquely through `quotientMap`, transported by `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.homEquivAlgHom`), and `inflate` for inclusions of finite-wild pieces, with identity and composition laws and the orientation opposite to the coordinate map (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.3.1, Definition VIII.3.1 and Proposition VIII.3.2, pp.285–287; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Propositions 3.2 and 3.10, pp.11–12 and 15–16). Prove `flatBaseChange`: for an `R`-algebra `S`, the canonical map `S ⊗_R A^Ĥ → (S ⊗_R A)^{Ĥ_S}`, `s ⊗ a ↦ s ⊗ a`, exists always and is bijective when `S` is flat over `R`, by exactness of `S ⊗ −` on `0 → A^Ĥ → A → O(Ĥ) ⊗ A`. The quotient is formed piece by piece; the infinite union is not made affine, and finite type and orbit classification are LP2e.2. *Needs:* LP1.1; LP1.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1; Mathlib `AlgHom.equalizer`, `Subalgebra`, `Module.Flat.lTensor_exact`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = 1` the invariant algebra is all of `A` and `quotientMap` is the identity; for a coaction equal to `a ↦ 1 ⊗ a` (a torus acting trivially on a free-group cocycle space) the equalizer is `A`.
- For the scaling action of `G_m` over `𝔽₂` on `𝔽₂[x]`, the coaction invariants are `𝔽₂`, while the abstract group `G_m(𝔽₂) = 1` fixes everything: invariants must be taken for the coaction, not for rational points.
- For `C₂` acting on `ℤ[x]` by `x ↦ −x` the invariants are `ℤ[x²]`; after reduction mod `2` the element `x` is invariant but not in the image of `𝔽₂ ⊗ ℤ[x²]`, so the base change map exists and is not surjective for the non-flat `ℤ → 𝔽₂`.
- For `Γ = ℤ`, `Ĥ = SL₂` over a field of characteristic zero, the unipotent `U = [[1, 1], [0, 1]]` and the identity have the same image in the coarse quotient (the curve `[[1, t²], [0, 1]]` is conjugate to `U` by `diag(t, t⁻¹)` for `t ≠ 0`), although they are not conjugate.

### LP2e.2 Reductive quotients over fields and DVRs

Consume ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Reductive quotients over fields and DVRs*, for a connected reductive group over a field acting on an integral affine variety, and for a reductive group scheme over an excellent DVR `O` acting on an integral affine flat `O`-scheme of finite type. Use its finite generation, affine universal property, normality, closed-orbit classification, separation of invariant closed subsets and flat base change. Apply this package to the integral parameter pieces of LP1.

Extend finite generation, closed images and separation of invariant closed subsets, and the unique closed orbit in every geometric fibre, to arbitrary affine schemes of finite type over a field and flat affine schemes of finite type over an excellent DVR, allowing reducible and nonreduced schemes. Prove universal submersiveness of the quotient map in these cases; integrality and normality are asserted only under the corresponding hypotheses on `X`. These are the geometric-reductivity consequences of Haboush and Seshadri used in Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 3.2 and the proof of Proposition 3.10, pp.11–12 and 15–16, and Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.3.2, p.287. For a closed residual orbit through `x` and any invariant open `U` containing it, prove that some `f ∈ O(X)^G` has `f(x) ≠ 0`, `D_X(f) ⊂ U` and `D_X(f)//G ≅ D_{X//G}(f)` (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 3.10(v), p.16). The geometric-point bijection does not assert a non-flat invariant-algebra base change isomorphism. *Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Reductive quotients over fields and DVRs*; LP2e.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 6; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- For `G` acting trivially on `X` and on itself by left translation, `(G × X)//G ≅ X`.
- Base change from `O` to its fraction field is flat and commutes with invariants, while reduction to the residue field is not flat (LP2e.1's mod-`2` example).
- For `G_m` acting on `𝔸²` with weights `1` and `−1` the quotient is `𝔸¹` with coordinate `xy`, and the fibre over `0` has the single closed orbit `{0}`.
- For that action, the invariant open `U = D(xy − 1)` contains the closed orbit `{0}` and is already an invariant principal neighbourhood, with quotient `D_{𝔸¹}(t − 1)`.
- A trivial action on `Spec k[ε]/(ε²)` has the same nonreduced quotient; finite generation and the geometric-orbit assertions do not require an integral source.

### LP2e.3 Complete reducibility and strong reductivity

Use `IsGCompletelyReducible`, `IsAbsolutelyGCompletelyReducible`, their conjugacy invariance and the `GL(V)` semisimplicity comparison from ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*. For connected reductive `G` over algebraically closed `k` and closed `H ⊂ G`, define `IsGIrreducible` (no proper parabolic contains `H`) and `IsStronglyReductive` (for a maximal torus `S` of `Z_G(H)`, no proper parabolic of `Z_G(S)` contains `H`); prove independence of `S`. Apply these predicates to homomorphisms through the Zariski closure of their image, and over general fields after extension to an algebraic closure. Define `IsStronglyGIrreducible`: every homomorphism with the same one-variable invariant values is absolutely `G`-irreducible. Prove conjugacy invariance for these additional predicates and `IsGIrreducible.completelyReducible` (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, Definitions 3.3 and 3.5, pp.11–12).

For generalized reductive `G` over algebraically closed `k`, extend complete reducibility and irreducibility using all R-parabolics `P_λ = {g : lim_{t→0} λ(t)gλ(t)⁻¹ exists}` and R-Levis `L_λ = Z_G(λ)`, for cocharacters `λ` of `G⁰`. Extend strong reductivity by the same maximal-torus centralizer condition with R-parabolics in `Z_G(S)`; prove independence of `S` and conjugacy invariance. In particular, for `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` and arbitrary prescribed `η : Γ → Q`, complete reducibility tests every R-parabolic containing the image. Only if `η` is surjective do these containing parabolics necessarily surject onto `Q` (Bate–Martin–Röhrle 2005, §6; Quast, §2.5, Definition 2.10; Fargues–Scholze, p.287). *Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 7; Mathlib `Subgroup`, `Representation`.

**Checks.**

- The diagonal torus of `SL₂` is completely reducible, strongly reductive and absolutely completely reducible, and its inclusion is not strongly `SL₂`-irreducible: it is itself reducible.
- The upper unipotent subgroup of `SL₂` lies in the upper Borel and in no Levi. It is neither strongly reductive nor absolutely completely reducible, and its inclusion is not strongly `SL₂`-irreducible.
- For `GL(V)`, the Zariski image of a representation is completely reducible exactly when `V` is semisimple.
- The trivial subgroup is strongly reductive and absolutely completely reducible, and is irreducible only when `G` is a torus. Over `ℂ`, the identity representation of `SL₂(ℂ)` is strongly `SL₂`-irreducible: equality of one-variable invariants gives equality of traces, so its semisimplification remains the irreducible standard module.
- In `J = SL₂² ⋊ C₂` with the swapping action and trivial `η`, send two free generators to `(U,D)` and `(1,W)`, with `U = [[1,1],[0,1]]`, `D = diag(2,1/2)`, `W = [[0,−1],[1,0]]`. The second projection is irreducible, so no proper component-surjective parabolic contains the image, but `diag(t,t⁻¹)` in the first factor sends `U` to `1` in the limit. Its orbit is not closed, and the all-R-parabolic criterion excludes it.

### LP2e.4 Closed tuples and Levi semisimplification

For connected reductive `G` over algebraically closed `k` in any characteristic, consume ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*: the simultaneous orbit of `x ∈ G(k)ⁿ` is closed exactly when the subgroup generated by its entries has `G`-completely reducible Zariski closure `H`, and minimal-parabolic projection to a Levi gives a cocharacter limit in the unique closed orbit of the orbit closure, independent up to conjugacy of the choices. Prove the additional equivalence with strong reductivity of `H`, and that the orbit is stable (closed with stabilizer finite modulo `Z_G`) exactly when `H` is `G`-irreducible. Prove that a completely reducible `H` is irreducible in any Levi containing it of a minimal parabolic containing it, and that all minimal parabolics containing `H` have the same dimension (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, Theorem 3.4 and Proposition 3.7, pp.12–13, after Richardson 1988 and Bate–Martin–Röhrle 2005).

Extend the closed-tuple/strong-reductivity/complete-reducibility equivalence to generalized reductive `G`, with simultaneous conjugation by `G⁰(k)` and all R-parabolics and R-Levis of LP2e.3. Extend Levi semisimplification with uniqueness up to `G⁰(k)`-conjugacy, and prove that minimal containing R-parabolics have the same dimension and component count (Quast, §2.5, Proposition 2.15, and Lemmas 3.4–3.6, pp.10 and 12–13; Fargues–Scholze, proof of Proposition VIII.3.2, pp.286–287). No very-good-characteristic hypothesis is needed; separability is LP3.22. *Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*; LP2e.3; LP2e.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- The `SL₂` tuple `(U)` limits to the identity under `t ↦ diag(t, t⁻¹)`, and its orbit is not closed.
- A diagonal semisimple tuple has closed orbit.
- A tuple generating an irreducible subgroup of `GL_n` has a stable orbit with finite stabilizer modulo the centre.
- The tuple with trivial projection to `C₂` in `SL₂² ⋊ C₂` from LP2e.3 has a nonclosed `SL₂²`-orbit: its first unipotent coordinate has a cocharacter limit outside that orbit, even though no proper component-surjective parabolic contains the tuple.

### LP2e.5 Semisimple L-parameters

For an algebraically closed `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L`, a parameter into `Ĥ(L) ⋊ W_E` is semisimple (`IsSemisimpleParameter`) if every parabolic subgroup of the L-group containing its image has a Levi subgroup containing the image; parabolics project onto the acting group. Prove `leviCriterion` (equivalently, whenever a conjugate factors through a standard action-stable parabolic `P̂ ⋊ W_E`, it is `Ĥ(L)`-conjugate to its Levi projection), `gauge` (invariance under gauge), and `GL` (for split `GL_n` it agrees with semisimplicity of the linear representation). For arbitrary `Γ → Q`, use all R-parabolics and R-Levis of LP2e.3, without requiring the parabolics to surject onto `Q`. For the full Weil parameter, `Q` is its actual quotient, so every containing R-parabolic does surject (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.3.1, Definition VIII.3.1, p.286). *Needs:* LP2e.3; LP0.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- Every parameter into a torus is semisimple, since a torus has no proper parabolic.
- A direct sum of characters into `GL_n` is semisimple.
- A nontrivial unipotent representation of `ℤ` in `SL₂` is not semisimple, although its invariant values agree with those of the trivial representation.

### LP2e.6 Closed parameter orbits

Prove that for every finite-wild piece over an algebraically closed `L`, the closed `Ĥ(L)`-orbits of cocycles are exactly the semisimple parameters, equivalently those conjugate to the Levi projection of every factorization through a standard parabolic; hence the `L`-points of the coarse quotient classify semisimple parameters up to conjugacy, with no condition on `π₁(Ĥ)_tors`. The proof applies the Hilbert–Mumford–Kempf criterion to the finite-generator presentation: for a dominant `λ` and `g ⋊ τ`, the limit of `λ(t) g λ(t)^{−τ}` exists if and only if `g` lies in the parabolic of `λ` and `λ^τ = λ`, and the limit is the Levi projection; then Richardson's criterion in the non-connected form (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.2 and Lemma VIII.3.3 with proofs, pp.286–287, citing Kempf 1978 Corollary 3.5 and Bate–Martin–Röhrle 2005 §6). *Needs:* LP2e.5; LP2e.1; LP2e.2; LP2e.4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For a nonsplit action the condition `λ^τ = λ` is needed: omitting it gives a limit that is not a Levi projection.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action every orbit is a point and closed. With inversion action of `Q = C₂`, `Γ = ℤ` and nontrivial generator projection, gauge sends `c` to `h²c`; over an algebraically closed field its orbit is all `G_m` and is closed.
- The `SL₂` unipotent parameter of `ℤ` has non-closed orbit and its closed orbit is the trivial parameter.

### LP2e.7 Finite free-group indexing

Use the index category of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *General invariant-word colimit*: objects `(n, u : F_n → Γ)`, including `n = 0`, and word maps `v : F_n → F_m` with `u′ ∘ v = u`. Free products give coproducts; prove that this supplied category is sifted. For `Γ → Q`, extend its untwisted coordinate diagram to `coordinateDiagram` for crossed cocycles: at `(n,u)` use `T_a = O(Ĥⁿ)` with action pulled back along `u`, and send each generator coordinate along `v` to the corresponding coordinate of the universal crossed cocycle evaluated at `v(x_i)`. Prove compatibility with gauge coactions and coefficient change. On a product word `x_i x_j` this uses `c_b(x_i) · u(x_i)(c_b(x_j))`. At finite-wild `Γ = W`, this is exactly the free-word cocone supplied by SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3; at trivial action it is the diagram supplied by IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Untwisted excursion comparison*. Both identifications fix every generator coordinate and hence every word map (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.3.2, p.287). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *General invariant-word colimit*, *Untwisted excursion comparison*; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra*; LP0.1; Mathlib `FreeGroup`, `FreeGroup.lift`.

**Checks.**

- The zero-generator object is initial: there is exactly one homomorphism from the trivial free group over `Γ`.
- The coproduct of an `n`- and an `m`-generator tuple is the `(n + m)`-generator concatenation.
- A word map `F_n → F_m` induces `O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ)) → O(Z¹(F_m, Ĥ))`, not the reverse ring map.
- For a nonsplit action, the substitution of `x₁x₂` multiplies `c(x₁)` by the twisted `u(x₁)(c(x₂))`; the untwisted product gives a different map.

### LP2e.8 Derived free-cocycle presentations

For a finite-wild `W`, prove that the natural map `colim_{(n, F_n → W)} O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ)) → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` is an isomorphism in `D(ℤ_ℓ)`, indeed of animated `ℤ_ℓ`-algebras: the colimit is the universal animated algebra with a `W`-cocycle, its `π₀` is the cocycle algebra, and LP1.8 forces it to be classical (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.5 and proof, p.288). No condition on `π₁(Ĥ)_tors` is needed. The isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)` is LP2i.1 and is not a consequence. *Needs:* LP2e.7; LP1.8; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *animation*, *presentability*.

**Checks.**

- For `W = F_n` the identity object is terminal and the map is the identity.
- For `W = 1` both sides are `ℤ_ℓ`.
- This underlying isomorphism does not by itself let rational `Ĥ`-invariants commute with the colimit in characteristic `ℓ`.

### LP2e.9 Excursion algebras of a discrete group over a finite quotient

For finite-wild `W`, use `Exc(W, Ĥ)` and `compare : Exc(W, Ĥ) → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))^Ĥ` from SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra* and *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*. These scalar comparisons have their proof owner in SR.6.3; LP2i.1 supplies the enhanced refinement and identifies its scalar map with this `compare`.

Extend to arbitrary discrete `Γ → Q` using LP2e.7's twisted diagram: `ExcursionAlgebra Γ Ĥ = colim_{(n,F_n → Γ)} O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ))^Ĥ`, with `ofFree`, `lift`, `lift_ofFree`, and `mapGroup` over `Q` with identity and composition laws. For finitely presented `Γ`, construct `compare` by evaluation on the universal `Γ`-cocycle. Identify the finite-wild specialization with the supplied SR.6.3 algebra by its colimit property, fixing every `ofFree` and `compare`; it uses that carrier. When `Q = 1`, use the general untwisted colimit and its maps from IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7. No universal-homeomorphism assertion is made for arbitrary `Γ` (Fargues–Scholze, Definition VIII.3.4 and §VIII.3.2, pp.287–288). *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra*, *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *General invariant-word colimit*, *Untwisted excursion comparison*; LP2e.7; LP2e.1; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Limits.HasColimits CommRingCat`.

### LP2e.10 The universal excursion relations

### LP2e.11 Continuous torsion-free excursion characters

Prove that the ℓ-torsion-free quotient `Exc(W, Ĥ)_tf` is flat over `ℤ_ℓ` and represents, on flat test algebras `A`, the families `Θ_n` of LP2e.10 that are maps of condensed sets on `(W_E/P)ⁿ` (with `A` relatively discrete over `ℤ_ℓ`); that it is canonically independent of the dense discrete `W`; that inflation of finite-wild pieces is compatible with evaluation and with the comparison map; and that geometric field-valued characters are unchanged by removing ℓ-power torsion, which is nilpotent (Fargues–Scholze, the discussion after Proposition VIII.3.7, pp.289–290). Continuity is imposed on `W_E/P`, not on a profinite topology of the discrete `W`. Independence of the full `Exc(W, Ĥ)` at bad primes is not claimed. *Needs:* LP2e.10; LP2e.9; LP0.4; LP2e.1; Mathlib `Module.Flat`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action and tame `W`, conjugation is trivial and `Exc(W, Ĥ) = O(G_m × μ_{q−1})`, which is already ℓ-torsion-free, so the quotient changes nothing.
- Flatness over `ℤ_ℓ` is torsion-freeness.
- The `𝔽̄_ℓ`-points of `Exc(W, Ĥ)` and of `Exc(W, Ĥ)_tf` coincide because the removed torsion is nilpotent.

### LP2e.12 Categorical Hecke data

For a `ℤ_ℓ`-linear category `C`, a categorical Hecke datum (`CategoricalHeckeDatum`) for `Γ → Q` assigns to every finite set `I` a `Rep(Q^I)`-linear monoidal functor `Rep((Ĥ ⋊ Q)^I) → End(C)^{BΓ^I}`, natural and coherent in maps of finite sets, with the unit and diagonal (fusion) identifications as part of the data; for a stable ∞-category the functors are exact and coherent, and the centre is `π₀ End(id_C)` (its ordinary shadow is `CategoryTheory.CatCenter`). Provide `unit` (the trivial representation acts as the identity), `reindex` (finite-set pullback and fusion commute coherently with the `Γ`-action), `create` (a diagonal-invariant `α : 1 → V` gives `id_C → T_V`) and `annihilate` (a diagonal-invariant `β : V → 1` gives `T_V → id_C`) (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.4, setup before Theorem VIII.4.1, pp.290–291). `C` is abstract: no `Bun_G` enters. *Needs:* ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategory`, `CategoryTheory.Functor.Monoidal`, `CategoryTheory.CatCenter`.

**Checks.**

- For `I = ∅` the unit object acts as the identity with trivial `Γ^∅`-action.
- Folding `I ⊔ I → I` identifies the external tensor product action with the tensor product action.
- The zero stable category has a unique datum, and every excursion operator on it is zero.

### LP2e.13 Excursion data

An excursion datum `ExcursionDatum` is `D = (I, V, α, β, (γ_i)_{i ∈ I})` with `I` finite, `V` a finite projective representation of `(Ĥ ⋊ Q)^I`, `α : 1 → V` and `β : V → 1` invariant under the diagonal `Ĥ`, and `γ_i ∈ Γ`. For a categorical Hecke datum define `operator D = T_β ∘ (γ_i)_i ∘ T_α`, a natural endomorphism of `id_C` and so an element of `π₀ End(id_C)`. Prove `reindex` (for `f : I → J` and a tuple `γ : J → Γ`, with `p_f : (Ĥ ⋊ Q)^J → (Ĥ ⋊ Q)^I` given by `(g_j) ↦ (g_{f(i)})`, the operator of `p_f^*V` on the `J`-tuple `γ` equals that of `V` on the `I`-tuple `γ ∘ f`; the compatibility of tuples is a hypothesis) and `tensor` (external tensor product on `I ⊔ J` with `α ⊗ α′`, `β ⊗ β′` and the concatenated tuple multiplies operators) (Fargues–Scholze, Definition VIII.4.2, p.291). Use the representation tuples, coefficient functions and coefficient-change maps of SR.6.3; construct their categorical operators on the same coefficients. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra*; LP2e.9; LP2e.12; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `Representation`, `Representation.tprod`, `Module.Finite`, `Module.Projective`.

**Checks.**

- For the unit representation with `α = β = id`, the operator is the identity.
- If `α = 0` the operator is zero.
- For an external tensor product the operator is the product `S_D S_{D′}`, and the product of scalar coefficients `2` and `3` is `6`, not `5`.

### LP2e.14 Excursion matrix coefficients

Use the coefficient function of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3 for `(I, V, α, β)`, written `excursionMatrixCoefficient`: `f_D((h_i, q_i)_i) = β((h_i, q_i)_i · α)`, with its supplied bi-invariance and tensor identity. Prove the categorical comparison `eval`, `canonicalPresentation` (the finite projective subrepresentation `V_f` of the regular functions generated by `f`, with `α_f = f` and `β_f` evaluation at the unit, presents the same operator), `operatorIndependent` (equal coefficient functions give equal operators for a fixed `I` and tuple), `reindex` (pullback of `f` matches reindexing of the datum) and `tensor` (on a concatenated tuple the coefficient of `D ⊗ D′` is `f_D(g) f_{D′}(g′)`) (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.4.1, pp.291–292). The reindexing square commutes but is not in general a pullback. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra*; LP2e.9; LP2e.13; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `V = 1` and `α = β = id` the coefficient is `1`; for `α = 0` it is `0`.
- The coefficient of an external tensor product is the product of the coefficients, so scalar coefficients `2` and `3` give `6`.
- For diagonal `a, b ∈ Ĥ`, `f_D(a g_i b) = f_D(g_i)`; a coefficient built from a non-invariant `α` fails this.
- An external tensor product with tuple entries `2` and `3` in `Γ = ℤ` keeps the legs in order: for two different characters on the two legs, exchanging the entries changes the value.

### LP2e.15 Abstract excursion centre maps

Prove that every categorical Hecke datum for `Γ` over `Q` on a `ℤ_ℓ`-linear stable ∞-category `C` induces a natural `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra map `Exc(Γ, Ĥ) → π₀ End(id_C)` sending an invariant function evaluated at a tuple to its excursion operator. Operator independence comes from LP2e.14; external tensors and folding give multiplicativity and the trivial representation the unit; inserting an extra coordinate over `1 ∈ Q` identifies the bi-invariant functions with the simultaneous-conjugation invariants on `n` coordinates; reindexing and the identity `γγ′⁻¹γ″` from evaluation and coevaluation give the relations of LP2e.10 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.4.1 and Definition VIII.4.2 with proofs, pp.290–293; Lafforgue, Lemma 10.1 and Proposition 10.8, pp.133–135 and 138–139). No condition on `ℓ` enters, and the Bernstein-centre application belongs to the consumers. For an ordinary category the target is `End(id_C)`. *Needs:* LP2e.14; LP2e.10; LP2e.12; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.CatCenter`.

**Checks.**

- The zero category gives the zero map into its zero centre.
- The unit datum maps to `1`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `Γ = ℤ` the map sends `t` to the operator of the standard character at the generator.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = SL₂` and `Γ = ℤ` in characteristic zero the unipotent and the trivial parameter have the same coarse image and the same excursion character; only the trivial one is semisimple. For `Ĥ = G_m` the excursion algebra of `ℤ` is `ℤ_ℓ[t, t⁻¹]` and every orbit is closed.

## Layer LP2c: pseudocharacters with prescribed projection and local continuity

This part of LP2 reconstructs semisimple parameters from invariant evaluations. The group is `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q`, whose component group may be nontrivial, so the reconstruction theorem is proved here for groups with reductive identity component, using only the unconditional invariant theory of LP2e. Algebraic reconstruction, discrete continuity and the relatively discrete characteristic-zero extension are separate statements. The trace adapter is a comparison on the group algebra, not a replacement for the full invariant families.

### LP2c.0 Algebra trace pseudocharacters and determinant laws

Use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 for an associative unital `A`-algebra `R` and `d ≥ 0`: *Pseudocharacters* supplies linear traces with `T(1)=d`, cyclic symmetry and the signed degree-`d+1` cycle identity, including fixed one-cycles; *Determinants (Chenevier)* supplies multiplicative homogeneous polynomial laws of degree `d`, natural on all commutative coefficient algebras; *Characteristic polynomial and trace of a determinant* fixes the trace as the coefficient of `t` in `D(1+tx)`. Use its coefficient change, *Determinant of a finite projective representation* for constant rank `d`, and *Over ℚ-algebras determinants are pseudocharacters* for the rational trace–determinant bijection. LP2c.6 extends these supplied traces to the group-algebra adapter; LP2c.7 compares that adapter with the supplied connected `GL_d` invariant families (Chenevier, arXiv:0809.0415v2, §§1.1–1.5, 1.10–1.12 and Proposition 1.27, pp.6–9, 12–14, 20). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§0.2, 0.5–0.6, the named targets above.

**Checks.**

- A degree-one determinant is an algebra homomorphism; a degree-zero determinant is the constant law `1` with trace zero.
- For `d = 2` over a `ℚ`-algebra, `D(x) = (T(x)²−T(x²))/2`; the trace of the identity is `2`.
- Over `𝔽₂`, the zero trace occurs for both rank zero and the trivial rank-two representation, whereas their determinant laws have different degrees; no trace-only reconstruction is asserted there.

### LP2c.1 Pseudocharacters with prescribed finite projection

Let `G` be a generalized reductive group scheme over a noetherian ring `O`: smooth affine, with reductive identity component `G⁰` and finite étale `G/G⁰` (Quast, Definition 2.3, p.6); the case used is `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` over `ℤ_ℓ`, with `J⁰ = Ĥ` and `J/J⁰ = Q` constant even when `ℓ` divides `|Q|`. A `G`-pseudocharacter of a group `Γ` over a commutative `O`-algebra `A` is a family of `O`-algebra maps `Θ_m : O[G^m]^{G⁰} → Map(Γ^m, A)`, `m ≥ 1`, compatible with reindexing along maps of finite sets and with multiplication of the last two coordinates (Quast, Definition 3.1, p.11). Use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Connected reductive pseudocharacter* and *Universal reductive pseudocharacter ring*, for connected `G`, including arity zero as the coefficient map. Extend these same families and their representing construction to generalized reductive `G` using `G⁰`-invariants; whole-`G` invariants do not give this extension. Prove that for connected `G` the extension and universal algebra identify with the supplied ones on every evaluation, and that `Q = 1` recovers those carriers. For nontrivial `Q`, impose the component equations as a quotient of this extended universal algebra, with its representing bijection. For fixed `η : Γ → Q`, define `ProjectedPseudocharacter η A` as the `J`-pseudocharacters satisfying, for every `n` and `q̄ ∈ Qⁿ`, `Θ_n(e_{q̄})(γ̄) = 1` if `η(γ̄) = q̄` and `0` otherwise, where `e_{q̄} ∈ O[Jⁿ]^Ĥ` is the component idempotent. Provide `forget` (to all `J`-pseudocharacters; injective), `ofLift` (the pseudocharacter of a lift `ρ : Γ → J(A)` with `π ∘ ρ = η`, evaluating invariants on `ρ`), `component_eval`, `map` (coefficient change preserves `η`, with identity and composition laws, and asserts no non-flat invariant base change), `precomp` (precomposition with `f : Γ′ → Γ` has projection `η ∘ f`), `ext` (equality of all `Θ_m`), and `IsContinuous` (continuity of every evaluation, with `η` continuous into the finite discrete `Q`) (Quast, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.5, pp.11–13; Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.7 and §VIII.3.3, pp.288–290; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Definition 4.1 and Lemma 4.3, pp.19–20). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Connected reductive pseudocharacter*, *Universal reductive pseudocharacter ring*; LP2e.10; LP2e.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Q = 1` and `Ĥ = G_m`, a character `χ` has `Θ_n(t₁^{a₁} ⋯ t_n^{a_n})(γ̄) = ∏ χ(γ_i)^{a_i}`, negative exponents included, and these families are exactly the characters `Γ → Aˣ`.
- `Ĥ`-conjugate lifts with the same `η` define the same element.
- For `Γ = 1` the trivial lift evaluates the identity component idempotent to `1` and the others to `0`.
- Over a nonzero ring, a lift with a different projection is not in the `η`-fibre: some component idempotent would have to evaluate to both `0` and `1`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m`, `Q = C₂` acting by inversion, `Γ = ℤ` and `η = 1`, the lifts with generator `2` and `1/2` are not `Ĥ`-conjugate and are separated by the identity-component coordinate `x` extended by zero, while every function invariant under conjugation by all of `J` takes the same values on them: invariants under `J` instead of `J⁰` would identify them.

### LP2c.2 Reconstruction for groups with reductive identity component

Extend reconstruction to generalized reductive groups, using the supplied connected pseudocharacter objects: for `G` generalized reductive over a noetherian `O`, any group `Γ`, an algebraically closed `O`-field `k` and `Θ ∈ PC_G^Γ(k)`, there is a `G`-completely reducible `ρ : Γ → G(k)` with `Θ_ρ = Θ`, unique up to `G⁰(k)`-conjugacy, with complete reducibility tested against all R-parabolics and R-Levis as in LP2e.3 (Quast, Theorem 3.7 and proof, pp.13–15; for split connected `G` over `ℤ`, Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Theorem 4.5, p.20). Prove it through the following statements, each a target. (i) Closed representatives: each point of `(Gⁿ//G⁰)(k)` has exactly one closed `G⁰(k)`-orbit above it, and the Zariski closure of the group generated by a closed tuple is `G`-completely reducible (Quast, Lemmas 3.4–3.5, p.12, from LP2e.2 and LP2e.4 in the non-connected form). (ii) The dimension and component count of a minimal parabolic containing such a subgroup do not depend on the parabolic (Quast, Lemma 3.6, p.13). (iii) An extremal tuple: some `δ ∈ Γⁿ` maximizes first the dimension and then the component count of the minimal parabolic, and among those minimizes first the dimension and then the component count of the centralizer (Quast, conditions (1)–(4) in the proof of Theorem 3.7, p.13). (iv) One-entry extension: with `g` the closed representative of `δ`, for each `γ` there is a unique `h ∈ G(k)` with `(g, h)` conjugate to the closed representative of `(δ, γ)` (Claim A, p.14). (v) Two-entry extension and multiplicativity: the analogous statement for `(δ, γ, γ′)`, the closedness of the orbits of `(g, h)`, `(g, h′)` and `(g, hh′)`, and hence `ρ(γγ′) = ρ(γ)ρ(γ′)`, `ρ(1) = 1` and `Θ_ρ = Θ` (Claims B–C and the end of the proof, pp.14–15). Then specialise to `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` with prescribed `η`: the one-variable component idempotents force `π ∘ ρ = η`, the `Ĥ`-coordinate of `ρ` is a crossed cocycle for the action through `η`, `Ĥ`-conjugation of the lift is gauge of the cocycle, and the section equivalence of LP0.1 makes the two constructions inverse. So the `η`-fibre of `J`-pseudocharacters over `k` is in bijection with `Ĥ(k)`-gauge classes of semisimple crossed cocycles `Γ → Ĥ(k)` (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.8 and proof, p.290). For `G = GL_d`, `d ≥ 1`, use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§1.2 and 1.7 for reconstruction. Prove that the generalized reconstruction specializes to that same conjugacy class by uniqueness and the supplied §0.7 determinant comparison, preserving every characteristic coefficient. The connected non-`GL_d` and nonconnected cases are included in this general target. No good-`π₁` or prime-to-`|Q|` hypothesis enters; its invariant theory is unconditional. *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Determinants and GLn excursion pseudocharacters*, §1.2, *Semisimple reconstruction of field determinants*, §1.7, *GL pseudocharacter reconstruction*; LP2c.1; LP2e.2; LP2e.4; LP2e.5; LP0.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Q = 1` and `G = GL_d`, the resulting conjugacy class is the supplied reconstruction; its determinant and all characteristic coefficients agree in every characteristic.
- For `Ĥ = 1` only the chosen `η` survives, so allowing every `Γ → Q` would give the wrong space.
- For `G = GL_r` in small characteristic the full invariant family keeps the multiplicities of the semisimplification while traces alone do not.
- For the unipotent representation of `ℤ` in `SL₂` the reconstructed representation is the trivial one.

### LP2c.3 Relatively discrete Weil continuity

For an algebraically closed `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L` of characteristic zero and the semisimple lift `W_E → Ĥ(L) ⋊ Q` reconstructed in LP2c.2, prove that condensed invariant evaluations in the relatively discrete convention make the lift a condensed L-parameter (LP0.2); for `L` with a rank-one valuation topology this is the usual continuity. Only compact inertia needs an argument; the degree quotient is discrete. The argument takes the extremal tuple of LP2c.2 with its last entry in inertia, expresses the finitely many coordinates of the reconstructed element as continuous functions of invariant evaluations, extends this to `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` with `Ĥ`-conjugation, and proves that these coordinate functions take values locally in finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-modules; the crossed cocycle law and the fixed `Q`-action transport the conclusion from inertia to its open Weil cosets (Lafforgue, Proposition 11.7, p.143, and its continuity argument, pp.146–147; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 4.7(ii), pp.23–24, connected and profinite; Quast, Theorem 3.8, p.15; Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1 and Proposition VIII.3.8, pp.278 and 290). The finite-type coefficient statement is part of this target; it does not follow from rank-one valued continuity alone, and the compactness argument of Quast's Theorem 3.8 for profinite `Γ` is not the extension to the Weil group. *Needs:* LP2c.2; LP0.2; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LP2c.1 (`IsContinuous`).

**Checks.**

- An unramified character with `ρ(Fr) = q` has infinite Weil image and trivial inertia image, and its coordinates on each compact inertia coset lie in a rank-one `ℤ_ℓ`-module.
- A split torus Weil character must satisfy `χ(τ)^{q−1} = 1`, so the restriction of an arbitrary infinite-image inertia character need not extend to the Weil group.
- For `Q = 1` and profinite `Γ` the rank-one valued specialization is the continuity assertion of Lafforgue, Proposition 11.7. The non-semisimple `GL₂` parameter of LP0.2 witnesses infinite inertia in the ambient condensed convention, without being an example of semisimple reconstruction.

### LP2c.4 Discrete-coefficient Weil continuity

Let `L` be an algebraically closed discrete `ℤ_ℓ`-field and `η : W_E → Q` the continuous action quotient. Prove that a semisimple lift reconstructed from continuous invariant evaluations has open kernel and finite image on inertia and is continuous on `W_E`; in characteristic `ℓ` this is exactly the relatively discrete convention, and the Frobenius image need not be finite. The proof chooses finitely many generators of the integral tuple-invariant algebra (finite generation from LP2e.2, with the component idempotents among them), uses the extremal tuple and the unique one-entry extension of LP2c.2 to show that on an open inertia neighbourhood where all generator values equal their values at `1` the lift is `1`, and transports continuity to `W_E` by the homomorphism law (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 4.7(iii) and proof, pp.23–24; Quast, Theorem 3.7, Claim A, p.14). The topological step is a target in its own right: for an open compact subgroup `I` of a topological group `W`, a homomorphism `ρ : W → J` and functions `f_1, …, f_n` from `J` to a discrete space with each `i ↦ f_j(ρ(i))` continuous on `I` and with `f_j(ρ(i)) = f_j(1)` for all `j` implying `ρ(i) = 1`, the kernel of `ρ|_I` is open (`inertia_kernel_open`), `ρ(I)` is finite (`finite_inertia_image`, using compactness) and `ρ` is continuous when `J` is discrete (`continuous_lift`, using openness of `I`). Non-flat integral invariants are not replaced by fibrewise invariants, and there is no good-`π₁` or prime-to-`|Q|` condition. *Needs:* LP2c.2; LP2e.2; ClassFieldTheory layer 9.

**Checks.**

- The unit homomorphism has the whole inertia subgroup as kernel and image `{1}`; with no coordinate functions, the separation hypothesis forces `ρ|_I = 1`.
- A constant coordinate on `C₂` is continuous but does not separate the nontrivial element from `1`: the separation hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- On the discrete degree group `ℤ`, `k ↦ 2^k` into `ℚˣ` is continuous, sends geometric Frobenius to `2`, is trivial on inertia and has infinite image: finite image holds on inertia only.
- For `Q = 1` and discrete `L`, the statement agrees on inertia with the connected profinite theorem.

### LP2c.6 Group-algebra trace adapter

For a group `Γ`, a commutative ring `A` and `r ≥ 0`, specialise LP2c.0's algebra trace pseudocharacters to `A[Γ]`. Define `GroupTraceAdapter.restrict`, sending a pseudocharacter `T` to `γ ↦ T([γ])`, and `GroupTraceAdapter.extend`, the unique `A`-linear extension `T(∑ a_γ [γ]) = ∑ a_γ τ(γ)` of a function `τ : Γ → A` using `Finsupp.linearCombination`. Prove `equiv` (restriction and extension are inverse bijections between pseudocharacters on `A[Γ]` and functions with `τ(1) = r`, `τ(xy) = τ(yx)` and the signed cycle identity on group elements, fixed one-cycles included), `cycleValue` (the cycle term on basis vectors is the group-word cycle term), `ofRepresentation` (the restriction of the trace of a representation is `γ ↦ tr ρ(γ)` using the supplied trace), and `map` (coefficient change commutes with both conversions) (Lafforgue, Remark 11.8, pp.143–144). The alternating identity is multilinear, so checking it on basis vectors suffices. The linear extension is uniquely determined by these basis values, and cyclic symmetry on group elements is equivalent to cyclic symmetry on the whole group algebra. No classification is asserted in small characteristic. *Needs:* LP2c.0; Mathlib `MonoidAlgebra`, `MonoidAlgebra.of`, `Finsupp.linearCombination`, `Equiv.Perm.cycleFactorsFinset`.

**Checks.**

- For `r = 1` the identity is `τ(x)τ(y) = τ(xy)`, so normalised rank-one pseudocharacters are characters; for `r = 0` the identity forces `τ = 0`.
- The trace of a direct sum of characters is a pseudocharacter of the sum of their ranks; the constant function `1` is not of rank two in characteristic zero because `τ(1)` must be `2`.
- For distinct `γ, δ`, `extend τ (2[γ] − [δ]) = 2τ(γ) − τ(δ)`.
- For `A = ℚ`, `Γ = 1` and `τ = 2`, `extend τ (1 · 1) = 2` while `extend τ 1 · extend τ 1 = 4`: the extension is linear, not multiplicative.

## Layer LP3: good filtrations, fixed groups and wild reduction

LP3 works over an algebraically closed field `L` of characteristic `ℓ`, in the stable ∞-category `Ind Perf(BG)`, where `G` plays the role of the dual group and its subgroups. It first states the highest-weight theory it needs, then builds the good-filtration t-structure, induced perfect complexes and their criteria, generation for adjoint and free-group quotients and for derived unit fibres, the theory of fixed groups of solvable groups of order prime to `ℓ`, and the gerbe comparisons that eliminate wild inertia. Its last four targets are the separability, étale-descent, slice and Chevalley-restriction inputs used by the integral theory.

### LP3.0 Highest-weight modules and good filtrations

Let `G` be a split reductive group over a field `k`, with a Borel subgroup `B` and maximal torus `T`, and let `λ` run over dominant characters of `T`. Define the induced module `∇_λ = H⁰(λ) = ind_B^G λ` (global sections of the line bundle `O(λ)` on `G/B`) and the Weyl module `Δ_λ = V(λ) = H⁰(−w₀λ)^*`, and prove that `H⁰(λ) ≠ 0` exactly when `λ` is dominant (Jantzen, Part II, 2.6, p.178, and 2.13, p.182). Consume SchubertGeometry S3.2 and S5.5–S5.6 for the section/induced-module comparison and dominant flag-line-bundle vanishing over fields. Construct the rational-group-cohomology comparison to deduce `H^i(G, ∇_λ) = 0` for `i > 0`, and prove that `H^i(G, ∇_λ ⊗ ∇_μ)` is `k` for `i = 0`, `μ = −w₀λ` and zero otherwise (Jantzen, II.4.5, p.204, and II.4.13, p.209; Kempf 1976). Define a good filtration of a `G`-module `V` as an exhaustive ascending filtration whose subquotients are direct sums of modules `∇_λ` (`HasGoodFiltration`), and prove Donkin's criterion: with the direct-sum version of the filtration, `V` has a good filtration if and only if `Ext¹_G(Δ_λ, V) = 0` for all `λ`, if and only if `H^i(G, V ⊗ ∇_λ) = 0` for all `i > 0` and all `λ` (Jantzen, II.4.16, Proposition and Remark 1, pp.210–211; Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.3, p.294, citing Donkin 1981). Define the good filtration dimension of `V`, the least `i` with `H^j(G, V ⊗ ∇_λ) = 0` for all `j > i` and all `λ`, equivalently the length of a resolution by modules with good filtrations (Jantzen, II.4.17, remark, p.212; Fargues–Scholze, p.294). Prove the tensor product theorem of Donkin and Mathieu: tensor products of modules with good filtrations have good filtrations (Jantzen, II.4.21, p.213; Fargues–Scholze, p.294); Koppinen and Donkin's theorem that `O(G)` under left and right multiplication has a good `G × G`-filtration with factors `∇_λ ⊗ ∇_{−w₀λ}` (Jantzen, II.4.20, p.213; Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.6, p.295); and the theorem of Touzé–van der Kallen: if `G` acts on a finitely generated commutative algebra `A` with a good filtration and compatibly on a noetherian `A`-module `M`, then `M` has finite good filtration dimension and `H^i(G, M)` vanishes for large `i` (Touzé–van der Kallen, arXiv:0809.1014v3, Theorem 1.5, p.3, cited by Fargues–Scholze as Corollary 1.5 of the published version, p.297). For `G` whose identity component is reductive with component group of order prime to `ℓ`, all these notions are taken for `G°`. These are the general highest-weight inputs; their applications to parameters are the rest of LP3. *Needs:* SchubertGeometry S3.2 and S5.5–S5.6; ReductiveGroups layer 9; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `G = GL₂` with the lower Borel, `∇_{(1,0)}` is the standard representation, `∇_{(0,−1)}` its dual, and `∇_{(0,1)} = 0`: a definition with the opposite Borel or the inverse character makes `(0, 1)` the nonzero case.
- For a torus every module is a direct sum of characters, every `∇_λ` is one-dimensional and every module has a good filtration of dimension `0`.
- For `G = SL₂` in characteristic `2`, the module `∇_2` has a good filtration but its submodule `L(2)` (the Frobenius twist of the standard representation) does not, because `Ext¹_G(Δ_0, L(2)) ≠ 0`: good filtrations are not inherited by submodules.
- For a positive-dimensional torus, `O(G) = k[X^*(G)]` under conjugation is an infinite direct sum of copies of `∇_0` and has a one-step good filtration under the direct-sum convention. Requiring finitely many finite-dimensional factors would exclude it. For `G = 1`, `O(G) = k` has a one-step good filtration.

### LP3.1 Good-filtration t-structures

Over algebraically closed `L` of characteristic `ℓ`, for `G` with reductive identity component and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, construct `goodFiltrationTStructure` on `Ind Perf(BG)`: `M` is connective when `H^i(G°, M ⊗ ∇_λ) = 0` for all `i > 0` and all `λ` (good filtration dimension at most `0`), coconnective when `H^i(G°, M ⊗ Δ_λ) = 0` for all `i < 0`; for connected `G`, the connective part is generated under colimits by `∇_λ`, and for possibly disconnected `G` by `Ind_{G°}^G ∇_λ`. Construct finite-index induction and its adjunction with restriction. Prove the generator comparison with these restriction-to-`G°` cohomology tests: restriction of an induced module is a finite sum of conjugate induced highest-weight modules, while averaging over `π₀G` splits the counit `Ind Res M → M` (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.5.1 and Definition VIII.5.4, pp.294–295). Prove `connective_iff`, `coconnective_iff`, `tensorConnective` (tensor products of connective objects are connective, by LP3.0's tensor theorem) and `homotopy` (its homotopy category carries a `CategoryTheory.Triangulated.TStructure`, which does not recover the enhancement). In general `Perf(BG)` need not be stable under good-filtration truncations; for tori it is stable. *Needs:* LP3.0; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*, *presentability*; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Triangulated.TStructure`.

**Checks.**

- For a torus every rational module is a sum of weights, and the good-filtration t-structure is the usual one.
- The zero object lies in both halves; for connected `G`, `∇_λ` in degree zero is connective.
- For `G = C₂` over `𝔽̄₃`, `G° = 1`: the sign representation is connective but is not generated by the trivial representation. `Ind_1^{C₂} 1 = 1 ⊕ sign` supplies both components.
- For a nonzero torus representation `V`, the object `V[−1]` sits in cohomological degree `1` and is not connective for the convention `D^{≤0}`.

### LP3.2 Separatedness of good filtrations

Prove that the good-filtration t-structure on `Ind Perf(BG)` is separated: an object that is infinitely connective is zero, since all `RΓ(G°, Res_{G°} M ⊗ ∇_λ)` then vanish and these detect zero, using finite-index induction and its adjunction from LP3.1 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.5 and proof, p.295). For groups with infinite rational cohomological dimension, the standard t-structure on `Ind Perf(BG)` can fail to be separated. For tori it is separated and agrees with the good-filtration t-structure; separatedness of the good-filtration one is what justifies the later bar-construction limits. *Needs:* LP3.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- An object whose underlying module vanishes in an Ind-quotient is not thereby zero: detection uses `∇_λ` for connected `G` and `Ind_{G°}^G ∇_λ` for disconnected `G`.
- For a torus separatedness is that of the standard t-structure.
- For connected `G`, a nonzero `∇_λ` is not infinitely connective because `H⁰(G, ∇_λ ⊗ ∇_{−w₀λ}) = L`.

### LP3.3 Good filtrations of free cocycle algebras

Prove that for every map `F_n → Aut(G)`, the algebra `O(Z¹(F_n, G)) = O(Gⁿ)` with twisted diagonal `G°`-conjugation has a good `G°`-filtration: `O(Gⁿ)` has a good `G^{2n}`-filtration by LP3.0, and restriction to the twisted diagonal preserves good filtrations by the tensor theorem. Hence its higher rational `G°`-cohomology vanishes, and so does its higher `G`-cohomology when `|π₀G|` is prime to `ℓ` (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.6 and Corollary VIII.5.7, p.295). *Needs:* LP3.0; LP3.1; LP1.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `F₀` the algebra is `L` with the trivial filtration.
- For `F₁` and trivial action it is `O(G)` under conjugation.
- For a torus every rational module splits into characters, hence has a good filtration, and higher rational cohomology vanishes. For `G_m`, `0 ⊂ L ⊂ L ⊕ L(1)` is a nontrivial good filtration.

### LP3.4 Perfect complexes generated from the classifying stack

For `X = Spec A` of finite type (possibly derived) with an algebraic `G`-action, define `InducedPerfectComplexes X G = Perf^ind(X/G)`, the smallest stable idempotent-complete full subcategory of `Perf(X/G)` containing the pullbacks of `Perf(BG)`. Provide `pullback`, `retract` (closure under shifts, cones and retracts), `minimal` (contained in every such subcategory) and `moduleFunctor`, the functor from its Ind-completion to `Mod_A(Ind Perf(BG))`, fully faithful on bounded induced objects (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.5.2, definition of `Perf^ind`, p.295). Equality with `Perf(X/G)` is a theorem under further hypotheses. *Needs:* LP1.7; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*, *presentability*, *animation*; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Idempotents.Karoubi`.

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spec L`, `Perf^ind(BG) = Perf(BG)`.
- For `G = 1`, finite cell complexes and their retracts give all of `Perf(A)`.
- A direct summand of an induced finite complex lies in `Perf^ind`.
- For `X = G` with conjugation and `ℓ` dividing `|π₁(G°)_tors|`, the skyscraper at the unit is not induced (LP3.7): the definition is not all of `Perf`.

### LP3.5 The induced-perfect bar criterion

Prove that `M ∈ Perf(X/G)` lies in `Perf^ind(X/G)` if and only if the natural map `colim[⋯ → M ⊗_L A ⊗_L M^∨ → M ⊗_L M^∨] → M ⊗_A M^∨` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BG)`, all tensor products formed there: for induced objects the augmented simplicial object has an extra degeneracy; conversely the isomorphism makes the image of `M` dualizable in `Mod_A(Ind Perf(BG))` with compact unit, hence compact and a retract of a finite induced complex. When `G°` is reductive with `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ` and `B ⊂ G°` is a Borel subgroup, it suffices that `M|_B` be generated by `Perf(BB)`, because `Perf(BG°) → Perf(BB)` is fully faithful by Kempf vanishing and restriction to `G°` is conservative (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.8 and Corollary VIII.5.9, p.296). *Needs:* LP3.4; LP3.0; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; DGAInfinity layer 8.

**Checks.**

- For `M = A` the augmented bar map is an isomorphism.
- For `G = 1` every perfect complex satisfies the criterion.
- For `X = G/G` with `G = PGL_ℓ` the unit skyscraper fails it.

### LP3.6 Tensor-connectivity criterion for induced perfectness

Assume `G°` reductive with `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, that `A` has a good `G°`-filtration, and that `M ∈ Perf(X/G)` is connective for the good-filtration t-structure after forgetting the `A`-action. Prove that `M ∈ Perf^ind(X/G)` if and only if `M ⊗_A N` is connective for every such connective `N ∈ Perf(X/G)`. For the converse, LP3.0's finite good filtration dimension writes the bar resolution of `M^∨` as a sequential colimit of finite colimits that become increasingly connective, and separatedness (LP3.2) recovers the bar criterion (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.10 and proof, p.297). *Needs:* LP3.0; LP3.1; LP3.2; LP3.5; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- The connectivity hypothesis on `M` itself is part of the statement.
- For `M = A` both sides hold.
- For `G` a torus the t-structure is the ordinary one and the derived tensor of the connective objects `M` and `N` is connective.

### LP3.7 Adjoint perfect generation and its prime restriction

For `G` acting on itself by conjugation with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, let `i : Spec L → G` be the unit. Prove that the following are equivalent: `ℓ ∤ |π₁(G°)_tors|`; `i_* L ∈ Perf^ind(G/G)`; `Perf^ind(G/G) = Perf(G/G)`. Sufficiency reduces through central covers of order prime to `ℓ` to simply connected derived group, then builds a Borel-equivariant flag of Cartier divisors whose line bundles come from characters of `B` and applies the Borel bar criterion; the diagonal kernel is pulled back from the unit along `(g, g′) ↦ g g′⁻¹`. Necessity: a central cover with ℓ-primary kernel would force invariant functions on that kernel to be surjected onto by conjugation invariants, which contradicts their constancy on the unipotent kernel, by LP3.6 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.11 and proof, pp.297–299). *Needs:* LP3.5; LP3.6; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- For a torus `π₁` has no torsion and the unit is induced.
- For `G = PGL_ℓ`, `π₁ = ℤ/ℓ` and generation fails.
- For `G = SL_n` with `ℓ | n` generation holds, since `π₁(SL_n) = 0` (the centre does not matter).

### LP3.8 Twisted free-group perfect generation

If `G°` is reductive and `|π₀G|` and `|π₁(G°)_tors|` are prime to `ℓ`, prove that for any action `F_n → Aut(G)`, `Perf(Z¹(F_n, G)/G)` is generated under cones and retracts by `Perf(BG)`: the diagonal kernel of the twisted `Gⁿ`-quotient is a pullback of the adjoint unit kernel of LP3.7 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.12 and proof, p.299). The action is twisted conjugation, not the untwisted diagonal one. *Needs:* LP3.7; LP1.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18.

**Checks.**

- For `F₀` this is `Perf(BG)`.
- For `F₁` with trivial action it is LP3.7.
- For `G = PGL_ℓ` and `F₁` it fails.

### LP3.9 Derived unit fibres and generation

For a `G`-equivariant map `X̃ → G` (conjugation on `G`), put `X = X̃ ×^R_G Spec L` at the unit, `Ã = O(X̃)` and `A = O(X)`. If `G°` is reductive and `|π₀G|`, `|π₁(G°)_tors|` are prime to `ℓ`, prove that `L ⊗_{O(G)} Ã → A` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BG)`; and if moreover `Perf(X̃/G) = Perf^ind` and `Ã` is connective, then `A` is connective and `Perf(X/G) = Perf^ind`. The second part writes the cone of the relative bar comparison as a sequential colimit of terms `M ⊗_A g^*K_n ⊗_A M^*` with `K_n` coherent on the derived self-intersection of the unit, gives `IndCoh` of that self-intersection the t-structure generated by good-filtration-connective objects and passes to its separated quotient, bounds amplitudes by finite good filtration dimension, and proves conservativity of the forgetful functor there using central covers and the Borel-invariant Cartier flag of LP3.7 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.13 and proof, pp.299–301). The derived fibre is not assumed classical. *Needs:* LP3.7; LP3.5; LP3.2; LP3.0; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *animation*, *presentability*.

**Checks.**

- For `X̃ = G` with the identity map the fibre is the unit and the formula is tautological.
- For `X̃ = G × G` with multiplication the fibre is `G` with `A = O(G)`.
- For `G = 1` the statement is the derived base change formula.

### LP3.10 Surface and tame relation fibres

Prove that the derived character stack of a closed orientable surface (relation `∏[a_i, b_i] = 1`) and the tame Weil parameter stack (relation `σ⁻¹ τ σ τ^{−q} = 1`, with the finite action) are unit fibres of equivariant maps `G^{2g} → G` and `G² → G`, so that under the good `π₁` and component hypotheses their coordinate algebras are connective and their `Perf` categories are generated by `Perf(BG)` (Fargues–Scholze, end of §VIII.5.2, p.301, and the tame step of §VIII.5.4, p.312). The surface case is an application of the same mechanism. *Needs:* LP3.3; LP3.8; LP3.9; LP0.4.

**Checks.**

- For genus one the relation is the commuting-pair fibre.
- The tame relation uses geometric Frobenius as displayed.
- For `G` a torus the commutator map is constant, so the derived fibre is not classical, and the statement concerns connectivity and generation, not classicality.

### LP3.11 Prime-to-ℓ components of fixed groups

Let `G` be smooth affine over `L` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, and let `P` be a finite group of order prime to `ℓ` acting on `G`. Prove that `|π₀(G^P)|` is prime to `ℓ`. The identity component of `G^P` is smooth and reductive by ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3; this target owns only the component statement. Reduce to connected `G`, embed `G/H` (`H = G^P`) as a closed orbit in a product of twisted copies of `G` whose coordinate algebra has a good filtration, apply the finite good filtration dimension of LP3.0 to `O(G/H′)` for `H′ ⊃ H°` with `π₀H′ = ℤ/ℓ`, and compare with `RΓ(ℤ/ℓ, L)`, which is nonzero in arbitrarily high degrees (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.14 and proof, pp.301–302). *Needs:* LP3.0; LP3.1; LP3.3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3, RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `P = 1`, `π₀(G^P) = π₀G`.
- Inversion on `G_m` (with `ℓ ≠ 2`) has fixed group `μ₂`, of order prime to `ℓ`.
- In characteristic `2`, inversion on `G_m` has the nonsmooth fixed group `μ₂`; its acting group has order `2`, so the order hypothesis is essential.

### LP3.12 Cyclic fixed-locus resolutions

Let an automorphism `Θ` of prime order `r ≠ ℓ` act on `G` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`. Put `X = {(g₀, …, g_{r−1}) : g₀ Θ(g₁) ⋯ Θ^{r−1}(g_{r−1}) = 1}` with twisted `G`-conjugation and cyclic `C_r`-permutation. Prove that for the augmented cosimplicial `G`-space `X^{C_r} → X ⇉ ∏_{C_r} X ⋯`, the realisation of coordinate algebras is `O(X^{C_r})` in `Ind Perf(BG)`: complete along the common fixed locus, and use the formal `r`-th root map (available because `r` is invertible) to build a `(G, C_r)`-equivariant retraction onto `X^{C_r}`, giving an extra degeneracy (Fargues–Scholze, Lemma VIII.5.16 and proof, pp.304–307). No `π₁` hypothesis. This target also constructs the equivariant completion bridge `IndProPerf(BG)`: freely generate a presentable stable category from the exact category of countable pro-systems of finite-dimensional `G`-representations, with the original `Ind Perf(BG)` embedded fully faithfully. Define the completed coordinate algebras by powers of the fixed-locus ideal, and prove their idempotence as algebras in this category and their compatibility with the bounded equivariant tensor products used in the resolution. For general `G`, reduce boundedness through a faithful embedding into `GL_n`, as in the proof of Lemma VIII.5.16, pp.305–306. SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.8 supplies ordinary formal completion; the equivariant pro-category and the completion/tensor comparison are owned here. *Needs:* LP3.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.fixedSubgroup`.

**Checks.**

- For the completion bridge, the constant pro-system gives the original representation, completed `O(G)` tensored with the unit residue object gives `L`, and the formal `r`-power map on the completion of `G_m` at `1` has tangent `x ↦ rx`, invertible precisely when `r` is invertible.
- For the completion bridge with trivial `G` and `A = L[t]`, the pro-system `A/(t^n)` records the completion `L[[t]]`, not `L[t]`; completing along the zero ideal leaves `A` and along the unit ideal gives zero.
- In characteristic `r` this tangent is zero and there is no inverse root map. On points, the `C_r`-fixed tuples are the diagonal tuples `(g, …, g)` with twisted norm `g Θ(g) ⋯ Θ^{r−1}(g) = 1`.
- Under `h · (g_i) = (h g_i Θ(h)⁻¹)` the stabilizer of `(1, …, 1)` is the fixed group `G^Θ`, which on points is `TauCeti.fixedSubgroup` of `Θ`.

### LP3.13 Solvable fixed groups as Donkin subgroups

If a finite solvable group `P` of order prime to `ℓ` acts on `G` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, prove that `H° = (G^P)°` is a Donkin subgroup of `G°`: restriction of a module with good `G°`-filtration has a good `H°`-filtration, equivalently induction preserves good filtrations. Reduce along a normal series to prime cyclic `P`, then to simple factors and central covers; factor-permuting actions give diagonal subgroups, handled by the tensor theorem, and inner and outer actions by the root and highest-weight computations of the source; the cyclic fixed-locus resolution gives the good filtration of `O(G/H)` (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.15 and proof, pp.302–304). Not asserted for arbitrary `P`; no `π₁` condition. *Needs:* LP3.11; LP3.12; LP3.0; LP3.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `C_r` permuting the factors of `K^r`, the diagonal `K` is a Donkin subgroup by the tensor theorem.
- For `P = 1` the statement is empty.
- For a torus every subgroup torus is Donkin.

### LP3.14 Fixed-group induction and good counit kernels

In the setting of LP3.13 with `H = G^P`, prove that a representation `W` of `H°` has a good `H°`-filtration if and only if `Ind_{H°}^{G°} W` has a good `G°`-filtration, the same for `H` and `G`, and that for good `W` the kernels of the restriction–induction counits have good `H°`-filtrations. Reduce to prime cyclic `P`; for simply connected `G` use the highest-weight surjections of the proof of Theorem VIII.5.15; descend along a central simply connected cover by the summand with trivial kernel character; and for general `G` use its derived group and linearly reductive quotient, splitting induced modules as retracts, keeping the actual (possibly non-smooth) centre (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.17(i)–(iv) and proof, pp.307–310). **Checks.**

- For `P = 1` induction and counit are the identity with zero kernels.
- For `G` a torus every module has a good filtration and all statements hold trivially.
- The full-group and identity-component statements differ when the central quotient is nontrivial.

### LP3.15 Generation by restriction to fixed groups

In the same setting prove that `Perf(BH°)` is generated under cones and retracts by restrictions from `Perf(BG°)`, and `Perf(BH)` by restrictions from `Perf(BG)`, with no `π₁` hypothesis: the successive restriction–induction counit resolution converges in `Ind Perf` by LP3.14 and separatedness, induction is conservative, and compactness cuts the resolution to finitely many cones and retracts (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.17(v)–(vi), pp.307–310). *Needs:* LP3.14; LP3.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18.

**Checks.**

### LP3.16 The central-character fixed-group counterexample

### LP3.17 Fundamental groups of solvable fixed groups

If `G°` is reductive, `P` finite solvable of order prime to `ℓ` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(G°)_tors|`, prove that `ℓ ∤ |π₁((G^P)°)_tors|`: reduce to prime-order actions and to simply connected simple factors through central isogenies of order prime to `ℓ`; permutation and outer actions preserve the condition; for inner actions use the list of root subsystems and track the image of the centre, including the exceptional types; any new prime is the acting prime, already distinct from `ℓ` (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.19 and proof, pp.310–311). ### LP3.18 Sifted parameter mapping approximations

### LP3.19 Free-group gerbe comparisons

### LP3.20 Solvable wild gerbe elimination

### LP3.21 Tame reduction of finite-wild parameter categories

### LP3.22 Very-good and reductive-pair centralizers

Over algebraically closed `k`, prove that every closed subgroup of a reductive `G` has smooth scheme-theoretic centralizer, and hence separable orbit maps on tuples, if the characteristic is very good for `G`, or if `G` has a faithful representation `V` with a `G`-equivariant splitting of `Lie(G) ⊂ Lie(GL(V))`. **Checks.**

- For `GL(V)` the reductive-pair condition holds with zero complement, so all closed-subgroup centralizers are smooth.
- `Lie(G_m)` is one-dimensional abelian, not semisimple, yet `G_m` has smooth centralizers.
- For `SL₂` in characteristic `2` (not very good) the centralizer of the unipotent element `[[1,1],[0,1]]` is not smooth.

### LP3.23 Descent of étaleness to reductive quotients

Let `G` be reductive over the excellent coefficient DVR `O`, `X` and `Y` normal integral affine flat `O`-schemes of finite type, and `φ : Y → X` finite and `G`-equivariant. If `y ∈ Y(k)` and `x = φ(y)` have closed residual orbits, `φ` is étale at `y`, and `φ` is injective on geometric points of these orbits, prove that `φ//G` is étale at the image of `y`. Pass to a finite Galois closure `L/K` of the function fields; take `K₀ = Frac(O[X]^G)`, `L₀` the relative algebraic closure of `K₀` in `L` and `E₀ = Frac(O[Y]^G)`; connectedness of `G` makes `K₀` algebraically closed in `K`, so `L₀/K₀` is finite Galois; the orbit hypotheses put the stabilizer of a point above `y` inside the image of `Gal(L/E)`; apply the following intermediate integral-closure criterion to `L₀/K₀` and localise using invariant neighbourhoods (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.2, Lemmas 3.11–3.12 and proofs, pp.16–18). *Needs:* LP2e.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 0 (excellent rings), SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.20–0.21, 0.30 and 4.1 (finite normalization, normal schemes and étale lifting); ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

Prove `integralClosure_etale_iff_stabilizer`: for an excellent normal domain `A`, a finite Galois extension `L/Frac(A)` with group `D`, a subgroup `H ⊂ D`, and the integral closures `B` in `L^H` and `C` in `L`, let `c̄` be a geometric point over a closed point of `Spec C` and `b̄` its image in `Spec B`. Then `Spec B → Spec A` is étale at `b̄` exactly when the stabilizer of `c̄` in `D` is contained in `H` (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Lemma 3.12, p.17). The stabilizer is for the geometric point, so it is the inertia group rather than the decomposition group of the underlying prime.

**Checks.**

- For `φ = id` every hypothesis holds and the quotient map is étale.
- For `G = 1` the statement says only that `φ` is étale at `y`.
- Normality of the quotients, used in the Galois argument, comes from LP2e.2. For `A = k[[t]]`, `L = k((u))`, `u² = t` and algebraically closed `k` of characteristic different from `2`, the closed geometric point has stabilizer `C₂`: the intermediate cover with `H = 1` is ramified and that with `H = C₂` is the identity. For an unramified extension of a DVR with nontrivial residue extension, the geometric stabilizer is trivial although the underlying closed prime has a nontrivial decomposition group.
- Substituting the latter would wrongly reject an étale cover.

### LP3.24 Formal quotient slices over coefficient DVRs

Let `G` be reductive over `O`, `X` integral affine smooth of finite type over `O`, and `x ∈ X(k)` with closed residual orbit and scheme-theoretically trivial stabilizer. Prove that on Artin local `O`-algebras with residue field `k` the formal neighbourhood of the identity in `G` acts freely on `X̂_x` and that `X̂_x/Ĝ ≅ (X//G)^_{π(x)}` as deformation functors: an `O`-smooth transversal splitting the orbit tangent space makes `G × S → X` étale near `(1, x)`, LP3.23 compares `S` with the quotient, and invariant neighbourhoods and formal étaleness identify the completed functors (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.2, Proposition 3.13 and proof, pp.17–19). Scheme-theoretically trivial stabilizer is stronger than trivial `k`-points; nothing is claimed for nontrivial stabilizers. *Needs:* LP3.23; LP2e.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1, SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `X = G` with left translation the formal quotient is a point.
- Tuple conjugation requires first passing to the adjoint group when the centre is nontrivial.
- For `μ_ℓ` acting on `𝔸¹` in characteristic `ℓ` the stabilizer of `0` has trivial points but is not trivial as a scheme, and the slice statement fails.

### LP3.25 Integral Chevalley restriction for Levi groups

For a split reductive standard dual Levi `M` over `ℤ` with maximal split torus `T` and Weyl group `W(M, T)`, prove that restriction gives an isomorphism `ℤ[M]^M ≅ ℤ[T]^{W(M,T)}` with no good-prime hypothesis. Injectivity follows from density of regular semisimple conjugates over `ℚ` and torsion-freeness; surjectivity from the triangular `ℤ`-basis of Weyl-invariant characters by dominant highest weights, realised by characters of stable `ℤ`-lattices in the irreducible rational representations (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 8.3 and proof, p.53; Jantzen, I.10.4, Lemma, p.143, for stable lattices). This is group Chevalley restriction, not the Lie algebra statement, which fails in bad characteristic. *Needs:* LP3.0; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 9; Mathlib `RootPairing`.

**Checks.**

- For `M = G_m` restriction is the identity of `ℤ[t, t⁻¹]`.
- For `GL₂` the target is `ℤ[t₁ + t₂, t₁t₂, (t₁t₂)⁻¹]`, realised by trace and determinant.
- For `SL₂` it is `ℤ[t + t⁻¹]`, realised by the trace.

### Examples

For `G = PGL_ℓ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ`, `π₁ = ℤ/ℓ`: the unit skyscraper of `G/G` is not induced (LP3.7), the free-group approximation is not the mapping category (LP3.18) and the gerbe comparison is not essentially surjective (LP3.19). For `G = SL_n`, `π₁ = 0` and all three statements hold whatever the centre.

### Dependencies

LP0; LP1; LP2e; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3 and RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 9; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5; SchemeAndStackFoundations layers 1 and 4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; DGAInfinity layer 8.

## Layer LP2i: the integral excursion comparison at good primes

This part of LP2 applies LP3 to exactly the conclusions that need it: the comparison in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, higher-cohomology vanishing and coefficient base change of invariants. Their hypothesis `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` is kept separate from the unconditional closed-orbit classification and universal homeomorphism of LP2e and LP2c.

### LP2i.1 Integral invariant comparison in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`

Assume `ℓ ≠ p` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|`. Prove that `colim_{F_n → W} O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ)) → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` for a finite-wild `W`. Prove that taking invariants of this enhanced map gives exactly the scalar `compare` of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, by agreement on every free-word invariant generator. Thus its scalar inverse is the supplied good-prime inverse; the scalar integral isomorphism has its proof owner in SR.6.3. The cone is detected after inverting `ℓ` (where representations of `Ĥ` are semisimple and LP2e.8 suffices) and after reduction to `𝔽̄_ℓ` (LP3.21); since the free cocycle algebras and the target have good filtrations modulo `ℓ` (LP3.3), taking `Ĥ`-invariants computes the ordinary invariant rings and commutes with the sifted colimit (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.3.6, p.288, and Theorem VIII.5.1 with the reduction to Theorem VIII.5.2, pp.293–294). *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*; LP3.21; LP3.3; LP2e.8; LP2e.9; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ` a torus the `π₁` condition holds automatically.
- At a bad prime (`Ĥ = PGL_ℓ`) the universal homeomorphism and the character bijection remain while this statement is not available.
- After inverting `ℓ` the statement holds for every `Ĥ`.

### LP2i.2 Cohomology vanishing and invariant base change

Under the same hypotheses, prove that `O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` has no higher rational `Ĥ`-cohomology, that its invariant algebra is flat over `ℤ_ℓ`, and that for every `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ` the canonical map `O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))^Ĥ ⊗ Λ → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ)_Λ)^{Ĥ_Λ}` is an isomorphism, the derived form coming from the equivariant colimit of LP2i.1 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorems VIII.5.1–VIII.5.2 and proof, pp.293–294). The base change to all coefficients, in particular to `𝔽_ℓ`, is a consequence of good filtrations here, not of LP2e.1's flat base change, and `ℤ_ℓ → 𝔽_ℓ` is not flat. *Needs:* LP2i.1; LP3.3; LP1.5; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; Mathlib `Module.Flat`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ` a torus with trivial action the invariants are the whole flat coordinate ring.
- For `W = F_n` the statement is LP3.3 together with flatness of `O(Ĥⁿ)`.
- The `C₂` example of LP2e.1 (disconnected acting group) shows that base change to `𝔽₂` can fail without these hypotheses.

### LP2i.3 The three excursion comparisons

Assemble the supplied SR.6.3 scalar comparisons with the enhancements proved here: at every `ℓ ≠ p` the excursion comparison is a universal homeomorphism and classifies semisimple geometric parameters (LP2e.9, LP2c.5); after inverting `ℓ` it is a ring isomorphism (LP2e.9); under `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` it is an integral isomorphism, with higher-cohomology vanishing and base change (LP2i.1, LP2i.2) (Fargues–Scholze, §§VIII.3.2–VIII.3.3, pp.287–290). The finite-wild pieces are treated separately; the underlying derived colimit of LP2e.8 stays distinct from its `Ind Perf(BĤ)` version. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*; LP2e.9; LP2c.5; LP2i.2.

**Checks.**

- Closed geometric points alone cannot give an integral algebra isomorphism.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` all three comparisons are equalities.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` the first two hold and the third is not asserted.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = SL₂`, `π₁` is trivial and all three comparisons are isomorphisms at every `ℓ ≠ p`; for `Ĥ = PGL₂` and `ℓ = 2` only the universal homeomorphism and the rational isomorphism are available.

### Dependencies

LP2e; LP2c; LP3; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5.

## Layer LP4: representation bundles and module comparison

The universal parameter defines representation bundles before any generation statement. Generation and the module comparison are theorems under the good-prime hypothesis; the spectral action that uses them is outside this roadmap and owned downstream.

### LP4.1 Universal representation bundles

For `X = [Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ]` over a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra and a finite set `I`, construct, from the universal `Ĥ ⋊ Q`-torsor with its `W`-equivariance, the exact `Rep(Q^I)`-linear symmetric monoidal functors `Rep((Ĥ ⋊ Q)^I) → Perf(X)^{BW^I}`, `V ↦ UniversalRepresentationBundle V`, on finite projective representations. Provide `unit` (the trivial representation gives `O_X`), `tensor`, `reindex` (fusion along maps of finite sets), `act` (tensoring defines an exact action on `Perf(X)` compatible with unit and composition) and `baseChange` (pullback along a coefficient change carries the bundle of `V` to that of its scalar extension, compatibly with the `W`-action, tensor and unit, with no invariant-ring base change assumed) (Fargues–Scholze, introduction to Chapter X, p.340). This construction precedes any generation theorem; it gives a categorical Hecke datum (LP2e.12) on `Perf(X)`. *Needs:* LP1.7; LP0.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*.

**Checks.**

- The trivial representation acts as the identity on `Perf(X)`.
- The fibre at a parameter `φ` is `V` with the `W`-action through `φ`.
- The fibre of a tensor product is the tensor product of the two parameter representations, and for `Ĥ = G_m` the bundle of the standard character is the universal line bundle with the universal character as `W`-action.
- For inversion action of `Q = C₂` on `G_m`, the standard weight-one character does not extend to `G_m ⋊ C₂`: conjugation by the involution changes weight `1` to weight `−1`. It therefore supplies no representation bundle by this construction.

### LP4.2 Perfect generation on parameter stacks

For a finite-wild `W` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|`, prove that `Perf(Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` is generated under cones and retracts by the image of `Perf(BĤ)`, that the same holds over `𝔽̄_ℓ`, and that over a field of characteristic zero no condition on `ℓ` is needed. Over `ℤ_ℓ`: reduce perfect amplitude by surjections from induced bundles; a vector bundle is a retract of an induced bundle after inverting `ℓ`, hence up to a power of `ℓ`; the remaining mod-`ℓ` object is generated by LP3.21 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.1 and the reduction proof, pp.293–294). Generation is a theorem, not the definition of `Perf`. *Needs:* LP3.21; LP3.4; LP4.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `W = 1` the category is `Perf(BĤ)`.
- The quotient `Z¹/Ĥ` and the unquotiented `Z¹` are not interchangeable here.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` and `W = ℤ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` the unit skyscraper is not generated (LP3.7).

### LP4.3 Parameter `Ind Perf` module comparison

Under the hypotheses of LP4.2, prove that `Ind Perf(Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ) ≅ Mod_{O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))}(Ind Perf(BĤ))`, compatibly with pullback, tensor products and the representation bundles; over a field of characteristic zero the same holds with no condition on `π₁`. Pullback from `BĤ` and its right adjoint form the affine adjunction; generation makes the right adjoint conservative; Barr–Beck–Lurie identifies the monad with tensoring by the cocycle algebra (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.1 and proof, p.293). Modules are taken in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, not in the derived category with the equivariance forgotten. *Needs:* LP4.2; LP2i.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = 1` this is the comparison of `Ind Perf(Spec A)` with `A`-modules.
- For `W = 1` it is the tautology `Ind Perf(BĤ) ≅ Mod_{ℤ_ℓ}(Ind Perf(BĤ))`.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` and `W = ℤ` the comparison is not asserted, since the unit skyscraper of LP3.7 is not in the subcategory generated by `Perf(BĤ)`.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = G_m`, `W` tame: `Z¹(W, G_m)/G_m = (G_m × μ_{q−1}) × BG_m`, and the module comparison identifies `Ind Perf` of it with graded modules over `O(G_m × μ_{q−1})`, the grading recording the `G_m`-weight.

### Dependencies

LP1; LP2i; LP3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5.
