# Roadmap: parameter stacks, invariant theory and spectral coefficients

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the stack of local Langlands parameters of a pinned split reductive dual group over `ℤ_ℓ`, `ℓ ≠ p`, together with the three structures that the spectral side of the local Langlands programme reads off it: its deformation theory and singularities, its coarse quotient and the algebra of excursion operators, and its categories of perfect complexes. It uses the finite-wild cocycle scheme and excursion algebra of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6, with the dual group and L-group of ReductiveGroupsPartII (layer RG2.5), and builds their condensed, derived and categorical refinements. It ends with three theorems of Fargues–Scholze, Chapter VIII: geometric points of the coarse quotient are semisimple parameters at every `ℓ ≠ p` (Proposition VIII.3.2 and VIII.3.8); every categorical action of Hecke data gives a map from the excursion algebra to the centre (Theorem VIII.4.1); and when `ℓ` does not divide the order of the torsion of `π₁(Ĝ)`, the category of perfect complexes on the parameter stack is generated by representations of the dual group and its Ind-completion is the category of modules over the cocycle algebra (Theorem VIII.5.1). We want it because the spectral action of Fargues–Scholze, the integral local Langlands correspondence in families of Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss and Zhu's coherent sheaves on the stack of parameters all take these three structures as their coefficients, and because its modular representation theory (good filtrations, fixed groups of finite automorphism groups, wild gerbes) is needed nowhere else in this form.

The roadmap has seven layers. Layer 0 fixes the coefficient convention and the Weil-group conventions, and defines the wild enhancements of Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens. Layer 1 identifies the supplied integral parameter schemes with condensed parameters, builds the derived parameter stacks and computes their deformation theory and singularities. Layer 2 (excursion presentation) builds the coarse quotient, the derived free-group presentation and the categorical excursion operators of an abstract categorical Hecke action, all without a restriction on `ℓ`. Layer 3 (semisimple characters) reconstructs semisimple parameters from pseudocharacters with a prescribed finite projection and proves the local continuity theorems. Layer 4 develops the modular representation theory: good filtrations, induced perfect complexes, fixed groups and wild gerbe elimination. Layer 5 (integral invariants) uses Layer 4 for the equivariant refinement of the supplied scalar excursion comparison at good primes. Layer 6 builds the universal representation bundles and proves generation and the module comparison.

`Suggested.lean` records why the headline theorems cannot be stated against the dependency revisions locked by this repository’s `lake-manifest.json`: the local Weil-group carrier, relatively discrete coefficient functor, parameter schemes, excursion algebra and stable ∞-categories of perfect complexes on quotient stacks are missing. The module docstring identifies the missing objects for each layer. The mathematical targets and their hypotheses are specified below.

## Conventions

Let `E` be a nonarchimedean local field with residue characteristic `p` and residue cardinality `q`, and fix a prime `ℓ ≠ p`. `Ĥ` is a pinned split reductive group scheme over `ℤ_ℓ` (or over `ℤ[1/p]` when the integral scheme is meant), `Q` a finite group acting on `Ĥ` by pinning-preserving automorphisms, and `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q`. A homomorphism `η : Γ → Q` is fixed whenever `Γ` replaces the Weil group: a parameter into `J` lifts this `η`, and changing `η` changes the problem.

Examples with `Ĥ = G_m` use `Q = 1` unless an action is explicitly specified.

A crossed cocycle for an action `α` of `Γ` on `Ĥ` satisfies `c(γδ) = c(γ) α(γ)(c(δ))`, and gauge by `h` is `(h·c)(γ) = h c(γ) α(γ)(h)⁻¹`. The section attached to `c` is `γ ↦ (c(γ), η(γ))` into `Ĥ ⋊ Q`, and gauge corresponds to conjugation of the section by `(h, 1)`. Cocycles are homomorphisms only for the trivial action. In section form, parameters are homomorphisms `Γ →* Ĥ ⋊[β] Q` whose composite with `SemidirectProduct.rightHom` is `η`; the cocycle is the first coordinate, and 0.1's section equivalence identifies this form with `CrossedCocycle (β.comp η)`.

For a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ`, the coefficient object is the relatively discrete condensed algebra `Λ_disc ⊗_{ℤ_{ℓ,disc}} ℤ_ℓ`. Its sections over a profinite set `S` are the maps `S → Λ` that take values in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-submodule `M ⊂ Λ` and are continuous for the ℓ-adic topology of `M`. For an `𝔽_ℓ`-algebra this is the discrete condensed structure; for `Λ = ℚ_ℓ` it admits continuous characters of inertia with infinite image. A continuous map `S → Λ` for some topology on `Λ` does not by itself specify this condition.

Choose a geometric Frobenius `σ` and a tame generator `τ` with `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ^q`. The geometric degree sends `σ` to `1`; the Weil–Deligne scaling is `q^{-deg(w)}`, so `Ad(φ₀(σ))(σ·N) = q⁻¹ N`. ClassFieldTheory and Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss use the arithmetic Frobenius `Fr = σ⁻¹`, whose degree is the negative of ours; the conversion is `d_geo = d⁻¹` in multiplicative notation, and the action, degree and Tate character are transported together.

The framed finite-presentation model over `ℤ[1/p]` is the scheme constructed in 1.1. Comparisons between different dense discrete models are canonical only after base change to `ℤ_ℓ`, through continuous extension; a canonical comparison over `ℤ[1/p]` is not used. Framed schemes have relative dimension `dim Ĥ` and absolute dimension `dim Ĥ + 1` over the one-dimensional base; quotient stacks have expected relative dimension zero.

Cohomological indexing is used throughout: a module in degree zero shifted by `[-1]` sits in degree `1`. Tangent and cotangent complexes are derived objects, and continuous Weil cochains are not the cochains of the abstract Weil group with its topology forgotten. Cotangent duality uses `Lie(Ĥ)^*` with the Tate twist; no invariant pairing `Lie(Ĥ) ≅ Lie(Ĥ)^*` is assumed. Singularities are `H^{-1}(L ⊗ T)` with coordinate algebra `Sym H¹(L^∨)`.

The good-filtration t-structure lives on `Ind Perf(BG)` with connective part `D^{≤0}`. `Perf^ind(X/G)` denotes the stable subcategory closed under retracts generated by pullbacks from `Perf(BG)`; equality with `Perf(X/G)` is a generation theorem, never a definition. The centre of a stable ∞-category is `π₀ End(id)`, and a Schur condition on an object `X` includes a specified `L`-algebra isomorphism `π₀ End(X) ≅ L`. In Layer 4, following Fargues–Scholze §VIII.5, `G` denotes a reductive group over an algebraically closed field `L` of characteristic `ℓ` (playing the role of the dual group), and dominant weights are characters of its maximal torus. "Reductive" means connected; a group whose identity component is reductive is called generalized reductive when its component group is finite étale.

The three strengths of the invariant comparison are kept apart:

| Result | Coefficient hypothesis | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Closed-orbit classification; excursion comparison a universal homeomorphism | every `ℓ ≠ p` | 2.6; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, used in 2.9 |
| Excursion comparison an isomorphism | after inverting `ℓ` | SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, used in 2.9 |
| Scalar integral isomorphism | `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` | SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3 |
| Isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, higher-cohomology vanishing, all coefficient base changes, generation of `Perf` | `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` | Layer 4, Layer 5, Layer 6 |

The component idempotents of `Q` are integral even when `ℓ` divides `|Q|`, and the finite-`Q` reconstruction theorem has no prime-to-`|Q|` restriction. For bad primes, independence of the full excursion algebra from the dense discrete model is not known (Fargues–Scholze, remark after Proposition VIII.3.7, p.289); the ℓ-torsion-free quotient is independent, and only that quotient is used where independence matters.

## How to read the build

Read Layers 0–6 in order: coefficients and wild enhancements; deformation theory; coarse quotients and excursion operators; semisimple characters; modular representation theory; integral invariants; representation bundles and generation. Layer 0 extends SR.6.1's cocycle and discretization interfaces to condensed coefficients and prescribed projections. Layer 1 needs Layer 0 and the derived foundations. Layers 2 and 3 need Layer 1 and unconditional reductive invariant theory, and in particular never the good-prime results of Layer 4: closed orbits, the excursion presentation, reconstruction and continuity hold at every `ℓ ≠ p`. Layer 4 is the modular representation theory in characteristic `ℓ`. Layer 5 applies Layer 4 to the equivariant refinement of the integral excursion comparison, and Layer 6 applies Layer 4 to the categories of perfect complexes. Prerequisites are cited by layer and subsection number, as §2.4.

## Layer 0: cocycles, coefficients and wild enhancements

Layer 0 uses SR.6.1's crossed cocycles and finite-wild discretizations, extends their restriction and section APIs to relatively discrete condensed coefficients, and constructs normal wild cutoffs and the geometric-degree normalisation. Its second half defines the wild enhancements of Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens using the intrinsic twisted centralizer, which differs from the component group of an ordinary centralizer.

### 0.1 Restriction, sections and continuity of crossed cocycles

For a group `Γ` acting on `H` by `α : Γ →* MulAut H`, use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Crossed cocycles and gauge action*, for `CrossedCocycle α`, its unit and inverse identities, gauge action, equivariant coefficient change and section homomorphism. Define `H¹(Γ,H)` as its gauge-orbit set, and extend the supplied objects as follows (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1, p.278; Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §1.2, pp.4–6).

- Restriction along `f : Δ →* Γ` sends `c` to `c ∘ f` for the pulled-back action; prove identity, composition, gauge and coefficient-change compatibility and descent to gauge-orbit sets.
- Prove that the section construction is an equivalence, with inverse given by the first coordinate. For fixed `η : Γ →* Q` and `β : Q →* MulAut H`, specialize it to cocycles for `β ∘ η` and lifts `φ : Γ →* H ⋊[β] Q` satisfying `SemidirectProduct.rightHom.comp φ = η`.
- Prove that gauge by `h` becomes conjugation of the lift by `SemidirectProduct.inl h`.
- For topological groups with continuous action, define continuous cocycles as the continuous maps among these cocycles. Prove preservation of continuity by gauge, continuous restriction and continuous coefficient change.

*Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Crossed cocycles and gauge action*; Mathlib `SemidirectProduct`, `MonoidHom`, `Subgroup`. SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1 supplies the comparison with Mathlib's distinct Čech cocycles `CategoryTheory.PresheafOfGroups.OneCocycle`.

**Checks.**

- For trivial `α`, crossed cocycles are exactly group homomorphisms `Γ →* H`; for `Γ = 1` there is exactly one cocycle.
- The gauge of the unit cocycle by `h` evaluates to `h α(γ)(h)⁻¹`; for `Γ = ℤ` acting on `ℚˣ` by inversion at the generator and `h = 2`, the value at the generator is `4`, which pins both the side and the inverse in the gauge formula.
- For `C₂` acting on `ℤ` by negation, `c(s) = 1` is a crossed cocycle (`c(s²) = 1 − 1 = 0`) that is not a homomorphism `C₂ → ℤ`: a definition requiring multiplicativity fails.
- Under the section equivalence, the projection of the section is the identity and its first coordinate is `c`; gauge by `h` becomes conjugation by `inl h`, and the lift attached to the unit cocycle with `η` is `inr ∘ η`.
- Restriction along the trivial map `ℤ → 1` sends every cocycle on `1` to the unit cocycle on `ℤ`, and restriction along `id` is the identity.
- For negation on `ℤ`, `H¹(C₂, ℤ_neg) ≅ ℤ/2`: a cocycle is determined by any integer at the involution, and gauge changes that integer by an even integer.
- `H¹(1,H)` is a singleton. For trivial action, `H¹(ℤ,H)` is the set of conjugacy classes of elements of `H`, by evaluation at a generator.

### 0.2 Condensed L-parameters

Fix the pinned split dual `Ĥ` over `ℤ_ℓ`, its action through finite `Q` and the projection `W_E → Q`. For a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ`, define the relatively discrete condensed coefficient algebra `Λ_disc ⊗_{ℤ_{ℓ,disc}} ℤ_ℓ` and an L-parameter as a condensed crossed cocycle `W_E → Ĥ(Λ)` for these coefficients (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1, p.278).

- Identify sections of the coefficient algebra on a profinite set `S` with maps `S → Λ` taking values in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-submodule on which they are ℓ-adically continuous.
- Identify condensed cocycles with sections of `Ĥ(Λ) ⋊ W_E → W_E` and with lifts of `W_E → Q` to `Ĥ(Λ) ⋊ Q`, naturally in `Λ`; passing to a lift preserves its first coordinate and prescribed projection.
- Prove extensionality by equality on every test profinite set and section.
- Prove that gauge `h·φ(w) = h φ(w) (w·h)⁻¹` preserves the coefficient and continuity conditions and satisfies identity and multiplication laws.
- Construct restriction to wild inertia with the same coefficient and gauge conventions.
- For a closed embedding `Ĥ ⋊ Q → GL_N`, prove that a cocycle is an L-parameter exactly when its inertia matrix coefficients satisfy the finite-type continuity condition. Compare the cyclotomic normalisation with other conventions after adjoining `√q`.

*Needs:* 0.1; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `Condensed`, `CondensedMod`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action, L-parameters are continuous characters `W_E → Λˣ` whose inertia values lie in a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-module.
- For an `𝔽_ℓ`-algebra `Λ` the relatively discrete structure is discrete, so every parameter is locally constant on inertia. A continuous map from compact inertia then has finite image; an arbitrary abstract homomorphism with non-open kernel fails this condition.
- For `Λ = ℚ_ℓ`, take the tame ℓ-primary coordinate `t_ℓ : I_E → ℤ_ℓ`, with `t_ℓ(Fr i Fr⁻¹) = q t_ℓ(i)`. In split `GL₂`, set `ρ(i) = [[1,t_ℓ(i)],[0,1]]` and `ρ(Fr) = diag(q,1)`. This is finite-type continuous with infinite inertia image; imposing discrete coefficients would exclude it. The ℓ-adic cyclotomic character itself is unramified for `ℓ ≠ p`, so it cannot witness infinite inertia.
- The projection of the section attached to `φ` at `w` is `w` and its first coordinate is `φ(w)`.

### 0.3 Finite wild ramification

A parameter has finite wild ramification when it is trivial on a relatively open subgroup of `P_E`. For an open `W_E`-normal subgroup `P ⊂ P_E` contained in the kernel of `W_E → Q`, define the finite-wild piece `Par_P` as the parameters trivial on `P` (Fargues–Scholze, Remark VIII.1.2 and Theorem VIII.1.3 with proof, pp.278–280).

- Prove every L-parameter has finite wild ramification: a continuous image of the pro-`p` group `P_E` in an ℓ-adic congruence kernel is trivial, and reduction modulo `ℓ` leaves finite image.
- For `P′ ⊂ P`, inflation gives an injective map `Par_P → Par_{P′}`, compatible with identity and composition.
- For a normal subgroup `P` killing the action, a cocycle trivial on `P` descends uniquely to `W_E/P`; on representatives its value is the original cocycle value. Prove that this is inverse to inflation and commutes with gauge, including for continuous parameters.
- For a continuous homomorphism on a topological group with compact wild subgroup and `T₁` target, prove that the kernel on that subgroup is open if and only if its image is finite.
- For a normal wild subgroup `P` and the section homomorphism `s_c(γ)=(c(γ),η(γ))`, construct the normal cutoff `U=P ∩ ker s_c`. Prove that it lies in `P ∩ ker η`, kills `c`, and contains every subgroup of `P ∩ ker η` killing `c`.
- Prove the cutoff is relatively open when `c` has finite image on `P` and `η|P` is continuous into discrete `Q`, is unchanged by gauge, and pulls back along a homomorphism to the cutoff of the restricted data, with wild subgroup pulled back as well.

*Needs:* 0.2; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4.

**Checks.**

- An unramified parameter lies in the piece `P = P_E` when `Q` is unramified; for `P′ ⊂ P`, triviality on `P` implies triviality on `P′`, which fixes the direction of inflation.
- For coefficients in a finite extension of `ℚ_ℓ`, the condition is finite image of wild inertia.
- For the identity cocycle of the discrete group `ℤ`, the kernel `{0}` is open but the image is infinite: compactness cannot be dropped from the open-kernel/finite-image equivalence. For the identity map of `C₂` with the indiscrete topology on both sides, the image is finite and the map continuous but the kernel is not open: the `T₁` hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- For `Γ = Q`, `η = id` and the unit cocycle, the cutoff is `{1}` even for trivial action, so replacing `ker η` by the kernel of the action is wrong; for trivial `Q` and the identity cocycle `Γ → Γ` the cutoff is `{1}`, which distinguishes it from `P ∩ ker η`; for trivial `H` and `Q` the cutoff is `P`.
- For `Γ = H = Q = S₃` with `η = id`, conjugation action and the unit cocycle, gauge by `(01)` changes the value at `(12)` to a nonidentity permutation, yet the cutoff stays `{1}`: the identity fibre of the cocycle alone is not gauge invariant.
- The unit cocycle of `C₂` acting on `ℤ` by negation kills the whole source but does not descend to the trivial quotient, because the action does not: the action-kernel hypothesis of descent cannot be dropped.

### 0.4 Discrete Weil models and the geometric degree

For an open `W_E`-normal subgroup `P ⊂ P_E` killing the action, use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Finite-wild Weil discretization*, for the finitely presented dense discrete `W ⊂ W_E/P`, and *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization* for unique extension and canonical ℓ-adic comparisons. Define geometric degree by negating ClassFieldTheory's arithmetic degree, and define the Tate character for a unit `q_Λ ∈ Λˣ` by `w ↦ q_Λ^{−d_geo(w)}` (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, pp.279–280; Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §4.1, Corollary 4.2, pp.30–31).

- Identify relatively ℓ-adic continuous cocycles on the dense model with condensed cocycles, naturally in every `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra and compatibly with gauge and restriction. Fix every value at `w ∈ W`; use the tame matrix's finite unipotent binomial expansion for locally finite-type coefficient continuity. The resulting extension agrees with the supplied extension, including identity and composition of model comparisons; no canonical independence over `ℤ[1/p]` is asserted.
- Prove that negating the degree twice is the identity, preserves its kernel, and commutes with precomposition. In multiplicative notation the conversion is inversion of the degree homomorphism, not inversion of the Weil group.
- Prove that the Tate character takes arithmetic Frobenius to `q_Λ`, geometric Frobenius to `q_Λ⁻¹`, and inertia to `1`, and commutes with precomposition.
- Transport action and degree together under `σ=Fr⁻¹`, turning the tame relation into `σ⁻¹ τ σ=τ^q`.

*Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *Finite-wild Weil discretization*, *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization*; 0.1–0.3; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; Mathlib `zpowersHom`.

**Checks.**

- For `d(Fr) = 1`, `d_geo(Fr)=−1` and `d_geo(Fr⁻¹)=1`; inertia, of degree zero, has geometric degree zero, and the zero degree map is fixed by the conversion.
- With `q = 3` the Tate character takes the value `3` at arithmetic Frobenius and `1/3` at geometric Frobenius, and the value `1` on inertia: a convention that forgot to invert would give `3` at `σ`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m`, the tame relation forces `χ(τ)^{q−1} = 1`; replacing geometric Frobenius by the arithmetic one in `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ^q` gives `Fr τ Fr⁻¹ = τ^q`, the arithmetic relation.
- Changing the tame generator scales the normalised monodromy coordinate; the comparison of parameter functors is canonical, not an equality of unnormalised monodromy operators.

### 0.5 Wild inertial parameters

For a group projection `π : L → W` and a homomorphism `i : P → W`, define a wild inertial parameter as a homomorphism `ρ : P → L` admitting some `φ : W → L` with `π ∘ φ=id` and `φ ∘ i=ρ`. The extension is a condition, not chosen data. For the classical case, let `G` be quasi-split classical over a local field of odd residual characteristic with complex dual. Define admissible parameters as continuous homomorphisms `φ : W_F × SL₂(ℂ) → ᴸG` with prescribed Weil projection, finite inertia image after projection to `Ĝ ⋊ Q`, Frobenius-semisimple Weil restriction and algebraic `SL₂` restriction. Define `S_φ=C_Ĝ(φ)/(C_Ĝ(φ)° Z(Ĝ)^{W_F})`, enhancements as irreducible complex representations of `S_φ`, and `Lang(G)` as their `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes. A classical wild parameter must admit such an admissible extension; the abstract section condition alone does not impose admissibility (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §§1.20–1.21, pp.8–9).

- Prove that a wild parameter satisfies `π ∘ ρ=i` and is determined by its underlying homomorphism.
- Construct restriction of admissible parameters to wild inertia, with underlying map `φ|P`, and its compatibility with conjugation.
- Construct the conjugation action of `ker π` on wild parameters, with identity and multiplication laws, and form the classical `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes.
- For `L=H ⋊ W`, prove that conjugation by `h ∈ H` changes the first coordinate to `h ρ(p)_H α(i(p))(h)⁻¹`, agreeing with gauge in 0.1. No wild local Langlands correspondence is asserted.

*Needs:* 0.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4.

**Checks.**

- The parameter `p ↦ (1, p)` with trivial dual-group component is extended by the standard unramified admissible parameter.
- A finite-image Weil homomorphism with trivial `SL₂` part is admissible. In split `SL₂`, the homomorphism trivial on inertia with nonidentity unipotent Frobenius has the projection and continuity properties but fails Frobenius semisimplicity. An infinite-image unipotent inertia homomorphism fails finite inertia even if it is a condensed parameter.
- Restricting `hφh⁻¹` gives `hρh⁻¹`; two admissible extensions with the same restriction define the same wild inertial parameter.
- When `i` is injective and `P` nontrivial, the constant homomorphism `p ↦ 1` is not a wild inertial parameter, since `π ∘ ρ = i` fails: a definition asking only that some extension exist without the section equation would accept it.
- In `L = W = S₃` with `π = i = id`, conjugation by `(01)` sends `(12)` to `(02)`, changing the prescribed value; only elements of `ker π` may conjugate.

### 0.6 Twisted wild centralizers

For a wild inertial parameter `ρ` with `P_F` normal in `W_F`, define its twisted wild centralizer as the set of `(g,w) ∈ ᴸG` satisfying `(g,w)ρ(w⁻¹pw)(g,w)⁻¹=ρ(p)` for all `p ∈ P_F` (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, p.8).

- Prove closure under identity, multiplication and inverse, so this is a subgroup.
- Prove that projection to `W_F` is surjective, with kernel `C_Ĝ(ρ)`, and that all values of an extending section lie in this subgroup.
- For a chosen admissible extension `φ`, construct its isomorphism with `C_Ĝ(ρ) ⋊_{Ad φ} W_F`, sending an L-group element over `w` to its quotient by `φ(w)` in the first coordinate and to `w` in the second. Prove that only this splitting depends on `φ`; the subgroup is intrinsic. It is not the ordinary centralizer of `ρ(P_F)` in the L-group.

*Needs:* 0.5; Mathlib `Subgroup`, `MonoidHom`.

**Checks.**

- For `ρ(p) = (1, p)` and trivial `P_F`-action on `Ĝ`, the twisted centralizer is the whole L-group.
- The elements above `w = 1` are exactly the usual centralizer of `ρ` in `Ĝ`.
- With `H = 1`, `P = W = S₃` and `ρ = inr ∘ P.subtype`, the value `inr (01)` lies in the twisted centralizer but does not commute with `ρ((12))`. Thus the ordinary centralizer excludes a genuine section value.
- For every admissible extension `φ`, each `φ(w)` lies in the twisted centralizer.

### 0.7 The centre of the local Weil group

For each nonarchimedean local field `F` of odd residual characteristic, prove that the centre of its local Weil group `W_F` is trivial. A central Weil element is central in the absolute Galois group by density. For each finite Galois `L/F`, it therefore acts trivially on the abelianization of `G_L`. In mixed characteristic, conjugation-equivariant local reciprocity and injectivity of the local Artin map force its restriction to fix `Lˣ`. In equal characteristic, construct the Artin–Schreier identification `H¹(G_L, 𝔽_p) = L/(x^p−x)` and prove that every nonidentity finite-order automorphism of `L` acts nontrivially on this quotient. For a wild automorphism `t ↦ t + c t^{r+1} + …`, use `t^{-n}` with `n > r` and `p ∤ n(n−r)`; its difference has a pole of order prime to `p`. Residue-field and tame automorphisms are detected by coefficients and prime-to-`p` pole orders. This proves triviality on every `L`, hence on the separable closure (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, equation (1.1), p.8; local reciprocity and conjugation as in ClassFieldTheory layers 3, 6–9; Artin–Schreier exact sequence, Stacks [0A3J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A3J)). The equal-characteristic Artin–Schreier identification and pole test are targets here; full Artin–Schreier–Witt reciprocity is not an input. *Needs:* ClassFieldTheory layers 3, 6–9 with their mixed-characteristic hypotheses; LocalFieldsRamification layers 1–4; 0.1.

**Checks.**

- In a mixed-characteristic finite Galois extension, a nonidentity automorphism moves an element of `Lˣ`, and its Artin image detects the conjugation action.
- For `L = 𝔽_p((t))`, `p` odd, the involution `t ↦ −t` moves the class of `t^{-1}` in `L/(x^p−x)`.
- The tame quotient alone cannot prove centre-freeness of the full Weil group: every element of wild inertia has trivial image there; finite ramified extensions and their abelian characters are essential.

### 0.8 Wild enhancement groups

Define the wild enhancement group `S_ρ=Z(C_{ᴸG}(ρ))/Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}`, using the twisted centralizer of 0.6 and the embedding `z ↦ (z,1)` of the invariant dual centre (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, equation (1.1), p.8).

- Given an admissible extension `φ`, identify the numerator with `Z(C_Ĝ(ρ))^{φ(W_F)}`, preserving the embedded dual centre; this uses the trivial centre of `W_F` from 0.7.
- Prove this numerator lies in `C_Ĝ(φ(W_F × SL₂(ℂ)))`; inflation from `S_φ` followed by restriction therefore gives a functor `Rep(S_φ) → Rep(S_ρ)` trivial on the invariant dual centre.
- Construct conjugation transport of `S_ρ` and its representations for `h ∈ Ĝ`, and prove that restriction of enhancements commutes with it. The group `S_ρ` is not `π₀ C_Ĝ(ρ)`.

*Needs:* 0.6; 0.7; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `Subgroup.center`, `Representation`.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial dual-group component `ρ(p) = (1, p)`, the numerator is `Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}` and `S_ρ` is trivial; the constant map `p ↦ 1`, which loses the projection, cannot serve as this test.
- For `G = Sp₂` and `Ĝ = SO₃`, a ramified cyclic cubic character of `W_{ℚ₃}` gives a nontrivial wild parameter `diag(θ, θ⁻¹, 1)`. Its centralizer is the diagonal torus `T`, the Weil action on `T` is trivial, and `S_ρ = T`; its full parameter centralizer is connected, so `S_φ = 1`.
- For the `S₃` quotient of `W_{ℚ₃}` from the splitting field of `X³−3`, use the orthogonal representation `std₂ ⊕ sign` into `SO₃`. Wild inertia maps onto `C₃`, its centralizer is `T`, and the tame involution inverts `T`. Hence `S_ρ = μ₂` and `S_φ = C₂`; both characters restrict nontrivially or trivially as expected.
- The restricted representation is trivial on `Z(Ĝ)^{W_F}`.
- Conjugating `φ` and `ρ` by `h` transports the restricted representation along the conjugation equivalence.

### 0.9 Extended wild inertial parameters

In the classical setting of 0.5, define an extended wild parameter as a pair `(ρ,χ_ρ)` where `χ_ρ` is the restriction to `S_ρ` of an irreducible enhancement of some admissible extension of `ρ`, and write `Wild(G)` for their `Ĝ`-conjugacy classes (Kurinczuk–Skodlerack–Stevens, §1.21, pp.8–9).

- Prove that the classical isotypic decomposition expresses the centralizer through general linear, symplectic and orthogonal factors, with the special-orthogonal determinant condition. Deduce that `S_φ` is an elementary abelian 2-group, hence every irreducible complex enhancement and its restriction is one-dimensional.
- Construct restriction `Lang(G) → Wild(G)`, sending `(φ,χ_φ)` to `(φ|P_F,χ_φ|S_ρ)`, and prove independence of the representative under conjugation.
- Construct the forgetful map from `Wild(G)` to conjugacy classes of wild inertial parameters, and prove that its composite with restriction is ordinary wild restriction. Retain quasi-split classical groups, odd residual characteristic and complex coefficients throughout.

*Needs:* 0.5; 0.8; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For trivial dual-group component `ρ(p) = (1,p)`, `S_ρ = 1` and every restricted enhancement is its one-dimensional trivial representation.
- For the ramified cyclic cubic parameter of 0.8, `S_ρ = T` but `S_φ = 1`: the trivial character extends, while a nontrivial character of `T` does not arise from any admissible extension of this same `ρ`, since `C_Ĝ(φ) ⊂ T` remains connected.
- For the dihedral parameter of 0.8, `S_ρ = S_φ = C₂`, and both the trivial and sign character give extended wild parameters.
- Conjugate enhanced Langlands parameters have the same image in `Wild(G)`.
- Forgetting the enhancement of the restricted pair `(φ,χ_φ)` gives the conjugacy class of `φ|_{P_F}`.

### Examples

The section form pins the conventions: for `Γ = ℤ` acting on `ℚˣ` by inversion, the gauge of the unit parameter by `2` has first coordinate `4` at the generator, and the geometric degree of arithmetic Frobenius is `−1`, with Tate character `1/3` at geometric Frobenius when `q = 3`. The `S₃` examples of 0.3 and 0.5 show that the cutoff must use the prescribed projection and that only `ker π` may conjugate wild parameters.

## Layer 1: integral parameter spaces and deformation theory

Finite wild cutoffs reduce the parameter space to finitely presented equations, whose union over all cutoffs is the full scheme. Flatness, the complete-intersection property and the dimension bound come first; the derived parameter stack and its classicality follow; the cotangent complex then gives deformation theory, the Weil–Deligne comparison and the singularity stack with its support theory.

### 1.1 ℓ-adic representability of finite-wild parameters

Use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *One integral finite-wild cocycle scheme*, for `Z¹(W,Ĥ)` over `ℤ[1/p]`, with its universal cocycle, point equivalence and gauge coaction (Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, §1, pp.4–7, §4.1, Theorem 4.1, pp.29–30; Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, p.280).

- Identify its base change to `ℤ_ℓ` with 0.3's finite-wild condensed parameter functor through 0.4, naturally in coefficients and equivariantly for gauge.
- Transport the supplied presentation: for `C=O(Ĥ)` and the coproduct `T` indexed by finite generators of `W`, quotient by the ideal `J` generated by `c(r)(a)−ε(a)` for relators `r` and algebra generators `a` of `C`; retain the nilpotents of `T/J`.
- Construct the presentation equivalence by the representing property and prove it fixes all universal evaluations, so changes of presentation identify the same scheme.

*Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.1, *One integral finite-wild cocycle scheme*, *ℓ-adic cocycle extension and changes of discretization*; 0.1; 0.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- For `W = F_n` the scheme is `Ĥⁿ` with twisted diagonal conjugation; for `W = 1` it is the base.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` with trivial action the tame scheme is `G_m × μ_{q−1}`; in the fibre at `2` for `q = 3`, the universal element `t − 1` is nonzero with square zero, so the scheme is not its reduction.
- For the unramified action with arithmetic Frobenius inverting `G_m`, the tame scheme is `G_m × μ_{q+1}`: its tame coordinate `b` satisfies `b⁻¹ = b^q`. For `q = 3` in characteristic `2`, `(b − 1)⁴ = 0` but `(b − 1)² ≠ 0`.
- Its `ℤ_ℓ`-points on `ℤ_ℓ`-algebras agree with the finite-wild condensed parameters of 0.3.

### 1.2 Clopen finite-wild pieces

Prove that the parameter scheme `Z¹(W_E, Ĥ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` is the filtered union of the finite-wild pieces `Z¹(W_E/P, Ĥ)`, that for `P′ ⊂ P` the `P`-piece is open and closed in the `P′`-piece, and that the union is a disjoint union of affine schemes of finite type after separating the clopen wild-kernel strata: the condition `φ(γ) = 1` is clopen for `γ` of finite `p`-power order because `p` is invertible, finitely many such conditions cut out the `P`-piece, and every parameter has an open wild kernel (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Theorem VIII.1.3, pp.279–280). Use SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.2, *Finite wild strata and reductive centralizers*, for the fixed-depth wild strata after integral-closure base change, with their smooth restriction orbits and centralizers (Dat–Helm–Kurinczuk–Moss, Theorem 3.1 and §4.1, equations (4.1)–(4.2)); distinguish these from varying the depth. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.2, *Finite wild strata and reductive centralizers*; 0.3; 1.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3.

**Checks.**

- The full union is not quasi-compact when infinitely many wild strata occur.
- For `P′ = P` the inclusion is the identity.
- For trivial `Ĥ` every piece is the base.

### 1.3 Weil cohomology: amplitude and Euler characteristic

For a free `Λ`-module `M` of finite rank with a condensed action of `W_E` in the relatively discrete convention, prove that `RΓ(W_E, M)` is a perfect complex of `Λ`-modules with amplitude in `[0, 2]` and Euler characteristic zero; for field coefficients, `dim H⁰ − dim H¹ + dim H² = 0`. The proof kills an open wild kernel (exact invariants for a finite `p`-group), then resolves tame ℓ-primary inertia and the Frobenius `ℤ` each by a two-term complex; the total complex has amplitude `[0, 2]` and alternating rank zero, and the same calculation on a finite-wild dense `W` gives the derived deformation complex (Fargues–Scholze, the dimension argument in the proof of Theorem VIII.1.3 and Proposition VIII.2.2, pp.280–282). Cohomology is continuous (condensed), not abstract cohomology of `W_E` with its topology forgotten. *Needs:* 0.4; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; LocalFieldsRamification layer 4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ContinuousCohomology.continuousCohomologyFunctor`; Mathlib `Module.Free`.

**Checks.**

- For the trivial rank-one `ℚ_ℓ`-representation, `H⁰` and `H¹` are one-dimensional and `H² = 0`.
- For `ℚ_ℓ(1)`, `H⁰ = 0`.
- The alternating sum is zero in both cases.

### 1.4 Unipotent classes and the Frobenius–tame dimension bound

For a smooth affine finite-type group `H` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` with reductive identity component, first prove that it has finitely many unipotent conjugacy classes. The connected finiteness input and its use here are stated in the proof of Fargues–Scholze Lemma VIII.1.4, p.281, which credits Lusztig 1976. For the disconnected step, descend `H` to a finite subfield and use Fulman–Guralnick 2012, Corollary 2.6, pp.3028–3029; that corollary assumes a finite-field model and transfers finiteness from the identity component. Let `σ` act on `ℤ/ℓ^mℤ` by multiplication by `q`. ### 1.12 Lie invariants and the coadjoint nullcone

Let `G` be a split connected reductive group over a field `k`, with split maximal torus `T`, Weyl group `W`, and Lie algebras `𝔤` and `𝔱`. In characteristic zero use the adjoint restriction map in the supplier contract and the complex zero-fibre construction of NilpotentOrbits N1 and N5.0, transporting it by rational descent to the split characteristic-zero setting. In positive characteristic assume the characteristic is very good for each irreducible root component: it does not divide `r+1` for type `A_r`, is not `2` for types `B`, `C`, `D`, is neither `2` nor `3` for `G₂`, `F₄`, `E₆`, `E₇`, and is not `2`, `3` or `5` for `E₈`; a torus imposes no root condition. Extend the invariant theorem to this setting, and construct the coadjoint quotient `χ* : 𝔤* → Spec k[𝔤*]^G` (Fargues–Scholze, proof of Proposition VIII.2.11, pp.284–285).

- Supply a `G`-invariant nondegenerate bilinear form on `𝔤` under these hypotheses, giving a linear equivariant isomorphism `𝔤 ≅ 𝔤*`. Use the central-torus/derived Lie decomposition under the stated characteristic hypothesis, a nondegenerate form on the central summand, and invariant forms on the simple summands (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, very-good-prime table and following paragraph, p.14). It is a choice, not the Killing form of a reductive Lie algebra with centre and not a standing identification in cotangent duality.
- Prove restriction `k[𝔤]^G ≅ k[𝔱]^W` in the positive-characteristic setting and transport it to `k[𝔤*]^G` through the chosen pairing. Prove that this is a polynomial ring on `rank G` homogeneous generators, with degree-one generators for the central torus and the fundamental invariant degrees of the Weyl group for the root components. These are degrees, not the degrees minus one sometimes called exponents.
- Prove that the geometric points of the zero fibre of `χ*` are exactly the covectors whose geometric orbit closure contains zero. Define the scheme-theoretic zero fibre by the positive-degree invariant ideal and distinguish it from this geometric-point criterion over nonreduced test algebras. The adjoint zero fibre and coadjoint zero fibre correspond through the pairing, independently of that choice as closed nullcone loci.
- Prove compatibility of the invariant quotient and its zero fibre with field extension; in characteristic zero extend the quotient construction to arbitrary coefficient algebras by base change. Thus a section over a nonreduced coefficient algebra lies in the scheme-theoretic zero fibre only when all defining invariant polynomials vanish there.
- For the dual group of the standing conventions, with `n` the residue degree of the extension killing its pinned Weil action, prove that `ℓ ∤ q^{ne}−1` for every fundamental invariant degree `e` implies the very-good-characteristic conditions just listed. Verify this implication on each irreducible root system; include the degree-one central factors. This supplies the positive-characteristic hypotheses of the nilpotence theorem, without imposing them on the coarse-point or reconstruction theorems (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.2.11 and its proof, pp.284–285).

*Needs:* ReductiveGroups §11C; NilpotentOrbits N1 and N5.0; FormalConnectionsOpers F4.3, finite Chevalley restriction and rational descent; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `RootPairing`, `CoxeterSystem`, `Module.Dual`; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- In `gl₂` over `𝔽₅`, the trace pairing identifies adjoint and coadjoint representations and the quotient coordinates are `(tr,det)` of degrees `1,2`. They take `E₁₂` to `(0,0)` and `diag(1,−1)` to `(0,4)`, so trace alone gives the wrong zero fibre. With `q=2`, `n=1`, the degree factors are `1` and `3`, both units.
- For `G_m`, the quotient is the identity on a line and its zero fibre is `{0}`. With `q=3`, `ℓ=2`, `n=1`, the degree-one factor is `0`; the nonzero singularity line of 1.16 is not forced into the cone. For the trivial group the quotient and zero fibre are points, with no degrees to test.
- In `sl₂` over characteristic `2`, the degree-two numerical test gives `q²−1=0` for every odd `q`; the trace form has the nonzero identity matrix in its radical. The hypotheses exclude this failed pairing.
- Over `ℚ_ℓ[ε]/(ε²)` for `G_m`, the section `ε` has zero image at every geometric point but its degree-one invariant is `ε ≠ 0`; it does not lie in the scheme-theoretic zero fibre. For `gl₂`, `diag(ε,−ε)` has trace and determinant both zero, hence does lie in that fibre.

### 1.13 Weil–Deligne parameters

- Prove that gauge sends `(φ₀,N)` to `(h·φ₀,Ad(h)N)` and preserves all conditions.
- For every discrete-inertia cocycle construct the parameter with monodromy zero, compatibly with gauge and coefficient change.

Nilpotence here is in the Lie algebra, distinct from the dual nilpotent cone of 1.16. For split `GL_n`, matrix nilpotence and scaling alone do not impose discrete inertia or the actual geometric Weil degree. Over nonreduced rings, matrix nilpotence is weaker than membership in the scheme-theoretic nilpotent cone.

**Checks.**

- An unramified `φ₀` with `N = 0` is a Weil–Deligne parameter.

Over a reduced ring, a nilpotent `1 × 1` matrix is zero; over `ℚ[ε]/(ε²)` with the zero degree map, `N = ε` satisfies algebraic matrix nilpotence and scaling but is not in the torus nilpotent cone.
- Gauge by `h` takes `(φ₀,0)` to `(h·φ₀,0)`.
- In split `GL₂` with `q = 3`, `N = E₁₂` and `φ₀(σ) = diag(1/3, 1)`, conjugation sends `N` to `N/3 = q⁻¹ N`, compatible with `σ⁻¹ τ σ = τ³`; the reciprocal matrix `diag(3, 1)` gives `3N` and fails the condition.

### 1.18 Nilpotence of singularity fibres

At `φ` over a `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L`, prove that the fibre of the relative singularity stack is contained in the dual nilpotent cone if either `L` has characteristic zero, or `ℓ ∤ q^{en} − 1` for every degree `e` of a homogeneous generator of the Chevalley invariants, where `n` is the residue degree of the extension cutting out the action. Use 1.12 for the homogeneous coadjoint invariants, their degrees and their zero-fibre criterion. A covector fixed by the Tate-twisted coadjoint action is conjugate to `q^n` times itself after the pinned action is killed, so an invariant of degree `e` satisfies `f(v)=q^{en}f(v)`. Thus all positive-degree invariants vanish when `q^{en} − 1` is invertible (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.2.11 with Remarks VIII.2.12–VIII.2.13, pp.284–285). This is an inclusion, not an equality, and nothing is asserted in the excluded case. *Needs:* 1.16; 1.12; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ClassFieldTheory layer 9.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `ℓ ∤ q − 1` both the fibre and the cone are zero.
- At a smooth point of the `GL₂` parameter stack the fibre is zero while the nilpotent cone is not, so equality fails.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `ℓ | q − 1` the hypothesis fails and the fibre is not in the cone (1.16).

### Examples

The tame torus `G_m × μ_{q−1}` is the running example: flat, a complete intersection of relative dimension one, nonreduced at `ℓ | q − 1`, with a one-dimensional singularity fibre exactly in that case. The `GL₂` matrix `diag(1/3, 1)` with `N = E₁₂` fixes the Weil–Deligne sign convention.

## Layer 2: coarse quotients, the excursion presentation and excursion operators

This layer builds the coarse quotient and the free-group presentation of the cocycle algebra with no restriction on `ℓ`, and the excursion operators of an abstract categorical Hecke action. ### 2.1 Coarse parameter quotients

For a finite-wild affine piece `X=Z¹(W,Ĥ)` with coordinate algebra `A` and gauge coaction `δ : A → O(Ĥ) ⊗ A`, define the coarse quotient `X//Ĥ=Spec A^Ĥ` using the coaction equalizer of the supplier contract. Its quotient map `X → X//Ĥ` is induced by the invariant inclusion (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.3.1, Definition VIII.3.1 and Proposition VIII.3.2, pp.285–287; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Propositions 3.2 and 3.10, pp.11–12 and 15–16).

- Transport `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.homEquivAlgHom` to prove unique factorization of invariant morphisms from `X` to affine schemes through the quotient map.
- Construct inflation for inclusions of finite-wild pieces, prove identity and composition laws, and fix its orientation opposite to the coordinate map.
- Specialize `AlgHom.tensorEqualizer` to `δ` and `Algebra.TensorProduct.includeRight`. Identify its target with `(S ⊗_R A)^{Ĥ_S}` by the tensor-associativity and coefficient-change isomorphisms for `O(Ĥ_S)`, carrying the two tensor maps to the base-changed coaction and unit coaction. The supplied comparison then sends `s ⊗ a` to `s ⊗ a`; use `AlgHom.tensorEqualizerEquiv` when `S` is flat over `R`. Prove compatibility of this identification with the quotient map.

Form the quotient piece by piece; the infinite union is not made affine. Finite type and orbit classification are 2.2.

*Needs:* 1.1; 1.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1; Mathlib `AlgHom.equalizer`, `AlgHom.tensorEqualizer`, `AlgHom.tensorEqualizerEquiv`, `Algebra.TensorProduct.includeRight`, `Subalgebra`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = 1` the invariant algebra is all of `A` and the quotient map is the identity; for a coaction equal to `a ↦ 1 ⊗ a` (a torus acting trivially on a free-group cocycle space) the equalizer is `A`.
- For the scaling action of `G_m` over `𝔽₂` on `𝔽₂[x]`, the coaction invariants are `𝔽₂`, while the abstract group `G_m(𝔽₂) = 1` fixes everything: invariants must be taken for the coaction, not for rational points.
- For `C₂` acting on `ℤ[x]` by `x ↦ −x` the invariants are `ℤ[x²]`; after reduction mod `2` the element `x` is invariant but not in the image of `𝔽₂ ⊗ ℤ[x²]`, so the base change map exists and is not surjective for the non-flat `ℤ → 𝔽₂`.
- For `Γ = ℤ`, `Ĥ = SL₂` over a field of characteristic zero, the unipotent `U = [[1, 1], [0, 1]]` and the identity have the same image in the coarse quotient (the curve `[[1, t²], [0, 1]]` is conjugate to `U` by `diag(t, t⁻¹)` for `t ≠ 0`), although they are not conjugate.

### 2.3 Complete reducibility and strong reductivity

Use `IsGCompletelyReducible`, `IsAbsolutelyGCompletelyReducible`, their conjugacy invariance and the `GL(V)` semisimplicity comparison from ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*. For connected reductive `G` over algebraically closed `k` and closed `H ⊂ G`, define `G`-irreducibility by containment in no proper parabolic, and strong reductivity by containment in no proper parabolic of `Z_G(S)` for a maximal torus `S` of `Z_G(H)`. A homomorphism is strongly `G`-irreducible when every homomorphism with the same one-variable invariant values is absolutely `G`-irreducible (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, Definitions 3.3 and 3.5, pp.11–12).

- Prove independence of the maximal torus in strong reductivity. Apply the conditions to homomorphisms through their Zariski image and over general fields after extension to an algebraic closure.
- Prove conjugacy invariance of the additional predicates and that irreducibility implies complete reducibility.
- For generalized reductive `G`, extend these notions using all R-parabolics `P_λ={g : lim_{t→0} λ(t)gλ(t)⁻¹ exists}` and R-Levis `L_λ=Z_G(λ)` for cocharacters of `G⁰`. Prove maximal-torus independence and conjugacy invariance for the extended strong-reductivity condition (Bate–Martin–Röhrle 2005, §6; Quast, §2.5, Definition 2.10; Fargues–Scholze, p.287).

For `J=Ĥ ⋊ Q` with arbitrary `η : Γ → Q`, test all containing R-parabolics. They necessarily surject onto `Q` only if `η` does.

*Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 7; Mathlib `Subgroup`, `Representation`.

**Checks.**

- The diagonal torus of `SL₂` is completely reducible, strongly reductive and absolutely completely reducible, and its inclusion is not strongly `SL₂`-irreducible: it is itself reducible.
- The upper unipotent subgroup of `SL₂` lies in the upper Borel and in no Levi. It is neither strongly reductive nor absolutely completely reducible, and its inclusion is not strongly `SL₂`-irreducible.
- For `GL(V)`, the Zariski image of a representation is completely reducible exactly when `V` is semisimple.
- The trivial subgroup is strongly reductive and absolutely completely reducible, and is irreducible only when `G` is a torus. Over `ℂ`, the identity representation of `SL₂(ℂ)` is strongly `SL₂`-irreducible: equality of one-variable invariants gives equality of traces, so its semisimplification remains the irreducible standard module.
- In `J = SL₂² ⋊ C₂` with the swapping action and trivial `η`, send two free generators to `(U,D)` and `(1,W)`, with `U = [[1,1],[0,1]]`, `D = diag(2,1/2)`, `W = [[0,−1],[1,0]]`. The second projection is irreducible, so no proper component-surjective parabolic contains the image, but `diag(t,t⁻¹)` in the first factor sends `U` to `1` in the limit. Its orbit is not closed, and the all-R-parabolic criterion excludes it.

### 2.4 Closed tuples and Levi semisimplification

For connected reductive `G` over algebraically closed `k` in any characteristic, consume ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*: the simultaneous orbit of `x ∈ G(k)ⁿ` is closed exactly when the subgroup generated by its entries has `G`-completely reducible Zariski closure `H`, and minimal-parabolic projection to a Levi gives a cocharacter limit in the unique closed orbit of the orbit closure, independent up to conjugacy of the choices. Prove that a completely reducible `H` is irreducible in any Levi containing it of a minimal parabolic containing it, and that all minimal parabolics containing `H` have the same dimension (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, Theorem 3.4 and Proposition 3.7, pp.12–13, after Richardson 1988 and Bate–Martin–Röhrle 2005).

Extend Levi semisimplification with uniqueness up to `G⁰(k)`-conjugacy, and prove that minimal containing R-parabolics have the same dimension and component count (Quast, §2.5, Proposition 2.15, and Lemmas 3.4–3.6, pp.10 and 12–13; Fargues–Scholze, proof of Proposition VIII.3.2, pp.286–287). No very-good-characteristic hypothesis is needed; separability is 4.23. *Needs:* ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.11, *Complete reducibility and closed tuples*; 2.3; 2.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- A diagonal semisimple tuple has closed orbit.
- A tuple generating an irreducible subgroup of `GL_n` has a stable orbit with finite stabilizer modulo the centre.

### 2.5 Semisimple L-parameters

### 2.6 Closed parameter orbits

Prove that for every finite-wild piece over an algebraically closed `L`, the closed `Ĥ(L)`-orbits of cocycles are exactly the semisimple parameters, equivalently those conjugate to the Levi projection of every factorization through a standard parabolic; hence the `L`-points of the coarse quotient classify semisimple parameters up to conjugacy, with no condition on `π₁(Ĥ)_tors`. **Checks.**

### 2.14 Excursion matrix coefficients

- For a coefficient `f`, use its finite projective regular-function presentation, with `α_f=f` and `β_f` evaluation at the unit, and prove that it presents the same operator.
- Prove that equal coefficient functions give equal operators for fixed `I` and tuple.
- Prove that pullback of coefficient functions agrees with reindexing of the datum, and that the coefficient of an external tensor product at a concatenated tuple is `f_D(g)f_{D′}(g′)`. The reindexing square commutes; it need not be a pullback.

*Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Finite-wild excursion algebra*; 2.9; 2.13; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `V = 1` and `α = β = id` the coefficient is `1`; for `α = 0` it is `0`.
- The coefficient of an external tensor product is the product of the coefficients, so scalar coefficients `2` and `3` give `6`.
- For diagonal `a, b ∈ Ĥ`, `f_D(a g_i b) = f_D(g_i)`; a coefficient built from a non-invariant `α` fails this.
- An external tensor product with tuple entries `2` and `3` in `Γ = ℤ` keeps the legs in order: for two different characters on the two legs, exchanging the entries changes the value.

### 2.15 Abstract excursion centre maps

Prove that every categorical Hecke datum for `Γ` over `Q` on a `ℤ_ℓ`-linear stable ∞-category `C` induces a natural `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra map `Exc(Γ, Ĥ) → π₀ End(id_C)` sending an invariant function evaluated at a tuple to its excursion operator. Operator independence comes from 2.14; external tensors and folding give multiplicativity and the trivial representation the unit; inserting an extra coordinate over `1 ∈ Q` identifies the bi-invariant functions with the simultaneous-conjugation invariants on `n` coordinates; reindexing and the identity `γγ′⁻¹γ″` from evaluation and coevaluation give the relations of 2.10 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.4.1 and Definition VIII.4.2 with proofs, pp.290–293; Lafforgue, Lemma 10.1 and Proposition 10.8, pp.133–135 and 138–139). No condition on `ℓ` enters, and the Bernstein-centre application belongs to the consumers. For an ordinary category the target is `End(id_C)`. *Needs:* 2.14; 2.10; 2.12; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.CatCenter`.

**Checks.**

- The zero category gives the zero map into its zero centre.
- The unit datum maps to `1`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` and `Γ = ℤ` the map sends `t` to the operator of the standard character at the generator.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = SL₂` and `Γ = ℤ` in characteristic zero the unipotent and the trivial parameter have the same coarse image and the same excursion character; only the trivial one is semisimple. For `Ĥ = G_m` the excursion algebra of `ℤ` is `ℤ_ℓ[t, t⁻¹]` and every orbit is closed.

## Layer 3: pseudocharacters with prescribed projection and local continuity

This layer reconstructs semisimple parameters from invariant evaluations. The group is `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q`, whose component group may be nontrivial, so the reconstruction theorem is proved here for groups with reductive identity component, using only the unconditional invariant theory of Layer 2. Algebraic reconstruction, discrete continuity and the relatively discrete characteristic-zero extension are separate statements. The trace adapter is a comparison on the group algebra, not a replacement for the full invariant families.

### 3.1 Algebra trace pseudocharacters and determinant laws

Use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants Layer 0 for an associative unital `A`-algebra `R` and `d ≥ 0`: *Pseudocharacters* supplies linear traces with `T(1)=d`, cyclic symmetry and the signed degree-`d+1` cycle identity, including fixed one-cycles; *Determinants (Chenevier)* supplies multiplicative homogeneous polynomial laws of degree `d`, natural on all commutative coefficient algebras; *Characteristic polynomial and trace of a determinant* fixes the trace as the coefficient of `t` in `D(1+tx)`. Use its coefficient change, *Determinant of a finite projective representation* for constant rank `d`, and *Over ℚ-algebras determinants are pseudocharacters* for the rational trace–determinant bijection. 3.7 extends these supplied traces to the group-algebra adapter; 3.8 compares that adapter with the supplied connected `GL_d` invariant families (Chenevier, arXiv:0809.0415v2, §§1.1–1.5, 1.10–1.12 and Proposition 1.27, pp.6–9, 12–14, 20). *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§0.2, 0.5–0.6, the named targets above.

**Checks.**

- A degree-one determinant is an algebra homomorphism; a degree-zero determinant is the constant law `1` with trace zero.
- For `d = 2` over a `ℚ`-algebra, `D(x) = (T(x)²−T(x²))/2`; the trace of the identity is `2`.
- Over `𝔽₂`, the zero trace occurs for both rank zero and the trivial rank-two representation, whereas their determinant laws have different degrees; no trace-only reconstruction is asserted there.

### 3.2 Pseudocharacters with prescribed finite projection

Let `G` be a generalized reductive group scheme over a noetherian ring `O`, meaning smooth affine with reductive identity component `G⁰` and finite étale `G/G⁰` (Quast, Definition 2.3, p.6). The case used is `J=Ĥ ⋊ Q` over `ℤ_ℓ`, even when `ℓ` divides `|Q|`. Define a `G`-pseudocharacter of `Γ` over a commutative `O`-algebra `A` as `O`-algebra maps `Θ_m : O[G^m]^{G⁰} → Map(Γ^m,A)`, `m≥1`, compatible with finite-set reindexing and multiplication of the last two coordinates; arity zero is the coefficient map (Quast, Definition 3.1, p.11). For fixed `η : Γ → Q`, define projected pseudocharacters by requiring `Θ_n(e_{q̄})(γ̄)` to equal `1` when `η(γ̄)=q̄` and `0` otherwise, where `e_{q̄}` is the component idempotent.

- Extend the connected families and universal ring of IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7 to generalized reductive `G` using `G⁰`-invariants. Prove agreement with the supplied carriers on every evaluation when `G` is connected; whole-`G` invariants do not give this extension.
- Impose the component equations by quotienting the extended universal algebra, and prove its representing bijection. The forgetful map to all `J`-pseudocharacters is injective.
- For a lift `ρ : Γ → J(A)` with `π ∘ ρ=η`, construct its pseudocharacter by invariant evaluation and prove the component equations.
- Prove coefficient-change identity and composition laws, preserving `η` without asserting non-flat invariant base change. Prove that precomposition by `f : Γ′ → Γ` changes the projection to `η ∘ f`.
- Prove extensionality by equality of all evaluations. For topological coefficients and continuous `η` into discrete `Q`, define continuity by continuity of every evaluation (Quast, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.5, pp.11–13; Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.7 and §VIII.3.3, pp.288–290; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Definition 4.1 and Lemma 4.3, pp.19–20).

*Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Connected reductive pseudocharacter*, *Universal reductive pseudocharacter ring*; 2.10; 2.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Q = 1` and `Ĥ = G_m`, a character `χ` has `Θ_n(t₁^{a₁} ⋯ t_n^{a_n})(γ̄) = ∏ χ(γ_i)^{a_i}`, negative exponents included, and these families are exactly the characters `Γ → Aˣ`.
- `Ĥ`-conjugate lifts with the same `η` define the same element.
- For `Γ = 1` the trivial lift evaluates the identity component idempotent to `1` and the others to `0`.
- Over a nonzero ring, a lift with a different projection is not in the `η`-fibre: some component idempotent would have to evaluate to both `0` and `1`.
- For `Ĥ = G_m`, `Q = C₂` acting by inversion, `Γ = ℤ` and `η = 1`, the lifts with generator `2` and `1/2` are not `Ĥ`-conjugate and are separated by the identity-component coordinate `x` extended by zero, while every function invariant under conjugation by all of `J` takes the same values on them: invariants under `J` instead of `J⁰` would identify them.

### 3.3 Reconstruction for groups with reductive identity component

Extend reconstruction to generalized reductive groups, using the supplied connected pseudocharacter objects: for `G` generalized reductive over a noetherian `O`, any group `Γ`, an algebraically closed `O`-field `k` and `Θ ∈ PC_G^Γ(k)`, there is a `G`-completely reducible `ρ : Γ → G(k)` with `Θ_ρ = Θ`, unique up to `G⁰(k)`-conjugacy, with complete reducibility tested against all R-parabolics and R-Levis as in 2.3 (Quast, Theorem 3.7 and proof, pp.13–15; for split connected `G` over `ℤ`, Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Theorem 4.5, p.20). Prove it through the following statements, each a target. (i) Closed representatives: each point of `(Gⁿ//G⁰)(k)` has exactly one closed `G⁰(k)`-orbit above it, and the Zariski closure of the group generated by a closed tuple is `G`-completely reducible (Quast, Lemmas 3.4–3.5, p.12, from 2.2 and 2.4 in the non-connected form). (ii) The dimension and component count of a minimal parabolic containing such a subgroup do not depend on the parabolic (Quast, Lemma 3.6, p.13). (iii) An extremal tuple: some `δ ∈ Γⁿ` maximizes first the dimension and then the component count of the minimal parabolic, and among those minimizes first the dimension and then the component count of the centralizer (Quast, conditions (1)–(4) in the proof of Theorem 3.7, p.13). (iv) One-entry extension: with `g` the closed representative of `δ`, for each `γ` there is a unique `h ∈ G(k)` with `(g, h)` conjugate to the closed representative of `(δ, γ)` (Claim A, p.14). (v) Two-entry extension and multiplicativity: the analogous statement for `(δ, γ, γ′)`, the closedness of the orbits of `(g, h)`, `(g, h′)` and `(g, hh′)`, and hence `ρ(γγ′) = ρ(γ)ρ(γ′)`, `ρ(1) = 1` and `Θ_ρ = Θ` (Claims B–C and the end of the proof, pp.14–15). Then specialise to `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` with prescribed `η`: the one-variable component idempotents force `π ∘ ρ = η`, the `Ĥ`-coordinate of `ρ` is a crossed cocycle for the action through `η`, `Ĥ`-conjugation of the lift is gauge of the cocycle, and the section equivalence of 0.1 makes the two constructions inverse. So the `η`-fibre of `J`-pseudocharacters over `k` is in bijection with `Ĥ(k)`-gauge classes of semisimple crossed cocycles `Γ → Ĥ(k)` (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.8 and proof, p.290). For `G = GL_d`, `d ≥ 1`, use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §§1.2 and 1.7 for reconstruction. Prove that the generalized reconstruction specializes to that same conjugacy class by uniqueness and the supplied §0.7 determinant comparison, preserving every characteristic coefficient. The connected non-`GL_d` and nonconnected cases are included in this general target. No good-`π₁` or prime-to-`|Q|` hypothesis enters; its invariant theory is unconditional. *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Determinants and GLn excursion pseudocharacters*, §1.2, *Semisimple reconstruction of field determinants*, §1.7, *GL pseudocharacter reconstruction*; 3.2; 2.2; 2.4; 2.5; 0.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Q = 1` and `G = GL_d`, the resulting conjugacy class is the supplied reconstruction; its determinant and all characteristic coefficients agree in every characteristic.
- For `Ĥ = 1` only the chosen `η` survives, so allowing every `Γ → Q` would give the wrong space.
- For `G = GL_r` in small characteristic the full invariant family keeps the multiplicities of the semisimplification while traces alone do not.
- For the unipotent representation of `ℤ` in `SL₂` the reconstructed representation is the trivial one.

### 3.4 Relatively discrete Weil continuity

For an algebraically closed `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L` of characteristic zero and the semisimple lift `W_E → Ĥ(L) ⋊ Q` reconstructed in 3.3, prove that condensed invariant evaluations in the relatively discrete convention make the lift a condensed L-parameter (0.2); for `L` with a rank-one valuation topology this is the usual continuity. Only compact inertia needs an argument; the degree quotient is discrete. The argument takes the extremal tuple of 3.3 with its last entry in inertia, expresses the finitely many coordinates of the reconstructed element as continuous functions of invariant evaluations, extends this to `J = Ĥ ⋊ Q` with `Ĥ`-conjugation, and proves that these coordinate functions take values locally in finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-modules; the crossed cocycle law and the fixed `Q`-action transport the conclusion from inertia to its open Weil cosets (Lafforgue, Proposition 11.7, p.143, and its continuity argument, pp.146–147; Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 4.7(ii), pp.23–24, connected and profinite; Quast, Theorem 3.8, p.15; Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.1.1 and Proposition VIII.3.8, pp.278 and 290). The finite-type coefficient statement is part of this target; it does not follow from rank-one valued continuity alone, and the compactness argument of Quast's Theorem 3.8 for profinite `Γ` is not the extension to the Weil group. *Needs:* 3.3; 0.2; ClassFieldTheory layer 9; 3.2 (continuity of invariant evaluations).

**Checks.**

- An unramified character with `ρ(Fr) = q` has infinite Weil image and trivial inertia image, and its coordinates on each compact inertia coset lie in a rank-one `ℤ_ℓ`-module.
- A split torus Weil character must satisfy `χ(τ)^{q−1} = 1`, so the restriction of an arbitrary infinite-image inertia character need not extend to the Weil group.
- For `Q = 1` and profinite `Γ` the rank-one valued specialization is the continuity assertion of Lafforgue, Proposition 11.7. The non-semisimple `GL₂` parameter of 0.2 witnesses infinite inertia in the ambient condensed convention, without being an example of semisimple reconstruction.

### 3.5 Discrete-coefficient Weil continuity

Let `L` be an algebraically closed discrete `ℤ_ℓ`-field and `η : W_E → Q` the continuous action quotient. Prove that a semisimple lift reconstructed from continuous invariant evaluations has open kernel and finite image on inertia and is continuous on `W_E`; in characteristic `ℓ` this is exactly the relatively discrete convention, and the Frobenius image need not be finite. The proof chooses finitely many generators of the integral tuple-invariant algebra (finite generation from 2.2, with the component idempotents among them), uses the extremal tuple and the unique one-entry extension of 3.3 to show that on an open inertia neighbourhood where all generator values equal their values at `1` the lift is `1`, and transports continuity to `W_E` by the homomorphism law (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 4.7(iii) and proof, pp.23–24; Quast, Theorem 3.7, Claim A, p.14). The topological step is a target in its own right: for an open compact subgroup `I` of a topological group `W`, a homomorphism `ρ : W → J` and functions `f_1, …, f_n` from `J` to a discrete space with each `i ↦ f_j(ρ(i))` continuous on `I` and with `f_j(ρ(i)) = f_j(1)` for all `j` implying `ρ(i) = 1`, the kernel of `ρ|_I` is open, `ρ(I)` is finite by compactness and `ρ` is continuous when `J` is discrete by openness of `I`. Non-flat integral invariants are not replaced by fibrewise invariants, and there is no good-`π₁` or prime-to-`|Q|` condition. *Needs:* 3.3; 2.2; ClassFieldTheory layer 9.

**Checks.**

- The unit homomorphism has the whole inertia subgroup as kernel and image `{1}`; with no coordinate functions, the separation hypothesis forces `ρ|_I = 1`.
- A constant coordinate on `C₂` is continuous but does not separate the nontrivial element from `1`: the separation hypothesis cannot be dropped.
- On the discrete degree group `ℤ`, `k ↦ 2^k` into `ℚˣ` is continuous, sends geometric Frobenius to `2`, is trivial on inertia and has infinite image: finite image holds on inertia only.
- For `Q = 1` and discrete `L`, the statement agrees on inertia with the connected profinite theorem.

### 3.6 Continuous semisimple parameter characters

For an algebraically closed `ℤ_ℓ`-field `L`, prove canonical bijections between (i) semisimple condensed L-parameters up to `Ĥ(L)`-conjugacy, (ii) `L`-points of the piecewise coarse parameter quotient, and (iii) condensed families `Θ_n` on `W_Eⁿ` with the reindexing and ordered-multiplication relations, at every prime `ℓ ≠ p` (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.3.8 and proof, p.290). Combine 3.3 for the algebraic bijection, 3.4 or 3.5 for continuity according to the characteristic, the dense-model extension of 0.4, and 2.9's universal homeomorphism for the comparison of excursion and coarse points. No good-`π₁` assumption, Layer 4 result or non-flat base change isomorphism is used. *Needs:* 2.6; 2.9; 3.3; 3.4; 3.5; 0.3.

**Checks.**

- Both `ℚ̄_ℓ` and `𝔽̄_ℓ` satisfy the classification with their own continuity conventions.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` all three sets are the continuous characters of `W_E`.
- The non-semisimple `SL₂` parameter is not in (i) and has the same image in (ii) and (iii) as the trivial one.

### 3.7 Group-algebra trace adapter

For a group `Γ`, a commutative ring `A` and `r≥0`, specialize 3.1's algebra trace pseudocharacters to `A[Γ]`. Define restriction by `T ↦ (γ ↦ T([γ]))` and extension by the unique `A`-linear map `T(∑ a_γ[γ])=∑ a_γτ(γ)`, using `Finsupp.linearCombination` (Lafforgue, Remark 11.8, pp.143–144).

- Prove restriction and extension give inverse bijections between these pseudocharacters and functions with `τ(1)=r`, cyclic symmetry `τ(xy)=τ(yx)` and the signed cycle identity on group elements, including fixed one-cycles.
- Identify cycle terms on basis vectors with the corresponding group-word cycle terms. Multilinearity makes the basis identities sufficient; cyclic symmetry extends from group elements to the whole group algebra.
- Identify restriction of the supplied representation trace with `γ ↦ tr ρ(γ)` and prove that coefficient change commutes with restriction and extension. No small-characteristic classification by traces is asserted.

*Needs:* 3.1; Mathlib `MonoidAlgebra`, `MonoidAlgebra.of`, `Finsupp.linearCombination`, `Equiv.Perm.cycleFactorsFinset`.

**Checks.**

- For `r = 1` the identity is `τ(x)τ(y) = τ(xy)`, so normalised rank-one pseudocharacters are characters; for `r = 0` the identity forces `τ = 0`.
- The trace of a direct sum of characters is a pseudocharacter of the sum of their ranks; the constant function `1` is not of rank two in characteristic zero because `τ(1)` must be `2`.
- For distinct `γ, δ`, the linear extension takes `2[γ]−[δ]` to `2τ(γ)−τ(δ)`.
- For `A = ℚ`, `Γ = 1` and `τ = 2`, `T(1·1)=2` while `T(1)T(1)=4`: the extension is linear, not multiplicative.

### 3.8 Trace and excursion character comparison

For an algebraically closed field `L` of characteristic zero, use IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7's `GL_r` determinant comparison, Layer 0's rational trace–determinant bijection and §1.7's semisimple reconstruction. Prove that their composite, applied to the group-algebra adapter of 3.7, matches the excursion evaluation: the invariant functions on tuples are generated by cycle-trace functions of permutations and inverse determinants, which Cayley–Hamilton and Newton identities express through traces of words (Lafforgue, Remark 11.8, pp.143–144, with the Procesi and Taylor inputs cited there). Identify the invariant-family/trace map on these generators with the supplied determinant–trace composite and 3.7's linear extension; the semisimple class is the supplied one, as identified in 3.3. For `r = 0` all three objects are unique, with zero trace and determinant law `1`. Characteristic zero is essential for the trace statement; in general characteristic the full invariant data of 3.3 are needed. *Needs:* IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants §0.7, *Determinants and GLn excursion pseudocharacters*, §1.7, *GL pseudocharacter reconstruction*; 3.7; 3.3; 3.1.

**Checks.**

- For `Γ = 1` the unique semisimple rank-`r` representation has `τ(1) = r`.
- The unipotent and trivial representations of `ℤ` in `GL₂` have the same trace function and correspond to the same class.
- In characteristic `2`, `τ = 0` is the trace of both the zero-dimensional and the two-dimensional trivial representations, so traces do not determine the rank there.

### 3.9 Parameters of Schur objects

If `L` is algebraically closed and a categorical Hecke datum for `Γ` acts on `C`, prove that every object `X` with a given `L`-algebra isomorphism `π₀ End_C(X) ≅ L` receives a unique semisimple cocycle `Γ → Ĥ(L)` up to `Ĥ(L)`-conjugacy whose invariant evaluations equal the excursion action on `X`: evaluate the centre map of 2.15 on `X`, compose with the given isomorphism, and apply 2.10 and 3.3 (Fargues–Scholze, Corollary VIII.4.3, pp.292–293). For an ordinary category the endomorphism algebra is the ordinary one. Continuity on `W_E` is not inferred from an arbitrary discrete datum. *Needs:* 2.15; 3.3.

**Checks.**

- For `C = Perf(L)` with the datum of a semisimple parameter `φ`, the object `L` recovers `φ`.
- The zero object has no `L`-algebra isomorphism `π₀ End = L` and gets no parameter.
- An object whose endomorphism algebra is `L × L` is excluded.

For `Ĥ = G_m`, `Q = C₂` acting by inversion, `Γ = ℤ` and trivial prescribed projection `η`, the parameters with generator `2` and `1/2` are distinct semisimple parameters with the same full-`J` invariants; the prescribed-projection pseudocharacter with `Ĥ`-invariants distinguishes them, which is why 3.2 uses identity-component invariants.

## Layer 4: good filtrations, fixed groups and wild reduction

Layer 4 works over an algebraically closed field `L` of characteristic `ℓ`, in the stable ∞-category `Ind Perf(BG)`, where `G` plays the role of the dual group and its subgroups. It first states the highest-weight theory it needs, then builds the good-filtration t-structure, induced perfect complexes and their criteria, generation for adjoint and free-group quotients and for derived unit fibres, the theory of fixed groups of solvable groups of order prime to `ℓ`, and the gerbe comparisons that eliminate wild inertia. Its last four targets are the separability, étale-descent, slice and Chevalley-restriction inputs used by the integral theory.

### 4.1 Highest-weight modules and good filtrations

Let `G` be split reductive over a field `k`, with positive Borel `B⁺`, opposite lower Borel `B=B⁻`, and maximal torus `T`. Use the induced modules `∇_λ=H⁰(λ)` and Weyl modules `Δ_λ=H⁰(−w₀λ)^*` of ReductiveGroups §10B, with its dominance/nonvanishing and arbitrary-field base change. Transport the convention `f(gb)=λ(b)⁻¹f(g)` and `(a·f)(g)=f(a⁻¹g)` to SchubertGeometry S3.2; ordinary duality changes the weight by `−w₀`.

Define a good filtration of a rational `G`-module `V` as an exhaustive ascending filtration whose successive quotients are direct sums of `∇_λ`. Define its good filtration dimension as the least nonnegative `i` for which `H^j(G,V ⊗ ∇_λ)=0` for all `j>i` and dominant `λ`, and as infinite if no such `i` exists (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.5.1, p.294).

- Construct the rational-group-cohomology comparison from the section comparison and dominant vanishing of SchubertGeometry S3.2 and S5.5–S5.6. Deduce `H^i(G,∇_λ)=0` for `i>0` and that `H^i(G,∇_λ ⊗ ∇_μ)` is `k` for `i=0`, `μ=−w₀λ`, and zero otherwise (Jantzen, II.4.5, p.204 and II.4.13, p.209; Kempf 1976).
- Prove Donkin's criterion: a good filtration is equivalent to `Ext¹_G(Δ_λ,V)=0` for every `λ`, and to `H^i(G,V ⊗ ∇_λ)=0` for every `i>0` and `λ`. Identify finite good filtration dimension with the least length of a resolution by good-filtration modules (Jantzen, II.4.16, Proposition and Remark 1, pp.210–211, II.4.17, remark, p.212; Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.3 and following discussion, p.294).
- Prove tensor stability of good filtrations (Jantzen, II.4.21, p.213; Fargues–Scholze, p.294, after Donkin and Mathieu).
- Prove that `O(G)` under left and right multiplication has a good `G × G`-filtration with factors `∇_λ ⊗ ∇_{−w₀λ}` (Jantzen, II.4.20, p.213; Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.6, p.295, after Koppinen and Donkin).
- If `G` acts on a finitely generated commutative algebra `A` with a good filtration and compatibly on a noetherian `A`-module `M`, prove that `M` has finite good filtration dimension and `H^i(G,M)=0` for sufficiently large `i` (Touzé–van der Kallen, arXiv:0809.1014v3, Theorem 1.5, p.3; Fargues–Scholze cites the published result as Corollary 1.5, p.297).

For a group with reductive identity component and component group of order prime to `ℓ`, take good filtrations with respect to `G°`.

*Needs:* ReductiveGroups §10B; SchubertGeometry S3.2 and S5.5–S5.6; ReductiveGroups layer 9; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `G = GL₂` with the lower Borel, `∇_{(1,0)}` is the standard representation, `∇_{(0,−1)}` its dual, and `∇_{(0,1)} = 0`: a definition with the opposite Borel or the inverse character makes `(0, 1)` the nonzero case.
- For a torus every module is a direct sum of characters, every `∇_λ` is one-dimensional and every module has a good filtration of dimension `0`.
- For `G = SL₂` in characteristic `2`, the module `∇_2` has a good filtration but its submodule `L(2)` (the Frobenius twist of the standard representation) does not, because `Ext¹_G(Δ_0, L(2)) ≠ 0`: good filtrations are not inherited by submodules.
- For a positive-dimensional torus, `O(G) = k[X^*(G)]` under conjugation is an infinite direct sum of copies of `∇_0` and has a one-step good filtration under the direct-sum convention. Requiring finitely many finite-dimensional factors would exclude it. For `G = 1`, `O(G) = k` has a one-step good filtration.

### 4.2 Good-filtration t-structures

Over algebraically closed `L` of characteristic `ℓ`, let `G°` be reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`. Construct the good-filtration t-structure on `Ind Perf(BG)` (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.5.1 and Definition VIII.5.4, pp.294–295).

- Prove that `M` is connective exactly when `H^i(G°,M ⊗ ∇_λ)=0` for all `i>0` and all dominant `λ`, and coconnective exactly when `H^i(G°,M ⊗ Δ_λ)=0` for all `i<0` and all `λ`.
- Construct finite-index induction and its adjunction with restriction. Prove that the connective part is generated under colimits by `∇_λ` for connected `G`, and by `Ind_{G°}^G ∇_λ` in general. Restriction of induction is a finite sum of conjugate highest-weight modules, and averaging over `π₀G` splits its counit.
- Prove that tensor products of connective objects are connective by 4.1's tensor theorem.
- Equip the homotopy category with the resulting `CategoryTheory.Triangulated.TStructure`; this does not recover the enhancement. For tori, perfect objects are stable under these truncations; that is not asserted for general reductive groups.

*Needs:* 4.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*, *presentability*; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Triangulated.TStructure`.

**Checks.**

- For a torus every rational module is a sum of weights, and the good-filtration t-structure is the usual one.
- The zero object lies in both halves; for connected `G`, `∇_λ` in degree zero is connective.
- For `G = C₂` over `𝔽̄₃`, `G° = 1`: the sign representation is connective but is not generated by the trivial representation. `Ind_1^{C₂} 1 = 1 ⊕ sign` supplies both components.
- For a nonzero torus representation `V`, the object `V[−1]` sits in cohomological degree `1` and is not connective for the convention `D^{≤0}`.

### 4.3 Separatedness of good filtrations

Prove that the good-filtration t-structure on `Ind Perf(BG)` is separated: an object that is infinitely connective is zero, since all `RΓ(G°, Res_{G°} M ⊗ ∇_λ)` then vanish and these detect zero, using finite-index induction and its adjunction from 4.2 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.5 and proof, p.295). For groups with infinite rational cohomological dimension, the standard t-structure on `Ind Perf(BG)` can fail to be separated. For tori it is separated and agrees with the good-filtration t-structure; separatedness of the good-filtration one is what justifies the later bar-construction limits. *Needs:* 4.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- An object whose underlying module vanishes in an Ind-quotient is not thereby zero: detection uses `∇_λ` for connected `G` and `Ind_{G°}^G ∇_λ` for disconnected `G`.
- For a torus separatedness is that of the standard t-structure.
- For connected `G`, a nonzero `∇_λ` is not infinitely connective because `H⁰(G, ∇_λ ⊗ ∇_{−w₀λ}) = L`.

### 4.4 Good filtrations of free cocycle algebras

Prove that for every map `F_n → Aut(G)`, the algebra `O(Z¹(F_n, G)) = O(Gⁿ)` with twisted diagonal `G°`-conjugation has a good `G°`-filtration: `O(Gⁿ)` has a good `G^{2n}`-filtration by 4.1, and restriction to the twisted diagonal preserves good filtrations by the tensor theorem. Hence its higher rational `G°`-cohomology vanishes, and so does its higher `G`-cohomology when `|π₀G|` is prime to `ℓ` (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.6 and Corollary VIII.5.7, p.295). *Needs:* 4.1; 4.2; 1.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `F₀` the algebra is `L` with the trivial filtration.
- For `F₁` and trivial action it is `O(G)` under conjugation.
- For a torus every rational module splits into characters, hence has a good filtration, and higher rational cohomology vanishes. For `G_m`, `0 ⊂ L ⊂ L ⊕ L(1)` is a nontrivial good filtration.

### 4.5 Perfect complexes generated from the classifying stack

### 4.6 The induced-perfect bar criterion

Prove that `M ∈ Perf(X/G)` lies in `Perf^ind(X/G)` if and only if the natural map `colim[⋯ → M ⊗_L A ⊗_L M^∨ → M ⊗_L M^∨] → M ⊗_A M^∨` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BG)`, all tensor products formed there: for induced objects the augmented simplicial object has an extra degeneracy; conversely the isomorphism makes the image of `M` dualizable in `Mod_A(Ind Perf(BG))` with compact unit, hence compact and a retract of a finite induced complex. When `G°` is reductive with `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ` and `B ⊂ G°` is a Borel subgroup, it suffices that `M|_B` be generated by `Perf(BB)`, because `Perf(BG°) → Perf(BB)` is fully faithful by Kempf vanishing and restriction to `G°` is conservative (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.8 and Corollary VIII.5.9, p.296). *Needs:* 4.5; 4.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; DGAInfinity layer 8.

**Checks.**

### 4.7 Tensor-connectivity criterion for induced perfectness

Assume `G°` reductive with `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, that `A` has a good `G°`-filtration, and that `M ∈ Perf(X/G)` is connective for the good-filtration t-structure after forgetting the `A`-action. Prove that `M ∈ Perf^ind(X/G)` if and only if `M ⊗_A N` is connective for every such connective `N ∈ Perf(X/G)`. For the converse, 4.1's finite good filtration dimension writes the bar resolution of `M^∨` as a sequential colimit of finite colimits that become increasingly connective, and separatedness (4.3) recovers the bar criterion (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.10 and proof, p.297). *Needs:* 4.1; 4.2; 4.3; 4.6; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- The connectivity hypothesis on `M` itself is part of the statement.
- For `M = A` both sides hold.
- For `G` a torus the t-structure is the ordinary one and the derived tensor of the connective objects `M` and `N` is connective.

### 4.8 Adjoint perfect generation and its prime restriction

For `G` acting on itself by conjugation with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, let `i : Spec L → G` be the unit. Prove that the following are equivalent: `ℓ ∤ |π₁(G°)_tors|`; `i_* L ∈ Perf^ind(G/G)`; `Perf^ind(G/G) = Perf(G/G)`. Sufficiency reduces through central covers of order prime to `ℓ` to simply connected derived group, then builds a Borel-equivariant flag of Cartier divisors whose line bundles come from characters of `B` and applies the Borel bar criterion; the diagonal kernel is pulled back from the unit along `(g, g′) ↦ g g′⁻¹`. Necessity: a central cover with ℓ-primary kernel would force invariant functions on that kernel to be surjected onto by conjugation invariants, which contradicts their constancy on the unipotent kernel, by 4.7 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.11 and proof, pp.297–299). *Needs:* 4.6; 4.7; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1.

**Checks.**

- For a torus `π₁` has no torsion and the unit is induced.
- For `G = PGL_ℓ`, `π₁ = ℤ/ℓ` and generation fails.
- For `G = SL_n` with `ℓ | n` generation holds, since `π₁(SL_n) = 0` (the centre does not matter).

### 4.9 Twisted free-group perfect generation

### 4.10 Derived unit fibres and generation

Let `X̃ = Spec Ã` be a possibly derived affine scheme of finite type over `L`, with an algebraic `G`-action and a `G`-equivariant morphism `X̃ → G`, where `G` acts on itself by conjugation. Put `X = X̃ ×^R_G Spec L = Spec A`, taking the fibre at the unit. If `G°` is reductive and `|π₀G|`, `|π₁(G°)_tors|` are prime to `ℓ`, prove that the natural map `L ⊗_{O(G)} Ã → A`, with tensor product formed in `Ind Perf(BG)`, is an isomorphism there; and if moreover `Perf(X̃/G) = Perf^ind(X̃/G)` and `Ã` is connective for the good-filtration t-structure, then `A` is connective for that t-structure and `Perf(X/G) = Perf^ind(X/G)`. The second part writes the cone of the relative bar comparison as a sequential colimit of terms `M ⊗_A g^*K_n ⊗_A M^*` with `K_n` coherent on the derived self-intersection of the unit, gives `IndCoh` of that self-intersection the t-structure generated by good-filtration-connective objects and passes to its separated quotient, bounds amplitudes by finite good filtration dimension, and proves conservativity of the forgetful functor there using central covers and the Borel-invariant Cartier flag of 4.8 (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.13 and proof, pp.299–301). **Checks.**

- For `X̃ = G` with the identity map, both sides are `L`, concentrated in degree zero; the fibre is the unit.
- For `X̃ = G × G` with multiplication, the fibre consists of `(g,g⁻¹)`, and both sides are `O(G)` with no extra Tor, since multiplication is smooth.
- For `G = G_m` and `X̃ = G_m × A¹` with projection, both sides are `L[x]`, concentrated in degree zero: tensoring `L[t,t⁻¹,x]` along `t ↦ 1` kills `t−1` without higher Tor.
- For `G = G_m` and the constant unit map `X̃ = Spec L → G_m`, both sides have `H⁰ = L` and `H⁻¹ = L`, with all other cohomology zero. The two-term resolution for `t−1` becomes `[L → L]` with zero differential in degrees `−1,0`; the ordinary fibre would lose `H⁻¹`. The algebra remains connective, and generation still holds.
- For `G = 1`, the fibre is `X̃` and the comparison is `L ⊗_L Ã → Ã`, the identity under the unit equivalence; no extra Tor is introduced.

### 4.12 Prime-to-ℓ components of fixed groups

Let `G` be smooth affine over `L` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, and let `P` be a finite group of order prime to `ℓ` acting on `G`. Prove that `|π₀(G^P)|` is prime to `ℓ`. The identity component of `G^P` is smooth and reductive by ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3; this target owns only the component statement. **Checks.**

### 4.13 Cyclic fixed-locus resolutions

Let an automorphism `Θ` of prime order `r ≠ ℓ` act on `G` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`. Put `X = {(g₀, …, g_{r−1}) : g₀ Θ(g₁) ⋯ Θ^{r−1}(g_{r−1}) = 1}` with twisted `G`-conjugation and cyclic `C_r`-permutation. Prove that for the augmented cosimplicial `G`-space `X^{C_r} → X ⇉ ∏_{C_r} X ⋯`, the realisation of coordinate algebras is `O(X^{C_r})` in `Ind Perf(BG)`: complete along the common fixed locus, and use the formal `r`-th root map (available because `r` is invertible) to build a `(G, C_r)`-equivariant retraction onto `X^{C_r}`, giving an extra degeneracy (Fargues–Scholze, Lemma VIII.5.16 and proof, pp.304–307). No `π₁` hypothesis. This target also constructs the equivariant completion bridge `IndProPerf(BG)`: freely generate a presentable stable category from the exact category of countable pro-systems of finite-dimensional `G`-representations, with the original `Ind Perf(BG)` embedded fully faithfully. Define the completed coordinate algebras by powers of the fixed-locus ideal, and prove their idempotence as algebras in this category and their compatibility with the bounded equivariant tensor products used in the resolution. For general `G`, reduce boundedness through a faithful embedding into `GL_n`, as in the proof of Lemma VIII.5.16, pp.305–306. SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.8 supplies ordinary formal completion; the equivariant pro-category and the completion/tensor comparison are owned here. *Needs:* 4.4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.fixedSubgroup`.

**Checks.**

- For the completion bridge, the constant pro-system gives the original representation, completed `O(G)` tensored with the unit residue object gives `L`, and the formal `r`-power map on the completion of `G_m` at `1` has tangent `x ↦ rx`, invertible precisely when `r` is invertible.
- For the completion bridge with trivial `G` and `A = L[t]`, the pro-system `A/(t^n)` records the completion `L[[t]]`, not `L[t]`; completing along the zero ideal leaves `A` and along the unit ideal gives zero.
- In characteristic `r` this tangent is zero and there is no inverse root map. On points, the `C_r`-fixed tuples are the diagonal tuples `(g, …, g)` with twisted norm `g Θ(g) ⋯ Θ^{r−1}(g) = 1`.
- Under `h · (g_i) = (h g_i Θ(h)⁻¹)` the stabilizer of `(1, …, 1)` is the fixed group `G^Θ`, which on points is `TauCeti.fixedSubgroup` of `Θ`.

### 4.14 Solvable fixed groups as Donkin subgroups

If a finite solvable group `P` of order prime to `ℓ` acts on `G` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, prove that `H° = (G^P)°` is a Donkin subgroup of `G°`: restriction of a module with good `G°`-filtration has a good `H°`-filtration, equivalently induction preserves good filtrations. Reduce along a normal series to prime cyclic `P`, then to simple factors and central covers; factor-permuting actions give diagonal subgroups, handled by the tensor theorem, and inner and outer actions by the root and highest-weight computations of the source; the cyclic fixed-locus resolution gives the good filtration of `O(G/H)` (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.15 and proof, pp.302–304). Not asserted for arbitrary `P`; no `π₁` condition. *Needs:* 4.12; 4.13; 4.1; 4.2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `C_r` permuting the factors of `K^r`, the diagonal `K` is a Donkin subgroup by the tensor theorem.
- For `P = 1` the statement is empty.
- For a torus every subgroup torus is Donkin.

### 4.15 Fixed-group induction and good counit kernels

In the setting of 4.14 with `H = G^P`, prove that a representation `W` of `H°` has a good `H°`-filtration if and only if `Ind_{H°}^{G°} W` has a good `G°`-filtration, the same for `H` and `G`, and that for good `W` the kernels of the restriction–induction counits have good `H°`-filtrations. Reduce to prime cyclic `P`; for simply connected `G` use the highest-weight surjections of the proof of Theorem VIII.5.15; descend along a central simply connected cover by the summand with trivial kernel character; and for general `G` use its derived group and linearly reductive quotient, splitting induced modules as retracts, keeping the actual (possibly non-smooth) centre (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.17(i)–(iv) and proof, pp.307–310). Induction is rational induction, not compact induction. *Needs:* 4.14; 4.3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*.

**Checks.**

- For `P = 1` induction and counit are the identity with zero kernels.
- Over `k = 𝔽̄₃`, let `G=G_m` and let `P=C₂` act by inversion. Then `H=μ₂`, `H°=1`; the nontrivial character `χ` of `μ₂` induces to the odd Laurent monomials `⊕_{j∈ℤ} k t^{2j+1}`. With left translation the monomial has weight `−(2j+1)`, restricting to `χ`, and the counit sends each `t^{2j+1}` to `1` in `χ`. Its kernel consists of finite sums whose coefficients sum to zero, and has a good filtration on `H°=1`.
- For the same action, induction from `H°=1` gives all of `k[t,t⁻¹]`, including the weight-zero vector `1`; induction from `H=μ₂` of `χ` excludes `1`. These are different induction functors.

### 4.16 Generation by restriction to fixed groups

In the same setting prove that `Perf(BH°)` is generated under cones and retracts by restrictions from `Perf(BG°)`, and `Perf(BH)` by restrictions from `Perf(BG)`, with no `π₁` hypothesis: the successive restriction–induction counit resolution converges in `Ind Perf` by 4.15 and separatedness, induction is conservative, and compactness cuts the resolution to finitely many cones and retracts (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.17(v)–(vi), pp.307–310). *Needs:* 4.15; 4.3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18.

**Checks.**

- For `P=1`, restriction is the identity, so a representation `V` is its own generator with no cone needed.
- For `G=K²` and the factor swap, restriction of `V ⊠ 1` to diagonal `K` is `V`, whereas its dimension is `dim V`, not `(dim V)²`.
- Over `𝔽̄₃`, inversion on `G_m` gives `H=μ₂` and `H°=1`. Weight `0` restricts to the trivial character of `H`, weight `1` to its nontrivial character; both restrict to the one-dimensional trivial representation of `H°`. Thus restriction generates both simple `H`-representations while the identity component forgets their distinction.

### 4.17 The central-character fixed-group counterexample

In characteristic `2`, for `G = (SL₂ × SL₂)/μ₂` with the factor swap `P = C₂`, prove that the fixed group is `H = PGL₂ × (μ₂ × μ₂)/μ₂` and that its nontrivial central character is not generated by restrictions of `Perf(BG)`: on the nontrivial central-character summand of a restricted perfect `G`-object, homotopy `C₂`-invariants in `PGL₂` stay perfect, a property stable under cones and retracts that fails for the character itself (Fargues–Scholze, Remark VIII.5.18, p.308). Preserving a Borel, a torus or a pinning cannot replace the order hypothesis. *Needs:* ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*, *presentability*.

**Checks.**

- Here `|P| = ℓ = 2`, so the prime-to-ℓ hypothesis of 4.16 fails. In characteristic `3`, the same factor swap has order `2` prime to `3`, and 4.16 applies.
- Identify the central factor `(μ₂ × μ₂)/μ₂` with `μ₂` by the ratio of the two coordinates. In characteristic `2`, its coordinate algebra is `L[u]/((u−1)²)`, of length `2`, whereas its reduced subgroup has coordinate algebra `L`, of length `1`. Its identity component is the entire `μ₂`; passing to that component does not remove the nilpotent `u−1`.
- The nontrivial central character on `Lv` has coaction `v ↦ v ⊗ u`, distinct from `v ↦ v ⊗ 1`, since their difference is `v ⊗ (u−1) ≠ 0`. Both agree on the sole geometric point `u=1`, so checking only points loses the character grading.
- Take the constant subgroup `C₂ ⊂ PGL₂` generated by the class of `[[1,1],[0,1]]`. The nontrivial central character is trivial on this subgroup, and its homotopy invariants have `dim_L Hⁿ(C₂,L) = 1` for every `n ≥ 0`: in the periodic resolution both maps `s−1` and `1+s` act as zero. On the regular module `L[C₂]`, both maps instead have image and kernel `L(1+s)`, so the cohomology dimensions are `1,0,0,…`. The character therefore has nonperfect homotopy invariants, unlike the nontrivial central-character summands of restricted perfect `G`-objects (Fargues–Scholze, Remark VIII.5.18 and footnote 3, p.308).

### 4.18 Fundamental groups of solvable fixed groups

If `G°` is reductive, `P` finite solvable of order prime to `ℓ` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(G°)_tors|`, prove that `ℓ ∤ |π₁((G^P)°)_tors|`: reduce to prime-order actions and to simply connected simple factors through central isogenies of order prime to `ℓ`; permutation and outer actions preserve the condition; for inner actions use the list of root subsystems and track the image of the centre, including the exceptional types; any new prime is the acting prime, already distinct from `ℓ` (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.19 and proof, pp.310–311). **Checks.**

Its square is conjugation by `−1`, hence the identity; fixed matrices have off-diagonal entries zero, so `(G^P)°=G_m`. The algebraic fundamental groups are `π₁(SL₂)=0` and `π₁(G_m)=ℤ`, both with torsion subgroup `0` (of order `1`), not equal fundamental groups.
- Over `𝔽̄₃`, the factor swap on `PGL₂²` fixes diagonal `PGL₂`: torsion changes from `(ℤ/2)²` of order `4` to `ℤ/2` of order `2`, both prime to `3`. The theorem does not assert preservation of the torsion subgroup.
- For `P=1` acting on `G_m`, the source and fixed-group fundamental groups are both `ℤ`, with torsion subgroup `0`; testing finiteness of the whole fundamental group would exclude this valid case.

### 4.20 Free-group gerbe comparisons

For a gerbe `𝒢` over `BΓ` banded by `G` with `G°` reductive and `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`, prove that `Ind Perf(Map^Σ_{BΓ}(BF_n, 𝒢)) → Ind Perf(Map_{BΓ}(BF_n, 𝒢))` is fully faithful with image generated by `Ind Perf(BG)`; for a connected gerbe with extension `E_G → Γ` its source is modules over `O(∏ π⁻¹(γ_i))` in `Ind Perf(BG)`; it is an equivalence when `ℓ ∤ |π₁(G°)_tors|`; and finite unions behave the same way. Write the circle as the homotopy pushout `* ← (* ⊔ *) → *`, use Barr–Beck for the `G`-torsor, base change module categories and take products over the generators (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.20 and proof, p.312). *Needs:* 4.19; 4.9; 4.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `F₀` the module algebra is the base and the comparison is the identity.
- For `G = PGL_ℓ` and `F₁` the comparison is fully faithful and not essentially surjective.
- For `G` a torus it is an equivalence.

### 4.21 Solvable wild gerbe elimination

For a finite solvable normal subgroup `P ⊂ Γ` of order prime to `ℓ`, and `𝒢 → BΓ` with fibre a finite union of `BG` (`G°` reductive, `|π₀G|` prime to `ℓ`), prove that the pushforward along `BΓ → B(Γ/P)` has fibre `Map_{BΓ}(BP, 𝒢)`, a finite union of `BH` with `H°` reductive and `|π₀H|` prime to `ℓ`; that the `Map^Σ(BP)` comparison is an equivalence; and that prime-to-`ℓ` `π₁`-torsion of every band passes to every new band. For cyclic prime `P`, 4.13 computes the free-resolution algebra and 4.16 gives the equivalence; when no section exists the mapping stack is empty and the invariant colimit vanishes; induct through a normal series, using 4.18 for the `π₁` clause (Fargues–Scholze, Proposition VIII.5.21 and proof, pp.313–315). *Needs:* 4.13; 4.16; 4.18; 4.20; 4.19; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*.

**Checks.**

- For `P = 1` the comparison is the identity.
- Finite `p`-groups coming from wild inertia are solvable of order prime to `ℓ`.
- For `P = C₂` acting by inversion on `G = G_m` (`ℓ ≠ 2`) one of the new bands is the fixed group `μ₂`.

### 4.22 Tame reduction of finite-wild parameter categories

For a finite-wild discrete `W` with finite normal `p`-subgroup `P` and tame quotient, and under `ℓ ≠ p`, `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|`, prove that `colim_{F_n → W} O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ)) → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)` over `𝔽̄_ℓ`, that the target is connective for the good-filtration t-structure, and that `Perf(Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ)` is generated by `Perf(BĤ)`: eliminate `P` by 4.21 (the new bands keep reductive identity components, prime-to-`ℓ` components and good `π₁`), apply the tame fibre result 4.11 and the free gerbe comparison 4.20, and translate back (Fargues–Scholze, §VIII.5.2 and the concluding proof of Theorem VIII.5.2, pp.294 and 312–315). *Needs:* 4.21; 4.11; 4.20; 4.4; 0.4.

**Checks.**

- For trivial wild `P` this is the tame fibre argument.
- For `Ĥ` a torus the hypothesis on `π₁` is automatic.
- For `W = ℤ` generated by Frobenius alone the statement is 4.9 for `F₁`.

### 4.23 Very-good and reductive-pair centralizers

Over algebraically closed `k`, prove that every closed subgroup of a reductive `G` has smooth scheme-theoretic centralizer, and hence separable orbit maps on tuples, if the characteristic is very good for `G`, or if `G` has a faithful representation `V` with a `G`-equivariant splitting of `Lie(G) ⊂ Lie(GL(V))`. The very good exclusions for simple root systems are `ℓ | n + 1` for `A_n`, `ℓ = 2` for `B, C, D, E, F, G`, `ℓ = 3` for `E, F, G` and `ℓ = 5` for `E₈`; characteristic zero or prime to `|W_G|` suffices (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.1, Theorem 3.8, the table and Lemma 3.9, pp.13–14, after Bate–Martin–Röhrle–Tange). The Lie algebra of a reductive group need not be semisimple: its central torus part remains. *Needs:* 2.4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; Mathlib `RootPairing`, `CoxeterSystem`.

**Checks.**

- For `GL(V)` the reductive-pair condition holds with zero complement, so all closed-subgroup centralizers are smooth.
- `Lie(G_m)` is one-dimensional abelian, not semisimple, yet `G_m` has smooth centralizers.
- For `SL₂` in characteristic `2` (not very good) the centralizer of the unipotent element `[[1,1],[0,1]]` is not smooth.

### 4.24 Descent of étaleness to reductive quotients

Let `G` be reductive over the excellent coefficient DVR `O`, `X` and `Y` normal integral affine flat `O`-schemes of finite type, and `φ : Y → X` finite and `G`-equivariant. If `y ∈ Y(k)` and `x = φ(y)` have closed residual orbits, `φ` is étale at `y`, and `φ` is injective on geometric points of these orbits, prove that `φ//G` is étale at the image of `y`. Pass to a finite Galois closure `L/K` of the function fields; take `K₀ = Frac(O[X]^G)`, `L₀` the relative algebraic closure of `K₀` in `L` and `E₀ = Frac(O[Y]^G)`; connectedness of `G` makes `K₀` algebraically closed in `K`, so `L₀/K₀` is finite Galois; the orbit hypotheses put the stabilizer of a point above `y` inside the image of `Gal(L/E)`; apply the following intermediate integral-closure criterion to `L₀/K₀` and localise using invariant neighbourhoods (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.2, Lemmas 3.11–3.12 and proofs, pp.16–18). *Needs:* 2.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 0 (excellent rings), SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.20–0.21, 0.30 and 4.1 (finite normalization, normal schemes and étale lifting); ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

Prove the following stabilizer criterion for étaleness: for an excellent normal domain `A`, a finite Galois extension `L/Frac(A)` with group `D`, a subgroup `H ⊂ D`, and the integral closures `B` in `L^H` and `C` in `L`, let `c̄` be a geometric point over a closed point of `Spec C` and `b̄` its image in `Spec B`. Then `Spec B → Spec A` is étale at `b̄` exactly when the stabilizer of `c̄` in `D` is contained in `H` (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Lemma 3.12, p.17). The stabilizer is for the geometric point, so it is the inertia group rather than the decomposition group of the underlying prime.

**Checks.**

- For `φ = id` every hypothesis holds and the quotient map is étale.
- For `G = 1` the statement says only that `φ` is étale at `y`.
- Normality of the quotients, used in the Galois argument, comes from 2.2. For `A = k[[t]]`, `L = k((u))`, `u² = t` and algebraically closed `k` of characteristic different from `2`, the closed geometric point has stabilizer `C₂`: the intermediate cover with `H = 1` is ramified and that with `H = C₂` is the identity. For an unramified extension of a DVR with nontrivial residue extension, the geometric stabilizer is trivial although the underlying closed prime has a nontrivial decomposition group.
- Substituting the latter would wrongly reject an étale cover.

### 4.25 Formal quotient slices over coefficient DVRs

Let `G` be reductive over `O`, `X` integral affine smooth of finite type over `O`, and `x ∈ X(k)` with closed residual orbit and scheme-theoretically trivial stabilizer. Prove that on Artin local `O`-algebras with residue field `k` the formal neighbourhood of the identity in `G` acts freely on `X̂_x` and that `X̂_x/Ĝ ≅ (X//G)^_{π(x)}` as deformation functors: an `O`-smooth transversal splitting the orbit tangent space makes `G × S → X` étale near `(1, x)`, 4.24 compares `S` with the quotient, and invariant neighbourhoods and formal étaleness identify the completed functors (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, §3.2, Proposition 3.13 and proof, pp.17–19). Scheme-theoretically trivial stabilizer is stronger than trivial `k`-points; nothing is claimed for nontrivial stabilizers. *Needs:* 4.24; 2.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 1, SchemeAndStackFoundations layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `X = G` with left translation the formal quotient is a point.
- Tuple conjugation requires first passing to the adjoint group when the centre is nontrivial.
- For `μ_ℓ` acting on `𝔸¹` in characteristic `ℓ` the stabilizer of `0` has trivial points but is not trivial as a scheme, and the slice statement fails.

### 4.26 Integral Chevalley restriction for Levi groups

For a split reductive standard dual Levi `M` over `ℤ` with maximal split torus `T` and Weyl group `W(M, T)`, prove that restriction gives an isomorphism `ℤ[M]^M ≅ ℤ[T]^{W(M,T)}` with no good-prime hypothesis. Injectivity follows from density of regular semisimple conjugates over `ℚ` and torsion-freeness; surjectivity from the triangular `ℤ`-basis of Weyl-invariant characters by dominant highest weights, realised by characters of stable `ℤ`-lattices in the irreducible rational representations (Böckle–Harris–Khare–Thorne, Proposition 8.3 and proof, p.53; Jantzen, I.10.4, Lemma, p.143, for stable lattices). This is group Chevalley restriction, not the Lie algebra statement, which fails in bad characteristic. *Needs:* 4.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 9; Mathlib `RootPairing`.

**Checks.**

- For `M = G_m` restriction is the identity of `ℤ[t, t⁻¹]`.
- For `GL₂` the target is `ℤ[t₁ + t₂, t₁t₂, (t₁t₂)⁻¹]`, realised by trace and determinant.
- For `SL₂` it is `ℤ[t + t⁻¹]`, realised by the trace.

### Examples

For `G = PGL_ℓ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ`, `π₁ = ℤ/ℓ`: the unit skyscraper of `G/G` is not induced (4.8), the free-group approximation is not the mapping category (4.19) and the gerbe comparison is not essentially surjective (4.20). For `G = SL_n`, `π₁ = 0` and all three statements hold whatever the centre.

### Dependencies

Layer 0; Layer 1; Layer 2; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.3 and RG2.5; ReductiveGroups layer 9 and §10B; SchubertGeometry S3.2 and S5.5–S5.6; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5; SchemeAndStackFoundations layers 1 and 4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; DGAInfinity layer 8.

## Layer 5: the integral excursion comparison at good primes

This layer applies Layer 4 to exactly the conclusions that need it: the comparison in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, higher-cohomology vanishing and coefficient base change of invariants. Their hypothesis `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` is kept separate from the unconditional closed-orbit classification and universal homeomorphism of Layer 2 and Layer 3.

### 5.1 Integral invariant comparison in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`

Assume `ℓ ≠ p` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|`. Prove that `colim_{F_n → W} O(Z¹(F_n, Ĥ)) → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` is an isomorphism in `Ind Perf(BĤ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` for a finite-wild `W`. Prove that taking invariants of this enhanced map gives exactly the scalar `compare` of SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, by agreement on every free-word invariant generator. Thus its scalar inverse is the supplied good-prime inverse; the scalar integral isomorphism has its proof owner in SR.6.3. The cone is detected after inverting `ℓ` (where representations of `Ĥ` are semisimple and 2.8 suffices) and after reduction to `𝔽̄_ℓ` (4.22); since the free cocycle algebras and the target have good filtrations modulo `ℓ` (4.4), taking `Ĥ`-invariants computes the ordinary invariant rings and commutes with the sifted colimit (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.3.6, p.288, and Theorem VIII.5.1 with the reduction to Theorem VIII.5.2, pp.293–294). *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*; 4.22; 4.4; 2.8; 2.9; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ` a torus the `π₁` condition holds automatically.
- At a bad prime (`Ĥ = PGL_ℓ`) the universal homeomorphism and the character bijection remain while this statement is not available.
- After inverting `ℓ` the statement holds for every `Ĥ`.

### 5.2 Cohomology vanishing and invariant base change

Under the same hypotheses, prove that `O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))` has no higher rational `Ĥ`-cohomology, that its invariant algebra is flat over `ℤ_ℓ`, and that for every `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra `Λ` the canonical map `O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))^Ĥ ⊗ Λ → O(Z¹(W, Ĥ)_Λ)^{Ĥ_Λ}` is an isomorphism, the derived form coming from the equivariant colimit of 5.1 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorems VIII.5.1–VIII.5.2 and proof, pp.293–294). The base change to all coefficients, in particular to `𝔽_ℓ`, is a consequence of good filtrations here, not of 2.1's flat base change, and `ℤ_ℓ → 𝔽_ℓ` is not flat. *Needs:* 5.1; 4.4; 1.5; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; Mathlib `Module.Flat`.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ` a torus with trivial action the invariants are the whole flat coordinate ring.
- For `W = F_n` the statement is 4.4 together with flatness of `O(Ĥⁿ)`.
- The `C₂` example of 2.1 (disconnected acting group) shows that base change to `𝔽₂` can fail without these hypotheses.

### 5.3 The three excursion comparisons

Assemble the supplied SR.6.3 scalar comparisons with the enhancements proved here: at every `ℓ ≠ p` the excursion comparison is a universal homeomorphism and classifies semisimple geometric parameters (2.9, 3.6); after inverting `ℓ` it is a ring isomorphism (2.9); under `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|` it is an integral isomorphism, with higher-cohomology vanishing and base change (5.1, 5.2) (Fargues–Scholze, §§VIII.3.2–VIII.3.3, pp.287–290). The finite-wild pieces are treated separately; the underlying derived colimit of 2.8 stays distinct from its `Ind Perf(BĤ)` version. *Needs:* SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3, *Excursion and cocycle quotient comparison*; 2.9; 3.6; 5.2.

**Checks.**

- Closed geometric points alone cannot give an integral algebra isomorphism.
- For `Ĥ = G_m` all three comparisons are equalities.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` the first two hold and the third is not asserted.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = SL₂`, `π₁` is trivial and all three comparisons are isomorphisms at every `ℓ ≠ p`; for `Ĥ = PGL₂` and `ℓ = 2` only the universal homeomorphism and the rational isomorphism are available.

### Dependencies

Layer 2; Layer 3; Layer 4; SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups SR.6.3; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5.

## Layer 6: representation bundles and module comparison

The universal parameter defines representation bundles before any generation statement. Generation and the module comparison are theorems under the good-prime hypothesis; the spectral action that uses them is outside this roadmap and owned downstream.

### 6.1 Universal representation bundles

For `X=[Z¹(W,Ĥ)/Ĥ]` over a `ℤ_ℓ`-algebra and a finite set `I`, construct from the universal `Ĥ ⋊ Q`-torsor with its `W`-equivariance the exact `Rep(Q^I)`-linear symmetric monoidal functor `Rep((Ĥ ⋊ Q)^I) → Perf(X)^{BW^I}` on finite projective representations (Fargues–Scholze, introduction to Chapter X, p.340).

- Identify the bundle of the trivial representation with `O_X`, and that of a tensor product with the tensor product of the associated bundles.
- Prove coherent fusion along maps of finite sets.
- Construct the exact tensor action on `Perf(X)`, preserving units and composition.
- Prove that pullback along coefficient change carries the bundle of `V` to the bundle of its scalar extension, compatibly with `W`-action, tensor and unit; no invariant-ring base change is asserted.

This construction supplies a categorical Hecke datum of 2.12 before any generation theorem.

*Needs:* 1.8; 0.1; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *abstract*.

**Checks.**

- The trivial representation acts as the identity on `Perf(X)`.
- The fibre at a parameter `φ` is `V` with the `W`-action through `φ`.
- The fibre of a tensor product is the tensor product of the two parameter representations, and for `Ĥ = G_m` the bundle of the standard character is the universal line bundle with the universal character as `W`-action.
- For inversion action of `Q = C₂` on `G_m`, the standard weight-one character does not extend to `G_m ⋊ C₂`: conjugation by the involution changes weight `1` to weight `−1`. It therefore supplies no representation bundle by this construction.

### 6.2 Perfect generation on parameter stacks

For a finite-wild `W` and `ℓ ∤ |π₁(Ĥ)_tors|`, prove that `Perf(Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ)` over `ℤ_ℓ` is generated under cones and retracts by the image of `Perf(BĤ)`, that the same holds over `𝔽̄_ℓ`, and that over a field of characteristic zero no condition on `ℓ` is needed. Over `ℤ_ℓ`: reduce perfect amplitude by surjections from induced bundles; a vector bundle is a retract of an induced bundle after inverting `ℓ`, hence up to a power of `ℓ`; the remaining mod-`ℓ` object is generated by 4.22 (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.1 and the reduction proof, pp.293–294). Generation is a theorem, not the definition of `Perf`. *Needs:* 4.22; 4.5; 6.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5.

**Checks.**

- For `W = 1` the category is `Perf(BĤ)`.
- The quotient `Z¹/Ĥ` and the unquotiented `Z¹` are not interchangeable here.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` and `W = ℤ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` the unit skyscraper is not generated (4.8).

### 6.3 Parameter `Ind Perf` module comparison

Under the hypotheses of 6.2, prove that `Ind Perf(Z¹(W, Ĥ)/Ĥ) ≅ Mod_{O(Z¹(W, Ĥ))}(Ind Perf(BĤ))`, compatibly with pullback, tensor products and the representation bundles; over a field of characteristic zero the same holds with no condition on `π₁`. Pullback from `BĤ` and its right adjoint form the affine adjunction; generation makes the right adjoint conservative; Barr–Beck–Lurie identifies the monad with tensoring by the cocycle algebra (Fargues–Scholze, Theorem VIII.5.1 and proof, p.293). Modules are taken in `Ind Perf(BĤ)`, not in the derived category with the equivariance forgotten. *Needs:* 6.2; 5.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5 *presentability*; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18.

**Checks.**

- For `Ĥ = 1` this is the comparison of `Ind Perf(Spec A)` with `A`-modules.
- For `W = 1` it is the tautology `Ind Perf(BĤ) ≅ Mod_{ℤ_ℓ}(Ind Perf(BĤ))`.
- For `Ĥ = PGL_ℓ` over `𝔽̄_ℓ` and `W = ℤ` the comparison is not asserted, since the unit skyscraper of 4.8 is not in the subcategory generated by `Perf(BĤ)`.

### Examples

For `Ĥ = G_m`, `W` tame: `Z¹(W, G_m)/G_m = (G_m × μ_{q−1}) × BG_m`, and the module comparison identifies `Ind Perf` of it with graded modules over `O(G_m × μ_{q−1})`, the grading recording the `G_m`-weight.

### Dependencies

Layer 1; Layer 5; Layer 4; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.5; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1.8 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.18; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E5.
