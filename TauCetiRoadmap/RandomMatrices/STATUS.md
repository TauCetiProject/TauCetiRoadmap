## Where this roadmap stands

**At a glance.** Two of the roadmap's four headlines are proved. The semicircle and Marchenko–Pastur laws hold almost surely for corners of one i.i.d. array, and the extreme Wigner eigenvalues converge to `±2`. Every other layer is partial. The Tracy–Widom and sine-kernel limits and the circular law are not yet in reach. Their first ingredients have started to land: determinantal ensembles, logarithmic potentials and least-singular-value tails.

### Named results

- **Wigner's semicircle law** — [`wigner_semicircle_ae`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/RandomMatrix/Wigner/SemicircleLaw.html#TauCeti.Probability.wigner_semicircle_ae): for i.i.d. complex upper entries with finite second moment and centred variance one, and a real diagonal with any common law, the empirical spectral measures converge almost surely to the standard semicircle. This is Bai's Theorem 2.1, with no fourth moment. A [Lindeberg version in probability](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/RandomMatrix/Wigner/Lindeberg/Semicircle.html#TauCeti.Probability.wigner_semicircle_in_probability_of_lindeberg) covers triangular arrays.
- **The Marchenko–Pastur law** — [`marchenkoPastur_ae`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/RandomMatrix/SampleCovariance/MarchenkoPastur.html#TauCeti.Probability.marchenkoPastur_ae): for an i.i.d. complex array with mean zero, unit variance and `p(n)/n → y > 0`, the spectral measures of `XₙXₙ*/n` converge almost surely to `MP_y`.
- **Edge limits of Wigner matrices** — [under a fourth moment](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/RandomMatrix/Wigner/Edge/Limit.html#ProbabilityTheory.iIndepFun.ae_tendsto_eigenvalues₀_zero_and_card_sub_one_wignerMatrix) the largest and smallest eigenvalues tend almost surely to `±2√v`, through a Füredi–Komlós trace bound.
- **The Hanson–Wright inequality** — [`hanson_wright`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/HansonWright.html#TauCeti.hanson_wright): a quadratic form in independent sub-Gaussian coordinates has the mixed Gaussian/exponential tail, in complex, decoupled and conditional versions.
- **The method of moments for determinate limits** — [weak convergence follows from moment convergence](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/WeakConvergence.html#MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_of_tendsto_integral_pow_of_isMomentDeterminate) whenever the limit is [moment-determinate](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Moments/Determinacy.html#MeasureTheory.Measure.IsMomentDeterminate). There are almost-sure and in-measure versions, and finite exponential moments imply determinacy.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Empirical spectral and singular-value measures**, built on [`empiricalFiniteMeasure`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Process/EmpiricalMeasure.html#TauCeti.Probability.empiricalFiniteMeasure) and [`singularValueMeasure`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/SpectralMeasure/SingularValues.html#Matrix.singularValueMeasure). They come with rank, Hoffman–Wielandt and resolvent-stability estimates, which carried both global laws through truncation, centring and diagonal removal.
- **Finite determinantal ensembles** [`determinantalEnsemble`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Determinantal/Ensemble.html#TauCeti.determinantalEnsemble), built from Andréief's identity. Their count generating functions and gap probabilities `det(I - G_B)` are what the orthogonal-polynomial kernels of Layer 8 will instantiate.
- **Large deviation principles** [`HasLDP`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/LargeDeviations/Basic.html#TauCeti.Probability.HasLDP), with weak principles, exponential tightness, exponential equivalence and contraction. The [logarithmic energy](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Probability/Distributions/BetaGas/LogarithmicEnergy.html#TauCeti.Probability.logarithmicEnergy) is a good rate function.

## The frontier

- **Sample-covariance edges (§4.5).** The edge reduction exists. What remains is the growing-moment trace bound for truncated entries, which makes `λ_max → (1+√y)²` unconditional, followed by the least-eigenvalue limits for `y < 1` and `y > 1`.
- **The Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev inequality (§2.2).** Herbst's argument and Lipschitz concentration under the inequality are proved, so the inequality alone unlocks §2.3's spectral concentration. Talagrand remains separate.
- **GOE/GUE eigenvalue densities (§5.2).** The spectral change of variables and almost-sure simple spectrum are in place. The Haar normalization, the `n!` count and the Gaussian Selberg constant remain. Layers 8 and 9 both need this.
- **The circular law (Layer 10).** What remains is Girko's identity `U_{L_A} = n⁻¹ log|det(A - zI)|`, the Rudelson–Vershynin bound with its Littlewood–Offord input, and the limit of the singular-value laws of `Xₙ/√n - zI`.
- **Matrix Bernstein and Chernoff (§2.6).** Not started. Golden–Thompson and Lieb concavity are needed first.
