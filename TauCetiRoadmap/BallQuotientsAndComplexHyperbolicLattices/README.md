# Roadmap: ball quotients and complex hyperbolic lattices

**Summary.** Start from complex hyperbolic n-space for every n ≥ 1 as the Riemannian symmetric space of G = PU(1,n), with its invariant Kähler form ω and its complex sub-balls (RealSemisimpleLieGroups), read it in the projective, bounded and Siegel models of the ball, prove that G is the full biholomorphism group of the ball, make the Kähler geometry of ω explicit (Ricci form, curvature, distance, Schwarz–Pick), build the horospherical geometry at the boundary, and extend the classification of totally geodesic complex submanifolds to analytic subvarieties. On this foundation the roadmap develops lattices Γ ⊂ G of every kind (arithmetic or not, cocompact or not, with or without torsion): their cusps, finite generation and Selberg's lemma; the analytic quotient Γ\𝔹ⁿ as a normal complex space; the toroidal compactification of a neat quotient by abelian varieties; Mok's L²-construction of pluricanonical sections, which makes the minimal compactification S*_Γ a normal projective variety with a finite boundary; a big Picard extension theorem into S*_Γ for torsion-free Γ, which makes holomorphic maps from algebraic varieties algebraic and the algebraic structure of S_Γ unique; the passage from an arithmetic lattice to a Shimura datum, which identifies S*_Γ with the Baily–Borel compactification; and finally totally geodesic subvarieties, Γ-special subvarieties and the algebraic subvarieties of 𝔹ⁿ × S_Γ, so that the theorems of Baldi–Ullmo on non-arithmetic ball quotients (finiteness of maximal totally geodesic subvarieties, Ax–Schanuel) become statements about objects of the library.

Suggested home: `TauCeti/Geometry/ComplexHyperbolic/` for Layers 1–4, `TauCeti/Geometry/ComplexHyperbolic/Lattice/` for Layers 5–7, and `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/BallQuotient/` for Layers 8–12.

The acceptance test, `Suggested.lean`, states the headline theorems that the pinned libraries can express: `biholomorphic_ball_iff` (§2.1), `margulis_lemma` (§4.4), `IsLattice.exists_finset_cuspPoint_orbits` and `IsLattice.compactSpace_quotient_iff` (§6.1), `IsLattice.isFinitelyPresented` (§6.2), `selberg_lemma` (§6.3) and `IsLattice.exists_neat` (§6.4). The other headlines (the classification of totally geodesic subvarieties of the ball, the analytic quotient, the toroidal and minimal compactifications, Mok's theorem, the extension and algebraicity theorems, the uniqueness theorems, the arithmetic bridge and the theorems on Γ-special subvarieties) are stated in this README; Scope and ownership says why the acceptance test does not state them.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the complex hyperbolic geometry that the theory of lattices needs beyond RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7, in all dimensions n ≥ 1, and the theory of lattices in PU(1,n) and their quotients, in this order: the comparison of the supplier's form, groups, ball and metric with the conventions here (§§1.1–1.4, 2.2) and the conjugation representation of PU(1,n) on Mₙ₊₁(ℂ) with its eigenvalues; the explicit action formulas, the explicit parameter of the Cartan (KAK) decomposition, and unimodularity; the Siegel model, the Cayley transform, the boundary sphere, the identification of the minimal parabolic subgroup MAN with the stabiliser of a boundary point in Siegel coordinates, the Heisenberg group and horospherical coordinates; the theorem that PU(1,n) is the full biholomorphism group of the ball (through H. Cartan's linearity theorem); the explicit Kähler geometry of ω = −i∂∂̄ log(1 − |w|²): its Siegel form and volume density, its Ricci form and curvature values, the closed distance formula and the Schwarz–Pick contraction for holomorphic maps between balls; the description of sub-balls by subspaces of signature (1,m), m ≥ 0, and the classification of totally geodesic irreducible closed analytic subsets of the ball; the classification of isometries, displacement estimates on horoballs, Zassenhaus neighbourhoods and the Margulis lemma for discrete subgroups of PU(1,n); lattices, their covolume and the cusp structure theorem; finite generation; Selberg's lemma in its eigenvalue form and neat lattices; the analytic quotient; the toroidal and minimal compactifications and their projective algebraicity; the extension theorem and Borel algebraicity for torsion-free lattices; the uniqueness of the algebraic model and of the finite-point normal compactification; the arithmetic bridge; Γ-special and totally geodesic subvarieties; and the algebraic subvarieties of 𝔹ⁿ × S_Γ.

## Conventions

- **Dimension and form.** n ≥ 1 throughout the text. The headlines of the acceptance test are stated for every n ≥ 0: for n = 0 the ball is a point, G is trivial, there is no boundary sphere, and each of them holds trivially. V = ℂⁿ⁺¹ with coordinates indexed by `Fin 1 ⊕ Fin n`, written z = (z₀, z₁, …, zₙ). The Hermitian form is conjugate-linear in the first slot, as in Mathlib: `⟨z,w⟩ = conj(z₀)w₀ − Σ_{i≥1} conj(zᵢ)wᵢ = zᴴ J w`, with J = `LieAlgebra.Orthogonal.indefiniteDiagonal (Fin 1) (Fin n) ℂ` = diag(1, −1, …, −1). Signature (1,n): one positive and n negative directions.
- **Comparison with RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7.** The supplier's form z₀w̄₀ − Σ_(i≥1) zᵢw̄ᵢ is linear in the first slot: it is the complex conjugate of the form here, with the same Gram matrix after the reindexing `Fin 1 ⊕ Fin n ≃ Fin (n + 1)` of the supplier contract, the same values ⟨z,z⟩, the same positive, null and negative lines and the same groups U(1,n), SU(1,n), PU(1,n). Its metric g_𝔹 is the Riemannian metric of ω here, and its Killing metric is 2(n + 1)·g_𝔹; every metric statement of this roadmap is for g_𝔹, never rescaled.
- **Groups.** U(1,n) = {g ∈ GLₙ₊₁(ℂ) : gᴴJg = J}; SU(1,n) its determinant-one subgroup; G = PU(1,n) = U(1,n)/U(1), where U(1) is the subgroup of unit scalars, with the quotient topology. Matrices act on column vectors, and the group law is composition: g·h acts as "first h, then g".
- **Three models.** The projective model is 𝔹ⁿ = {[z] ∈ ℙ(V) : ⟨z,z⟩ > 0}, open for Mathlib's quotient topology on `Projectivization ℂ V`. The bounded model is B = {w ∈ ℂⁿ : |w| < 1}, with [z] ↦ w = (z₁/z₀, …, zₙ/z₀). The Siegel model is 𝔖 = {(u,v) ∈ ℂⁿ⁻¹ × ℂ : ρ(u,v) > 0} with ρ(u,v) = Im v − |u|², and the Cayley transform C(w′, wₙ) = (w′/(1 − wₙ), i(1 + wₙ)/(1 − wₙ)) sends B to 𝔖 and the boundary point b₀ = (0, …, 0, 1) to ∞. For n = 1 the u-coordinate is absent and 𝔖 is the upper half-plane.
- **Action on the bounded model.** Writing g = [[a, bᵀ],[c, D]] in blocks of sizes 1 and n, g·w = (c + Dw)/(a + bᵀw). The denominator is nonzero on B for g ∈ U(1,n).
- **The Kähler form and the metric.** On B, ω = −i∂∂̄ log(1 − |w|²). With ComplexKahlerGeometry's convention g(u,v) = ω(u,Jv), ω₀ = iΣ dwⱼ∧dw̄ⱼ and the Riemannian metric at the origin is twice the Euclidean one. The holomorphic sectional curvature is −2, sectional curvatures lie in [−2, −1/2], the Ricci form is −(n+1)ω, which is the Chern curvature iΘ(K_B⁻¹) of the anticanonical bundle, so that the canonical bundle K_B with the metric induced by ω^n has iΘ(K_B) = (n+1)ω (ComplexKahlerGeometry K4's convention: iΘ = −i∂∂̄ log of the squared norm of a holomorphic frame), and the distance is given by `cosh²(d([z],[w])/√2) = |⟨z,w⟩|²/(⟨z,z⟩⟨w,w⟩)`. Volumes are those of ω^n/n!.
- **Horospherical coordinates.** At ∞ ∈ ∂𝔖: the Heisenberg group N = ℂⁿ⁻¹ × ℝ acts by n_(a,t)(u,v) = (u + a, v + 2i⟨a,u⟩ + i|a|² + t), with ⟨a,u⟩ = Σ conj(aⱼ)uⱼ; its group law is (a,t)·(a′,t′) = (a + a′, t + t′ − 2 Im⟨a,a′⟩); the centre Z(N) is the vertical translations T_t(u,v) = (u, v + t). A = {a_s : (u,v) ↦ (su, s²v), s > 0}, M = U(n−1) acting on u. Horoballs at ∞ are H(c) = {ρ > c}, c > 0. For n = 1, N = Z(N) = ℝ.
- **Neat.** A subgroup Γ ⊆ G is neat when, for every γ ∈ Γ, the eigenvalues of Ad(γ) generate a torsion-free subgroup of ℂ^×, where Ad: G → GL(Mₙ₊₁(ℂ)) is conjugation by a lift (§1.2); that is, Ad(γ) is neat in the sense of AdelicAlgebraicGroups §4.3 (`Neat.IsNeatAut`) for every γ ∈ Γ. In the acceptance test this is `IsNeat`, with Ad of a lift g given by `conjugationMap`. This is the condition of WitteMorris2015 Remark 4.8.5(2), line 3002 to line 3005, where such subgroups are called net, and the conclusion of Borel1969 Corollary 17.7 (uncertified; cited at WitteMorris2015 line 3445 for that remark). It is strictly stronger than asking that no eigenvalue of Ad(γ) be a nontrivial root of unity, the property of WitteMorris2015 Theorem 4.8.4 (line 2972): the products of eigenvalues count too (Checks of §6.4).
- **Algebraic geometry.** Varieties are reduced separated schemes of finite type over ℂ; X^an is ComplexComparisonPartII's `complexSchemeAnalytification`. "Quasi-projective model of Γ\𝔹ⁿ" means a pair (S, φ) of a quasi-projective variety and an isomorphism of complex analytic spaces φ: S^an ≅ Γ\𝔹ⁿ. A compactification by a finite set is a compact Hausdorff space containing the given space as an open dense subset with finite complement; the Hausdorff condition is part of the definition, because ComplexComparisonPartII's `ComplexAnalyticSpace` imposes no global separation axiom and open gluing produces compact non-Hausdorff analytic spaces (§10.5).

**Checks.**

- For n = 1 and t ∈ ℝ, h_t = [[cosh t, i sinh t],[−i sinh t, cosh t]] satisfies h_tᴴ J h_t = J (|cosh t|² − |sinh t|² = 1 and cosh t · i sinh t − conj(−i sinh t) · cosh t = 0). It acts by w ↦ (−i sinh t + w cosh t)/(cosh t + i w sinh t), sends 0 to −i tanh t, and d(0, −i tanh t) = √2|t| by the distance formula (|⟨z,w⟩|² = 1 and ⟨w,w⟩ = 1/cosh² t). The transposed convention w ↦ (b + Dw)/(a + cw) would send 0 to +i tanh t.
- The swap S = [[0,1],[1,0]] has SᴴJS = −J: it is not in U(1,1), and it sends the interior point [1:0] (⟨z,z⟩ = 1) to [0:1] (⟨z,z⟩ = −1), outside 𝔹¹. A definition of U(1,n) by "preserves the form up to a nonzero real factor" would admit it.
- At the origin, −log(1 − |w|²) = |w|² + O(|w|⁴), so ω₀ = iΣdwⱼ∧dw̄ⱼ; for n = 1, ω = i dw∧dw̄/(1 − |w|²)², whose Gaussian curvature is −2 (half the curvature −1 metric 4|dw|²/(1 − |w|²)², scaled by 1/2), and Ric(ω) = −2ω = −(n+1)ω.
- ρ(C(w)) = (1 − |w|²)/|1 − wₙ|², so C(0) = (0, i) has ρ = 1, and −log ρ∘C differs from −log(1 − |w|²) by the pluriharmonic function log|1 − wₙ|².
- For n = 2, a = 1, a′ = i: (1,0)·(i,0) = (1 + i, −2) and (i,0)·(1,0) = (1 + i, 2), checked against the formula for n_(a,t) on (u,v); the commutator is the vertical translation by −4. A law with + 2 Im⟨a,a′⟩ would fail this computation.
- On the vertical geodesic {u = 0}, the metric is (dx² + dy²)/(2y²) in v = x + iy, so a_s has translation length √2|log s|, and the vertical translation T_t displaces (0, iy) by √2 arcsinh(|t|/(2y)), which tends to 0 as y → ∞.

### 2.1 Cartan's theorem and the biholomorphism group

### 2.3 Uniqueness, Ricci form and curvature

Prove that every G-invariant Kähler form on B is cω for a constant c > 0; that the Ricci form of ω is −(n+1)ω; and that the holomorphic sectional curvature of ω is −2 at every point and in every direction, with sectional curvatures in [−2, −1/2] (Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified; Mok2012 line 247 to line 254 and line 383 to line 389 call the metric of ω the canonical Kähler–Einstein metric, without stating the Einstein constant; the three statements are proved here). The first statement is the supplier's uniqueness of invariant Riemannian metrics (RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7, from its §5.3) read on Kähler forms: an invariant Kähler form ω′ has the invariant metric g′ = ω′(·, i·) = c·g_𝔹 with c > 0, and ω′ = g′(i·, ·) = cω. The Ricci form Ric(ω) = −i∂∂̄ log det(h) is the Chern curvature form iΘ(K_B⁻¹) of the anticanonical bundle K_B⁻¹ = Λⁿ T^(1,0)B, in whose induced metric the frame ∂/∂w₁ ∧ … ∧ ∂/∂wₙ has squared norm det(h), because ComplexKahlerGeometry K4's Chern connection ∂ log ‖e‖² on a line bundle with holomorphic frame e has iΘ = −i∂∂̄ log ‖e‖². Dually, the frame dw₁ ∧ … ∧ dwₙ of the canonical bundle K_B has squared norm det(h)⁻¹ for the metric induced by ω^n, so iΘ(K_B) = i∂∂̄ log det(h) = −Ric(ω) = (n + 1)ω and iΘ(K_B^m) = m(n + 1)ω for every integer m; in particular iΘ(K_B^(ℓ−1)) = (ℓ − 1)(n + 1)ω, the form in which Layer 9 uses it. The Riemannian counterpart of Ric(ω) is the supplier's Ricci tensor −½B, which gives Ric(g_𝔹) = −(n+1)·g_𝔹, the Ricci tensor being unchanged by constant rescaling. The curvature values follow from the supplier's curvature R(Y,Z)W = −[[Y,Z],W] at o (RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.3) on 𝔭 ≅ ℂⁿ: the sectional curvatures of g_𝔹 are 2(n + 1) times those of the Killing metric. *Needs:* §§1.4, 2.2; RealSemisimpleLieGroups §§5.3, 5.7; ComplexKahlerGeometry K4 (Chern curvature of the canonical bundle) and K6.0.

**Checks.**

- For n = 1 the Gaussian curvature is −2 and Ric = −2ω.
- Canonical against anticanonical, n = 1: det(h) = (1 − |w|²)⁻², so ‖dw‖² = (1 − |w|²)² and iΘ(K_B) = −i∂∂̄ log(1 − |w|²)² = +2ω, while ‖∂/∂w‖² = (1 − |w|²)⁻² gives iΘ(K_B⁻¹) = −2ω = Ric(ω). At ℓ = 2 the input of Layer 9 is iΘ(K_B^(ℓ−1)) = +2ω, strictly positive; reading the Ricci form as the curvature of K_B itself would give −2ω, and the L² estimate of §9.1 would not apply.
- The potential −2 log(1 − |w|²) gives 2ω, also invariant: invariance does not fix c.
- For n = 2, the Hermitian form i dw₁∧dw̄₁ + 2i dw₂∧dw̄₂ at 0 is not invariant under the coordinate swap in K, so it has no G-invariant extension.
- Scaling witness, n = 2: the Killing metric has curvature −1/(n + 1) = −1/3 on complex lines and −1/(4(n + 1)) = −1/12 on totally real planes; multiplying by 2(n + 1) = 6 gives −2 and −1/2 for g_𝔹. A comparison that forgot the factor would give holomorphic sectional curvature −1/3.

### 2.4 Distance, geodesics and completeness

By RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.3, read through §§1.4 and 2.2, (B, g_𝔹) is a complete Hadamard manifold, any two points are joined by a unique geodesic, and the geodesics through 0 are the orbits t ↦ lieExp(tY)·0, Y ∈ 𝔭; completeness and uniqueness of geodesics are unchanged by the constant rescaling from the Killing metric. Prove the explicit forms: since lieExp(tY_v)·0 = tanh(t)·v for a unit vector v ∈ ℂⁿ (§1.4), the unit-speed geodesics through 0 are s ↦ tanh(s/√2)·v; the Riemannian distance of g_𝔹 satisfies cosh²(d([z],[w])/√2) = |⟨z,w⟩|²/(⟨z,z⟩⟨w,w⟩), which in the bounded model, with z = (1, w), w′ the second point and ⟪w, w′⟫ = Σ conj(wⱼ)w′ⱼ Mathlib's inner product, reads cosh²(d(w, w′)/√2) = |1 − ⟪w, w′⟫|²/((1 − |w|²)(1 − |w′|²)), so that d is `ballDist`, √2 times `Real.arcosh` of the square root of the right side, on B × B (the acceptance test defines `ballDist` by the same expression on all of ℂⁿ × ℂⁿ, and no statement reads its junk values off B × B); and the holomorphic isometries of (B, ω) are exactly the elements of G (Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified; Mok2012 line 319 to line 321 for the direction that automorphisms of the ball are isometries of its Kähler–Einstein metric, a constant multiple of ω). The distance formula follows from invariance and its value on a diameter; a holomorphic isometry is a biholomorphism, hence in G by §2.1. *Needs:* §§1.4, 2.1–2.2; RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.3; RiemannianGeometry's supplier contract with HopfRinow (geodesics, distance); Mathlib `Real.arcosh`.

**Checks.**

- d(0, tanh t·e₁) = √2|t|, and the right side of the formula is cosh² t.
- The right side is unchanged under g ∈ U(1,n), since ⟨gz,gw⟩ = ⟨z,w⟩, and under complex conjugation of the form, since it involves only |⟨z,w⟩|².
- d(0, w) → ∞ as |w| → 1, so no Cauchy sequence escapes to the boundary.
- `ballDist` at w = 0 and w′ = (1/2, 0, …, 0): the argument of arcosh is 1/√(3/4) = 2/√3, and arcosh(2/√3) = log(2/√3 + 1/√3) = (1/2) log 3, so the distance is (√2/2) log 3 ≈ 0.7768, equal to √2 artanh(1/2) from the geodesic s ↦ tanh(s/√2)·e₁. The argument is at least 1 on B because (1 − |w||w′|)² − (1 − |w|²)(1 − |w′|²) = (|w| − |w′|)² ≥ 0 and |1 − ⟪w, w′⟫| ≥ 1 − |w||w′|; it equals 1 exactly when w′ = w. The Euclidean distance is not invariant: for n = 1 the boost w ↦ (w + 1/2)/(1 + w/2) of U(1,1) (tanh t = 1/2) sends 0 and 1/2 to 1/2 and 4/5, at Euclidean distances 1/2 and 3/10, while the argument of arcosh is 2/√3 for both pairs (|1 − 2/5| = 3/5 and √((3/4)(9/25)) = 3√3/10).
- Killing comparison: the supplier's distance d(o, lieExp(tY_v)·o) = B(tY_v, tY_v)^(1/2) for the Killing form B(X,Y) = 2(n + 1)tr(XY), with tr(Y_v²) = 2|v|² = 2, is 2√(n + 1)|t|, and the distance here is √2|t|: the ratio is √(2(n + 1)), as Killing = 2(n + 1)·g_𝔹 requires.

### 2.5 The Schwarz–Pick contraction between balls

Prove that for m, n ≥ 1 every holomorphic map f: Bᵐ → Bⁿ satisfies f*g_(Bⁿ) ≤ g_(Bᵐ) as quadratic forms on tangent vectors, hence d(f(x), f(y)) ≤ d(x,y) (Kiernan1972 line 64 to line 65 for the corresponding property of the Kobayashi distance; Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified). By transitivity reduce to x = 0 = f(0); for a unit tangent vector X at 0 and the unit vector v along Df(0)X, the function ζ ↦ ⟨v, f(ζX)⟩ maps the disc to the disc and fixes 0, so |Df(0)X| ≤ 1 by the Schwarz lemma, and both metrics are twice the Euclidean metric at 0. *Needs:* §§1.4, 2.2; Mathlib `Complex.dist_le_div_mul_dist_of_mapsTo_ball`; Completed/ConformalMapping Layer 2.

**Checks.**

- f(ζ) = (ζ, 0) and f(ζ) = (ζ, ζ)/√2 are isometric at 0 (|Df(0)·1| = 1).
- f(ζ) = (ζ/2, ζ/2) has |Df(0)·1| = 1/√2 < 1, a strict contraction.
- ζ ↦ 2ζ does not map B¹ into B¹, and would expand the metric at 0 by 2.

### Examples

On B¹, ω is the curvature −2 Poincaré metric and §2.1 is the classification of disc automorphisms. On B², the complex line {w₂ = 0} and the real plane {Im w₁ = Im w₂ = 0} are both totally geodesic; on the first the curvature is −2, on the second −1/2. The horoballs H(c) of the Siegel model have finite volume modulo any lattice in N.

## Layer 3: Sub-balls and totally geodesic subvarieties of the ball

### 3.1 Sub-balls and their stabilisers

Take from RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7, for a complex subspace W ⊂ V of dimension m + 1 on which the form has signature (1,m), 1 ≤ m ≤ n (a condition that does not depend on the convention of the form, §1.1), the sub-ball 𝔹(W) = {[z] ∈ 𝔹ⁿ : z ∈ W} — a closed connected totally geodesic complex submanifold of dimension m, equal in the bounded model to the intersection of B with a complex affine subspace, biholomorphic to 𝔹ᵐ — and its stabiliser H_W = {g ∈ G : gW = W}, the image of U(W) × U(W^⊥) (W^⊥ negative definite), acting on 𝔹(W) through PU(W) ≅ PU(1,m). Extend the definition to m = 0, where W is a positive line and 𝔹(W) a point, and write 𝔹ᵐ = {wₘ₊₁ = … = wₙ = 0} for the standard sub-ball (BaldiUllmo2023 line 496 to line 506). Prove what this roadmap adds: that W ↦ 𝔹(W) is a bijection from such subspaces, m ≥ 0, onto sub-balls; that every nonempty intersection of B with a complex affine subspace is a sub-ball, since a positive vector p of the corresponding linear subspace W gives W = ℂp ⊕ (p^⊥ ∩ W) with p^⊥ negative definite; that G acts transitively on sub-balls of each dimension, since a basis of W orthonormal for the form followed by one of W^⊥ is carried to any other such basis by an element of U(1,n); that restriction gives a continuous surjective homomorphism r_W: H_W → Aut(𝔹(W)) ≅ PU(1,m) (§2.1 for 𝔹(W) ≅ 𝔹ᵐ) whose kernel, the image of U(1)·1_W × U(W^⊥), is compact; and that ω restricts on 𝔹(W) to the ball form of 𝔹(W). *Needs:* RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7; §§1.1–1.4, 2.1–2.2.

- Sub-balls of dimension 0 are points and of dimension n is B; 𝔹(W) ∩ 𝔹(W′) = 𝔹(W ∩ W′) or is empty.
- The restriction r_W is compatible with conjugation: r_(gW)(ghg⁻¹) corresponds to r_W(h).
- ω|_(𝔹(W)) is the invariant form of 𝔹(W), by §2.2 and transitivity.

**Checks.**

- m = 0: W is a positive line, 𝔹(W) is a point, H_W is a conjugate of K, and PU(1,0) is trivial.
- {w₁ = 1/2} ∩ B (n = 2) is the sub-ball of W = {z₁ = z₀/2}, on which the form is (3/4)|z₀|² − |z₂|², of signature (1,1): Euclidean-flat sub-balls are not centred at 0.
- A negative definite W, or a degenerate one such as the span of e₀ + e₁, meets 𝔹ⁿ in the empty set; signature (1,m) is needed.

### 3.2 Totally geodesic complex submanifolds through a point

For p ∈ B and a complex subspace T ⊂ T_pB = ℂⁿ of dimension m ≥ 1, put W_(p,T) = ℂ(1, p) ⊕ {(0, t) : t ∈ T}. Prove that 𝔹(W_(p,T)) is the unique sub-ball through p with tangent space T at p, in the bounded model (p + T) ∩ B, of signature (1,m) because (1, p) is a positive vector (⟨(1,p),(1,p)⟩ = 1 − |p|² > 0); and that a connected totally geodesic complex submanifold M ⊂ B with p ∈ M is an open subset of 𝔹(W_(p,T_pM)). The second statement is RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7's local classification (M is an open subset of 𝔹(W) for a unique W) with W identified by its tangent space at p; that sub-balls are totally geodesic is also the supplier's (Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified). *Needs:* §3.1; RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7; RiemannianGeometry R5a (second fundamental form, the meaning of "totally geodesic").

**Checks.**

- The complex line ℂv ∩ B through 0 is totally geodesic and is 𝔹(W_(0,ℂv)), the sub-ball of span(e₀, (0, v)).
- The curve {w₂ = w₁²} ∩ B (n = 2) is a complex submanifold through 0 tangent to {w₂ = 0} that is not contained in 𝔹(W_(0,ℂe₁)) = {w₂ = 0}, so it is not totally geodesic.
- The real plane {Im w₁ = Im w₂ = 0} is totally geodesic (a totally real one of RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.7) but not complex; the statement concerns complex submanifolds.
- For p = (1/2, 0) and T = ℂe₂ (n = 2), W_(p,T) = span((1, 1/2, 0), (0, 0, 1)) = {z₁ = z₀/2}, so the sub-ball is {w₁ = 1/2} ∩ B of §3.1, not the line through 0 parallel to T; a definition through T alone, without p, would give {w₁ = 0}.

### 3.3 Classification of totally geodesic complex subvarieties

Prove that a complete connected totally geodesic complex submanifold M of B of dimension m ≥ 1 is a sub-ball g𝔹ᵐ, whose stabiliser is H_W, with PU(1,m) acting on it as its full biholomorphism group through r_W; and the analytic version: an irreducible closed analytic subset Y ⊂ B of dimension m ≥ 1 whose regular locus is totally geodesic is a sub-ball (BaldiUllmo2023 line 496 to line 506; Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified). For the first, M is open in a sub-ball 𝔹(W) (§3.2), which is isometric to 𝔹ᵐ (§3.1) and hence a Hadamard manifold (§2.4); every x ∈ 𝔹(W) is exp_y(v) for y ∈ M and v ∈ T_yM = T_y𝔹(W), and completeness of M makes the geodesic of M with initial vector v, a geodesic of 𝔹(W), reach x; so M = 𝔹(W), and G is transitive on sub-balls of dimension m (§3.1). For the analytic version, apply §3.2 at a regular point p: Y ∩ 𝔹(W) contains a neighbourhood of p in Y^reg, Y^reg is connected, so Y ⊂ 𝔹(W), and a closed analytic subset of the connected m-manifold 𝔹(W) of dimension m is all of it. *Needs:* §§2.1, 2.4, 3.1–3.2; ComplexKahlerGeometry K3 (irreducible components, connectedness of the regular locus of an irreducible analytic subset, dimension of proper analytic subsets); RiemannianGeometry's HopfRinow contract (geodesics of a complete manifold).

**Checks.**

- The union of the two sub-balls {w₁ = 0} and {w₂ = 0} of B² has totally geodesic regular locus but is reducible; irreducibility is needed.
- A geodesic segment (real dimension 1) is totally geodesic but not a complex analytic subset.

### 3.4 Intersections of sub-balls and fixed sets of elements with an interior fixed point

Prove that two distinct sub-balls of the same dimension meet in a sub-ball of smaller dimension or not at all, and that the fixed set in B of an element g ∈ G ∖ {1} that fixes at least one point of B is a single proper sub-ball: g is conjugate into the compact group K (§1.4), so a lift of g is diagonalizable with unit eigenvalues, its eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal for the form, and exactly one of them has signature (1, ·), the others being negative definite (Goldman1999 Ch. 3, uncertified). These are the elliptic elements of §4.1, which takes this description as its definition. *Needs:* §§1.4, 3.1.

**Checks.**

- [diag(1, eⁱᶿ, 1)] (n = 2, θ ∉ 2πℤ) fixes the sub-ball {w₁ = 0}; [diag(1, ζ, ζ)] with ζ ≠ 1 fixes only 0.
- The complex reflection [diag(1, 1, −1)] fixes the divisor {w₂ = 0} of B².
- The sub-balls {w₁ = 0} and {w₂ = 0} of B² meet exactly in {0}.

### Examples

In B² the sub-balls of dimension 1 are the complex geodesics; through each point and each complex tangent line there is exactly one. The fixed set of a complex reflection is a sub-ball of codimension one, which is why quotients by groups generated by reflections can be smooth (§7.1).

## Layer 4: Isometries, horoballs and the Margulis lemma

### 4.1 Elliptic, parabolic and loxodromic elements

Define g ∈ G ∖ {1} to be elliptic if it fixes a point of B, parabolic if it fixes no point of B and exactly one point of ∂B, and loxodromic if it fixes no point of B and exactly two points of ∂B, and define its translation length ℓ(g) = inf_(x∈B) d(x, gx). On a lift g ∈ U(1,n) the acceptance test writes the parabolic condition as `IsParabolic g`: `ballMap g` has no fixed point in B and exactly one on the unit sphere ∂B; it does not depend on the lift, and it excludes the identity, which fixes B pointwise. `IsParabolic` is defined by the same condition for every g ∈ GLₙ₊₁(ℂ) and is applied only to lifts of elements of G. Prove that every nontrivial element is of exactly one type; that ℓ(g) = 0 is attained for elliptic, ℓ(g) = 0 is not attained for parabolic, and ℓ(g) > 0 is attained on an axis for loxodromic elements; that ℓ(a_s) = √2|log s|; and that an elliptic element of a discrete subgroup has finite order (Mok2012 line 325 to line 335). Every lift g̃ ∈ U(1,n) has an eigenvector v with ⟨v,v⟩ ≥ 0: an eigenvalue of modulus different from 1 has a null eigenvector; eigenvectors for distinct unit eigenvalues are orthogonal, so a diagonalizable g̃ with unit eigenvalues has a positive eigenvector; and a nontrivial Jordan block at a unit eigenvalue λ, with (g̃ − λ)w = v, forces λ̄⟨v,v⟩ = 0. Fixed points in B are positive eigenlines and fixed points in ∂B are null eigenlines. *Needs:* §§1.4–1.5, 2.4; Mathlib eigenvectors and Jordan decomposition of matrices over ℂ.

**Checks.**

- a_s (s ≠ 1) is loxodromic with fixed points (0,0) and ∞ of 𝔖, and translation length √2|log s|.
- T_t (t ≠ 0) is parabolic: its displacement √2 arcsinh(|t|/(2ρ)) tends to 0 as ρ → ∞ and is positive everywhere.
- The screw parabolic m·T_t (n ≥ 2, m a nontrivial rotation of u, t ≠ 0) is parabolic but not unipotent: "parabolic" does not mean "conjugate into N".
- Fixed points off the closed ball (n = 2): in the homogeneous coordinates y = (z₀ − z₂, z₁, i(z₀ + z₂)) of the Siegel model, T_t is y ↦ (y₀, y₁, y₂ + ty₀), which fixes the whole hyperplane {y₀ = 0}, that is the affine line {w₂ = 1} of the bounded model. So `ballMap` of a lift of T_t fixes every point (c, 1), c ∈ ℂ, of norm √(1 + |c|²); only c = 0, the point b₀, lies on the sphere. This is why `IsParabolic` counts fixed points on the sphere only, and why `IsCuspPoint` asks that b lie on ∂B.

### 4.2 Horoballs and displacement estimates

Prove that for a parabolic g fixing ∞ (g ∈ M·N), the displacement x ↦ d(x, gx) tends to 0 along every vertical ray, and that for every c > 0 there is ε > 0 with {x : d(x, gx) < ε} ⊂ H(c); that for a vertical translation the displacement depends only on ρ and equals √2 arcsinh(|t|/(2ρ)); and that the distance between the horospheres {ρ = c} and {ρ = c′} is (1/√2)|log(c′/c)| (Mok2012 line 383 to line 403; BaldiUllmo2023 line 566 to line 581). *Needs:* §§1.5, 2.2, 2.4, 4.1.

**Checks.**

- For T_t at ρ = |t|/2 the displacement is √2 arcsinh(1).
- For n_(a,0), a ≠ 0, at (0, iy) the displacement is of order |a|/√y as y → ∞, while on a fixed horosphere it is unbounded in the horizontal direction i·a.
- A loxodromic a_s has displacement at least √2|log s| everywhere: no horoball estimate holds for it.

### 4.3 Parabolic and loxodromic elements of discrete groups

Prove that in a discrete subgroup Δ ⊂ G a parabolic element and a loxodromic element have no common fixed point, so that the stabiliser in Δ of a fixed point of a parabolic element of Δ lies in M·N after conjugation (Mok2012 line 338 to line 342, after Eberlein–O'Neill). Conjugating a parabolic p ∈ M·N by powers of a loxodromic h = m a_s n fixing ∞ shrinks the N-component of p by powers of s while the M-components stay in the compact M, producing infinitely many distinct elements of Δ in a compact set. *Needs:* §§1.5, 4.1; Mathlib discreteness of subgroups.

**Checks.**

- ⟨a_(1/2), T_1⟩ is not discrete: a_(1/2)^k T_1 a_(1/2)⁻ᵏ = T_(4⁻ᵏ) → 1.
- ⟨T_1⟩ and ⟨a_2⟩ are each discrete; it is their combination that fails.
- A finite group of rotations fixing ∞ together with ⟨T_1⟩ generates a discrete group fixing ∞ with no loxodromic element.

### 4.4 Zassenhaus neighbourhoods and the Margulis lemma

Prove that a closed subgroup L of GL_N(ℂ) has a neighbourhood U of 1 such that for every discrete subgroup Δ ⊂ L the subgroup generated by Δ ∩ U is nilpotent, and deduce the Margulis lemma for 𝔹ⁿ: there are ε_n > 0 and m_n ∈ ℕ such that for every discrete Δ ⊂ G and every x ∈ B, the subgroup generated by {γ ∈ Δ : d(x, γx) < ε_n} has a nilpotent subgroup of index at most m_n (Raghunathan1972 Ch. VIII, uncertified; Mok2012 line 342 to line 349 for the torsion-free consequence; BaldiUllmo2023 line 573 to line 575). In the acceptance test this is `margulis_lemma`, with d = `ballDist` (§2.4), the displacement of γ computed by `ballMap` on a lift, and the index of the nilpotent subgroup required to be finite and at most m_n. The first statement is applied to the closed subgroup SU(1,n) of GLₙ₊₁(ℂ): the preimage of a discrete Δ ⊂ G is discrete because the kernel μₙ₊₁ of SU(1,n) → G is finite (§1.2), so the image U ⊂ G of a neighbourhood it provides has the same property for discrete subgroups of G, nilpotency passing to images. The passage from U to displacement is a covering argument and uses no theorem on finite linear groups. Choose an open V ∋ 1 with V⁻¹V ⊂ U. The set B₁ = {g ∈ G : d(0, g·0) ≤ 1} is compact, since the orbit map G → B is proper (K is compact, §1.4) and d(0, w) = √2 artanh|w| (§2.4) makes the closed ball of radius 1 a closed Euclidean ball inside B; cover it by finitely many translates g₁V, …, g_mV and put m_n = m, ε_n = 1/m. After conjugating x to 0, let F = {γ ∈ Δ : d(0, γ·0) < ε_n}, Λ = ⟨F⟩ and Λ₀ = Λ ∩ ⟨Δ ∩ U⟩, which is nilpotent. A product of at most m elements of F lies in B₁ by the triangle inequality, and two such products in the same translate g_iV differ by an element of Λ ∩ V⁻¹V ⊂ Λ₀, so these products meet at most m left cosets of Λ₀. The sets C_j of cosets met by products of at most j elements of F increase with j, and Cⱼ₊₁ = C_j forces C_j = Λ/Λ₀ because F is symmetric and generates Λ; a strictly increasing chain C₀ ⊊ … ⊊ C_m would put at least m + 1 cosets in C_m, so C_j = Cⱼ₊₁ for some j < m and [Λ : Λ₀] = |C_j| ≤ m. *Needs:* §§1.2, 1.4, 2.4 (`ballDist`); Mathlib matrix norms (the commutator estimate behind the first statement), `Group.IsNilpotent`, `Subgroup.FiniteIndex`.

**Checks.**

- For n = 1 and the cyclic group C_m ⊂ K generated by [diag(1, exp(2πi/m))], acting by w ↦ exp(2πi/m)w, every displacement at x = 0 vanishes and C_m is abelian, a nilpotent subgroup of index 1 in itself for every m; the trivial subgroup has index m, unbounded in m, so the uniform bound m_n is a bound for the index of a nilpotent subgroup and is not obtained from finiteness of the group.

### 4.5 Discrete groups fixing a boundary point

Prove that if Δ ⊂ M·N is discrete and acts cocompactly on a horosphere at ∞, then Δ ∩ N is a lattice in N of finite index in Δ; that the image Λ of Δ ∩ N in N/Z(N) = ℂⁿ⁻¹ is a lattice of rank 2n − 2; and that, for n ≥ 2, Δ ∩ Z(N) is infinite cyclic, generated by a vertical translation T_τ with τ > 0 (Mok2012 line 373 to line 472; Raghunathan1972 Ch. ## Layer 5: Lattices in PU(1,n)

### 5.1 Lattices, cocompact lattices and commensurability

Specialise VonNeumannAlgebras Layer 9 to G: a lattice is a discrete subgroup Γ ⊂ G of finite covolume for Haar measure, cocompact when G/Γ is compact; two subgroups are commensurable in the sense of Mathlib's `Subgroup.Commensurable` (WitteMorris2015 line 1893 and line 1940; BaldiUllmo2023 line 355 to line 357). G qualifies because it is second countable and locally compact (§1.2) and unimodular (§1.4). In the acceptance test a lattice is `IsLattice Γ`: Γ is discrete and, for some Haar measure on G, Γ acting by left multiplication has a fundamental domain of finite measure, and cocompactness is compactness of the coset space G/Γ. Prove that this is VonNeumannAlgebras Layer 9's notion: the σ-algebra the acceptance test puts on G, `QuotientGroup.measurableSpace` coinduced from the entrywise σ-algebra of matrices through `GL` and U(1,n), is the Borel σ-algebra (the quotient map by the compact centre is continuous and surjective between Polish spaces, so a set E with Borel preimage is the image of that preimage and its complement is the image of the complementary preimage; both are analytic, and E is Borel by Suslin's theorem), and any two Haar measures are proportional. The supplier measures covolume on a fundamental domain of the right action g ↦ gγ, for a right Haar measure, with G/Γ the space of left cosets gΓ; inversion g ↦ g⁻¹ carries a fundamental domain s of the left action to the fundamental domain s⁻¹ of the right action, carries a Haar measure μ to the right Haar measure μ ∘ inv with (μ ∘ inv)(s⁻¹) = μ(s), which equals μ because G is unimodular (§1.4), and induces a homeomorphism Γ\G ≅ G/Γ, Γg ↦ g⁻¹Γ; so finite covolume, the covolume itself and cocompactness mean the same on the two sides. *Needs:* VonNeumannAlgebras Layer 9; Mathlib `MeasureTheory.covolume`, `MeasureTheory.IsFundamentalDomain`, `MeasureTheory.Measure.IsHaarMeasure`, `QuotientGroup.measurableSpace`, `Subgroup.Commensurable`; §§1.2, 1.4.

- A subgroup of finite index in a lattice is a lattice, with covolume multiplied by the index, and a subgroup containing a lattice with finite index is a lattice; commensurable discrete subgroups are lattices together.
- In a unimodular second-countable locally compact group L (G here, and in §11.1 the identity component of the real points of a connected semisimple ℚ-group), a discrete subgroup Λ containing a lattice Γ is a lattice, Γ has finite index in Λ, and covol(Γ) = [Λ : Γ]·covol(Λ): for a measurable fundamental domain F of Λ for left multiplication, of positive Haar measure because countably many translates of F cover L, and a set R of representatives of the right cosets Γλ (the space Γ\Λ), the set R·F, the union of the rF for r ∈ R, is a fundamental domain of Γ for left multiplication, of measure |R|·μ(F) by left invariance, since Λ is the disjoint union of the Γr (WitteMorris2015, Exercise 10 of §4.1, line 2000 to line 2003, whose hint works with the right action and so with representatives of Λ/Γ placed on the right).
- Conjugates gΓg⁻¹ are lattices of the same covolume, G being unimodular.
- A lattice is cocompact if and only if Γ\B is compact (§5.3).

### 5.2 Proper discontinuity and finite stabilisers

### 5.3 Covolume and the volume of the quotient

By RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.6, read through §§1.4 and 2.2 (vol_ω is (2(n + 1))⁻ⁿ times the Riemannian volume of the Killing metric), vol_ω is the pushforward of a Haar measure of G along the orbit map p: G → B, and a measurable fundamental domain in B of a discrete subgroup has finite volume exactly when the subgroup has finite covolume. Normalise the Haar measure μ_G so that μ_G(p⁻¹(A)) = vol_ω(A) for every Borel A ⊂ B, and prove: that a discrete Γ, which is countable because G is second countable, has a measurable fundamental domain F ⊂ B with pairwise disjoint translates (Tau Ceti `MeasureTheory.Measure.exists_isFundamentalDomain_of_properlyDiscontinuousSMul`, applied to the properly discontinuous action of §5.2 and to vol_ω, under which the non-free locus, a locally finite union of proper sub-balls, is null); that for every measurable fundamental domain F ⊂ B for Γ, p⁻¹(F) is a fundamental domain for Γ acting on G and covol(Γ) = vol_ω(F), an equality and not only a comparison of finiteness; hence Γ is a lattice if and only if Γ\B has finite ω-volume; and that Γ is cocompact if and only if Γ\B is compact, p being proper because K is compact (WitteMorris2015 line 1899 to line 1921, Proposition 4.1.11; BaldiUllmo2023 line 338 to line 339). *Needs:* §§1.4, 2.2, 5.1–5.2; RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.6; Mathlib `MeasureTheory.IsFundamentalDomain.covolume_eq_volume`; Tau Ceti `MeasureTheory.Measure.exists_isFundamentalDomain_of_properlyDiscontinuousSMul` and `TauCeti.freeLocus` (existence of F); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.riemannianVolume` (TauCeti/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/VolumeDensity/Volume.lean:82) for the volume of ω.

**Checks.**

- A horoball H(c) modulo a lattice of N has finite volume, ∫_c^∞ ρ⁻⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ dρ < ∞, while the strip {0 < ρ < 1} modulo the same lattice has infinite volume.

## Layer 6: Cusps, finite generation and Selberg's lemma

### 6.1 The cusp structure theorem

Define the cusp points of a subgroup Γ ⊂ G, discrete in every use below, as the points of ∂B fixed by a parabolic element of Γ (`IsCuspPoint`, through a lift and `IsParabolic`), and the cusp classes as the Γ-orbits of cusp points. Prove that for a lattice Γ: the cusp classes are finite in number; for every cusp point b, the stabiliser Γ_b lies in M_b·N_b, acts cocompactly on the horospheres at b, Γ_b ∩ N_b is a lattice in N_b of finite index in Γ_b, and for n ≥ 2, Γ ∩ Z(N_b) is infinite cyclic; there are horoballs H_b at the cusp points with γH_b = H_(γb), pairwise disjoint, precisely invariant (γH_b ∩ H_b ≠ ∅ implies γ ∈ Γ_b), and every smaller concentric family has the same properties; the image of B ∖ ⋃_b H_b in Γ\B is compact; and Γ is cocompact if and only if it has no cusp points. The first and last statements are the acceptance test's `IsLattice.exists_finset_cuspPoint_orbits` (the cusp points lie in the Γ-orbits of a finite set) and `IsLattice.compactSpace_quotient_iff` (Mok2012 line 309 to line 475 for torsion-free Γ; BaldiUllmo2023 Theorem 3.4.3, line 649 to line 661, the theorem of Garland–Raghunathan for every lattice; Raghunathan1972 Ch. XIII, uncertified; SiuYau1982 pp. 363–380, uncertified, for manifolds). The argument recalled at Mok2012 line 336 to line 349 for torsion-free Γ divides B into points where only the identity has displacement less than ε and cusp or tube regions; with torsion this two-case division fails, since every element of the finite stabiliser Γ_x of a point x has displacement 0 there. The proof for every lattice uses ε = ε_n of §4.4 and the group Γ_ε(x) generated by the finite set {γ ∈ Γ : d(x, γx) < ε} (finite by §5.2), through three intermediate statements.

(1) Trichotomy. Γ_ε(x) is virtually nilpotent (§4.4) and finitely generated, so it is finite or has an element of infinite order, which is parabolic or loxodromic by §4.1. If Γ_ε(x) contains a parabolic element with fixed point b, it fixes b and lies in Γ_b ⊂ M_b·N_b; if it is infinite without parabolic elements, it contains a loxodromic element and preserves its axis; if it is finite, it fixes a point of B, by Cartan's fixed-point theorem (RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.4) for the finite group Γ_ε(x) acting by isometries on the Hadamard manifold B (§2.4). The finite case consists of the points x whose whole group Γ_ε(x) is finite. A point fixed by an elliptic element of Γ lies in it only when Γ_ε(x) has no element of infinite order; otherwise it is of parabolic or loxodromic type (the last Check below). The first two cases use §4.3 and ping-pong: high powers of two parabolic elements with distinct fixed points, or of two loxodromic elements with distinct pairs of fixed points, generate a free group of rank 2, which is not virtually nilpotent.

(2) Reduction theory. If Γ is not cocompact, there are a finite set Ξ ⊂ G and a Siegel set Σ at b₀ — the points (u, v) of the Siegel model with ρ(u, v) ≥ c whose N-coordinate lies in a compact Ω ⊂ N — such that Γ ∩ ξNξ⁻¹ is a lattice in ξNξ⁻¹ for every ξ ∈ Ξ, the union ΞΣ of the ξΣ is a fundamental set (Γ·ΞΣ = B and {γ : γΞΣ ∩ ΞΣ ≠ ∅} is finite), and {γ : γξΣ′ ∩ ξΣ′ ≠ ∅} is finite for every ξ ∈ Ξ and every Siegel set Σ′ at b₀ (BaldiUllmo2023 line 649 to line 658, quoting GarlandRaghunathan1970 Theorem 4.10 for admissible discrete subgroups together with its Theorem 0.7 that every lattice is admissible; Raghunathan1972 Ch. XIII, uncertified). This is the statement that carries the torsion: for torsion-free Γ it follows from (1) by the volume argument, since an ε/2-ball about a point with Γ_ε(x) = {1} embeds in Γ\B, whereas for Γ with torsion the finite groups of (1) have no bound on their order fixed in advance and the volume argument gives no lower bound; Garland–Raghunathan's proof is the one carried out here.

(3) Deduction. A cocompact Γ has no parabolic element: if Γ·C = B with C compact, only finitely many conjugates of p ∈ Γ move a point of C by at most 1 (§5.2), so inf_x d(x, px) is attained, which §4.1 excludes for parabolic p. For non-cocompact Γ, each Γ ∩ ξNξ⁻¹ is infinite, and its nontrivial elements are parabolic with fixed point ξb₀. Every cusp point b is Γ-equivalent to some ξb₀: for p ∈ Γ parabolic fixing b and x_k → b along a geodesic ray, d(x_k, px_k) → 0 (§4.2); writing x_k = γ_k y_k with y_k ∈ ΞΣ, the conjugates γ_k⁻¹pγ_k have displacement tending to 0 at y_k, which therefore leaves every compact set (finitely many conjugates otherwise, one of them fixing a limit point) and rises in some ξΣ, where the central elements of Γ ∩ ξNξ⁻¹ given by §4.5 (the translations if n = 1) also have displacement tending to 0; by (1), γ_k⁻¹b = ξb₀. Hence the cusp classes are at most |Ξ| in number; Γ_b ⊂ M_b·N_b by §4.3, Γ_b ∩ N_b is a lattice in N_b, so Γ_b acts cocompactly on horospheres (§1.5), and §4.5 gives the finite index and Γ ∩ Z(N_b). For precisely invariant horoballs, take Ω to contain a fundamental domain of Γ_b ∩ N_b in N_b: the elements γ with γH ∩ H ≠ ∅ for H = {ρ_b > t} lie in Γ_b·E·Γ_b with E finite by (2), and each γ ∈ E ∖ Γ_b moves b to γb ≠ b, so the horoballs at b and γb of large height are disjoint; disjointness for distinct cusp classes is the same argument. Finally ΞΣ minus the horoballs is relatively compact and maps onto the image of B ∖ ⋃_b H_b in Γ\B. *Needs:* §§1.5, 2.3–2.4, 4.1–4.5, 5.2–5.3; RealSemisimpleLieGroups §5.4 (Cartan's fixed-point theorem).

### 6.3 Selberg's lemma in eigenvalue form

Prove that for a field F, a finite set ι and a finitely generated subgroup Λ ⊂ GL_ι(F), there is a normal subgroup Λ′ ⊂ Λ of finite index such that, for every g ∈ Λ′, the subgroup of the multiplicative group of an algebraic closure generated by the eigenvalues of g contains no nontrivial root of unity; in particular no eigenvalue of an element of Λ′ is a nontrivial root of unity, and when F has characteristic 0, Λ′ is torsion-free, an element of finite order being diagonalizable with root-of-unity eigenvalues. This is `selberg_lemma` in the acceptance test, the eigenvalues being the roots of the characteristic polynomial in `AlgebraicClosure F`; for ι empty, GL_ι(F) is trivial and the statement is empty (WitteMorris2015 Theorem 4.8.4, line 2972, whose proof, line 2974 to line 2988, treats SL(ℓ,ℤ) only, and Remark 4.8.5(2), line 3002 to line 3005, for the form with the group generated by the eigenvalues; Theorem 4.8.2, line 2910, and Remark 4.8.5(3), line 3008, for the torsion-free form over any field of characteristic 0; Borel1969 §17, Corollary 17.7, and Raghunathan1972 Theorem 6.11, both uncertified, cited at WitteMorris2015 line 3445 as proving Remark 4.8.5(2) and (3) for finitely generated linear groups; Alperin1987 pp. 269–273, uncertified). The statement in this generality, with the normality of Λ′, is this roadmap's, by the following route. The entries of the generators and their inverses lie in a finitely generated subring R ⊂ F, a finitely generated algebra over ℤ or over 𝔽_p; R is a Jacobson ring with finite residue fields at maximal ideals, and Λ′ is the kernel of reduction modulo an ideal 𝔞 of finite index, normal as a kernel. For g ∈ Λ′ with eigenvalues λ₁, …, λ_N, the ring S = R[λ₁, …, λ_N] inside the algebraic closure is a domain integral over R; det g = λ₁⋯λ_N is a unit of R, the entries of g⁻¹ lying in R, so every λᵢ is a unit of S and the group generated by the eigenvalues lies in S^×. The characteristic polynomial of g is (X − 1)^N modulo 𝔞, so for every prime 𝔐 of S over a maximal ideal 𝔪 ⊇ 𝔞 (such 𝔐 exist by lying over) (λᵢ − 1)^N lies in 𝔞S ⊂ 𝔐, hence λᵢ ≡ 1 modulo 𝔐, and therefore every product ζ of eigenvalues and their inverses satisfies ζ ≡ 1 modulo 𝔐. Let such a ζ have exact order d. For a root of unity η ≡ 1 modulo 𝔐 of exact order e > 1, Π_(0<j<e)(1 − η^j) = e lies in 𝔐, so e is divisible by the residue characteristic ℓ of 𝔪 (the characteristic-polynomial argument of WitteMorris2015 line 2974 to line 2988). In characteristic p every root of unity has order prime to p, so any single maximal ideal 𝔞 = 𝔪 forces d = 1. In characteristic 0 take 𝔞 = 𝔪₁𝔪₂ with residue characteristics two distinct primes ℓ₁ ≠ ℓ₂, which exist because the image of Spec R in Spec ℤ contains a nonempty open set; the product argument applied to η = ζ^(ℓ₁^a), where ℓ₁^a is the ℓ₁-part of d, shows that d is a power of ℓ₁, and likewise of ℓ₂, so d = 1. *Needs:* Mathlib `IsJacobsonRing`, `isJacobsonRing_of_finiteType`, `finite_of_finite_type_of_isJacobsonRing` (Mathlib/RingTheory/Jacobson/Ring.lean:60, :681, :691), `Ideal.exists_ideal_over_maximal_of_isIntegral` (Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/GoingUp.lean:366, lying over), `Group.FG`, `Matrix.charpoly`, `AlgebraicClosure`.

**Checks.**

- In SL(2,ℤ), the principal congruence subgroup Γ(3) has the property. The eigenvalues λ, λ⁻¹ of an element generate the cyclic group ⟨λ⟩, which contains a nontrivial root of unity exactly when λ is one, and an element with a nontrivial root-of-unity eigenvalue has trace −2, −1, 0 or 1; elements of Γ(3) have trace ≡ 2 mod 3, which excludes −2, 0 and 1, and trace −1 would force order 3, impossible in the torsion-free Γ(3). The subgroup Γ(2) contains −1 and fails.
- Λ = {±1} ⊂ GL_1(ℚ): Λ′ = {1}.
- Products of eigenvalues count. In GL_2(ℚ) let g = diag(2, −1/2) and Λ = ⟨g⟩ ≅ ℤ. No eigenvalue 2^k, (−1/2)^k of g^k (k ≠ 0) is a root of unity, so the conclusion for single eigenvalues holds with Λ′ = Λ; but 2 · (−1/2) = −1 lies in the group generated by the eigenvalues of g, and of every odd power of g, so Λ′ = Λ fails here and Λ′ = ⟨g²⟩, of index 2, is the largest admissible choice (g^(2k) has eigenvalues 4^k, 4^(−k), which generate the torsion-free ⟨4^k⟩). ### 6.4 Neat lattices

Define a subgroup of G to be neat when, for each of its elements γ, the eigenvalues of Ad(γ) generate a torsion-free subgroup of ℂ^× (Conventions; `IsNeat`). Subgroups are neat because the condition is imposed element by element, and conjugates because Ad(hγh⁻¹) = Ad(h)Ad(γ)Ad(h)⁻¹ has the eigenvalues of Ad(γ). For γ = m·η ∈ Γ_b with m ∈ M, η ∈ N, the finite index of Γ_b ∩ N_b makes m of finite order, the eigenvalues of Ad(γ) are those of Ad(m), all roots of unity, and neatness forces them to be 1; Ad(m), of finite order and so diagonalizable, is then 1, hence m = 1. *Needs:* §§1.2 (Ad, `conjugationMap`), 6.1–6.3.

**Checks.**

### 8.5 Finite volume of analytic subvarieties

Prove that for every irreducible compact analytic subset Y ⊂ X^M of dimension k ≥ 1 not contained in D, ∫_(Y^reg ∖ D) ω^k < ∞ (BaldiUllmo2023 line 586 to line 601 for the Poincaré-growth input; Mok2012 Proposition 1, line 295). **Checks.**

- n = 1, Y = X^M: ∫_0^δ dr/(r log² r) < ∞ near each cusp.

## Layer 9: Projective algebraicity of the minimal compactification

Γ is a neat lattice and X^M, D, E are as in Layer 8 unless stated otherwise; K_X carries the metric h induced by ω^n, and "L²" refers to h^ℓ and ω^n.

### 9.1 L² pluricanonical sections with prescribed jets

Prove that for every finite set P ⊂ X there is ℓ₀ such that for every ℓ ≥ ℓ₀ and every assignment of 1-jets at the points of P (values in the fibres of K_X^ℓ and first derivatives in local holomorphic coordinates), there is a holomorphic L² section of K_X^ℓ on X with these 1-jets; and that for every compact Q ⊂ X there is ℓ₀ such that for ℓ ≥ ℓ₀ and every x ∈ Q some holomorphic L² section of K_X^ℓ does not vanish at x (Mok2012 Theorem 2, line 495 to line 514, and line 681 to line 772 and line 817 to line 901). The route: (X, ω) is complete Kähler (§7.2), iΘ(K_X^(ℓ−1)) = (ℓ − 1)(n + 1)ω by §2.3, so ComplexComparisonPartII §C1.4 applies to (n,1)-forms with values in K_X^(ℓ−1) with the smooth weights φ_ε = Σ_(x∈P) (n + 1)χ_x log(|z_x|² + ε), whose curvature is bounded below uniformly in ε ≤ 1 outside a compact set; the solutions u_ε of ∂̄u = ∂̄(χ s₀) satisfy a bound uniform in ε, a subsequence converges by Montel (ComplexComparisonPartII §C1.6), and non-integrability of |z|⁻²ⁿ (resp. |z|⁻²ⁿ⁻²) forces the limit to vanish to order 1 (resp. 2) at the points of P. *Needs:* §§2.3, 6.4 (a neat lattice is torsion-free), 7.2; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C1.4, C1.6; ComplexKahlerGeometry K6.0 (curvature lower bound on (n,1)-forms).

**Checks.**

- n = 1, Γ cocompact of genus g ≥ 2: every section of K^ℓ is L², deg K^ℓ = ℓ(2g − 2) ≥ 2g + 1 for ℓ ≥ 3 (6g − 6 ≥ 2g + 1 for g ≥ 2), and also for ℓ = 2 when g ≥ 3, so K^ℓ separates points and tangents, consistent with the conclusion; for g = 2 and ℓ = 2, deg K² = 4 < 5 and the bicanonical map factors through the hyperelliptic involution, so ℓ₀ depends on Γ, as the theorem allows.
- In real dimension 2n, ∫_(|z|<1) |z|⁻²ⁿ dV diverges (dr/r), which forces u(x) = 0; the weight (n − 1/2) log|z|² is integrable and forces nothing.
- With ω replaced by an incomplete metric on a ball, ComplexComparisonPartII §C1.4 does not apply: completeness of ω_Γ is used.

### 9.2 Meromorphic extension of L² sections and sections nonvanishing on a boundary component

Prove that for every integer ℓ ≥ 1 a holomorphic L² section of K_X^ℓ on X extends to a holomorphic section of E^ℓ on X^M vanishing on D, and conversely; that for every ℓ ≥ 0 the space of holomorphic sections of E^ℓ on X^M is finite-dimensional; and that for every ℓ ≥ 2 and every component T_b of D there is a section of E^ℓ nowhere zero on T_b and zero on the other components (Mok2012 Main Theorem, line 546 to line 553, and line 591 to line 678). The extension follows from the volume estimate of §8.3 and the mean-value inequality, which bound |q|^e f for some e and so make f meromorphic across D, and from §8.4, which limits the pole order; the section nonvanishing on T_b is χν^ℓ − u with u the L² solution of ∂̄u = ∂̄(χν^ℓ) given by §C1.4. *Needs:* §§8.2–8.4, 9.1; ComplexKahlerGeometry K3 (Riemann extension on manifolds); ComplexComparisonPartII §§C1.4, C1.6.

**Checks.**

- n = 1, Γ = Γ(2)/{±1} (neat, X^M = ℙ¹, three cusps): E = O(1), sections of E^ℓ form a space of dimension ℓ + 1, and those vanishing on D, the sections of O(ℓ − 3), a space of dimension ℓ − 2 for ℓ ≥ 2 (and 0 for ℓ = 1), the dimension of the weight-2ℓ cusp forms for Γ(2).
- For ℓ = 1 the section ν itself has |ν|² ≍ (−log|q|)ⁿ⁺¹, not L²: the hypothesis ℓ ≥ 2 enters through iΘ(K^ℓ) + Ric = (ℓ − 1)(n + 1)ω > 0.
- For a cocompact Γ, D = ∅, E = K_X and the statement is the finite-dimensionality of pluricanonical sections.
- The equivalence fails for ℓ = 0: for Γ = Γ(2)/{±1} the constant function 1 is L² (its integral is the ω-area π) and its extension to X^M = ℙ¹ is nonzero at the three cusps.

### 9.3 Mok's projective embedding theorem

Prove that there is ℓ such that the sections of E^ℓ on X^M have no common zero, the induced holomorphic map Φ_ℓ: X^M → ℙ^(N_ℓ) is constant on each T_b with pairwise distinct values ζ_b not in Φ_ℓ(X), and Φ_ℓ restricted to X is an injective immersion; so Z = Φ_ℓ(X^M) is a closed analytic subset of ℙ^(N_ℓ), hence the analytification of a projective variety, and Z ∖ {ζ_b} = Φ_ℓ(X) is smooth (Mok2012 Main Theorem, line 546 to line 562, with the steps of line 681 to line 930). Base points on D are excluded by §9.2 and on the compact common zero set by §9.1; ramification and identifications are eliminated by Noetherian induction on the analytic sets where they occur, each step replacing ℓ by a multiple and using §9.1 at one point of each top-dimensional component; constancy on T_b holds because every section of E^ℓ restricts on the compact connected T_b to a constant multiple of the section nowhere zero there. *Needs:* §§9.1–9.2; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C4.1 (`remmertProperImage`), C4.2 (`projectiveChowNonreduced`); ComplexKahlerGeometry K3.

**Checks.**

- n = 1, Γ(2)/{±1}: E = O(1) on ℙ¹; Φ_ℓ is a Veronese-type embedding for ℓ ≥ 2 and contracts nothing, since each T_b is a point.
- Γ cocompact: Φ_ℓ is a pluricanonical embedding, the case MilneSVI Remark 3.13(c) (line 1734) attributes to the Kodaira embedding theorem.
- For n = 1, K_(X^M) = O(−2) on ℙ¹ has no sections at all: the twist by O(D) is needed.

### 9.4 The minimal compactification of a neat ball quotient

Construct S*_Γ as the normalization of the projective variety Z of §9.3, ∂ as the preimage of {ζ_b}, and S_Γ = S*_Γ ∖ ∂. Prove that S*_Γ is a normal projective variety; that ∂ is finite with exactly one point for each cusp class; that S_Γ is a smooth quasi-projective variety with an isomorphism φ_Γ: S_Γ^an ≅ X_Γ; that Φ_ℓ lifts to a holomorphic map X^M → (S*_Γ)^an that is the identity on X and contracts each T_b to the point of ∂ of its class; that the images of the horoball quotients H_b(c)/Γ_b, together with that point, form a neighbourhood basis of it; and that S*_Γ does not depend on ℓ, up to unique isomorphism restricting to φ_Γ (Mok2012 Main Theorem, line 559 to line 562; Theorem 1, line 479 to line 482). Independence of ℓ: for two admissible values the identity of X extends to a homeomorphism of the two normalizations, both of which are projective, so with compact Hausdorff analytifications (ComplexComparisonPartII §C-1.1 with ReductiveGroups §§3A.5b–3A.5c), and have one point over each end of X, and this homeomorphism is holomorphic by gap:normal-riemann-extension and algebraic by ComplexComparisonPartII §C4.4. The point of ∂ over ζ_b is unique because Φ_ℓ is proper with fibre T_b over ζ_b, so Z near ζ_b is the image of a neighbourhood of T_b whose complement of T_b is connected, and Z is therefore locally irreducible at ζ_b. *Needs:* §§8.2, 9.3; Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.normalization`; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.4; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C-1.1, C4.4; ReductiveGroups §§3A.5b–3A.5c; gap:normality-analytification; gap:normal-riemann-extension.

**Checks.**

- n = 1: S*_Γ is the smooth projective curve compactifying X_Γ, and ∂ is the set of cusp classes.
- Γ cocompact: ∂ = ∅ and S*_Γ = S_Γ is a smooth projective variety.
- ∂ has one point per cusp class, not per cusp point: the cusp points of Γ form an infinite set while ∂ is finite.

### 9.5 The minimal compactification of an arbitrary lattice

Prove that for every lattice Γ and every normal neat Γ′ ◁ Γ of finite index (§6.4), the finite group F = Γ/Γ′ acts on S*_Γ′ by algebraic automorphisms preserving ∂_Γ′, and that S*_Γ := S*_Γ′/F (SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.17), with ∂_Γ the image of ∂_Γ′ and S_Γ := S*_Γ ∖ ∂_Γ, satisfies: S*_Γ is a normal projective variety, ∂_Γ is finite and in bijection with the cusp classes of Γ, ∂_Γ = ∅ if and only if Γ is cocompact, S_Γ is a normal quasi-projective variety with an isomorphism φ_Γ: S_Γ^an ≅ X_Γ, smooth when Γ is torsion-free; and that (S*_Γ, φ_Γ) does not depend on Γ′ up to unique isomorphism (Mok2012 Main Theorem for neat Γ; MilneSVI Remark 3.13(a), line 1722 to line 1724, in the arithmetic case; BaldiUllmo2023 Theorem 3.1.12, line 455 to line 462). An element of F acts on X_Γ′ by an analytic automorphism, extends to a homeomorphism of (S*_Γ′)^an permuting the cusp points, is holomorphic there by gap:normal-riemann-extension, and is algebraic by ComplexComparisonPartII §C4.4; the analytification of S*_Γ′/F is the analytic quotient by ShimuraVarieties §P.6, which on S_Γ′ is X_Γ by §7.3. *Needs:* §§6.4, 7.3, 9.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.17; ShimuraVarieties §P.6; ComplexComparisonPartII §C4.4; gap:normal-riemann-extension; gap:normality-analytification.

**Checks.**

- PSL(2,ℤ) (n = 1): S*_Γ = ℙ¹, ∂ = {∞}, S_Γ = 𝔸¹ through the j-function.
- A cocompact lattice with torsion gives a normal projective S*_Γ = S_Γ. When n ≥ 2 it is singular at the image of every point x with Γ_x ≠ 1 acting freely on a punctured neighbourhood of x: small punctured neighbourhoods of the image are (U ∖ {x})/Γ_x with U ∖ {x} a punctured ball, simply connected, so their fundamental group is Γ_x ≠ 1, whereas a smooth point has simply connected punctured ball neighbourhoods (for Γ_x = {1, w ↦ −w} and n = 2, the quadric cone of §7.1). A point that is the isolated fixed point of some element of Γ_x can still have a smooth image: for Γ_x generated by the complex reflections [diag(1, −1, 1)] and [diag(1, 1, −1)] (n = 2), whose product [diag(1, −1, −1)] acts by w ↦ −w and fixes only 0, the quotient near 0 has coordinates (w₁², w₂²) and is smooth.
- Two neat subgroups Γ′, Γ″ give the same S*_Γ through S*_(Γ′∩Γ″), which maps finitely onto both.

### 9.6 The case n = 1

Prove that for n = 1 and any lattice Γ, (S*_Γ)^an is FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4's compactified quotient of the corresponding Fuchsian group, compatibly with X_Γ and with the cusp points; in particular that compact Riemann surface is projective algebraic (MilneSVI line 1690 to line 1694, the embedding of the compactified quotient by modular forms of high weight, for torsion-free arithmetic Γ). *Needs:* §§7.4, 9.5; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4.

## Layer 10: Extension, Borel algebraicity and uniqueness

### 10.1 Distance-decreasing maps and hyperbolic embedding

Prove, for a torsion-free lattice Γ with Riemannian distance d_Γ of ω_Γ on X_Γ: every holomorphic map from the disc to X_Γ is distance-decreasing from the curvature −2 metric of the disc, and every holomorphic map from the punctured disc Δ* to X_Γ is distance-decreasing from the complete metric |dq|²/(2|q|² log²|q|) of Δ*, in which the circle |q| = r has length √2π/|log r|; and that X_Γ is hyperbolically embedded in (S*_Γ)^an for d_Γ: if x_k → p and y_k → p′ in (S*_Γ)^an with x_k, y_k ∈ X_Γ and p ≠ p′, then d_Γ(x_k, y_k) does not tend to 0 (Kiernan1972 line 57 to line 75 for the properties and the definition; Deng2020 line 61 to line 63 for the arithmetic case). Maps from discs lift to B (the disc is simply connected) and §2.5 applies; maps from Δ* lift from its universal covering by the upper half-plane; distinct boundary points are cusp points, and points deep in distinct cusp neighbourhoods are far apart by §4.2. *Needs:* §§2.5, 4.2, 7.2, 9.4–9.5.

**Checks.**

- The circle |q| = r has length 2πr/(√2 r|log r|) = √2π/|log r| → 0.
- For the image of Γ(3), points tending to two different cusps are at distance tending to ∞.
- PSL(2,ℤ)\ℍ is ℂ as a complex space, and the identity of ℂ is a holomorphic map from a space with vanishing Kobayashi pseudo-distance: torsion-freeness is needed for any hyperbolicity statement.

### 10.2 The extension theorem

Prove that for a torsion-free lattice Γ and r, s ≥ 0, every holomorphic map (Δ*)^r × Δ^s → X_Γ extends uniquely to a holomorphic map Δʳ⁺ˢ → (S*_Γ)^an (Kiernan1972 Theorem 1, line 111 to line 116, and Theorem 2, line 157 to line 160, whose proofs use only the properties established in §10.1; MilneSVI Lemma 3.15, line 1746 to line 1748, and Borel1972 Theorem A, uncertified, in the arithmetic case). *Needs:* §10.1; ComplexKahlerGeometry K3 (Riemann extension on manifolds); Mathlib one-variable removable singularities.

**Checks.**

- The inverse of the cusp coordinate of the image of Γ(3) near a cusp extends with value the cusp point.
- The map Δ* → PSL(2,ℤ)\ℍ ≅ ℂ given, through the j-identification, by z ↦ exp(1/z) has an essential singularity at 0 and does not extend to Δ → ℙ¹ = S*_Γ: the target has torsion.
- For r = 0 there is nothing to extend.

### 10.3 Borel algebraicity and minimality

Prove that for a torsion-free lattice Γ and a smooth variety V over ℂ, every holomorphic map V^an → S_Γ^an is the analytification of a unique morphism V → S_Γ; and that for every smooth projective compactification V̄ of S_Γ with strict-SNC boundary there is a unique morphism V̄ → S*_Γ extending the identity of S_Γ. The argument is deduced here from §10.2 and the algebraicity of maps out of proper schemes; MilneSVI Theorem 3.14, line 1742 to line 1744, its proof, line 1764 to line 1769, and the minimality property, line 1775 to line 1780, give the case of a torsion-free arithmetic group and its compactification argument. Deng2020 Theorem A, line 87 to line 90, is a different criterion, with different hypotheses: it makes a quasi-compact Kähler manifold carrying a complex polarized variation of Hodge structure whose period map on the universal cover has zero-dimensional fibres Borel hyperbolic; this roadmap constructs no such variation on S_Γ^an and does not use that route. For quasi-projective V take a strict-SNC compactification, extend by §10.2 in its local charts, glue by uniqueness and algebraize by ComplexComparisonPartII §C4.4; glue over a quasi-projective open cover in general. *Needs:* §10.2; AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §9.5; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C-1.1, C4.4.

**Checks.**

- For Γ′ ⊂ Γ both torsion-free of finite index, the covering S_Γ′^an → S_Γ^an is algebraic.
- exp: 𝔸¹(ℂ) → Y(1)(ℂ) = ℂ is holomorphic and not algebraic; PSL(2,ℤ) has torsion.
- The identity of S_Γ is its own algebraization, so the morphism is unique.

### 10.4 Uniqueness of the algebraic model and the ball quotient S_Γ

Prove: (a) for a torsion-free lattice Γ and quasi-projective models (S₁, φ₁), (S₂, φ₂) of X_Γ, there is a unique algebraic isomorphism f: S₁ → S₂ with φ₂ ∘ f^an = φ₁; (b) for an arbitrary lattice Γ, (S_Γ, φ_Γ) of §9.5 is, up to unique isomorphism, the only quasi-projective model (S, φ) of X_Γ for which some torsion-free normal Γ′ ◁ Γ of finite index and some quasi-projective model S′ of X_Γ′ admit a finite morphism S′ → S lifting the projection X_Γ′ → X_Γ, and then S ≅ S′/(Γ/Γ′); (c) the uniformising map π: B → S_Γ^an, the composite of the projection with φ_Γ⁻¹, is holomorphic, and for torsion-free Γ a covering map (MilneSVI Corollary 3.16, line 1770 to line 1774, in the arithmetic case; BaldiUllmo2023 Theorem 3.1.12, line 455 to line 465, line 102 to line 104, Definition 3.1.8, line 431). For (a), §10.3 covers only maps into S_Γ^an, so compare each model with (S_Γ, φ_Γ) of §9.5, which is smooth because Γ is torsion-free. Each S_i is smooth, its analytification being isomorphic to the complex manifold X_Γ (gap:normality-analytification), so §10.3 with V = S_i makes φ_Γ⁻¹ ∘ φ_i: S_i^an → S_Γ^an the analytification of a morphism g_i: S_i → S_Γ. Its analytification is an isomorphism, so g_i is an isomorphism by the criterion proved in ShimuraVarieties §3.3 for a morphism of smooth finite-type complex schemes: the tangent maps are invertible (ComplexComparisonPartII §§C-1.1–C-1.2), so g_i is étale, and an étale morphism bijective on complex points is an isomorphism. Then f = g₂⁻¹ ∘ g₁ satisfies φ₂ ∘ f^an = φ₁, and f is unique because two morphisms from the reduced variety S₁ to the separated S₂ that agree on complex points are equal. For (b), the model (S_Γ, φ_Γ) has the property: for a neat Γ₀ ◁ Γ of finite index (§6.4), S_Γ is S_Γ₀/(Γ/Γ₀), the part off the boundary of the quotient S*_Γ₀/(Γ/Γ₀) of §9.5, and the quotient map S_Γ₀ → S_Γ is finite (ShimuraVarieties §3.4a) and lifts X_Γ₀ → X_Γ (§§7.3, 9.5). Conversely, let (S, φ) have it through Γ′, a model (S′, φ′) of X_Γ′ and a finite ψ: S′ → S, and put F = Γ/Γ′. Γ′ is a lattice (§5.1), and by (a) for the torsion-free Γ′, (S′, φ′) is identified with (S_Γ′, φ_Γ′), and each element of F, acting on X_Γ′ (§7.3), is the analytification of a unique automorphism of S_Γ′. Choose Γ″ ⊂ Γ′ neat and normal in Γ of finite index (the intersection of Γ′ with a subgroup given by §6.4); by the independence statement of §9.5, S_Γ′ = S_Γ″/(Γ′/Γ″) and S_Γ = S_Γ″/(Γ/Γ″), so S_Γ = S_Γ′/F, categorical quotients by finite groups being formed in stages by their universal property (ShimuraVarieties §3.4a), and the action of F induced from Γ/Γ″ is the one above, both having the same analytification. The morphism ψ is F-invariant on complex points, because X_Γ′ → X_Γ is, hence F-invariant (S_Γ′ is reduced and S separated), and it factors through a morphism h: S_Γ → S, finite because ψ is, with h^an = φ⁻¹ ∘ φ_Γ. So h is bijective on complex points. S_Γ is normal (§9.5) and S is normal because S^an ≅ X_Γ is (§7.1; gap:normality-analytification); both have the connected analytification X_Γ, so both are irreducible. A finite surjective morphism of irreducible complex varieties has as many points in a general fibre as its degree, so h has degree 1 and is birational, and a finite birational morphism onto a normal variety is an isomorphism. It is the unique one compatible with φ_Γ and φ, as in (a), and S ≅ S_Γ′/F = S′/F. For (c), π is the holomorphic projection of §7.1, a covering map for torsion-free Γ by §7.2, followed by the isomorphism φ_Γ⁻¹. *Needs:* §§5.1, 6.4, 7.1–7.3, 9.5, 10.3; ShimuraVarieties §§3.3, 3.4a; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C-1.1–C-1.2, C4.4; gap:normality-analytification.

**Checks.**

- For torsion-free Γ, every biholomorphism of X_Γ lifts to an element of the normaliser of Γ in G (§2.1), and all of them are algebraic, as (a) predicts.
- PSL(2,ℤ): S_Γ = 𝔸¹ through j, and the analytic automorphisms of ℂ are affine, hence algebraic.
- The bijective morphism 𝔸¹ → {y² = x³}, t ↦ (t², t³), is not an isomorphism; smoothness of S_Γ for torsion-free Γ is what (a) uses.

### 10.5 Uniqueness of the finite-point normal compactification

Prove that for every lattice Γ and every compact Hausdorff normal complex analytic space Y containing X_Γ as an open dense subspace with Y ∖ X_Γ finite, the identity of X_Γ extends to a unique biholomorphism Y ≅ (S*_Γ)^an; if Y is the analytification of a normal projective variety, the extension is algebraic. This theorem is proved here from §§9.4–9.5. ### 10.6 Functoriality in the lattice

Prove that for lattices Γ′ ⊆ Γ of finite index the projection X_Γ′ → X_Γ is the analytification of a unique finite surjective morphism S_Γ′ → S_Γ, which extends to S*_Γ′ → S*_Γ and maps ∂_Γ′ onto ∂_Γ, of degree [Γ:Γ′]; that for g ∈ G the map induced by g is an isomorphism S_Γ ≅ S_(gΓg⁻¹); and that these morphisms compose. This theorem is proved here from §§9.5 and 10.4–10.5; BaldiUllmo2023 line 102 to line 104 uses only the reduction to a torsion-free subgroup of finite index that it makes possible. Choose Γ‴ ⊂ Γ′ normal in Γ, of finite index and neat: the normal core in Γ of a neat normal subgroup of finite index in Γ′ (§6.4), neat because subgroups of neat groups are neat. By the independence statement of §9.5, S*_Γ′ = S*_Γ‴/(Γ′/Γ‴) and S*_Γ = S*_Γ‴/(Γ/Γ‴), and the morphism is the map of quotients induced by Γ′/Γ‴ ⊂ Γ/Γ‴, finite and surjective. Its degree is [Γ : Γ′] because Γ/Γ‴ acts generically freely on X_Γ‴: an element acting trivially on X_Γ‴ agrees with an element of Γ‴ on an open subset of B (Baire), hence everywhere, and G acts faithfully on B (§1.4). It maps ∂_Γ′ onto ∂_Γ because a parabolic p ∈ Γ has a parabolic power in Γ′ with the same fixed point, so Γ and Γ′ have the same cusp points. For g ∈ G, x ↦ gx induces X_Γ ≅ X_(gΓg⁻¹), algebraic and extending to the minimal compactifications by §§10.4(b) and 10.5; composition holds by the uniqueness in those statements. *Needs:* §§1.4, 5.1, 6.1, 6.4, 9.5, 10.4–10.5.

## Layer 11: Arithmetic lattices and Shimura data

### 11.1 Arithmetic lattices in PU(1,n)

Read AdelicAlgebraicGroups §3.4's arithmetic lattices in a real Lie group on the connected group G, for which G⁰ = G and Γ ∩ G⁰ = Γ: a lattice Γ ⊂ G is arithmetic when there are a connected semisimple ℚ-group 𝐆 (the supplier's 𝐇), a continuous surjective homomorphism p: 𝐆(ℝ)⁰ → G with compact kernel and an arithmetic subgroup Γ_𝐆 ⊂ 𝐆(ℚ) (`Reduction.IsSArithmetic ∅ Γ_𝐆`) such that p(Γ_𝐆 ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰) is commensurable with Γ (WitteMorris2015 Definition 5.1.19, line 3732 to line 3739; BaldiUllmo2023 line 280 to line 284, which states the definition with p defined on the whole of 𝐆(ℝ) and with the integral points of a faithful representation, as in the witness form of (a) below). The independence of Γ_𝐆 within its commensurability class, the invariance under commensurability of Γ and under automorphisms of G, conjugations included, and the theorem that p(Γ_𝐆 ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰) is a lattice in G are the supplier's. Prove the three statements this roadmap adds.

(a) Witness form. A subgroup Γ ⊂ G, not assumed to be a lattice, is an arithmetic lattice if and only if there are 𝐆 and p as above and a faithful ℚ-representation 𝐆 ⊂ GL_N such that p(𝐆(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰) is commensurable with Γ, where 𝐆(ℤ) = 𝐆(ℚ) ∩ GL_N(ℤ). The group 𝐆(ℤ) is the group of integral points of the schematic closure of 𝐆 in GL_(N,ℤ) (AdelicAlgebraicGroups §1.1), hence arithmetic; every arithmetic Γ_𝐆 is commensurable with it by model independence; commensurable subgroups of 𝐆(ℝ)⁰ have commensurable images under p; and a subgroup of G commensurable with the lattice p(𝐆(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰) is a lattice (§5.1).

(b) Reduction to a ℚ-simple group. 𝐆 may be replaced by the unique ℚ-simple factor 𝐆₁ whose real points map onto G, and 𝐆(ℤ) by 𝐆₁(ℤ) = 𝐆₁(ℚ) ∩ GL_N(ℤ). The ℚ-simple factors 𝐆ᵢ are normal ℚ-subgroups whose identity components 𝐆ᵢ(ℝ)⁰ generate 𝐆(ℝ)⁰ (the multiplication map is an isogeny), so their images under p are commuting connected normal subgroups generating G; G has simple Lie algebra 𝔰𝔲(1,n) and is not abelian, so exactly one image is G and the others are trivial, which makes the other 𝐆ᵢ(ℝ) compact and their arithmetic subgroups finite. Then p restricts to a surjection 𝐆₁(ℝ)⁰ → G with compact kernel, p(𝐆₁(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆₁(ℝ)⁰) is a lattice (supplier) contained in the lattice p(𝐆(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰), hence of finite index in it (§5.1), and Γ is commensurable with it.

(c) Integral points across an isogeny. For an isogeny φ: 𝐆 → 𝐆′ of connected semisimple ℚ-groups (a surjective ℚ-homomorphism with finite kernel), in particular the central isogeny π: 𝐆 → 𝐆^ad onto the adjoint group, and faithful ℚ-representations 𝐆 ⊂ GL_N and 𝐆′ ⊂ GL_M, the group φ(𝐆(ℤ)) is commensurable with 𝐆′(ℤ) (WitteMorris2015 Proposition 9.4.5, line 8668 to line 8669, which proves it for every surjective ℚ-homomorphism). The representation of 𝐆 on ℚ^N ⊕ ℚ^M through the inclusion and through φ is faithful, so by model independence its integral points Γ₁ = 𝐆(ℤ) ∩ φ⁻¹(𝐆′(ℤ)) have finite index in 𝐆(ℤ), and φ(Γ₁) ⊂ 𝐆′(ℤ). The map φ_ℝ: 𝐆(ℝ)⁰ → 𝐆′(ℝ)⁰ is surjective, its differential being an isomorphism of Lie algebras, and has finite kernel, so φ(Γ₁ ∩ 𝐆(ℝ)⁰) is a lattice in 𝐆′(ℝ)⁰ by the supplier's image theorem (for the real Lie group 𝐆′(ℝ), with 𝐇 = 𝐆 and p = φ_ℝ); it lies in the discrete subgroup 𝐆′(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆′(ℝ)⁰, hence has finite index in it (§5.1), and it has finite index in φ(𝐆(ℤ)); as 𝐆′(ℤ) ∩ 𝐆′(ℝ)⁰ has finite index in 𝐆′(ℤ), the groups φ(𝐆(ℤ)) and 𝐆′(ℤ) share a subgroup of finite index in both.

*Needs:* §5.1 (lattices, and a discrete subgroup containing a lattice); AdelicAlgebraicGroups §1.1 (integral models of a faithful matrix representation), §3.4 (`Reduction.IsSArithmetic`, model independence, arithmetic lattices in a real Lie group and the image theorem); Mathlib `Subgroup.Commensurable`.

- Arithmeticity is invariant under commensurability and conjugation in G, and independent of the faithful representation (supplier; for the witness form, by (a)).
- 𝐆 may be replaced by its unique ℚ-simple factor with noncompact real points, by (b).

### 11.4 Comparison with the Baily–Borel compactification

Prove that for every compact open K_f and component group Γ_a^eff of §11.3, acting on X⁺ ≅ B, the identity of X_(Γ_a^eff) extends to a unique isomorphism from S*_(Γ_a^eff) onto the corresponding component of the Baily–Borel compactification of ShimuraVarieties §2.7, restricting to the algebraization of ShimuraVarieties §§1.2, 3.3 and 3.5 on S_(Γ_a^eff); and that for an arbitrary arithmetic Γ and a component group Γ_c commensurable with it, the finite morphisms S*_(Γ∩Γ_c) → S*_Γ and S*_(Γ∩Γ_c) → S*_(Γ_c) of §10.6 relate S*_Γ to that Baily–Borel compactification (MilneSVI Theorem 3.12, line 1686, and Remark 3.13, line 1722 to line 1727; BailyBorel1966 Theorem 10.11, uncertified; BaldiUllmo2023 line 457). Both compactifications are normal projective varieties, so their analytifications are compact Hausdorff (ComplexComparisonPartII §C-1.1 with ReductiveGroups §§3A.5b–3A.5c), and both add finitely many points to X_(Γ_a^eff), so §10.5 applies. For S*_(Γ_a^eff) this is §9.5. For the Baily–Borel compactification: the boundary components of the ball are its boundary points, since a holomorphic map f from the disc to the closed ball with |f(0)| = 1 is constant (the function ζ ↦ ⟨f(0), f(ζ)⟩ has modulus at most 1 and the value 1 at 0, so it is identically 1 by the maximum principle, and equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality forces f(ζ) = f(0)); so the rational boundary components of ShimuraVarieties §2.1 are points, and by ShimuraVarieties §2.2 they form finitely many Γ_a^eff-orbits, the boundary strata of the compact quotient (Mok2012 line 123 to line 128 recalls this for arithmetic ball quotients). *Needs:* §§9.5, 10.5–10.6, 11.3; ShimuraVarieties §§1.2, 2.1–2.2, 2.7, 3.3, 3.5; ComplexComparisonPartII §C-1.1 with ReductiveGroups §§3A.5b–3A.5c (compact Hausdorff analytification of a projective variety).

**Checks.**

- PSL(2,ℤ): both compactifications are X(1) = ℙ¹.
- For a Picard modular surface the Baily–Borel boundary and ∂ are both the set of cusp classes, of codimension 2.
- Boundary components are points for the ball but not for every bounded symmetric domain: the boundary of the bidisc Δ × Δ contains the holomorphic discs {1} × Δ, which are boundary components, while in the closed unit ball of ℂ² the only holomorphic disc through (1, 0) is constant, since ⟨(1, 0), f(ζ)⟩ ≡ 1 forces f(ζ) = (1, 0).
- For a non-arithmetic Γ (DeligneMostow1986 line 151 to line 152) there is no Shimura datum, while S*_Γ exists by §9.5.

### 11.5 The Borel embedding

Prove that the open embedding of B into the affine chart z₀ ≠ 0 of ℙⁿ (§1.3) is, under X⁺ ≅ B, the restriction to X⁺ of the Borel embedding of X into its compact dual for the datum of §11.3, G-equivariant for the action of G on ℙⁿ through PGLₙ₊₁(ℂ) (ShimuraData §3.8; BaldiUllmo2023 line 533 to line 540). *Needs:* §§1.3, 11.3; ShimuraData §3.8.

**Checks.**

- n = 1: the disc in the Riemann sphere; the full X = ℍ⁺ ⊔ ℍ⁻ goes onto the complement of the circle {⟨z,z⟩ = 0}, its second component onto {⟨z,z⟩ < 0}, the outside of the disc, which is why the comparison is with X⁺ only.
- The boundary sphere is the real hypersurface {⟨z,z⟩ = 0} of ℙⁿ.
- In the Siegel model the embedding is 𝔖 ⊂ ℂⁿ ⊂ ℙⁿ in the chart Z₀ ≠ 0, and 𝔖 is the set of lines on which the Hermitian form Im(Zₙ conj(Z₀)) − |Z′|², of signature (1,n), is positive.

## Layer 12: Totally geodesic subvarieties and the Ax–Schanuel setting

Γ is a lattice, S_Γ its ball quotient with φ_Γ, and π: B → S_Γ^an the uniformising map of §10.4.

### 12.1 Γ-special sub-balls and totally geodesic subvarieties

Define a Γ-special sub-ball as a sub-ball X_H = 𝔹(W) of dimension m ≥ 1 such that Γ_H = Γ ∩ H_W has image r_W(Γ_H) a lattice in Aut(X_H) ≅ PU(1,m); a Γ-special subvariety of S_Γ as the image π(X_H) of a Γ-special sub-ball, with its reduced structure; and a totally geodesic subvariety of S_Γ as an irreducible closed subvariety Z ⊂ S_Γ of dimension at least 1 such that every irreducible component of π⁻¹(Z^an) has totally geodesic regular locus (BaldiUllmo2023 line 49 to line 50, Definition 3.1.6, line 421 to line 423, Definition 3.1.13, line 468 to line 473). *Needs:* §§3.1, 10.4; ComplexKahlerGeometry K3 (irreducible components of π⁻¹(Z^an)); RiemannianGeometry R5a.

- For torsion-free Γ, Z is totally geodesic exactly when Z^reg is totally geodesic in the Kähler manifold (X_Γ, ω_Γ).
- Being Γ-special depends only on the Γ-orbit of X_H, and is invariant under passing to a subgroup of finite index.
- Γ_H is a lattice in H_W exactly when r_W(Γ_H) is a lattice in PU(1,m), the kernel of r_W being compact.

**Checks.**

- Z = S_Γ is totally geodesic and Γ-special, with H = G and Γ_H = Γ.
- Points are excluded by dim Z ≥ 1, although every point is the image of a sub-ball of dimension 0.
- A preimage component that is a smooth curve not contained in an affine complex line, such as {w₂ = w₁²} ∩ B, has regular locus that is not totally geodesic (§3.2); a subvariety with such a preimage component is not totally geodesic.

### 12.2 Γ-special subvarieties are algebraic and totally geodesic

Prove that for a Γ-special sub-ball X_H of dimension m: π(X_H) is closed in S_Γ^an and is Z^an for an irreducible closed subvariety Z ⊂ S_Γ of dimension m; the map r_W(Γ_H)\X_H → X_Γ is the analytification of a finite morphism j: S_(Γ_H) → S_Γ with image Z, birational onto Z, so that S_(Γ_H) is the normalization of Z; j extends to a finite morphism S*_(Γ_H) → S*_Γ sending ∂ into ∂; and Z is totally geodesic, the irreducible components of π⁻¹(Z^an) being the translates γX_H (BaldiUllmo2023 Corollary 3.4.6, line 686 to line 702, line 433 to line 434 and line 474). Reduce to neat Γ by §§6.4 and 10.6; algebraicity of j is §10.3 applied to S_(Γ_H) (quasi-projective by §9.5 applied in dimension m); the extension sends cusp points of Γ_H to cusp points of Γ because the monodromy is parabolic (§10.2), which makes j proper, hence finite; birationality holds because X_H ∩ γX_H, γ ∉ Γ_H, are proper sub-balls (§3.4), and they form a locally finite family since the translates γX_H are the irreducible components of the closed analytic set π⁻¹(Z^an). *Needs:* §§3.1, 3.4, 6.4, 9.5, 10.2–10.3, 10.6, 12.1; ComplexComparisonPartII §C4.1 (images of proper maps); AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry §5.4.

**Checks.**

- m = n: Z = S_Γ and j is the identity.
- For a neat subgroup of the Picard modular group over ℚ(i) and the sub-ball {w₂ = 0}, Γ_H contains a subgroup of finite index in the image of U(1,1; ℤ[i]) × U(1; ℤ[i]), an arithmetic lattice, and Z is a modular curve on the Picard surface.
- For a sub-ball whose stabiliser in Γ is trivial (m ≥ 1), r_W(Γ_H) is not a lattice and the sub-ball is not Γ-special.

### 12.3 Totally geodesic subvarieties are Γ-special

Prove that every totally geodesic subvariety Z ⊂ S_Γ of dimension m ≥ 1 is Γ-special: every irreducible component of π⁻¹(Z^an) is a sub-ball X_H = 𝔹(W) of dimension m, and r_W(Γ ∩ H_W) is a lattice in PU(1,m) (BaldiUllmo2023 line 135 to line 137, Theorem 1.3.2, line 151 to line 152, and Corollary 5.5.2, line 1346 to line 1352). Components are sub-balls by §3.3; r_W(Γ_H) is discrete because the kernel of r_W is compact; its quotient has finite volume because, after passing to a neat subgroup, the closure of Z ∩ X_Γ in X^M is a compact analytic subset not contained in D (§9.4), of finite ω-volume by §8.5, and r_W(Γ_H)\X_H maps onto Z^reg, generically injectively, with ω restricting to the form of X_H (§3.1); §5.3 in dimension m concludes. *Needs:* §§3.1, 3.3, 5.3, 6.4, 8.5, 9.4, 10.6 (the finite morphism S_Γ′ → S_Γ, for the reduction to a neat subgroup), 12.1.

**Checks.**

- n = 1: the only totally geodesic subvariety is S_Γ itself.
- For non-arithmetic Γ and n ≥ 2, BaldiUllmo2023 Theorem 1.2.1 (line 59) asserts that only finitely many such Z are maximal; this roadmap supplies the objects of that statement, not its proof.
- The image of a sub-ball whose stabiliser is not a lattice is not closed algebraic, so it is not a subvariety and there is no contradiction with the theorem.

### 12.4 Algebraic subvarieties of 𝔹ⁿ × S_Γ

Define an algebraic subvariety of B × S_Γ as an irreducible component of (B × S_Γ^an) ∩ Y^an, for a closed subvariety Y ⊂ ℙⁿ × S_Γ and the Borel embedding B ⊂ (ℙⁿ)^an (§11.5), and Π as the graph of π. Prove that algebraic subvarieties are closed irreducible analytic subsets of B × S_Γ^an; that Π is a closed irreducible analytic subset of pure dimension n, biholomorphic to B by the first projection; and that for every algebraic subvariety Q and every irreducible component U of Q ∩ Π, dim U ≥ dim Q − n, that is codim U ≤ codim Q + codim Π in B × S_Γ^an (BaldiUllmo2023 Theorem 1.2.2, line 91 to line 97, which compares these codimensions). For torsion-free Γ the ambient space is a manifold and the inequality is the intersection inequality, from Krull's height theorem in the Noetherian analytic local rings; for general Γ pull back along the finite map B × S_Γ′ → B × S_Γ of §10.6, under which Π_Γ is the union of the translates (1 × f)Π_Γ′, f ∈ Γ/Γ′. *Needs:* §§10.4, 10.6, 11.5; ComplexComparisonPartII §§C-1.1, C0.6; ComplexKahlerGeometry K3 (Noetherian analytic local rings of dimension n); Mathlib `Ideal.height_le_spanFinrank` (Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/KrullsHeightTheorem.lean:282).

**Checks.**

- n = 1, Q = B × {s}: the components of Q ∩ Π are the points (x, s) with π(x) = s, and dim U = 0 = dim Q − n.
- Q = B × S_Γ: U = Π, and dim U = n = 2n − n.
- Q = {x₀} × S_Γ: U = {(x₀, π(x₀))}, of dimension 0 = n − n.

### Examples

For the Picard modular surface over ℚ(i), the images of the sub-balls {w₂ = 0} and {w₁ = 0} are Γ-special curves; in the Ax–Schanuel inequality of BaldiUllmo2023 line 91 to line 95, the atypical case is codim U < codim Q + n.
