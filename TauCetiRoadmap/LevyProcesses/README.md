# Roadmap: Lévy processes

**Summary.** A Lévy process is a real process `X = (X_t)_{t ≥ 0}` that starts at `0`, has
independent and stationary increments and is continuous in probability. This roadmap builds the
theory of such processes on the Lévy–Khintchine theory of infinitely divisible laws: the law of
`X_1` has a triplet `(b, σ², ν)` (drift, Gaussian variance, Lévy measure, truncation
`1{|x| ≤ 1}`) with characteristic function `exp ψ`, and every statement below is phrased through
that triplet. Two notions are kept apart: a Lévy process in law (no condition on paths) and a
Lévy process (paths almost surely càdlàg). No triplet is part of a definition; theorems take a
triplet of the law of `X_1` as a hypothesis.

Headline theorems:

- For a Lévy process in law and every `t ≥ 0`, `E e^{iuX_t} = exp(t ψ(u))`. The law of `X_1`
  determines the law of the whole process, and every triplet is the triplet of the law of `X_1`
  for some Lévy process in law, built on the path space `ℝ≥0 → ℝ`.
- A Lévy process has almost surely continuous paths iff `ν = 0`; then, if moreover `σ² > 0`,
  `(X_t − bt)/σ` is a standard Brownian motion.
- A Lévy process has almost surely nondecreasing paths iff `σ² = 0`, `ν` is carried by
  `(0, ∞)`, `∫ min(1, x) dν < ∞` and `b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν ≥ 0`. For a Lévy process in law with
  `X_1 ≥ 0` almost surely, `E e^{−sX_t} = exp(−tΦ(s))` for all `t ≥ 0` and `s ≥ 0`, where
  `Φ(s) = (b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν) s + ∫ (1 − e^{−sx}) dν`.
- Let `X` be a Lévy process in law every path of which is right-continuous, adapted to a
  filtration whose `F_s` is independent of each increment after `s`, and `τ` an almost surely
  finite stopping time. Then `(X_{τ+t} − X_τ)_t` is a Lévy process in law with the law of `X`,
  independent of `F_τ`. For a Lévy process in law and every Schwartz function `f`,
  `t⁻¹(E f(x + X_t) − f(x))` converges as `t ↓ 0`, uniformly in `x`, to
  `b f'(x) + σ² f''(x)/2 + ∫ (f(x + y) − f(x) − y f'(x) 1{|y| ≤ 1}) ν(dy)`.

The roadmap rests on InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays, whose triplets, exponent and
theorems (existence and representation of triplets, distinguished logarithms, affine images,
half-line laws, second moments, convergence through triplets) it consumes; on Mathlib's
independent increments, Brownian motion, martingales and stopping times; and on Tau Ceti's
Kolmogorov extension over a countable index and its compound Poisson laws. What
StochasticCalculus and MarkovChainsAndRenewal state about Brownian motion and the Poisson process
is not restated.

Layer 1 defines the two notions and proves the correspondence with infinitely divisible laws.
Layer 2 treats small times and continuity of paths, Layer 3 subordinators, Layer 4 the
martingales of a Lévy process, and Layer 5 the strong Markov property and the generator.

## Conventions

- Time is `ℝ≥0`, values are real. A process is `X : ℝ≥0 → Ω → ℝ` on a measure space `(Ω, P)`;
  `μ_t` is the law of `X_t` (`P.map (X t)`). Path spaces are `ℝ≥0 → ℝ` with the product
  σ-algebra (`MeasurableSpace.pi`).
- A **Lévy process in law** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw X P`): every `X_t` is measurable, `X_0 = 0`
  almost surely, Mathlib's `HasIndepIncrements X P` holds, `X_{s+t} − X_s` has the law of `X_t`
  for all `s, t` (`IdentDistrib`), and `X_s → X_t` in probability as `s → t`
  (`TendstoInMeasure`). This is Sato's Definition 1.6 of a Lévy process in law. A **Lévy
  process** (`IsLevyProcess X P`) is moreover almost surely càdlàg (Mathlib's `IsCadlag`).
  `HasIndepIncrements` at zero increments forces `P` to be a probability measure (Mathlib's
  `iIndepFun.isProbabilityMeasure`), so no separate hypothesis is needed.
- Triplets are those of InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.1: `LevyTriplet` (drift
  `b`, Gaussian variance `σ² ≥ 0`, Lévy measure `ν` with `ν{0} = 0` and `∫ min(1, x²) dν < ∞`),
  the integrand `levyIntegrand`, the exponent
  `ψ(u) = LevyTriplet.exponent T u = ibu − σ²u²/2 + ∫ (e^{iux} − 1 − iux 1{|x| ≤ 1}) ν(dx)`, and
  `HasLevyTriplet μ T`, which says `charFun μ = exp ∘ ψ`. There is no minus sign: Sato and
  Bretagnolle write `e^{−tψ}` with the negative of this `ψ`, and Papapantoleon truncates with
  `1{|x| < 1}`. `Suggested.lean` uses verbatim local copies of these four definitions.
- For `t ≥ 0` and a triplet `T = (b, σ², ν)`, `t·T = (tb, tσ², tν)` is again a triplet (`tν` has no
  atom at `0` and `∫ min(1, x²) d(tν) = t ∫ min(1, x²) dν`), and its exponent is `tψ`, by
  linearity of the integral. It is built where a route needs it and is not a definition here.
- A filtration is Mathlib's `Filtration ℝ≥0`. Layers 4 and 5 use a filtration `F` to which `X` is
  adapted (`StronglyAdapted F X`) and such that, for `s ≤ t`, the increment `X_t − X_s` is
  independent of `F_s` (Bretagnolle's Definition 1.1 (c)). For the natural filtration of a Lévy
  process in law this holds: `F_s` is generated by the vectors `(X_{r_1}, …, X_{r_k})` with
  `r_i ≤ s`, functions of `X_0`, which is almost surely constant, and of the increments over a
  grid ending at `s`, which are independent of `X_t − X_s` by `HasIndepIncrements`.
- At a stopping time `τ : Ω → WithTop ℝ≥0`, the value `X_τ` uses Mathlib's `WithTop.untopA`, so
  on the event `τ = ∞` it is the value at a fixed arbitrary time; the statements here assume
  `τ < ∞` almost surely.
- "Proved here" means that the route below is the whole proof, written out; it claims no novelty.

## Layer 1: Lévy processes and their laws

### 1.1 Lévy processes

Define `IsLevyProcessInLaw X P` and `IsLevyProcess X P` as in the Conventions.

**Checks.**

- A Brownian motion in Mathlib's sense (`IsBrownianReal B P`) with measurable coordinates is a
  Lévy process (Mathlib's definition asks only for almost everywhere measurable ones): its
  increments are independent (Mathlib's `IsPreBrownianReal.hasIndepIncrements`),
  `B_{s+t} − B_s` is centred Gaussian with variance `t`, as is `B_t`, `E(B_s − B_t)² = |s − t|`
  gives continuity in probability, and its paths are almost surely continuous. Brownian motion
  with drift `b` and variance `σ²`, `X_t = bt + σB_t`, has triplet `(b, σ², 0)`.
- A rate-`λ` Poisson process (StochasticCalculus §0.6) is a Lévy process in law, and its
  regularized version there is a Lévy process; its triplet is `(λ, 0, λδ₁)`, the drift being
  `λ` because the jump `1` lies inside the truncation (InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays,
  the Poisson Check of Layer 3).
- The deterministic drift `X_t = bt` has triplet `(b, 0, 0)`; the zero process has `(0, 0, 0)`.
- A Lévy process in law need not have càdlàg paths. Let `N` be a rate-`1` Poisson process and
  `U` an independent uniform variable on `[0, 1]`, and set `Y_t = N_t` for `t ≠ U` and `Y_U = −1`.
  Each `Y_t` is measurable and `P(Y_t ≠ N_t) = P(U = t) = 0`, so `Y` has the finite-dimensional
  laws of `N` and is a Lévy process in law; but `Y_U = −1 < 0 = Y_0` almost surely, so its paths
  are not monotone, and they are not right-continuous at `U`. This separates the two notions
  and shows that 3.1 needs càdlàg paths.

### 1.2 The law at time t

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.charFun_map`). If `X` is a Lévy process in law and the law of
`X_1` has triplet `T` with exponent `ψ`, then `charFun μ_t u = exp(t ψ(u))` for every `t ≥ 0`
and `u ∈ ℝ`. Equivalently, `μ_t` has triplet `t·T`.

(Papapantoleon, Theorem 4.9; Bretagnolle–Ouwehand, §1, the consequences of Definition 1.1;
Sato, Theorem 7.10.) Route, written for `φ_t(u) = charFun μ_t u`:

1. `φ_{s+t} = φ_s φ_t`: `X_{s+t} = X_s + (X_{s+t} − X_s)`, the two summands are independent
   (Mathlib's `HasIndepIncrements.indepFun_eval_sub` with `X_0 = 0` almost surely), and the
   second has law `μ_t`; Mathlib's `IndepFun.charFun_map_add_eq_mul`.
2. `ψ` is continuous with `ψ(0) = 0`: `|levyIntegrand u x| ≤ max(u²/2, 2) min(1, x²)` (from
   `|e^{iv} − 1 − iv| ≤ v²/2` and `|e^{iv} − 1| ≤ 2`), and dominated convergence (Mathlib's
   `continuous_of_dominated`) with the bound `max(R²/2, 2) min(1, x²)` for `|u| ≤ R`.
3. For `n ≥ 1`, `φ_{1/n}ⁿ = φ_1 = exp ∘ ψ` by step 1, so `φ_{1/n}` has no zeros; it is
   continuous (Mathlib's `continuous_charFun`) with value `1` at `0`. By
   InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §2.2 (`existsUnique_distLog`) it has a continuous
   logarithm `χ` vanishing at `0`. Both `nχ` and `ψ` are continuous logarithms of `φ_1` vanishing
   at `0`, so the uniqueness half of `existsUnique_distLog`, applied to `φ_1`, gives `nχ = ψ`.
   Hence `φ_{1/n} = exp(ψ/n)`, and by step 1, `φ_q = exp(qψ)` for every `q ∈ ℚ≥0`.
4. For fixed `u`, `t ↦ φ_t(u)` is continuous on `ℝ≥0`: along a sequence `s_n → t`, `X_{s_n} → X_t`
   in probability, hence in distribution (Mathlib's `TendstoInMeasure.tendstoInDistribution`),
   and so `φ_{s_n}(u) → φ_t(u)` (Mathlib's `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`).
5. `t ↦ exp(tψ(u))` is continuous as well, and the two agree on the dense set `ℚ≥0`.

*Needs:* InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §2.2; Mathlib's `continuous_charFun`,
`HasIndepIncrements.indepFun_eval_sub`, `IndepFun.charFun_map_add_eq_mul`,
`continuous_of_dominated`, `TendstoInMeasure.tendstoInDistribution`,
`ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process of rate `λ`, triplet `(λ, 0, λδ₁)`:
  `ψ(u) = iλu + λ(e^{iu} − 1 − iu) = λ(e^{iu} − 1)`, and
  `exp(tψ(u)) = Σ_k e^{−λt}(λt)^k/k! e^{iuk}`, the characteristic function of the Poisson law of
  mean `λt`.
- Brownian motion with drift, triplet `(b, σ², 0)`: `exp(t(ibu − σ²u²/2))` is the characteristic
  function of `N(bt, σ²t)`.
- Sign: with `exp(−tψ)` instead, Brownian motion (`σ² = 1`, `b = 0`) at `t = u = 1` would have
  a characteristic function of modulus `e^{1/2} > 1`, which no law has.
- At `t = 0` both sides are `1`, since `X_0 = 0` almost surely.

### 1.3 Uniqueness in law

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.map_path_eq`). Let `X` on `(Ω, P)` and `X'` on `(Ω', P')` be
Lévy processes in law, on any two spaces, with `X_1` and `X'_1` equal in law. Then the laws of
the paths `ω ↦ (t ↦ X_t ω)` and `ω' ↦ (t ↦ X'_t ω')` on `ℝ≥0 → ℝ` are equal.

(Sato, Theorem 7.10.) Route:

1. `μ_1` is infinitely divisible: the increments of `X` over `[(j − 1)/n, j/n]`, `j ≤ n`, are
   independent (`HasIndepIncrements`) with law `μ_{1/n}` (stationarity) and sum to `X_1`
   almost surely, so `μ_1` is the law of the sum of `n` independent draws from `μ_{1/n}` (Mathlib's
   `iIndepFun_iff_map_fun_eq_pi_map`). By InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.2
   (`IsInfinitelyDivisible.exists_hasLevyTriplet`) it has a triplet `T`, which is also a triplet
   of the law of `X'_1`. By 1.2, `μ_h` and `μ'_h` have the characteristic function `exp(hψ)` for
   every `h ≥ 0`, so `μ_h = μ'_h` (Mathlib's `Measure.ext_of_charFun`).
2. For finite times `0 = t_0 ≤ t_1 ≤ … ≤ t_k`, the vector `(X_{t_1}, …, X_{t_k})` is almost surely
   the image of the increments `(X_{t_j} − X_{t_{j−1}})_j` under the partial-sum map. The
   increments are independent with laws `μ_{t_j − t_{j−1}}`, so their joint law is the product
   law (`iIndepFun_iff_map_fun_eq_pi_map`), the same for `X'`. Hence all finite-dimensional laws
   agree.
3. The path maps are measurable (Mathlib's `Measurable.of_eval`), so each path law is the
   projective limit of its finite-dimensional laws (Mathlib's `isProjectiveLimit_map`), and
   projective limits of finite measures are unique (Mathlib's `IsProjectiveLimit.unique`).

*Needs:* 1.2; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.2; Mathlib's
`iIndepFun_iff_map_fun_eq_pi_map`, `Measure.ext_of_charFun`, `Measurable.of_eval`,
`isProjectiveLimit_map`, `IsProjectiveLimit.unique`.

### 1.4 Existence in law

**Theorem** (`exists_isLevyProcessInLaw`). For every triplet `T` there is a probability measure
`Q` on `ℝ≥0 → ℝ` with the product σ-algebra under which the coordinate process `(ω ↦ ω_t)_t` is a
Lévy process in law and the law of the coordinate at time `1` has triplet `T`.

The statement is about laws on the path space and asserts nothing about paths: the set of càdlàg
paths is not in the product σ-algebra, whose sets depend on countably many coordinates.

(Bretagnolle–Ouwehand, §1, the consequences of Definition 1.1, by Kolmogorov's theorem; Sato,
Theorem 7.10.) Route, written for `ψ` the exponent of `T`:

1. For `t ≥ 0` let `μ_t` be a law with triplet `t·T`: InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays
   §3.2 (`LevyTriplet.exists_hasLevyTriplet`) gives an infinitely divisible law, in particular a
   probability measure, with `charFun μ_t = exp(tψ)`. Then `μ_0 = δ_0` and
   `μ_s ∗ μ_t = μ_{s+t}` (Mathlib's `charFun_conv`, `charFun_dirac` and
   `Measure.ext_of_charFun`).
2. If `h_n → h` in `ℝ≥0`, then `μ_{h_n} → μ_h` weakly, since `exp(h_nψ(u)) → exp(hψ(u))` for every
   `u` (Mathlib's `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`). With a bounded continuous
   `g`, `1{|x| > ε} ≤ g ≤ 1{|x| > ε/2}`, this gives `μ_h{|x| > ε} → 0` as `h → 0` (Mathlib's
   `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_forall_integral_tendsto`).
3. For a finite `J ⊂ ℚ≥0` with elements `q_1 < … < q_k` and `q_0 = 0`, let `P_J` be the image of
   the product of the `μ_{q_j − q_{j−1}}` under the partial-sum map, a law on `J → ℝ`. Deleting
   `q_j` replaces two consecutive increments by their sum, whose law is
   `μ_{q_{j+1} − q_{j−1}}` by step 1 (deleting the last time drops the last coordinate), so the
   family is projective. Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Measure.exists_isProjectiveLimit_of_countable`
   (`ℚ≥0` is countable, `ℝ` standard Borel) gives a probability measure `Q₀` on `ℚ≥0 → ℝ` with
   these finite-dimensional laws.
4. For real `t`, choose inductively rationals `q_n(t) ↓ t` (`q_n(t) = t` when `t` is rational) with
   `Q₀(|ω_{q_{n+1}(t)} − ω_{q_n(t)}| > 2^{−n}) ≤ 2^{−n}`, possible by step 2 because this
   probability is `μ_{q_n(t) − q_{n+1}(t)}{|x| > 2^{−n}}`. By Borel–Cantelli (Mathlib's
   `measure_limsup_atTop_eq_zero`) the sequence `ω_{q_n(t)}` is Cauchy for `Q₀`-almost every `ω`.
   Let `Z_t(ω)` be its limit on the measurable set where it converges and `0` elsewhere; `Z_t` is
   measurable (Mathlib's `measurable_of_tendsto_metrizable`) and `Z_q = ω_q` for rational `q`.
5. For real times `0 = t_0 < t_1 < … < t_k`, eventually `q_n(t_0) < … < q_n(t_k)`. For
   `v ∈ ℝ^k`, the characteristic function of the increments of `Z`,
   `E exp(i Σ_j v_j (Z_{t_j} − Z_{t_{j−1}}))`, is by dominated convergence the limit of the same
   expression at the times `q_n(t_j)`, which is `Π_j exp((q_n(t_j) − q_n(t_{j−1})) ψ(v_j))` by
   step 3, and tends to `Π_j exp((t_j − t_{j−1}) ψ(v_j))`. So the increments of `Z` over
   `t_0 < … < t_k` are independent (Mathlib's `iIndepFun_iff_charFun_pi`) with laws
   `μ_{t_j − t_{j−1}}`.
6. Let `Q` be the image of `Q₀` under `ω ↦ (t ↦ Z_t ω)`, measurable by `Measurable.of_eval`.
   The coordinates are measurable; the coordinate at `0` has law `μ_0 = δ_0`; increments over
   monotone times are independent by step 5 (a zero increment is almost surely `0`, independent
   of everything); `ω_{s+t} − ω_s` has law `μ_t`; `Q(|ω_s − ω_t| > ε) = μ_{|s−t|}{|x| > ε} → 0` as
   `s → t` by step 2; and the coordinate at `1` has law `μ_1`, whose triplet is `T`.

*Needs:* InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.2; Tau Ceti's
`TauCeti.Measure.exists_isProjectiveLimit_of_countable`; Mathlib's `charFun_conv`,
`charFun_dirac`, `Measure.ext_of_charFun`, `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`,
`ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_forall_integral_tendsto`, `measure_limsup_atTop_eq_zero`,
`measurable_of_tendsto_metrizable`, `iIndepFun_iff_charFun_pi`, `Measurable.of_eval`.

### Examples

- With `T = (0, 1, 0)`, 1.4 builds the finite-dimensional laws of Brownian motion on the path
  space, and 1.3 identifies the law of every Brownian motion with measurable coordinates; with
  `T = (λ, 0, λδ₁)` it gives the laws of the Poisson process.

### Dependencies

InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §2.2 and §3.2; 1.2 before 1.3; Tau Ceti's countable
Kolmogorov extension (1.4); Mathlib's independent increments, characteristic functions and

## Layer 2: small times and continuous paths

### 2.1 Small-time behaviour

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.tendsto_integral_div`). Let `X` be a Lévy process in law and `T`
a triplet of the law of `X_1`, with Lévy measure `ν`. For every bounded continuous `f` vanishing
on a neighbourhood `(−ε, ε)` of `0`, `t⁻¹ E f(X_t) → ∫ f dν` as `t ↓ 0`.

(Sato, Corollary 8.9.) Route, along an arbitrary sequence `t_n ↓ 0`, `t_n > 0`:

1. `∫ f dν` is finite: `f` is bounded, vanishes on `(−ε, ε)`, and
   `ν{|x| ≥ ε} ≤ max(1, ε⁻²) ∫ min(1, x²) dν < ∞`.
2. Let `C_t` be Tau Ceti's compound Poisson law `compoundPoissonMeasure (1/t) μ_t`. By
   `charFun_compoundPoissonMeasure` and 1.2, `charFun C_t u = exp((exp(tψ(u)) − 1)/t)`, which
   tends to `exp ψ(u) = charFun μ_1 u`, because `(e^{tz} − 1)/t → z`. So `C_{t_n} → μ_1` weakly
   (Mathlib's `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`).
3. `C_t` has the triplet `S_t = (t⁻¹ ∫_{|x| ≤ 1} x dμ_t, 0, λ_t)` with `λ_t` the restriction of
   `t⁻¹μ_t` to `x ≠ 0`, a finite measure: `t⁻¹(φ_t(u) − 1) = ∫ (e^{iux} − 1) dλ_t`, and splitting
   off `iu ∫_{|x| ≤ 1} x dλ_t` leaves `∫ levyIntegrand u x dλ_t`.
4. If `ν{|x| = 1} = 0`, InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §4.1
   (`tendsto_iff_levyTriplet`, direction `⇒`, which assumes `ν{|x| = 1} = 0` of the limit)
   gives `TendstoAwayFromZero λ_{t_n} ν`. Since `f` vanishes near `0`,
   `∫ f dλ_t = t⁻¹ ∫ f dμ_t = t⁻¹ E f(X_t)`, which is the claim along `t_n`.
5. In general, only countably many `c > 0` have `ν{|x| = c} > 0` (each `{|x| ≥ δ}` has finite
   mass), so some `a > 0` has `ν{|x| = 1/a} = 0`. Apply step 4 to the images of `C_{t_n}` and
   `μ_1` under `x ↦ ax`: they still converge weakly (their characteristic functions are those
   of `C_{t_n}` and `μ_1` at `au`), and by §3.3 (`HasLevyTriplet.map_affine` with `d = 0`) their
   triplets have Lévy measures the images of `λ_{t_n}` and `ν`, the latter not charging
   `{|y| = 1}`. Test with `y ↦ f(y/a)`, bounded, continuous and vanishing on `(−aε, aε)`.
6. Convergence along every sequence `t_n ↓ 0` is convergence along the filter of right
   neighbourhoods of `0` (Mathlib's `Filter.tendsto_iff_seq_tendsto`).

*Needs:* 1.2; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.3 and §4.1; Tau Ceti's
`compoundPoissonMeasure`, `charFun_compoundPoissonMeasure`; Mathlib's
`ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`, `Filter.tendsto_iff_seq_tendsto`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process of rate `λ` and `f` with `f(0) = 0`, `f(k) = k/(1 + k)` for integers `k ≥ 1`:
  `t⁻¹ Σ_{k ≥ 1} e^{−λt}(λt)^k/k! f(k) → λ f(1) = λ/2 = ∫ f d(λδ₁)`.
- Negative control for the hypothesis that `f` vanishes near `0`: `f ≡ 1` gives `t⁻¹ → ∞`, while
  `∫ 1 d(λδ₁) = λ`.
- Brownian motion (`ν = 0`): `t⁻¹ P(|X_t| > 1) = t⁻¹ erfc(1/√(2t)) → 0`.

### 2.2 Continuous paths

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcess.ae_continuous_iff`). Let `X` be a Lévy process and `T = (b, σ², ν)`
a triplet of the law of `X_1`. Then `X` has almost surely continuous paths iff `ν = 0`. If
`ν = 0` and `σ² > 0`, the process `((X_t − bt)/σ)_t` is a Brownian motion (Mathlib's
`IsBrownianReal`: the finite-dimensional laws of standard Brownian motion and almost surely
continuous paths).

(Papapantoleon, §2, the remark after Definition 2.1, stated without proof; Sato,
Theorem 21.1.) Proved here, with `q_n(ε) = P(|X_{1/n}| > ε)`:

1. (`⇒`) Fix `ε > 0` and a bounded continuous `g` with `1{|x| > 2ε} ≤ g ≤ 1{|x| > ε}`. By 2.1
   along `t = 1/n`, `liminf n q_n(ε) ≥ lim n E g(X_{1/n}) = ∫ g dν ≥ ν{|x| > 2ε}`. The increments
   `X_{k/n} − X_{(k−1)/n}`, `k ≤ n`, are independent with law `μ_{1/n}`, so
   `P(max_k |X_{k/n} − X_{(k−1)/n}| ≤ ε) = (1 − q_n(ε))ⁿ ≤ exp(−n q_n(ε))`. On the event of
   continuous paths the maximum tends to `0` (uniform continuity on `[0, 1]`), so the left side
   tends to `1`, and `1 ≤ exp(−ν{|x| > 2ε})`. Hence `ν{|x| > 2ε} = 0` for every `ε > 0`, and with
   `ν{0} = 0`, `ν = 0`.
2. (`⇐`) Let `ν = 0` and `ε > 0`. With a bounded continuous `g`, `1{|x| > ε} ≤ g ≤ 1{|x| > ε/2}`,
   2.1 gives `limsup n q_n(ε) ≤ ∫ g dν = 0`. For `N ∈ ℕ` let
   `A_n = {max_{k ≤ nN} |X_{k/n} − X_{(k−1)/n}| > ε}`; then `P(A_n) ≤ nN q_n(ε) → 0`, and by Fatou
   `P(liminf A_n) = 0`.
3. Let `ω` have a càdlàg path with a jump `|X_s − X_{s−}| > ε` at some `s ∈ (0, N]`. With
   `k_n = ⌈ns⌉`, `k_n/n ↓ s` and `(k_n − 1)/n ↑ s` strictly from below, so by right-continuity at
   `s` and the left limit at `s`, `X_{k_n/n} − X_{(k_n−1)/n} → X_s − X_{s−}`; hence `ω ∈ A_n` for
   all large `n`, that is, `ω ∈ liminf A_n`. Intersecting over rational `ε` and `N ∈ ℕ`, almost
   every path is càdlàg without jumps, hence continuous.
4. (Brownian motion) If `ν = 0`, then `ψ(u) = ibu − σ²u²/2`, so by 1.2 `X_t` has law `N(bt, σ²t)`
   (Mathlib's `charFun_gaussianReal`, `Measure.ext_of_charFun`), and `(X_t − bt)/σ` has law
   `N(0, t)` (`gaussianReal_map_add_const`, `gaussianReal_map_const_mul`). Its increments are
   images of those of `X` under the maps `x ↦ (x − b(t − s))/σ`, so it has independent increments
   (Mathlib's `iIndepFun.comp`); Mathlib's `HasIndepIncrements.isPreBrownianReal_of_hasLaw` makes
   it pre-Brownian, and its paths are almost surely continuous by steps 2 and 3.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1; Mathlib's `charFun_gaussianReal`, `Measure.ext_of_charFun`,
`gaussianReal_map_add_const`, `gaussianReal_map_const_mul`, `iIndepFun.comp`,
`HasIndepIncrements.isPreBrownianReal_of_hasLaw`.

**Checks.**

- Brownian motion with drift has `ν = 0` and continuous paths; the Poisson process has
  `ν = λδ₁ ≠ 0` and jumps.
- The deterministic drift `X_t = bt` has `ν = 0` and continuous paths but `σ² = 0`: the theorem
  says "continuous iff `ν = 0`", not "continuous iff Gaussian", and the Brownian conclusion needs
  `σ² > 0`.

### Examples

- Brownian motion is, up to drift and scale, the only Lévy process with continuous paths and
  `σ² > 0`. Lévy's characterisation of Brownian motion by its martingales is StochasticCalculus
  §5.5 and is not restated.

### Dependencies

Layer 1 (1.2); InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.3 and §4.1 (2.1); Tau Ceti's compound
Poisson laws (2.1); Mathlib's Brownian motion and Gaussian laws (2.2).

## Layer 3: subordinators

### 3.1 Nondecreasing paths

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcess.ae_monotone_iff`). Let `X` be a Lévy process and `T = (b, σ², ν)` a
triplet of the law of `X_1`. Then the paths of `X` are almost surely nondecreasing iff
`σ² = 0`, `ν(−∞, 0] = 0`, `∫ min(1, x) dν < ∞` and `b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν ≥ 0`. A Lévy process with
these properties is a **subordinator**.

(Sato, Theorem 21.5.) Route:

1. (`⇒`) Almost surely `X_1 ≥ X_0 = 0`, so `μ_1(−∞, 0) = 0`, and
   InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.6 (`HasLevyTriplet.measure_Iio_zero_eq_zero_iff`)
   gives the four conditions.
2. (`⇐`) The four conditions for `T` give the same four for `t·T`: the integrals scale by `t`
   (Mathlib's `integral_smul_measure`, `lintegral_smul_measure`). By 1.2, `μ_t` has triplet
   `t·T`, so §3.6 gives `μ_t(−∞, 0) = 0` for every `t`. For rational `s ≤ t`, the increment
   `X_t − X_s` has law `μ_{t−s}`, so it is almost surely `≥ 0`; intersecting over countably many
   pairs, almost every path is nondecreasing on `ℚ≥0`. For real `s < t` take rationals `q ↓ s`,
   `q' ↓ t` with `q ≤ q'`; right-continuity on the càdlàg event gives `X_s ≤ X_t`.

*Needs:* 1.2; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.6; Mathlib's
`integral_smul_measure`, `lintegral_smul_measure`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process, triplet `(λ, 0, λδ₁)`: `∫ min(1, x) dν = λ` and `b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν = 0`, so
  it is a subordinator.
- The compensated Poisson process `N_t − λt` has triplet `(0, 0, λδ₁)` and
  `b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν = −λ < 0`: it decreases between jumps. A plausible wrong criterion `b ≥ 0`
  would accept it.
- The drift `X_t = −t` has `b = −1 < 0` and is decreasing.
- The modified Poisson process `Y` of the Checks of 1.1 is a Lévy process in law whose triplet
  satisfies the four conditions but whose paths are not monotone: the direction `⇐` needs
  càdlàg paths.

### 3.2 The Laplace exponent

Define the **Laplace exponent** `laplaceExponent T s = (b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν) s + ∫ (1 − e^{−sx}) dν`.
It is meaningful for the triplets of laws carried by `[0, ∞)`; for other triplets its integrals
can be undefined, and the Bochner integral then takes the value `0`.

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.integral_exp_neg`). Let `X` be a Lévy process in law, `T` a
triplet of the law of `X_1`, and `X_1 ≥ 0` almost surely. Then for every `t ≥ 0` and every
`s ≥ 0`, `E e^{−sX_t} = exp(−t laplaceExponent T s)`.

(Sato, Theorem 24.11.) Proved here, with `b₀ = b − ∫_{(0,1]} x dν` and `t ≥ 0`, `s ≥ 0` fixed:

1. As in 3.1 step 1, `T` satisfies the four conditions of §3.6, and so does `t·T`; since `μ_t` has
   triplet `t·T` (1.2), §3.6 gives `μ_t(−∞, 0) = 0`. The integrand `1 − e^{−sx}` lies between `0`
   and `max(1, s) min(1, x)` on `(0, ∞)`, so `laplaceExponent T s` is a finite integral.
2. Because `ν` is carried by `(0, ∞)` and `∫_{(0,1]} x dν < ∞`, the exponent splits:
   `ψ(u) = i b₀ u + ∫ (e^{iux} − 1) dν`.
3. For `k ≥ 1` let `ν_k` be the restriction of `tν` to `(1/k, ∞)`, a finite measure, and
   `ρ_k = δ_{t b₀} ∗ compoundPoissonMeasure (ν_k(ℝ)) (ν_k/ν_k(ℝ))` (`ρ_k = δ_{t b₀}` when
   `ν_k = 0`). By Tau Ceti's `charFun_compoundPoissonMeasure` and Mathlib's `charFun_dirac`,
   `charFun_conv`, `charFun ρ_k u = exp(i t b₀ u + ∫ (e^{iux} − 1) dν_k)`, which tends to
   `exp(tψ(u)) = charFun μ_t u` by dominated convergence (`|e^{iux} − 1| ≤ max(2, |u|) min(1, x)`
   on `(0, ∞)`) and step 2. So `ρ_k → μ_t` weakly.
4. Every `ρ_k` and `μ_t` is carried by `[0, ∞)` (`t b₀ ≥ 0`, jumps positive), where
   `e^{−sx} = g(x)` for the bounded continuous `g(x) = e^{−s max(x, 0)}`. Weak convergence gives
   `∫ g dρ_k → ∫ g dμ_t = E e^{−sX_t}` (Mathlib's
   `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_forall_integral_tendsto`).
5. `∫ g dρ_k = exp(−t b₀ s − ∫ (1 − e^{−sx}) dν_k)`: by Tau Ceti's
   `lintegral_compoundPoissonMeasure`, `∫ e^{−sx} d(compoundPoissonMeasure r ρ)` is the Poisson
   average of `∫ e^{−sx} d(convolutionPower ρ n) = (∫ e^{−sx} dρ)ⁿ` (Tonelli for convolutions,
   Mathlib's `Measure.lintegral_conv`), which is `exp(−r(1 − ∫ e^{−sx} dρ))`. As `k → ∞`,
   monotone convergence gives `∫ (1 − e^{−sx}) dν_k → t ∫ (1 − e^{−sx}) dν`, so
   `∫ g dρ_k → exp(−t laplaceExponent T s)`.

*Needs:* 1.2; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.6; Tau Ceti's
`compoundPoissonMeasure`, `charFun_compoundPoissonMeasure`, `lintegral_compoundPoissonMeasure`,
`MeasureTheory.Measure.convolutionPower`; Mathlib's `charFun_dirac`, `charFun_conv`,
`Measure.lintegral_conv`, `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun`,
`ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_forall_integral_tendsto`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process, triplet `(λ, 0, λδ₁)`: `laplaceExponent T s = λ(1 − e^{−s})`, and
  `Σ_k e^{−λt}(λt)^k/k! e^{−sk} = exp(−λt(1 − e^{−s}))`.
- Gamma process with `X_1` exponential of mean `1`: the triplet is `(1 − e^{−1}, 0, x⁻¹e^{−x} dx`
  on `(0, ∞))` (a Check of InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays Layer 3), so `b₀ = 0` and
  `laplaceExponent T s = ∫_0^∞ (1 − e^{−sx}) x⁻¹e^{−x} dx = log(1 + s)` (Frullani); and `X_t` has
  the gamma law of shape `t`, with `E e^{−sX_t} = (1 + s)^{−t} = exp(−t log(1 + s))`.
- Stable subordinator of index `1/2`, `ν(dx) = x^{−3/2} dx` on `(0, ∞)`, `b₀ = 0`:
  `∫_0^∞ (1 − e^{−sx}) x^{−3/2} dx = 2√π √s`.
- Negative control for `X_1 ≥ 0`: Brownian motion has triplet `(0, 1, 0)`, so
  `laplaceExponent T s = 0`, but `E e^{−sB_t} = e^{s²t/2} ≠ 1` for `s, t > 0`.
- Negative control for `s ≥ 0`: for the exponential law and `s = −2`, `E e^{2X_1} = ∞`, so the
  Bochner integral is `0`, while the right side is a positive number.

### Examples

- For a triplet with the four properties of 3.1, `laplaceExponent T s` equals Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.bernsteinLevyKhintchineExponent 0 b₀ ν' s` for `s ≥ 0`, with `ν'` the image of `ν` on
  `ℝ≥0`, because `ν` is carried by `(0, ∞)`; Tau Ceti proves that the latter is a Bernstein
  function (`TauCeti.isBernsteinFunction_bernsteinLevyKhintchineExponent_of_integrable_min_one`,
  with `b₀ ≥ 0` and `min(1, x)` integrable against `ν'`).

### Dependencies

Layer 1 (1.2); InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.6; Tau Ceti's compound Poisson laws
and convolution powers (3.2).

## Layer 4: martingales

### 4.1 Martingales of a Lévy process

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.martingale_of_indep`). Let `X` be a Lévy process in law, `T` a
triplet of the law of `X_1` with exponent `ψ`, and `F` a filtration to which `X` is adapted and
such that `X_t − X_s` is independent of `F_s` for `s ≤ t`. Then:

1. for every `u ∈ ℝ`, `exp(iuX_t − tψ(u))` is an `F`-martingale;
2. if `X_1` is integrable, `X_t − t E X_1` is an `F`-martingale;
3. if `X_1` is square integrable, `(X_t − t E X_1)² − t Var X_1` is an `F`-martingale.

(Bretagnolle–Ouwehand, the martingales `M^u` of the proof of Theorem 1.2, for 1.) Proved here.
In each case `M_t − M_s` is a function of `F_s`-measurable quantities and of the increment
`D = X_t − X_s`, independent of `F_s` and with law `μ_{t−s}` (stationarity with
`s + (t − s) = t`). Mathlib's `condExp_indep_eq` computes `E[h(D) | F_s] = E h(D)`, and
`condExp_bilin_of_stronglyMeasurable_left` (with multiplication) pulls out the
`F_s`-measurable factor.

1. `M_t = M_s exp(iuD − (t − s)ψ(u))`, and `E exp(iuD) = exp((t − s)ψ(u))` by 1.2. `|M_t|` is the
   constant `|exp(−tψ(u))|`, so `M_t` is integrable.
2. Integrability: if `Y` and `Z` are independent and `Y + Z` is integrable, then `Y` is
   integrable, since by Tonelli `∫ E|Y + z| μ_Z(dz) < ∞`, so `E|Y + z| < ∞` for some `z`, and
   `|Y| ≤ |Y + z| + |z|`. With `X_1 = X_t + (X_1 − X_t)` for `t ≤ 1`, and `X_t` a sum of `⌈t⌉`
   independent increments with law `μ_{t/⌈t⌉}` for `t > 1`, every `X_t` is integrable. Mean:
   for a law `ρ` with a first moment, `(charFun ρ u − 1)/u → i ∫ x dρ` as `u → 0` (dominated
   convergence with `|e^{iux} − 1|/|u| ≤ |x|`). Applied to `μ_1`, `(exp ψ(u) − 1)/u → iE X_1`.
   Write `e^z − 1 = z·h(z)` with `h(z) = (e^z − 1)/z` for `z ≠ 0` and `h(0) = 1`; `h` is
   continuous. Since `ψ(u) → 0`, `h(ψ(u)) → 1` and is nonzero near `u = 0`, so
   `ψ(u)/u = ((exp ψ(u) − 1)/u) / h(ψ(u)) → iE X_1`. Then
   `(exp(tψ(u)) − 1)/u = h(tψ(u)) · t·ψ(u)/u → t · iE X_1`, while the same lemma applied to
   `μ_t` gives the limit `iE X_t`. Hence `E X_t = t E X_1`. Then
   `E[X_t − X_s | F_s] = E D = (t − s)E X_1`.
3. By 1.2, `μ_t` has triplet `t·T`; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.4
   (`HasLevyTriplet.memLp_two_iff`), applied to `T` (direction `⇒`) and to `t·T` (direction `⇐`,
   the tail integral scales by `t`), gives `X_t ∈ L²` and `Var X_t = tσ² + ∫ x² d(tν) = t Var X_1`
   (Mathlib's `memLp_map_measure_iff`, `variance_map`). With `m = E X_1`,
   `(X_t − tm)² = (X_s − sm)² + 2(X_s − sm)(D − (t − s)m) + (D − (t − s)m)²`, whose conditional
   expectation given `F_s` is `(X_s − sm)² + (t − s) Var X_1`.

*Needs:* 1.2; InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.4; Mathlib's `condExp_indep_eq`,
`condExp_bilin_of_stronglyMeasurable_left`, `memLp_map_measure_iff`, `variance_map`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process of rate `λ`: `E(N_t − λt) = 0` and `E(N_t − λt)² = λt`, so `N_t − λt` and
  `(N_t − λt)² − λt` have constant mean `0`, as the martingales of 2 and 3 must. For Brownian
  motion, 3 is the martingale `B_t² − t`.
- Negative control for the independence hypothesis: for a rate-`1` Poisson process and a
  filtration with `{N_1 = 0} ∈ F_0`, the event `{N_1 = 0}` has probability `e^{−1}` and its
  intersection with itself has probability `e^{−1} ≠ e^{−2}`, so `N_1 − N_0` is not independent
  of `F_0`, although `N` is adapted; then `E[N_1 − 1 | F_0] = −1` on `{N_1 = 0}`, not `0`.

### Examples

- The Poisson martingales of StochasticCalculus §2.1 (the compensated Poisson process) are the
  case `T = (λ, 0, λδ₁)` of 2 and 3.

### Dependencies

Layer 1 (1.2); InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays §3.4; Mathlib's conditional
expectations and martingales.

## Layer 5: the strong Markov property and the generator

### 5.1 The strong Markov property

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.strongMarkov`). Let `X` be a Lévy process in law every path of
which is right-continuous, `F` a filtration to which `X` is adapted with `X_t − X_s` independent of
`F_s` for `s ≤ t`, and `τ` a stopping time of `F` with `τ < ∞` almost surely. Then
`Y_t = X_{τ+t} − X_τ` is a Lévy process in law, the law of its paths is that of `X`, and its path
map is independent of the σ-algebra `F_τ` (Mathlib's `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace`).

(Bretagnolle–Ouwehand, Theorem 1.4; Sato, Theorem 40.10.) Proved here:

1. Fixed times. For `s ≥ 0`, the σ-algebra `G_s` of the increments `X_{s+r} − X_s`, `r ≥ 0`, is
   independent of `F_s`. For `s ≤ r_1 ≤ … ≤ r_k`, `A ∈ F_s` and Borel `B_j`, induction on `k`
   gives `P(A ∩ ⋂_j {D_j ∈ B_j}) = P(A) Π_j P(D_j ∈ B_j)` for the successive increments `D_j`:
   the last increment is independent of `F_{r_{k−1}}`, which contains `A` and the earlier
   increments by adaptedness. For a fixed grid, the boxes `⋂_j {D_j ∈ B_j}` form a π-system
   generating `σ(D_1, …, D_k)`, which is also `σ(X_{r_j} − X_s, j ≤ k)`; with `F_s` as the other
   π-system, Mathlib's `IndepSets.indep` gives that `σ(D_1, …, D_k)` is independent of `F_s`.
   These σ-algebras, indexed by the finite sets of times `≥ s`, are directed under inclusion,
   because a finer grid generates a larger σ-algebra, and their supremum is `G_s`; so Mathlib's
   `ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_directed_le` gives that `G_s` is independent of `F_s`. The
   restarted process `(X_{s+r} − X_s)_r` is a Lévy process in law with
   `X_{s+1} − X_s` distributed as `X_1`, so by 1.3 its path law is that of `X`.
2. Discretisation. `τ_n = ⌈2ⁿτ⌉/2ⁿ` (`∞` where `τ = ∞`) takes values in `2^{−n}ℕ ∪ {∞}`, with
   `τ ≤ τ_n ≤ τ + 2^{−n}`. For `A ∈ F_τ`, `A ∩ {τ_n = 0} = A ∩ {τ ≤ 0} ∈ F_0`, and for `k ≥ 1`,
   `A ∩ {τ_n = k2^{−n}} = (A ∩ {τ ≤ k2^{−n}}) ∖ (A ∩ {τ ≤ (k − 1)2^{−n}}) ∈ F_{k2^{−n}}`.
3. Let `Y^n_t = X_{τ_n+t} − X_{τ_n}`. Splitting over the values of `τ_n` and using step 1 at each
   `s = k2^{−n}`: for `A ∈ F_τ` and every measurable set `C` of paths,
   `P(A ∩ {Y^n ∈ C}) = P(A) P(X ∈ C)` (the event `τ = ∞` is null).
4. Every path is right-continuous, so `Y^n_t(ω) → Y_t(ω)` for every `ω` (on `τ = ∞` both use the
   same fixed time). Each `Y_t` is therefore measurable (Mathlib's
   `measurable_of_tendsto_metrizable`), and so is the path map (`Measurable.of_eval`).
5. For times `t_1, …, t_k` and bounded continuous `h` on `ℝ^k`, step 3 gives
   `E[1_A h(Y^n_{t_1}, …, Y^n_{t_k})] = P(A) E h(X_{t_1}, …, X_{t_k})`, and dominated convergence
   gives the same for `Y`. Bounded continuous functions determine finite measures on `ℝ^k`
   (Mathlib's `ext_of_forall_integral_eq_of_IsFiniteMeasure`), so
   `P(A ∩ {Y ∈ C}) = P(A) P(X ∈ C)` for every finite-dimensional cylinder `C`. With `A = Ω` the
   finite-dimensional laws of `Y` and `X` agree, so their path laws agree (as in 1.3, step 3);
   and since cylinders form a π-system generating the product σ-algebra and `F_τ ≤ mΩ`
   (Mathlib's `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace_le`), `IndepSets.indep` gives independence.
6. `Y` is a Lévy process in law: its coordinates are measurable (step 4), `Y_0 = 0` everywhere,
   and the three other conditions depend only on the path law, which is that of `X`.

*Needs:* 1.3; Mathlib's `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace`, `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace_le`,
`IndepSets.indep`, `ProbabilityTheory.indep_iSup_of_directed_le`,
`measurable_of_tendsto_metrizable`, `Measurable.of_eval`,
`ext_of_forall_integral_eq_of_IsFiniteMeasure`, `IsProjectiveLimit.unique`.

**Checks.**

- The fixed time `τ ≡ s` gives the Markov property at time `s` (step 1).
- Negative control for `τ < ∞` almost surely: let `N` be a rate-`1` Poisson process every path
  of which is right-continuous (StochasticCalculus §0.6), `Z` an independent fair coin, `F_t`
  generated by `Z` and `N_r`, `r ≤ t` (the independence hypothesis holds), and `τ = 0` on
  `{Z = 0}`, `τ = ∞` on `{Z = 1}`, a stopping time. Let `c` be the fixed time used on
  `{τ = ∞}`. The event `A = {Z = 1, N_{c+1} − N_c ≥ 1}` lies in `F_τ`, since it lies in the
  σ-algebra generated by all the `F_t` and does not meet `{τ ≤ t}`; and `Y_1 = N_{c+1} − N_c` on
  `A`. With `p = 1 − e^{−1}`,
  `P(A ∩ {Y_1 ≥ 1}) = p/2` but `P(A) P(Y_1 ≥ 1) = p²/2`.

### 5.2 The generator

Define the **Lévy generator**
`levyGenerator T f x = b f'(x) + σ² f''(x)/2 + ∫ (f(x + y) − f(x) − y f'(x) 1{|y| ≤ 1}) ν(dy)`,
with `f'` and `f''` Mathlib's `deriv f` and `iteratedDeriv 2 f`.

**Theorem** (`IsLevyProcessInLaw.tendstoUniformly_generator`). Let `X` be a Lévy process in law
and `T` a triplet of the law of `X_1`. For every Schwartz function `f : ℝ → ℝ`,
`t⁻¹(E f(x + X_t) − f(x)) → levyGenerator T f x` as `t ↓ 0`, uniformly in `x ∈ ℝ`.

(Sato, Theorem 31.5, which states it for twice continuously differentiable functions vanishing
at infinity with their derivatives.) Proved here, with Mathlib's Fourier transform
`𝓕g(ξ) = ∫ e^{−2πixξ} g(x) dx`:

1. Let `g = (f : ℝ → ℂ)`, a Schwartz function (Mathlib's `SchwartzMap.postcompCLM` with the real
   embedding), and `ĝ = 𝓕g`, again a Schwartz function, so `ĝ` and `(1 + ξ²)ĝ(ξ)` are integrable.
   Fourier inversion (Mathlib's `Continuous.fourierInv_fourier_eq`) gives
   `g(x) = ∫ e^{2πixξ} ĝ(ξ) dξ`, and the same for `g'` and `g''` with `ĝ` multiplied by `2πiξ`
   and `(2πiξ)²` (Mathlib's `Real.fourier_deriv`).
2. By Fubini and 1.2, `E f(x + X_t) = ∫ ĝ(ξ) e^{2πixξ} exp(tψ(2πξ)) dξ`, so
   `t⁻¹(E f(x + X_t) − f(x)) = ∫ ĝ(ξ) e^{2πixξ} (exp(tψ(2πξ)) − 1)/t dξ`.
3. `Re ψ ≤ 0`, so `|(exp(tψ(v)) − 1)/t| ≤ |ψ(v)|` (from `|e^z − 1| ≤ |z|` for `Re z ≤ 0`), and
   `|ψ(v)| ≤ |b||v| + σ²v²/2 + max(v²/2, 2) ∫ min(1, x²) dν ≤ C(1 + v²)` (1.2, step 2).
4. The difference between the expression of step 2 and `∫ ĝ(ξ) e^{2πixξ} ψ(2πξ) dξ` is at most
   `∫ |ĝ(ξ)| |(exp(tψ(2πξ)) − 1)/t − ψ(2πξ)| dξ`, which does not depend on `x` and tends to `0`
   by dominated convergence, with the bound `2C(1 + 4π²ξ²)|ĝ(ξ)|`, along every sequence `t_n ↓ 0`;
   this is uniform convergence along the right neighbourhoods of `0`.
5. Identification. In `ψ(2πξ) = 2πiξ b − σ²(2πξ)²/2 + ∫ levyIntegrand (2πξ) y ν(dy)`, the first
   two terms give `b f'(x) + σ² f''(x)/2` by step 1. For the third, `ν` is σ-finite (finite on
   each `{|y| ≥ 1/k}`, and `ν{0} = 0`), and the integrand is bounded by
   `max(2π²ξ², 2) min(1, y²) |ĝ(ξ)|`, so Fubini and step 1 at `x + y` and `x` give
   `∫ (f(x + y) − f(x) − y f'(x) 1{|y| ≤ 1}) ν(dy)`.

*Needs:* 1.2; Mathlib's `SchwartzMap.postcompCLM`, the Fourier transform of Schwartz maps,
`Continuous.fourierInv_fourier_eq`, `Real.fourier_deriv`.

**Checks.**

- Poisson process of rate `1`, triplet `(1, 0, δ₁)`, `f(x) = e^{−x²}`, `x = 1/2`:
  `levyGenerator T f x = f'(1/2) + f(3/2) − f(1/2) − f'(1/2) = e^{−9/4} − e^{−1/4}`, which is the
  derivative at `t = 0` of `Σ_k e^{−t}t^k/k! f(1/2 + k)`. With the drift `0` instead of `1` the two
  differ by `f'(1/2) = −e^{−1/4}`: the drift `b` belongs with the truncation `1{|y| ≤ 1}`.
- Brownian motion, triplet `(0, 1, 0)`, `f(x) = e^{−x²}`:
  `E f(x + B_t) = (1 + 2t)^{−1/2} e^{−x²/(1+2t)}`, whose derivative at `t = 0` is
  `(2x² − 1)e^{−x²} = f''(x)/2`.

### Examples

- For Brownian motion the generator is `f''/2`; for the Poisson process of rate `λ` it is
  `λ(f(x + 1) − f(x))`; for the deterministic drift `X_t = bt` it is `b f'`.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns Lévy processes as processes: the two definitions of 1.1, the correspondence
with infinitely divisible laws (1.2–1.4), small-time behaviour and continuity of paths (Layer 2),
subordinators (Layer 3), their martingales (Layer 4), the strong Markov property and the
generator (Layer 5), and the definitions `laplaceExponent` and `levyGenerator`.

It is built on InfinitelyDivisibleLawsAndTriangularArrays, by another contributor, and restates
none of its results. Each law-level fact is used there in the form stated: the triplet, its
integrand, its exponent and `HasLevyTriplet` (§3.1, copied verbatim); the distinguished
logarithm (§2.2) in 1.2; existence and representation of triplets (§3.2) in 1.3 and 1.4; affine
images (§3.3) and convergence through triplets (§4.1) in 2.1; second moments (§3.4) in 4.1;
the half-line criterion (§3.6) in 3.1 and 3.2. The moment criteria for `X_t` (finiteness of
`E|X_t|^p` and `E e^{θX_t}`) are the criterion for submultiplicative functions that its §3.4
states in prose (`∫ g dμ < ∞` iff `∫_{|x| > 1} g dν < ∞`), applied with `g = max(1, |x|^p)` and
`g = e^{θx}` and, by 1.2, to the triplet `t·T`, for `t > 0` (at `t = 0`, `X_0 = 0` has every
moment); they are not restated here.

The other boundaries:

- Brownian motion: Mathlib's `IsBrownianReal`; its filtered form (`IsFilteredBrownian`, §1.3),
  its strong Markov property (§6.1) and Lévy's characterisation (§5.5) are StochasticCalculus's.
  StochasticCalculus §6.1 treats Brownian motion with almost surely continuous paths at optional
  times, with the σ-algebra `F_{τ+}`; 5.1 treats Lévy processes in law every path of which is
  right-continuous, at stopping times of `F`, with `F_τ`, and does not restate §6.1.
- The Poisson process: its construction, regularisation, restart at fixed times and jump times
  are StochasticCalculus §0.6, and its characterisations by exponential waits, thinning and
  superposition are MarkovChainsAndRenewal Layer 7; here it is the case `T = (λ, 0, λδ₁)`.
- Stable and gamma laws are StandardDistributions (Layer 15 for stable laws) and Tau Ceti's;
  stable subordinators and the gamma process appear only in Checks.
- Not treated here: the càdlàg modification of a Lévy process in law (its proof needs a
  regularity theory of martingales along a dense set of times), the Lévy–Itô decomposition, the
  criteria for finite variation and for finite or infinite activity, the characterisation of
  compound Poisson processes by piecewise constant paths, Poisson random measures, Wald identities
  at stopping times, and Feller semigroups beyond the generator limit of 5.2.
