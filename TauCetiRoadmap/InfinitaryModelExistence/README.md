# Roadmap: infinitary model existence, omitting types and interpolation

The model theory of Lω₁ω beyond back-and-forth is built from one engine: the **model existence
theorem** for consistency properties. Over a countable language, a family of sets of sentences that
is closed under the decomposition of each connective and quantifier, and that never contains an
outright contradiction, gives each of its countable members a countable model. Every classical
construction in this roadmap is an instance: downward Löwenheim–Skolem for countable fragments, the
omitting types theorem, Craig, Lyndon and Malitz interpolation for sentences, and Marker's
well-ordering argument, which shows that the well-ordered models of a sentence have uniformly
bounded countable order type and that no Lω₁ω sentence defines the class of well-orders.

The [InfinitaryLogic roadmap](../InfinitaryLogic/README.md) built the syntax and semantics of Lω₁ω
and L∞ω, back-and-forth equivalence, Karp's theorem and Scott's isomorphism theorem, and listed
model existence and downward Löwenheim–Skolem for countable fragments among the topics it left to
separate roadmaps. This roadmap specifies them, together with the material that rests on them:
countable fragments and fragment elementarity, infinitary types and their isolation, Morleyization
of a countable fragment into first-order logic with omitted types, projective (PC) classes, and PC
separation.

The summits are:

* **model existence**: a countable member of a consistency property over the Henkin expansion
  `L[[ℕ]]` of a countable language has a countable model in which every element names a Henkin
  constant, and the model can be made to omit countably many partial types whenever the property
  lets each of them be refuted at every tuple (Layer 3);
* **the omitting types theorem** for countable formation-closed fragments (Layer 4);
* **Craig interpolation and PC separation** for sentences of a language of any cardinality, with the
  Lyndon (relation polarity, logical equality) and Malitz (universal consequent) refinements, all
  for arbitrary languages except Malitz, which is relational (Layer 7);
* **the boundedness theorems for well-orderings** of Marker's Chapter 4 and the undefinability of
  well-ordering, for arbitrary languages (Layer 8).

The interpolation and well-ordering theorems are proved for relational languages and transferred to
languages with function symbols by the **graph translation** of Layer 6, a proof device with its own
API.

Interpolation here is **sentence-level** throughout. The analogous statements for sets of sentences
are false for Lω₁ω (see [Out of scope](#out-of-scope)), and no theorem of this roadmap claims them.

## Standing conventions

Pin these once; every statement below uses them.

1. **Languages.** `L : FirstOrder.Language.{u, v}` is arbitrary unless a statement says otherwise.
   Countability is carried as the separate instance arguments `[Countable (Σ n, L.Functions n)]` and
   `[Countable (Σ n, L.Relations n)]`, only where a proof needs them; there is no bundled
   countable-language class (the open
   [mathlib4#42189](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/42189) relates these two
   instances to `Language.card ≤ ℵ₀`, and its names are the ones to use). The interpolation and
   well-ordering theorems hold for languages of any cardinality, with no countability hypothesis, by
   passing to the countable sublanguage of the symbols that occur. `[L.IsRelational]` appears in the
   Lean statement of their relational cores, which are named milestones, and of Malitz's theorem.
2. **Syntax.** Lω₁ω is the parent's `BoundedFormulaω L α n = BoundedFormulaInf L ℕ α n`, with
   `Sentenceω L` its sentences. Members of fragments and types have **no free variables**: they are
   `BoundedFormulaω L Empty n`, and a tuple `a : Fin n → M` supplies the `n` bound positions as
   parameters. Countable families are coded into the carrier `ℕ` with the parent's `iInfAlong` and
   `IndexCoding.ofEncodableWith`. Mathlib's `[Finite β]` connectives are never used for infinitary
   conjunctions.
3. **Semantics.** Models are **nonempty**, as in Mathlib's `Theory.ModelType`. `IsSatisfiable` and
   `Entails` quantify over nonempty models in the universe `max u v`, the universe Mathlib fixes in
   `Theory.IsSatisfiable` and `Theory.ModelsBoundedFormula`. Layer 2 proves that for countable
   theories nothing depends on this choice. Theorems that produce a model produce a countable one in
   an arbitrary universe `w`.
4. **Henkin constants.** The constants added by every consistency property are `L[[ℕ]]`
   (Mathlib's `Language.withConstants`), written `L.con c`; a closed term is an
   `L[[ℕ]].Term (Empty ⊕ Fin 0)`, the term type of sentences.
5. **Negation.** `¬φ` is `φ.imp ⊥`. No negation normal form and no formal negation `∼` are
   built: polarity and quantifier class are signed traversals of the existing syntax, and double
   negation is the negated-implication clause at `ψ = ⊥`.
6. **Ordinals.** `ω₁` is `Ordinal.omega 1`; a countable ordinal is an `α` with
   `α < Ordinal.omega 1`, carried as a hypothesis.
7. **Sentence level.** Interpolation and separation are stated for single sentences, or for
   countable theories reduced to one sentence by a countable conjunction. Statements about
   arbitrary sets of sentences are excluded (see [Out of scope](#out-of-scope)).

## Layer 0: the signature calculus of infinitary formulas

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/LanguageMap.lean` and `SubstBounds.lean`.

Every later layer moves sentences between languages: to the Henkin expansion `L[[ℕ]]`, to the
countable sublanguage of the symbols a sentence mentions, to the Morleyization, to the expansions
that define projective classes, and back. The parent roadmap built none of this; it is built here
once, generic in the carrier `ι` wherever the statement allows.

### 0.1 Language maps

* `onBoundedFormulaInf (g : L →ᴸ L')`, the symbol-by-symbol action of a language homomorphism on
  `BoundedFormulaInf L ι α n`, through Mathlib's `LHom.onTerm`; with constructor equations, the
  functor laws for `LHom.id` and `LHom.comp`, and commutation with `toInf`, `reindex`, `mapBound`,
  `subst`, `relabel`, `alls`/`exs` and the coded connectives.
* `realize_onBoundedFormulaInf`: if `g.IsExpansionOn M`, the image of `φ` is realized in the
  `L'`-structure exactly when `φ` is realized in the `L`-structure. This is the infinitary form of
  Mathlib's `LHom.realize_onBoundedFormula`, and every transfer along reducts uses it.
* For an injective `g` (`LHom.Injective`), `onBoundedFormulaInf g` is injective.
* Mathlib's `LEquiv` acts by an equivalence of formula types.

### 0.2 Occurrences and symbol locality

* `termFunctionsIn`, and `functionsIn`, `relationsIn : BoundedFormulaInf L ι α n → Set (Σ n, …)`,
  the symbols occurring in a formula. Equality is logical: it contributes the function symbols of
  its terms and no relation symbol. Constructor equations, and the laws for the derived
  connectives, `alls`/`exs`, `iInfAlong`/`iSupAlong` (padding contributes nothing), `reindex`,
  `substBounds` (the function symbols of the substituted terms are added) and
  `onBoundedFormulaInf` (images of the occurrence sets).
* `functionsIn_countable`, `relationsIn_countable`: at a countable carrier the occurrence sets are
  countable, since a formula is a countably branching well-founded tree. Use sets, not `Finset`s.
* **Symbol locality** `realize_congr_of_agreeOn`: two structures on one carrier that agree on the
  function and relation symbols occurring in `φ` give `φ` the same truth value at every
  assignment.
* For `L[[γ]]`-formulas, the **constant support** is the set of `c : γ` whose constant occurs, read
  off `functionsIn`. Follow the set-valued shape of the free-variable support of
  [Tau Ceti #17547](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/pull/17547).

### 0.3 Sublanguages and expansions

* `symbSublang F R`, the sublanguage of `L` whose function and relation symbols are the members of
  `F : Set (Σ n, L.Functions n)` and `R : Set (Σ n, L.Relations n)`, with its injective inclusion
  `symbSublangIncl F R`; countable when `F` and `R` are.
* `restrictSymbols`: a formula whose occurrence sets lie in `F` and `R` restricts to the
  sublanguage, and `onBoundedFormulaInf (symbSublangIncl F R)` sends the restriction back to it.
* `exists_eq_onBoundedFormulaInf_of_subset_range`: for any `g : L →ᴸ L'`, a formula of `L'` whose
  occurring symbols all lie in the ranges of `g.onFunction` and `g.onRelation` is
  `onBoundedFormulaInf g ψ` for some formula `ψ` of `L`. No injectivity is needed: choose a
  preimage for each occurring symbol. This pulls an interpolant back from a combined language.
* `expandSymbStructure`: on a nonempty carrier, a `symbSublang F R`-structure expands to an
  `L`-structure (symbols outside `F` and `R` get arbitrary interpretations), the inclusion is an
  expansion, and by symbol locality a formula whose symbols lie in `F` and `R` has the same truth
  value in both. For relational `L` no nonemptiness is needed.
* The occurrence sets of a single sentence generate the **symbol sublanguage of the sentence**,
  which is countable at carrier `ℕ`; likewise for a countable set of sentences.
* **The small presentation** of a language with countably many function and relation symbols
  (`[Countable (Σ n, L.Functions n)]`, `[Countable (Σ n, L.Relations n)]`, function symbols
  allowed): `smallPresentation L : FirstOrder.Language.{0, 0}`, whose symbols of each arity are
  `Shrink.{0}` of those of `L`, and `smallPresentationEquiv L : L ≃ᴸ smallPresentation L`, a
  Mathlib `LEquiv` built from `equivShrink`. Realization transport `realize_smallPresentation`: an
  `L`-structure, reinterpreted along the inverse map, realizes the image of `φ` exactly when it
  realizes `φ`. Composed with the symbol sublanguage, it moves any countable family of sentences
  into `Language.{0, 0}`, where constructions that live in a fixed universe can run.

### 0.4 Substitution for bound positions and constants as variables

* `substBounds (σ : Fin m → L.Term (α ⊕ Fin k))`, replacing bound position `i` by `σ i`, with
  binders handled by `liftSubst` (old positions shift by `Fin.castSucc`, the new position goes to
  itself). Its laws: `realize_substBounds` (the realization is that of `φ` at the evaluated terms),
  composition, identity, `mapBound f = substBounds (fun i => var (Sum.inr (f i)))`, and preservation
  of the component structure (each component of `substBounds σ φ` is a substitution instance of a
  component of `φ`).
* `inst φ t` for `φ : BoundedFormulaInf L ι Empty 1` and a closed term `t`, the instantiation used
  by the quantifier clauses of Layer 3, and the **instances** `substBounds (fun i => L.con (c i))`
  of a formula with `n` bound positions at a tuple `c : Fin n → γ` of constants.
* `BoundedFormulaInf.toFormula` (bound positions become free variables `α ⊕ Fin n`) and
  `constantsVarsEquiv : BoundedFormulaInf L[[γ]] ι α n ≃ BoundedFormulaInf L ι (γ ⊕ α) n`, the
  infinitary forms of Mathlib's `BoundedFormula.toFormula` and `BoundedFormula.constantsVarsEquiv`,
  with their realization laws. They are how constants are abstracted into variables, and back,
  in the Craig and omitting-types arguments.
* `onBoundedFormulaInf_substBounds`: language maps commute with bound substitution,
  `onBoundedFormulaInf g (substBounds σ φ) = substBounds (g.onTerm ∘ σ) (onBoundedFormulaInf g φ)`.
* `IsAtomic`, the predicate picking out equalities and relation instances.

**Acceptance example.** `realize_onBoundedFormulaInf` for `lhomWithConstants`: a sentence of `L`
holds in an `L`-structure exactly when its image holds in any `L[[ℕ]]`-expansion.

## Layer 1: theories, entailment and fragments

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/Theory.lean` and `Fragment.lean`.

### 1.1 Theories and entailment

* `Theoryω L := Set (Sentenceω L)`, `Theoryω.Model`, with monotonicity, the empty theory,
  invariance under isomorphism, and transfer along expansions (`onBoundedFormulaInf`).
* `Theoryω.IsSatisfiable T`: a nonempty model in `Type (max u v)`. `Theoryω.Entails T φ`: every
  nonempty model in `Type (max u v)` satisfies `φ`. `Sentenceω.Entails φ ψ := Entails {φ} ψ`.
  The API: reflexivity, transitivity, monotonicity, cut,
  `entails_iff_not_isSatisfiable` (Mathlib's `models_iff_not_satisfiable`), and compatibility
  with first-order entailment: for first-order `φ`, `ψ`,
  `Sentenceω.Entails (toOmega φ) (toOmega ψ)` iff `{φ} ⊨ᵇ ψ` in Mathlib's sense.
* `Theoryω.conjunction T hT` for countable `T`, the countable conjunction along
  `IndexCoding.ofEncodableWith`, with `realize_conjunction_iff`. It is how a countable theory is
  reduced to a single sentence wherever the sentence-level theorems are applied to theories.
* `OmitsType M p` for `p : Set (BoundedFormulaω L Empty n)`: no `n`-tuple of `M` realizes every
  member of `p`.

**Non-compactness.** Lω₁ω is not compact, and the statement is a required example. In the language
`{0, S, c}`, the theory `{⋁ₖ c = Sᵏ0} ∪ {c ≠ Sᵏ0 : k ∈ ℕ}` is unsatisfiable while each finite
subset has a model (ℕ with `c` interpreted large). Consequently a union of a chain of satisfiable
sets need not be satisfiable, and model existence (Layer 3) is a countable enumeration, not a
Zorn argument.

### 1.2 Fragments

A **fragment** `A : Fragment L` is a set of tagged formulas `⟨n, φ⟩`,
`φ : BoundedFormulaω L Empty n`, closed under constructor components: both sides of an implication,
the body of a universal quantifier (at arity `n + 1`), and every component of a countable
conjunction or disjunction. In this de Bruijn syntax the component relation is the subformula
relation.

No atomic formula is required to belong, so a countable fragment exists over a language with
uncountably many symbols, and the fragment generated by a countable set is countable with no
hypothesis on the language. Formation closure is a separate predicate:

* `Fragment.FormationClosed A`: every atomic formula belongs; `⊥` belongs at every arity; and `A`
  is closed under implication, universal quantification and renaming of bound positions
  (`substBounds` along variable terms, which includes `mapBound` and `castLE`). Formation-closed
  fragments play the role of Marker's fragments. They omit his formal negation, which this syntax
  does not need, and his closure under substitution of terms, which no proof here uses: atomic
  formulas over arbitrary terms are already members. This is **not** a fragment in the sense of
  Gao, *Invariant Descriptive Set Theory*, whose fragments contain the
  first-order formulas over a countable language; results stated for those need the
  formation-closure hypothesis explicitly.

API:

* the complete lattice structure (`⊤`, intersections, `SetLike`), and `Fragment.generated S`, the
  least fragment containing `S`, given by the inductive reachability predicate `GeneratedFrom`;
* `Fragment.generated_countable`: a countable `S` generates a countable fragment, over any
  language, because each member is reached by a finite path of component steps indexed by
  natural numbers; with `Fragment.ofSentence φ` and `Fragment.ofTheory T` (countable `T`);
* `Fragment.formationClosure S`, the least formation-closed fragment containing `S`, countable
  when `S` is countable and the language has countably many function and relation symbols; and
  conversely, a countable formation-closed fragment forces countably many symbols of each sort
  (every symbol occurs in an atomic formula);
* `Fragment.negationClosure S` and the predicate `Fragment.NegationClosed`, with countability;
* the first-order image: the set of formulas `toOmega φ` with `φ` first-order is a formation-closed
  fragment, countable for a countable language.

**Acceptance example.** The fragment generated by a single sentence is countable, over a language
with uncountably many relation symbols.

## Layer 2: fragment elementarity and downward Löwenheim–Skolem

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/ModelExistence/{AElementary,LowenheimSkolem}.lean`.

### 2.1 `A`-elementary embeddings

`AElementary A f` for an embedding `f : N ↪[L] M` (the carriers may live in different universes):
for every member `⟨n, φ⟩` of `A` and every `a : Fin n → N`, `φ` holds of `f ∘ a` in `M` exactly when
it holds of `a` in `N`. It is stated as an iff because a fragment need not be negation-closed. API:
identity, composition, the two-out-of-three lemma (`AElementary A (f.comp g)` and `AElementary A f`
give `AElementary A g`), transport along isomorphisms, monotonicity in `A`, the sentence and theory
forms (`A`-sentences and `A`-theories hold in `N` iff in `M`), and agreement with Mathlib's
elementary embeddings on the first-order image: an elementary embedding is `A`-elementary for the
first-order image fragment.

### 2.2 Tarski–Vaught and the witness hull

* `aElementary_of_tarskiVaught`: a substructure `N ≤ M` is `A`-elementary if, for every member
  `⟨n, φ.all⟩` of `A` and every tuple `a` from `N`, a counterexample to `φ` at `a` in `M` can be
  found in `N`. Witnesses are elements, so no syntactic substitution enters; the induction runs over
  the fragment using exactly its component closures.
* The **witness hull** of `X ⊆ M`: alternate Mathlib's `Substructure.closure` with a chosen
  counterexample for each failing universal member of `A` at each tuple, for `ω` stages. It is
  `A`-elementary by Tarski–Vaught. Witnesses come from existence proofs, so no `Nonempty M` and no
  dummy element are needed.

### 2.3 Löwenheim–Skolem

* `exists_aElementary_substructure`: every `X ⊆ M` lies in an `A`-elementary substructure `N` with

  ```math
  |N| \le \max(\aleph_0,\ |X|,\ |A|,\ |\textstyle\sum_n L_{\mathrm{fun},n}|),
  ```

  stated with `Cardinal.lift` into `max u v w`, since the three cardinals start in three universes.
  Function symbols appear separately because a fragment need not contain an atomic formula
  mentioning them. Marker's bound `max(|A|, |X|)` (Marker 2016, Chapter 1), stated for fragments that
  contain every atomic formula, is the corollary for infinite formation-closed fragments, where the
  function-symbol term is absorbed; state the absorption lemma rather than assuming it.
* `exists_countable_aElementary_substructure`: with countably many function symbols, a countable
  fragment and a countable `X`, the substructure is countable. This is the form the sibling
  roadmaps consume.
* `exists_aElementary_substructure_of_eq_univ`, the same-universe form, where the lifts are
  identities.

### 2.4 Unions and limits

* `aElementary_iSup`: a nonempty directed family of `A`-elementary substructures of one structure
  `M` has an `A`-elementary supremum, and each member is `A`-elementary in the supremum
  (`aElementary_inclusion_iSup`, by two-out-of-three); with the linearly ordered corollary covering
  ω- and ω₁-chains.
* `aElementary_of_cocone`: for a directed system of `A`-elementary transition embeddings and a
  compatible family of embeddings into some `M` that jointly cover it, every map into `M` is
  `A`-elementary. Mathlib's `DirectLimit` is such a cocone (`aElementary_directLimit_of`), so
  `A`-sentences hold in a direct limit iff in any component. No countability of `A` is assumed.
* Omission transfers: `OmitsType.of_aElementary` (down to an `A`-elementary substructure, for
  types inside `A`) and `OmitsType.iSup` (through directed unions).

### 2.5 Sentence-level consequences

* `Theoryω.exists_countable_model`: a satisfiable countable theory has a countable nonempty model
  in every universe. Pass to the symbol sublanguage of `T` (Layer 0), take a countable
  `A`-elementary substructure, containing a chosen element, of a nonempty model for the fragment
  generated by `T`, transport it into the target universe, and expand back on the nonempty carrier.
  The chosen element matters: without it the substructure can be empty (for `T = {⊤}` over a
  language without constants).
* `Theoryω.entails_iff_countable`: for countable `T`, `T.Entails φ` holds iff every countable
  nonempty model of `T` in a given universe satisfies `φ`. In particular entailment between
  sentences does not depend on the universe of models, and the universe `max u v` of Convention 3
  is a normalization, not a restriction.
* `Theoryω.Entails.realize`: for countable `T`, `T.Entails φ` gives `φ` in every nonempty model of
  `T`, of any cardinality and in any universe. Proof: a countable `A`-elementary substructure,
  containing a chosen element, for the fragment generated by `T ∪ {φ}`, then
  `entails_iff_countable`. This is what makes the universe invariance usable for uncountable
  models.
* Transfer along language maps: entailment between sentences is preserved by `onBoundedFormulaInf g`
  for every `g` and reflected for injective `g` (expanding a model with `LHom.defaultExpansion`).
  The two languages may live in different universes, which is where universe invariance enters.

**Acceptance example.** In a dense linear order without endpoints of cardinality ℵ₁, every
countable subset lies in a countable `A`-elementary
substructure for the fragment generated by the dense-linear-order axioms, which is itself a
countable dense linear order without endpoints.

## Layer 3: consistency properties and model existence

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/ModelExistence/{ConsistencyProperty,ModelExistence}.lean`.

### 3.1 The definition

A `ConsistencyProperty L` is a family `sets : Set (Set (Sentenceω L[[ℕ]]))` such that for every
member `s` (writing `s + φ` for `insert φ s`):

| Clause | Condition |
|---|---|
| `no_falsum` | `⊥ ∉ s` |
| `no_contradiction` | not both `φ ∈ s` and `¬φ ∈ s` |
| `imp` | `φ → ψ ∈ s` gives `s + ¬φ` or `s + ψ` in the family |
| `not_imp` | `¬(φ → ψ) ∈ s` gives `s + φ` and `s + ¬ψ` in the family (at `ψ = ⊥` this is double negation) |
| `iInf`, `not_iInf` | `⋀ₖ φₖ ∈ s` gives every `s + φₖ`; `¬⋀ₖ φₖ ∈ s` gives some `s + ¬φₖ` |
| `iSup`, `not_iSup` | `⋁ₖ φₖ ∈ s` gives some `s + φₖ`; `¬⋁ₖ φₖ ∈ s` gives every `s + ¬φₖ` |
| `all` | `∀φ ∈ s` gives `s + φ[c]` for every Henkin constant `c` |
| `not_all` | `¬∀φ ∈ s` gives `s + ¬φ[c]` for some Henkin constant `c` |
| `eq_refl` | `s + (t = t)` for every closed term `t` |
| `eq_subst` | for atomic `φ` with one bound position, `t = t' ∈ s` and `φ[t] ∈ s` give `s + φ[t']` |
| `eq_name` | for every closed term `t`, `s + (c = t)` for some Henkin constant `c` |

Here `φ[t]` is `inst φ t`. This follows Marker 2016, Chapter 4, and Keisler 1971, adapted to the
implication-and-falsum syntax: the existential quantifier is `¬∀¬`, so `not_all` is the witness
clause, and the universal clause instantiates only constants because every element of the model
will be named by one.

Two clauses familiar from finitary presentations are deliberately absent. There is no
chain-closure clause and no clause adding `φ` or `¬φ` to every member: Lω₁ω is not compact (Layer
1), the family of satisfiable sets has neither property, and the inseparable pairs used for
interpolation fail both. Substitution of equals is required for atomic `φ` only, which is all the
term model needs and is what a countable enumeration domain can be closed under.

### 3.2 Model existence

* `model_existence`: let `L` have countably many function and relation symbols. If `s` is a
  countable member of a consistency property, then `s` has a countable model `M` in any universe,
  and every element of `M` is the interpretation `L.con c` of a Henkin constant.
* `model_existence_omitting`: given in addition a countable family `P` of countable partial types
  over `L[[ℕ]]` such that for every `p ∈ P`, every member `s` and every tuple `c` of Henkin
  constants some `ψ ∈ p` has `s + ¬ψ[c]` in the family, the model also omits every `p ∈ P`.

The proof is a fair enumeration. Fix the countable closure `U` of `s` (and of the chosen
refutations) under the decomposition targets of every clause and the atomic sentences over the
countable language; enumerate requests by a surjection `ℕ → Request U` and sweep prefixes, so that
each request fires after every stage, each stage being a member. The union `s*` satisfies the
**Henkin completeness** predicate: every clause's target lies in `s*`. It is never claimed to be a
member. The model is the quotient of the Henkin constants by `c ∼ d ⟺ (c = d) ∈ s*`; a function
symbol applied to classes is the class named by `eq_name`, well defined by `eq_subst` and `eq_refl`.
The **truth lemma** consists of two forward implications proved by one simultaneous induction on
sentences: `σ ∈ s*` gives `M ⊨ σ`, and `¬σ ∈ s*` gives `M ⊭ σ`. It uses no maximality and no
excluded middle on membership; `¬χ ∈ s*` is handled by the negative half for `χ`, not by the
implication clause.

Name the pieces: `ConsistencyProperty.HenkinComplete`, the request type and schedule, the
quotient model with its atomic semantics, `truth_pos`/`truth_neg`, and the constant-naming
surjection, so that Layers 4, 7 and 8 can instantiate them.

### 3.3 The satisfaction instance

* `ConsistencyProperty.ofModel`: for a nonempty structure `M` and a surjection `e : ℕ → M`, the
  sets of `L[[ℕ]]`-sentences true in the expansion of `M` interpreting `c` as `e c` form a
  consistency property.
* `ConsistencyProperty.ofSatisfiable T A`: for a countable fragment `A` and a theory `T ⊆ A`, the
  sets `T ∪ σ` with `σ` a finite set of `A`-instances (Layer 0) such that `T ∪ σ` has a nonempty
  model interpreting the constants form a consistency property, provided `A` is formation-closed.
  Each clause is checked by modifying the interpretation of finitely many constants.

**Acceptance example.** `ConsistencyProperty.ofModel` for a fixed countable structure, and
`model_existence` applied to its theory, giving a countable model of the same Lω₁ω-theory.

## Layer 4: types and omitting types

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/ModelExistence/{Types,OmittingTypes}.lean`.

### 4.1 Types

* `infinitaryType M a`, the complete Lω₁ω-type of `a : Fin n → M` (the set of members of
  `BoundedFormulaω L Empty n` true of `a`), and `realizedTypes M n`. Invariance under isomorphism,
  and `InfEquivAt ℕ M N` iff `realizedTypes M 0 = realizedTypes N 0`.
* `fragmentType A M a`, the members of `A` true of `a`; restriction along `A ≤ B`;
  `AElementary.fragmentType_comp` (an `A`-elementary embedding preserves `A`-types); the arity-`n`
  slice `Fragment.slice A n` and the realized truth assignment `A.slice n → Prop`, transported along
  isomorphisms and along `A ≤ B` by precomposition with the slice map.
* `exists_isolatingFormula`: if `M` realizes countably many complete `n`-types, every realized type
  `p` is isolated among the realized types by one formula `χ_p = ⋀_q θ_{p,q}`, a countable
  conjunction of separators (a formula in `p` and not in `q`, or the negation of one in `q` and not
  in `p`): `χ_p` holds of `b` iff `infinitaryType M b = p`. No smallness predicate, fragment or
  relational hypothesis is involved; the smallness predicate and its consequences belong to the
  HanfNumbers roadmap.
* The countable seed of isolators: for a structure realizing countably many types at every arity,
  the set of all `χ_p` and their one-variable existential closures generates a countable fragment.

### 4.2 Isolation over a theory and omitting types

* `Theoryω.IsolatesIn A T p`: some member `φ` of `A` at arity `n` has `T ∪ {∃̄φ}` satisfiable and
  `T ⊨ ∀̄(φ → ψ)` for every `ψ ∈ p`, where `∃̄` and `∀̄` close off the `n` bound positions.
* `Theoryω.exists_countable_model_omitting`: let `A` be a countable formation-closed fragment, `T`
  a satisfiable set of sentences of `A`, and `P` a countable family of partial types inside `A`,
  none isolated over `T` in `A`. Then `T` has a countable nonempty model, in any universe, omitting
  every `p ∈ P`. No separate countability of the language is assumed: it follows from countability
  of `A` (Layer 1).

  Route: apply `model_existence_omitting` to `ConsistencyProperty.ofSatisfiable T A`. For a member
  `T ∪ σ` and a tuple `c` of constants, abstract the constants of `σ` into bound positions with
  `constantsVarsEquiv`, conjoin, rename so that `c` comes first, and existentially close the other
  positions. The result `φ` lies in `A` by formation closure, so non-isolation gives `ψ ∈ p` with
  `T ∪ {∃̄(φ ∧ ¬ψ)}` satisfiable, and reinstating the constants makes `T ∪ σ ∪ {¬ψ[c]}` satisfiable.
  The reduct to `L` of the named model omits `p` because every element is named.
* Corollaries: a partial type inside `A` realized in every countable model of `T` is isolated over
  `T` in `A`; and the **first-order omitting types theorem**: for a countable language, a
  satisfiable first-order theory and countably many first-order partial types none isolated by a
  first-order formula modulo the theory (in the sense of Mathlib's `Theory.IsSatisfiable`), there
  is a countable model omitting them all. Derive it by taking `A` to be the first-order image
  fragment.

**Acceptance example.** A partial type realized in every countable model of `T` is isolated.

## Layer 5: Morleyization

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/ModelExistence/Morleyization.lean`.

### 5.1 The canonical definitional expansion

For any set `Φ` of tagged formulas (no closure assumed):

* `morleyize L Φ : FirstOrder.Language.{u, max u v}`: the function symbols of `L`, and as `n`-ary
  relation symbols those of `L` together with one symbol `R_φ` for each member `⟨n, φ⟩ ∈ Φ`;
  `lhomMorleyize Φ : L →ᴸ morleyize L Φ`; relational and countable instances when `L` is
  relational or countable and `Φ` is countable.
* `morleyExpansion Φ M`, interpreting `R_φ(a)` as `φ(a)`; it is an expansion, and its reduct is
  the original structure by `rfl`.
* `definingTheory Φ`, the Lω₁ω sentences `∀̄(R_φ ↔ φ)`; the canonical expansion is its unique model
  among expansions (`eq_morleyExpansion_of_model_definingTheory`).
* `unMorleyize`, the back-translation replacing each atom `R_φ(t̄)` by `φ` with `t̄` substituted for
  its bound positions (`substBounds`), and `realize_unMorleyize`: realization in the canonical
  expansion equals realization of the back-translation.
* `morleyEquiv`: isomorphisms of `L`-structures lift to isomorphisms of canonical expansions with
  the same underlying bijection, and restrict back (`nonempty_morleyEquiv_iff`).
* For a fragment `A`: an embedding is `A`-elementary iff it is an embedding of the canonical
  expansions by `A` (`exists_morleyEmbedding_iff_aElementary`), and the lift is unique.

### 5.2 The first-order presentation

Let `A` be a fragment. Define the first-order `morleyize L A`-theory `morleyTheory A`, with one
group of axioms for each member `⟨n, φ⟩ ∈ A` according to its outermost constructor, each
universally closed over the `n` positions `x̄`:

| `φ` | axiom |
|---|---|
| `⊥` | `¬R_φ(x̄)` |
| `t₁ = t₂`, `R(t̄)` | `R_φ(x̄) ↔ φ(x̄)` (a first-order atomic formula) |
| `φ₁ → φ₂` | `R_φ(x̄) ↔ (R_{φ₁}(x̄) → R_{φ₂}(x̄))` |
| `∀ φ₁` | `R_φ(x̄) ↔ ∀y R_{φ₁}(x̄, y)` |
| `⋀ₖ φₖ` | `R_φ(x̄) → R_{φₖ}(x̄)`, for each `k` |
| `⋁ₖ φₖ` | `R_{φₖ}(x̄) → R_φ(x̄)`, for each `k` |

and the first-order partial types `morleyTypes A`: for each conjunction member,
`{¬R_φ(x̄)} ∪ {R_{φₖ}(x̄) : k ∈ ℕ}`, and for each disjunction member,
`{R_φ(x̄)} ∪ {¬R_{φₖ}(x̄) : k ∈ ℕ}`. The symbols `R_{φ₁}`, `R_{φₖ}` exist because `A` is closed
under components.

* `morleyTheory_countable`, `morleyTypes_countable` for countable `A`, with no countability of the
  language.
* `exists_firstOrder_presentation` (Marker 2016, Chapter 1): an expansion
  `S` of an `L`-structure along `lhomMorleyize A` models `morleyTheory A` and omits every type of
  `morleyTypes A` **iff** `S` is the canonical expansion. The backward direction is an induction
  over `A`; omission supplies exactly the conjunction and disjunction steps.
* Corollary: for a sentence `φ` with `⟨0, φ⟩ ∈ A`, the models of `φ` are exactly the reducts of the
  models of the countable first-order theory `morleyTheory A ∪ {R_φ}` that omit `morleyTypes A`.

**Acceptance example.** For `φ_fin = ⋁ₙ` "there are exactly `n` elements" and `A` the fragment it
generates: every finite structure's canonical expansion models `morleyTheory A ∪ {R_{φ_fin}}`; and
first-order compactness (Mathlib's `Theory.isSatisfiable_iff_isFinitelySatisfiable`) gives an
infinite model of that first-order theory, which therefore realizes the type attached to `φ_fin`.
The omitted types do mathematical work.

## Layer 6: the graph translation

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Infinitary/GraphTranslation.lean`.

The model-existence engines of Layers 7 and 8 are run over relational languages, where every closed
term of `L[[ℕ]]` is a constant. This layer transfers sentence-level statements between `L` and a
relational language by the standard graph construction: an `n`-ary function symbol becomes an
`(n + 1)`-ary relation symbol for its graph. It is a proof device for the theorems of Layers 7 and
8, not a general relationalization: it translates single sentences and entailments together with the
totality and functionality axioms of the symbols they mention, and it does not transfer the
back-and-forth and Scott analysis of the parent roadmap to languages with function symbols, which
belongs to the separate relationalization roadmap.

* **The graph language.** `GraphRelation L n` with constructors `base (R : L.Relations n)` and
  `graph (f : L.Functions k)` at arity `k + 1`; `graphLanguage L`, with no function symbols and
  these relation symbols, is relational; it has countably many relation symbols when `L` has
  countably many symbols of both sorts. The symbol maps `baseRelSym`, `graphRelSym` are injective
  with disjoint images, `relSym F R` is the set of base symbols from `R` and graph symbols from `F`,
  and the **shared-vocabulary identity** `relSym F₁ R₁ ∩ relSym F₂ R₂ = relSym (F₁ ∩ F₂) (R₁ ∩ R₂)`
  holds, with the laws for unions and indexed unions.
* **The graph expansion** `graphExpansion L M` of an `L`-structure: base relations unchanged and
  `G_f(x̄, y)` interpreted as `f(x̄) = y`.
* **Formula translation**
  `relationalize : BoundedFormulaω L α n → BoundedFormulaω (graphLanguage L) α n`: an atom is
  flattened through its terms with existentially quantified witnesses for their values (one witness
  for an equality, one per argument for a relation atom), and connectives and quantifiers are
  translated structurally. Its laws: `realize_relationalize` (over the graph expansion, the
  translation is realized exactly when the original is); the **exact occurrence identity**
  `relationsIn (relationalize φ) = relSym (functionsIn φ) (relationsIn φ)`; no function symbol
  occurs in the translation; and base relations keep their polarity exactly (a base symbol occurs
  positively, resp. negatively, in the translation iff it does in `φ`), while graph symbols may
  occur with either sign.
* **Graph axioms and reconstruction.** `graphAxioms F` for a countable set `F` of function symbols:
  the countable conjunction of totality `∀x̄ ∃y G_f(x̄, y)` and functionality
  `∀x̄ y z (G_f(x̄, y) → G_f(x̄, z) → y = z)` for `f ∈ F`. The graph expansion satisfies them. On a
  model of `graphAxioms F`, `reconstructStructure F` is the `L`-structure whose functions in `F` are
  read off the graph relations (and are arbitrary outside `F`) and whose relations are the base
  relations; `realize_relationalize_reconstruct`: for `φ` with `functionsIn φ ⊆ F`, the translation
  of `φ` in the graph structure is realized exactly when `φ` is realized in the reconstruction.
* **Back-translation**
  `backTranslate : BoundedFormulaω (graphLanguage L) α n → BoundedFormulaω L α n`, sending
  `G_f(x̄, y)` to `f(x̄) = y` and base atoms to themselves; `realize_backTranslate` (over the graph
  expansion of any `L`-structure, the back-translation is realized exactly when the graph formula
  is); its occurrence calculus (the function symbols of `backTranslate θ` are the `f` with `G_f`
  occurring in `θ`; its relation symbols, positive and negative, are the base symbols occurring in
  `θ` with that sign, since graph atoms become equalities, which carry no polarity).
* **Entailment transfer** `entails_iff_graphTranslation`: `φ ⊨ ψ` iff
  `graphAxioms F₁ ∧ relationalize φ ⊨ graphAxioms F₂ → relationalize ψ` in the graph language,
  where `Fᵢ` are the function symbols of `φ` and `ψ`. Reconstruction over `F₁ ∪ F₂` is the only
  semantic step; it uses nonemptiness of models for the symbols outside `F₁ ∪ F₂`.

**Acceptance example.** `backTranslate (relationalize φ)` and `φ` have the same realization in every
`L`-structure (no syntactic identity is claimed).

## Layer 7: projective classes and interpolation

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/Interpolation/{PCClass,Craig,Lyndon,Malitz}.lean`.

### 7.1 Projective classes

* `PCMem g Θ M` for `g : L →ᴸ L'` and `Θ : Sentenceω L'`: the `L`-structure `M` itself, on the
  same carrier, has an `L'`-structure that is an expansion along `g` and satisfies `Θ` (Marker's
  `PC_{ω₁ω}` classes). No nonemptiness is built in.
* API: invariance under isomorphism; every model class is projective (`g = LHom.id`);
  projective classes along `LHom.sumInl : L →ᴸ L.sum L₁` are closed under binary intersection
  and, for relational `L₁` and `L₂`, binary union (both in `L.sum (L₁.sum L₂)`), and under
  composition of expansions (a projective class of a projective class is projective).

### 7.2 Craig interpolation and PC separation

* `craig_interpolation_relational`, the relational core: for relational `L` of any cardinality and
  sentences with `Sentenceω.Entails φ ψ`, there is `θ` with
  `relationsIn θ ⊆ relationsIn φ ∩ relationsIn ψ`, `φ ⊨ θ` and `θ ⊨ ψ`.
* `craig_interpolation`: the same for an arbitrary language of any cardinality, with no hypothesis
  on `L`, and with both bounds `functionsIn θ ⊆ functionsIn φ ∩ functionsIn ψ` and
  `relationsIn θ ⊆ relationsIn φ ∩ relationsIn ψ`. Equality is logical, so `θ` may use it freely;
  when `φ` is unsatisfiable `θ = ⊥` works, and when `ψ` is valid `θ = ⊤` does. Proof: translate the
  entailment by `entails_iff_graphTranslation`, apply the relational core in `graphLanguage L`, use
  the exact occurrence identity and the shared-vocabulary identity to bound the interpolant's
  symbols by `relSym (F₁ ∩ F₂) (R₁ ∩ R₂)`, and back-translate; both entailments are transported
  along the graph expansion, without reconstruction.
* `craig_pcSeparation`: if `ψ₁ ⊨ ¬ψ₂`, some sentence of
  `symbSublang (functionsIn ψ₁ ∩ functionsIn ψ₂) (relationsIn ψ₁ ∩ relationsIn ψ₂)` holds in the
  reduct of every nonempty model of `ψ₁` and fails in the reduct of every nonempty model of `ψ₂`,
  in every universe;
  with `craig_pcSeparation_relational`, its relational special case, which falls out of the
  relational core before the graph translation is applied.
* `pcSeparation`: for arbitrary languages `L`, `L₁`, `L₂`, if no countable nonempty `L`-structure
  lies in both `PCMem LHom.sumInl Θ₁` and `PCMem LHom.sumInl Θ₂`, some `θ : Sentenceω L` holds in
  every nonempty member of the first class and fails in every nonempty member of the second, in
  every universe. The hypothesis is only about countable structures `M : Type` (by Layer 2), and
  the conclusion holds for nonempty `M : Type w`; this is the interface the López–Escobar theorem
  of CountableStructureSpaces consumes, so its signature is fixed as in `Suggested.lean`.

  Route. Work in `L⁺ := L.sum (L₁.sum L₂)`, where the two new vocabularies are disjoint even when
  `L₁ = L₂`. Let `Θ₁⁺` and `Θ₂⁺` be the images of `Θ₁` and `Θ₂` along
  `(LHom.id L).sumMap LHom.sumInl` and `(LHom.id L).sumMap LHom.sumInr`. (1) If `Θ₁⁺ ∧ Θ₂⁺` had a
  nonempty model, `Theoryω.exists_countable_model` would give a countable nonempty one in `Type`,
  whose reducts along the two maps are expansions of its `L`-reduct satisfying `Θ₁` and `Θ₂`,
  against the hypothesis; hence `Sentenceω.Entails Θ₁⁺ Θ₂⁺.not`. (2) `craig_interpolation` in `L⁺`
  gives `θ⁺` whose symbols lie in those of both `Θ₁⁺` and `Θ₂⁺.not`, hence in the range of
  `LHom.sumInl : L →ᴸ L⁺`, and `exists_eq_onBoundedFormulaInf_of_subset_range` (Layer 0) gives
  `θ : Sentenceω L` with `onBoundedFormulaInf LHom.sumInl θ = θ⁺`. (3) A nonempty member
  `M : Type w` of the first class, with its expansion `S₁`, expands to `L⁺` by interpreting the
  symbols of `L₂` arbitrarily (this is where `Nonempty M` is used); the result models `Θ₁⁺`, so by
  `Theoryω.Entails.realize` (Layer 2; `M` may be uncountable and `w` arbitrary) it models `θ⁺`, and
  by `realize_onBoundedFormulaInf` `M` models `θ`. The second class is symmetric, with `L₁`
  interpreted arbitrarily. The proof gives the same `θ` in every universe.
  Corollary: if every countable nonempty `L`-structure lies in
  exactly one of two such classes, the first is the class of countable nonempty models of `θ`.

Route for the relational core. Reduce to the countable symbol sublanguage of `φ` and `ψ` (Layer 0)
and work in `L[[ℕ]]`. The members of the consistency property are finite pairs `(Γ, Δ)`, presented
as the set `Γ ∪ Δ`, with `Γ` in the symbols of `φ`, `Δ` in those of `ψ`, both with finite constant
support, such that no sentence in the shared relation symbols, with constants inside the pair's
support, is entailed by `Γ` and refuted on `Δ`. A separator parameterized by its allowed constant
support makes the root gate immediate: at empty support a separator is constant-free and is an
interpolant. Fresh witnesses for `not_all` pass temporarily to a larger support, and constant
abstraction (`constantsVarsEquiv`) returns the separator. If `φ` had no interpolant, `{φ, ¬ψ}` would
be a member, and `model_existence` would give a nonempty model of `φ ∧ ¬ψ`. The mixed equality cases
of `eq_subst` (an atom from one side, an equality from the other) are where equality being logical
is used.

### 7.3 Lyndon interpolation

* `relationsInSigned s φ` with `positiveRelationsIn`, `negativeRelationsIn`: the signed
  traversal in which an antecedent flips the sign, quantifiers and the countable connectives
  preserve it, and equality contributes to neither sign; the swap laws for `not`, the laws for the
  derived connectives, `relationsIn = positiveRelationsIn ∪ negativeRelationsIn`, and countability.
* `realize_mono_of_signed` (relational `L`): if a second structure on the same carrier enlarges
  every relation occurring positively in `φ` and shrinks every relation occurring negatively, truth
  of `φ` passes from the first to the second. Relationality is used once, to make term evaluation
  structure-independent.
* `lyndon_interpolation_relational`: for relational `L` and `φ ⊨ ψ`, an interpolant `θ` whose
  positively occurring relation symbols occur positively in both `φ` and `ψ`, and likewise
  negatively.
* `lyndon_interpolation`: the same for an arbitrary language, with no hypothesis on `L`, adding the
  Craig bound on function symbols. The relational core is applied through Layer 6: base relations
  keep their polarity under the translation, and the graph symbols, which may occur with either
  sign, back-translate into equalities and drop out of the signed bounds.
  `craig_of_lyndon_interpolation` recovers Craig from the two signed bounds.

This is the **relation-polarity, logical-equality form** of López-Escobar's interpolation theorem:
his polarity clause is proved in full, and his clause restricting where equality may occur in `θ`
is not part of this theorem. The proof refines the Craig engine: only the separator class changes, the
pair maintaining the flipped classes `(P₁ ∩ N₂, N₁ ∩ P₂)`.

### 7.4 Malitz interpolation

* `universalSigned s φ`, with `IsUniversal := universalSigned true` and
  `IsExistential := universalSigned false`: atomic formulas are in both classes, an antecedent flips
  the class, countable conjunctions and disjunctions preserve it and are not counted as
  quantifiers, and `∀` is universal only (so `∃ = ¬∀¬` is existential). Laws for the derived
  connectives, `castLE`, renaming, `substBounds` and `onBoundedFormulaInf`. This is the `∀₁`/`∃₁`
  hierarchy, not `Π₁`/`Σ₁`: `⋀ᵢ ∃x θᵢ(x)` is existential.
* Universal sentences are preserved under substructures (`IsUniversal.realize_of_embedding`), and
  existential ones under extensions.
* `malitz_interpolation`: for relational `L` (of any cardinality), a universal `ψ` and `φ ⊨ ψ`,
  there is a universal interpolant with the Craig occurrence bound. This is the function-free
  formulation, stated as Theorem 4.5 of Harrison-Trainor and Kretschmer (2024). The roadmap states
  it in that form because the graph translation of Layer 6 does not preserve the universal syntax
  the theorem needs: an atom translates to an existential formula and the totality axioms are
  `∀∃`, so neither `graphAxioms F ∧ relationalize φ` nor `graphAxioms F → relationalize ψ` is
  universal. The engine is a side-labelled budgeted pair whose separators carry two quantifier
  permissions; the right root `¬ψ` has no positive universal occurrence exactly when `ψ` is
  universal, so no separator acquires an existential occurrence.

**Acceptance example.** The degenerate interpolants: if `φ` is unsatisfiable, `⊥` interpolates with
empty occurrence sets; if `ψ` is valid, `⊤` does.

## Layer 8: well-ordering

Suggested home: `TauCeti/ModelTheory/WellOrdering.lean`.

Fix a binary relation symbol `lt : L.Relations 2` of an arbitrary language and write
`RelChain lt f` for `∀ x y, x < y → lt(f x, f y)`, for any map `f` from a linearly ordered type.

* `HasWellOrderedChains φ lt`: for every `α < ω₁` some nonempty model of `φ` carries a chain
  `f : α.ToType → M`.
* `exists_model_relChain_rat` (Marker 2016, Chapter 4, raw positive form): under that
  hypothesis some countable nonempty model of `φ` carries `f : ℚ → M` with `RelChain lt f`.
  Injectivity is the separate corollary `RelChain.injective_of_irreflexive`, for irreflexive `lt`.
* `wellFounded_boundedness`: if `lt` is well-founded in every model of `φ`, some countable ordinal
  has a chain in no model of `φ`.
* `wellOrder_type_boundedness` (Marker 2016, Chapter 4): if `lt` well-orders every model of
  `φ`, some `α < ω₁` strictly exceeds the order type of every model.
* `wellOrdering_undefinable`: no sentence has as its models exactly the structures in which `lt`
  is a well-order. It is a different target from `not_exists_modelsOf_eq_wellOrderClass` of
  CountableStructureSpaces, which is about codes on `ℕ`; this one is about all structures.

Every theorem below holds for arbitrary `L` of any cardinality, with no hypothesis on `L`; the
relational cores (`exists_model_relChain_rat_relational` and its consequences) are named
milestones. The transfer uses the symbol sublanguage of `φ` and `lt` (Layer 0) to remove symbol
countability, and Layer 6 to remove function symbols: the well-ordered-chain hypothesis passes to
`graphAxioms F ∧ relationalize φ` along the graph expansion, the conclusion returns along the
reconstruction, and `lt` is a base relation, preserved on the nose by both.

Route for the relational core. Add Henkin constants and rational constants `d_q` (coded into `ℕ`,
rational constants at even indices). The members are `{φ} ∪ {d_q < d_r : q < r} ∪ σ` with `σ` finite
and, for every `α < ω₁`, a model of `φ` with a well-ordered chain in which the constants of `σ` can
be placed with gaps of length at least `α` between consecutive rational constants (Marker's
condition (*)). The disjunction clause uses regularity of `ω₁` (uncountably many `α` choose one of
countably many disjuncts) and downward closure of (*) in `α`; the witness clause for a constant
between two rational constants inserts the new point inside a gap, at `b_s + α` in a model chosen
for some `β > α + α`. Marker checks two clauses and leaves the rest as an exercise; all of them are
targets. `model_existence` then realizes the full positive diagram, and `q ↦ d_q` is the map.
Boundedness is the contrapositive (a well-founded relation has no chain from the negative
rationals), and undefinability follows because each `α + ω` with `α < ω₁` is the order type of a
well-order.

**Acceptance example.** A map `f : ℚ → M` with `RelChain lt f` makes `lt` ill-founded in `M`, via
`f (-1), f (-2), …`.

## Worked examples

Discharge these alongside the layers; each instantiates the final interfaces.

1. **Non-compactness** (Layer 1): the theory in `{0, S, c}` of Layer 1, finitely satisfiable and
   unsatisfiable, and the resulting failure of chain closure for satisfiable sets.
2. **A countable fragment over an uncountable language** (Layer 1): with relation symbols indexed
   by `ℝ`, the fragment generated by `⋁ₙ ∃x Rₙ(x)` is countable and contains no `R_r` for `r ∉ ℕ`.
3. **Löwenheim–Skolem for dense orders** (Layer 2): the acceptance example of Layer 2, and the
   universe-invariance check that `Sentenceω.Entails` between the dense-linear-order axioms and
   "no endpoints" can be tested on countable models.
4. **First-order omitting types** (Layer 4): the corollary of Layer 4 for a countable first-order
   theory, compared with Mathlib's `Theory.IsSatisfiable`.
5. **Isolators in (ℚ, <)** (Layer 4): (ℚ, <) realizes one complete 1-type and three complete
   2-types (`x < y`, `x = y`, `x > y`); compute the isolating formulas, which are atomic.
6. **Finiteness** (Layer 5): the acceptance example of Layer 5.
7. **Parity of finite sets** (Layer 7): over the empty language, the class of finite sets of even
   size is projective (a new binary relation that is a fixed-point-free involution, plus
   finiteness), as is the class of finite sets of odd size (an involution with exactly one fixed
   point). `pcSeparation` yields a pure-equality sentence separating them, and
   `⋁ₙ` "exactly `2n` elements" is one.
8. **Well-orders of bounded type** (Layer 8): for `α < ω₁`, the disjunction over `β < α` of the
   Scott sentences of `(β, <)` (InfinitaryLogic, `scott_isomorphism`) has as countable models
   exactly the well-orders of type below `α`, consistent with `wellOrder_type_boundedness`; no
   single sentence covers all countable well-orders.

## Completion criteria

Each object comes with its basic API, the transport lemmas along language maps and isomorphisms,
and the stated examples. The headline theorems are assembled from these APIs without hypotheses
asserting model existence, compactness, satisfiability or isolation that this roadmap is meant to
prove. The following chains must be visible:

* language maps and symbol locality → symbol sublanguages → removal of symbol countability from
  every headline;
* component closure → generated fragments → witness hull → Löwenheim–Skolem → universe invariance
  of entailment;
* consistency property → fair enumeration → Henkin completeness → quotient model → two-polarity
  truth lemma → model existence (with omission requests);
* formation-closed fragment → satisfaction instance → omitting types → first-order omitting
  types;
* fragment → Morley theory and types → first-order presentation;
* inseparable pairs → model existence → relational Craig → graph translation → Craig → PC
  separation, with Lyndon and Malitz as refinements of the separator class;
* Marker's gap condition → model existence → the rational chain → graph translation →
  boundedness → undefinability.

A theorem proved for a privately redefined entailment, fragment notion or consistency property
does not discharge its target.

## Out of scope

The following belong to separate roadmaps or to the named sibling.

* **Theory-level interpolation and separation.** For sets of sentences the interpolation theorem
  is false in Lω₁ω: López-Escobar (1965) gives disjoint `PC_Δ(Lω₁ω)` classes that no
  class closed under Lω₁ω-elementary equivalence separates. Likewise there is a set of Lω₁ω
  sentences closed under substructures that is equivalent to no set of universal Lω₁ω sentences.
  No theorem here is stated for arbitrary sets of sentences.
* **Forms of Malitz interpolation for languages with function symbols**, and **preservation
  theorems** — Malitz's relative existential preservation theorem, the
  characterization of sentences preserved by substructures as universal ones, and López-Escobar's
  homomorphism preservation theorem — and the equality-occurrence clause of López-Escobar's
  interpolation theorem, which needs a separate equality-occurrence recursion.
* **Relationalization of the back-and-forth and Scott spine** for languages with function and
  constant symbols. The graph translation of Layer 6 is a sentence-level proof device; transferring
  the parent's analysis belongs to the separate relationalization roadmap.
* **A deductive calculus for Lω₁ω and Karp's completeness theorem.** Every theorem here uses
  semantic entailment. An infinitary Hilbert calculus and its completeness belong with admissible
  sets and Barwise compactness, which also needs the calculus.
* **Admissible fragments, Barwise compactness and Nadel's bound.**
* **Building uncountable models**: Keisler's elementary end-extension theorem and the uncountable
  form of Marker 4.4.1.
* **Spaces of countable structures**, Borel model classes, López–Escobar and the non-Borelness of
  the well-order class (CountableStructureSpaces); **indiscernibles, Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models,
  Hanf numbers, smallness, small models and complete sentences** (HanfNumbers); **counting
  countable models** (CountableModelCounting).
* **Borel equivalence relations and Silver's theorem** ([BorelEquivalenceRelations](../BorelEquivalenceRelations/README.md) ([#1317](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/pull/1317))). Scott
  processes; the general Glimm–Effros dichotomy of Harrington, Kechris and Louveau and
  Gandy–Harrington effective topology; and the coanalytic form of Silver's theorem and Burgess's
  dichotomy for analytic equivalence relations belong to separate roadmaps.
* L_κλ for `κ > ω₁` beyond L∞ω, many-sorted logic, and effective versions of these theorems. Craig
  interpolation fails in L_κω for `κ > ω₁` (Malitz), so nothing here generalizes past ω₁.
