# Roadmap: orders of the sporadic groups from permutation representations

The goal of **SporadicOrders** is to give each sporadic simple group of small permutation degree a
concrete permutation model whose order is a theorem, and to connect that model to the presentation
that [CFSGStatement](../CFSGStatement/README.md) uses to define the group. For each covered name we
want:

```lean
theorem card_M24 : Nat.card SporadicOrders.M24 = 244823040
theorem exists_surjective_M24 :
    ∃ f : TauCeti.SporadicName.M24.Group →* SporadicOrders.M24, Function.Surjective f
```

where `SporadicOrders.M24 : Subgroup (Equiv.Perm (Fin 24))` is the subgroup generated by explicit
permutations and `TauCeti.SporadicName.M24.Group` is the `PresentedGroup` of the presentation that
CFSGStatement pins for that name. The first
theorem pins the order of the model. The second shows that the transcribed relators hold of the
model's generators, so the presented group is at least as large as the model; in particular it is
nontrivial, which is exactly the check that catches a relator transcribed wrongly enough to collapse
the group.

This roadmap does not prove simplicity, does not identify the presented group with the model (that
needs an upper bound on the presented group, see *Beyond this roadmap*), and does not treat groups of
Lie type.

Suggested home in Tau Ceti: `TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Orders/`.

## Dependency: kernel certificates for permutation groups

The orders are proved by the `perm_group` tactic of the Hex library `hex-perm-group-mathlib`, which
checks a stabilizer-chain certificate in the kernel and applies the soundness theorem
`Hex.PermGroup.Kernel.card_closure`. Its specification is
[hex-perm-group, Kernel certificates](https://github.com/kim-em/hex-dev/blob/main/HexPermGroup/SPEC/hex-perm-group.md#kernel-certificates).
The tactic proves, from closed generators,

```lean
Nat.card (Subgroup.closure ({σ₁, …, σₖ} : Set (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)))) = N
g ∈ Subgroup.closure ({σ₁, …, σₖ} : Set (Equiv.Perm (Fin n)))
Subgroup.closure ({σ₁, …, σₖ} : Set (Equiv.Perm (Fin n))) = ⊤
```

and their negations, with only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` as axioms. Tau Ceti
depends on the released `hex-perm-group-mathlib` package; adding that requirement is the first
target (O0).

## Existing work

[FiniteSimpleGroups](https://github.com/KitaKen1/finite-simple-groups-lean) (Apache-2.0) proves the
orders and simplicity of `M11 M12 M22 M23 M24 J1 J2 HS McL Co3 Suz Co2 Fi22 He` as subgroups of
`Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, by kernel-checked stabilizer chains closed with Witt designs, strongly regular
graphs and parity certificates, some needing a 128 GiB host. This roadmap takes a different route,
one general certificate checker rather than per-group arguments, and its order targets overlap that
work for ten names. Coordinate with its author before starting O1: the permutation models there
may be adopted as the definitions here, with the orders re-proved by `perm_group`, or cited as an
independent cross-check.

## O0: the Hex dependency

Add `hex-perm-group-mathlib` to `lakefile.toml` at a released version and pin it in

its axioms with `#guard_msgs` on `#print axioms`.

## O1: permutation models and their orders

For each name below, define `SporadicOrders.<Name> : Subgroup (Equiv.Perm (Fin n))` as the closure of
its ATLAS standard generators, written with `Hex.PermGroup.Kernel.permOfImages n [...]` so that the
statement elaborates in time linear in `n`, and prove `Nat.card SporadicOrders.<Name> = N` by
`perm_group`. Record for each name the ATLAS representation identifier the generators come from, as a
comment next to the definition.

| name | degree | order |
|---|---|---|
| `M11` | 11 | 7920 |
| `M12` | 12 | 95040 |
| `M22` | 22 | 443520 |
| `M23` | 23 | 10200960 |
| `M24` | 24 | 244823040 |
| `J1` | 266 | 175560 |
| `J2` | 100 | 604800 |
| `HS` | 100 | 44352000 |
| `McL` | 275 | 898128000 |
| `Co3` | 276 | 495766656000 |

Also prove, for each name, that the generators given are the ATLAS standard generators in the sense
recorded there: their orders and the order of their product, by `decide` on the image lists. That is
what lets O2 match the model's generators to the presentation's generator convention.

Every proof must build within the default `maxHeartbeats` and without `set_option maxHeartbeats`.

## O2: the presentations hold in the models

For each name in O1, construct the hom from CFSGStatement's `PresentedGroup` to the model by
`PresentedGroup.toGroup`, sending each presentation generator to an explicit element of the model,
and prove it surjective. Where the presentation's generators are not the ATLAS standard generators,
express each presentation generator as a word in the standard generators, citing the source that
relates the two conventions. The relator obligations are closed permutation equations and are proved
by `decide +kernel` on image lists; surjectivity follows from the standard generators lying in the
image. CFSGStatement records, as review artifacts, comparisons of its presentations with the
FiniteSimpleGroups models for fourteen names; O2 makes that comparison a theorem for the ten names
here.

Derive `Nontrivial TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group` and
`0 < Nat.card TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group → N ≤ Nat.card TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group`
from the surjection and O1.

## Beyond this roadmap

This section is motivation for later roadmaps, not work to attempt here.

The remaining sporadic groups with a faithful permutation representation of a few thousand points
(`He` 2058, `Suz` 1782, `Co2` 2300, `Fi22` 3510, `Ru` 4060, `J3` 6156) are within reach of the same
certificates once their kernel cost is measured; the rest (`Co1`, `Fi23`, `Fi24'`, `HN`, `Ly`, `Th`,
`O'N`, `J4`, `B`, `M`) need matrix-group certificates. Identifying each presented group with its
model needs an upper bound on the presented group, by a kernel-checked coset enumeration
certificate, which would turn O2's surjection into an isomorphism and give `Nat.card` of the
CFSGStatement group itself.
