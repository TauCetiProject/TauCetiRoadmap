# Roadmap: orders of the sporadic groups from permutation representations

**Summary.** For the ten sporadic simple groups `M11 M12 M22 M23 M24 J1 J2 HS McL Co3`, this
roadmap builds a concrete permutation model, the subgroup of `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` generated by the
two permutations of a named file of the ATLAS of Group Representations, proves its order, and
connects it to the presentation by which [CFSGStatement](../CFSGStatement/README.md) defines the
group: the group `TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group` presented by that presentation maps onto the
model. The general input is the stabilizer-chain form of the orbit–stabilizer theorem (Layer 0).
The end theorems are, for each of the ten names,

```lean
theorem card_M24 : Nat.card M24 = 244823040
theorem exists_surjective_M24 :
    ∃ f : TauCeti.SporadicName.M24.Group →* M24, Function.Surjective f
```

and their analogues `card_<Name>` and `exists_surjective_<Name>`, all in the namespace
`TauCetiRoadmap.SporadicOrders`. The order pins the model; the surjection shows that the
transcribed relators hold of permutations that generate the model, so the presented group is at
least as large as the model and in particular nontrivial, which is the property that a relator
transcribed wrongly enough to collapse the group would violate.

## Scope and ownership

- **Owned here.** The stabilizer-chain order formula and the orbit description of a generated
  permutation group (Layer 0, cited downstream as O0); the ten carriers `M11 M12 M22 M23 M24 J1 J2
  HS McL Co3`, each fixed by the literal generator images of its named ATLAS files, with their
  orders (Layer 1, O1); for each of them, explicit images of the presentation generators, the
  relator equations, the generation equality and the surjection from
  `TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group` (Layer 2, O2).
- **Supplied by Tau Ceti at the pin.** The twenty-six sporadic presentations,
  `TauCeti.SporadicName.presentation` and `TauCeti.SporadicName.Group`, which are CFSGStatement S1
  (*S1: the twenty-six sporadic presentations*) and its presentation format S0. They are consumed,
  not restated.
- **Supplied by Mathlib at the pin.** Orbit–stabilizer (`MulAction.index_stabilizer`,
  `Subgroup.card_mul_index`), Schreier's lemma (`Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`), pointwise
  stabilizers (`fixingSubgroup`), presented groups and their lifts
  (`PresentedGroup.toGroup`), and multiple transitivity (`MulAction.IsMultiplyPretransitive`).
  None of these is planned again here.
- **Left to [CFSGBasicProperties](../CFSGBasicProperties/README.md).** Its *Starting points and
  ownership* and its P1 take O0–O2 for the ten names and own what follows from them: the upper
  bounds on the presented groups (its P0, item 2), the recognition that turns these surjections into
  `MulEquiv`s (its P0, item 3, and P1), `Finite` and `IsSimpleGroup` for the presented groups, the
  four further P1 models `Co2 Suz Fi22 He`, and the twelve P2 names. It does not redefine the ten
  carriers. Its presented-group certificates (coset tables, normal forms, Tietze and normal-closure
  arguments) are its own; for orders and memberships of finite permutation groups it consumes
  Layer 0 and Layer 1.
- **Not in scope.** Simplicity, the identification of the presented group with the model (which
  needs an upper bound on the presented group), and the groups of Lie type.
- **Carriers are identified by data, not by name.** A theorem about another permutation
  realization of the same group, for instance one of the simplicity proofs in
  [FiniteSimpleGroups][fsg], reaches the carriers here only through an explicit equivalence of
  carriers (forward and recovery words between the two generating pairs, and an intertwiner of the
  two actions where the actions differ). Equal degree, equal order or a shared ATLAS label does not
  identify two carriers. CFSGBasicProperties P1 owns these comparisons.

## Conventions

- **Points.** A permutation of degree `n` acts on `Fin n`. An ATLAS permutation file lists the
  images of the points `1, …, n`; the point `i` of the file is the point `i - 1` of `Fin n`, and
  the generator is the `σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` with `σ (i - 1) = image(i) - 1`. Images, not
  preimages: reading the list as preimages replaces each generator by its inverse.
- **Products.** Products of permutations follow Mathlib, `(σ * τ) x = σ (τ x)`. GAP and the ATLAS
  compose left to right (`x^(στ) = (x^σ)^τ`), so a word `s₁ s₂ ⋯ sₖ` in the source's letters is
  the function `sₖ ∘ ⋯ ∘ s₁`. In Mathlib's convention a source relator `w = 1` that holds of the
  source's generators `g₁, …, gₘ` holds of the assignment `xᵢ ↦ gᵢ⁻¹`, because inversion turns
  the reversed word into the original word in the inverses.
- **Cycle notation.** Lean statements write a permutation as the product of its disjoint cycles
  `c[x₀, x₁, …, x_{l-1}]`, Mathlib's notation for `Cycle.formPerm`, which sends `xⱼ` to `x_{j+1}`
  and `x_{l-1}` to `x₀`; fixed points are omitted.
- **Orders.** `Nat.card` throughout, with Mathlib's junk value `0` for infinite types; every
  carrier here is a subgroup of a finite group, so its `Nat.card` is its order.
- **Stabilizer chains.** For a subgroup `G` of `Equiv.Perm α` and points `b₁, …, bₖ`, write
  `G₍ᵢ₎ = G ⊓ fixingSubgroup (Equiv.Perm α) {b₁, …, b_{i-1}}` for the pointwise stabilizer in `G`
  of the first `i - 1` points (so `G₍₁₎ = G`). The *basic orbits* are `Δᵢ = G₍ᵢ₎ · bᵢ`. The
  points `b₁, …, bₖ` form a *base* when `G₍ₖ₊₁₎` is trivial. The base used in every Check below
  is `0, 1, 2, …` in that order.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 proves, for an arbitrary permutation group, that its order is the product of the lengths
of the basic orbits of a stabilizer chain and that the orbit of a point under a generated subgroup
of a finite permutation group is the least set containing the point and closed under the
generators. Layer 1 defines the ten carriers from their ATLAS files and proves their orders; each
order is an instance of Layer 0, with the basic orbits computed through Schreier's lemma. Layer 2
evaluates CFSGStatement's relators on explicit permutations, shows that these permutations generate
the Layer 1 model, and lifts the assignment to the surjection. Layers 1 and 2 are independent of
each other except that Layer 2's corollaries on the size of the presented group use the Layer 1
orders.

## Layer 0: stabilizer chains (O0)

### 0.1 The stabilizer-chain order formula

Let `G` be a subgroup of `Equiv.Perm α` for an arbitrary type `α`, and let `b₁, …, bₖ` be points
of `α`. Prove that

    Nat.card G = (∏ i in 1..k, Set.ncard (G₍ᵢ₎ · bᵢ)) * Nat.card G₍ₖ₊₁₎

and, when `b₁, …, bₖ` is a base, that `Nat.card G` is the product of the basic-orbit lengths. The
route is induction on `k`: `G₍ᵢ₊₁₎` is the stabilizer of `bᵢ` in `G₍ᵢ₎`, so
`MulAction.index_stabilizer` identifies its index with `Set.ncard (G₍ᵢ₎ · bᵢ)` and
`Subgroup.card_mul_index` multiplies (Dixon–Mortimer, §1.4, Theorem 1.4A, for the orbit–stabilizer
step; the iterated form is Seress, §4.1). The identity needs no finiteness hypothesis, because
neither lemma has one; when `G` is infinite both sides are `0`. *Needs:* Mathlib `MulAction.index_stabilizer`, `Subgroup.card_mul_index`,
`fixingSubgroup`.

- For `k = 0` the formula reads `Nat.card G = Nat.card G`.
- For a base, `G₍ₖ₊₁₎` is trivial and its `Nat.card` is `1`.
- If `G` is `k`-transitive on a set of `n` points (`MulAction.IsMultiplyPretransitive G α k`), the
  first `k` basic orbits for any `k` distinct points have lengths `n, n - 1, …, n - k + 1`.

**Checks.**

- `G = Equiv.Perm (Fin 3)` with base `0, 1`: basic orbits of lengths 3 and 2, product 6, the order
  of `S₃`. A formula that multiplied the lengths of the orbits of `G` itself on each point, instead
  of those of the successive stabilizers, would give `3 · 3 = 9`.
- `G` the cyclic group generated by `c[0, 1, 2]` in `Equiv.Perm (Fin 3)`: the base `0` has basic
  orbit of length 3 and `G₍₂₎` trivial, product 3; the base `0, 1` adds a basic orbit of length 1
  and the product stays 3, so a redundant base point does not change the formula.
- The trivial subgroup with the empty base: the empty product is `1 = Nat.card ⊥`; a formula that
  required `k ≥ 1` would leave the trivial group without an order.
- `α = ℤ`, `G` generated by the shift `x ↦ x + 1`, base `0`: the basic orbit is infinite, so
  `Set.ncard` gives `0`, `G₍₂₎` is trivial, and both sides are `0 = Nat.card G`. This is the junk
  value case; it shows the identity needs no finiteness hypothesis.

### 0.2 Orbits of a generated permutation group

Let `α` be finite and `S` a set of permutations of `α`. Prove that the orbit of `b` under
`Subgroup.closure S` is the least subset of `α` that contains `b` and is mapped into itself by
every `s ∈ S`. Inverses need not be included: on a finite set a permutation that maps a finite set
into itself maps it onto itself, so the set is also closed under `s⁻¹`. *Needs:* Mathlib
`Subgroup.closure_induction`, `MulAction.orbit`.

- Combined with Schreier's lemma (`Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`) applied in `G₍ᵢ₎` to the
  stabilizer of `bᵢ`, this gives generators of `G₍ᵢ₊₁₎` from generators of `G₍ᵢ₎` and a
  transversal, hence each basic orbit `Δᵢ₊₁` as a closure under finitely many permutations.
- A permutation `g` lies in `G` if and only if there is `t ∈ G` with `t b₁ = g b₁` and `t⁻¹ g` lies
  in `G₍₂₎`; iterating along a base decides membership. This is a direct consequence of the
  definitions and carries no separate target.

**Checks.**

- `α = Fin 4`, `S = {c[0, 1]}`: the least `S`-closed set containing `0` is `{0, 1}`, which is the
  orbit; containing `2` it is `{2}`.
- Finiteness is needed: for `α = ℤ` and `S = {x ↦ x + 1}`, the set `ℕ` contains `0` and is closed
  under `S`, but the orbit of `0` is all of `ℤ`. A statement without `Finite α` (or without closing
  under `S⁻¹`) is false.
- `α = Fin 3`, `S = ∅`: the closure is trivial and the least closed set containing `b` is `{b}`.

### Examples

- `S₃ = Equiv.Perm (Fin 3)`: base `0, 1`, basic orbits 3, 2, order 6.
- The model `M11` of Layer 1: base `0, 1, 2, 3`, basic orbits 11, 10, 9, 8, order 7920.

### Dependencies

Mathlib only: `MulAction.index_stabilizer`, `Subgroup.card_mul_index`, `fixingSubgroup`,
`Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`, `Subgroup.closure_induction`.

API of each carrier, inherited from `Subgroup.closure` rather than proved again:

- `a` and `b` lie in the carrier, and the carrier is contained in every subgroup containing both
  (`Subgroup.subset_closure`, `Subgroup.closure_le`).
- Membership is closed under products and inverses and every element is a word in `a` and `b`
  (`Subgroup.closure_induction`).
- The carrier acts on `Fin n` through `Equiv.Perm (Fin n)`, so the orbit, stabilizer and
  `fixingSubgroup` API of Layer 0 applies to it directly.

### 1.2 Orders

Prove `card_M11`, `card_M12`, `card_M22`, `card_M23`, `card_M24`, `card_J1`, `card_J2`, `card_HS`,
`card_McL` and `card_Co3`: `Nat.card <Name> = N` with `N` from the table. The route is Layer 0.1
applied to the base `0, 1, 2, …` of the table: each basic orbit is the least set closed under
Schreier generators of the previous pointwise stabilizer (Layer 0.2), and the last pointwise
stabilizer is trivial. Any other base, or any other proof (for instance a kernel-checked
stabilizer-chain certificate, as in [Hex][hex], or the per-group arguments of [FiniteSimpleGroups][fsg]),
is acceptable; the statement prescribes no route. Proofs elaborate within Lean's default
resource limits. *Needs:* Layer 0.1, Layer 0.2, Mathlib `Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`.

**Checks.** Each check is computed on the generators of the named file with the conventions above.

- `M11`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 3 fixed points and `b` has order 4 with 3 fixed points; `a * b` has
    order 11. The first generator of a different pair of `M11`, such as the 11-cycle
    `c[0, 1, …, 10]`, has order 11, so the generator orders tell this pair apart from other
    generating pairs of the same group.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 3` but `b⁻¹ 0 = 7`; a transcription that read the file as preimages gives
    `7`.
  - The basic orbits 11, 10, 9, 8 multiply to 7920; a checker that returned the order of the
    cyclic group generated by `a * b` would give 11.
- `M12`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 4 fixed points, `b` order 3 and fixed-point-free; `a * b` has order 11.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 7`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 8`. Product convention: `(a * b) 0 = a (b 0) = 7`, while
    `(b * a) 0 = 1`.
  - The base `0, 1, 2, 3, 4` has basic orbits 12, 11, 10, 9, 8, so the model is 5-transitive on 12
    points; the point stabilizer, a copy of `M11`, has order `95040 / 12 = 7920`.
- `M22`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 6 fixed points, `b` order 4 with 2 fixed points; `a * b` has order 11.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 21`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 20`. `(a * b) 0 = 5`, `(b * a) 0 = 1`.
  - The basic orbits 22, 21, 20, 16, 3: the model is 3-transitive but the fourth basic orbit has
    16, not 19, points, so the pointwise stabilizer of three points is not transitive on the
    remaining 19; a model claimed to be 4-transitive fails here.
- `M23`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 7 fixed points, `b` order 4 with 3 fixed points; `a * b` has order 23.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 15`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 2`. `(a * b) 0 = 14`, `(b * a) 0 = 8`.
  - For the base `0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5` the basic orbits are 23, 22, 21, 20, 3, 16: the fifth point lies
    in an orbit of length 3 of the four-point stabilizer, and the order of basic orbits depends on
    the base, while their product 10200960 does not.
- `M24`.
  - `a` is a fixed-point-free involution (twelve 2-cycles, beginning `c[0, 3]`, `c[1, 6]`), `b` has
    order 3 with six fixed points, and `a * b` has order 23. A transcription slip that exchanges two
    images of the second file composes `b` with a transposition and makes it odd, which no element
    of order 3 is.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 3` but `b⁻¹ 0 = 5`. The orders of `a`, `b` and `a * b` do not fix the
    direction: `a * b⁻¹` also has order 23. `(a * b) 0 = 0`, `(b * a) 0 = 5`.
  - The basic orbits for the base `0, …, 6` are 24, 23, 22, 21, 20, 16, 3. The first five are
    those of a 5-transitive group; the pointwise stabilizer of `0, 1, 2, 3, 4` has order
    `244823040 / (24 · 23 · 22 · 21 · 20) = 48` and its orbits on the other 19 points have lengths
    3 (the points `10, 16, 23`, which complete `0, …, 4` to an octad) and 16. The number 48 is the
    order of a stabilizer, not the length of an orbit, which is at most 24.
- `J1`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 10 fixed points, `b` order 3 with 5 fixed points; `a * b` has order 7.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 145`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 20`. `(a * b) 0 = 122`, `(b * a) 0 = 254`.
  - The base `0, 1, 2` has basic orbits 266, 110, 6, product 175560: the model is transitive but
    not 2-transitive (110 < 265), and three points already form a base.
- `J2`.
  - `a` is a fixed-point-free involution on 100 points, `b` has order 3 with 4 fixed points; `a * b`
    has order 7.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 79`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 21`. `(a * b) 0 = 78`, `(b * a) 0 = 83`.
  - Basic orbits 100, 63, 24, 4, product 604800. The second factor 63 is the length of an orbit of
    the point stabilizer `U₃(3)` of order `604800 / 100 = 6048`; adding to the generators an
    element of the automorphism group J₂.2 outside the model would give product 1209600.
- `HS`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 20 fixed points, `b` order 5 and fixed-point-free; `a * b` has order 11.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 85`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 46`. `(a * b) 0 = 48`, `(b * a) 0 = 73`.
  - Basic orbits 100, 77, 60, 32, 3, product 44352000. The point stabilizer, a copy of `M₂₂` of
    order `44352000 / 100 = 443520`, has orbits of lengths 1, 22 and 77 on the 100 points (22 is
    the valency of the Higman–Sims graph), and the point `1` lies in the orbit of length 77. The
    same degree 100 with the `J2` files gives 63 in this place, so the second factor separates
    the two models of degree 100.
- `McL`.
  - `a` has order 2 with 35 fixed points, `b` order 5 and fixed-point-free; `a * b` has order 11.
  - Direction: `b 0 = 23`, `b⁻¹ 0 = 154`. `(a * b) 0 = 81`, `(b * a) 0 = 24`.
  - Basic orbits 275, 112, 81, 36, 5, 2, product 898128000; the second factor 112 is the valency of
    the McLaughlin graph.
- `Co3`.
  - `a` has order 3 with 6 fixed points, `b` order 4 with 16 fixed points; `a * b` has order 14.
  - Direction and product: `a * b⁻¹` has order 10, not 14, so a transcription that inverted only
    `b` is caught by an order; inverting both generators is not (`a⁻¹ * b⁻¹ = (b * a)⁻¹` has
    order 14), and it is caught by `b 0 = 203` against `b⁻¹ 0 = 81`.
  - Basic orbits 276, 275, 162, 56, 45, 16, product 495766656000: the model is 2-transitive on 276
    points, with point stabilizer McL:2 of order `495766656000 / 276 = 1796256000`, twice the
    order of `McL`.

### Examples

- `M24`: the order `244823040 = 2¹⁰ · 3³ · 5 · 7 · 11 · 23` from seven basic orbits.
- `Co3`: the largest order of the ten, `495766656000 = 2¹⁰ · 3⁷ · 5³ · 7 · 11 · 23`, from six basic
  orbits on 276 points.

Layer 0. Mathlib `Subgroup.closure`, `Equiv.Perm`, `Cycle.formPerm`, `Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`.

## Layer 2: the presentations hold in the models (O2)

### 2.1 Relators and generation

For each name in Layer 1, write `P = TauCeti.SporadicName.presentation <Name>` with generators
indexed by `Fin m`. Choose permutations `τ₁, …, τₘ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n)` and prove:

- every transcribed relator of `P` evaluates to `1` at `τ` (`TauCeti.Relator.eval`), a closed
  equation between permutations of `Fin n`;
- `Subgroup.closure {τ₁, …, τₘ}` equals the Layer 1 carrier: each `τᵢ` lies in the carrier, and
  each of `a` and `b` lies in the closure of the `τᵢ`.

How the `τᵢ` are found is not part of the target; the generation condition makes any choice that
satisfies the relators acceptable, whether or not the presentation's generators are the ATLAS
standard generators. When the presentation is on the ATLAS standard generators and the source
composes left to right, the conventions above give the candidate `τ = (a⁻¹, b⁻¹)`. *Needs:* Tau
Ceti `TauCeti.SporadicName.presentation`, `TauCeti.Relator.eval`; Layer 1 carriers; Layer 0.2 for
the membership of `a`, `b` in the closure of the `τᵢ`.

**Checks.**

- `M24`, whose presentation `M24G1-P1` is on the standard generators `a, b` read left to right:
  with `τ = (a, b)` the first five and the seventh relators hold but the sixth,
  `(ab · ab · ab⁻¹)³ (ab · ab⁻¹ · ab⁻¹)³`, sends `0` to `20`; with `τ = (a, b⁻¹)`, which is
  `(a⁻¹, b⁻¹)` since `a` is an involution, all seven hold, and `{a, b⁻¹}` generates the same
  subgroup as `{a, b}`. This pins the product convention: Mathlib's order of composition with the
  source's generators fails a relator.
- The relators `a²`, `b³`, `(ab)²³` of `M24` hold for both `(a, b)` and `(a, b⁻¹)`, so a check that
  tested only the power relators would not detect the convention.
- The trivial assignment `τᵢ = 1` satisfies every relator of every presentation but generates the
  trivial subgroup, so the generation condition is what excludes it.

### 2.2 The surjections

Prove `exists_surjective_M11`, `exists_surjective_M12`, `exists_surjective_M22`,
`exists_surjective_M23`, `exists_surjective_M24`, `exists_surjective_J1`, `exists_surjective_J2`,
`exists_surjective_HS`, `exists_surjective_McL` and `exists_surjective_Co3`: there is a surjective
hom from `TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group` onto the carrier `<Name>`. The route is
`PresentedGroup.toGroup` (equivalently `TauCeti.GroupPresentation.homEquiv`) applied to the
assignment of 2.1, whose range is the closure of the `τᵢ` by `PresentedGroup.closure_range_of`,
hence the carrier; corestrict to the carrier. *Needs:* 2.1, Mathlib `PresentedGroup.toGroup`,
`PresentedGroup.closure_range_of`, Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GroupPresentation.homEquiv`.

From a surjection and the Layer 1 order, derive that `TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group` is
nontrivial and that `0 < Nat.card (TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group)` implies
`N ≤ Nat.card (TauCeti.SporadicName.<Name>.Group)`. The positivity hypothesis is needed because a
surjection gives no finiteness: with Mathlib's junk value an infinite presented group has
`Nat.card` equal to `0`.

**Checks.**

- A surjection onto the carrier alone does not give an isomorphism or finiteness: the free group
  on the presentation's generators also maps onto the carrier. The upper bound that closes the gap
  is CFSGBasicProperties P0, item 2, not a target here.
- The lower bound for `M11` is `7920 ≤ Nat.card`; were the presented group trivial (a relator
  transcribed as a single generator, say) the surjection onto a group of order 7920 could not
  exist.
- Without the positivity hypothesis the bound `N ≤ Nat.card` would assert that the presented group
  is finite, which a surjection cannot show.

### Examples

- `M24`: `τ = (a, b⁻¹)` satisfies the seven relators of `M24G1-P1` and generates `M24`, so
  `PresentedGroup.toGroup` gives the surjection and the presented group has at least 244823040
  elements once it is known to be finite.

### Dependencies

Layer 1 (the carriers, and their orders for the corollaries). Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.SporadicName.presentation`, `TauCeti.SporadicName.Group`,
`TauCeti.GroupPresentation.homEquiv`, `TauCeti.Relator.eval` (CFSGStatement S0–S1). Mathlib

## Downstream consumers

[CFSGBasicProperties](../CFSGBasicProperties/README.md), P1, consumes the ten carriers, their
orders and the O2 surjections, and adds the upper bounds on the presented groups that turn the
surjections into isomorphisms and give `Nat.card` of the CFSGStatement group itself. Its P0 uses
the Layer 0 order and membership theorems for finite permutation groups. The same roadmap owns the
other sporadic groups: the permutation models of `He`, `Suz`, `Co2` and `Fi22` in its P1, and in
its P2 the finite models (permutation, matrix and lattice) and the recognition of the remaining
twelve names.
