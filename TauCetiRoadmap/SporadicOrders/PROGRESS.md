## SporadicOrders: 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-09 (`25a4576` to `d92acc5`)

The general theory this roadmap rests on landed in full. The [stabilizer-chain order formula](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Stabilizer/Chain.html#Subgroup.prod_ncard_orbit_mul_card_stabilizerChain) says the order of a permutation group is the product of its basic-orbit lengths times the order of the last pointwise stabilizer, with no finiteness hypothesis. Alongside it come the [membership criterion along a base](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Stabilizer/Chain.html#Subgroup.mem_iff_exists_prod_eq), which decides membership by sifting through orbit representatives, and the description of [the orbit of a generated group](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Orbit/Closure.html#MulAction.isLeast_orbit_closure) as the least set closed under the generators and their inverses. When the type is finite, the generators alone are enough.

Four of the ten ATLAS models now have their orders: [M₁₁](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Mathieu/Eleven.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_M11), [M₁₂](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Mathieu/Twelve.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_M12), [M₂₂](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Mathieu/TwentyTwo.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_M22) and [Higman–Sims](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/HigmanSims/Order.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_HS), the last of order 44352000. The Mathieu orders go through a reusable [order theorem for sifting](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/GroupAction/Stabilizer/Sift.html#Subgroup.natCard_eq_prod_length_of_siftCheck). If lists of stabilizer-chain elements absorb each other's products under sifting, the group's order is the product of the list lengths, and every hypothesis is a finite check that `decide` runs. Higman–Sims was done one basic orbit at a time instead. McLaughlin has its first two basic orbits, 275 and 112 (TauCeti#26359).

On the presentation side, the presented groups of M₁₁, M₁₂, [M₂₄](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/CFSG/Sporadic/Mathieu/TwentyFour/PermutationModel.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.exists_surjective_M24), J₁, Higman–Sims and McLaughlin now map onto their models. In each case the transcribed relators hold of permutations that generate the model, so none of these presentations collapses to the trivial group. Where the order is known, this becomes a lower bound: the presented M₂₂ and Higman–Sims groups, if finite, are at least as large as their models. M₂₃ and Co₃ have no surjection yet.

## SporadicOrders: 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-19 (`d92acc5` to `b235000`)

Three more models now have their ATLAS orders. The model of the [first Janko group](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/CFSG/Sporadic/Janko/One/Order.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_J1) has order 175560 = 266 · 110 · 6, [the second Janko group](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Janko/Two/Order.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_J2) has order 604800 = 100 · 63 · 24 · 4, and [M₂₄](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/CFSG/Sporadic/Mathieu/TwentyFour/Order.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.card_M24) has order 244823040, from its seven basic orbits 24, 23, 22, 21, 20, 16 and 3. Each order is the product of the basic-orbit lengths along a proved base. Each also comes with its size corollary: the presented group, if finite, is at least as large as the model, as in [the bound for M₂₄](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/CFSG/Sporadic/Mathieu/TwentyFour/Order.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.le_natCard_group_M24). Part of the groundwork, the first basic orbits of J₁ and J₂, landed earlier and is recorded here late.

The two largest models moved forward without being finished. For McLaughlin, the points 0 to 5 [form a base](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/McLaughlin/Orbits.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.McL.stabilizerChain_six_eq_bot), and the five-point stabilizer [has order two](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/McLaughlin/Orbits.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.McL.card_fixing_zero_one_two_three_four). The order itself still needs the fifth basic orbit, of length 5. The degree-276 [model of Co₃](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Conway/Three/Basic.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.Co3) is now defined and [2-transitive](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Conway/Three/Orbits.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.Co3.instIsMultiplyPretransitive). Its first three basic orbits [contribute 276 · 275 · 162](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/SpecificGroups/Sporadic/Conway/Three/Orbits.html#TauCeti.Sporadic.Co3.card_eq_12295800_mul_card_stabilizerChain) to the order, and the orbits 56, 45 and 16 remain.

There is also a second way to prove that points form a base, besides sifting. A [rigidity lemma](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Logic/Function/Rigidity.html#Function.apply_eq_self_of_rank) says that a relation-preserving map fixing some seed points fixes every point that is determined by its relations to points of lower rank. It is used together with invariants such as [relation profiles](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/InvariantRelation.html#TauCeti.relationProfile) relative to fixed anchors. This is how the McLaughlin and J₂ bases were proved (TauCeti#35702).
