# Roadmap: error guarantees for markov chain monte carlo on finite state spaces

**Summary.** A Markov chain Monte Carlo estimate of a mean `E_π f` is an ergodic average
`(1/n) Σ_{j<n} f(X_{B+j})` along a chain whose stationary law is `π`, after a burn-in of `B`
steps. This roadmap gives error guarantees for such averages when the state space is a finite
set; chains on infinite or continuous state spaces are not treated. A chain is a Markov kernel
`κ` on a finite type with transition matrix `P`, `π` is a stationary probability, `ν` is any
initial law, and `f` is a real function on the states. Everything rests on the Poisson equation
`g − P g = f − E_π f`, its solution `g = Z f` through the fundamental matrix `Z`, and the
asymptotic variance `σ²(f)`.

Headline theorems:

- **Poisson martingale.** For every finite chain, every initial law and every `g`, the increments
  `D_j = g(X_{j+1}) − (P g)(X_j)` are martingale differences with conditional variance
  `P(g²)(X_j) − (P g)(X_j)²`. When `g − P g = f − m`, telescoping gives
  `Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − m) = Σ_{j<n} D_j + g(X_0) − g(X_n)` on every path.
- **Mean-square error after burn-in.** If the mixing time satisfies `t_mix(1/4) ≤ T`, then
  `Z f` solves the Poisson equation and oscillates by at most `2T` times the oscillation of `f`,
  without any irreducibility hypothesis; and if `g` is a solution with oscillation at most `c`,
  then from every initial law
  `E[(Σ_{j<n} (f(X_{B+j}) − E_π f))²] ≤ (√(n σ²(f) + T c²/(2 · 2^⌊B/T⌋)) + c)²`, with leading
  term `n σ²(f)`. The chain need not be reversible.
- **Hoeffding's inequality.** For every finite chain and every initial law, if
  `g − P g = f − m` and `g` oscillates by at most `c`, then
  `P(Σ_{j<n} (f(X_{B+j}) − m) ≥ t) ≤ exp(−2(t − c)²/(n c²))` for every `t ≥ c`.
- **Output analysis.** For an irreducible chain, periodic or not, and from every initial law,
  the batch-means estimator with `a → ∞` batches of length `b → ∞` converges in probability to
  `σ²(f)`; if `σ²(f) > 0`, the interval `±z √(σ̂²/n)` around the average covers `E_π f` with
  probability tending to the standard Gaussian mass of `[−z, z]`; and for `f` with values in `ℝ^k`
  the asymptotic covariance matrix is positive semidefinite and the normalised sums converge in
  law to the centred Gaussian with that covariance.

It is built on five roadmaps: MarkovChainMixing (distance to stationarity, mixing time, spectral
gap, ergodic theorem, and a Chebyshev bound for time averages of reversible chains),
MCMCAsymptoticVariance (fundamental matrix, Poisson equation, asymptotic variance),
MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem (the central limit theorem for finite irreducible chains),
MarkovChainsAndRenewal (the total variation formula behind every bias estimate) and
MathematicalStatisticsFoundations (convergence in probability).

Layer 1 below is the Poisson martingale and the size of the Poisson solution; Layer 2 the bounds at
a fixed sample size; Layer 3 batch means, the confidence interval and the multivariate central
limit theorem.

## Conventions

- **State spaces.** `S` is a finite type with a σ-algebra in which singletons are measurable
  (`[Fintype S] [MeasurableSpace S] [MeasurableSingletonClass S]`), with `[DecidableEq S]` where
  the fundamental matrix occurs and `[Nonempty S]` where a maximum over states is taken.
- **Chains.** A chain is a kernel `κ : Kernel S S` with `[IsMarkovKernel κ]`; `π` and `ν` are
  probability measures on `S`. Stationarity is Mathlib's `κ.Invariant π`; irreducibility is
  Mathlib's `Kernel.IsIrreducible Measure.count κ` (every state reaches every state; a chain on one
  state is irreducible), as in MarkovChainMixing. No statement assumes reversibility or
  aperiodicity.
- **Path law and filtration.** The chain started from `ν` is Tau Ceti's `markovChainLaw ν κ` on
  `ℕ → S` with the product σ-algebra, and `X_j = ω j`. The filtration is Mathlib's
  `Filtration.piLE`, generated by the coordinates up to time `j`. Every probability and
  expectation is under `markovChainLaw ν κ` unless a statement says otherwise.
- **Matrices and functions.** `P = transitionMatrix κ` with `P x y = κ x {y}` acts by
  `(P g) x = Σ_y P x y · g y`; `Π = stationaryMatrix π` has every row equal to `π`;
  `Z = fundamentalMatrix κ π = (1 − P + Π)⁻¹ − Π`, with Mathlib's inverse, which is `0` at a
  singular matrix. `E_π f = ∫ f ∂π = Σ_y π {y} f y`, `f̄ = f − E_π f`, `Var_π f = Var[f; π]`, and
  `σ²(f) = asymptoticVariance κ π f = 2 ⟨f̄, Z f̄⟩_π − ⟨f̄, f̄⟩_π`. For a solution `g` of a Poisson
  equation, `h = P(g²) − (P g)²`; `h x` is the variance of `g` under `κ x`.
- **Distances.** `d(t) = tvToStationary κ π t`, the largest over `x` of
  `tvDist (κ^t(x, ·)) π / 2`, and `t_mix(ε) = mixingTime κ π ε` in `ℕ∞`; `d̄(t)` is the
  largest distance between two rows of `κ^t` (MarkovChainMixing 1.2, used in routes only).
  `γ = spectralGap κ π` is the spectral gap of MarkovChainMixing 2.4, `0` when no function has
  nonzero variance.
- **Oscillation.** A bound on the oscillation of `g` is written as the hypothesis
  `∀ x y, |g x − g y| ≤ c`; no oscillation function is defined (Mathlib's `oscillation` is the
  oscillation of a function at a point of a topological space, a different object).
- **Copied definitions.** `tvToStationary`, `mixingTime`, `transitionMatrix`, `dirichletForm`
  and `spectralGap` are copied verbatim from MarkovChainMixing (1.2, 1.3, 2.1, 2.4), and
  `stationaryMatrix`, `fundamentalMatrix` and `asymptoticVariance` from MCMCAsymptoticVariance
  (1.1, 2.1), because a roadmap's file cannot import another's.
- **Junk values.** Real division by zero is `0`, natural division by zero is `0`, and an infimum
  over an empty index type is `0`. Each statement says what this gives in its degenerate cases.

## Layer 1: the Poisson martingale and the Poisson solution

### 1.1 The Poisson martingale

Define `poissonIncrement κ g ω j = g(ω (j + 1)) − (P g)(ω j)` (written `D_j`). If
`g − P g = f − m` for a real `m`, then on every path and for all `B`, `n`,

    Σ_{j<n} (f(ω (B + j)) − m) = Σ_{j<n} D_{B+j} + g(ω B) − g(ω (B + n)),

by writing `f − m = g − P g` and telescoping `g(ω (B + j)) − g(ω (B + j + 1))`; this is the
decomposition every target below uses. Prove `martingale_sum_poissonIncrement`: for every Markov
kernel `κ` on a finite type, every initial probability `ν` and every `g : S → ℝ` (no
stationarity, irreducibility or reversibility),

- `n ↦ Σ_{j<n} D_j` is a martingale for `Filtration.piLE` under `markovChainLaw ν κ`;
- for every `n`, Mathlib's martingale part of the process `n ↦ g(X_n)` (the martingale of its
  Doob decomposition) is almost surely `g(X_0) + Σ_{j<n} D_j`;
- `E[D_j² | piLE j] = P(g²)(X_j) − (P g)(X_j)²` almost surely, for every `j`.

Consequences used below, each one line from these: `E D_j = 0`; `E[D_i D_j] = 0` for `i ≠ j`;
`E D_j² = E h(X_j) = (ν κ^j) h`; `|D_j| ≤ c` when `g` oscillates by at most `c`, because
`(P g)(x)` is an average of values of `g`; and `0 ≤ h ≤ c²/4`, `h x` being the variance under
`κ x` of a function with values in an interval of length `c` (Mathlib `variance_le_sq_of_bounded`).

Route. Fix `j`. The function `F ω' = g(ω' 1)` of the path shifted by `j` is `g(X_{j+1})`, and by
Tau Ceti's Markov property `Measure.condExp_shift_iterate_markovChainLaw`,
`E[g(X_{j+1}) | piLE j] = ∫ F d(markovChainLaw (dirac X_j) κ)`, which is `(P g)(X_j)` by
`Measure.markovChainLaw_map_eval` at time `1` and `Kernel.coe_toMatrix_apply`. The same with `g²`
gives `E[g(X_{j+1})² | piLE j] = P(g²)(X_j)`, and expanding `D_j²` gives the conditional
variance. `D_j` depends on the coordinates `j` and `j + 1` only, so the sums are adapted, and every
function of finitely many coordinates is bounded and measurable, hence integrable. Mathlib's
`martingalePart` of a process `Y` is `Y_n − Σ_{k<n} E[Y_{k+1} − Y_k | piLE k]`; with
`Y_k = g(X_k)` the conditional expectation is `(P g)(X_k) − g(X_k)`, and the sum telescopes to
`g(X_0) + Σ_{k<n} D_k`. (The decomposition is the one used inside the proofs of
MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.2 and MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem 4.1, which do not state it; proved
here.)

**Checks.**

- `n = 0`: both sides of the decomposition are `0`, and the martingale part at `0` is `g(X_0)`.
- One state, or `g` constant: `D_j = 0` and `h = 0`.
- The two-state chain with `κ(a, b) = 1/3`, `κ(b, a) = 1/2` and `g = (12/25, −18/25)`:
  `P g = (2/25, −3/25)` and `h = (2/9) · (6/5)² = 8/25` at `a`, `(1/4) · (6/5)² = 9/25` at `b`.
  The martingale part is not `g(X_n)` itself: started at `a`, `E g(X_1) = 2/25 ≠ g(a)`.
- A reducible, periodic chain: for two disjoint copies of the exchange chain, `(P g)(x)` is the
  value of `g` at the partner of `x`, the next state is that partner, and `D_j = 0` and `h = 0`
  for every `g`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Filtration.piLE`, `Martingale`, `martingalePart`, `condExp`,
`variance_le_sq_of_bounded`; Tau Ceti `markovChainLaw`,
`Measure.condExp_shift_iterate_markovChainLaw`, `Measure.markovChainLaw_map_eval`,
`Kernel.coe_toMatrix_apply`; MarkovChainMixing 2.1 (`transitionMatrix`).

### 1.2 The Poisson solution under a mixing-time bound

Prove `fundamentalMatrix_of_mixingTime_le`: let `π` be stationary, `T : ℕ` with
`t_mix(1/4) ≤ T`, and `|f x − f y| ≤ c` for all states. Then

- `1 − P + Π` is invertible, and `Σ_t (P^t − Π)` converges to `Z`;
- `g = Z f` solves `g − P g = f − E_π f`;
- `|g x| ≤ 2 T c` and `|g x − g y| ≤ 2 T c` for all states;
- `σ²(f) = Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²)`.

Neither irreducibility nor aperiodicity is assumed: a chain with transient states and one
closed class can satisfy the hypothesis, and so can the Glauber dynamics for colourings of
MarkovChainMixing 6.2, which runs on all colourings and is not irreducible when the graph has an
edge.

Route. The facts of MarkovChainMixing used are `MarkovChainMixing.mixingTime_le_iff` (`d(T) ≤ 1/4`),
`MarkovChainMixing.tvToStationary_add_le` (`d(s + t) ≤ d(s) d̄(t)` for stationary `π`) and
`tvBetweenStarts_add_le` (`d̄(s + t) ≤ d̄(s) d̄(t)`), and three facts of its 1.2 that carry no Lean
name, proved here in one line each from MarkovChainsAndRenewal 3.5
(`tvDist μ ν = Σ_y |μ {y} − ν {y}|`, a README item there; on a finite type it follows from
Mathlib's `etvDist_eq_iSup_finPartition_enorm`, since the partition into singletons attains the
supremum and every part `A` has `|μ A − ν A| ≤ Σ_{y ∈ A} |μ {y} − ν {y}|`): `d` is
nonincreasing, because
`κ^{t+1}(x, ·) − π = Σ_z κ(x, z) (κ^t(z, ·) − π)`; `d̄(t) ≤ 2 d(t)`, by the triangle inequality
through `π`; and for probability measures `μ`, `μ'` and a function `u` with oscillation at most `c`,
`|μ u − μ' u| ≤ c · tvDist μ μ' / 2`, by subtracting the midpoint of the range of `u` (the positive
and negative parts of `μ − μ'` have equal mass `tvDist μ μ' / 2`). Taking `u` an indicator,
`|(P^t − Π) x y| ≤ d(t)`.

1. If `T = 0`, then `d(0) = max_x (1 − π {x}) ≤ 1/4`, so `S` has one state, `P = Π = 1`, `Z = 0`,
   `f − E_π f = 0`, and every clause holds. Let `T ≥ 1`.
2. `d̄(T) ≤ 2 d(T) ≤ 1/2`, so `d((ℓ + 1) T) ≤ d(ℓ T)/2` and `d(ℓ T) ≤ 2^{−ℓ−1}` for `ℓ ≥ 1`;
   likewise `d̄(ℓ T) ≤ 2^{−ℓ}`. As `d` and `d̄` are nonincreasing and bounded by `1`, summing over
   blocks of length `T` gives `Σ_t d(t) ≤ T (1 + 1/2) = 3T/2` and `Σ_t d̄(t) ≤ 2T`.
3. `P Π = Π` (rows of `P` sum to `1`), `Π P = Π` (stationarity) and `Π Π = Π`, so
   `(P − Π)^t = P^t − Π` for `t ≥ 1`. The entries of `P^t − Π` are at most `d(t)`, so the series
   `W = Σ_t (P^t − Π)` converges absolutely. With `A = P − Π`,
   `(1 − A)(Π + Σ_{t ≤ N} (P^t − Π)) = (1 − A) Σ_{t ≤ N} A^t = 1 − A^{N+1} = 1 − P^{N+1} + Π`,
   which tends to `1`. So `1 − P + Π` has the inverse `W + Π`, and `Z = W`.
4. `Π W = 0` term by term, so `(1 − P) Z = (1 − P + Π) Z = (1 − P + Π)(Z + Π) − (1 − P + Π) Π
   = 1 − Π`, and `g − P g = f − Π f = f − E_π f`.
5. `g x = Σ_t ((κ^t(x, ·) − π) f)`, each term at most `c d(t)` in absolute value, so
   `|g x| ≤ 3Tc/2 ≤ 2Tc`; and `g x − g y = Σ_t ((κ^t(x, ·) − κ^t(y, ·)) f)`, each term at most
   `c d̄(t)`, so `|g x − g y| ≤ 2Tc`.
6. `Z 1 = W 1 = 0`, so `Z f̄ = Z f = g` and `f̄ = g − P g`. With `⟨u, v⟩ = Σ_x π {x} u x v x`,
   `σ²(f) = 2 ⟨g − P g, g⟩ − ⟨g − P g, g − P g⟩ = ⟨g, g⟩ − ⟨P g, P g⟩`, by expanding.

(The series for `Z` is the definition of the fundamental matrix in Aldous–Fill, Chapter 2,
(2.6), as quoted in MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.2; the bounds by the mixing time and the statement
without irreducibility are proved here.)

**Checks.**

- The two-state chain with `p = κ(a, b) = 1/3`, `q = κ(b, a) = 1/2` and `f` the indicator of `a`:
  `d(t) = (3/5)(1/6)^t`, so `T = 1`; `Z f = (12/25, −18/25)` oscillates by `6/5 ≤ 2`, and
  `σ²(f) = 42/125` from both formulas.
- Equality in the oscillation bound: the lazy walk on the `4`-cycle (MarkovChainMixing 4.3 with
  `p = 1/2`) has `T = 1`, and for `f = (1, 0, −1, 0)` around the cycle, `Z f = (2, 0, −2, 0)`
  oscillates by `4 = 2 · 1 · 2`.
- A transient state: on `{a, b, c}` with rows `(1/2, 1/2, 0)`, `(1/2, 1/2, 0)`,
  `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)` and `π = (1/2, 1/2, 0)`, `T = 2`, `1 − P + Π` is invertible, and for
  `f = (1, 0, 5)`, `Z f = (1/2, −1/2, 27/4)` solves the Poisson equation and oscillates by
  `29/4 ≤ 2 · 2 · 5`.
- The hypothesis excludes what it must: for the exchange chain `d(t) = 1/2` for every `t`, and
  for the identity kernel on two states with `π` uniform, `d(t) = 1/2` while `1 − P + Π = Π` is
  singular and `Z` is the junk value `−Π`.
- One state: `T = 0`, `Z = 0`.
- Stationarity is needed: on three states with rows `(1/4, 1/4, 1/2)`, `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)`,
  `(1/3, 1/3, 1/3)` and `π = (1/4, 1/4, 1/2)`, which is not stationary (the stationary law is
  `(4/13, 4/13, 5/13)`), `d(0) = 3/4` and `d(1) = 1/6`, so `T = 1`; `1 − P + Π` is invertible, but
  for `f` the indicator of the first state `Z f = (3/4, −9/52, −9/52)` and
  `Z f − P Z f = f − E_π f − 3/52`.

*Needs:* MarkovChainMixing 1.2 (`tvToStationary`, `MarkovChainMixing.tvToStationary_add_le`,
`tvBetweenStarts_add_le`), 1.3 (`mixingTime`, `MarkovChainMixing.mixingTime_le_iff`), 2.1
(`transitionMatrix`); MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.1 (`stationaryMatrix`, `fundamentalMatrix`), 2.1
(`asymptoticVariance`); MarkovChainsAndRenewal 3.5; Mathlib `etvDist_eq_iSup_finPartition_enorm`,
`Matrix.inv`, `Matrix.mul_nonsing_inv`,
`HasSum`, `Kernel.Invariant`, `integral_fintype`.

### 1.3 The Poisson solution and the spectral gap

Prove `variance_le_of_poisson`: if `π` is stationary and `g − P g = f − E_π f`, then

    γ² Var_π g ≤ Var_π f,   and   π {x} (g x − E_π g)² γ² ≤ Var_π f  for every state x.

No irreducibility, aperiodicity or reversibility is assumed, and `g` is any solution. For a
positive `π` this gives `|g x − g y| ≤ 2 √(Var_π f) / (γ √π_min)` when `γ > 0`. With
MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3 (`poisson_iff`) it bounds `Z f` for every irreducible chain, periodic
ones included, where the mixing-time hypothesis of 1.2 fails.

Route. For a stationary `π`, `E(g) = ⟨g, g⟩_π − ⟨g, P g⟩_π` (MarkovChainMixing 2.4, which states
this for a stationary `π`; it follows from `Σ_x π {x} κ(x, {y}) = π {y}` by expanding the
square). So `E(g) = ⟨g, f̄⟩_π = ⟨g − E_π g, f̄⟩_π ≤ √(Var_π g · Var_π f)` by Cauchy–Schwarz,
since `f̄` has mean `0`. The Poincaré inequality `γ Var_π g ≤ E(g)` (MarkovChainMixing 2.4) holds
by the definition of `γ` as an infimum of nonnegative ratios when `Var_π g ≠ 0`, and trivially
otherwise. With `γ ≥ 0`, squaring `γ Var_π g ≤ √(Var_π g · Var_π f)` gives the first claim, and
`π {x} (g x − E_π g)² ≤ Var_π g` gives the second. (The same two inequalities enter the route of
MCMCAsymptoticVariance 3.2, which bounds `σ²(f)` and not `Var_π g`; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- Equality for two states: with `κ(a, b) = p`, `κ(b, a) = q`, `γ = p + q` and `f̄` is an
  eigenfunction for `1 − p − q`, so `g = f̄/(p + q)` up to a constant and `γ² Var_π g = Var_π f`.
  For `p = 1/3`, `q = 1/2`: `Var_π g = 216/625`, `γ² = 25/36`, `Var_π f = 6/25`.
- A periodic chain: the exchange chain has `γ = 2`, `Z f = (1/4, −1/4)` for the indicator of `a`,
  and `4 · 1/16 = 1/4 = Var_π f`.
- A chain that is not reversible: the cycle `x ↦ x + 1` on three states has `γ = 3/2`
  (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 3.2, Checks), and `(9/4) Var_π (Z f) ≤ Var_π f` for every `f`; for the
  indicator of one state, `Z f = (1/3, −1/3, 0)` has variance `2/27`, and
  `(9/4)(2/27) = 1/6 ≤ 2/9`.
- `γ = 0` (one state, or the identity kernel on two states with `π` uniform): both claims read
  `0 ≤ Var_π f`.
- Stationarity is needed: for the chain and the non-stationary `π = (1/4, 1/4, 1/2)` of the last
  Check of 1.2 and `f = (−1, 1, 0)`, `P f = 0`, so `g = f` solves `g − P g = f − E_π f`;
  `Var_π g = Var_π f = 1/2`, while `γ = 25/24` (the least root of `det(E − l V) = 0` for the
  matrices of the quadratic forms `E` and `Var_π` on the functions vanishing at the third state),
  and `γ² Var_π g = 625/1152 > 1/2`.

*Needs:* MarkovChainMixing 2.4 (`dirichletForm`, `spectralGap`, the form of `E` for a stationary
law, the Poincaré inequality); Mathlib `variance`, `Kernel.Invariant`, `integral_fintype`.

## Layer 2: non-asymptotic guarantees from every initial law

### 2.1 Bias and mean-square error after burn-in

Prove `integral_sum_sub_sq_le`: let `π` be stationary, `t_mix(1/4) ≤ T`, `ν` any initial
probability, `g` a solution of `g − P g = f − E_π f` with `|g x − g y| ≤ c`, and
`S = Σ_{j<n} (f(X_{B+j}) − E_π f)`. Then

    |E S| ≤ 2 c d(B),
    E S² ≤ (√(n σ²(f) + T c² / (2 · 2^⌊B/T⌋)) + c)²,

where `⌊B/T⌋` is natural division (`0` when `T = 0`). For a fixed chain and `f`, the mean-square
error of the average is therefore at most `σ²(f)/n + O(n^{−3/2})` as `n → ∞`; for an irreducible
chain the leading term is the exact rate, `n⁻¹ Var S → σ²(f)` (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.2).
Reversibility is not assumed.

Route. By 1.2, `Z f` solves the Poisson equation; `u = g − Z f` has `P u = u`, so `u = P^t u → Π u`
and `u` is constant; adding a constant to `g` changes `Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²)` by
`2 u ⟨1, g − P g⟩_π = 2 u E_π f̄ = 0`. So `σ²(f) = Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²)` by 1.2, and this is
`E_π h = E_π P(g²) − E_π (P g)²` because `E_π P(g²) = E_π g²` by stationarity. By 1.1, `S = M + R`
with `M = Σ_{j<n} D_{B+j}` and `R = g(X_B) − g(X_{B+n})`, `|R| ≤ c`.

- Bias: `E M = 0`, and `E g(X_t) − E_π g = (ν κ^t − π) g` (Tau Ceti `markovChainLaw_map_eval`) is
  at most `c d(t)` in absolute value (the oscillation bound of 1.2's route, applied row by row),
  so `|E S| ≤ c d(B) + c d(B + n) ≤ 2 c d(B)`, `d` being nonincreasing.
- Variance of `M`: `E M² = Σ_{j<n} (ν κ^{B+j}) h` by 1.1. Since `0 ≤ h ≤ c²/4`,
  `(ν κ^t) h ≤ E_π h + (c²/4) d(t)`, so `E M² ≤ n σ²(f) + (c²/4) Σ_{t ≥ B} d(t)`. If `T = 0` the
  space has one state and `S = 0`. Otherwise, with `q = ⌊B/T⌋`, step 2 of 1.2 gives
  `Σ_{t ≥ B} d(t) ≤ T Σ_{ℓ ≥ q} d(ℓ T) ≤ 2T · 2^{−q}` (`3T/2` for `q = 0`, `T 2^{−q}` for
  `q ≥ 1`), and `(c²/4) · 2T · 2^{−q} = T c²/(2 · 2^q)`.
- Minkowski's inequality in `L²`: `√(E S²) ≤ √(E M²) + c`.

(Proved here. For reversible chains Rudolf proves bounds of the form
`2 ‖f‖²/(n(1 − β₁)) + O(n⁻²)`, with the burn-in measured through the density of the initial law
with respect to `π`: Theorem 2.20, (2.13)–(2.14), p. 24, on finite state spaces, and Theorem 3.34,
p. 56, in general. The leading constant `σ²(f)`, the burn-in measured by the mixing time and the
form without reversibility are proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `t_mix(1/4) ≤ T` cannot be dropped, even with `T = 0`: on `{0, 1, 2}` let `0` move to `1` or
  `2` with probability `1/2` each and let `1`, `2` exchange; `π = (0, 1/2, 1/2)` is stationary,
  `g = (1/2, 1/2, −1/2)` solves the Poisson equation for `f = (1/2, 1, −1)`, `E_π f = 0`, `c = 1`
  and `σ²(f) = 0`. From `0` with `B = 0`, `n = 2`, `E S² = ((3/2)² + (1/2)²)/2 = 5/4`, above the

### 2.2 Hoeffding's inequality

Prove `measureReal_le_sum_sub_le`: for every Markov kernel on a finite type, every initial
probability `ν`, every real `m`, every solution `g` of `g − P g = f − m` with
`|g x − g y| ≤ c`, all `B`, `n` and every `t ≥ c`,

    P(Σ_{j<n} (f(X_{B+j}) − m) ≥ t) ≤ exp(−2 (t − c)² / (n c²)).

At `n = 0` or `c = 0` the right side is `exp 0 = 1`. Applied to `−f`, `−g`, `−m`, it gives the
same bound for the lower tail, so `P(|Σ_{j<n} (f(X_{B+j}) − m)| ≥ t) ≤ 2 exp(−2(t − c)²/(n c²))`.
No stationarity, irreducibility, aperiodicity or reversibility is assumed; the chain enters only
through `g`, and 1.2 and 1.3 supply `c`.

Route. By `Measure.markovChainLaw_map_shift_iterate` the shifted path has law
`markovChainLaw (κ^B ∘ ν) κ`, so let `B = 0`. If `n = 0`, `c = 0` or `t = c` the bound is `1`;
otherwise `c > 0`. By 1.1, `Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − m) = M_n + g(X_0) − g(X_n) ≤ M_n + c`, so the event
lies in `{M_n ≥ t − c}`. For `s > 0`, the Markov property (as in 1.1) gives
`E[e^{s D_j} | piLE j] = Σ_y κ(X_j, {y}) e^{s (g y − (P g)(X_j))}`, the moment generating function
under `κ x` of a function of mean `0` with values in an interval of length `c`; Hoeffding's lemma
(Mathlib `mgf_le_of_mem_Icc_of_integral_eq_zero`) bounds it by `e^{s² c²/8}`. By induction on
`n`, conditioning on `piLE (n − 1)`, `E e^{s M_n} ≤ e^{n s² c²/8}`. Chernoff's bound
(Mathlib `measure_ge_le_exp_mul_mgf`) gives `P(M_n ≥ t − c) ≤ e^{−s(t − c) + n s² c²/8}`, and
`s = 4(t − c)/(n c²)` gives `exp(−2(t − c)²/(n c²))`. (Proved here. Glynn and Ormoneit proved a
Hoeffding inequality for uniformly ergodic chains; Fan–Jiang–Sun, Section 1, p. 4, place it among
the bounds through minorization and drift conditions.)

**Remark (sample size).** Let `π` be stationary, `t_mix(1/4) ≤ T` with `T ≥ 1`, and let `f`
oscillate by at most `c_f > 0`. With `c = 2T c_f` from 1.2 and `t = nη`, for `nη ≥ 2T c_f`,
`P(|(1/n) Σ_{j<n} f(X_{B+j}) − E_π f| ≥ η) ≤ 2 exp(−(nη − 2T c_f)²/(2n T² c_f²))`, from every
initial law and after any burn-in. In particular `n ≥ max(4T c_f/η, 8 T² c_f² log(2/δ)/η²)`
gives probability at most `δ`, for `0 < δ ≤ 1`: then `nη − 2T c_f ≥ nη/2`. For reversible chains
MarkovChainMixing 6.4 asks for `n ≥ 4 Var_π f/(η² δ γ)` steps after a burn-in of `t_mix(δ/2)`,
through Chebyshev's inequality; its dependence on `δ` is `1/δ`, against `log(2/δ)` here.

**Checks.**

- `t = c` and `n = 0`: the right side is `1`.
- The oscillation of `g`, not of `f`, is the right constant. For the two-state chain with
  `p = 3/10`, `q = 1/10`, `f` the indicator of `a`, `m = E_π f = 1/4` and the start at `a`,
  `Z f = (15/8, −5/8)` oscillates by `5/2`. With `n = 20` and `t = 5`, the probability
  `P(Σ_{j<20} (f(X_j) − 1/4) ≥ 5)` is `1165973576739823103/5000000000000000000`, more than
  `0.233`, while the bound with `c = 1`, the oscillation of `f`, would be `e^{−8/5} < 0.202`; with
  `c = 5/2` the bound is `e^{−1/10}`.
- A reducible, periodic chain: two disjoint copies of the exchange chain, `f = (1, −1, 0, 0)`,
  `m = 0`, `g = (1/2, −1/2, 0, 0)`, `c = 1`: the sums take the values `−1, 0, 1` only, so the
  probability is `0` for `t > 1`, within the bound.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `mgf_le_of_mem_Icc_of_integral_eq_zero`, `measure_ge_le_exp_mul_mgf`;
Tau Ceti `Measure.markovChainLaw_map_shift_iterate`, `Measure.condExp_shift_iterate_markovChainLaw`.
The remark uses 1.2 and MarkovChainMixing 6.4.

## Layer 3: asymptotic inference

**Remark (the central limit theorem with `σ²(f)`).** For an irreducible chain with stationary law
`π`, every initial law and every `f`, `n^{−1/2} Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − E_π f)` converges in law to
`N(0, σ²(f))`. Route: `g = Z f` solves the Poisson equation (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3,
`poisson_iff`, with the constant `0`); in the notation of MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem 4.1,
`(P g)(x) = Σ_y κ x {y} g y` (Tau Ceti `Kernel.coe_toMatrix_apply`) and `E_π f = Σ_y π {y} f y`
(Mathlib `integral_fintype`), so its hypothesis holds, and its limit variance
`Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²)` is `σ²(f)` by MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.1
(`MCMCAsymptoticVariance.asymptoticVariance_eq_of_poisson`). Periodic chains are allowed. This
combination of the two suppliers is used in 3.2 and 3.3 and is not stated separately.

### 3.1 Consistency of batch means

Define `batchMeansVariance f a b ω` (written `σ̂²`): with the batch means
`Y_k = b⁻¹ Σ_{i<b} f(ω (k b + i))` for `k < a` and their mean `Ȳ`,
`σ̂² = b/(a − 1) · Σ_{k<a} (Y_k − Ȳ)²` (Flegal–Jones, Section 3, (6), p. 7). The batch mean `Y_k`
is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Probability.blockAverage` of the process `j ↦ f(ω j)` over the selection
`i ↦ k b + i` of `Fin b`, also for `b = 0`, where both are `0`. The estimator is nonnegative,
and it is `0` for `a ≤ 1` (for `a = 1` through `b/0 = 0`, for `a = 0` through the empty sum) and
for `b = 0`.

Prove `tendstoInMeasure_batchMeansVariance`: for an irreducible chain with stationary law `π`,
every initial probability `ν`, every `f`, and sequences `a m → ∞`, `b m → ∞`, `σ̂²` with `a m`
batches of length `b m` converges in probability to `σ²(f)` under `markovChainLaw ν κ`.
Aperiodicity is not assumed.

Route. Let `g = Z f` (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3), `c` its oscillation, `D_j` and `h` as in 1.1,
`n = a b`, and take `m` large, so that `a ≥ 2`, `b ≥ 1`. With the centred batch sums
`B_k = Σ_{i<b} (f(X_{kb+i}) − E_π f)` and `S_n = Σ_k B_k`,

    σ̂² = (a/(a − 1)) · n⁻¹ Σ_k B_k²  −  (a − 1)⁻¹ · S_n²/n.

1. `S_n²/n` is bounded in probability in the sense of MathematicalStatisticsFoundations 8.2:
   by 1.1, `E S_n² ≤ (√n c/2 + c)²`, so `E S_n²/n ≤ 9c²/4` and Markov's inequality applies. As
   `(a − 1)⁻¹ → 0`, the second term tends to `0` in probability
   (MathematicalStatisticsFoundations 8.3, `tendstoInMeasure_mul_zero_of_isBoundedInProbability`).
2. By 1.1 within each batch, `B_k = M_k + r_k` with `M_k = Σ_{i<b} D_{kb+i}` and
   `r_k = g(X_{kb}) − g(X_{(k+1)b})`, `|r_k| ≤ c`. Then `n⁻¹ Σ_k r_k² ≤ c²/b`, and
   `E |n⁻¹ Σ_k M_k r_k| ≤ n⁻¹ Σ_k c √(E M_k²) ≤ c²/(2√b)`, since `E M_k² ≤ b c²/4`.
3. `Σ_k M_k² = Σ_{j<n} D_j² + 2 Σ_{j<n} D_j U_j`, with `U_j` the sum of the increments of the
   batch of `j` before `j`; for `j = kb + i`, `U_j` is `piLE j`-measurable with
   `E U_j² ≤ i c²/4`. The terms `D_j U_j` are martingale differences and `|D_j| ≤ c`, so
   `E (Σ_j D_j U_j)² = Σ_j E D_j² U_j² ≤ c² Σ_j E U_j² ≤ a b (b − 1) c⁴/8`, and
   `E (n⁻¹ Σ_j D_j U_j)² ≤ c⁴/(8a)`.
4. `V_j = D_j² − h(X_j)` has `E[V_j | piLE j] = 0` by 1.1 and `|V_j| ≤ c²`, so
   `E (n⁻¹ Σ_{j<n} V_j)² ≤ c⁴/n`.
5. `n⁻¹ Σ_{j<n} h(X_j) → E_π h` almost surely (MarkovChainMixing 6.3, `ae_tendsto_timeAverage`,
   along `n = a m b m → ∞`), hence in probability (Mathlib `tendstoInMeasure_of_tendsto_ae`), and
   `E_π h = Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²) = σ²(f)` by stationarity and MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.1
   (`MCMCAsymptoticVariance.asymptoticVariance_eq_of_poisson`).
6. Steps 2–5 and Markov's inequality give `n⁻¹ Σ_k B_k² → σ²(f)` in probability; with
   `a/(a − 1) → 1` and step 1, sums of terms tending to `0` in probability tend to `0`
   (MathematicalStatisticsFoundations 8.3; a union bound,
   `P(|U + V| > ε) ≤ P(|U| > ε/2) + P(|V| > ε/2)`), and `σ̂² → σ²(f)`.

(Flegal–Jones, p. 8, attribute strong consistency of batch means with `a`, `b → ∞` to Jones et
al. for geometrically ergodic chains and note that in general the estimator is not
consistent; their Remark 7, p. 9, gives the growth conditions on `b` used there. The
statement here, for finite chains with only `a, b → ∞`, from every initial law and for periodic
chains, is weak consistency, and it is proved here.)

**Checks.**

- The two-state chain with `p = 1/3`, `q = 1/2`, `f` the indicator of `a`: `σ²(f) = 42/125`.
- Both `a → ∞` and `b → ∞` are needed. With one-step batches (`b = 1`) the estimator is the sample
  variance of the `f(X_j)`, which tends to `Var_π f = 6/25 ≠ 42/125` for the chain above. With
  `a = 2` batches and independent fair bits (the chain on two states with both rows uniform, `f` the
  indicator of `a`, `σ²(f) = 1/4`), `σ̂² = (B_0 − B_1)²/(2b)` has mean `1/4` and variance
  `1/8 − 1/(16 b)` for every `b`, and it converges in law to `(1/4) χ²₁`, not to `1/4`.
- `σ²(f) = 0`: for the exchange chain and the indicator of `a`, every batch of even length has
  mean `1/2`, so `σ̂² = 0` whenever `b` is even.
- Stationarity is needed: for the chain and `π = (1/4, 1/4, 1/2)` of the last Check of 1.2 and
  `f = (−1, 1, 0)`, the definition gives `σ²(f) = 1/2`, while the estimator converges to `8/13`,
  the asymptotic variance under the stationary law `(4/13, 4/13, 5/13)`.
- Irreducibility is needed: for the identity kernel on two states, `π` uniform and `f` the
  indicator of `a`, every batch mean equals `f(X_0)`, so `σ̂² = 0`, while `σ²(f) = −1/4`.
- The identity behind step 1, `σ̂² = (Σ_k B_k² − (Σ_k B_k)²/a)/((a − 1) b)`, holds for any centring
  constant in place of `E_π f`.

*Needs:* 1.1; MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3 (`poisson_iff`), 2.1 (`asymptoticVariance`,
`MCMCAsymptoticVariance.asymptoticVariance_eq_of_poisson`); MarkovChainMixing 6.3

Mathlib `TendstoInMeasure`, `tendstoInMeasure_of_tendsto_ae`.

### 3.2 The batch-means confidence interval

Prove `tendsto_measureReal_abs_sub_le_mul_sqrt`: under the hypotheses of 3.1 and `σ²(f) > 0`, with
`n = a m · b m` and `A_n = n⁻¹ Σ_{j<n} f(X_j)`, for every real `z`,

    P(|A_n − E_π f| ≤ z √(σ̂²/n))  →  N(0, 1)([−z, z]).

For `z < 0` both sides are `0` in the limit (the interval `[−z, z]` is empty).

Route. For `n ≥ 1` the event is `{|T_m| − z √σ̂² ≤ 0}` with
`T_m = n^{−1/2} Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − E_π f)`, because `σ̂² ≥ 0`. The remark above gives convergence
in law along `n → ∞`; composing its `tendsto` clause with `n = a m b m → ∞` (eventually
`b m ≥ 1`, so `a m b m ≥ a m`) gives `T_m → Z ∼ N(0, σ²(f))` in law along `m`; by 3.1,
`σ̂² → σ²(f)` in probability; so by
Mathlib's Slutsky lemma `TendstoInDistribution.continuous_comp_prodMk_of_tendstoInMeasure_const`
with the continuous map `(x, s) ↦ |x| − z √s`, the variable `|T_m| − z √σ̂²` converges in law to
`|Z| − z σ(f)`. Its law has no atom at `0` because `σ(f) > 0`, so by the portmanteau theorem
(Mathlib `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_measure_of_null_frontier_of_tendsto`, for `(−∞, 0]`) the
probabilities converge to `P(|Z| ≤ z σ(f)) = N(0, 1)([−z, z])` (Mathlib
`gaussianReal_map_const_mul`). (Flegal–Jones, p. 2, describe the interval with a Student
quantile; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `σ²(f) > 0` is needed: for the exchange chain, `f` the indicator of `a` and `b m = 2m`,
  `a m = m`, both `σ̂² = 0` and `A_n = 1/2 = E_π f` hold on every path, so the probability is `1`
  for every `z`, while `N(0, 1)([−z, z]) < 1`.
- `z = 0`: the event is `{A_n = E_π f}`, whose probability tends to `0 = N(0, 1)({0})`.

*Needs:* 3.1, the remark above (MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem 4.1, MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3 and
2.1); Mathlib `TendstoInDistribution.continuous_comp_prodMk_of_tendstoInMeasure_const`,

### 3.3 The multivariate central limit theorem

Define `asymptoticCovariance κ π f`, for `f : S → ℝ^k` (Mathlib's `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin k)`),
as the matrix `Σ` with
`Σ a b = (σ²(f_a + f_b) − σ²(f_a) − σ²(f_b))/2`, `f_a` the coordinate `a` of `f`. Prove
`tendstoInDistribution_markovChainLaw_euclidean`: for an irreducible chain with stationary law
`π`, every initial probability `ν` and every `f`,

- `Σ` is positive semidefinite;
- `n^{−1/2} Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − E_π f)` converges in law to `multivariateGaussian 0 Σ`.

The matrix `Σ` is also the limit of `n⁻¹` times the covariance matrix of `Σ_{j<n} f(X_j)` (Tau
Ceti's `TauCeti.covMatrix` of its law), by MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.2 applied to `f_a`, `f_b` and
`f_a + f_b`; that limit is not used here. The first clause is what makes the second meaningful:
Mathlib's `multivariateGaussian 0 Σ` is the point mass at `0` when `Σ` is not positive semidefinite
(`multivariateGaussian_of_not_posSemidef`).

Route.

1. `σ²` is a quadratic form: `σ²(f) = Q(f̄)` with `Q(u) = 2⟨u, Z u⟩_π − ⟨u, u⟩_π`, and `f ↦ f̄` is
   linear. Its polar form `B(u, v) = ⟨u, Z v⟩_π + ⟨v, Z u⟩_π − ⟨u, v⟩_π` is symmetric, so
   `Σ a b = B(f̄_a, f̄_b)` and, for `t ∈ ℝ^k` and `f_t = ⟪f, t⟫`,
   `tᵀ Σ t = B(Σ_a t_a f̄_a, Σ_a t_a f̄_a) = σ²(f_t)`.
2. `σ²(f_t) ≥ 0`: with `g = Z f_t` (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3) and
   `MCMCAsymptoticVariance.asymptoticVariance_eq_of_poisson`,
   `σ²(f_t) = Σ_x π {x} (g x² − (P g)(x)²)`, which by stationarity is `Σ_x π {x} h x ≥ 0`. With the
   symmetry of `Σ`, it is positive semidefinite.
3. By the Cramér–Wold theorem (Mathlib
   `tendstoInDistribution_iff_tendstoInDistribution_inner`) it suffices that
   `⟪n^{−1/2} Σ_{j<n} (f(X_j) − E_π f), t⟫ = n^{−1/2} Σ_{j<n} (f_t(X_j) − E_π f_t)` (Mathlib
   `integral_inner`) converges in law to `⟪Z, t⟫` for every `t`. By the remark above, the left
   side converges to `N(0, σ²(f_t)) = N(0, tᵀ Σ t)`.
4. `multivariateGaussian 0 Σ` is Gaussian (`isGaussian_multivariateGaussian`), so its image under
   the continuous linear functional `x ↦ ⟪x, t⟫` is `gaussianReal` with the mean and the variance
   of that functional (`IsGaussian.map_eq_gaussianReal`). The mean is `⟪0, t⟫ = 0`, the integral
   commuting with the functional and the law having mean `0`
   (`integral_id_multivariateGaussian`). The variance is `covarianceBilin t t`
   (`covarianceBilin_self`, the law having second moments by `IsGaussian.memLp_two_id`), which is
   `tᵀ Σ t` by `covarianceBilin_multivariateGaussian`, using step 2.

(Proved here.)

**Checks.**

- The reversible chain on three states with rows `(1/2, 3/10, 1/5)`, `(3/10, 1/2, 1/5)`,
  `(1/10, 1/10, 4/5)` and `f = (indicator of the first state, (0, 1, 2))`:
  `π = (1/4, 1/4, 1/2)`, `Σ = [[7/16, −15/16], [−15/16, 39/16]]`, with nonnegative diagonal and
  determinant `3/16`, and
  `σ²(t₁ f₁ + t₂ f₂) = tᵀ Σ t` for `t = (1, 2)`, `(−3, 1)` and `(1/2, −5/7)`.
- `k = 1`: `Σ = (σ²(f))`, and the statement is the remark above.
- Irreducibility is needed: for the identity kernel on two states with `π` uniform, `1 − P + Π`
  is singular, `σ²(f) = −Var_π f` (MCMCAsymptoticVariance 2.1, Checks), and for `k = 1` and `f` the
  indicator of `a` the matrix `(−1/4)` is not positive semidefinite.

*Needs:* the remark above (MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem 4.1; MCMCAsymptoticVariance 1.3, 2.1);
Mathlib `tendstoInDistribution_iff_tendstoInDistribution_inner`, `multivariateGaussian`,
`isGaussian_multivariateGaussian`, `IsGaussian.map_eq_gaussianReal`,
`integral_id_multivariateGaussian`, `covarianceBilin_self`, `IsGaussian.memLp_two_id`,

**Remark (ratio estimators).** For `f = (u, w)` with `E_π w ≠ 0`, the delta method of

martingale and the bounds on its solution are what a central limit theorem, a concentration
bound or a stochastic-approximation analysis with Markovian noise (temporal-difference learning
along one trajectory, stochastic gradients with Markov data) needs. The functions `u − P u` have
mean `0` under `π` and serve as control variates for ergodic averages; with `u = g` the corrected
function `f − (g − P g)` is the constant `E_π f`. Layer 2 gives sample sizes for samplers
whose mixing time is bounded, such as the colouring sampler of MarkovChainMixing 6.2, which that
roadmap names as the input of approximate counting. Layer 3 is the output analysis of a Markov
chain Monte Carlo run: a standard error, a confidence interval and the joint law of several
averages.

## Provenance and prior work

MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem attributes the martingale route to central limit theorems for
Markov chains through a solution of the Poisson equation to Gordin (1969); it is the route of its
4.1. No outside formalization was used.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap is built on MarkovChainMixing, MCMCAsymptoticVariance and
MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem and on two roadmaps by other contributors, MarkovChainsAndRenewal
and MathematicalStatisticsFoundations, and restates none of their theorems.

Owned here, for finite state spaces: the Poisson martingale and its conditional variances (1.1),
the Poisson solution and its bounds under a mixing-time bound without irreducibility (1.2) and
by the spectral gap (1.3), the bias and mean-square error of ergodic averages after burn-in from
every initial law (2.1), Hoeffding's inequality for finite chains (2.2), the batch-means
estimator and its consistency (3.1), the confidence interval (3.2), and the asymptotic covariance
matrix with the multivariate central limit theorem (3.3).

Each layer is meant as a foundation for other roadmaps. Layer 1 is the interface for work on
Markov-dependent samples: central limit theorems with an explicit variance, concentration bounds,
and stochastic-approximation and temporal-difference analyses with Markovian samples, which need
the Poisson martingale and a bound on the Poisson solution. Layer 2 gives sample sizes for samplers
whose mixing time is bounded. Layer 3 is the output analysis of a run: a standard error, a
confidence interval, and the joint law of several averages, which with the delta method gives
ratio estimators.

Left to other roadmaps and to the libraries:

- MarkovChainMixing owns the distances `d`, `d̄`, the mixing time, the spectral gap and the
  Poincaré inequality (1.2, 1.3, 2.4), the cycle walk and the colouring sampler (4.3, 6.2), the
  ergodic theorem (6.3), and the error bound for time averages of reversible chains (6.4): the
  stationary second-moment bound `γ E S_t² ≤ 2t Var_π f` and the Chebyshev bound after a burn-in
  of `t_mix(ε/2)`. 2.1 above has the leading constant `σ²(f)` in place of `2 Var_π f/γ`, holds
  without reversibility and from every initial law, and does not restate 6.4.
- MCMCAsymptoticVariance owns the fundamental matrix and its series for irreducible chains, the
  Poisson equation (`poisson_iff`), the asymptotic variance and its formulas, the limit of
  `Var S_n/n` (2.2) and the spectral bound `σ²(f) ≤ (2/γ − 1) Var_π f` (3.2). Its scope leaves
  out estimators of `σ²(f)`, which 3.1 above supplies. 1.2 above treats chains that need not be
  irreducible under a mixing-time bound, and 1.3 bounds the solution itself.
- MartingaleCentralLimitTheorem owns Gordin's central limit theorem for finite irreducible chains
  (4.1), taken with a given Poisson solution; the remark of Layer 3 combines it with
  MCMCAsymptoticVariance and is not a target.
- MarkovChainsAndRenewal owns the qualitative theory of finite chains and the formula for total
  variation on a countable space (3.5), used in every bias estimate here.
- MathematicalStatisticsFoundations owns boundedness in probability, the algebra of convergence
  in probability and the delta method (8.2–8.4), and the Metropolis–Hastings kernel with its
  reversibility (9.4), which appears here only in Examples.
- Mathlib owns Hoeffding's lemma, the Chernoff and Azuma–Hoeffding bounds, Slutsky's lemma, the
  portmanteau theorem, the Cramér–Wold theorem, Doob's decomposition (`martingalePart`) and the
  multivariate Gaussian laws; RandomMatrices 2.1 lists the scalar Hoeffding and Azuma–Hoeffding
  interfaces. 2.2 above applies Hoeffding's lemma to one transition at a time.

Not treated here: chains on countable or general state spaces (geometric and uniform ergodicity,
spectral gaps on `L²(π)`, the Kipnis–Varadhan theorem); Hoeffding and Bernstein bounds through the
spectral gap (León and Perron, as quoted in Levin–Peres, Notes to Chapter 12, p. 178; Fan–Jiang–Sun,
Theorems 1 and 3, pp. 5–6); Chernoff bounds for walks on expander graphs through the spectral
parameter; large deviation principles for Markov chains; Berry–Esseen bounds;
overlapping batch means and spectral variance estimators (Flegal–Jones, (7), p. 8); fixed-width
stopping rules; continuous-time chains; Langevin and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo
(LangevinAndHamiltonianMonteCarlo, on general state spaces).
