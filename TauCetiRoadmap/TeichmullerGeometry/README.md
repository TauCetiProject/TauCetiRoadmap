# Roadmap: Teichmüller geometry of closed surfaces

**Summary.** This roadmap builds the marked Teichmüller theory of a closed connected oriented smooth surface `Σ` of genus `g ≥ 2`: the space `Teich(Σ)` of marked complex structures, constructed from the integrable almost-complex structures on `Σ` modulo the identity component of the diffeomorphism group and compared with marked Riemann surfaces, marked hyperbolic metrics and conjugacy classes of discrete faithful representations of the surface group into `PSL₂(ℝ)` of Euler number `2 − 2g`; its complex deformation atlas of dimension `3g − 3`; the action of the extended mapping class group `Mod±(Σ) = π₀ Diff(Σ)` and the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem in its smooth form, with the smooth Baer–Epstein kernel theorem as an owned input, resting on an owned PL theory of compact surfaces (PL approximation, invariance of the boundary, PL structures, orientations and Epstein's isotopy theorems), the topological classification of compact surfaces by polygon schemas, branched covers and the topological Riemann–Hurwitz formula, and the topological and PL mapping class groups, over GeometricTopology's two-dimensional comparison of the topological, PL and smooth categories, its surface mapping class groups, its isotopy extension and Smale's theorems on the disc and the sphere; Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates, quasiconformal maps, Teichmüller's extremal theorem and the Teichmüller metric; measured laminations, earthquakes and Kerckhoff's proof of the Nielsen realization theorem; the dimension of fixed loci of finite subgroups and the genus-two hyperelliptic kernel; the arithmetic locus of `Teich(Σ)`, from FuchsianOrbifolds' finiteness of arithmetic lattices of fixed signature; the isometry group of the hyperbolic plane as `PGL₂(ℝ)` and the open dense locus of marked surfaces whose uniformizing group is maximal among discrete subgroups of `PGL₂(ℝ)`. The end theorems are the three open dense loci of Layers 8, 9 and 10 and their common generic locus of Layer 11: marked hyperbolic surfaces with trivial isometry group, nonarithmetic uniformizing group, and uniformizing group maximal among discrete `PGL₂(ℝ)` overgroups. It is built from the deformation theory of ComplexCurveModuli, the Dolbeault and Serre-duality foundations of ComplexKahlerGeometry, the flows and orientations of DifferentialGeometry, the diffeomorphism group of Tau Ceti and GeometricTopology, GeometricTopology's surface theory (Radó's theorem, the two-dimensional Hauptvermutung, smoothing, isotopy to diffeomorphisms, the smoothing of isotopies, Whitehead's smooth triangulations, the surface mapping class groups and Smale's theorems), the simplicial complexes, PL maps, isotopies and Schoenflies theorem of Tau Ceti, the covering-space library of Tau Ceti, the fundamental-group, Euler-characteristic and Euler-class suppliers AlgebraicTopology and CharacteristicClasses, the first realized at the pin by Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.eulerChar`, the hyperbolic space forms of RiemannianGeometry, the Fuchsian quotient theory and the arithmetic Fuchsian lattices of FuchsianOrbifolds, the branch-deficit bound of Tau Ceti and the harmonic-analysis estimates of PDE. All statements are in the author's words with locators; sources that are not openly available are cited by their locators alone and are marked so in the references.

## Conventions

- A **closed surface** is a compact connected boundaryless smooth surface; `Σ` is closed, oriented, of genus `g ≥ 2` except in Layers 0 and 5, whose theorems hold for every closed orientable surface, or every closed surface, and say so. The standard oriented smooth genus-`g` model is `S_g`, with a fixed orientation-preserving diffeomorphism `S_g ≃ Σ` (Layer 0.1).
- A **marking** `m : Σ → X` is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism onto a Riemann surface. Marked Riemann surfaces `(X, m)` and `(Y, n)` are equivalent when a biholomorphism `f : X ≃ Y` makes `f ∘ m` smoothly isotopic to `n`; the isotopy is part of the relation.
- A **topological surface** is a type with `[TopologicalSpace M] [ChartedSpace (EuclideanHalfSpace 2) M]`, compact when also `T2Space` and `CompactSpace`, with boundary `(𝓡∂ 2).boundary M`; a smooth surface adds `[IsManifold (𝓡∂ 2) ∞ M]`, and a closed surface charted on `𝓡 2` is read as one with empty boundary through the comparison of the two models (§0.0e). A **PL structure** is a triangulation by a finite combinatorial surface (`PLStructure`, Layer 0.0b); distances on a realization are taken in the sup norm of barycentric coordinates.
- `Diff(M)` is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) M ∞` for a closed surface charted on `𝓡 2` and `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡∂ 2) M ∞` for a compact surface with boundary, the two agreeing on closed surfaces by the comparison of the two models (§0.0e), with the weak Whitney topology `Diffeomorph.weakWhitneyTopology`; `Diff₀(M)` is its identity path component; `Diff⁺(M)` the orientation-preserving subgroup; `Diff₀⁺ = Diff₀`. Two diffeomorphisms are **smoothly isotopic** when a `C^∞` diffeotopy (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diffeotopy`) joins them; `Mod±(Σ) = π₀ Diff(Σ)` and `Mod⁺(Σ) = π₀ Diff⁺(Σ)` (Layer 3.1).
- The Laplacian is positive: `Δ⁺ = −div grad`. For `h = e^(2u) h₀` the curvature-`−1` equation is `Δ⁺_(h₀) u + K_(h₀) + e^(2u) = 0`, the convention of ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 4.
- `π₁ⁿᵃᵗ(Σ, x₀)` is Mathlib's `FundamentalGroup`, whose product is `p * q = q.trans p` (`FundamentalGroup.mul_def`). The **geometric group** is `Π := (π₁ⁿᵃᵗ(Σ, x₀))ᵐᵒᵖ`, so that `op(p) * op(q) = op(p.trans q)` follows first-traverse concatenation; `ι : Π ≃* π₁ⁿᵃᵗ`, `ι(op p) = p⁻¹`, is the inversion equivalence. Commutators are `[a, b] = a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹`.
- `PSL₂(ℝ)` acts on `ℍ` through FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 0 item 2's restriction of Mathlib's `UpperHalfPlane.glAction` along `PSL(2,ℝ) → PGL(2,ℝ)`; `PGL₂(ℝ) = GL (Fin 2) ℝ` modulo its centre acts by that same `glAction`, which conjugates when the determinant is negative. The boundary circle `∂ℍ` is oriented as the positive boundary of `ℍ`.
- The mapping class action on complex structures is `f ⋅ J = ε(f) · (f⁻¹)^* J` with `ε(f) = +1` for orientation-preserving `f` and `−1` otherwise; on marked surfaces `f ⋅ (X, m) = (X, m ∘ f⁻¹)` or `(X̄, m ∘ f⁻¹)` (Layer 3.2).
- Fenchel–Nielsen twists are arclength-valued in `ℝ`; the positive Dehn twist about the `i`-th cuff changes `t_i` by `−ℓ_i` (Layer 7.2).
- The Euler number `e(ρ)` of a representation `ρ : Π → PSL₂(ℝ)` is the Euler number of its flat oriented circle bundle with the boundary-circle fibre orientation; the uniformizing holonomy has `e(ρ) = 2 − 2g` (Layer 4.4).
- An **arithmetic Fuchsian group** is a lattice of `PSL₂(ℝ)` commensurable, up to conjugacy, with the image of the norm-one unit group of an order in a quaternion algebra over a totally real field split at exactly one real place (Layer 9.1).
- Universes and coefficients: all surfaces are types in one universe; cohomology is with `ℤ` coefficients unless written otherwise; Sobolev exponents are `p > 2` and integer `k ≥ 1` as in ComplexCurveModuli D3 item 5.

### 0.0a Piecewise-linear approximation of planar and surface homeomorphisms

The plane is `EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)`, the model of `𝓡 2`; the closed half-plane `{x | 0 ≤ x 0}`, with its boundary line `{x | x 0 = 0}`, is the model `EuclideanHalfSpace 2` of `𝓡∂ 2`. A map of the plane is piecewise linear on a set when it is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsPLOn` there. For finite complexes `K` and `L`, a map `|K| → |L|` of their realizations (`AbstractSimplicialComplex.Realization`) is piecewise linear when in barycentric coordinates it is the restriction of a map that is `TauCeti.IsPLOn` on the coordinate image of `|K|`, the criterion by which Tau Ceti's `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.PLHomeomorphic` compares complexes; a PL homeomorphism is a homeomorphism that is piecewise linear in both directions. Distances on a realization are measured in the sup norm of barycentric coordinates. An error function `φ` on an open set `U` is admissible when every point of `U` has a neighbourhood in `U` on which `φ` is bounded below by a positive constant; continuity is not asked.

Prove the PL approximation theorem in four forms. **Open form**, `exists_pl_approximation_open`: a homeomorphism `f` between open subsets of the plane, given as an `OpenPartialHomeomorph`, and an admissible error `φ` on its source admit a homeomorphism `g` with the same source and target, piecewise linear in both directions, with `dist (f x) (g x) < φ x` on the source. **Half-plane form**, `exists_pl_approximation_halfPlane`: if `f` carries the half-plane part of its source onto that of its target and the boundary-line part onto the boundary-line part, `g` can be chosen with the same two properties. **Local relative form**: if `f` is already piecewise linear on a closed, locally finite polyhedral subset of its source (a locally finite union of points, segments and convex polygons), `g` can be chosen equal to `f` there, in the open and in the half-plane form. **Compact relative form**: for finite combinatorial surfaces `K` and `L` (Tau Ceti's `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold` in dimension two), a homeomorphism `f : |K| → |L|`, a subcomplex `A` of `K` on which `f` is piecewise linear, and `ε > 0`, there is a PL homeomorphism `g` within `ε` of `f` and equal to `f` on `|A|`; the empty subcomplex gives the absolute compact form.

Route (Moise, §6). First the **polygonal Schoenflies theorem in PL form**: a simple closed polygon in the plane bounds, by Tau Ceti's Jordan curve theorem (`TauCeti.IsJordanCurve`, `TauCeti.filledHull`), a compact region that is triangulated by diagonals with vertices among those of the polygon (induction on the number of vertices: a simple polygon with at least four vertices has a diagonal inside it), and a PL homeomorphism between two simple polygons extends to a PL homeomorphism of these closed regions, by coning from an interior vertex after subdividing both. Second, **general position**: finitely many polygonal arcs can be moved by arbitrarily small amounts, keeping their endpoints, so that they meet only at prescribed endpoints. For the open form, triangulate the source by a locally finite triangulation so fine that the image of every closed vertex star has diameter less than a third of the local lower bound of `φ`; move the images of the vertices slightly, replace the image of each edge by a polygonal arc between the new vertex images within the error and in general position, so that the image of each triangle boundary is a simple polygon, and fill each triangle by the polygonal Schoenflies theorem. The filled triangles tile the target, so the map is a homeomorphism onto it with PL inverse. For the local relative form, put the given polyhedral set in the 1-skeleton and leave it in place. For the half-plane form, extend `f` across the boundary line by reflection, approximate it on the line first by a PL homeomorphism of the line within the error, approximate the two closed sides relative to that common approximation, glue, and restrict to the half-plane. For the compact relative form, cover `|L|` by the open stars of its vertices, which are PL homeomorphic to open subsets of the plane or of the half-plane (the vertex charts behind Tau Ceti's `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold.realization_isManifold`), and apply the local forms star by star with errors tending to zero at the frontier of each star, keeping in place what `|A|` and the earlier stars already made piecewise linear.

**Checks.**

- The constant `φ = ε`, the function `1 / (1 + ‖x‖)` on the whole plane, which has no positive global lower bound, and a discontinuous function taking only the values `1` and `2` are admissible.
- The function equal to `1/q` at points whose first coordinate is a reduced fraction `p/q` and to `1` elsewhere is positive but not admissible: near every point it takes the values `1/q` with `q` unbounded, so it has no positive lower bound on any open set. An approximation `g` within it would satisfy `dist (f x) (g x) < 1/q` along points with first coordinates `p/q` converging to any given point with `q → ∞`, so by continuity `g = f` everywhere, and the homeomorphism `(x, y) ↦ (x³, y)`, which is not piecewise linear, would have no approximation. The hypothesis excludes it.
- The homeomorphism `x ↦ ‖x‖ x` of the plane is not piecewise linear near the origin and is approximated within every constant `ε > 0`, so the open form is not discharged by `g = f`.
- The homeomorphism `(x₀, x₁) ↦ (x₀, x₁ + x₀²)` preserves the half-plane and fixes its boundary line pointwise; the half-plane form keeps the line in place, which the open form applied to the reflected map does not guarantee.
- In the compact relative form, `A = K` with `f` piecewise linear allows `g = f`, and a boundary subcomplex on which `f` is piecewise linear stays fixed.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsPLOn`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsJordanCurve`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.filledHull`; Tau Ceti `AbstractSimplicialComplex.Realization`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold.realization_isManifold`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.PLHomeomorphic`; Mathlib `OpenPartialHomeomorph`.

### 0.0b Invariance of the boundary and PL structures

A topological surface is a type with `[TopologicalSpace M] [ChartedSpace (EuclideanHalfSpace 2) M]`; a compact surface is in addition `T2Space` and `CompactSpace`; its boundary is `(𝓡∂ 2).boundary M`. Prove **invariance of the boundary**, `image_boundary`: a homeomorphism of surfaces carries the boundary onto the boundary; in particular whether a point is a boundary point does not depend on the chart that tests it, which for topological charts is not formal. Route: a point of the boundary line has no neighbourhood in the half-plane homeomorphic to an open subset of the plane, because by Tau Ceti's invariance of domain (`IsOpen.image_of_continuousOn_of_injOn`, `Homeomorph.coe_apply_mem_interior_iff`) such a homeomorphism would put the point in the interior of its neighbourhood, while it lies on the frontier of the half-plane.

Define a **PL structure** on a space `M`, `PLStructure`: a finite vertex type, an abstract simplicial complex on it that is a combinatorial surface (Tau Ceti's `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold` in dimension two: every vertex link is a combinatorial circle or arc), and a homeomorphism from its realization onto `M`; transport PL structures along homeomorphisms by `PLStructure.map`. The homeomorphism is data: precomposing it with a self-homeomorphism of the realization that is not piecewise linear gives a second PL structure on the same complex whose comparison with the first is not piecewise linear. Prove that the realization of a finite combinatorial surface is a compact surface whose boundary is the union of the edges lying in exactly one triangle, the boundary subcomplex, from Tau Ceti's `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold.realization_isManifold` and invariance of the boundary.

Read GeometricTopology's two-dimensional comparison through these PL structures. **Radó's theorem** is GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 3: every surface is `TauCeti.IsCombinatoriallyTriangulable M 2`. For a compact surface the triangulating complex is finite, its realization being compact, so `M` carries a PL structure; its boundary subcomplex realizes `(𝓡∂ 2).boundary M`, because the boundary of the realization is the union of the edges in exactly one triangle (above, with GeometricTopology Layer 11.3's boundary of a combinatorial manifold) and a homeomorphism carries boundary to boundary (`image_boundary`). Hence a compact surface is `TauCeti.IsTriangulable`, has finite CW type (`TauCeti.IsTriangulable.finiteCWType`), and Tau Ceti's Euler characteristic `TauCeti.eulerChar M` is defined. The **two-dimensional Hauptvermutung** and **isotopy to a PL homeomorphism** are GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 4: the complexes of two PL structures on homeomorphic spaces have isomorphic linear subdivisions, so they are PL homeomorphic in Tau Ceti's sense `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.PLHomeomorphic`, and for PL structures `s` on `M` and `t` on `N` every homeomorphism `f : M → N` is isotopic (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Isotopic`) to a homeomorphism `g` with `t.e⁻¹ ∘ g ∘ s.e` piecewise linear. Layers 0.0c, 0.0d and 0.0h use these two statements in this form.

**Checks.**

- The closed unit disc has the unit circle as its boundary and the sphere has empty boundary, so by `image_boundary` they are not homeomorphic; Euler characteristic is not needed for this.
- On the closed half-plane `{x | 0 ≤ x 0}` the origin is a boundary point and `(1, 0)` an interior point; the content of `image_boundary` at the origin is that no neighbourhood of it in the half-plane is homeomorphic to an open subset of the plane, so no other atlas makes it an interior point.
- The complex with one triangle is a combinatorial surface whose three edges each lie in exactly one triangle, and its realization is a closed disc with those three edges as boundary; the cone on a hexagon has an interior vertex whose link is the hexagon, a combinatorial circle, and its boundary is the hexagon. Two boundaries of tetrahedra sharing one vertex form a complex whose link at that vertex is two disjoint circles, which is not a combinatorial surface.
- The PL structure is data: precomposing the radial triangulation of the round sphere by the boundary of the tetrahedron with the homeomorphism of the realization that is `x ↦ (‖x‖/ε) x` on a disc of radius `ε` about an interior point of a face, and the identity off it, gives a second PL structure on the same complex whose comparison map with the first is not piecewise linear; the Hauptvermutung provides a PL homeomorphism between them, not the identity.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `IsOpen.image_of_continuousOn_of_injOn`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.coe_apply_mem_interior_iff`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.IsCombinatorialManifold.realization_isManifold`; Tau Ceti `PreAbstractSimplicialComplex.PLHomeomorphic`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsTriangulable`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsCombinatoriallyTriangulable`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsTriangulable.finiteCWType`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Isotopic`; Mathlib `ModelWithCorners.boundary`; GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 3; GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 4; GeometricTopology Layer 11.3.

### 0.0c The Euler characteristic and orientations

The Euler characteristic of a compact surface is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.eulerChar` (AlgebraicTopology Stage 7), defined because the surface has finite CW type by Radó's theorem (§0.0b) and `TauCeti.IsTriangulable.finiteCWType`; it is not defined again here. For a PL structure `s` it is the alternating count of the faces of `s.K`, a face with `k + 1` vertices counted with sign `(−1)^k` (`AbstractSimplicialComplex.eulerChar_eq_finsum_of_homeomorph`, after relabelling the vertices into the universe of `M` as `TauCeti.IsTriangulable.finiteCWType` does), so `V − E + F` on a finite combinatorial surface; it is invariant under homeomorphisms (`Homeomorph.eulerChar_eq`) and `2` on the round sphere (`TauCeti.eulerChar_sphere`). Compute from triangulations the value `1` for the closed disc and `0` for the annulus, the Möbius band and the torus.

Define a **coherent orientation** of a finite combinatorial surface: a cyclic order of the vertices of each triangle such that the two triangles on an interior edge induce opposite directions on it; the complex is orientable when one exists. Prove that simplicial isomorphisms and linear subdivisions induce bijections of coherent orientations. Construct the **orientation double cover**: the finite complex whose triangles are the oriented triangles, two of them glued along an edge exactly when they induce opposite directions on it. Prove that it is a combinatorial surface, that the projection is a two-sheeted covering map (Mathlib's `IsCoveringMap`) with evenly covered disc and half-disc neighbourhoods, also at boundary vertices, that reversing the orientation of every triangle is a fixed-point-free deck involution whose orbits are the fibres, that the boundary of the cover is the inverse image of the boundary, and that over a connected surface the cover has two components, the two coherent orientations, when the surface is orientable, and is connected otherwise (for the interior, a surface without boundary, this is the orientable two-sheeted cover of Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, §3.3, pp. 234–235, and its Proposition 3.25). Define the **orientations of a compact surface** `M` as the coherently oriented PL structures on `M` modulo the relation in which a PL homeomorphism isotopic to the comparison map (§0.0b) carries one orientation to the other; transport them along homeomorphisms; and prove that **isotopic homeomorphisms act equally on orientations**: lift the isotopy, followed by the projection, through the orientation double cover of the target by Mathlib's `IsCoveringMap.liftHomotopy`, starting from the lift that the orientation determines at time zero. Hence the relation does not depend on the PL homeomorphism chosen, and a connected orientable compact surface has exactly two orientations. Orient each boundary circle so that the surface lies on its left; the boundary of the closed unit disc runs counterclockwise.

**Checks.**

- The boundary of the tetrahedron gives `4 − 6 + 4 = 2` and its barycentric subdivision `14 − 36 + 24 = 2`: the face counts change and the alternating sum does not.
- The full tetrahedron, of dimension three, gives `4 − 6 + 4 − 1 = 1`, the Euler characteristic of the contractible ball; a sum stopping at triangles would give `2`, so the face count of `AbstractSimplicialComplex.eulerChar_eq_finsum_of_homeomorph` runs over faces of every dimension.
- The seven-vertex torus gives `7 − 21 + 14 = 0`; a triangulated disc gives `1`, a triangulated annulus and a triangulated Möbius band `0`.
- The orientation double cover of a triangulated disc is two disjoint discs and that of a triangulated sphere two disjoint spheres; that of a triangulated Möbius band is a connected annulus with two points over each point, so the cover is not a trivial product.
- Complex conjugation of the closed disc reverses its orientation and the direction of its boundary circle, so it is not isotopic to the identity.

*Needs:* §0.0b; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.eulerChar`; Tau Ceti `AbstractSimplicialComplex.eulerChar_eq_finsum_of_homeomorph`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.eulerChar_eq`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.eulerChar_sphere`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsTriangulable.finiteCWType`; Mathlib `IsCoveringMap`; Mathlib `IsCoveringMap.liftHomotopy`.

### 0.0d Epstein's theorems on curves, arcs and homeomorphisms

Work on a finite combinatorial surface, with piecewise-linear embeddings of the circle and of the interval; closed curves lie in the interior, homotopy is free unless a relative condition is stated, and every conclusion is an ambient isotopy (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.AmbientIsotopy`) whose slices are PL homeomorphisms. Prove the four **curve and arc isotopy theorems**:

- **two-sided curves**: homotopic PL embeddings of the circle whose first image is two-sided and bounds no disc are ambient isotopic by an isotopy fixed outside a compact subset of the interior, hence fixing the boundary (Epstein, Theorem 2.1);
- **one-sided curves**: homotopic PL embeddings of the circle whose first image is one-sided are ambient isotopic by an isotopy supported in a compact subset of the interior, through a Möbius-band regular neighbourhood and the orientation double cover of §0.0c (Epstein, Theorems 3.3 and 3.4);
- **proper arcs**: two PL arcs with the same ordered endpoints on the boundary, meeting it only there and homotopic with both endpoints fixed, are ambient isotopic by an isotopy fixing the whole boundary (Epstein, Theorem 3.1);
- **disc-bounding curves**: in an oriented closed disc neighbourhood, two PL curves bounding discs in its interior and inducing the same boundary orientation are ambient isotopic by an isotopy supported in it and fixed on its boundary. This statement and its proof are the roadmap's own: by the polygonal Schoenflies theorem of §0.0a each curve bounds a PL disc and cobounds a PL annulus with the boundary of the neighbourhood, so an orientation-preserving PL homeomorphism of the neighbourhood fixed on its boundary carries one curve onto the other, and coning it to the identity is the isotopy; the orientation condition cannot be dropped (Epstein, remark following Theorem 2.1).

Then prove **Epstein's theorem** for homeomorphisms in its two forms: `isotopic_of_homotopic_closedSurface`, homotopic homeomorphisms between closed surfaces are isotopic (Epstein, Theorem 6.4), and `isotopicRel_of_homotopicRel`, homeomorphisms between compact surfaces that are homotopic relative to the boundary are isotopic relative to the boundary (Epstein, Theorem 6.3 and its proof, pp. 99–101: the theorem concludes an isotopy from a homotopy of pairs `(M, ∂M) → (M, ∂M)`, and when the homeomorphism is the identity on `∂M` and the homotopy is constant there, the isotopies constructed in the proof fix `∂M`). Prove its PL form, **PL isotopy**: two isotopic PL homeomorphisms are PL isotopic, that is, joined by a level-preserving PL homeomorphism of `[0, 1] × |K|` for the product triangulation.

Route. First the finite homotopy lemmas, by simplicial approximation relative to the prescribed PL data, by lifting to the universal cover (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.UniversalCover`) and by the polygonal Schoenflies theorem of §0.0a: a null-homotopic simple PL loop in the interior bounds an embedded PL disc (Epstein, Theorem 1.7); two disjoint homotopic two-sided loops bounding no disc cobound an embedded PL annulus (Epstein, Lemma 2.4); two homotopic curves, or two proper arcs homotopic with fixed endpoints, in general position and meeting away from their endpoints have an innermost bigon, and the isotopy across it lowers the number of intersection points (Epstein, Lemmas 2.5 and 3.2). The curve and arc theorems follow: put the two curves in general position, remove bigons until they are disjoint, and move one onto the other across the annulus, Möbius band or disc that they cobound, by coning on discs. For homeomorphisms `f` and `g` that are homotopic, replace both by PL homeomorphisms in their isotopy classes (§0.0b); cut the surface along a finite system of curves and proper arcs read off the triangulation; move `g⁻¹ ∘ f` successively by the curve and arc theorems, with relative homotopies at each step, until it fixes the system and carries each complementary disc to itself; and finish with Alexander's trick on the discs (Tau Ceti's `Homeomorph.alexanderIsotopyClosedBall`), the annulus and Möbius-band pieces being base cases treated directly. The relative statement carries homotopies relative to the boundary throughout. The hypotheses of the curve theorems are internal to the proof and do not restrict the statements for homeomorphisms. PL isotopy is the same argument in the PL category, with coning in place of Alexander's trick.

**Checks.**

- Two parallel core circles of an annulus test the two-sided theorem, a core circle of a Möbius band and a small PL perturbation of it the one-sided theorem, and two proper arcs in a disc with the same ordered endpoints the arc theorem.
- Two proper arcs crossing an annulus whose difference winds once around it are not homotopic with fixed endpoints, and they are not ambient isotopic fixing the boundary; the relative hypothesis is used.
- The clockwise and counterclockwise parametrizations of the unit circle in the plane are homotopic but not isotopic through embeddings: they bound a disc, which the two-sided theorem excludes, and they induce opposite boundary orientations, which the disc theorem excludes (Epstein, remark following Theorem 2.1).
- On the closed disc the identity and complex conjugation are homotopic through continuous maps but not isotopic through homeomorphisms, having opposite local orientations (§0.0c); they are not homotopic relative to the boundary, and the closed theorem does not apply to the disc.
- A Dehn twist of the annulus is homotopic to the identity, but not relative to the boundary, and it is not isotopic to the identity relative to the boundary.

*Needs:* §0.0a; §0.0b; §0.0c; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Isotopic`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.AmbientIsotopy`; Tau Ceti `Homeomorph.alexanderIsotopyClosedBall`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalCover`; Mathlib `ContinuousMap.HomotopicRel`; Mathlib `ContinuousMap.HomotopyRel`; Mathlib `FundamentalGroup`.

### 0.0e Compatible PL structures, the two models and the orientation comparison

A smooth surface is a compact surface with `[IsManifold (𝓡∂ 2) ∞ M]`. A homeomorphism from the realization of a complex onto `M` is a **smooth triangulation** when it is a smooth immersion on every closed simplex, Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsSmoothTriangulation` (Whitehead's condition; Munkres, Definition 8.3). Define the **compatibility** of a PL structure `s` with the smooth structure of `M`: some smooth triangulation `g` of `M` differs from `s` by a PL homeomorphism, `s.e⁻¹ ∘ g` being piecewise linear in barycentric coordinates. Prove that this holds exactly when some linear subdivision of the complex of `s`, followed by `s.e`, is a smooth triangulation (a linear subdivision of a smooth triangulation is one, as Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsSmoothTriangulation.barycentricSubdivisionRealizationHomeomorph_trans` proves for the barycentric subdivision), and that compatibility is carried along diffeomorphisms (if `s` is compatible and `Φ : M → N` is a diffeomorphism, `PLStructure.map s Φ` is compatible), from Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsSmoothTriangulation.trans_diffeomorph`.

The existence and uniqueness of compatible smoothings and the isotopy of homeomorphisms to diffeomorphisms are GeometricTopology's, read here for these PL structures. Every PL structure is compatible with some smooth structure on its space, constructed by rounding the corners of the triangles after one subdivision, and two smooth structures compatible with it are related by a diffeomorphism isotopic to the identity (GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 4, with item 2). Every compact smooth surface has a compatible PL structure, a smooth triangulation existing by GeometricTopology Layer 11.3. **Isotopy to a diffeomorphism**: every homeomorphism between compact smooth surfaces is isotopic, by an isotopy moving points less than any given `ε`, to a diffeomorphism, and a self-homeomorphism fixing the boundary pointwise is isotopic relative to the boundary to a diffeomorphism fixing it pointwise (GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 2, in the relative form that its item 5 uses).

Prove the **comparison of the two models** for surfaces without boundary. For a smooth surface `S` charted on the boundaryless model, `[ChartedSpace (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 2)) S] [IsManifold (𝓡 2) ∞ S]`, compose each chart with Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.EuclideanHalfSpace.interiorEmbedding 1`, a diffeomorphism of the plane onto the open half-plane `{x | 0 < x 0}`; the result is a `ChartedSpace (EuclideanHalfSpace 2) S` atlas that is smooth for `𝓡∂ 2`, because its transition maps are those of `S` conjugated by `interiorEmbedding 1` on open subsets of the open half-plane, and every point is an interior point, `(𝓡∂ 2).boundary S = ∅`. A map between two such surfaces is `C^∞` for the new atlases exactly when it is `C^∞` for the old ones, so the identity of `S` induces a group isomorphism `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) S ∞ ≃* TauCeti.Diff (𝓡∂ 2) S ∞` compatible with the underlying homeomorphisms, and, applied to `I × S` with the product atlases, a bijection between the diffeotopies `TauCeti.Diffeotopy (𝓡 2) ∞ S` and `TauCeti.Diffeotopy (𝓡∂ 2) ∞ S` with the same slices. Conversely, a smooth `𝓡∂ 2` surface with empty boundary has an `𝓡 2` atlas with the same smooth maps: every point is an interior point (Mathlib's `ModelWithCorners.interior`), so its preferred chart, restricted to the preimage of the open half-plane, is a chart at it with values where the inverse of `interiorEmbedding 1` is defined, and the transitions of these restricted charts are restrictions of the given ones; the two constructions are inverse up to the identity diffeomorphism. Every passage between `𝓡 2` and `𝓡∂ 2` in this roadmap (§§0.1, 0.2, 0.0h, 3.1, 5.4, 5.4a) is this comparison.

Prove the **orientation comparison**: for a PL structure compatible with the smooth structure, transporting the cyclic order of each oriented triangle by its smooth immersion is a bijection from the coherent orientations of §0.0c onto the smooth orientations of `M` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Manifold.Orientation`); in particular a compatible smoothing of an orientable PL surface is `TauCeti.Orientable`, and a connected one has exactly two smooth orientations.

**Checks.**

- The radial projection of the boundary of the tetrahedron onto the round sphere is a smooth triangulation, each flat triangle projecting by a smooth immersion, so this PL structure is compatible with the round smooth structure.
- The closed disc triangulated as one triangle is not a smooth triangulation for any smooth structure: at each corner two boundary edges leave the vertex, a half-plane chart sends both into its boundary line, and the differential of the triangle there has rank at most one. Its barycentric subdivision is one for the smoothing that rounds the corners, so compatibility is asked up to a PL homeomorphism, equivalently of a subdivision, and not of the given complex.
- Compatibility is not the requirement that the smooth charts be piecewise linear: a map that is both smooth and piecewise linear is locally affine, and an affine atlas on the sphere would develop into a local diffeomorphism to `ℝ²` with open compact image.
- The model comparison on the round sphere: the stereographic charts composed with `interiorEmbedding 1` give an `𝓡∂ 2` atlas with empty boundary, while the closed unit disc with its `𝓡∂ 2` atlas has the unit circle as boundary and so admits no `𝓡 2` atlas; the converse construction asks for empty boundary.
- On the torus the shear `(x, y) ↦ (x, x + y)` is a diffeomorphism for both atlases, and the rotation family `(x, y) ↦ (x + t, y)` is a diffeotopy for both, so the comparison does not change which maps are smooth.
- The orientation comparison on the torus with the seven-vertex triangulation, made compatible with the flat structure by a subdivision: its two coherent orientations go to the two smooth orientations `dx ∧ dy` and `−dx ∧ dy`; a triangulated Möbius band has no coherent orientation and its smoothing no smooth orientation, so the bijection does not invent orientations.

### 0.0f Polygon schemas and the classification of compact surfaces

A **polygon schema** is a finite word of sides (`SchemaSide`), each an edge letter `i` with a flag saying whether it is read against the counterclockwise direction, or a free side. Its realization `schemaRealization w` is the closed unit disc of `ℂ`, its boundary circle cut into `m` equal arcs for a word of length `m`, the `j`-th arc parametrized counterclockwise by `schemaPoint m j t = exp(2πi(j + t)/m)` for `t ∈ [0, 1]`, modulo the equivalence relation generated by `schemaGluing w`: the point at parameter `t` of a side carrying the letter `i` is identified with the point of every other side carrying `i` at parameter `t` when the two flags agree and at parameter `1 − t` when they differ, so that the arrows of the letter match; free sides are not glued. The realization is compact as a quotient of the disc. The **standard surfaces** `StandardSurface` are `StandardSurface.orientable g b`, of genus `g` with `b` boundary circles, and `StandardSurface.nonorientable k b` with `k ≥ 1` crosscaps and `b` boundary circles. Their canonical word `StandardSurface.word` is `a a⁻¹` for the sphere `orientable 0 0`, and otherwise the `g` handle blocks `aᵢ bᵢ aᵢ⁻¹ bᵢ⁻¹`, respectively the `k` crosscap blocks `aᵢ aᵢ`, followed by the `b` hole blocks `cⱼ hⱼ cⱼ⁻¹` with `hⱼ` free (`StandardSurface.holeWord`); the model `StandardSurface.model` is the realization of the canonical word. Record the invariants of a standard surface: `StandardSurface.eulerChar`, equal to `2 − 2g − b` and `2 − k − b`; `StandardSurface.genus`, equal to `g` and `k`; `StandardSurface.boundaryCount`, equal to `b`; and the predicate `StandardSurface.IsOrientable` of the orientable family.

For a topological surface `M` define `Surface.boundaryComponentCount`, the number of connected components of `(𝓡∂ 2).boundary M`, and `Surface.IsOrientable`, the existence of a coherent orientation of some PL structure on `M` (§0.0c); by the orientation comparison of §0.0e this holds exactly when some smooth structure on `M` compatible with its topology is `TauCeti.Orientable`, the condition the Lean signature states. Both are invariant under homeomorphisms: the boundary count by `image_boundary` (§0.0b), orientability because homeomorphisms transport orientations (§0.0c).

Prove `StandardSurface.isSurface_model`: every model is a compact connected Hausdorff surface with `b` boundary circles, orientable exactly in the orientable family; and `StandardSurface.eulerChar_model`: `TauCeti.eulerChar S.model = S.eulerChar`, the model having finite CW type by Radó's theorem. Route. The relation identifies finitely many boundary arcs of the disc by explicit homeomorphisms, so the quotient map is closed and the quotient Hausdorff. Charts: at the image of an interior point of the disc, the disc itself; at an interior point of a glued side, the two half-discs at its two copies glued along their diameters; at an interior point of a free side, a half-disc; at a vertex class, the corner sectors of its members glued cyclically into a disc, or in order into a half-disc when the class meets a free side. The cone triangulation of the polygon from the centre, after two barycentric subdivisions, is compatible with the gluing and has no simplex two of whose vertices are identified, so it descends to a PL structure on the model (§0.0b). The model is also a finite CW complex with one open disc, one open edge per class of sides and one vertex per vertex class, and `TauCeti.eulerChar_eq_cwEulerChar` makes its Euler characteristic the alternating count of these cells. The corners of the handle and crosscap blocks and the first corners of the hole blocks form one vertex class and each hole block adds one more, which gives `(1 + b) − (2g + 2b) + 1 = 2 − 2g − b` and `(1 + b) − (k + 2b) + 1 = 2 − k − b`, and `2 − 1 + 1 = 2` for `a a⁻¹`. The free sides `hⱼ` form the `b` boundary circles. On the orientable models the counterclockwise orientation of the disc is coherent, every gluing `x … x⁻¹` reversing the direction of the side; on a nonorientable model a strip across the two sides of a crosscap block `aᵢ aᵢ` is a Möbius band, whose orientation double cover is connected (§0.0c), so there is no coherent orientation.

Prove the **classification of compact surfaces**: existence, every compact connected surface, with or without boundary, is homeomorphic to a standard model; `StandardSurface.homeomorph_unique`, homeomorphic models are equal; `classification_of_surfaces`, the unique-existence form; and `homeomorph_iff_invariants`, two compact connected surfaces are homeomorphic exactly when they have the same Euler characteristic `TauCeti.eulerChar`, orientability and number of boundary components. Route for existence (Massey, Chapter 1, §§5–7 and 10). Radó's theorem (§0.0b) gives a PL structure whose boundary edges triangulate the boundary. Since `M` is connected and every vertex link is connected, the triangles can be listed so that each shares an edge with an earlier one; gluing them in that order along a spanning tree of the dual graph builds one polygon, a disc at each stage. Every interior edge outside the tree occurs twice on its boundary and every boundary edge once, so `M` is the realization of the boundary word, boundary edges read as free sides. The elementary moves change a word without changing its realization up to homeomorphism: rotating the word cyclically and renaming letters (a rotation of the disc), cancelling an adjacent pair `x x⁻¹` (a fold), and cutting the polygon along a chord and regluing the two pieces along a paired side (both realizations are quotients of the same two discs). Massey's reduction applies them to collect the free sides into hole blocks `c h c⁻¹`, to bring each pair `x … x` together into a crosscap block `x x`, to make each interlocked pair `x … y … x⁻¹ … y⁻¹` a handle block `x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹`, to reduce the corners outside the hole blocks to one vertex class, and to trade a handle next to a crosscap for two crosscaps (`x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹ z z` becomes `x x y y z z`); the end word is a canonical word. Route for uniqueness: the invariants of a model are `S.eulerChar`, `S.IsOrientable` and `S.boundaryCount` (`StandardSurface.isSurface_model`, `StandardSurface.eulerChar_model`), all homeomorphism invariants (`Homeomorph.eulerChar_eq` and the invariance above); orientability decides the family, the boundary count gives `b`, and the Euler characteristic then gives `g = (2 − χ − b)/2` or `k = 2 − χ − b`. The complete invariant follows from existence and uniqueness. Define the **genus** `Surface.genus M` as the genus of the standard model homeomorphic to `M`, and `0` for a space homeomorphic to none, formally the least genus of a standard model homeomorphic to `M`, which needs no choice and is the genus of the model by uniqueness; on a model it is the genus of that model, by `StandardSurface.homeomorph_unique`, and `Surface.eulerChar_eq_two_sub_two_mul_genus` gives `TauCeti.eulerChar M = 2 − 2·Surface.genus M` for a compact connected orientable surface with empty boundary, whose model is `orientable g 0` by the classification. On the underlying surface of a compact connected Riemann surface, closed and orientable, `Surface.genus` is therefore FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 5 item 1's topological genus, which that roadmap defines by `χ = 2 − 2g` from the same Euler characteristic.

**Checks.**

- The sphere `orientable 0 0` is the two-gon `a a⁻¹` whose lower half-circle is glued to the upper one by complex conjugation: `2 − 1 + 1 = 2`. The disc `orientable 0 1` is `c h c⁻¹`, the arc `c` folded onto `c⁻¹` and `h` left free: `2 − 2 + 1 = 1`, with one boundary circle.
- The projective plane `nonorientable 1 0` is `a a`, antipodal points of the circle identified: `1 − 1 + 1 = 1`; the Klein bottle `nonorientable 2 0` has `χ = 0`.
- Each invariant is needed: the torus and the closed genus-two surface agree on orientability and boundary count, the torus and the Klein bottle on Euler characteristic and boundary count, and the torus and the annulus `orientable 0 2` on Euler characteristic and orientability.
- Dyck's theorem: the connected sum of a torus and a projective plane, the word `x y x⁻¹ y⁻¹ z z`, is `nonorientable 3 0`, with `χ = −1`; this is why the nonorientable family carries no handle parameter, and uniqueness would fail with one.
- A genus read from the Euler characteristic alone, `(2 − χ)/2`, would give the Klein bottle genus one like the torus and the disc genus one half; `Surface.genus` reads the family and the boundary count through the classification.

### 0.0g Branched covers and the topological Riemann–Hurwitz formula

Let `X` and `Y` be topological surfaces. A map `p : X → Y` has a **power model** of exponent `n ≥ 1` at `x`, `HasPowerMapModelAt p x n`, when there are topological charts `φ` at `x` and `ψ` at `p x`, onto the open unit disc of `ℂ`, with sources in the interiors and centred at `x` and `p x`, such that `p` carries the source of `φ` into that of `ψ` and `ψ ∘ p = φⁿ` there; no analytic compatibility is asked. A **branched cover** with a finite set `B` of marked interior values and exponent function `e`, `IsBranchedCover p B e`, is a continuous surjection with finite fibres that restricts to a covering map (Mathlib's `IsCoveringMap`) over the complement of `B`, satisfies `p⁻¹(∂Y) = ∂X` and is a local homeomorphism along `∂X` (Mathlib's `IsLocalHomeomorphOn`), and has a power model of exponent `e x` at every interior point, with `e = 1` off `p⁻¹(B)` and on the boundary.

Prove the basic theory. The preimage `p⁻¹(B)` is finite, a finite union of finite fibres. `IsBranchedCover.local_degree_unique`: for Hausdorff surfaces the exponent function is determined by `p`, independently of the charts and of regular values added to `B`; `e x` is the number of sheets of the covering that `p` restricts to over a small punctured disc around `p x`, on the punctured neighbourhood of `x` that a power model provides, and for `z ↦ zⁿ` that covering is connected with `n` sheets. `IsBranchedCover.exists_degree`: over compact connected Hausdorff surfaces the weighted fibre count `Σ_{p x = y} e x` is a positive constant `d`, the **degree**. Route: `p` is closed, as a continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff one, so a small enough disc around `y` has its preimage inside disjoint power-model neighbourhoods of the points of the fibre, and each of them contributes `e x` points over every nearby value off `B`; the count is therefore locally constant, hence constant on the connected `Y`, and positive by surjectivity. Prove the **Riemann–Hurwitz formula** `riemannHurwitz`: for compact connected surfaces, of finite CW type by Radó's theorem, `TauCeti.eulerChar X = d·TauCeti.eulerChar Y − Σ_{x ∈ p⁻¹(B)} (e x − 1)`. Route: by Radó's theorem and subdivision (§0.0b), `Y` has a PL structure with the points of `B` among its vertices and the boundary a subcomplex. Each closed simplex with its vertices in `B` removed is contractible, locally path connected and disjoint from `B`, so the covering restricted to it is trivial with `d` sheets, the fibre cardinality off `B` (Mathlib's `IsCoveringMap.existsUnique_continuousMap_lifts`); the closures of the sheets are the lifted closed simplices. At a preimage `x` of a point of `B` the power model shows the lifts closing up around `x` in `e x` sectors for each sector downstairs, and at a boundary vertex the local homeomorphism keeps the half-disc link. After one barycentric subdivision of the triangulation of `Y` the lifted simplices form a finite combinatorial surface triangulating `X`, with `d` times as many edges and triangles and `d·V − Σ(e x − 1)` vertices, and `AbstractSimplicialComplex.eulerChar_eq_finsum_of_homeomorph` (§0.0c) gives the formula. Neither the classification nor homology enters.

**Checks.**

- The identity of a surface, with all exponents `1`, has degree one and no correction; assigning it exponent `2` at a marked interior point fails the power model, since the identity is injective near the point while `z ↦ z²` is not.
- `z ↦ zⁿ` on the Riemann sphere, `n ≥ 1`, has exponent `n` at `0` and `∞`: `2 = 2n − 2(n − 1)`. On the closed unit disc it has the single branch value `0` and is an unramified `n`-fold cover along the boundary circle: `1 = n − (n − 1)`.
- The orientation double cover of the projective plane (§0.0c) is unbranched of degree two: `2 = 2·1`. An unbranched cover of degree `k` of a closed genus-`g` surface has `χ = k(2 − 2g)`, which for unbranched covers is already Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.eulerChar_eq_mul_of_isCoveringMap`, hence genus `1 + k(g − 1)` when it is connected and orientable (§0.0f); Layer 10.2 uses that lemma directly.
- At a branch value of `z ↦ zⁿ` the fibre has one point while the weighted count is `n`; a degree defined by raw fibre cardinality would not be constant.

### 0.0h Mapping class groups of compact surfaces

For a topological space `M`, the homeomorphisms isotopic to the identity in Tau Ceti's sense (`TauCeti.Isotopic`, homotopies through embeddings) form a normal subgroup `isotopicToIdSubgroup M` of `M ≃ₜ M`: from isotopies `F_t` from `f` to the identity and `G_t` from `g` to the identity, `F_t ∘ g` followed by `G_t` joins `f ∘ g` to the identity, `f⁻¹ ∘ F_t` joins the identity to `f⁻¹`, and `h ∘ F_t ∘ h⁻¹` joins `h f h⁻¹` to the identity, every slice remaining an embedding. The **mapping class group** `MappingClassGroupFull M` is the quotient group. The smooth groups are GeometricTopology's: for a compact smooth surface `F`, Layer 1.7.2's `Mod(F)` is the group of diffeomorphisms fixing `∂F` pointwise modulo isotopies fixing `∂F` pointwise, with `Mod⁺(F)` for oriented `F`, and its Layer 11.2.1 items 2 and 5 identify `Mod(F)` with the isotopy classes of homeomorphisms fixing `∂F` pointwise. For a closed surface the boundary condition is empty, and the forgetful homomorphism `Mod(F) → MappingClassGroupFull F`, induced by Tau Ceti's `Diffeomorph.toHomeomorphHom`, is an isomorphism: surjective because every homeomorphism is isotopic to a diffeomorphism (§0.0e) and injective by item 5; it descends because the slices of a diffeotopy form an isotopy (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.AmbientIsotopy.isotopic` applied to `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.toAmbientIsotopy`). For a surface with nonempty boundary `MappingClassGroupFull` lets the boundary move and differs from `Mod(F)`, as the annulus below shows.

For a PL structure `s`, a self-homeomorphism is **piecewise linear**, `PLStructure.IsPLHomeomorph`, when in the barycentric coordinates of `s` it is the restriction of a map that is piecewise linear on the realization. The homeomorphisms piecewise linear in both directions form the subgroup `PLStructure.plHomeomorphSubgroup s`, closed under composition by Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.IsPLOn.comp`; prove that the inverse of a piecewise-linear self-homeomorphism is piecewise linear, so that the second condition follows from the first (on each simplex of a subdivision on which the map is affine and injective its inverse is affine). Two of them are **PL isotopic**, `PLStructure.IsPLIsotopic`, when an isotopy through homeomorphisms joins them whose level-preserving map of `[0, 1] × |K|` is piecewise linear in these coordinates; those PL isotopic to the identity form a normal subgroup: prove that PL isotopies concatenate after rescaling time and conjugate by PL homeomorphisms. The Lean form `PLStructure.plIsotopicToIdSubgroup s` is the normal subgroup generated by them, which by this target consists of exactly them, and the quotient is the **PL mapping class group** `MappingClassGroupPL s`. The **forgetful homomorphism** `MappingClassGroupPL.toTop s` is induced by the inclusion of the PL homeomorphisms; it descends because a PL isotopy is an isotopy.

Prove `MappingClassGroupPL.toTop_bijective`: surjectivity is isotopy to a PL homeomorphism (§0.0b, from GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 item 4; Epstein, Appendix, Theorem A4, p. 107), and injectivity is the PL isotopy theorem (§0.0d; Epstein, Theorems 6.3 and 6.4 a) in their piecewise-linear readings, pp. 99–101), two PL homeomorphisms that are isotopic being PL isotopic.

**Checks.**

- On the closed disc complex conjugation is a nontrivial element of `MappingClassGroupFull`, since it reverses the orientation (§0.0c), while an orientation-preserving homeomorphism is isotopic to one fixing the boundary circle and then to the identity by Alexander's trick (Tau Ceti's `Homeomorph.alexanderIsotopyClosedBall`); so the group has two elements.
- On the annulus `ℝ/ℤ × [0, 1]` the twist `([θ], t) ↦ ([θ + t], t)` is trivial in `MappingClassGroupFull`, through `([θ], t) ↦ ([θ + s t], t)` whose slices turn the outer circle, while relative to the boundary it is a Dehn twist of infinite order in GeometricTopology Layer 1.7.2's `Mod`; a definition of `MappingClassGroupFull` by isotopies fixing the boundary would make it nontrivial.
- The subgroup laws compose on the left: `f⁻¹ ∘ F_t` joins the identity to `f⁻¹` with embedded slices, without asking `(t, x) ↦ F_t⁻¹(x)` to be continuous, which can fail off compact spaces.
- On the boundary of the tetrahedron, radially projected to the round sphere, the homeomorphism `x ↦ (‖x‖/ε) x` on a disc of radius `ε` in a face, extended by the identity, is not piecewise linear, so it is not in `PLStructure.plHomeomorphSubgroup`, but it is isotopic to the identity through `x ↦ ((1 − s) + s‖x‖/ε) x`, `s ∈ [0, 1]`, each slice an embedding fixing the circle of radius `ε`; its class in `MappingClassGroupFull` is the image of the identity class, so the surjectivity of `MappingClassGroupPL.toTop` is not discharged by the given map.
- A simplicial automorphism of the boundary of the tetrahedron induced by an even permutation of the vertices preserves the orientation and is trivial in `MappingClassGroupPL`, while one induced by a transposition reverses it and is not; the PL group of the sphere has two elements although the simplicial automorphism group of this complex is `S₄`, so PL isotopy, not equality, is the relation.

### 0.2 The diffeomorphism group, paths and smooth isotopies

For a compact surface `M` (closed, or with boundary and a prescribed boundary collar) let `Diff(M)` be Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡∂ 2) M ∞` with `Diffeomorph.weakWhitneyTopology`, identified for a closed surface charted on `𝓡 2` with `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) M ∞` by the comparison of the two models (§0.0e), and for the closed disc containing Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.DiscDiff 2` of diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise; it is a topological group by `Diffeomorph.isTopologicalGroup`, and GeometricTopology Layer 3 is the owner of that structure. Define `Diff₀(M)` as the path component of the identity. A smooth isotopy from `f` to `g` is a diffeotopy `Φ` with `Φ.final = f⁻¹ g`, equivalently a `C^∞` map `F : [0,1] × M → M` whose slices are diffeomorphisms with `F_0 = f` and `F_1 = g`. Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.joined_one_final` gives one half of the comparison: a smooth isotopy is a continuous path in `Diff(M)`. Prove the converse, the **parameter-smoothing theorem**: a continuous path in `Diff(M)` is homotopic relative to its endpoints to a smooth isotopy, relative to the boundary collar when the path fixes it. Route: cover the compact track of the path by finitely many chart neighbourhoods, convolve the parameter dependence chartwise with a partition of unity in the time variable while keeping the slices diffeomorphisms (a `C^∞`-small perturbation of a diffeomorphism of a compact manifold is a diffeomorphism), and keep the endpoints and the collar data by cutting off the mollification near `t = 0, 1` and near the collar. (For a closed surface `M`, `Diff(M)` is an open submanifold of the manifold `C^∞(M, M)` whose charts at `f` are `s ↦ exp ∘ s` for sections `s` of `f^*TM` and a Riemannian exponential map, and the smooth curves into it are exactly the smooth maps `ℝ × M → M`: Kriegl–Michor, *The Convenient Setting of Global Analysis*, Theorem 42.1, p. 439, Lemma 42.5, p. 442, and Theorem 43.1, pp. 454–455, whose manifolds have no boundary. For `D²` with a collar, apply the same theorems to the open disc: the diffeomorphisms of `D²` equal to the identity on the collar are the compactly supported diffeomorphisms of the open disc, and by Lemma 42.5 the smooth curves through them are the smooth maps `ℝ × D → D` that are constant in `t` off a compact set on each compact time interval.) Prove the **local isotopy lemma**: there is a `C^∞` neighbourhood of the identity in `Diff(M)` every element of which is smoothly isotopic to the identity, by chartwise geodesic or linear interpolation from the identity on a compact chart cover with a partition of unity, equivalently along the chart `s ↦ exp ∘ s` at the identity cited above, `t ↦ exp ∘ (t s)` being a smooth isotopy; translating by a diffeomorphism gives the same statement at every point of `Diff(M)`. Conclude that `Diff₀(M)` is exactly the set of diffeomorphisms smoothly isotopic to the identity, that it is open and closed in `Diff(M)`, and that `π₀ Diff(M)` is the set of smooth isotopy classes. For the surfaces used below take `M = Σ`, `M = S²` and `M = D²` with a boundary collar, the last with the `𝓡∂ 2` model.

The identity component and the isotopy relation have the following API.

- `Diff₀(M)` is a normal subgroup of `Diff(M)`, open and closed.
- `f` and `g` are joined by a path in `Diff(M)` if and only if they are smoothly isotopic (`joined_one_final` one way, the parameter-smoothing theorem the other).
- Smooth isotopy is an equivalence relation compatible with composition on both sides (`Diffeotopy.trans`, `Diffeotopy.symm`, `transDiffeomorph`).
- In the relative case the same statements hold for the closed subgroup fixing a collar pointwise.

**Checks.**

- The rotation `z ↦ e^(iθ) z` of `S²` about an axis is in `Diff₀(S²)` through the rotation path; the antipodal map is not, since it reverses orientation and orientation is locally constant on paths in `Diff(S²)`.
- On the torus the shear `(x, y) ↦ (x, x + y)` is not in `Diff₀(T²)`: it acts on `H₁` by a nontrivial matrix, and an isotopy acts trivially on homology.
- A diffeomorphism of `D²` fixing a collar and equal to a rotation by `2π` of the inner disc, spliced by an increasing angle function, is in `Diff₀(D², rel collar)` by shrinking the angle function; its identity-near-boundary condition is what Smale's disc theorem (GeometricTopology Layer 3.4) needs, not only fixing the boundary pointwise.
- A path in `Diff(M)` that is merely continuous in `t` (for example `t ↦ f_t` with `f_t` the time-`|t − 1/2|` flow of a vector field) has a smooth reparametrization through the same diffeomorphisms; a definition of isotopy that demanded the given path itself to be smooth would reject it.

*Needs:* §0.0e; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diff`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.DiscDiff`; Tau Ceti `Diffeomorph.weakWhitneyTopology`; Tau Ceti `Diffeomorph.isTopologicalGroup`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diffeotopy`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.joined_one_final`; Tau Ceti `Diffeomorph.ofSmoothFamily`; Mathlib `SmoothPartitionOfUnity`; GeometricTopology Layer 3.

### 0.3 Isotopy extension on surfaces

Isotopy extension is GeometricTopology Layer 3.3's: a smooth family of embeddings of a compact manifold into a boundaryless manifold extends to a `TauCeti.Diffeotopy` compactly supported in a prescribed neighbourhood of the trace of the family (Hirsch, *Differential Topology*, Chapter 8, §1). Read it on compact surfaces, with or without boundary: for a compact surface `M`, a compact submanifold `K` (a disc, an arc, a circle or a finite union of them) and a smooth isotopy of embeddings `e_t : K → M`, `t ∈ [0, 1]`, extended to an open interval by constancy, whose images lie in the interior of `M`, there is a smooth isotopy `ψ_t ∈ Diff₀(M)` with `ψ_0 = id` and `ψ_t ∘ e_0 = e_t` that fixes a neighbourhood of the boundary. Route: for closed `M` this is Layer 3.3 itself; when `M` has boundary apply Layer 3.3 to the boundaryless interior, with support in a neighbourhood of the trace whose closure is a compact subset of the interior, and extend by the identity. Families that move along the boundary are not covered by this reading, and Layer 5.5 does not use them: its arcs are carried relative to their endpoints. Layer 5.5 consumes this form for point pushes and for aligning curves and arcs.

**Checks.**

- For `K` a point moving along an embedded arc the extension is a point-pushing isotopy, supported in a neighbourhood of the arc; its final diffeomorphism is the point-pushing diffeomorphism along the arc.
- For `K = D²` moving by the rotation `e^(iθt)` about its centre inside a chart, the extension restricted to a larger concentric disc is a spliced rotation, and is the identity outside.
- Compactness of `K` is what the route uses, through the compactness of the track `{e_t(x)}`: on the open plane the line `ℝ × {0}` translated by `e_t(s) = (s + t, 0)` has no compactly supported extension, since any extension moves every point of the line by `t`, while a compact `K` moving in a noncompact surface, a point pushed along a segment of the plane for instance, has a compactly supported extension by the same cutoff and flow.

*Needs:* GeometricTopology Layer 3.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diffeotopy`; §0.2.

### 0.4 Markings and their equivalence

Define a marking of a Riemann surface `X` by `Σ` as an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism `m : Σ → X` of the underlying smooth surfaces, and the relation `(X, m) ∼ (Y, n)` when some biholomorphism `f : X ≃ Y` makes `f ∘ m` smoothly isotopic to `n`. Prove that `∼` is an equivalence relation (reflexive by the identity, symmetric by `f⁻¹` and `Diffeotopy.symm`, transitive by composition and `Diffeotopy.trans`). Prove that pullback of complex structures along diffeomorphisms is continuous for the `C^∞` topologies and satisfies the action laws `(f ∘ g)^* = g^* ∘ f^*` and `id^* = id`.

**Checks.**

- `(X, m) ∼ (X, m ∘ φ)` for `φ ∈ Diff₀(Σ)`; for other `φ` it holds exactly when `m ∘ φ ∘ m⁻¹` is isotopic to an automorphism of `X`, and such an automorphism acts on `H₁(X)` as `φ` does. So it fails for the shear of a torus `ℂ/Λ` with `Λ` generic, whose automorphisms `z ↦ ±z + c` act on `H₁` by `±1` while the shear acts by `[[1, 1], [0, 1]]`, and it holds in genus two for `φ = m⁻¹ ∘ h ∘ m` with `h` the hyperelliptic involution, which acts on `H₁` as `−1` and is realized by `f = h` (third Check); acting nontrivially on `H₁(Σ)` does not by itself separate the two marked surfaces.
- `(X, m) ∼ (X, m)` with `f = id`; a definition requiring `f ∘ m = n` on the nose would separate `(X, m)` from `(X, m ∘ φ)` for `φ` a small diffeomorphism.
- For `X` with a nontrivial automorphism `σ`, `(X, m) ∼ (X, σ ∘ m)` with `f = σ`; the two markings differ by the mapping class of `m⁻¹ σ m`, which is nontrivial by Layer 0.5, so the relation identifies points that `Mod⁺` moves to each other only when an automorphism realizes the class.

*Needs:* §0.2; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.trans`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.symm`.

### 0.5 The trivial automorphism kernel

Prove `Aut(X) ∩ Diff₀(Σ) = {id}` for a closed Riemann surface `X` of genus `g ≥ 2` marked by `Σ`, by the following argument, which is the roadmap's own: ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 4 gives the unique conformal curvature-`−1` metric, so a holomorphic automorphism is an isometry; an isometry smoothly isotopic to the identity lifts to the universal cover `ℍ` (Completed/UniversalCovers Stage 2, through `IsCoveringMap.existsUnique_continuousMap_lifts_of_range_le`) as an isometry commuting with the deck group, hence centralizing the cocompact surface group in `PSL₂(ℝ)`; prove that the centralizer of a nonelementary discrete group in `PSL₂(ℝ)` is trivial, because an element commuting with two hyperbolic elements with distinct fixed-point pairs would preserve both pairs. The same argument, with the centralizer taken in `PGL₂(ℝ)`, proves that the map from the group of conformal and anticonformal isometries of a closed hyperbolic surface to `Mod±(Σ)` is injective; Layer 5.6 uses this form.

**Checks.**

- On the torus `ℂ/Λ` the translations are automorphisms isotopic to the identity, so the hypothesis `g ≥ 2` is used; the statement fails for `g = 1`.
- For the hyperelliptic involution of a genus-two curve the induced mapping class is nontrivial: it acts as `−1` on `H₁`.
- The centralizer argument fails for an elementary group: the cyclic group generated by one hyperbolic element has an infinite centralizer, the one-parameter group through it.

*Needs:* ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.UniversalCover.deckFundamentalGroupEquiv`; Mathlib `IsCoveringMap.existsUnique_continuousMap_lifts_of_range_le`; RiemannianGeometry R4 item 7; §0.2.

### Examples

- The genus-two surface `S_2` as the smoothed standard surface, with the transported triangulation, its hyperelliptic involution as an element of `Diff⁺(S_2)` outside `Diff₀(S_2)`, and the isotopy extension of a point push along an embedded arc.

## Layer 1: Complex structures and the marked quotient

### 1.1 Almost-complex structures and integrability in dimension two

Use the smooth almost-complex endomorphism `J : TΣ → TΣ` with `J² = −1` as the tensor carrier, and define orientation compatibility as `(v, Jv)` being positively oriented for every nonzero `v`. Prove that the Nijenhuis tensor of `J` vanishes in real dimension two: it is tensorial, skew-symmetric and `J`-antilinear in each argument, so on a two-dimensional space spanned by `v, Jv` it is determined by `N(v, Jv)`, which the antilinearity forces to be zero. Apply ComplexKahlerGeometry K1's Newlander–Nirenberg equivalence to construct the complex atlas of `J`, and prove that the resulting native curve induces exactly the chosen smooth structure and orientation. Conversely recover `J` from a complex atlas as multiplication by `i` in holomorphic charts and prove that the two constructions are inverse under atlas equivalence. Build `𝒥(Σ)`, the space of orientation-compatible integrable almost-complex structures, as a closed subset of the sections of `End(TΣ)` with the `C^∞` topology.

**Checks.**

- On `ℝ²` with `J = [[0, −1], [1, 0]]` the induced atlas is the identity chart of `ℂ`, and `(v, Jv) = (e₁, e₂)` is positive; `−J` induces the conjugate atlas and is not orientation compatible with the standard orientation.
- The Nijenhuis tensor of the standard `J` on `ℝ⁴` with a non-integrable perturbation is nonzero, so the dimension-two argument uses `dim = 2`; the proof must not claim vanishing in general.
- `𝒥(S²)` is nonempty and a single `Diff₀`-orbit (every complex structure on the sphere is biholomorphic to `ℙ¹`, by ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 3); `𝒥(T²)` has orbit space `ℍ`, not a point.

*Needs:* ComplexKahlerGeometry K1; §0.1; §0.2.

### 1.2 The marked quotient

The group `Diff₀⁺(Σ) = Diff₀(Σ)` acts on `𝒥(Σ)` by pullback. Define `Teich(Σ)` as the quotient `𝒥(Σ)/Diff₀(Σ)` with the quotient topology, and prove that it is naturally bijective with the marked-biholomorphism classes of Layer 0.4 on the carrier of compact complex structures on the fixed smooth surface: a class `[J]` goes to `(Σ_J, id)` and a marked surface `(X, m)` to `m^* J_X`. Give the marked carrier the transported topology and prove it agrees with the marked-slice topology of Layer 2.1. The quotient relation uses isotopy through `Diff₀`; unmarked conformal structures enter only after the separate forgetful quotient of Layer 3.3.

The quotient has the following API.

- The quotient map `𝒥(Σ) → Teich(Σ)` is open and continuous.
- `[J] = [J']` if and only if `J' = φ^* J` for some `φ ∈ Diff₀(Σ)`, if and only if `(Σ_J, id) ∼ (Σ_(J'), id)`.
- The bijection with marked surfaces is natural under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of `Σ`.

**Checks.**

- The structures `J` and `φ^* J` for `φ` a point push give the same point; `J` and `φ^* J` for `φ` the hyperelliptic involution of a genus-two curve give the same point too, by Layer 0.4's third check, although `φ ∉ Diff₀`; the quotient by `Diff₀` does not separate them because the involution is realized by an automorphism.
- For the shear `φ` of a torus, `J` and `φ^* J` are different points of the analogous quotient for `g = 1`, since the shear is not isotopic to any automorphism of a generic torus; this distinguishes the marked quotient from the unmarked one.
- A set of structures converging in `C^∞` to `J` gives a convergent sequence in `Teich(Σ)`; a definition with the discrete topology on the quotient would not.

*Needs:* §1.1; §0.4; §0.2.

### Examples

- Genus two: the point of `Teich(S_2)` of a curve `y² = f(x)` with the identity marking and its image under the hyperelliptic involution, which is the same point.

## Layer 2: Marked deformation atlas and dimension

### 2.1 The marked holomorphic chart adapter

### 2.2 Transition maps and the cocycle law

### 2.3 Hausdorff and second countable

Prove that `Teich(Σ)` is Hausdorff: use D0 item 5's properness of the actual diffeomorphism action, Layer 0.5's freeness and the slices of Layer 2.1 to show that the orbit relation is closed and that distinct orbits have disjoint saturated neighbourhoods. These are properties of the global marked carrier, proved from its construction, not assumptions on an atlas.

**Checks.**

## Layer 3: Mapping-class actions and the moduli groupoid

### 3.1 The mapping class groups

Define `Mod±(Σ) := π₀ Diff(Σ)`, the `ZerothHomotopy` of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) Σ ∞` with the weak Whitney topology, with the group law induced by composition; it descends because `Diff(Σ)` is a topological group (`Diffeomorph.isTopologicalGroup`), and by Layer 0.2 it is the group of smooth isotopy classes of diffeomorphisms. Define `Mod⁺(Σ)` as the image of `Diff⁺(Σ)`, of index two. Identify `Mod±(Σ)` with GeometricTopology Layer 1.7.2's mapping class group `Mod(Σ)` of the closed surface, the `𝓡 2` atlas of `Σ` read as an `𝓡∂ 2` atlas with empty boundary by the comparison of the two models (§0.0e), which identifies `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) Σ ∞` with `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡∂ 2) Σ ∞` and their diffeotopies: both are the diffeomorphisms modulo smooth isotopy, by Layer 0.2's identification of `π₀ Diff(Σ)` with the smooth isotopy classes, and the group laws agree because both are induced by composition; `Mod⁺(Σ)` is Layer 1.7.2's `Mod⁺(Σ)` for the chosen orientation. Composed with the forgetful isomorphism `Mod(Σ) → MappingClassGroupFull Σ` of §0.0h (GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 items 2 and 5), this identifies `Mod±(Σ)` with `MappingClassGroupFull Σ`; Layers 3 and 4 use only the smooth carrier, because the action on `Teich(Σ)` is by pullback of smooth structures.

**Checks.**

- `Mod±(S²)` has two elements and `Mod⁺(S²)` is trivial, by GeometricTopology Layer 3.4's `Diff(S²) ≃ O(3)`; §5.4a's `mcg_sphere_full` gives the same answer for the topological group.
- `Mod⁺(T²) ≃ SL₂(ℤ)` and `Mod±(T²) ≃ GL₂(ℤ)` through the action on `H₁`, matching §5.4a's `mcg_torus_full` and its orientation-preserving form; a definition of `Mod±` by homotopy classes of continuous self-maps would give the same group for the torus but would not be a group of diffeomorphism classes.
- The hyperelliptic involution of `S_2` is a nontrivial element of `Mod⁺(S_2)` of order two.

### 3.2 The action on marked structures

On the positively oriented complex-structure carrier define, for a diffeomorphism `f`, `f ⋅ J = ε(f) · (f⁻¹)^* J` with `ε(f) = +1` if `f` preserves orientation and `−1` otherwise; on marked surfaces `f ⋅ (X, m) = (X, m ∘ f⁻¹)` for orientation-preserving `f` and `f ⋅ (X, m) = (X̄, m ∘ f⁻¹)` for orientation-reversing `f`, where `X̄` is the conjugate curve. Multiplication by `−1` on the carrier is complex conjugation and restores the fixed orientation. Prove that the two formulas define the same left action of `Mod±(Σ)` on `Teich(Σ)`, that `Mod⁺(Σ)` acts holomorphically by changing markings, and that the orientation-reversing coset acts antiholomorphically in the atlas of Layer 2.

**Checks.**

- For `f` the orientation-reversing involution of `S_2` that reflects the standard embedded model in a plane, `f ⋅ J` equals the conjugate of `(f⁻¹)^* J`; without the sign `ε(f)` the pulled-back structure would induce the opposite orientation and not lie in `𝒥(Σ)`.
- Action law on a reversing pair: for two orientation-reversing diffeomorphisms `f, g`, `ε(fg) = +1` so `(fg) ⋅ J = ((fg)⁻¹)^* J`, while `f ⋅ (g ⋅ J) = (−1)(−1)(f⁻¹)^*((g⁻¹)^* J) = (g⁻¹ ∘ f⁻¹)^* J = ((fg)⁻¹)^* J`; the two agree. A convention `f ⋅ J = ε(f) f^* J` would give `f ⋅ (g ⋅ J) = (gf)^* J ≠ (fg)^* J`, a right action.
- For `f ∈ Diff₀(Σ)` the action is trivial on `Teich(Σ)`, and for `f` the hyperelliptic involution of genus two it is trivial too (Layer 8.5); a definition of the action on `𝒥(Σ)` rather than on the quotient would not have the second property.

*Needs:* §1.2; §2.2; §3.1.

### 3.3 Proper discontinuity and the moduli groupoid

Consume ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 5's properness of the diffeomorphism action on stable marked curves and D3 item 5's local-slice subclause. For convergent pairs related by mapping classes choose smoothly convergent slice representatives and intertwining diffeomorphisms; D0 item 5 gives a convergent subsequence of diffeomorphisms and inverses; Layer 0.2's local isotopy lemma shows the mapping classes eventually agree. This proves finite transporters on compact sets, first for `Mod⁺` and then for `Mod±` by its two cosets: the action is properly discontinuous. Define the oriented moduli orbifold as the proper étale action groupoid `Teich(Σ) ⋊ Mod⁺(Σ)` and its coarse quotient, and separately the quotient by the full group, which forgets orientation. Properness gives finite isotropy in quotient charts, and the isotropy at a point is its stabilizer, identified in Layer 5.6.

**Checks.**

### 4.1 The isometry group of the hyperbolic plane

Route: the Möbius maps of positive determinant preserve the hyperbolic distance by Mathlib's instance `IsIsometricSMul SL(2, ℝ) ℍ` after scaling the matrix to determinant one, and the conjugation `z ↦ −z̄` preserves the metric `(|dx|² + dy²)/y²` directly, the only new isometry computation; an isometry is determined by its values on three points in general position, so after composing with a Möbius map it fixes `i` and a direction and is the identity or the reflection; two matrices inducing the same map are proportional. **Checks.**

### 4.2 The geometric surface group and the deck dictionary

Define the geometric group `Π := (π₁ⁿᵃᵗ(Σ, x₀))ᵐᵒᵖ`; then `op(p) * op(q) = op(p.trans q)`, so multiplication in `Π` is first-traverse concatenation. For every native `FundamentalGroup` or outer-automorphism construction state the inversion equivalence `ι : Π ≃* π₁ⁿᵃᵗ`, `ι(op p) = p⁻¹`, and the induced maps on automorphism and inner-automorphism groups. Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.UniversalCover.deckFundamentalGroupEquiv x₀ : deck proj ≃* (FundamentalGroup Σ x₀)ᵐᵒᵖ` is, with `Π`, an isomorphism `deck(proj) ≃* Π`. Define `η : Π ≃* deck(proj)` by the endpoint of a lifted based loop: `η(op p)` is the deck transformation sending the base lift `x̃₀` to the endpoint of the lift of `p` from `x̃₀`. The pinned `deckFundamentalGroupEquiv_symm_op` gives `η(op p) = loopDeck(p⁻¹)`, the inverse of `deckFundamentalGroupEquiv`, because `loopDeck(p⁻¹)` acts by `p⁻¹ •` and the monodromy of `p` from the base lift is `p⁻¹ • x̃₀` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.UniversalCover.monodromy_basepointLift`); prove this endpoint formula `η(op p)(x̃₀) = end of the lift of p from x̃₀` and `η(op p * op q) = η(op p) ∘ η(op q)` by path lifting. Prove that `Π` is torsion-free once a hyperbolic metric exists (Layer 4.5): a finite-order element acts on `ℍ` as a finite-order orientation-preserving isometry, hence fixes a point, contradicting freeness of the deck action.

The deck dictionary has the following API.

- `η` is a group isomorphism `Π ≃* deck(proj)`, with `η(op p)` the deck transformation sending the base lift to the endpoint of the lift of `p`: lifting `p.trans q` from `x̃₀` follows the lift of `p` to `η(op p)(x̃₀)` and then the `η(op p)`-translate of the lift of `q`, so it ends at `η(op p)(η(op q)(x̃₀))`, which is the homomorphism law for `op p * op q = op(p.trans q)`.
- Under `ι`, inner automorphisms of `Π` correspond to inner automorphisms of `π₁ⁿᵃᵗ`, and `Out(Π) ≃ Out(π₁ⁿᵃᵗ)`.
- For a normal subgroup `N ◁ Π` the deck group of the cover attached to `ι(N)` is identified with `Π/N` through `deckSubgroupQuotientProjEquivOfNormal` and the same inversion.

**Checks.**

- For two loops `p, q` at `x₀`, the lift of `p.trans q` ends at `η(op p)(η(op q)(x̃₀))` only with the stated inverse in `loopDeck(p⁻¹)`; with `loopDeck(p)` the endpoint formula would be an antihomomorphism.
- On the circle, `Π ≃ ℤ` and `η(op(generator))` is the unit translation of `ℝ` in the direction of the loop; the opposite convention gives the inverse translation.
- A free-group deck transformation is never of finite order; this check is the torsion-freeness in the case of a wedge of two circles, before any metric.

### 4.4 The Euler number of a representation and the commutator formula

### 5.2 Dehn–Nielsen–Baer realization by the Douady–Earle extension

An isomorphism of cocompact surface groups `α : Π ≃ Π'` gives an equivariant quasi-isometry of their hyperbolic universal covers: extend the orbit map between the Cayley graphs by the cocompact orbit maps into each `ℍ`. Prove that its boundary extension `φ : S¹ → S¹` is a homeomorphism with `φ ∘ γ = α(γ) ∘ φ` for every `γ` in the source group. Apply the Douady–Earle barycentric extension `E(φ)`: for every homeomorphism of the circle it is a homeomorphism of the closed disc whose restriction to the open disc is a real-analytic diffeomorphism (Douady–Earle 1986, Theorem 1), and it is conformally natural, `E(g ∘ φ ∘ h) = g ∘ E(φ) ∘ h` for Möbius automorphisms `g, h` of the disc (Douady–Earle 1986, Theorem 1). Prove here, from the definition of the conformal barycentre as the unique `w` with `∫ (ζ − w)/(1 − w̄ ζ) dμ(ζ) = 0` for the harmonic measure pushed forward by `φ`, that the same naturality holds for the anticonformal involution `ζ ↦ ζ̄` and hence for the full group of conformal and anticonformal automorphisms; quasisymmetry of `φ` is not needed for any of this. Naturality gives `E(φ) ∘ γ = α(γ) ∘ E(φ)`, including for orientation-reversing `φ`, so `E(φ)` descends to a real-analytic diffeomorphism `Σ → Σ'` inducing the prescribed outer automorphism; no separate smoothing step is used. ### 5.4 The smooth Baer–Epstein kernel theorem

Prove `baer_epstein_smooth`: a diffeomorphism of a closed surface, orientable or not, homotopic to the identity through continuous maps is the final slice of a smooth diffeotopy. Route: read the `𝓡 2` atlas of the surface as a `𝓡∂ 2` atlas with empty boundary by the comparison of the two models (§0.0e), which carries diffeotopies to diffeotopies; Epstein's theorem `isotopic_of_homotopic_closedSurface` (§0.0d; Epstein, Theorem 6.4 a), p. 101, which asks orientation preservation only of the plane and the cylinders) on the underlying homeomorphism gives a topological isotopy to the identity; GeometricTopology's smoothing of isotopies (Layer 11.2.1 item 5, with empty boundary, for surfaces orientable or not), applied to the identity and the diffeomorphism, gives a diffeotopy ending at the diffeomorphism, which Layer 0.2 identifies with a path in `Diff(Σ)`. This is the smooth kernel adapter to the topological theorem, not a second proof of Epstein's theorem; it is uniform in the genus, the sphere case agreeing with GeometricTopology Layer 3.4's `Diff(S²) ≃ O(3)`. Verify the closedness hypothesis explicitly; for surfaces with boundary use the relative version `isotopicRel_of_homotopicRel` with its homotopy-rel-boundary hypothesis, never the false absolute statement on the disc. Conclude that `Mod±(Σ) → Out(Π)` is injective, hence by Layer 5.2 an isomorphism, with `Mod⁺(Σ) ≃ Out⁺(Π)`, the Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem (Farb–Margalit, Chapter 8).

**Checks.**

- A Dehn twist is not homotopic to the identity (it acts nontrivially on `H₁` for a nonseparating curve, and on `π₁` for a separating one), so the kernel theorem does not trivialize it; the theorem is used only on diffeomorphisms homotopic to the identity.
- On the disc the identity and the reflection are homotopic through continuous maps but not isotopic through homeomorphisms; the closed-surface hypothesis of the theorem excludes this, and the relative version requires the homotopy to fix the boundary.
- On the Klein bottle, the quotient of `(ℝ/ℤ)²` by `(x, y) ∼ (x + 1/2, −y)`, the map induced by `(x, y) ↦ (x, −y)` equals the one induced by `(x, y) ↦ (x + 1/2, y)`, so it is the final slice of the diffeotopy induced by `(x, y) ↦ (x + t/2, y)`, as the theorem asserts for a map homotopic to the identity; on the torus `(x, y) ↦ (x, −y)` acts on `H₁` by `diag(1, −1)` and is not homotopic to the identity, so the nonorientable case is not read off the orientable double cover.

### 5.4a The mapping class groups of the sphere and the torus

Prove `mcg_sphere_full`: the homomorphism from `O(3)` to the mapping class group of the round sphere `S²` sending `r` to the class of its restriction `TauCeti.orthogonalToDiffSphere 2 ∞ r` is surjective with kernel `SO(3)`, the matrices of determinant one; so the group has two elements, the class of a reflection being the nontrivial one, and the orientation-preserving classes form the trivial subgroup for either orientation. Route: with the `𝓡 2` atlas of `S²` read as an `𝓡∂ 2` atlas with empty boundary by the comparison of the two models (§0.0e), the forgetful isomorphism of §0.0h (GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 items 2 and 5) identifies the group with GeometricTopology Layer 1.7.2's `Mod(S²)`, which is `π₀ Diff(S²)` by Layer 0.2; GeometricTopology Layer 3.4's `Diff(S²) ≃ O(3)`, through Tau Ceti's reference inclusion `TauCeti.orthogonalToDiffSphere 2 ∞`, identifies it with `π₀ O(3) = ℤ/2`, and the identity component `SO(3)` is the orientation-preserving part. Define the linear homeomorphism `torusLinearHomeomorph A` of the torus `T² = (ℝ/ℤ)²` for `A = [[a, b], [c, d]] ∈ GL₂(ℤ)`: `(x, y) ↦ (a x + b y, c x + d y)` in the coordinates of the two circle factors, with inverse induced by `A⁻¹`; it is a diffeomorphism and `torusLinearHomeomorph (A * B) = torusLinearHomeomorph A * torusLinearHomeomorph B`. Prove `mcg_torus_full` (Farb–Margalit, §2.2): the homomorphism sending `A` to the class of `torusLinearHomeomorph A` is a bijection of `GL₂(ℤ)` onto the mapping class group of `T²`; its inverse is the action on `π₁(T², 0) = ℤ²` in the basis of the two circle factors, and it restricts to an isomorphism of `SL₂(ℤ)` with the orientation-preserving subgroup. Route, on homeomorphisms directly. As `π₁(T²)` is abelian, its outer automorphism group is `Aut(ℤ²) = GL₂(ℤ)` and a change of basepoint acts trivially, so every homeomorphism, whether or not it fixes `0`, has an action on `π₁(T², 0)`, constant on isotopy classes, and `torusLinearHomeomorph A` acts by `A`. Injectivity: if `torusLinearHomeomorph A` is isotopic to the identity, it acts trivially and `A = 1`. Surjectivity: if a homeomorphism `f` acts by `A`, then `(torusLinearHomeomorph A)⁻¹ ∘ f` acts trivially on `π₁` of the aspherical torus, so it is homotopic to the identity (§5.1), hence isotopic to it by Epstein's theorem `isotopic_of_homotopic_closedSurface` (§0.0d). The linear map of `A` has degree `det A`, so the orientation-preserving classes are those of `SL₂(ℤ)`.

**Checks.**

- The antipodal map and the reflection of `S²` in a plane both represent the nontrivial class: their composite is the rotation by `π` about the normal of the plane, in `SO(3)`.
- On the torus the shear `(x, y) ↦ (x, x + y)` goes to `[[1, 0], [1, 1]]`; the involution `(x, y) ↦ (−x, −y)` goes to `−I`, of determinant `1`, a nontrivial orientation-preserving class fixing four points; the swap `(x, y) ↦ (y, x)` has determinant `−1` and lies outside the orientation-preserving subgroup.
- The orientation-preserving subgroup has index two in the full group for both surfaces, `{1} ⊂ ℤ/2` and `SL₂(ℤ) ⊂ GL₂(ℤ)`; a computation of the full group from orientation-preserving maps alone would miss the reflection classes.

### 5.5 Curve systems and the smooth adapters for the Alexander method

On the polygon model construct the admissible Alexander system: a chain of `2g + 1` essential pairwise non-isotopic simple closed curves, consecutive curves meeting once transversely, all other pairs disjoint, no triple intersections and two open discs as complement; compute that its regular neighbourhood has Euler characteristic `−2g`, genus `g` and two boundary circles. Own the three curve and arc statements that the Alexander method needs, in smooth form on `Σ`, with the piecewise-linear curve and arc theorems of §0.0d as the input and GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 items 2 and 5, read through the transported compatible triangulation of Layer 0.1, as the bridge. The **bigon criterion**: two transverse simple closed curves on `Σ` are in minimal position, their number of intersection points equal to the geometric intersection number of their isotopy classes, if and only if no component of the complement is an embedded bigon, a disc bounded by one arc of each curve (Farb–Margalit, Proposition 1.7); route: lift to the universal cover `ℍ` of Layer 4.5 and compare with the intersection count of the geodesic representatives. **Homotopy implies isotopy** for curves and arcs: two homotopic essential simple closed curves are ambient isotopic by an isotopy with compact support, and two proper arcs homotopic relative to their endpoints are ambient isotopic relative to the boundary (the two-sided curve and proper arc theorems of §0.0d, Epstein 1966, Theorems 2.1 and 3.1, stated for piecewise-linear embeddings whose image is two-sided and does not bound a disc, which on orientable `Σ` is every essential simple closed curve); pass from the piecewise-linear to the given smooth curves by the compact relative PL approximation theorem of §0.0a and GeometricTopology Layer 11.2.1 items 2 and 5 (isotopy to a diffeomorphism, and smooth isotopy of isotopic diffeomorphisms), retaining the crossing neighbourhoods, and convert the ambient isotopy into a diffeotopy by Layer 0.2. The **alignment of admissible systems**, the Alexander method (Farb–Margalit, Proposition 2.8): two finite admissible systems with matching isotopy classes, incidences and cyclic orders at every vertex are carried one onto the other by a diffeomorphism smoothly isotopic to the identity; route: align the curves one at a time by the previous statement and Layer 0.3's isotopy extension, align the crossing neighbourhoods using the cyclic orders, extend across the complementary discs by Smale's disc theorem in the form of GeometricTopology Layer 3.4 (a diffeomorphism of `D²` equal to the identity near `∂D²` is smoothly isotopic to the identity through such diffeomorphisms), and arrange the identity on boundary collars; matching intersection counts alone are insufficient. These are the smooth marked-system adapters consumed by Layer 7; §0.0d owns the piecewise-linear curve, arc and homeomorphism statements, and this subsection their smooth forms on `Σ`.

**Checks.**

- On the torus two curves of classes `(1,0)` and `(0,1)` meet once and bound no bigon; two curves of classes `(1,0)` and `(1,0)` pushed to meet twice bound a bigon, and the minimal-intersection criterion removes it.
- The regular neighbourhood of the chain of `2g + 1` curves has `χ = (2g + 1)·0 − 2g = −2g` (one vertex per crossing counted with the four incident edge-ends); with `2g` curves the neighbourhood has one boundary circle and the complement is one disc, not two.
- Two systems with the same intersection counts but different cyclic orders at a vertex are not isotopic; the alignment statement carries the cyclic order.
- Epstein's hypothesis is used: on the orientable `Σ` a simple closed curve bounding a disc and its reversal are homotopic but not isotopic through embeddings, since the isotopy would have to reverse the boundary orientation of the disc (Epstein 1966, remark after Theorem 2.1); the statements are for essential curves, which bound no disc.

## Layer 7: Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates and the Teichmüller metric

### 7.1 Pants decompositions, filling collections and marked length compactness

For every closed oriented `Σ` of genus `g ≥ 2` choose a pants decomposition of `3g − 3` curves and an oriented seam and transversal convention, using ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 1's marked and collared cut-system refinement on Layer 0.1's standard model. Prove the simple-geodesic representative and disjointness results from Layer 5.5's bigon and homotopy-implies-isotopy adapters: every essential simple closed curve has a unique simple closed geodesic representative, disjoint curves have disjoint representatives (Farb–Margalit, §1.2 and Chapter 1). Choose a finite **strongly filling** collection `C` containing all cuffs and, for every cuff `β`, a transverse curve `γ ∈ C` with `i(β, γ) > 0`, such that the complement of `C` is a union of discs. Prove **marked filling-length compactness** in Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates: a bound `ℓ_γ ≤ B` for all `γ ∈ C` bounds the cuff lengths above; if `γ` crosses a cuff of length `ℓ` the collar lemma gives `B ≥ 2 asinh(1/sinh(ℓ/2))`, hence `ℓ ≥ 2 asinh(1/sinh(B/2))`; the right-angled hexagon identities bound seam lengths on this compact cuff interval; for each transverse curve prove the collar foot-displacement inequality, that when the cuff lengths range over the compact interval and its length is at most `B` the lifted twist displacement is bounded by constants depending only on the interval and the fixed pants arcs; the constants come from the collar metric and the hexagon bounds. This gives a compact box for all twists and proves that the sublevel set `{ℓ_γ ≤ B for all γ ∈ C}` is compact; compactness follows from the full set of bounds, not from one curve.

**Checks.**

- The collar width `w(ℓ) = asinh(1/sinh(ℓ/2))` satisfies `w(ℓ) → ∞` as `ℓ → 0`; a crossing curve of length at most `B` therefore forces `ℓ ≥ 2 asinh(1/sinh(B/2)) > 0`, and for `B = 2 asinh 1` the bound is `ℓ ≥ 2 asinh(1/sinh(asinh 1)) = 2 asinh 1`.
- The pants decomposition alone is not filling: the Dehn twist about a cuff preserves all cuff lengths, so the sublevel set of the cuff lengths alone is noncompact; the transverse curves are needed.
- In genus two a pants decomposition has three cuffs and a strongly filling collection can be taken with six curves; a collection whose complement contains an annulus is not filling.

### 7.2 The twist coordinate

Define cuff lengths `ℓ_i > 0`. Fix an oriented collar `(r, θ) ∈ [0,1] × (ℝ/ℤ)` of the `i`-th cuff with `dr ∧ dθ` positive, left seam `r = 0`, right seam `r = 1`, and the positive twist `τ_i(r, θ) = (r, θ + φ(r))` with `φ` increasing from `0` to `1`. Choose a marked transversal `β_i` crossing left to right. Define the **twist displacement** `t_i ∈ ℝ` (this normalization and its sign are the roadmap's own convention, fixed by the witness below) from the homotopy class rel seams of the marked image of `β_i`, lifted to the universal cover of the cuff: right foot minus left foot measured in the positive angular direction, with the integer lift fixed by the marking. Under Layer 3.2's left action `f ⋅ (X, m) = (X, m ∘ f⁻¹)`, the transversal after applying `τ_i` is `m ∘ τ_i⁻¹ ∘ β_i`; `τ_i⁻¹` changes the left-to-right lift by `−1`, so `τ_i` changes `t_i` by `−ℓ_i`; verify this by cutting and regluing. The twist is arclength-valued in `ℝ`, neither divided by the cuff length nor reduced modulo an integer. Establish the marked-pants/twist comparison: carry the topological pants decomposition and transversals from the marked refinement to their geodesic representatives and prove that the twist read from the lifted seam-foot displacement equals the collar definition, naturally under marking changes and across each elementary pants move (ComplexCurveModuli D6 item 2 for the geodesic cuts and collars).

**Checks.**

- Witness for the sign: on a genus-two surface with cuff `β₁` of length `ℓ₁` and transversal `β` crossing it once, the positive Dehn twist `τ₁` sends the point `(ℓ₁, t₁, …)` to `(ℓ₁, t₁ − ℓ₁, …)`; a right-action convention `(X, m ∘ τ₁)` would give `t₁ + ℓ₁`.
- `τ_i²` changes `t_i` by `−2ℓ_i`, and `τ_i⁻¹` by `+ℓ_i`; a coordinate reduced modulo `ℓ_i` would not see the twists at all, and one divided by `ℓ_i` would be shifted by `−1`.
- The twist of the identity-marked surface built in Layer 7.3 with all twists zero is zero on every cuff: the seams match across each cuff.

*Needs:* §7.1; §3.2; ComplexCurveModuli D6 item 2.

### 7.3 The Fenchel–Nielsen homeomorphism

Prove that the **Fenchel–Nielsen map** `Teich(Σ) → (0,∞)^(3g−3) × ℝ^(3g−3)`, `[(X, m)] ↦ ((ℓ_i), (t_i))`, is a homeomorphism (Imayoshi–Taniguchi, §3.2; Farb–Margalit, §10.6; Hubbard 2006, Chapter 7). For the inverse, construct the hyperbolic pair of pants with three prescribed cuff lengths by the right-angled hexagon identities, glue along cuffs with the prescribed arclength twists, and recover the coordinates from the unique geodesic cuffs, seams and lifted foot displacements. Prove continuity in both directions, derive the elementary pants-move transition maps from the hexagon identities, and record that these are real-analytic transition maps, not holomorphic charts. Prove contractibility of `Teich(Σ)` by `((ℓ_i), (t_i)) ↦ (((1−s)ℓ_i + s), ((1−s)t_i))`, `s ∈ [0,1]`, which stays in the positive cone and is continuous.

**Checks.**

- The dimension count `2(3g − 3) = 6g − 6` agrees with the real dimension of the complex manifold of Layer 2.4; for `g = 2` the coordinates are three lengths and three twists.
- The hexagon with alternate sides `ℓ₁/2, ℓ₂/2, ℓ₃/2` exists and is unique for every triple of positive lengths (`cosh c = (cosh a cosh b + cosh γ)/(sinh a sinh b)` with the right-angle condition), so the inverse map is defined on all of `(0,∞)^(3g−3)`; a formula with `cos` in place of `cosh` has no solution for large lengths.
- The point with all twists `t_i = 0` and all lengths equal is fixed by the orientation-reversing involution reflecting each pair of pants across its seams; the point with one twist `t_1 ≠ 0` is not fixed, since reflection negates twists.

### 7.4 Wirtinger derivatives, quasiconformal maps and the Beltrami equation

Define the classical Wirtinger derivatives of a real-differentiable map `f` of an open set of `ℂ`, written in words as the z-derivative `(f_x − i f_y)/2` and the z-bar-derivative `(f_x + i f_y)/2`, and prove that `f` is complex differentiable at a point if and only if its z-bar-derivative there vanishes. Define the weak (Sobolev) Wirtinger derivatives of a `W^(1,2)_loc` map and prove that they agree almost everywhere with the classical ones at points of differentiability. Define an orientation-preserving **quasiconformal map** as a `W^(1,2)_loc` homeomorphism whose weak derivatives satisfy the Beltrami equation `f_z̄ = μ f_z` almost everywhere for a measurable `μ` with `‖μ‖_∞ < 1`, with dilatation `K(f) = (1 + ‖μ‖_∞)/(1 − ‖μ‖_∞)`; prove chart invariance, so that the notion makes sense on Riemann surfaces, and the composition, inverse and dilatation laws `K(f ∘ g) ≤ K(f) K(g)`, `K(f⁻¹) = K(f)` (Imayoshi–Taniguchi, Chapter 4). Prove the **measurable Riemann mapping theorem** with holomorphic parameter dependence, normalization and uniqueness (Ahlfors–Bers 1960, Theorem 1 and the parameter theorem; Imayoshi–Taniguchi, §4.4). Route: prove that the planar Beurling transform is an `L²` isometry by its Fourier multiplier of modulus one, consuming Mathlib's Plancherel; PDE Lane B item 10 gives Calderón–Zygmund `Lᵖ` bounds and item 9 gives Riesz–Thorin interpolation, hence `‖S‖_p → 1` as `p → 2`; solve for small `‖μ‖_∞` by the Neumann series and extend to `‖μ‖_∞ < 1` by finite composition. Prove the **normal-family compactness** theorem for normalized `K`-quasiconformal self-maps of the sphere, with equicontinuity of the maps and their inverses and preservation of homeomorphisms in limits, under the stated normalization.

**Checks.**

- For `f(z) = z + a z̄` with `|a| < 1`, the z-bar-derivative is `a`, the z-derivative is `1`, `μ = a` and `K = (1 + |a|)/(1 − |a|)`; for `|a| ≥ 1` the map is not a homeomorphism, so the condition `‖μ‖_∞ < 1` is used.
- For a holomorphic `f` the z-bar-derivative is zero and `K = 1`; for `f(z) = z̄`, the z-derivative is zero, the z-bar-derivative is one, and the map is orientation reversing, excluded by the definition.
- The Beurling transform `Sf(z) = −(1/π) p.v. ∫ f(ζ)/(z − ζ)² dA(ζ)` has Fourier multiplier `ξ̄/ξ` of modulus one, so `‖S‖_2 = 1` and `S(f_z̄) = f_z` for compactly supported smooth `f`; a claimed bound `‖S‖_1 < ∞` would be false, as PDE Lane B item 10 records.
- The affine map `z ↦ z + a z̄` solves the Beltrami equation with constant `μ = a` and is normalized by fixing `0` and `1` after dividing by `1 + a`; uniqueness of the normalized solution excludes `z ↦ λ(z + a z̄)` for `λ ≠ 1/(1 + a)`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `MeasureTheory` and Fourier Plancherel; PDE Lane B item 9; PDE Lane B item 10; §1.1.

### 7.5 Teichmüller's theorem and the Teichmüller metric

Prove **Teichmüller's existence and uniqueness theorem** on closed marked surfaces: every marked equivalence class contains an extremal quasiconformal representative; prove the Hamilton–Krushkal variational inequality and the Reich–Strebel fundamental inequality with their exact hypotheses; combine them with the normal-family compactness of Layer 7.4 to identify every nontrivial extremal map by a holomorphic quadratic differential `q ∈ H⁰(X, K_X^⊗2)` and a Teichmüller parameter `k ∈ [0,1)`, `μ = k q̄/|q|`, and deduce uniqueness from equality in the fundamental inequality (Imayoshi–Taniguchi, Chapter 5; Hubbard 2006, Chapter 6; Farb–Margalit, Chapter 11). Define the **Teichmüller metric** `d_T(X, Y) = ½ inf_f log K(f)` over quasiconformal maps in the marked class and prove separation, symmetry, the triangle inequality, mapping-class invariance and agreement with the topology of Layer 2. Prove **Wolpert's length distortion** `ℓ_γ(Y) ≤ K(f) ℓ_γ(X)` for `f` a `K`-quasiconformal map in the marked class (Wolpert 1979, Lemma 3.1). Prove completeness: for a `d_T`-Cauchy sequence fix a tail base point; Wolpert's inequality bounds every curve of the finite filling collection on the tail, so the sequence lies in a compact sublevel set of Layer 7.1, and a convergent subsequence of a Cauchy sequence makes the sequence converge. Prove that geodesics exist by the extremal-map theorem.

**Checks.**

- `d_T(X, X) = 0` through `f = id` with `K = 1`; `d_T(X, Y) > 0` for distinct points because a conformal map in the marked class would make them equal.
- For a Teichmüller map with parameter `k`, `K = (1 + k)/(1 − k)` and `d_T = ½ log((1 + k)/(1 − k)) = artanh k`; the factor `½` makes the Teichmüller disc `k ↦ [X_k]` isometric to the hyperbolic disc of curvature `−4`, and dropping it would give curvature `−1`.
- Wolpert's inequality with `K = 1` gives `ℓ_γ(Y) = ℓ_γ(X)` for all `γ`, consistent with `Y = X`; applied to a Dehn twist (which is not quasiconformally close to the identity) it bounds the length of the twisted curve only through `K`, which grows with the twist.
- Mapping-class invariance: `d_T(f ⋅ X, f ⋅ Y) = d_T(X, Y)` because `f` composes the marked classes on both sides by the same diffeomorphism; a metric defined with markings on one side only would not be invariant.

*Needs:* §7.4; §7.1; §2.4; ComplexKahlerGeometry K5b; §3.2.

## Layer 8: Finite actions, fixed loci and automorphism-free structures

### 8.1 Measured laminations and earthquakes

Construct the carrier `ML(Σ)` of **measured geodesic laminations**, the intersection pairing `i(·,·)` and the left earthquake deformation `E^t_μ(X)` on marked hyperbolic structures, with its continuity and the **earthquake theorem** that any two points of `Teich(Σ)` are joined by a unique left earthquake path (Thurston; Kerckhoff 1983, §2). Prove the **first-variation formula** for the length of a closed curve along an earthquake (for a weighted curve the sum over crossings of the weights times the cosines of the crossing angles, extended to laminations by approximation) and Kerckhoff's **convexity theorem**: `t ↦ ℓ_γ(E^t_μ(X))` is convex, strictly convex when `i(γ, μ) > 0` (Kerckhoff 1983, §3). These rest on four owned inputs. (i) The topology of `ML(Σ)` is the weak topology of transverse measures, in which `μ_n → μ` when `∫ τ dμ_n → ∫ τ dμ` for every compactly supported continuous `τ` on every transverse arc, equivalently `i(μ_n, γ) → i(μ, γ)` for every simple closed curve `γ`. (ii) Marking independence: for two marked hyperbolic structures `X, Y`, straightening geodesics through the boundary map of a lifted marking (Layer 5.2) gives a homeomorphism `ML_X(Σ) ≃ ML_Y(Σ)` preserving `i(·,·)` and natural under marking changes, so `ML(Σ)` is a carrier over `Teich(Σ)` rather than over one metric. (iii) Density: positive multiples of simple closed geodesics are dense in `ML(Σ)`, and `i(·,·)` and lengths are continuous, so first variation and convexity extend from weighted curves to all of `ML(Σ)`. (iv) The filling criterion: if a finite collection `C` fills, every nonzero `μ ∈ ML(Σ)` has `i(γ, μ) > 0` for some `γ ∈ C`; for a pants decomposition with one transverse curve per cuff this holds because the support of `μ` either crosses a cuff or contains a cuff as a closed leaf, and then the transverse curve crosses it.

The lamination carrier and the earthquake have the following API.

- `ML(Σ)` is a topological space with a continuous `ℝ_(>0)`-action by scaling and a continuous symmetric pairing `i(·,·)` extending the geometric intersection number of simple closed curves.
- `E^t_μ` is a continuous map `ℝ × ML(Σ) × Teich(Σ) → Teich(Σ)` with `E^0_μ = id`, `E^(s+t)_μ = E^s_μ ∘ E^t_μ` and `E^t_(cμ) = E^(ct)_μ`.
- For a weighted simple closed curve `μ = c·γ`, `E^t_μ` is the fractional twist of displacement `ct` along `γ`; so `E^(ℓ_γ)_γ(X, m) = (X, m ∘ τ_γ) = τ_γ⁻¹ ⋅ (X, m)` for the positive Dehn twist `τ_γ` of Layer 7.2, and `E^t_γ` raises the twist coordinate `t_γ` by `t`.

**Checks.**

- Witness for the direction: for `μ = γ` a cuff, the full earthquake `E^(ℓ_γ)_γ(X, m)` is the surface `X` with the marking sheared one full turn to the left across the collar, `(X, m ∘ τ_γ) = τ_γ⁻¹ ⋅ (X, m)`, so it changes the twist coordinate by `+ℓ_γ` and `E^t_γ` changes `t_γ` by `+t`; the positive Dehn twist `τ_γ` changes it by `−ℓ_γ` (Layer 7.2). A convention with `E^(ℓ_γ)_γ = τ_γ ⋅ (X, m)` would be a right earthquake.
- First variation at `t = 0` for `γ` crossing `μ = c·δ` once at angle `θ` is `c cos θ`; it vanishes at a right angle and is negative past it, while the second variation is a positive multiple of `c sin²θ`, so convexity is strict exactly when a crossing exists.
- The curve `γ` disjoint from `μ` has constant length along `E^t_μ`; strict convexity fails there, which is why the filling criterion (iv) is needed in Layer 8.2.
- `ML(Σ)` for the torus is `ℝ²` with `i` the absolute value of the determinant; two laminations `(1,0)` and `(2,0)` have `i = 0`, and the scaling action is not free on classes of closed curves, which the definition records.

### 8.2 Nielsen realization for finite subgroups of `Mod±(Σ)`

Use Layer 7.1's strongly filling collection `C`. For a finite subgroup `F ≤ Mod±(Σ)` form the orbit collection with multiplicity `F·C` and set `L_F(X) = Σ_(f∈F) Σ_(γ∈C) ℓ_(fγ)(X)`. It is strongly filling and `F`-invariant, including for orientation-reversing classes, which act on lengths through Layer 3.2's conjugate-linear action. Layer 7.1's compactness gives compact sublevel sets of `L_F`. Along every nonconstant earthquake path the lamination `μ` is nonzero, so some `γ ∈ C` has `i(γ, μ) > 0`; that term is strictly convex and the others convex, so `L_F` is strictly convex along earthquakes. Properness gives a minimum, strict convexity and earthquake connectivity give uniqueness, and `F`-invariance forces the unique minimum to be fixed by `F`. This is Kerckhoff's proof of the **Nielsen realization theorem** in its finite-filling-collection form (Kerckhoff 1983, Theorem 1; Farb–Margalit, Chapter 7): every finite subgroup of `Mod±(Σ)` fixes a point of `Teich(Σ)`, hence by Layer 5.6 is realized as a group of conformal and anticonformal isometries of a hyperbolic surface.

State its **lifting form**, `nielsen_realization`, which needs no Teichmüller carrier: for a closed connected orientable smooth surface `S` of any genus and a finite subgroup `F` of the topological mapping class group `MappingClassGroupFull S` (which is `Mod±(S)` by §3.1), there is a finite subgroup `H` of `TauCeti.Diff (𝓡 2) S ∞` that the quotient map sends bijectively onto `F`. For genus at least two, let `X` be a point fixed by `F`: the isometries of its hyperbolic metric, transported to `S` by the marking, whose classes lie in `F` form a subgroup, which maps onto `F` because the stabilizer of `X` is the isometry group (Layer 5.6) and injectively because isometries map injectively to `Mod±` (Layer 0.5); they are smooth, being isometries of a smooth metric. For the sphere and the torus the lifts come from §5.4a: for `S²` the group `F` is trivial or generated by the class of a reflection, lifted by `{1, r}` with `r ∈ O(3)` a reflection; for `T²` the linear diffeomorphisms `torusLinearHomeomorph A`, `A` in the subgroup of `GL₂(ℤ)` corresponding to `F` under `mcg_torus_full`, form a subgroup because `torusLinearHomeomorph` is a homomorphism, transported along a diffeomorphism of `S` with `(ℝ/ℤ)²`.

**Checks.**

- For `F` trivial the theorem says `L_C` has a minimum, which the compactness alone gives; the minimum is unique by strict convexity.
- For `F = ⟨h⟩` the hyperelliptic class in genus two, every point is fixed and the theorem is vacuous there; its content is that the fixed set is nonempty for every finite `F`, and for `F` generated by a Dehn twist (infinite order) the hypothesis fails and the twist fixes no point, since point stabilizers are finite (§5.6) and a Dehn twist has infinite order in `Mod±(Σ)`.
- An orientation-reversing involution of `Mod±(S_2)` is realized by an anticonformal involution, so the fixed point is a real curve; a proof using only holomorphic convexity would not reach the `Mod±` case.
- The lifting form is false for infinite subgroups: the group generated by a Dehn twist is infinite cyclic, and a finite `H` cannot map onto it. For `F` generated by the class of `−I` in `MappingClassGroupFull(T²) ≃ GL₂(ℤ)`, both `{1, (x, y) ↦ (−x, −y)}` and `{1, (x, y) ↦ (1/2 − x, −y)}` are lifts, so `H` is not unique; the Klein four-group generated by `(x, y) ↦ (−x, −y)` and the translation `(x, y) ↦ (x + 1/2, y)` is finite and maps onto `F` but not injectively, the translation being isotopic to the identity, which is why the statement asks a bijection and not only a surjection.

*Needs:* §8.1; §7.1; §3.1; §3.2; §5.6; §0.5; §5.4a.

### 8.3 Quotient charts for a finite isometry group

For a finite group `F` of isometries of a closed hyperbolic surface define the full quotient carrier by finite local isometry charts: at `x`, choose a metric disc invariant under the stabilizer `F_x` and disjoint from its translates by elements outside `F_x`; exponential coordinates conjugate `F_x` to a finite subgroup of `O(2)`. A rotation subgroup gives a cone chart `D/C_m`, a reflection a half-disc mirror chart, a dihedral stabilizer a sector of angle `π/m` with a corner reflector. Free orientation-reversing elements can make the quotient nonorientable without mirrors. Record each chart's stabilizer, orbit size `|F|/|F_x|` and local quotient map. The orientation-preserving subgroup `F⁺` has its separate Riemann-surface orbifold quotient, by FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 1 applied to the lift of `F⁺` to a discrete cocompact subgroup of `PSL₂(ℝ)` (Layer 5.6 realizes `F⁺` by isometries); it is used for the complex deformation dimensions. The quotient map `X → X/F⁺` is a branched cover in the sense of §0.0g, of degree `|F⁺|`, with the power model `u ↦ u^m` at a point of stabilizer order `m` (FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 1 item 2), and over the `i`-th branch value, of order `mᵢ`, lie `|F⁺|/mᵢ` points of exponent `mᵢ`. So `riemannHurwitz` (§0.0g) with `Surface.eulerChar_eq_two_sub_two_mul_genus` (§0.0f) gives the **Riemann–Hurwitz formula for the quotient**, `2g − 2 = |F⁺|(2h − 2 + Σᵢ(1 − 1/mᵢ))` with `h` the genus of `X/F⁺`, which Layers 8.4, 8.5 and 10.4 use.

**Checks.**

- The hyperelliptic involution of genus two has six fixed points, each with stabilizer `C_2`, so the quotient of `F⁺ = ⟨h⟩` has signature `(0; 2,2,2,2,2,2)`; a chart construction forgetting the fixed points would give a smooth genus-zero surface with `χ = 2`, contradicting Riemann–Hurwitz `−2 = 2·(2 − 6/2)`.
- A reflection of a real genus-two curve with fixed-point set three circles has quotient a sphere with three boundary mirrors and `F⁺` trivial; its full quotient is not a Riemann surface.
- A fixed-point-free orientation-reversing involution gives a nonorientable quotient of Euler characteristic `−1` for genus two; its `F⁺` is trivial and the complex quotient is the surface itself.

*Needs:* §5.6; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 1; §4.5; §0.0f; §0.0g.

### 8.4 The dimension of fixed loci

The statements of this subsection are proved by the route given here, which is the roadmap's own. For a finite `F⁺ ≤ Mod⁺(Σ)` fixing a point `X`, prove finite-group holomorphic linearization at `X` and identify the tangent space of the fixed locus `Fix(F⁺)` with `H¹(X, T_X)^(F⁺)`; Serre duality (ComplexKahlerGeometry K5b) identifies its dimension with that of `H⁰(X, K_X^⊗2)^(F⁺)`. Under the quotient map to the orientable quotient of genus `h` with `r` branch values, invariant quadratic differentials correspond to quadratic differentials on the quotient with at most simple poles at the branch values; prove the local branch-order computation giving exactly this pole condition. Use the divisor Riemann–Roch comparison of ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 2 to prove `dim_ℂ Fix(F⁺) = 3h − 3 + r`, including the stable low-signature cases `h = 0, r ≥ 3` and `h = 1, r ≥ 1`. For an orientation-reversing coset prove that its antiholomorphic involution on the `F⁺` fixed locus has real fixed submanifold of real dimension equal to the complex dimension of that locus, by the real-linear finite-order version of Cartan's linearization.

**Checks.**

- `F⁺ = ⟨h⟩` the hyperelliptic involution of genus two: `h = 0`, `r = 6`, `3h − 3 + r = 3 = dim Teich(S_2)`, so the fixed locus is everything, as Layer 8.5 proves.
- A `ℤ/3` action in genus three with quotient of genus one and `r = 2`: Riemann–Hurwitz `4 = 3(0 + 2·(2/3))`, dimension `3 − 3 + 2 = 2 < 6`; a formula `3h − 3 + 2r` would give `4`.
- The involution of genus `g` with quotient of genus `h` and `r = 2g + 2 − 4h` fixed points: dimension `3h − 3 + 2g + 2 − 4h = 2g − 1 − h`, with codimension `g − 2 + h`, zero exactly for `g = 2, h = 0`.
- Pole condition: a quadratic differential `q` invariant under `z ↦ −z` near a fixed point descends through `w = z²` to `q = (a + b z² + …) dz² = (a/(4w) + …) dw²`, a simple pole with residue determined by `a`; demanding holomorphy on the quotient would lose the `a` term and give dimension `3h − 3` instead of `3h − 3 + r`.

*Needs:* ComplexKahlerGeometry K5b; ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 2; §8.3; §2.4.

### 8.5 The open dense locus of trivial stabilizers and the genus-two kernel

For `g ≥ 3` prove that every nontrivial finite subgroup `F ≤ Mod±(Σ)` has a proper fixed locus. If `F⁺` is nontrivial, the Riemann–Hurwitz formula of §8.3, `2g − 2 = |F⁺|(2h − 2 + Σᵢ(1 − 1/mᵢ))`, with `|F⁺| ≥ 2` and `1 − 1/mᵢ ≥ 1/2` gives `r ≤ 2g + 2 − 4h`, so the fixed locus of dimension `3h − 3 + r` has complex codimension at least `g + h − 2 > 0`. If `F⁺` is trivial, `F` has order two and its antiholomorphic involution has real fixed dimension `3g − 3 < 6g − 6`. Proper discontinuity (Layer 3.3) makes the locus of points with nontrivial stabilizer a locally finite union of closed nowhere-dense fixed loci, so its complement, the set of marked surfaces with trivial conformal and anticonformal isometry group, is open and dense. For `g = 2` first prove the existence of a hyperelliptic involution: Riemann–Roch gives `dim H⁰(K) = 2` and `dim H⁰(K(−p)) = 1` for every `p`, since a genus-two curve has no degree-one map to `ℙ¹`, so the canonical system is basepoint-free and defines a degree-two map to `ℙ¹` whose deck involution `h` is hyperelliptic. Its fixed locus is closed of complex dimension three (Layer 8.4) in the connected threefold `Teich(S_2)`, hence everything, so `[h]` lies in the global kernel `K` of the action. `K` is finite, as it lies in a point stabilizer, and orientation preserving, since a nontrivial antiholomorphic action is not the identity on a positive-dimensional complex manifold; the dimension formula for the nontrivial group `K` gives `3h − 3 + r = 3`, so `r = 6 − 3h`; `h = 1, r = 3` and `h = 2, r = 0` are excluded by Riemann–Hurwitz for a nontrivial action, so `h = 0, r = 6` and `2 = |K|(−2 + Σᵢ(1 − 1/mᵢ))` with each summand at least `1/2` forces `|K| = 2` and all `mᵢ = 2`. Thus the kernel is exactly the order-two hyperelliptic subgroup, every genus-two point has nontrivial stabilizer, and the first open dense locus is defined for `g ≥ 3` only. Descend the loci to the moduli groupoid and the coarse quotient.

**Checks.**

- `g = 3`: the codimension bound `g + h − 2 ≥ 1` is attained by the hyperelliptic locus (`h = 0`, `r = 8`, dimension `5` in `Teich` of dimension `6`); a bound `g + h − 3` would allow codimension zero.
- `g = 2`: `r = 6 − 3h` with `h = 1` gives `r = 3` and Riemann–Hurwitz `2 = |K|(0 + Σ(1 − 1/mᵢ)) ≥ |K|·3/2 ≥ 3`, impossible; with `h = 2`, `r = 0` gives `2 = 2|K|`, so `|K| = 1`, excluded; the case analysis is complete.
- The locus of trivial stabilizers is empty for `g = 2` and nonempty for `g = 3`: a generic genus-three plane quartic has no automorphisms, and Layer 3.3's local finiteness makes the complement of the fixed loci open.

*Needs:* §8.3; §8.4; §3.3; §5.6; ComplexCurveModuli D0 item 2; §2.3.

## Layer 9: Arithmetic exceptions

### 9.1 Arithmetic Fuchsian groups and the arithmetic locus

Arithmetic lattices of `PSL₂(ℝ)` are FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 7 item 1's: for a totally real number field `k` (Mathlib's `NumberField` with `NumberField.IsTotallyReal`), a quaternion algebra `B` over `k` (Mathlib's `QuaternionAlgebra`) split at exactly one real place `v₀` and an order `O`, the projective image `Γ(O)` of the norm-one group under a splitting `B ⊗_(k, v₀) ℝ ≃ M₂(ℝ)` is a lattice, cocompact exactly when `B` is division, and an arithmetic lattice is a subgroup commensurable up to conjugacy with some `Γ(O)`, which is Takeuchi's definition (Takeuchi 1975, §1 and the Definition, pp. 600–601). Define the **arithmetic locus** `Arith(Σ) ⊂ Teich(Σ)` as the set of marked hyperbolic surfaces whose uniformizing group, the image of the holonomy of Layer 4.5, is arithmetic. Prove that it is well defined and invariant: it does not depend on the representative of the holonomy conjugacy class, since conjugation in `PSL₂(ℝ)` preserves arithmeticity; it is `Mod⁺(Σ)`-invariant, since a change of marking does not change the image group; and it is `Mod±(Σ)`-invariant, since an orientation-reversing class replaces the group by its conjugate under a reflection `c ∈ PGL₂(ℝ)` (Layer 6.1), and `c Γ(O) c⁻¹` is the group `Γ(O)` of the splitting composed with conjugation by `c`, an algebra automorphism of `M₂(ℝ)`. A point of `Arith(Σ)` has a cocompact torsion-free arithmetic uniformizing group, so its algebra is division.

**Checks.**

- `PSL₂(ℤ)` is `Γ(M₂(ℤ))` for `k = ℚ` and `B = M₂(ℚ)`, arithmetic and not cocompact, so it is the uniformizing group of no point of `Teich(Σ)`, consistent with the division-algebra remark.
- The `(2,3,7)` triangle group is arithmetic with `k = ℚ(cos(2π/7))` of degree three and `B` ramified at the two other real places; its torsion-free normal subgroup of index `168` uniformizes the Klein quartic, a point of `Arith(Σ)` for `g = 3`, which a definition requiring `k = ℚ` would miss.
- A lattice whose trace field is not a number field is not arithmetic. Most points of `Teich(S_2)` give one: the character of the uniformizing representation of the finitely generated `Π` is determined by the traces of finitely many elements and determines the point, so the points all of whose traces are algebraic form a countable set, while `Teich(S_2)` is uncountable; commensurability invariance of arithmeticity is used because the definition is up to commensurability and conjugacy.

*Needs:* FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 7 item 1; Mathlib `QuaternionAlgebra`; Mathlib `NumberField`; Mathlib `NumberField.IsTotallyReal`; Mathlib `NumberField.InfinitePlace`; §4.1; §4.5; §3.2; §6.1.

### 9.2 Finiteness of arithmetic lattices of fixed genus

Consume FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 7 item 4 in the signature `(g; −)`: for each `g ≥ 2` there are finitely many `PSL₂(ℝ)`-conjugacy classes of arithmetic lattices whose quotient is a closed surface of genus `g`; such a lattice is torsion free and cocompact of covolume `4π(g − 1)` by FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4 item 5. (Borel 1981, Theorem 8.2, gives the same finiteness for the arithmetic subgroups of `PGL₂(ℝ)` of covolume at most `c`.) Transfer the finite set to the coarse moduli space of Layer 3.3: two points of `Teich(Σ)` have the same image in the quotient by `Mod±(Σ)` exactly when their uniformizing groups are conjugate in `PGL₂(ℝ)` (Layers 5.6 and 6.1), and each `PSL₂(ℝ)`-conjugacy class lies in one `PGL₂(ℝ)`-conjugacy class, so the arithmetic points of the coarse moduli space are finitely many and `Arith(Σ)` is the union of the finitely many `Mod±(Σ)`-orbits over them.

**Checks.**

- Genus two: the finite set is nonempty (Takeuchi's list of arithmetic genus-two surfaces, e.g. the Bolza surface, whose group is a torsion-free normal subgroup of index `48` in the arithmetic `(2,3,8)` triangle group); finiteness does not mean emptiness.
- The theorem is false without arithmeticity: `Teich(S_2)` is a positive-dimensional family of pairwise nonconjugate cocompact lattices of genus two.
- The signature formula bounds the shape of a signature of bounded covolume and not its cone orders: a lattice of signature `(h; m₁, …, m_r)` with `mᵢ ≥ 2` and covolume at most `c` has `2h − 2 + r/2 ≤ c/2π`, so `2h + r`, and with it the number of generators, is bounded in terms of `c` (Borel 1981, Lemma 8.1), while the signatures `(0; 2, 3, m)`, `m ≥ 7`, are pairwise distinct of covolume `2π(1/6 − 1/m) < π/3`. So there are infinitely many signatures of covolume below `π/3`, and the finiteness of Layer 7 item 4 is arithmetic, not a count of signatures.

*Needs:* FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 7 item 4; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4 item 5; §9.1; §3.3; §5.6; §6.1.

### 9.3 The arithmetic locus is closed and nowhere dense

Transfer the finitely many unmarked moduli points of Layer 9.2 to `Teich(Σ)`: their preimage is a finite union of `Mod±(Σ)`-orbits, not a finite set. Prove that each orbit is closed and locally finite under Layer 3.3's properly discontinuous action, so that the arithmetic locus is closed with open dense complement, the second generic locus. Nonarithmeticity of a point means that its uniformizing group is not arithmetic; this follows from Layer 9.2 and not from maximality.

**Checks.**

- An orbit of a properly discontinuous action of an infinite group is infinite and closed, and discrete; the arithmetic locus in `Teich(S_2)` is a countable closed discrete set, nowhere dense because `Teich(S_2)` has no isolated points.
- A nonarithmetic surface commensurable with an arithmetic one does not exist, since arithmeticity is a commensurability invariant; so the arithmetic locus is saturated under finite covers.
- Conjugating an arithmetic group by a reflection in `PGL₂(ℝ)` gives an arithmetic group, so the locus is `Mod±`-invariant, not only `Mod⁺`-invariant.

*Needs:* §9.1; §9.2; §3.3; §6.1.

- The Bolza surface of genus two and the Klein quartic of genus three as arithmetic points; a surface with transcendental Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates as a nonarithmetic point.

## Layer 10: The open dense locus of groups maximal among discrete `PGL₂(ℝ)` overgroups

### 10.1 The area bound and the index bound

A compact orientable hyperbolic orbifold `ℍ/Λ`, `Λ ≤ PSL₂(ℝ)` cocompact of signature `(h; m₁, …, m_r)`, has area `2π(2h − 2 + Σᵢ(1 − 1/mᵢ))` by FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4 item 5, and this deficit is positive, so it is at least `1/42` by Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.one_div_forty_two_le_hyperbolic_deficit` (indices `mᵢ ≥ 2`); hence the area is at least `π/21`. The value `1/42` at `(0; 2, 3, 7)` is `TauCeti.hyperbolic_deficit_two_three_seven`, and the equality case, that `(0; 2, 3, 7)` is the only signature of area `π/21`, is FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4a's minimum-area theorem; the index bound below uses only the inequality. Prove the **index bound**: for a discrete `Γ ⊊ Λ ≤ PGL₂(ℝ)` with `Γ` the torsion-free uniformizing group of a closed surface of genus `g`, put `Λ⁺ = Λ ∩ PSL₂(ℝ)`; then `[Λ : Λ⁺] ≤ 2` and `Γ ≤ Λ⁺`. The inclusion gives an orbifold covering `ℍ/Γ → ℍ/Λ⁺` (FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 1), of finite degree by compactness of the source and discreteness of the fibres, so `Λ⁺` is cocompact and `Area(ℍ/Γ) = [Λ⁺ : Γ] Area(ℍ/Λ⁺)`. Hence `[Λ⁺ : Γ] ≤ 4π(g − 1)/(π/21) = 84(g − 1)` and the total index `d = [Λ : Γ]` satisfies `2 ≤ d ≤ 168(g − 1) =: D`.

**Checks.**

- `(2,3,7)`: `−2 + 1/2 + 2/3 + 6/7 = −2 + 85/42 = 1/42`, area `π/21`; `(2,3,8)`: `−2 + 1/2 + 2/3 + 7/8 = 1/24 > 1/42`; `(2,2,2,3)`: `−2 + 3/2 + 2/3 = 1/6`; `(1; 2)`: `0 + 1/2 = 1/2`.
- `(2,2,2,2)`, `(2,3,6)`, `(2,4,4)` and `(3,3,3)` give `0`, the Euclidean signatures, and `(0; 2,2,2)` gives `−1/2`; none is hyperbolic, and the positivity hypothesis of `TauCeti.one_div_forty_two_le_hyperbolic_deficit` excludes them, so a bound stated without it would be false.
- For `g = 2` the index bound is `d ≤ 168`. The Bolza surface's uniformizing group has index `48` in its normalizer in `PSL₂(ℝ)`, the `(2,3,8)` group, of area `2π/24 = π/12 = 4π/48`, and index `96` in its full isometry group in `PGL₂(ℝ)`, with `96 ≤ 168`; a bound `84(g − 1) = 84` on the total index would fail here (`96 > 84`), so the factor two from `[Λ : Λ⁺] ≤ 2` is needed.

*Needs:* FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 4 item 5; FuchsianOrbifolds Layer 1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.one_div_forty_two_le_hyperbolic_deficit`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.hyperbolic_deficit_two_three_seven`; §4.1; §4.5.

### 10.2 Bounded normal cores, marked regular covers and the deck dictionary

Fix a marking and the geometric group `Π` of Layer 4.2. If `ρ_X : Π ≃ Γ` is the uniformizing isomorphism of `X` and `Γ ⊊ Λ` is a discrete overgroup of index `d ≤ D`, put `C = core_Λ(Γ) ◁ Λ` and `N_X = ρ_X⁻¹(C) ◁ Π`; `N_X` is the kernel of the action of `Π` on the `d` cosets, so `[Π : N_X] ≤ D!` and `Π/N_X ≃ Γ/C` embeds in `Λ/C`. A finitely generated group has finitely many normal subgroups of index at most `D!`; let `𝒩_D(Π)` be this finite set. For `N ∈ 𝒩_D(Π)` construct the regular oriented smooth cover `p_N : Σ_N → Σ` attached to `ι(N)` through Tau Ceti's covering-space classification transported across `ι`, of degree `k = [Π : N]` and genus `1 + k(g − 1)`, with deck group `Q_N = Π/N` identified with the deck group through `deckSubgroupQuotientProjEquivOfNormal` and Layer 4.2's endpoint dictionary; verify that the identification is a homomorphism into deck composition, not an antihomomorphism. The genus comes from Tau Ceti's finite-cover multiplicativity `TauCeti.eulerChar_eq_mul_of_isCoveringMap`, which gives `TauCeti.eulerChar Σ_N = k(2 − 2g)`, and `Surface.eulerChar_eq_two_sub_two_mul_genus` (§0.0f) for the closed connected oriented `Σ_N`. Prove the **outer-action identity**: with the based cover group identified with `N`, the deck map represented by `γN` induces the outer automorphism of `N` represented by `n ↦ γ n γ⁻¹`, and changing `γ` within its coset changes it by an inner automorphism; prove it for the endpoint maps and transport it to the native `Out`. After uniformization it is also the identity for the left normalizer action `z ↦ ρ(γ) z` on `ℍ/ρ(N)`. The deck group embeds in `Mod⁺(Σ_N)` (an isometry isotopic to the identity is trivial by Layer 0.5, and deck maps are isometries for the pulled-back metric), with the same topological deck classes at every point of `Teich(Σ)`. Construct the smooth cover and lift isotopies using Completed/UniversalCovers and RiemannianGeometry R4 item 7, for the fixed marked cover; the construction is invariant under changing the standard model by Layer 0.1.

**Checks.**

- `D = 168(g − 1)`, so for `g = 2` the bound on `[Π : N_X]` is `168!`, finite though enormous; the set `𝒩_D(Π)` is finite because a finitely generated group has finitely many homomorphisms to each finite group of order at most `D!`.
- For `N = Π` the cover is the identity, `Q_N` trivial; for `N` of index two the cover has genus `2g − 1` (`1 + 2(g − 1)`), as finite-cover multiplicativity gives for an unbranched double cover.
- The outer-action identity on the index-two cover of the torus by `N = 2ℤ ⊕ ℤ`: the deck involution is translation by `(1, 0)`, acting trivially on `N ≃ ℤ²` by conjugation since `Π` is abelian; on a nonabelian `Π` conjugation by `γ ∉ N` is a nontrivial outer automorphism of `N` because the centralizer of `N` in `Π` is trivial (Layer 10.3).

### 10.3 The pullback map and the deck fixed locus

Define the **cover-pullback map** `Φ_N : Teich(Σ) → Teich(Σ_N)` sending a marked complex structure to its pullback along `p_N`, descending through the equivalence relation by lifting isotopies with the orientation convention; its image lies in the fixed locus `Fix(Q_N)` of the deck action. Prove that `Φ_N` is a holomorphic embedding whose image is exactly `Fix(Q_N)`, for the fixed topological cover and marking. Reverse inclusion: take a point of `Fix(Q_N)` and realize its stabilizing deck classes by conformal isometries (Layer 5.6); write its uniformizing representation `σ : N → PSL₂(ℝ)` and `Ỹ = ℍ/σ(N)`. Let `E_Q` be the preimage of `Q_N ≤ Out(N)` in `Aut(N)`. Conjugation gives `Π ≃ E_Q`: injective because the centralizer of the finite-index normal subgroup `N` in `Π` is trivial, surjective by the outer-action identity and because a discrepancy in `Aut(N)` with trivial outer class is inner; it is an isomorphism of extensions restricting to the inner inclusion on `N` with quotient `Q_N`, so `E_Q` is torsion free. Let `L_Q` be the full preimage of the realized deck group in the normalizer of `σ(N)` in `PSL₂(ℝ)`; it fits in `1 → σ(N) → L_Q → Q_N → 1`, is discrete (finitely many cosets of a discrete group) and cocompact; conjugation embeds it in `Aut(N)` because the centralizer of `σ(N)` in `PSL₂(ℝ)` is trivial (two hyperbolic elements with distinct fixed-point pairs), with image exactly `E_Q`; hence `L_Q ≃ E_Q ≃ Π` and `L_Q` is torsion free. A nonidentity deck isometry with a fixed point would lift, after multiplying by an element of `σ(N)`, to a nonidentity elliptic element of `L_Q`, of finite order by discreteness, a contradiction; so the realized deck action is free and `Y = Ỹ/Q_N` is a closed oriented surface. The extension gives a homotopy equivalence of `Y` with `Σ`, realized by a diffeomorphism `f : Σ → Y` by Layer 5.2, whose lift `f̃ : Σ_N → Ỹ` has the prescribed marking up to the deck normalization; naturality of fundamental classes in `p_Y ∘ f̃ = f ∘ p_N` gives `k deg(f̃) = k deg(f)`, so `deg(f) = +1` and the marking is orientation preserving. Pullback of the quotient structure represents the original fixed point. No complex structure is assumed literally deck invariant because its class is fixed. Prove global injectivity: if two base points have equal marked pullbacks, the marked conformal map upstairs gives `a ∈ PSL₂(ℝ)` with `a ρ_X(n) a⁻¹ = ρ_Y(n)` for `n ∈ N` after the based inner adjustment; for `γ ∈ Π`, normality of `N` makes `ρ_Y(γ)⁻¹ a ρ_X(γ) a⁻¹` centralize `ρ_Y(N)`, so it is trivial and `a` conjugates the full representations: the base points coincide. Prove holomorphy and the local embedding: pullback of Beltrami differentials commutes with the parameter-dependent solutions of Layer 7.4 used in the charts, so `Φ_N` is holomorphic; its differential is injective because on quadratic-differential cotangent spaces the trace over the unbranched cover satisfies `p_* p^* = k·id` and the trace is the dual differential; the tangent space of `Fix(Q_N)` is the invariant subspace, by finite averaging exactly the pulled-back base tangent space, and the holomorphic inverse function theorem on `Fix(Q_N)` gives a local holomorphic inverse. With the global bijection this is the claimed holomorphic embedding. Finally prove, for an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism `f : Y → Y` preserving the regular-cover subgroup with a lift `f̃`, that `f` is isotopic to the identity exactly when the outer class of `f̃` on `N` lies in `Q_N`: in the reverse direction normalize the lift by a deck transformation to trivial outer class, so its automorphism of `N` is inner, and an automorphism of `Π` fixing `N` pointwise is the identity because the centralizer of `N` in `Π` is trivial; then Layer 5.4 gives the isotopy; the forward direction is that an isotopically trivial `f` induces an inner automorphism of `Π`, whose restriction to `N` has outer class in `Π/N = Q_N`.

The pullback map has the following API.

- `Φ_N` is a holomorphic embedding with closed image `Fix(Q_N)`.
- `Φ_N` is `Mod⁺(Σ)`-equivariant for the lifted action of the classes that preserve `N`, and `Φ_Π = id`.
- `d Φ_N` is injective, with image the `Q_N`-invariant tangent vectors.

**Checks.**

- For `N = Π`, `Φ_N` is the identity and `Fix` is everything.
- For the index-two cover of a genus-two surface (genus three), `dim Fix(Q_N) = 3 = dim Teich(S_2)` inside `dim Teich(S_3) = 6`, and `3h − 3 + r` for the quotient `h = 2, r = 0` gives `3`, consistent with Layer 8.4's formula.
- The trace identity `p_* p^* q = k q` for `q` a quadratic differential on the base and `k = 2`: the pullback to the double cover has the same local expression on both sheets and the trace adds the two sheets.
- Injectivity of `Φ_N` fails for the unmarked quotients: two nonisomorphic genus-two surfaces can have isomorphic unmarked double covers of genus three only if related by a deck-preserving isometry, which the marked statement excludes.

*Needs:* §10.2; §5.6; §5.2; §5.4; §7.4; §2.4; §0.5; ComplexKahlerGeometry K5b.

### 10.4 The bad loci and the open dense theorem

For `g ≥ 3` let `Max_g ⊂ Teich(Σ)` be the set of marked hyperbolic surfaces whose uniformizing group `Γ ≤ PSL₂(ℝ)` is maximal among discrete subgroups of `PGL₂(ℝ)` containing it. Define, for `N ∈ 𝒩_D(Π)`, `B_N = {X ∈ Teich(Σ) : Stab_(Mod±(Σ_N))(Φ_N(X))` strictly contains `Q_N}`. Prove `Max_g = Teich(Σ) ∖ ⋃_N B_N`: for a proper discrete overgroup `Λ`, its core `C` is normal in `Λ`, so `Λ/C` is a finite subgroup of `N_(PGL₂(ℝ))(C)/C = Isom(ℍ/C)` strictly containing `Q_N ≃ Γ/C`, and by Layer 5.6 a point stabilizer strictly containing `Q_N`; conversely a finite stabilizer `F > Q_N` is realized by conformal or anticonformal isometries of the compact surface `ℍ/C`, lifts through `N_(PGL₂(ℝ))(C)/C` to a discrete group `Λ` normalizing `C` and strictly containing `Γ`, using Layer 0.5's trivial kernel; the quotient map induced by `Q_N ≤ F⁺` has degree `[F⁺ : Q_N]` whether or not `Q_N` is normal in `F⁺`. Prove that each `B_N` is closed and locally finite: for `a ∈ Mod±(Σ_N) ∖ Q_N`, `Fix(a)` is closed and `Φ_N⁻¹(Fix(a))` is closed; for compact `K ⊂ Teich(Σ)`, `Φ_N(K)` is compact and proper discontinuity (Layer 3.3) gives finitely many `a` with `a · Φ_N(K)` meeting `Φ_N(K)`, so the family is locally finite and its union is closed; there are finitely many `N`, so `B = ⋃_N B_N` is closed. Prove that every member of the family has empty interior: at a point fixed by `Q_N` and `a ∉ Q_N`, let `F = ⟨Q_N, a⟩` in the finite stabilizer and `F⁺` its orientation-preserving part. If `F⁺ > Q_N`, `e = [F⁺ : Q_N] ≥ 2` and the quotient `Σ ≃ Σ_N/Q_N → Σ_N/F⁺` is a finite holomorphic map of degree `e` to a quotient of genus `h` with cone orders `m₁, …, m_r`; the Riemann–Hurwitz formula of §0.0g, applied to this quotient map as in §8.3, gives `2g − 2 = e(2h − 2 + Σᵢ(1 − 1/mᵢ))`, hence `r ≤ 2g + 2 − 4h`, and Layer 8.4's dimension `3h − 3 + r` has codimension at least `g + h − 2 > 0` in `Teich(Σ) ≃ Fix(Q_N)`. If `F⁺ = Q_N`, `F/Q_N` has order two and induces an antiholomorphic involution on `Teich(Σ)` whose fixed locus has real dimension `3g − 3 < 6g − 6`. So each `B_N` is nowhere dense, `B` is closed nowhere dense, and `Max_g` is open and dense (Greenberg 1963, Theorem 2, states the dichotomy for finitely generated Fuchsian groups; the proof here is the owned marked-cover argument above).

**Checks.**

- `g = 2`: every genus-two surface is hyperelliptic (Layer 8.5), so `Γ` is never maximal in `PSL₂(ℝ)`: the extension by the involution is a discrete overgroup of index two; this is the case `Max` empty of Greenberg's Theorem 2 and the reason the theorem is stated for `g ≥ 3`.
- `g = 3`: the Klein quartic has `|Aut| = 168 = 84(g − 1)`, the maximal overgroup is the `(2,3,7)` group of index `168` in `PSL₂(ℝ)`-terms and `336` in `PGL₂(ℝ)`, which is `D = 168(g − 1) = 336` exactly, so the bound `D` is sharp at the Hurwitz surface.
- A point of `Teich(S_3)` with trivial conformal and anticonformal isometry group is in `Max_3`: a proper overgroup would give a nontrivial stabilizer of `Φ_N(X)` for some `N`, hence a nontrivial isometry of a finite cover normalizing `C`, which descends to an isometry of `X` by the core construction; so the locus of Layer 8.5 is contained in `Max_g`, and `Max_g` is strictly larger, containing surfaces with automorphisms whose group is not contained in a larger discrete group.

*Needs:* §10.3; §10.1; §5.6; §0.5; §3.3; §0.0g; §8.3; §8.4; §4.1.

## Layer 11: The common generic locus

### 11.1 Intersection of the three loci

For each `g ≥ 3` intersect the open dense loci of Layers 8.5 and 10.4 with the complement of the closed nowhere-dense arithmetic locus of Layer 9.3. Prove that the intersection is open and dense in `Teich(Σ)` (a finite intersection of open dense sets in a Baire space) and that it descends to the moduli groupoid and the coarse quotient, being `Mod±(Σ)`-invariant. Every point of it has trivial conformal and anticonformal isometry group, is nonarithmetic, and has uniformizing group maximal among discrete `PGL₂(ℝ)` overgroups; nonarithmeticity comes from Layer 9, not from maximality.

**Checks.**

- The three conditions are independent: the Klein quartic is arithmetic with nontrivial automorphisms and nonmaximal group; a generic genus-three surface with an involution has nontrivial stabilizer, is nonarithmetic and may or may not be maximal; a surface in `Max_3` with trivial isometry group is nonarithmetic unless it is one of finitely many orbits.
- For `g = 2` the locus of Layer 8.5 is empty and the intersection is empty; the theorem is stated for `g ≥ 3`.
- The descended locus in the coarse moduli space is open dense because the coarse quotient map is open and the locus is saturated.

*Needs:* §8.5; §9.3; §10.4; §3.3.
