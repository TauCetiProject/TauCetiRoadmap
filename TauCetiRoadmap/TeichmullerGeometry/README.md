# Roadmap: Teichmüller geometry of closed surfaces

It develops marked complex and hyperbolic structures on closed oriented surfaces, their deformation spaces, mapping-class actions, and the genericity results used by the Fuchsian-cover applications.

## Scope and conventions

For every closed connected oriented smooth surface `Σ` of genus `g ≥ 2`, define `Teich(Σ)` using orientation-preserving markings and smooth isotopy. For constructions on a standard representative, choose the standard oriented smooth genus-`g` model `S_g` and a fixed orientation-preserving diffeomorphism `S_g ≃ Σ`; prove that changing this choice induces a canonical homeomorphism intertwining the mapping-class actions. A marking `m : Σ → X` is orientation preserving. Marked Riemann surfaces `(X,m)` and `(Y,n)` are equivalent when a biholomorphism `f : X ≃ Y` makes `f ∘ m` smoothly isotopic to `n`; isotopy is part of the equivalence relation.

Construct and compare the following carriers rather than identifying them by convention:

1. orientation-compatible integrable complex structures on `Σ` modulo pullback by `Diff₀⁺(Σ)`;
2. marked Riemann surfaces modulo marked biholomorphism;
3. marked complete curvature-`−1` metrics modulo orientation-preserving isometry isotopic to the identity;
4. conjugacy classes of discrete faithful cocompact representations `Π=(π₁ⁿᵃᵗ(Σ))ᵐᵒᵖ → PSL₂(ℝ)` in the subset with Euler number `χ(Σ)=2−2g`, selected by the positive complex orientation. No connectedness of this representation subset is asserted.

The resulting space `Teich(Σ)` has its complex analytic topology from the deformation atlas. Fenchel–Nielsen coordinates are a real topological coordinate system on the hyperbolic model; they are not declared holomorphic. Put `Δ⁺ = −div grad`. For `h=e^(2u)h₀`, use

`Δ⁺_(h₀) u + K_(h₀) + e^(2u) = 0`.

The sign, curvature, and orientation conventions are checked against the Riemannian and complex orientations in the comparison theorem.

### T3. Mapping-class actions and the moduli groupoid

Define `Mod⁺(Σ)=π₀(Diff⁺(Σ))` and `Mod±(Σ)=π₀(Diff(Σ))` using T0's `C^∞` path-component convention. The orientation-preserving group acts holomorphically by changing markings. On the same positive-oriented complex-structure carrier, define for a diffeomorphism `f`

`f ⋅ J = ε(f) · (f⁻¹)^*J`, where `ε(f)=+1` if `f` preserves orientation and `−1` otherwise.

On marked Riemann surfaces the corresponding action is `f⋅(X,m)=(X,m∘f⁻¹)` for orientation-preserving `f`, and `f⋅(X,m)=(X̄,m∘f⁻¹)` for orientation-reversing `f`, where `X̄` is the conjugate complex curve. On the complex-structure carrier, multiplication by `−1` is complex conjugation and restores the fixed positive orientation. Prove these formulas define the same group action and that the reversing coset acts antiholomorphically in the deformation atlas.

Consume ComplexCurveModuli D0.5 properness of the actual diffeomorphism action on stable marked curves and D3.5's arbitrary-complement smooth-offset local-slice subclause. For convergent pairs related by mapping classes, choose smoothly converging local-slice representatives and intertwining diffeomorphisms; D0.5 gives a convergent subsequence of diffeomorphisms and inverses. T0's close-diffeomorphism isotopy lemma implies their mapping classes eventually agree. This proves finite transporters on compact sets, first for `Mod⁺`, then for `Mod±` by its two cosets. Define the oriented moduli orbifold as the proper étale action groupoid `Teich(Σ)⋊Mod⁺(Σ)` and its coarse quotient separately; the full group gives the quotient that forgets orientation. Properness gives finite isotropy in quotient charts.

### T6. Mapping classes, automorphisms, and stabilizers

For any closed oriented genus-`g≥2` surface `Σ`, use the hyperbolic universal cover from T4a to prove it is a `K(π₁Σ,1)`. For an isomorphism `α:Π(Σ)≃Π(Σ')` between the geometric opposite groups (convert native `π₁` isomorphisms by inversion), construct a based map `f:Σ→Σ'` inducing `α`: use AlgebraicTopology Stage 1.5 to identify the one-skeleton with a wedge of circles and its fundamental group with the corresponding free group, choose the map on those generators, extend across the relator two-cell using the defining relation, and use `π_n(Σ')=0` for `n≥2` to prove uniqueness up to based homotopy. The inverse group isomorphism gives a homotopy inverse. This includes the self-map case `Σ'=Σ` and provides the K(π,1) mapping property used for quotient surfaces. Native `K(π₁,1)` declarations are transported across the inversion equivalence.

Prove the two-dimensional Dehn–Nielsen–Baer realization step. An isomorphism of cocompact surface groups gives an equivariant quasi-isometry of their hyperbolic universal covers: extend the orbit map between Cayley graphs using the cocompact orbit maps in each `H²`. Prove its boundary extension `φ:S¹→S¹` is a circle homeomorphism satisfying `φ∘γ=α(γ)∘φ` for every `γ` in the source group. Apply the Douady–Earle barycentric extension `E(φ)`: for every homeomorphism of the circle it is a homeomorphism of the closed disk whose restriction to the open disk is a real-analytic diffeomorphism (Douady–Earle 1986, §3, Theorem 1), and it is natural for the full group of conformal and anticonformal automorphisms of the disk, `E(g∘φ∘h)=g∘E(φ)∘h` (Douady–Earle 1986, §3, display before Lemma 2). Quasisymmetry of `φ` is not needed for this step. Naturality gives `E(φ)∘γ=α(γ)∘E(φ)`, including for an orientation-reversing `φ`, so `E(φ)` descends to a real-analytic diffeomorphism `Σ→Σ'` inducing the prescribed outer automorphism; no separate smoothing step is used for realization. In the based form, this gives `exists_diffeomorph_mapOfEq_eq`: for a smooth closed connected orientable surface with nonabelian `π₁(Σ,x₀)`, every automorphism of `π₁(Σ,x₀)` is induced by a diffeomorphism fixing `x₀` (inner automorphisms are realized by point pushing along a loop, composed with the outer realization). This proves surjectivity of `Mod±(Σ)→Out(Π)` and of its orientation-preserving restriction onto `Out⁺(Π)`. Injectivity is proved by the following Baer–Epstein kernel argument, after which conclude the two isomorphisms. Here `Out(Π)` is transported from native `Out(π₁Σ)` through inversion `Π=(π₁Σ)ᵐᵒᵖ≃π₁Σ`; verify that transport identifies the inner-automorphism subgroups.

Prove that `f` is isotopic to the identity exactly when `[f̃_*]∈Q_N`. In the reverse direction, compose `f̃` with the inverse of the deck transformation represented by its class; the normalized lift has trivial outer class, hence its automorphism of `N` is inner. Compare its induced automorphism of `Π` with that inner automorphism; an automorphism of `Π` fixing the finite-index normal subgroup `N` pointwise is the identity, since conjugating any element of `Π` by its discrepancy centralizes `N`, whose centralizer in `Π` is trivial. Thus the induced outer class of `f` on `Π` is trivial, and T6's Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem gives the isotopy. The forward direction follows because an isotopy-trivial `f` induces an inner automorphism of `Π`, whose restriction to `N` has outer class represented by an element of `Π/N=Q_N`. This identifies the reverse image with the deck fixed locus by choosing the unique deck normalization used in the proof. Define

`B_N={X∈Teich(Σ) : Stab_{Mod±(Σ_N)}(Φ_N(X)) strictly contains Q_N}`.

Prove `Max_g=Teich(Σ)\⋃_{N∈𝒩_D(Π)} B_N` as follows. For a proper discrete overgroup `Λ`, its core `C` is normal in `Λ`; hence `Λ/C` is a finite subgroup of `N_{PGL₂(ℝ)}(C)/C=Isom(H²/C)` strictly containing `Q_N≃Γ/C`. For the converse, a point stabilizer of `Φ_N(X)` is realized by conformal or anticonformal isometries of its compact hyperbolic surface. The finite stabilizer `F>Q_N` lifts through `N_{PGL₂(ℝ)}(C)/C` to its full preimage, a discrete group `Λ` strictly containing `Γ` and normalizing `C`; it contains `Γ` because `Γ/C=Q_N`. Use T6's theorem that the kernel of the isometry-to-mapping-class map is trivial for a closed hyperbolic surface of genus at least two. The quotient map induced by `Q_N≤F⁺` has degree `[F⁺:Q_N]`; subgroup inclusion defines it whether or not `Q_N` is normal in `F⁺`.

Each `B_N` is closed and locally finite in the following explicit sense. For `a∈Mod±(Σ_N)\Q_N`, `Fix(a)` is closed and `Φ_N⁻¹(Fix(a))` is closed. If `K⊂Teich(Σ)` is compact, then `Φ_N(K)` is compact; proper discontinuity of the `Mod±(Σ_N)` action gives only finitely many `a` for which `a·Φ_N(K)` meets `Φ_N(K)`. Thus the family of these closed pullback loci is locally finite, and its union `B_N` is closed and locally finite. There are finitely many `N`, so `B=⋃_N B_N` is closed.

Show every nonempty locus in this locally finite family has empty interior. At a point fixed by `Q_N` and `a∉Q_N`, let `F=⟨Q_N,a⟩` inside the finite point stabilizer and let `F⁺` be its orientation-preserving subgroup. If `F⁺>Q_N`, put `e=[F⁺:Q_N]≥2`. The quotient map induced by the subgroup inclusion `Q_N≤F⁺` gives a finite holomorphic map `Σ≃Σ_N/Q_N→Σ_N/F⁺` of degree `e`, even if `Q_N` is not normal in `F⁺`. If the quotient has genus `h` and cone orders `m₁,…,m_r`, prove

`2g−2=e(2h−2+Σᵢ(1−1/mᵢ))`, by Fuchsian L5 for this finite holomorphic quotient.

Since `e≥2` and `1−1/mᵢ≥1/2`, this gives `r≤2g+2−4h`. The finite-group fixed-locus theorem from T7 gives complex dimension `3h−3+r`; hence its codimension inside the deck fixed locus `Teich(Σ)` is at least `g+h−2>0`. If `F⁺=Q_N`, then `F/Q_N` has order two and induces an antiholomorphic involution on `Teich(Σ)`. By finite-action linearization its fixed locus is a real submanifold of real dimension `3g−3`, strictly below the real dimension `6g−6`. Thus each `B_N` is nowhere dense; local finiteness makes `B` closed nowhere dense. Its complement is exactly `Max_g`, proving openness and density.

T9 supplies the bounded-index core family, its marked regular-cover pullback and deck fixed-locus identification, the stabilizer/overgroup correspondence, local finiteness, the fixed-locus codimension argument, and the open-dense complement theorem. It consumes T3’s proper discontinuity, T6’s stabilizer and isometry-kernel theorems, T7’s finite-action fixed-locus theorem, Fuchsian L1/L4 quotient and signature data, L4a’s compact orientable area minimum, and L5’s Riemann–Hurwitz theorem, and `Completed/UniversalCovers`' path and covering-space constructions plus RiemannianGeometry R4.7's Killing–Hopf, isometry lifting and quotient metric for the covering and isometry lifts. It is independent of arithmeticity.

### T9a. Marked-cover comparison and deformation API

For `N∈𝒩_D`, construct the regular oriented smooth cover `p_N:Σ_N→Σ` using the native covering-space classification transported across T4a's inversion equivalence `ι(op p)=p⁻¹`. Its degree is `k=[Π:N]`, genus `1+k(g−1)`, and deck group is `Q_N=Π/N`. The concrete deck identification is required to be a homomorphism into deck composition, not an antihomomorphism. T4a's endpoint formula is

`η(op p)=loopDeck(p⁻¹)`, and `η(αβ)=η(α)∘η(β)`.

Prove its normal-cover version and the following precise outer-action identity. With the based cover group identified with `N`, the deck map represented by `γN` induces the outer automorphism represented by

`n ↦ γ n γ⁻¹`.

Changing `γ` by an element of `N` changes this automorphism by an inner automorphism. Prove the identity first for the endpoint/deck maps and then transport it to the native `Out` construction; it is the identity that identifies the two extensions in the next section. After uniformization, it is also the identity for the left normalizer action `z↦ρ(γ)z` on `ℍ²/ρ(N)`.

The deck subgroup embeds in `Mod⁺(Σ_N)`: an isometry isotopic to the identity is trivial by T0/T6, and deck maps for the pulled-back metric are isometries. The same topological deck classes are used for every point of Teichmüller space.

#### Holomorphic pullback and the full deck fixed locus

Define `Φ_N:Teich(Σ)→Teich(Σ_N)` by pullback along this fixed marked cover. Descend through the equivalence relation on marked structures by lifting isotopies, with the given orientation convention. Its image lies in `Fix(Q_N)`.

For the reverse inclusion, take a point of `Fix(Q_N)` and realize its stabilizing deck mapping classes by their unique conformal isometries using T6. Write its uniformizing representation as `σ:N→PSL₂(ℝ)` and `Ỹ=ℍ²/σ(N)`. Let `E_Q` be the preimage of the deck subgroup in `Aut(N)→Out(N)`. Conjugation gives an isomorphism `Π≃E_Q`: injectivity is `C_Π(N)=1`; surjectivity follows from the outer-action identity of the preceding deck-action comparison and the fact that any discrepancy in `Aut(N)` is inner. This is an isomorphism of extensions, with restriction to `N` the inner-automorphism inclusion and quotient the specified `Q_N`.

Let `L_Q` be the full preimage of the realized deck group in `N_{PSL₂(ℝ)}(σ(N))`. It fits into `1→σ(N)→L_Q→Q_N→1`. It is discrete (a finite union of cosets of the closed discrete subgroup `σ(N)`) and cocompact. Conjugation embeds it in `Aut(N)` because `C_{PSL₂}(σ(N))=1`. Its image is exactly `E_Q`, again using the specified outer action and the inner discrepancies. Hence the extension isomorphism `L_Q≃Π` fixes the marked subgroup `N` and quotient `Q_N`. It follows that `L_Q` is torsion-free. A nontrivial realized deck isometry fixing a point lifts, after multiplication by an element of `σ(N)`, to a nonidentity elliptic element of `L_Q`; discreteness makes it finite-order. This contradiction proves the realized deck action free. No representative complex structure has been assumed literally deck invariant merely because its marked class was fixed.

Consequently `Y=Ỹ/Q_N` is an ordinary closed oriented surface. The extension above gives a homotopy equivalence of the base with `Σ`, realized smoothly by T6's Dehn–Nielsen–Baer theorem. Its lift has the prescribed marking on the cover, up to the permitted deck normalization. Check the orientation before using the marked Teich carrier: the lift induces the positive oriented marking on `Σ_N`; if `f:Σ→Y` is the descended equivalence and `f̃` its lift, naturality of fundamental classes in `p_Y∘f̃=f∘p_N` gives `k deg(f̃)=k deg(f)`. Since `deg(f̃)=+1`, `deg(f)=+1`. Thus this is an orientation-preserving marking, not an appeal to an unoriented classification. Pullback of its quotient complex structure represents the original fixed point.

Prove global injectivity separately. If two base points have equal marked pullbacks, the corresponding marked conformal map upstairs gives `a∈PSL₂(ℝ)` which, after the based inner adjustment, satisfies `aρ_X(n)a⁻¹=ρ_Y(n)` for every `n∈N`. For `γ∈Π`, normality of `N` implies that `(ρ_Y(γ))⁻¹ aρ_X(γ)a⁻¹` centralizes `ρ_Y(N)`, so it is one. Thus `a` conjugates the full marked representations of `Π`; the descended map is in the identity marked class. This also proves that no extra identification of base points was introduced by changing deck representatives.

Prove holomorphy and the local embedding claim in the deformation charts. Pullback of Beltrami differentials commutes with the parameter-dependent solutions used to build these charts; hence `Φ_N` is holomorphic. Its differential is injective: on quadratic-differential cotangents the trace map over the unbranched cover satisfies `p_*p^*=k id`, and the trace is the dual differential. Identify the tangent of `Fix(Q_N)` by finite averaging and descent; it is precisely the pulled-back base tangent. The holomorphic inverse-function theorem applied to `Fix(Q_N)` now gives a local holomorphic inverse. Together with the preceding global bijection this proves the claimed holomorphic embedding and fixed-locus identification. Neither global injectivity nor this differential comparison follows from the word “pullback” alone.

### T10. Common generic locus and Fuchsian consumer

Every point has trivial full conformal/anticonformal isometry group, is nonarithmetic, and is maximal among discrete `PGL₂(ℝ)` overgroups. Nonarithmeticity follows from T8, not from maximality.
