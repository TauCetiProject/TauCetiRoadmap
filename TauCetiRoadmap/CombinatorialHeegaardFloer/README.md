# Roadmap: combinatorial Heegaard Floer and grid homology

**Summary.** Heegaard Floer homology and its knot-theoretic sibling, knot Floer homology, are
defined by counting holomorphic curves. They categorify the Alexander polynomial, detect the
genus of a knot, bound its slice genus and unknotting number, and drive much of modern work on
surgery, concordance and contact topology. Three parts of the theory have definitions and
invariance proofs that use no holomorphic curves at all, and this roadmap builds those three:
grid homology of knots and links (Manolescu–Ozsváth–Szabó–Thurston, and the book of Ozsváth,
Stipsicz and Szabó); the stable combinatorial `HF̂` of closed three-manifolds (Ozsváth, Stipsicz
and Szabó); and lattice homology of negative-definite plumbings (Némethi, with Zemke's
characteristic model). Coefficients have characteristic two, except in the targets that build
integer coefficients from sign assignments. A link is, to begin with, a grid diagram up to grid
moves; its identification with links in `S³` is a layer of its own, on which no grid theorem
waits.

A grid diagram of size `n` is an `n × n` toroidal grid with one `O` and one `X` in each row and
column; its complexes are generated by the `n!` grid states, with differentials counting empty
rectangles. The headline theorems for grids are these. If two grid diagrams related by grid moves
present a knot, their unblocked grid homologies `GH⁻` are isomorphic as bigraded `K[U]`-modules,
and their simply blocked homologies `GĤ` as bigraded `K`-modules, for every commutative ring `K` of
characteristic two; so the integer `τ` read off the `U`-tower of `GH⁻` is a knot invariant. For a
grid knot, `|τ(K)| ≤ u(K)`, where `u` counts crossing changes; and the `(p, q)` torus knot, for
coprime positive `p` and `q`, has unknotting number `(p − 1)(q − 1)/2`: the Milnor conjecture, proved
combinatorially.

For plumbings the headline is invariance under Némethi's blow-ups: if `P` is a negative-definite
plumbing graph of spheres, `k` a characteristic covector and `ε` odd, then the characteristic-two
lattice homology of the blow-up of `P` at a vertex or along an edge, in the covector extending `k`
by `ε`, is isomorphic in every cubical degree to that of `P` in `k`. For three-manifolds it is the
invariance under the nice-move calculus of the stable class `HF̂_st` of a nice multi-pointed
Heegaard diagram: the homology of its hat complex together with its number of basepoints, each
further basepoint matched by one tensor factor `𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽`. If `Y = Y₁ # n(S¹ × S²)` and `Y₁` has no
`S¹ × S²` summand, the class of a convenient diagram of `Y₁`, with `n` more factors `𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽`, is
the invariant `HF̂_st(Y)` of the three-manifold.

Tau Ceti already defines grid diagrams, the three grid complexes and their homologies, `τ`, the
unknotting number of grid links, the plumbing lattice with its cubical complex, and the data of
Heegaard diagrams, and it proves the Alexander-graded invariance of `GH⁻` and of `τ`; the layers
build on all of it. [GeometricTopology](../GeometricTopology/README.md) supplies the knot theory
of `S³` to which grid diagrams are connected, and [MorseTheory](../MorseTheory/README.md) the
handle theory behind the identification of Heegaard diagrams with three-manifolds.
[HeegaardFloer](../HeegaardFloer/README.md) builds the holomorphic theory and proves that each of
the three combinatorial theories agrees with its holomorphic counterpart.

Layer 1 assembles bigraded and filtered homological algebra. Layer 2 identifies grid links with
links in `S³`. Layer 3, the spine, builds grid homology from the diagrams to invariance, `τ`, the
Milnor conjecture, the slice-genus bound, Legendrian invariants, integer coefficients and the
filtered theory. Layer 4 builds lattice homology in four models with their comparisons and the
blow-up invariance. Layer 5 builds the stable combinatorial `HF̂`.

## How to read the build

The layers keep the lane names that other roadmaps cite: Layer 1 is Lane ALG, Layer 2 is Lane K,
Layer 3 is Lane G (§3.n is G.n), Layer 4 is Lane L (§4.2, §4.3 and §4.4 are L.1, L.2 and L.3) and
Layer 5 is Lane H (§5.n is H.n for `n ≤ 4`). The roadmap is not linear. Layers 1, 2 and 4 and the
first sections of Layer 3 can start at once and independently; Layer 3 needs Layer 2 only in
§§3.4, 3.7, 3.8, 3.9 and 3.10, where a statement is about the knot in `S³` that a grid presents, and
Layer 1 only in §§3.5, 3.6 and 3.13. The spine to push hardest is Layer 3: it reaches the Milnor
conjecture with combinatorics alone. Layer 5 follows the idiom of Layer 3 (an invariant defined on
diagrams, invariance proved under moves, the identification with smooth objects a separate
target). Nothing here waits for HeegaardFloer, whose reconciliation theorems are downstream. Each
target's `*Needs:*` line lists what its statement and its route use, so that a target proved by
another route is not blocked behind an unrelated section.

## Conventions

1. **Coefficients.** Characteristic two first. The grid complexes `GC⁻` and `GĈ` are taken over a
   commutative ring `K` with `[CharP K 2]`, the fully blocked `GC̃` over `ZMod 2`, the lattice
   complex over `𝔽₂[U] = Polynomial (ZMod 2)` (Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.PlumbingCoefficient`) and the
   hat complex of a Heegaard diagram over `ZMod 2`. Integer coefficients are separate targets
   (§§3.12, 4.4 and 5.4), built from sign assignments (Manolescu–Ozsváth–Szabó–Thurston; Gallais).
   Every characteristic-two theorem is stated so that the coefficient ring can be generalised
   without restating the topology.
2. **Links are grid diagrams up to grid moves.** A grid link is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.GridLink`, the
   quotient of grid diagrams by the grid moves (cyclic permutations of rows and columns,
   commutations, stabilizations and destabilizations), and a grid knot is a grid link with one
   component (`TauCeti.GridLink.IsKnot`). Knot theory needs no privileged presentation: Layer 3 is
   self-contained with this definition, and Layer 2 identifies grid links with links in `S³` by
   Cromwell's theorem.
3. **Invariance is stated ready for naturality.** An invariant may be an isomorphism class, a
   module, or a transitive system of modules and isomorphisms (Juhász–Thurston–Zemke); here
   invariance is phrased through specified isomorphisms attached to elementary moves, and their
   composition laws are separate targets (§3.5), even where a theorem proves only the isomorphism
   class. Basepoint-moving maps act nontrivially (Zemke); they are not quotiented away.
4. **Gradings.** On a grid of size `n`, the Maslov gradings are `M_O` and `M_X` and, for a knot,
   the Alexander grading is `A = (M_O − M_X)/2 − (n − 1)/2` (Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.GridDiagram.maslovO`, `TauCeti.GridDiagram.maslovX`, `TauCeti.GridDiagram.alexander`).
   The Maslov grading of `GH⁻` and `GĤ` is `M_O`. Each variable `Vᵢ`, and `U`, has bidegree
   `(−2, −1)` in `(M, A)`. For a link of `ℓ` components the collapsed Alexander grading uses the
   correction `(n − ℓ)/2` in place of `(n − 1)/2` (§3.2); the knot shift is never reused for links,
   and integrality is a lemma, never part of a definition.
5. **Stabilization factors.** `W = 𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽` with its two summands in bidegrees `(0, 0)` and
   `(−1, −1)`. For an `ℓ`-component link on a grid of size `n`, the fully blocked homology satisfies
   `GH̃(G) ≅ GĤ(L) ⊗ W^⊗(n−ℓ)` (§3.5); a knot has `ℓ = 1` and exponent `n − 1`. The graded vector
   space up to `⊗W` is built once, as API (§1.1). The three-manifold invariant of Layer 5 is
   stable in a finer sense: its class records the number of basepoints, and each factor `𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽`
   is matched by one basepoint (§5.3).
6. **The sign of `τ`.** `τ` is minus the largest Alexander degree of a non-torsion homogeneous class
   of `GH⁻` (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Definition 6.1.5; Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.tau`).
   The standard grid `TauCeti.GridDiagram.torusLink p q`, of size `p + q + 2`, presents the
   `(p + 1, q + 1)` torus link, and when that is a knot it is the mirror image of the positive
   torus knot: on it `2τ = −pq`. The positive torus knot `T_{p,q}` has `τ = (p − 1)(q − 1)/2`, and
   `τ` changes sign under mirroring.
7. **Small grids are finite.** A grid of size `n` has `n!` states, at most `120` for `n ≤ 5`. The
   grid Checks are finite computations on such grids, and the definitions are chosen so that their
   differentials on these grids can be evaluated.
8. **Lattice models.** Four lattice complexes are kept apart, and each comparison states its
   carrier, coefficient ring, degree, weight and domain before asserting an isomorphism. Section
   numbers refer to Némethi, arXiv:0709.0841v1, and Zemke, arXiv:2111.14962v4.

9. **Seifert-fibered plumbings.** Normalized Seifert data `(g₀, b; (α₁, β₁), …, (αᵣ, βᵣ))` have
   `αᵢ > βᵢ > 0` and `gcd(αᵢ, βᵢ) = 1`. Write the unique negative continued fraction
   `αᵢ/βᵢ = [cᵢ₁, …, cᵢₛ]⁻ = cᵢ₁ − 1/(cᵢ₂ − ⋯ − 1/cᵢₛ)` with every `cᵢⱼ ≥ 2`. The plumbing has a
   central vertex of genus `g₀` and weight `−b` and, for each `i`, a linear leg of weights
   `−cᵢ₁, …, −cᵢₛ` attached to it; the boundary is oriented as the boundary of this oriented
   plumbing (outward normal first), and the rational Euler (Schur-complement) number is
   `e = −b + Σᵢ βᵢ/αᵢ`. Every output records this convention, the orientation and `e`; an
   orientation reversal is an explicit sign conversion, never a silent change of `b` or of the leg
   fractions.
10. **Names.** The headline theorems are declared in `TauCetiRoadmap.CombinatorialHeegaardFloer` in
    `Suggested.lean`. Every other target is named in prose; a name in code font is a declaration of
    Mathlib, of Tau Ceti, or of `Suggested.lean`.

## Layer 1: bigraded and filtered homological algebra (Lane ALG)

Mostly the assembly of Mathlib's and Tau Ceti's homological algebra into a working API for the
grid complexes, plus two pieces that are new: the bigraded structure theorem for `𝔽[U]`-modules and
filtered chain homotopy. No spectral sequence is needed anywhere on the main line of Layer 3, and
none is built here.

### 1.1 Bigraded modules over polynomial rings (ALG)

A bigraded module over `R = MvPolynomial (Fin n) K` is an `R`-module with a Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.InternalGrading` indexed by `ℤ × ℤ`, in which multiplication by each `Vᵢ` maps the piece
of bidegree `(d, s)` into that of bidegree `(d − 2, s − 1)`; a bigraded map of bidegree `(a, b)` is
a linear map that is `TauCeti.LinearMap.IsHomogeneous` of degree `(a, b)`. Define the shift
`M⟦a, b⟧`, whose piece of bidegree `(d, s)` is the piece of `M` of bidegree `(d + a, s + b)`, the
tensor product of bigraded modules, and the bigraded vector space `W` of Convention 5. Two
bigraded vector spaces `X` and `Y` of finite total dimension over a field are *stably equivalent*
when `X ⊗ W^⊗a ≅ Y ⊗ W^⊗b` for some `a, b ≥ 0`.

- Shifts compose additively, `M⟦0, 0⟧ = M`, `W = 𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽⟦1, 1⟧` for `𝔽` in bidegree `(0, 0)`, and a
  bigraded map of bidegree `(a, b)` from `M` to `N` is a bigraded map of bidegree `(0, 0)` from `M`
  to `N⟦a, b⟧`.
- The bigraded Poincaré series of `X ⊗ Y` is the product of those of `X` and `Y`; for `Y = W` it is
  Tau Ceti's series `TauCeti.Bigraded.W` times that of `X`.
- Cancellation: for `X` and `Y` of finite total dimension over a field, `X ⊗ W ≅ Y ⊗ W` implies
  `X ≅ Y`, since such spaces are determined by their Poincaré series and `ℤ[ℤ × ℤ]` is a domain.
  Hence stable equivalence is an equivalence relation, and `X` and `Y` are stably equivalent iff
  their Poincaré series are, in the sense of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Bigraded.IsStablyEquiv`.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.InternalGrading`, `TauCeti.LinearMap.IsHomogeneous`,
`TauCeti.Bigraded.Series`, `TauCeti.Bigraded.W`, `TauCeti.Bigraded.IsStablyEquiv`; Mathlib
`MvPolynomial`, `DirectSum.Decomposition`, `TensorProduct`.

**Checks.**
- `W` lowers both gradings on its second summand: `W = 𝔽_(0,0) ⊕ 𝔽_(−1,−1)`, so its graded Euler
  characteristic `Σ (−1)^M t^A` is `1 − t⁻¹`. A convention that shifts only the Maslov grading,
  `𝔽_(0,0) ⊕ 𝔽_(−1,0)`, gives `0` instead, and would make the Euler characteristic of `GH̃` of
  every grid vanish (compare the values in §3.4).

### 1.2 Mapping cones of bigraded complexes (ALG)

Take mapping cones from Mathlib, `HomologicalComplex.homotopyCofiber` and
`CochainComplex.mappingCone`, and the long exact sequence of a short exact sequence of complexes
from `CategoryTheory.ShortComplex.ShortExact.δ`. Give them the bigraded form used by
stabilization invariance (§3.5): for a chain map `f : C → D` of bigraded complexes over
`MvPolynomial (Fin n) K`, homogeneous of bidegree `(0, 0)` with differentials of bidegree
`(−1, 0)`, the cone carries the bigrading in which `D` keeps its bidegrees and `C` is raised by one
in the Maslov grading, as `C⟦−1, 0⟧`.

- The cone's differential has bidegree `(−1, 0)`, and the inclusion of `D` and the projection to
  the shifted `C` are bigraded chain maps of bidegree `(0, 0)`.
- In the long exact sequence of bigraded homology of `0 → D → Cone(f) → C⟦−1, 0⟧ → 0`, up to the
  sign conventions of Mathlib's cone, the connecting map is the map induced by `f`.
- `f` is a quasi-isomorphism iff its cone is acyclic, and a bigraded homotopy equivalence
  induces a bigraded isomorphism on homology (Mathlib `HomotopyEquiv.toHomologyIso`).

*Needs:* §1.1; Mathlib `HomologicalComplex.homotopyCofiber`, `CochainComplex.mappingCone`,
`CategoryTheory.ShortComplex.ShortExact.δ`, `HomotopyEquiv.toHomologyIso`, `QuasiIso`.

**Checks.**
- The cone of multiplication by `U` on `𝔽[U]` has homology `𝔽`, one class in the bidegree of the
  generator of the target, because `U` is injective with cokernel `𝔽`; the cone of the zero map
  has homology `𝔽[U] ⊕ 𝔽[U]⟦−1, 0⟧`. A cone that forgot the shift of the source would put the two
  towers of the second in the same bidegrees.

### 1.3 The structure theorem for finitely generated bigraded 𝔽[U]-modules (ALG)

Tau Ceti proves the singly graded form,
`TauCeti.InternalGrading.exists_isInternal_span_singleton`: over a field `k`, a finitely generated
`ℤ`-graded `k[X]`-module on which `X` lowers degree by a fixed `d ≠ 0` is the internal direct sum
of cyclic submodules generated by homogeneous elements, each free or with annihilator `(X^m)`,
`m ≥ 1`. Prove the bigraded refinement: if `M` is a finitely generated bigraded `𝔽[U]`-module over
a field `𝔽`, with `U` of bidegree `(−2, −1)`, then `M` is the internal direct sum of finitely many
cyclic submodules generated by bihomogeneous elements, each free (a tower `𝔽[U]` shifted to the
bidegree of its generator) or isomorphic to a shift of `𝔽[U]/(U^m)` with `m ≥ 1`
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Proposition A.4.3). Prove that the multiset of bidegrees of the tower
generators and the multiset of pairs (bidegree, `m`) of the torsion summands are determined by
`M`: the piece of `U^i M / U^(i+1) M` in bidegree `(d − 2i, s − i)` has dimension the number of
summands generated in bidegree `(d, s)` whose length exceeds `i`, a tower having infinite length.
This is the algebraic home of `τ` (§3.6) and of every tower-plus-torsion decomposition below.

- The number of towers is the rank of `M` over `𝔽[U]`, and the largest Alexander degree of a tower
  generator is the supremum of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.InternalGrading.nonTorsionDegrees` for the
  Alexander grading.

*Needs:* §1.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.InternalGrading.exists_isInternal_span_singleton`,
`TauCeti.InternalGrading.nonTorsionDegrees`; Mathlib `Ideal.torsionOf`, `DirectSum.IsInternal`.

**Checks.**
- `𝔽[U]/(U²)` generated in bidegree `(0, 0)` has classes in bidegrees `(0, 0)` and `(−2, −1)`, as
  has `𝔽_(0,0) ⊕ 𝔽_(−2,−1)`; the two have the same Poincaré series but are not isomorphic, since
  `U` acts nontrivially on the first. The decomposition is a statement about modules, not series.

### 1.4 Filtered chain complexes and filtered homotopy equivalence (ALG)

Define a filtered chain complex over a ring `R` as a chain complex with an exhaustive increasing
filtration `⋯ ⊆ F_s C ⊆ F_{s+1} C ⊆ ⋯`, indexed by `s ∈ ℤ`, by subcomplexes; a filtered chain map
as a chain map with `f(F_s C) ⊆ F_s D` for every `s`; a filtered chain homotopy as a homotopy `H`
with `H(F_s C) ⊆ F_s D`; a filtered homotopy equivalence as a pair of filtered chain maps whose
composites are filtered chain homotopic to the identities; and a filtered quasi-isomorphism as a
filtered chain map inducing quasi-isomorphisms on every associated graded piece
`F_s C / F_{s−1} C`. This is needed only from §3.13 on, and is reusable far beyond this roadmap.

- Filtered homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation, and filtered chain homotopy is
  compatible with composition.
- A filtered homotopy equivalence restricts to homotopy equivalences `F_s C ≃ F_s D` for every
  `s` and induces homotopy equivalences of the associated graded pieces; in particular it is a
  filtered quasi-isomorphism.

*Needs:* Mathlib `HomologicalComplex`, `Homotopy`, `HomotopyEquiv`, `QuasiIso`.

**Checks.**
- Let `C = 𝔽a ⊕ 𝔽b` with `∂b = a`, `F_0 C = 𝔽a` and `F_1 C = C`. Then `C` is acyclic, hence
  homotopy equivalent to `0` as an unfiltered complex, but its associated graded pieces `𝔽a` and
  `𝔽b` have zero differential, so it is not filtered homotopy equivalent to `0`. The filtered
  notion sees what the unfiltered one forgets.

### Examples

- For a knot grid `G`, `GH⁻(G)` with its (Maslov, Alexander) bigrading is a finitely generated
  bigraded `𝔽[U]`-module (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.finite_unblockedHomology` over a
  Noetherian ring), and §1.3 decomposes it into towers and torsion.

### Dependencies

Mathlib: homological complexes, homotopies and homotopy equivalences, mapping cones, the long
exact sequence, quasi-isomorphisms, multivariate polynomials, internal direct sums and tensor
products. Tau Ceti: internal gradings, homogeneous linear maps, bigraded Poincaré series with `W`
and stable equivalence of series, and the singly graded structure theorem with non-torsion
degrees.

## Layer 2: grid diagrams and links in S³ (Lane K)

This layer connects grid diagrams to the knot theory built in
[GeometricTopology](../GeometricTopology/README.md) Layer 4: knot and link types, diagrams, braid
closures, Seifert surfaces, the Alexander polynomial and the unknotting number. Grid diagrams are
one more presentation of links, and what this layer adds is grid-specific: the link a grid
presents, the adequacy theorem (Cromwell's theorem), and the comparison of grid invariants with
GeometricTopology's. Prior art (the Isabelle knot theory in the Archive of Formal Proofs, the Lean
`leanknot` experiment) shows that adequacy of a diagram calculus is where such projects stall,
which is why no grid homology result in Layer 3 waits for this layer except those stated about
the knot in `S³`. The braid-group and diagram foundations are GeometricTopology's and build on
Mathlib's presented monoids (Hannah Fechtner's braid-group program); they are used here, not
duplicated.

### 2.1 The link presented by a grid diagram (K)

For a grid diagram `G : TauCeti.GridDiagram n`, join in each column the `X` to the `O` and in each
row the `O` to the `X`, with every vertical segment crossing over every horizontal one, and orient
the vertical segments from `X` to `O` and the horizontal ones from `O` to `X`. Define the oriented
link diagram this gives, as a `TauCeti.OrientedPDCode`, and the oriented link in `S³` it realizes
(GeometricTopology §4.2). Prove that each grid move (cyclic permutation of rows or columns,
commutation, stabilization) changes the presented link by an ambient isotopy, so that the
construction descends to a map from `TauCeti.GridLink` to oriented link types (GeometricTopology
§4.1), and that every oriented link in `S³` is presented by a grid diagram (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó,
Appendix B).

- The number of components of the presented link is Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.GridDiagram.componentCount`, so a grid knot presents a knot.
- The cross-commutations of `TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsCrossCommutation` change the presented link by
  one crossing change (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Proposition 3.1.13).

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GridDiagram`, `TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsMove`, `TauCeti.GridLink`,
`TauCeti.GridDiagram.componentCount`, `TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsCrossCommutation`,
`TauCeti.OrientedPDCode`; GeometricTopology §4.1 (link types), §4.2 (realization of diagrams).

**Checks.**
- The `2 × 2` grid `TauCeti.GridDiagram.twoByTwo` presents the unknot, while
  `TauCeti.GridDiagram.torusLink 1 2` presents the left-handed trefoil, whose Jones polynomial
  `t⁻¹ + t⁻³ − t⁻⁴` (GeometricTopology §4.1) is not `1`: a construction forgetting the crossing
  information would present the unknot for both.
- `TauCeti.GridDiagram.torusLink 1 1` has two components (`TauCeti.GridDiagram.componentCount_torusLink`, the gcd of
  `2` and `2`) and presents the Hopf link; with the orientation of either component reversed the
  linking number changes sign, so the orientation convention above is part of the definition.

### 2.2 Cromwell's theorem (K)

Prove Cromwell's theorem: two grid diagrams present ambient isotopic oriented links iff they are
related by grid moves (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Appendix B.4; elementary but fiddly diagrammatic
combinatorics). With §2.1, the map from grid links to oriented link types is a bijection.

- Under this bijection, two grid links differ by `TauCeti.GridLink.IsCrossingChange` iff the links
  they present differ by one crossing change, using that every crossing change of links in `S³`
  is realized by a cross-commutation of suitable grid diagrams (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó,
  Lemma 3.3.10, a grid realization of the skein triple at that crossing); hence
  `TauCeti.GridLink.unknottingNumber` is the unknotting number `u(K)` of GeometricTopology §4.7.1.

*Needs:* §2.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.GridDiagram.MovesTo`, `TauCeti.GridLink.IsCrossingChange`,
`TauCeti.GridLink.unknottingNumber`; GeometricTopology §4.2 (Reidemeister's theorem), §4.7.1 (the
unknotting number).

**Checks.**

## Layer 3: grid homology (Lane G)

Grid homology is combinatorial from end to end (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Chapters 1–15): invariance
reduces to Cromwell's theorem (Appendix B), spectral sequences are avoided, and the holomorphic
identification (Chapter 16) is needed for nothing here, neither for invariance, nor `τ`, nor the
combinatorial proof of the Milnor conjecture, nor the slice-genus bound, nor the Legendrian and
transverse invariants, nor integer coefficients. What is lost without holomorphic input is sharpness
and detection: detection of the genus, of fiberedness and of the unknot.

### 3.5 Invariance (G.5)

Grid moves are commutations and stabilizations, with cyclic permutations. Commutation invariance
goes through pentagon-counting chain maps with hexagon-counting homotopies; stabilization
invariance identifies `GC⁻(G′)` with the mapping cone of `V₁ − V₂ : GC⁻(G)[V₁] → GC⁻(G)[V₁]`
(§1.2). Tau Ceti carries this out for `GH⁻` of a knot as an Alexander-graded `K[U]`-module:
`TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.unblockedHomologyIsomorphic_of_movesTo`, hence for `τ`,
`TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.tau_eq_of_movesTo`; these are consumed, not restated. It also has

(`TauCeti.GridDiagram.fullyBlockedHomologyCommutationMap_bijective`), the cone identification
`TauCeti.GridDiagram.unblockedComplexStabilizeXIsoHomotopyCofiber`, and for one corner placement
of an `X`-stabilization the doubling of `GH̃`
(`TauCeti.OddComponentGridDiagram.bigradedFullyBlockedHomologyPoincareSeries_stabilizeX`).

Prove `bigradedUnblockedHomologyIsomorphic_of_movesTo`: if `G` and `G′` are related by grid moves
and `G` presents a knot, then `GH⁻(G)` and `GH⁻(G′)` are isomorphic as bigraded `K[U]`-modules
(`BigradedUnblockedHomologyIsomorphic`: a `K[U]`-linear equivalence preserving the Maslov grading
`M_O` and the Alexander grading), for every commutative ring `K` of characteristic two
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Theorem 5.3.1). Prove `simplyBlockedHomology_equiv_of_movesTo`: under the
same hypotheses, and for any choice of the blocked `O`-markings, `GĤ(G)` and `GĤ(G′)` are
isomorphic as bigraded `K`-modules (Theorem 5.3.1, with Corollary 4.6.17 for the choice of the
blocked marking). The source works over `𝔽₂`; the complexes over `K` are the base changes
`C ⊗_𝔽₂ K`, the differentials having coefficients in `𝔽₂`, and `K` is flat over the field `𝔽₂`,
so homology commutes with this base change and the isomorphisms over `𝔽₂` give those over `K`.
Prove the stabilization formula for the fully blocked theory: for a grid of size `n` presenting an

and 8.2.8), so that the stable class of `GH̃` (§1.1) is an invariant while `GH̃` itself depends on
the grid size.

### 3.8 Symmetries and the genus bound (G.8)

Tau Ceti proves that `GH⁻` of a knot grid is unchanged, as an Alexander-graded `K[U]`-module, by
the diagonal reflection `TauCeti.GridDiagram.transpose`
(`TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.unblockedHomologyIsomorphic_transpose`) and the half-turn
`TauCeti.GridDiagram.rotate` (`TauCeti.GridDiagram.IsKnot.unblockedHomologyIsomorphic_rotate`).
Define the mirror image and the orientation reverse of a grid link, presented by reflecting the
grid in a vertical axis and by exchanging the `O`- and `X`-markings, and prove that they present
the mirror image and the reverse of the link in `S³` (§2.1). For a knot `K` prove the symmetries
of `GĤ`: `GĤ_M(m(K), A) ≅ GĤ_(−M)(K, −A)` for the mirror `m(K)`, `GĤ_M(K, A) ≅ GĤ_(M−2A)(K, −A)`,
and `GĤ(−K) ≅ GĤ(K)` for the reverse; and `τ(m(K)) = −τ(K)`. Prove the genus bound
`g(K) ≥ max{A : GĤ(K, A) ≠ 0}`, where `g(K)` is the Seifert genus of the knot in `S³` that the
grid presents (GeometricTopology §4.3; Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Proposition 7.2.2). That the bound
is an equality for every knot is known only through the holomorphic theory, which belongs to
HeegaardFloer, so the bound, not detection, is the target.

in `S³`); GeometricTopology §4.1 (mirror, reverse), §4.3 (Seifert genus).

**Checks.**

  `(M, A) = (2, 1), (1, 0), (0, −1)`, consistent with `χ = t − 1 + t⁻¹` (§3.4). The Alexander

### 3.9 The skein exact sequence, thin knots and links (G.9)

Prove the oriented skein exact sequence relating the grid homologies of a skein triple
`(K₊, K₋, K₀)` presented by grids that differ in one crossing. Prove that alternating knots are
thin: `GĤ(K)` is supported on one line `M − A = c` with `c` determined by the signature, so its
total rank is the determinant `|Δ_K(−1)|`. For links, fix the symmetric Alexander lattice and the
flavor conventions and prove the Euler characteristic formulas, which have two branches. For a
knot (`ℓ = 1`) it is §3.4: `χ(GĤ) = Δ_K(t)`, normalized and symmetrized. For a link with `ℓ > 1`,
`χ(GĤ(L))` is `∏ᵢ (tᵢ^½ − tᵢ^−½) Δ_L(t₁, …, t_ℓ)` with the source's unit and sign normalization;
for `ℓ > 1`, with `nᵢ` the number of `O`-markings on the `i`-th component, the fully blocked theory
has `±Δ_L ∏ᵢ (1 − tᵢ⁻¹)^nᵢ tᵢ^½` and the unblocked theory `±Δ_L ∏ᵢ tᵢ^½`
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, Theorem 11.6.1; Manolescu–Ozsváth–Szabó–Thurston, v2 §6, Theorem 6.1);
the fully blocked factor carries the grid-size dependence. The knot formula is not obtained by
substituting `ℓ = 1` into the link factor. Prove that the grid polytope bounds the Thurston norm of
the link complement, as a bound and not as detection.

*Needs:* §3.4, §3.5, §3.6; §2.3 (the multivariable Alexander polynomial of the presented link);
GeometricTopology §6.2 (the signature).

**Checks.**
- The two branches on the unknot and the two-component unlink: the knot branch gives

### 3.10 The slice-genus bound (G.10)

Prove the normal form of knot cobordisms: if knots `K₁` and `K₂` are connected by an oriented
cobordism of genus `g` in `[0, 1] × S³` (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.KnotCobordism`), there are knots `K₁′`,
`K₂′` and integers `b, d ≥ 0` such that `K₁′` and the split union `U_b(K₁)` of `K₁` with `b` unknots
are related by `b` simultaneous oriented saddle moves, `K₁′` and `K₂′` by `2g` oriented saddle
moves, and `K₂′` and `U_d(K₂)` by `d` simultaneous oriented saddle moves (Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó,
Proposition B.5.1, with saddle moves as in Definition 2.6.10). Route: present the cobordism by a
movie of births, saddles, deaths and planar isotopies, move the births below and the deaths above
every saddle, and order the saddles so that the first `b` join the born unknots to the knot and the
last `d` split off the unknots that die; the Euler characteristic `−2g` of the cobordism leaves `2g`
saddles between them. Prove `|τ(K)| ≤ g_s(K)` for a grid knot, where `g_s` is Tau Ceti's slice genus
`TauCeti.sliceGenus` of the smooth knot the grid presents (§2.3), through maps on grid homology
induced by saddle, birth and death moves and the normal form (Corollary 8.1.2, from
`|τ(K₀) − τ(K₁)| ≤ g` for knots connected by a cobordism of genus `g`, Theorem 8.1.1): the most
topologically substantial step of the combinatorial line. With the Seifert surface of the positive
torus knot it gives the combinatorial form of the Kronheimer–Mrowka theorem,
`g_s(T_{p,q}) = (p − 1)(q − 1)/2`.

*Needs:* §3.5, §3.6; §2.3 (grid diagrams to smooth embeddings); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.sliceGenus`,
`TauCeti.KnotCobordism`, `TauCeti.KnotCobordism.genus`; KhovanovHomology K7 (the movie

**Checks.**

  `τ = −τ = 0`, while its slice genus is `1`.

## Layer 4: lattice homology (Lane L)

Four models are kept apart, as in Convention 8: Tau Ceti's native characteristic-two complex,
Némethi's cohomological weight-function model (L.1), Zemke's spherical characteristic model

### 4.1 The native complex and blow-up invariance

Tau Ceti's native complex fixes a characteristic covector `k` and has finite-support generators
`(x, E)` over `𝔽₂[U]`: it is `P.latticeChainComplex k : ChainComplex (ModuleCat PlumbingCoefficient) ℕ`,
with degree object `(P.latticeChainComplex k).X q`, differential
`(P.latticeChainComplex k).d (q+1) q` and homology `P.latticeChainHomology k q`; its `∂² = 0`
(`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.latticeDifferential_comp_self`) and homology API are used, not
recreated. It is the genus-zero, characteristic-two specialization of the homological form of L.1
(Némethi, Remark 3.4.3(a)), with free `𝔽₂[U]` coefficients instead of `T⁺`. Prove that grading
`U^j·c` by `−2(w(c) + j)` makes the degree `−2n` part the cellular chain complex of the sublevel
cube complex `S_n = ⋃{c : w(c) ≤ n}` (Némethi §3.1.11), with `U` induced by `S_n ⊂ S_{n+1}`, so that
`P.latticeChainHomology k q ≅ ⊕ₙ H_q(S_n; 𝔽₂)` as `𝔽₂[U]`-modules. Prove the two headlines for a
negative-definite `P` and every odd value `ε` on the exceptional class:
`nonempty_latticeChainHomology_blowUpVertex` (blow-up at a vertex, the new vertex of valence one)
and `nonempty_latticeChainHomology_blowUpEdge` (blow-up along an edge, valence two), each an
isomorphism of `𝔽₂[U]`-modules in every cubical degree. Route: Némethi's proof of Proposition
3.4.2 gives homotopy equivalences `S_n(Γ′, k′) ≃ S_n(Γ, k)` compatible with the inclusions.

Némethi's value is `ε = −1`, for which the blown-up weight is `χ_k(x) + s(s + 1)/2` on the total
transform `φ(x, s)` of `x` with exceptional multiplicity `s`; for a general odd `ε` it is
`χ_k(x) + s(s − ε)/2` (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.two_mul_characteristicWeight_blowUpCharacteristic`).
The value `ε − 2m` lies in the same orbit, as `k′ + 2Q(mE_new)`, and the covector shift is not a
constant shift of the weight (it adds `ms` on `φ(x, s)`); translating the lattice by `mE_new`
carries the weight function of `ε − 2m` to that of `ε` minus the constant `m(m − ε)/2` (Némethi,
Lemma 3.3.2, `χ_{k+2Q(l)}(y) = χ_k(y + l) − χ_k(l)`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicWeight_add_two_mulVec_eq_sub`), and the translation is an
isomorphism of cube complexes, so neither the exponents of the differential nor the module change.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.latticeChainComplex`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.latticeChainHomology`, `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.blowUpVertex`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.blowUpEdge`, `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.blowUpCharacteristic`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.blowUpEdgeCharacteristic`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.isNegativeDefinite_blowUpVertex_iff`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.isNegativeDefinite_blowUpEdge_iff`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.two_mul_characteristicWeight_blowUpCharacteristic`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicWeight_add_two_mulVec_eq_sub`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.latticeWeightSublevelComplex`; Némethi §3.1.11, Proposition 3.4.2,
Remark 3.4.3(a).

- For `TauCeti.oneVertexPlumbing w` with `w < 0`, both sides of
  `nonempty_latticeChainHomology_blowUpVertex` are `𝔽₂[U]` in cubical degree `0` and zero in higher

  `TauCeti.coefficientEquivOneVertexPlumbingLatticeHomology` on one side, the two-vertex chain with

### 4.2 Némethi's cohomological weight-function model (L.1)

On the negative-definite decorated-graph domain of Némethi §§3.1–3.2, with genus `g_v ≥ 0` at

and negative-definiteness hypotheses (Némethi §3.4). This is not promoted to arbitrary Neumann

### 4.3 Zemke's spherical characteristic model (L.2)

For a finite tree of genus-zero vertices with integral weights, a characteristic covector `K` and a
finite vertex subset `E`, use cube degree `|E|`. In the source normalization, for `I ⊆ E` write
`f_K(I) = (⟨K, E_I⟩ + Q(E_I, E_I))/2` with `E_I = Σ_{v∈I} E_v`, and `g(K, E) = min_{I⊆E} f_K(I)`.
For `v ∈ E`, set `A_v(K, E) = g(K, E∖{v})` and `B_v(K, E) = min_{I⊆E, v∈I} f_K(I)`. Over `𝔽₂[U]` the
two faces of `[K, E]` in the direction `v` are `U^(A_v−g)[K, E∖{v}]` and `U^(B_v−g)[K + 2v*, E∖{v}]`,
summed over `v ∈ E`, where `v*` is the covector `Q(v, ·)` in the chosen lattice convention
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, *A spectral sequence on lattice homology*, arXiv:1206.1654v1, §3,
equations (3.2)–(3.3); Zemke's equation (2.3), §2.3, writes the same differential but does not
define its exponents). Prove that the exponents are nonnegative, that `d² = 0`, and the source's
absolute or relative grading and boundary `Spin^c` label on their actual hypotheses; then compute
the full homology in every cube degree, not only degree zero. State the orientation and the weight
normalization. Define the completed variant over `𝔽₂[[U]]` (a product over the generators, Zemke
§2.3), stating the completion used. Signs and an integral coefficient system are separate targets;
they are not supplied by this characteristic-two formula.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Finset.min'` over the subsets `I ⊆ E`, `Polynomial (ZMod 2)`, `PowerSeries`; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph`, `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicVectors` for `K`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicWeight` for the dictionary `K = k + 2Qx`;
Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:1206.1654v1, §3, equations (3.2)–(3.3) (`f`, `g`, `A_v`, `B_v`);
Zemke §2.3 (the completed carrier).

**Checks.**
- One vertex of weight `w` with `K(v) = c` (`c ≡ w mod 2`): `f_K(∅) = 0`, `f_K({v}) = (c + w)/2`,
  `g(K, {v}) = min(0, (c + w)/2)`, so `A_v − g = max(0, −(c + w)/2)` and `B_v − g = max(0, (c + w)/2)`:
  both are nonnegative and at least one is zero. For `w = −1`, `c = −1` the exponents are `1, 0`; a
  definition that forgets the `−g` normalization would give `0, −1`.
- Zero-framed vertex, `K = 0`: `v* = Q(v, ·) = 0`, so the two faces of `[0, {v}]` are both `[0, ∅]`
  with exponent `0` and cancel over `𝔽₂`; `d = 0` and `H₀ = H₁ = 𝔽₂[U]`. A definition that
  identified `[K, E]` with the native `(x, E)` would instead give `H₀ = 𝔽₂[U]`, `H₁ = 0`.
- Dictionary with the native complex on the `(−1)` vertex: `K = −1 − 2x` has `f_K({v}) = −1 − x`, so
  the exponents `max(0, 1 + x)` and `max(0, −1 − x)` are the native weight drops
  `max(χ(x), χ(x + 1)) − χ(x)` and `max(χ(x), χ(x + 1)) − χ(x + 1)` for `χ(x) = x(x + 1)/2`, and the
  faces `[K, ∅]`, `[K + 2v*, ∅] = [−1 − 2(x + 1), ∅]` are the native vertices `x` and `x + 1`.

### 4.4 The integral graph/surface chain model (L.3)

For a finite plumbing graph `Γ` with integral symmetric intersection form `Q` and a nonnegative
genus `g_v` at each vertex (cycles are allowed), fix a characteristic covector `K`
(`K(x) ≡ Q(x, x) mod 2`) and use `χ_K(l) = −(K(l) + Q(l, l))/2`. The edge correction is the fixed
function `M^{g_v}` of §4.2 applied to the absolute change of `χ_K`, not a free choice: for the
oriented edge from `l` to `l + E_v`, `w_K(l, [v]) = max(χ_K(l), χ_K(l + E_v)) + M^{g_v}(|χ_K(l + E_v) − χ_K(l)|)`.
Vertices keep `w_K(l, ∅) = χ_K(l)`, and for `c = (l, I)` with `|I| ≥ 2` the weight `w_K(c)` is the
maximum of its one-dimensional edge weights. This fixes the graph, the surface labels, the
characteristic sector, the support and the weight, including the genus-one `(−1)` case of §4.2; the
edge weight is not replaced by the spherical maximum-of-vertices rule. For `I = {v₁ < ⋯ < v_q}`, let
`C_q^{Γ,K}` be the finite-support free `ℤ[U]`-module on oriented `q`-cubes, with chain degree `q`,
`deg U = −2`, and the source's absolute or relative grading kept separate. Its signed cubical
boundary, with the product orientation from the displayed vertex order, is

    ∂(l, I) = Σ_j (−1)^(j−1) ( U^(w(c) − w(l, I∖{v_j})) (l, I∖{v_j})
                               − U^(w(c) − w(l + E_vj, I∖{v_j})) (l + E_vj, I∖{v_j}) ),

which lowers cube degree by one. Prove that the exponents are nonnegative integers, that `∂² = 0`,
and that a change of the vertex order induces the orientation isomorphism. On graphs outside
Némethi's domain the integral model is retained without any claim of Némethi invariance.

- On genus-zero graphs this is Tau Ceti's signed differential
  `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential` over `ℤ`, whose sign `(−1)^i` counts the
  position `i` from one and so differs from `(−1)^(j−1)` by an overall sign; the two complexes are
  isomorphic by `(−1)^q` in degree `q`. Tau Ceti's homology `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeHomology`
  is the total one; the cubically graded complex is built here.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Finsupp` over `Polynomial ℤ` for `C_q`, `Finset.sup'` for the cube weight and
`(−1)^(j−1)` for the orientation sign; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicWeight` for `χ_K`
on genus-zero vertices, `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential_comp_self`; §4.2's `M^g` and `T⁺` (Némethi §3.1.4,
§§3.2.1–3.2.2).

**Checks.**
- `A₂` (two `(−2)` vertices joined by an edge, genus `0`), `K = 0`: `χ_K(x₁, x₂) = x₁² − x₁x₂ + x₂²`;
  the square at `(0, 0)` has vertex weights `0, 1, 1, 1`, every edge weight `1` (`M⁰ = 0`) and cube
  weight `1`, so `∂(square) = (0,0;{v₂}) − (1,0;{v₂}) − (0,0;{v₁}) + (0,1;{v₁})` with every exponent

  Without the sign `(−1)^(j−1)` the same sum is `2U·(0,0) − 2·(1,0) − 2·(0,1) + 2·(1,1) ≠ 0` in
  `ℤ[U]`, which vanishes only after reduction mod `2`: the sign is what the native unsigned complex
  can omit and the integral one cannot.
- Genus-one `(−1)` vertex, `K(E) = 1`: `χ_K(xE) = x(x − 1)/2`, the vertices `0` and `E` have weight

  exponents `1`; the spherical rule gives exponents `0, 0` and a different complex (§4.2's case at
  the chain level).
- Zero-framed vertex, `K = 0`, genus `0`: `χ_K = 0`, every exponent is `0`,

### 4.5 Comparisons between the models

Three scopes are kept separate: the integral finite-support chain complex of §4.4; Némethi's
`Hom_ℤ(−, T⁺)` cochain complex of §4.2 on the negative-definite decorated-graph hypotheses of
§§3.1–3.2; and the native characteristic-two coordinate chain complex of §4.1.

- **Coefficient reduction.** Reduction `ℤ[U] → 𝔽₂[U]` gives a chain map
  `ρ_q : C_q^int → C_q^native` only to the characteristic-two finite-support chain model, and only
  after an explicit equivalence of generators, weights and characteristic coordinates is
  constructed; state those conditions and prove the chain-map equation (on genus-zero graphs Tau
  Ceti's `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential_zmod_two` is its chain-level form). It is
  not a map to Némethi's cochain module merely by reducing coefficients.
- **Némethi's cochains are a different carrier.** Let `C_q(Γ, K; ℤ)` be the free abelian group on the
  oriented source cubes. The source cochains `F^q = Hom_ℤ(C_q(Γ, K; ℤ), T⁺)` carry the source grading
  (a degree-`d` cochain has value of degree `d − 2w(c)` on a cube `c`) and the source `U`-action; this
  Hom is the full linear dual, and finite support is not imposed. Its coboundary
  `δ : F^q → F^(q+1)` evaluates on an oriented `(q+1)`-cube by the source's weighted incidence sum of
  values on its `q`-faces, with Némethi's exact coefficient and weight conventions, including the
  augmentation and the reduced output. It raises degree while `∂` of §4.4 lowers it, and even the
  point-cube coefficient modules differ: every element of `T⁺` is killed by a power of `U`, while no
  nonzero element of the free module `ℤ[U]` is. So no same-generator equality or direct
  identification is implied. Keep the evaluation pairing `ev_q : C_q^source × F^q → T⁺` and the
  coboundary `δ_q`; any map between the native chain model and the source cochains is supplied with
  an explicit degree rule (including any reversal or shift), support or completion, coefficient
  action, characteristic-label map and chain-cochain compatibility equation. If it needs an
  intermediate completed dual, name its carrier and both maps; the source augmentation and reduced
  map stay in the diagram.
- **Native coordinates and characteristic labels.** The coordinate-to-characteristic reindexing is
  `K = k + 2Qx`. For nonsingular `Q` the affine map `x ↦ K₀ + 2Qx` identifies the lattice points with
  the characteristic covectors of its orbit; for singular `Q` it can identify different
  coordinates and need not be onto all characteristic covectors, so an isomorphism with the full
  characteristic-labelled complex is not automatic: specify the orbit or prove a different
  comparison.
- **Némethi to `[K, E]`.** The comparison of §4.2 with §4.3 is a third, separate theorem on the exact
  overlap of their hypotheses. It states whether it compares source cohomology with a dual or with
  homology, and gives the coefficient, support, completion, degree, sign, grading and
  characteristic-covector translations.
- **Completion.** For the completed characteristic complex of §4.3, establish the coefficient-change
  chain map from the finite-support complex, the precise completion (direct sum, restricted product
  or full product), its support and topology, and the derived, universal-coefficient or limit
  hypotheses used; an infinite product and its homology are not silently an algebraic tensor
  product or an unrestricted `U`-adic limit.

*Needs:* §§4.1–4.4; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential_zmod_two`,
`TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.signedLatticeDifferential_mapRange`, `TauCeti.PlumbingGraph.characteristicOrbits`;
Mathlib `PowerSeries`.

**Checks.**
- The zero-framed one-vertex case, before any completion claim: in the native finite-support
  coordinate complex `d(e_x) = v_x + v_(x+1)`, giving `H₀ = 𝔽₂[U]` and `H₁ = 0`; in the
  characteristic `K = 0` sector of §4.3 the two faces coincide and cancel, giving `H₀ = H₁ = 𝔽₂[U]`;
  the source's completed characteristic version has `𝔽₂[[U]]` in both degrees. These are different

## Layer 5: the stable combinatorial HF̂ of three-manifolds (Lane H)

The Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó stable invariant (arXiv:0912.0830) is the only published
definition of a Heegaard Floer invariant of three-manifolds that is combinatorial together with its
invariance proof: `HF̂` up to tracked tensor factors `𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽`, and exactly `HF̂` when `b₁(Y) = 0` by a
twisted refinement. The serious topological input is the Reidemeister–Singer theorem with
basepoints, that any two pointed Heegaard diagrams of `Y` are connected by moves, which rests on
Morse and Cerf theory. It is treated the way Layer 3 treats Cromwell: diagrams up to moves are the
input equivalence class, the invariance theorem is stated against it, and the identification with
smooth three-manifolds is the separate target §5.5. This package has no absolute gradings and no
`U`-flavors, and none is promised.

### 5.1 Multi-pointed Heegaard diagrams (H.1)

Tau Ceti has the combinatorics of a pointed multi-pointed Heegaard diagram as data, without smooth
topology: the region system `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem` (attaching-curve incidence, regions and
several basepoints), its generators, domains `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.IsDomainBetween`,
periodic domains `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.periodicDomains`, weak admissibility
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.WeaklyAdmissible` and strong admissibility
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.StronglyAdmissible`, Lipshitz's index
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.maslovIndex` and the relative grading
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.relativeGrading`. Complete the data with the surface genus: a
multi-pointed Heegaard diagram of genus `g` with `k` basepoints is a region system with `g + k − 1`
`α`-curves and as many `β`-curves, an Euler measure on its regions summing to `2 − 2g`, and one
basepoint in each component of the complement of the `α`-curves and of the `β`-curves
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830, §3, opening paragraph).

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem`, `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.periodicDomains`,
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.WeaklyAdmissible`, `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.StronglyAdmissible`,
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.maslovIndex`, `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.relativeGrading`.

**Checks.**
- The genus-one diagram `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.lensSpace p q r` of `L(p, q)` has `p`
  generators, no nonzero periodic domain and is weakly admissible; the diagram
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.circleTimesSphere r` of `S¹ × S²` is weakly admissible only for one
  placement of the basepoint (Tau Ceti
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.weaklyAdmissible_circleTimesSphere_iff`). With the basepoint in the
  large region the periodic domains are the multiples of the difference of the two bigons, which has
  coefficients of both signs; with it in one bigon, twice the other bigon plus the large region is a
  nonzero periodic domain with no negative coefficient. Admissibility is a real condition.

### 5.2 Nice moves, convenient diagrams and the chain complex (H.2)

A diagram is nice when every region without a basepoint is a bigon or a square (Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.IsNice`; Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830, Definition 3.1).
Prove that on a nice diagram the domains counted by the hat differential
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.hatDifferential` are exactly the empty bigons and the empty rectangles
(Propositions 6.9 and 6.10); Tau Ceti proves the consequence that on a nice weakly admissible
diagram a nonzero coefficient joins generators differing in one or two coordinates
(`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.IsNice.hammingDist_eq_one_or_eq_two_of_hatCoefficient_ne_zero`).
Prove that the hat differential of a nice diagram squares to zero (Theorem 6.11); no admissibility
hypothesis is needed, since by the identification above its coefficients count the finitely many
empty bigons and rectangles.
Define the nice moves of Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó: nice isotopies, nice handle slides, and nice
stabilizations of type `b`, which add a basepoint, and of type `g`, which raise the genus
(Definitions 3.3–3.6); prove that nice moves take nice diagrams to nice diagrams (Theorem 3.8).
Define the convenient diagrams, the output of Algorithm 4.1 applied to an essential pair-of-pants
diagram (both curve systems pair-of-pants decompositions of the Heegaard surface by homologically
essential curves) of a three-manifold with no `S¹ × S²` summand (Definition 4.2), and prove that
they are nice (Proposition 4.3). A convenient diagram has genus at least two, since a torus has no
pair-of-pants decomposition.

*Needs:* §5.1; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.IsNice`,
`TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.hatDifferential`, `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.WeaklyAdmissible`;
Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830 (nice moves, convenient diagrams).

### 5.3 The stable invariant HF̂_st (H.3)

For a nice diagram `D` with `b(D)` basepoints, let `HF̃(D)` be the homology over `𝔽₂` of its hat
complex (§5.2). Define `HF̂_st(D)` as the class `[HF̃(D), b(D)]` of the pair under the equivalence
relation on pairs (finite-dimensional `𝔽₂`-vector space, nonnegative integer) generated by
`(V₁, b₁) ~ (V₂, b₂)` whenever `b₁ ≥ b₂` and `V₁ ≅ V₂ ⊗ (𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽)^⊗(b₁ − b₂)`
(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830, Definitions 1.1 and 6.13). The class records the number
of basepoints: `dim V / 2^b` is constant on a class and determines it, so `[𝔽, 1]` and `[𝔽², 1]`
differ, whereas a relation that adds factors `𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽` on either side without counting them, as the
stable equivalence of §1.1 does, identifies them. Prove that a nice isotopy, a nice handle slide and
a nice type-`g` stabilization leave `HF̃(D)` unchanged up to isomorphism, and that a nice type-`b`
stabilization replaces it by `HF̃(D) ⊗ (𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽)` (Theorem 7.1), so that diagrams joined by a sequence
of nice moves have the same `HF̂_st` (Corollary 7.2): the purely topological invariance proof of
Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó. The invariant `HF̂_st(Y)` of a three-manifold is taken on convenient
diagrams and is defined in §5.5.

*Needs:* §§5.1–5.2; Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830.

**Checks.**
- For `L(p, q)` the genus-one diagram `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.lensSpace p q r` is nice with
  one basepoint and its hat differential is zero (Tau Ceti
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.isNice_lensSpace`,
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.hatDifferential_lensSpace`), so its class is `[𝔽^p, 1]`, one
  generator per `Spin^c` structure; for `p = 1` it is the standard diagram of `S³`, with class
  `[𝔽, 1]`.
- For the genus-one diagram of `S¹ × S²` with the basepoint in the annulus the differential is
  zero, the two bigons from one generator to the other cancelling (Tau Ceti
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.hatDifferential_circleTimesSphere_zero`), so its class is
  `[𝔽², 1]`. It differs from the class `[𝔽, 1]` of the diagram of `S³`, which a relation forgetting
  the basepoint count would identify with it, since `𝔽 ⊗ (𝔽 ⊕ 𝔽) ≅ 𝔽²`. With the basepoint in a
  bigon the diagram is neither nice nor weakly admissible (§5.1,
  `TauCeti.HeegaardRegionSystem.isNice_circleTimesSphere_iff`), and no class is formed from it.

### 5.5 Heegaard diagrams of smooth three-manifolds

Define the closed oriented three-manifold presented by a multi-pointed Heegaard diagram, prove that
every closed oriented smooth three-manifold has one, and prove the Reidemeister–Singer theorem with
basepoints: two diagrams present diffeomorphic manifolds iff they are connected by Heegaard moves.

moves and basepoint comparisons here. Prove that a three-manifold with no `S¹ × S²` summand has

(Ozsváth–Stipsicz–Szabó, arXiv:0912.0830, Theorem 5.2), using that two markings of a surface
determine the same handlebody iff they are joined by flips (Theorem 2.3, after Luo, in the form
with generalized flips proved in the source's Appendix, Theorem 11.1). For a closed oriented
three-manifold `Y = Y₁ # n(S¹ × S²)` whose summand `Y₁` has no `S¹ × S²` summand, define

and prove that it is an invariant of `Y` (Theorem 1.3): it does not depend on `D`, by Theorem 5.2
and §5.3, nor on the decomposition, since `Y` determines `n` and `Y₁` by the uniqueness of prime
decompositions (GeometricTopology §8.2).

*Needs:* §§5.1–5.3; MorseTheory Layers 1–2 (Morse functions, handles); GeometricTopology §8.2
