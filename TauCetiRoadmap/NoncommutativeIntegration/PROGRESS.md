## NoncommutativeIntegration: 2026-11-09 to 2026-11-11 (`b89abf4` to `1c6a1a4`)

The headline of this first window is the formula for Peetre's K-functional of the couple (L¹, L∞): for an a.e. strongly measurable f, [the integral of the decreasing rearrangement of ‖f‖ over (0, t)](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/DecreasingRearrangement.html#MeasureTheory.Measure.lintegral_decreasingRearrangement_enorm_eq_iInf_eLpNorm) is the infimum of ‖f₀‖₁ + t‖f₁‖_∞ over splittings f = f₀ + f₁, attained by the radial retraction at level f*(t) (TauCeti#33838). It rests on a new scalar [decreasing rearrangement](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/DecreasingRearrangement.html#MeasureTheory.Measure.decreasingRearrangement) for an arbitrary measure, with the generalized-inverse relation to the distribution function, right continuity, [equimeasurability](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/DecreasingRearrangement.html#MeasureTheory.Measure.volume_restrict_setOf_lt_decreasingRearrangement) and hence equal Lᵖ norms, and the shifted bounds [(f + g)*(s + t) ≤ f*(s) + g*(t)](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Function/DecreasingRearrangement.html#MeasureTheory.Measure.decreasingRearrangement_add_le) and its multiplicative twin. This is the commutative model of the singular numbers μ_t of Layer 1; the operator-algebraic μ_t itself has not been started, and the K-functional formula is stated directly for Lᵖ seminorms rather than through the library's compatible-couple K-functional.

On the abstract side, the real interpolation spaces now [embed continuously](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Interpolation/Exponent.html#TauCeti.CompatibleCouple.KMethodSpace.inclusion) as the secondary exponent grows, (X₀, X₁)_{θ,p} ⊆ (X₀, X₁)_{θ,q} for p ≤ q including q = ∞, with a norm bound independent of the couple. The key step bounds every weighted value of the K-functional by the interpolation norm. A small comparison of Lᵖ seminorms across exponents on infinite measure spaces came along with it.
