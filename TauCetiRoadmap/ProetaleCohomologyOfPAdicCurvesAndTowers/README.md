# Roadmap: pro-étale cohomology of p-adic curves, Drinfeld half-spaces and the rank-two Drinfeld tower

**Summary.** Build the p-adic pro-étale cohomology of smooth p-adic analytic curves, of semistable Stein spaces of every dimension, of the Drinfeld half-spaces `Ω^d` and of the rank-two Drinfeld tower over an arbitrary finite extension `F/ℚ_p`. The input is the semistable geometry of curves (skeleta and triangulations), the log rigid Hyodo–Kato and crystalline machinery, the local Fontaine–Messing comparison with p-adic nearby cycles, the `A_inf` cohomology of semistable formal schemes and the tower spaces with their group actions. The output is: the fundamental diagrams relating `H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1))` of an affinoid, overconvergent affinoid, Stein or proper curve to its de Rham complex and its Hyodo–Kato cohomology; the symbol description of `H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_ℓ(1))` for every prime `ℓ`; the combinatorial filtrations of ℓ-adic and Hyodo–Kato cohomology and the similarity of their Weil–Deligne actions; the period isomorphism for the universal cover of the p-divisible group of a Jacobian; the `G_K`-equivariant fundamental diagram of a semistable Stein weak formal scheme in every degree; the cohomology of `Ω^d` in every coefficient theory as duals of generalized Steinberg representations, including integral p-adic étale cohomology and Dat's compactly supported ℓ-adic complex; and, for the rank-two Drinfeld tower, equivariant semistable models, the Hyodo–Kato and de Rham multiplicities, the multiplicity form of the fundamental diagram, the finite copresentation of mod `p^k` cohomology and its non-admissibility when `F ≠ ℚ_p`.

## Scope and ownership

## Conventions

- `p` is a prime. In Layers 2–4, `C` is a complete algebraically closed extension of `ℚ_p` with `v_p(p) = 1` and value group `v_p(C^×) = ℚ`; this is the hypothesis of the source (CDN-CURVES line 92), whose footnote (CDN-CURVES line 124) attributes it to the use of `B_st` and not to the geometry. It holds for the completion of an algebraic closure of any discretely valued field. `O_C`, `m_C`, `k_C` are the integers, maximal ideal and residue field, `O_C̆ = W(k_C)` and `C̆ = W(k_C)[1/p]`. For a closed subfield `K ⊂ C`, `G_K` is the group of continuous automorphisms of `C` fixing `K`.
- Fix a homomorphism `r ↦ p^r` from `(ℚ, +)` to `C^×` with `p^1 = p` (CDN-CURVES line 586). It defines `p̃_r = [(p^r)^♭] ∈ A_inf`, with `θ(p̃_r) = p^r` and `θ_0(p̃_r) = 0` for `r > 0`, where `θ: A_cris → O_C` and `θ_0: A_cris → O_C̆` are the usual maps, and the embedding `ι: B_st^+ → B_dR^+` sending `u` to `log([p^♭]/p)`.
- `B_st^+ = B_cris^+[u]` with `φ(u) = pu` and `N(u) = −1`, so `Nφ = pφN`: on `u`, `N(φ(u)) = N(pu) = −p` and `pφ(N(u)) = pφ(−1) = −p`. Hyodo–Kato tensors carry the diagonal `φ` and `N = N_HK ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ N_Bst`.
- `ℚ_p(1)` on the pro-étale site is `Ẑ_p(1)[1/p]`, with `Ẑ_p = lim_r ν^*ℤ/p^r` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3), and has Hodge–Tate weight `+1`. The same construction gives `ℚ_ℓ(1)` for every prime `ℓ`. Witness: on the open annulus with coordinate `z`, the Kummer class of `z` in `H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_p(1))` maps under `dlog` to `dz/z`, whose class spans `H¹_dR(Y)`.
- A twist `(−1)` on a Hyodo–Kato or crystalline module multiplies its Frobenius by `p` (CDN-CURVES line 387). Witness: on the open annulus, `φ(t) = t^p` gives `φ(dt/t) = p·dt/t`, so `H¹_HK = C̆·dlog t ≅ C̆(−1)` has slope one, whereas `H¹(Γ, C̆)` carries `φ = σ` and slope zero.
- `M^sep` is the maximal Hausdorff quotient `M/closure(0)` of a topological vector space. `ℚ_p ⊗̂ M = ℚ_p ⊗_{ℤ_p} lim_n M/p^n M` for a `ℤ`-module `M` (CDN-CURVES line 130). `O(Y)^{**}` is the group of units `f` with `f − 1` topologically nilpotent. The dual `(−)^*` of a locally convex space is its continuous dual with the weak topology.
- Generalized Steinberg representations are indexed as in CDN-STEIN §5.2.1 (line 1916): `Δ = {1, …, d}`, `s_i = (i, i+1)`, `P_J` the standard parabolic of the upper triangular Borel generated by the `s_i`, `i ∈ J`, and `Sp_r = Sp_{{1,…,d−r}}` for `0 ≤ r ≤ d`, `Sp_r = 0` for `r > d`. In the Lean carrier the simple reflections are those of Tau Ceti's standard Tits system `TauCeti.glTitsSystem K d`, indexed from `0`: `s_i` is the reflection of index `k = i − 1`, exchanging the basis vectors `e_{i−1}` and `e_i` of `K^{d+1} = ⊕_{k=0}^{d} Ke_k`. Witness: for `d = 1`, `Sp_0(A) = A` (trivial) and `Sp_1(A) = LC(ℙ¹(K), A)/A` (Steinberg); for `d = 2`, `Sp_1 = Sp_{{1}}` is formed on the planes of `K³` (§0.3, the indexing Check).
- For towers, the action conventions are those of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.2 and the rank-two notation of CDN23: `G = GL₂(F)`, `Ǧ = D^×` for the quaternion division algebra `D/F`, `G′ = G/ϖ^ℤ`, `M_n` the Drinfeld levels and `M_n^ϖ` their quotients by `ϖ^ℤ`. The diagonal `F^×` acts trivially (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.7), so the quotient by `ϖ` in the centre of `G` equals the quotient by `ϖ^{−1}` in the centre of `Ǧ`: the pair `(ϖ, ϖ)` acts as the identity. Accordingly `(g, b, w) ∈ G × Ǧ × W_F` translates the `F^×`-torsor of connected components of the infinite-level towers by `det(g)·Nrd(b)^{−1}·rec_F(w)` (§7.1), and `(ϖ, ϖ, 1)` by `ϖ²ϖ^{−2} = 1`.
- Weil–Deligne groups follow CDN-CURVES §8.2.5: for `Y` defined over a finite `K/ℚ_p`, `W_Y ⊂ Aut_K(Y) × φ^ℤ` and `WD_Y = 𝔾_a ⋊ W_Y` with `(λ₁, g₁)(λ₂, g₂) = (λ₁ + p^{−deg g₁}λ₂, g₁g₂)`, `deg: W_K → fℤ` sending a geometric Frobenius to `f` where `q = p^f`. A representation of `WD_Y` restricts on `𝔾_a` to `λ ↦ exp(λN)` for a nilpotent `N`, and the group law is equivalent to `g exp(λN) g^{−1} = exp(p^{−deg g}λN)`, that is `Ng = p^{deg g} gN`. (CDN-CURVES §8.2.5, line 4432, prints the exponent `+deg g₁`, which is incompatible with its own relation `Ng = p^{deg g}gN` at line 4437; the group law here is the one that the standard relation requires.) Witness: on `L e₀ ⊕ L e₁` with `Ne₁ = e₀`, `Ne₀ = 0` and a geometric Frobenius `Φ = diag(1, q)`, `NΦe₁ = qe₀ = qΦNe₁`, and `ΦNΦ^{−1}e₁ = q^{−1}e₀`, so `Φ exp(λN) Φ^{−1} = exp(q^{−1}λN)`, as the law with `p^{−deg}` requires; the exponent `+deg` would demand `ΦNΦ^{−1} = qN`, which at `q = 2` asks for the coefficient `2` where `1/2` is computed. Two smooth representations over fields `L₁`, `L₂` are similar when they become isomorphic over a common extension.
- Curves and affinoids in Layers 2–4 are connected, and a disjoint union is treated componentwise, with the locally constant functions `H⁰_dR(Y)` in place of the constants `C` (Layer 2 preamble; §4.2 for countable unions). Witness: on two discs `(0, 1)` has zero differential but is nonzero modulo the diagonal `C`, and is zero modulo `H⁰_dR = C²`.
- Universes: all spaces are separated, taut and countable at infinity; sheaf categories are formed in a fixed universe enlarged once for sheaves.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 is algebra and combinatorics close to Mathlib: cohomology of graphs with legs and the generalized Steinberg representations. Layer 1 fixes the coefficient sheaves and the Fréchet period spaces in which every diagram lives, and relates the `p`-normalized and `ϖ`-normalized period data of a semistable model. Layers 2–3 treat a quasi-compact curve with a fine triangulation: its pattern, symbols, ℓ-adic, de Rham, Hyodo–Kato and syntomic cohomology, ending with the affinoid and proper fundamental diagrams. Layer 4 passes to limits (overconvergent affinoids, Stein curves), defines the special fibre of a curve without boundary and its Weil–Deligne group, compares ℓ-adic and Hyodo–Kato cohomology, and treats Jacobians. Layer 5 is independent of Layers 2–4 and proves the semistable Stein comparison in every dimension. Layer 6 computes the cohomology of `Ω^d`. Layer 7 uses Layers 4–6 for the rank-two Drinfeld tower over any finite `F/ℚ_p`.

## Layer 0: Graphs with legs and generalized Steinberg representations

### 0.1 Cohomology of graphs with legs

Define an oriented graph with legs as a locally finite oriented graph `Γ = (S, A, A_c)` with finitely many connected components: `S` is the discrete set of vertices, `A` the set of edges, `A_c ⊂ A` the relatively compact edges (those whose closure is compact, each with an origin `s₁(a)` and an end `s₂(a)`, possibly equal), and every `a ∈ A ∖ A_c` (a leg) has exactly one endpoint, recorded as its origin or its end (CDN-CURVES §§1.1.1–1.1.2). For an abelian group `L`, write `L^X` for all functions and `L^{(X)}` for finitely supported functions on `X`. Define `∂: L^S → L^{A_c}` and `∂: L^{(S)} → L^{(A)}` by `∂φ(a) = φ(s₂(a)) − φ(s₁(a))`, a missing endpoint contributing `0`, and set `H⁰(Γ, L) = ker(∂: L^S → L^{A_c})`, `H⁰_c(Γ, L) = ker(∂: L^{(S)} → L^{(A)})`, `H¹(Γ, L) = coker(∂: L^S → L^{A_c})` and `H¹_c(Γ, L) = coker(∂: L^{(S)} → L^{(A)})` (the kernels are `H⁰` and `H⁰_c` of the graph because no edge lacks an endpoint and every compact component has a vertex, CDN-CURVES Remarque 1.2(i), lines 731–736). Define `∂^*: L^A → L^S` by `∂^*φ(s) = Σ_{s₁(a)=s} φ(a) − Σ_{s₂(a)=s} φ(a)` and `H¹_c(Γ, L)^* = ker(∂^*: L^A → L^S)` (CDN-CURVES §1.2.1–1.2.2, lines 723–757).

- For a commutative ring `L`, `H¹_c(Γ, L)^*` is the `L`-linear dual `Hom_L(H¹_c(Γ, L), L)`: `Hom_L(L^{(A)}, L) = L^A`, and `Hom_L(−, L)` is left exact. For an abelian group `L` the `ℤ`-linear dual is in general larger, so the kernel is the definition.
- `H¹`, `H¹_c` and `H¹_c(−)^*` are functorial for morphisms of oriented graphs with legs and for homomorphisms `L → L′`; reversing the orientation of an edge `a` is compensated by the sign change of the `a`-coordinate.
- If `S` is finite there is an exact sequence `0 → H⁰_c(Γ, L) → H⁰(Γ, L) → L^{A∖A_c} → H¹_c(Γ, L) → H¹(Γ, L) → 0` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 1.2(ii)); if `Γ` is compact, `H¹_c = H¹`.
- The bipartite subdivision `Γ^{(2)}` (a vertex added on each relatively compact edge), with vertex set `I = S ⊔ A_c`, edge set `I₂ = {(a, s) : a ∈ A, s an endpoint of a}` and relatively compact edges `I_{2,c} = {(a, s) ∈ I₂ : a ∈ A_c}`, so that `I₂ = I_{2,c} ⊔ (A ∖ A_c)`, has the same cohomology, computed by `L^I → L^{I_{2,c}}` and `L^{I_2} → L^I` (CDN-CURVES §1.2.3, lines 772–775, and Remarque 1.4).
- For the Bruhat–Tits tree of `PGL₂(K)` (ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2), a locally finite graph without legs, oriented arbitrarily, `H¹(Γ, L) = 0` and `H¹_c(Γ, L) ≅ LC(ℙ¹(K), L)/L`, compatibly with `PGL₂(K)`.

**Checks.**

- One vertex with one loop (`A = A_c = {a}`, `s₁(a) = s₂(a)`): `∂ = 0`, so `H¹ = H¹_c = L` and `H¹_c(Γ, L)^* = L`. Using `L^{(A_c)}` in place of `L^{A_c}` makes no difference here but fails for an infinite graph.
- One vertex with one loop and `L = 𝔽₂²` as an abelian group: `H¹_c(Γ, L)^* = L` has order `4`, while `Hom_ℤ(H¹_c(Γ, L), L) = M₂(𝔽₂)` has order `16`; with `L = 𝔽₂` as a ring both have order `2`. The identification with the dual needs ring coefficients and linear duals.
- One vertex with two legs (the graph of an open annulus): `H¹ = 0`, `H¹_c = coker(L → L², x ↦ (−x, −x)) = L` and `H¹_c^* = {(φ₁, φ₂) : φ₁ + φ₂ = 0} ≅ L`; with `H⁰_c = ker(x ↦ (−x, −x)) = 0` and `H⁰ = ker(L → L^∅) = L`, the exact sequence of the third bullet reads `0 → 0 → L → L² → L → 0 → 0`.
- One vertex with one leg (an open disc): `H¹ = H¹_c = H¹_c^* = 0`. A definition of `H¹_c` that ignored legs (taking `∂` into `L^{(A_c)}`) would give `0` for the annulus as well and lose its residue class.
- For the infinite Bruhat–Tits tree, finitely supported functions give `H¹_c ≠ 0` while all functions give `H¹ = 0`: interchanging the two cokernels fails.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Submodule.mkQ`; ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.2.

### 0.2 The monodromy operator of a metrized graph

For a graph with legs whose relatively compact edges carry lengths `µ(a) ∈ ℚ_{>0}` and a `ℚ`-module `L`, define `N_µ: H¹_c(Γ, L)^* → H¹(Γ, L)` as the composite of `φ ↦ (a ↦ µ(a)φ(a))|_{A_c}` from `L^A` to `L^{A_c}` with the projection to `H¹(Γ, L)` (CDN-CURVES §1.2.4). Prove that `N_µ` is an isomorphism when `Γ` is compact (CDN-CURVES Remarque 1.5). A compact `Γ` has finitely many vertices and edges and no legs, `φ ↦ µφ` carries `ker ∂^*` onto the cochains of zero `µ`-weighted divergence, and for `L = ℚ` the statement is the harmonic decomposition `C¹ = Harm_µ ⊕ im d` of ColemanIntegrationPartII §1.2 (lengths `ℓ = µ`, `k = ℚ`; its differential `f(tail) − f(head)` is `−∂` and has the same image); for a `ℚ`-module `L` every term of the finite complexes commutes with `⊗_ℚ L`, `ℚ` being a field. Prove that `N_µ` is natural for length-preserving maps and multiplies by `c` when all lengths are multiplied by `c ∈ ℚ_{>0}`, and that for a module `M` with `H¹_c(Γ, L)^*` as quotient and `H¹(Γ′, L)` as submodule (`Γ′ ⊂ Γ`) the induced operator `N` on `M` satisfies `N ∘ N = 0` when the image of `H¹(Γ′, L)` in `H¹_c(Γ, L)^*` is zero (CDN-CURVES Remarque 1.6).

**Checks.**

- One vertex with one loop of length `µ`: `N_µ` is multiplication by `µ`, an isomorphism of `L`; this is the monodromy of a Tate curve with `v_p(q) = µ`.
- A segment with two legs at each end (an affinoid with two holes in the annulus pattern): `A_c` consists of one edge, `H¹(Γ, L) = 0`, so `N_µ = 0` although `H¹_c^*` has rank three; the compactness hypothesis of the isomorphism statement cannot be dropped.
- Doubling all lengths doubles `N_µ`; a definition ignoring `µ` (the projection alone) would not.

*Needs:* §0.1; ColemanIntegrationPartII §§1.1–1.2 (the compact case).

### 0.3 Generalized Steinberg representations

Let `K` be a finite extension of `ℚ_p`, `G = GL_{d+1}(K)`, `d ≥ 1`, with Borel `B`, simple reflections `s_i = (i, i+1)` for `i ∈ Δ = {1, …, d}`, standard parabolics `P_J = B W_J B` and compact `X_J = G/P_J`. For an abelian group `A` define `Sp_J(A)` as the quotient of `LC(X_J, A)` by the sum, over `i ∈ Δ ∖ J`, of the images of `LC(X_{J∪{i}}, A)` under pullback along `X_J → X_{J∪{i}}`, with `G` acting by left translation `(g·f)(x) = f(g⁻¹x)`; set `Sp_r(A) = Sp_{{1,…,d−r}}(A)` for `0 ≤ r ≤ d` and `Sp_r(A) = 0` for `r > d` (CDN-STEIN §5.2.1). For a topological ring `Λ` define `Sp^cont_J(Λ)` as the same quotient of continuous functions `C(X_J, Λ)` (CDN-STEIN §5.2.3), and give `Sp_J(Λ)` the locally convex inductive-limit topology of `LC(X_J, Λ) = colim_n LC(X_{n,J}, Λ)` for a presentation `X_J = lim_n X_{n,J}` by finite sets (CDN-STEIN §5.2.2).

For a commutative ring `A` the same construction is a representation, and it is defined here in that form. The parabolic subgroups are Tau Ceti's: `P_J` is `TauCeti.TitsSystem.standardParabolic` of the standard Tits system `TauCeti.glTitsSystem K d` of `GL_{d+1}(K)`, whose `B` is the upper triangular group and whose simple reflections are the classes of the adjacent transpositions exchanging the basis vectors of index `k` and `k + 1` of `K^{d+1}`, `k ∈ {0, …, d − 1}`; `J` is a set of these simple reflections, the reflection `s_i` of the Conventions being the one of index `k = i − 1`. `TauCeti.TitsSystem.standardParabolic_monotone` gives `P_J ⊂ P_{J′}` for `J ⊂ J′`, so that `X_J → X_{J′}` is defined. The Tits system needs `Nontrivial K^×`, which holds since `K` has characteristic `0`. Define `translation G H A`, for a topological group `G` and a subgroup `H`, as the representation of `G` on `LC(G/H, A)` by `(g·f)(x) = f(g⁻¹x)`; `steinbergRelations K A d J` as the relation submodule `Σ_{s ∉ J} LC(X_{J∪{s}}, A)` of `LC(X_J, A)`; `steinbergRelations_le_comap` records that it is stable under translation, because `X_J → X_{J∪{s}}` is `G`-equivariant; and `generalizedSteinberg K A d J` as the quotient representation `Sp_J(A)`. For an abelian group `A` the definition above is the same quotient of `ℤ`-modules.

- `Sp_J(A)` is a smooth `A[G]`-module, functorial in `A`; the natural map `Sp_J(ℤ) ⊗ A → Sp_J(A)` is an isomorphism and `Sp_J(A)` is a free `A`-module.
- `Sp_∅(A)` is the Steinberg representation and `Sp_Δ(A) = A` with trivial action.
- For a finite extension `L` of `ℚ_p`, `Sp_J(L)` is an LF-space, and its continuous dual `Sp_J(L)^*` is the annihilator in `LC(X_J, L)^*` of the relation submodule `Σ_{i∉J} LC(X_{J∪{i}}, L)`; writing `V_n = image(LC(X_{n,J}, L) → Sp_J(L))`, a finite-dimensional `L`-space, `Sp_J(L) = colim_n V_n` and `Sp_J(L)^* = lim_n V_n^*` is a nuclear Fréchet space (CDN-STEIN §5.2.2, line 1936, writes `lim_n LC(X_{n,J}, L)^*`, which is the dual of `LC(X_J, L)` and not of its quotient unless `J = Δ`), `Sp_J(O_L)^*` is a compact torsion-free `O_L`-module, `Sp^cont_J(L)` is a Banach space with unit ball `Sp^cont_J(O_L)`, the action of `G` is continuous, and the smooth vectors of `Sp^cont_J(L)` are `Sp_J(L)`.
- The natural maps `Sp_r(ℚ_p)^* → Sp_r(F)^* → Sp_r(K)^*` for `ℚ_p ⊂ F ⊂ K` are closed embeddings.
- The relation submodule is stable under `G`, and the quotient is functorial in `J`: for `J ⊂ J′` the pullback `LC(X_{J′}, A) → LC(X_J, A)` is `G`-equivariant, injective because `X_J → X_{J′}` is surjective, and sends the relations for `J′` into those for `J`: for `s ∉ J′` also `s ∉ J`, and `X_J → X_{J′∪{s}}` factors through `X_{J∪{s}}`. It therefore induces a `G`-map `Sp_{J′}(A) → Sp_J(A)`.
- `translation G H A` is a representation: each `translation G H A g` is `A`-linear, `translation G H A 1` is the identity and `translation G H A (g * h) = translation G H A g ∘ translation G H A h`. It fixes the constant functions, `(h · f)(H) = f(H)` for `h ∈ H`, and for `H = G` the quotient `G ⧸ G` is a point and the representation is `A` with trivial action.

### 0.4 Irreducibility, lattices and completions

Prove: (a) if `A` is a field of characteristic `0` or `p`, then the `Sp_J(A)`, `J ⊂ Δ`, are irreducible (`generalizedSteinberg_isIrreducible`, for every `d ≥ 0`, `K` a finite extension of `ℚ_p` with its absolute value and every set `J` of simple reflections of `TauCeti.glTitsSystem K d`; the instance `Nontrivial K^×` that the Tits system needs holds automatically, `K` having characteristic `0`), pairwise non-isomorphic, and are the irreducible constituents of `LC(G/B, A)`, each with multiplicity one (CDN-STEIN Proposition 5.1; Casselman in characteristic `0`: BW00, Chapter X, Theorem 4.11, as cited at CDN-STEIN line 1920; GK14 Corollaries 4.3 and 4.4 in characteristic `p`). The cited characteristic-`0` theorem is over `ℂ`; for an arbitrary field `A` of characteristic `0` the route passes through `Sp_J(A) = Sp_J(ℚ) ⊗_ℚ A`: for a compact open `U ⊂ G` the invariants `Sp_J(ℚ)^U` are finite-dimensional (`U` has finitely many orbits on `X_J`) and taking `U`-invariants commutes with `⊗_ℚ A`; when `Sp_J(ℚ)^U ⊗ ℂ` is nonzero it is a simple module over the Hecke algebra of `U` (the irreducibility over `ℂ`), so by Schur's lemma over `ℂ` and Burnside's theorem the rational Hecke algebra maps onto `End_ℚ(Sp_J(ℚ)^U)`, so `Sp_J(A)^U` is simple for every such `A`; a nonzero `G`-stable `W ⊂ Sp_J(A)` then has `W^U = Sp_J(A)^U` for all small `U`, hence `W = Sp_J(A)`, and pairwise non-isomorphism and multiplicity one descend in the same way; (b) for a complete discrete valuation ring `O` of residue characteristic `p`, `Sp_J(O)` is, up to homothety, the unique `G`-stable `O`-lattice in `Sp_J(Frac O)` (CDN-STEIN Corollary 5.3; GK14 Corollary 4.5); (c) for `L/ℚ_p` finite, the universal unitary completion of `Sp_J(L)` is `Sp^cont_J(L)`, and the `G`-bounded vectors of `Sp_J(L)^*` are `Sp^cont_J(L)^*` (CDN-STEIN Corollary 5.4). Consequently `Sp^cont_r(ℚ_p)^* ≅ Sp_r(ℤ_p)^* ⊗ ℚ_p` and `Sp_r(ℤ_p)/p ≅ Sp_r(𝔽_p)`.

**Checks.**

- For a field of characteristic `ℓ ≠ p` the multiplicity-one statement fails in general (CDN-STEIN Remarque 5.2); (a) is not stated for such fields.
- For `d = 1`, `LC(ℙ¹(K), A)` has the two constituents `A` and `Sp_1(A)` when `A` has characteristic `0` or `p`.
- A `G`-bounded vector of `Sp_1(ℚ_p)^*` is a measure on `ℙ¹(K)` of total mass zero; a distribution of unbounded growth on shrinking discs is not bounded, matching (c).

### 0.5 The Schneider–Stuhler and Iovita–Spiess presentation

Let `𝓗 = ℙ((K^{d+1})^*)` be the profinite set of `K`-rational hyperplanes. Prove that `Sp_r(ℤ)` is isomorphic, `G`-equivariantly, to the group `LC_c(𝓗^{r+1}, ℤ)` of locally constant functions on `𝓗^{r+1}` that satisfy the cocycle identity in their `r+2` arguments and vanish on tuples whose equations are linearly dependent (CDN-STEIN Proposition 5.6 and §5.4.1, lines 2046–2054). For an abelian group `A`, define the degenerate distributions `D(𝓗^{r+1}, A)^{deg}` as the kernel of the strict surjection `D(𝓗^{r+1}, A) = Hom(LC(𝓗^{r+1}, ℤ), A) → Hom(Sp_r(ℤ), A)`. For a complete discretely valued field `L` with integers `O_L`, define the measures `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L) ⊂ D(𝓗^{r+1}, L)` as the bounded distributions: those `μ` for which the values `μ(1_U)` on the characteristic functions of the compact open subsets `U ⊂ 𝓗^{r+1}` lie in `ϖ_L^{−N}O_L` for one `N`; equivalently `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L) = D(𝓗^{r+1}, O_L) ⊗_{O_L} L`, with unit ball `D(𝓗^{r+1}, O_L)`.

- `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L)` is `G`-stable, and a measure extends uniquely, by uniform approximation of continuous functions by locally constant ones, to a continuous functional on the Banach space `C(𝓗^{r+1}, L)`; this identifies `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L)` with the continuous dual of `C(𝓗^{r+1}, L)`.
- Restriction along `Sp_r(ℤ) ⊂ LC(𝓗^{r+1}, ℤ)` sends `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L)` into `Sp_r(O_L)^* ⊗ L`, the bounded functionals; the degenerate measures are `M(𝓗^{r+1}, L) ∩ D(𝓗^{r+1}, L)^{deg}`.

**Checks.**

- `r = 1`, `d = 1`: a locally constant `f(H₀, H₁)` with `f(H₁, H₂) − f(H₀, H₂) + f(H₀, H₁) = 0` and `f(H, H) = 0` is `f(H₀, H₁) = g(H₁) − g(H₀)` with `g ∈ LC(ℙ¹(K), ℤ)/ℤ`, recovering `Sp_1(ℤ)`.
- `r = 0`: the cocycle condition forces constant functions, and `Sp_0(ℤ) = ℤ`.
- Measures, `d = 1`, `r = 0`, `𝓗 = ℙ¹(K)`: a Dirac mass `δ_a` is a measure, with values in `{0, 1}`; since `Sp_0(ℤ) = ℤ` is the constants, restriction to it is the total mass, so `δ_a − δ_b` is a degenerate measure and `δ_a` is not. The distribution `μ = Σ_{n≥1} p^{−n}(δ_{x_n} − δ_{y_n})`, for distinct points `x_n, y_n` converging to a point `z` outside the sequence, is well defined (a compact open set contains both `x_n` and `y_n` or neither for `n ≫ 0`) but not a measure: on a small compact open ball around `x_n` containing no other `x_m`, `y_m`, its value is `p^{−n}`, unbounded.
- `d = 1`, `r = 2`: any three hyperplanes of `K²` have dependent equations, so the vanishing condition gives `0 = Sp_2(ℤ)`, whereas the cocycles without it contain the nonzero coboundaries `g(H₁, H₂) − g(H₀, H₂) + g(H₀, H₁)` of locally constant `g`; the vanishing condition cannot be dropped. At `r = 1` the cocycle identity at `H₀ = H₁ = H₂` already forces `f(H, H) = 0`.

### 1.4 Uniformizer normalizations of semistable period data

Let `K ⊂ C` be a closed subfield that is complete and discretely valued, with perfect residue field `k`, `F = W(k)[1/p] ⊂ C̆`, `e = [K:F]` its absolute ramification index and `ϖ` a uniformizer, and let `log_K` be the logarithm of `O_K^×` that vanishes on Teichmüller units. Let `M` be an `F`-space presented as in §1.2, with a bijective `σ`-semilinear Frobenius `φ` and a nilpotent `N_raw` satisfying `N_raw φ = pφN_raw`, normalized by `ϖ` (a node `xy = ϖ^h` of a model contributes `h`), and put `N_p = N_raw/e`; let `ι_HK^ϖ: K ⊗̂_F M → M_dR` be a continuous `K`-linear map to a `K`-space, attached to `ϖ` (in the applications, the Hyodo–Kato map of a semistable model over `O_K`, §§4.3 and 5.2). Define:

- for `b ∈ O_K^×` and a compatible system `b^♭` of its `p`-power roots, `ℓ_b = log([b^♭]/b) + log_K(b)`;
- the embeddings `ι_ϖ, ι_p: B_st^+ → B_dR^+`, `u ↦ log([ϖ^♭]/ϖ)` (CDN-STEIN lines 327–328) and `u ↦ log([p^♭]/p)` (the embedding `ι` of the Conventions), both for `N(u) = −1`, and on `B_st^+ ⊗̂_F M` the total monodromies `D_raw = N_raw ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ N` and `D_p = N_p ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ N`; `X^r_st(M)` of §1.3 formed with `D_p` and paired with `ι_p` is the `p`-normalized period space, formed with `D_raw` and paired with `ι_ϖ` the `ϖ`-normalized one, and in both the right vertical map is `θ` after `u ↦ 0` (for either embedding `θ(ι(u)) = 0`, since `θ([x^♭]/x) = 1`);
- the `B_cris^+`-algebra automorphism `A_e: u ↦ eu` of `B_st^+`, with inverse `u ↦ u/e`, and, for `a = ϖ^e/p ∈ O_K^×` with `a^♭ = (ϖ^♭)^e/p^♭`, the `B_cris^+`-algebra isomorphism `Ψ_ϖ: u ↦ eu − ℓ_a` from the `p`-normalized to the `ϖ`-normalized presentation of `B_st^+`;
- the `p`-normalized Hyodo–Kato map `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^ϖ ∘ exp(−log_K(a)N_p) = ι_HK^ϖ ∘ exp(−e^{−1}log_K(a)N_raw)`, the exponential being the finite exponential of a nilpotent operator of CohomologyComparisons §4.5, whose monodromy transport along `c` is `exp(−cN)`.

Prove:

- *Logarithms of units.* Writing `b = [b̄]b₁` with `[b̄]` the Teichmüller lift of the residue of `b` and `b₁ ∈ 1 + m_K`, and `b₁^♭ ∈ 1 + m_{C^♭}` for a compatible system of roots of `b₁`, `ℓ_b = log[b₁^♭] + ct` with `c ∈ ℤ_p` fixed by the choice of roots; hence `ℓ_b ∈ (B_cris^+)^{φ=p}` (the logarithm of `[x]`, `x ∈ 1 + m_{C^♭}`, converges in `B_cris^+` with `φ = p`), `θ(ℓ_b) = log_K(b)`, and `ℓ_{bb′} = ℓ_b + ℓ_{b′}` for the product systems of roots.
- *Scaling.* `A_e` commutes with `φ` and with `u ↦ 0`, and `D_raw ∘ A_e = e·A_e ∘ D_p`: on `f(u) ⊗ m` both sides are `f(eu) ⊗ N_raw m − e f′(eu) ⊗ m`. So `A_e` sends `p`-normalized horizontal elements (`D_p = 0`) to raw ones (`D_raw = 0`), and `A_e^{−1}` sends raw ones to `p`-normalized ones.
- *Unit factor.* `e·log([ϖ^♭]/ϖ) = log([p^♭]/p) + log([a^♭]/a)` in `B_dR^+`; `Ψ_ϖ` commutes with `φ` (as `φ(ℓ_a) = pℓ_a`) and with `G_K` (the Kummer cocycles satisfy `c_a = e·c_ϖ − c_p`), satisfies `D_raw ∘ Ψ_ϖ = e·Ψ_ϖ ∘ D_p`, and so maps the `p`-normalized `X^r_st(M)` onto the `ϖ`-normalized one. It equals `A_e` when `ℓ_a = 0`, for instance when `ϖ^e = p` and `(ϖ^♭)^e = p^♭`.
- *Hyodo–Kato maps.* `ι_ϖ(Ψ_ϖ(u)) = ι_p(u) − log_K(a)`, and `Ψ_ϖ` followed by `u ↦ 0` and `θ` sends `u` to `−log_K(a)`. On a `p`-normalized horizontal element `exp(uN_p)(b ⊗ m)`, `b ∈ B_cris^+`, the `ϖ`-normalized right vertical map therefore takes the value `θ(b) ⊗ ι_HK^ϖ(exp(−log_K(a)N_p)m)`, so `(θ ⊗ ι_HK^ϖ) ∘ Ψ_ϖ = θ ⊗ ι_HK^{(p)}`: `Ψ_ϖ` identifies the right-hand terms and the right vertical maps formed in the two normalizations. With `c = log_K(a)`, `exp(−cN_p) − 1 = −cN_p ∘ g(cN_p)`, where `g(x) = Σ_{k≥0} (−x)^k/(k+1)!` is a finite sum on the nilpotent `cN_p` with constant term `1`, so `g(cN_p)` is invertible; hence `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^ϖ` exactly when `ι_HK^ϖ ∘ cN_p = 0`. When `ι_HK^ϖ` is injective, as are the Hyodo–Kato isomorphisms of §§4.3 and 5.2, this holds exactly when `log_K(a)N_p = 0`, so for such a map and a general uniformizer neither the scaling `A_e` alone nor the unchanged map `ι_HK^ϖ` identifies the right vertical maps; for a non-injective map (the zero map, say) the two can agree although `log_K(a)N_p ≠ 0`.
- *Independence of the uniformizer.* If the maps attached to the uniformizers of `K` satisfy the transport law `ι_HK^{ϖw} = ι_HK^ϖ ∘ exp(log_K(w)N_raw)` for `w ∈ O_K^×` (CohomologyComparisons §4.5, stated there for the evaluation maps, which go in the inverse direction), then `ι_HK^{(p)}` does not depend on `ϖ`: replacing `ϖ` by `ϖw` multiplies `a` by `w^e` and replaces `ι_HK^ϖ` by `ι_HK^ϖ ∘ exp(e log_K(w)N_p)`, and by the cocycle property of CohomologyComparisons §4.5 the two changes cancel.

**Checks.**

- Scaling direction, `e = 2`: take `ϖ² = p` and `(ϖ^♭)² = p^♭`, so `ℓ_a = ℓ_1 = 0` and `Ψ_ϖ = A₂`; let `N_raw e₁ = 2e₀`, `N_raw e₀ = 0`, so `N_p e₁ = e₀`. The `p`-normalized horizontal element `e₁ + ue₀` goes to the raw horizontal element `A₂(e₁ + ue₀) = e₁ + 2ue₀`, and `A₂^{−1}(e₁ + 2ue₀) = e₁ + ue₀`. Applying `A₂` to the raw element instead gives `e₁ + 4ue₀`, with `D_p(e₁ + 4ue₀) = e₀ − 4e₀ = −3e₀ ≠ 0`, and mixing the normalizations fails, `D_p(e₁ + 2ue₀) = −e₀ ≠ 0`.
- Unit factor, `e = 1`: `p = 3`, `K = ℚ_3`, `ϖ = 12`, so `a = 4 ∈ 1 + 3ℤ_3`, and `N e₁ = e₀`, `N e₀ = 0`. The scaling is the identity, but `Ψ_ϖ(u) = u − ℓ_4` with `θ(ℓ_4) = log_3(4) = 3 − 9/2 + 27/3 − ⋯`, of valuation `1`, and `ι_HK^{(p)}(e₁) = ι_HK^{12}(e₁ − log_3(4)e₀)`. Equivalently, the evaluation maps of CohomologyComparisons §4.5 satisfy `ρ_{12} = ρ_3 ∘ exp(−log_3(4)N)`: the horizontal section whose `ρ_3`-value is `e₁` has `ρ_{12}`-value `e₁ − log_3(4)e₀`.
- Sign of the correction: with the transport law, `ι_HK^{12} = ι_HK^3 ∘ exp(log_3(4)N)`, so `ι_HK^{(p)}(e₁) = ι_HK^3(e₁)`, which is what `ϖ = 3` with `ϖ^♭ = p^♭` (`a = 1`, `a^♭ = 1`, `ℓ_1 = 0`) gives directly; the opposite sign `ι_HK^{12} ∘ exp(+log_3(4)N)` would give `ι_HK^3(e₁ + 2log_3(4)e₀)`, which for an injective `ι_HK^3` (the identity of `ℚ_3 ⊗ M`, say) differs from `ι_HK^3(e₁)` by `2log_3(4)e₀ ≠ 0` and so depends on the uniformizer.
- Detection needs injectivity: on the same `M = ℚ_3e₀ ⊕ ℚ_3e₁` with `φ = diag(1, 3)` and `Ne₁ = e₀`, `Ne₀ = 0` (so `Nφe₁ = 3e₀ = 3φNe₁`), take `ι_HK^{12} = 0`. Then `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^{12} = 0` although `log_3(4)N_p e₁ = log_3(4)e₀ ≠ 0`, in accordance with the criterion `ι_HK^{12} ∘ log_3(4)N_p = 0`; with `ι_HK^{12}` the identity of `ℚ_3 ⊗ M` instead, `ι_HK^{(p)}(e₁) − ι_HK^{12}(e₁) = −log_3(4)e₀ ≠ 0`.
- Degenerate cases: for `N_raw = 0`, `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^ϖ` for every `ϖ`, and `Ψ_ϖ` changes `u` but not the horizontal elements `b ⊗ m`. For `e = 1`, `ϖ = p` and the same roots `ϖ^♭ = p^♭`: `a = 1`, `a^♭ = 1`, `ℓ_a = 0`, so `A_1`, `Ψ_ϖ` and the correction `exp(−log_K(a)N_p)` are identities and `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^ϖ`, which is the given map and not the identity in general (for `N = 0` and `ι_HK^ϖ = 2·id` it stays `2·id`). For `ϖ = p` with `ϖ^♭ = εp^♭`, `ε = (1, ζ_p, ζ_{p²}, …)`, still `a = 1` and the correction is the identity (`log_K(1) = 0`), but `a^♭ = ε`, `ℓ_a = log[ε] = t` and `Ψ_ϖ(u) = u − t ≠ u`; equality of uniformizers without equality of roots does not make `Ψ_ϖ` the identity.

## Layer 2: Patterns, symbols and ℓ-adic cohomology of quasi-compact curves

Throughout Layers 2–4 a curve is a smooth connected separated rigid analytic curve over `C`, and an affinoid is a connected affinoid curve (CDN-CURVES lines 129 and 806–807); a curve is quasi-compact if it is an affinoid or proper. Connectedness is what makes the constants a single copy: `ker(d: O(Y) → Ω¹(Y)) = C`, the constant principal units are `1 + m_C` and `H⁰_syn(Y_S, 1) = (F¹A_cris)^{φ=p}` (§3.3). A finite disjoint union `Y = Y_1 ⊔ ⋯ ⊔ Y_m` of curves is treated componentwise: each statement of Layers 2–4 holds for `Y` as the product of the statements for the `Y_i`, with the locally constant functions `H⁰_dR(Y) = LC(Y, C) = C^m` in place of `C`, `LC(Y, O_C) = O_C^m` in place of `O_C`, `(1 + m_C)^m` in place of `1 + m_C` and `((F¹A_cris)^{φ=p})^m` in place of `(F¹A_cris)^{φ=p}`; countable disjoint unions of curves without boundary are §4.2. A triangulation of a quasi-compact curve is fine when the special fibre of the associated semistable model has at least two irreducible components, all smooth, any two meeting in at most one point (CDN-CURVES §3.5.2, §6.1).

### 2.1 Shorts, legs, ghost circles and residues

Let `R` be an `O_C̆`-algebra that is local with residue field `k_C` and separated and complete for the `I`-adic topology of a finitely generated ideal `I ∋ p` (CDN-CURVES §0.4.2, lines 591–598). The cases used are `R = O_C̆` and `R = A_cris` with `I = (p)`, `R = O_C` with `I = (p)`, and `R = A_inf` with `I = (p, [p^♭]) = θ^{−1}(pO_C)`; `O_C` and `A_inf` are thickenings of `O_C` in the sense of CDN-CURVES §3.2.1 (lines 1431–1435). `A_inf` and `A_cris` are not valuation rings, and nothing below uses that the ideals of `R` are totally ordered. Define over `R`:

- a ghost circle: `Spf R[[T, T^{−1}⟩`, where `R[[T, T^{−1}⟩` is the completion of `R[[T]][T^{−1}]` for the topology of the ideal of definition (CDN-CURVES §0.4.2–0.4.3);
- a leg of length `µ ∈ ℚ_{>0}` over `O_C`: `Spf O_C[[T₁, T₂]]/(T₁T₂ − p^µ)`, oriented by the choice of `T₁`, with boundary the two ghost circles in `T₁` and `T₂`;
- a short: the formal scheme of a connected one-dimensional affinoid with good reduction, i.e. `Spf A` with `A` a formally smooth quotient of `O_C⟨x₁, …, x_n⟩` whose generic fibre is a connected affinoid curve; it has a model `Y̆` over `O_C̆`, unique up to isomorphism, and its special fibre is a smooth proper curve with finitely many marked points of multiplicity `0+` (CDN-CURVES §3.3.1, Remarque 3.13).

For `ω = Σ_k α_k T^k dT/T` on a ghost circle define `Res(ω) = α₀` (CDN-CURVES §0.4.4).

- `Res` does not depend on the parameter: for a unit `u` of `O_C[[T, T^{−1}⟩[1/p]`, `Res(du/u)` is the integer `v′(u)` of CDN-CURVES Lemme 4.2, and the general case follows by linearity and continuity.
- On a leg, `dT₁/T₁ + dT₂/T₂ = 0`, so reversing the orientation changes the sign of the residue, and `Res_Y(ω) = Res_{Y₁}(ω) = −Res_{Y₂}(ω)`.
- Shorts, legs and ghost circles base change along `O_C̆ → R`, and a Frobenius lift on `Y̆` induces one on the `A_inf`-short `Ỹ`.

**Checks.**

- `Res(dT/T) = 1` and `Res(d(T^k)) = 0` for `k ≠ 0`.
- On the leg `T₁T₂ = p^µ`, `Res(dT₁/T₁) = 1` in the `T₁`-orientation and `−1` in the `T₂`-orientation.
- `O_C[[T, T^{−1}⟩` contains `Σ_{k≥0} T^k`, whose coefficients do not tend to `0`, so it is not in `O_C⟨T, T^{−1}⟩`; whereas `Σ_{k≥1} T^{−k}` is not in `O_C[[T, T^{−1}⟩`, since negative coefficients must tend to `0` p-adically. The ghost circle is neither the annulus of width zero nor its Laurent completion in both directions.
- The base rings are not valuation rings: in `A_inf`, `p` and `[p^♭]` are incomparable for divisibility, since reduction modulo `p` rules out `p ∣ [p^♭]` (the image `p^♭` is nonzero in `O_C^♭`) and `θ_0` rules out `[p^♭] ∣ p` (`θ_0([p^♭]) = 0` while `θ_0(p) = p ≠ 0`); the class above admits `A_inf` with its `(p, [p^♭])`-adic topology all the same.
- The topology matters: `Σ_{k≥1} [p^♭]^k T^{−k}` lies in `A_inf[[T, T^{−1}⟩` for the `(p, [p^♭])`-adic topology of `A_inf`, but not in the completion for the `p`-adic topology, in which `[p^♭]^k` does not tend to `0`.

*Needs:* AdicSpacesPartIII F0; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.6.

### 2.2 The pattern of a triangulated curve

Let `Y` be a quasi-compact curve with a fine triangulation `S` and semistable model `Y_S` (TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §§2.11 and 2.16). Construct its pattern: the marked bipartite graph `Γ = (I, I₂, µ)` with `I = S ⊔ A_c`, built from the dual graph of the special fibre with an edge of length `0+` for every missing boundary point (CDN-CURVES §0.2.1, §3.5.2); the shorts `Y_s` (tubes of the smooth loci of the components, `s ∈ S`); the legs `Y_a` (tubes of the nodes, of length `µ(a) = v_p(α)` for a local equation `xy = α`, `a ∈ A_c`); the ghost circles `Y_{s,a}`, `Y_{a,s}` given by the rank-two valuations at the nodes; and the gluing isomorphisms `ι_{a,s}: Y_{s,a} ≅ Y_{a,s}`, from the ghost circle of the short to that of the leg (§2.3). With `I₂ ⊃ I_{2,c}` as in §0.1, write `Y_i` for the short or leg indexed by `i ∈ I` and, for `(i, j) ∈ I_{2,c}`, `Y_{i,j}` for the ghost circle along which `Y_i` and `Y_j` are glued (`Y_{a,s}`, identified with `Y_{s,a}` by `ι_{a,s}`). Write `∂Y ⊂ S` for the vertices whose component is not proper, `S_int = S ∖ ∂Y`, `Γ_int` the subgraph obtained by deleting `∂Y` and the edges meeting it, and `Σ(Y) ⊂ S` the set of nodes of the skeleton.

- Refining `S` subdivides `Γ` without changing `H¹(Γ, −)`, `H¹_c(Γ, −)` and `H¹_c(Γ, −)^*` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.14(i)).
- Lengths lie in `ℚ_{>0}` because `v_p(C^×) = ℚ`.

**Checks.**

- The closed unit disc with `S` the Gauss points of `|T| ≤ 1` and `|T| ≤ |p|` (two components meeting in one point, so `S` is fine): vertices `s₁` (outer) and `s₂`, one relatively compact edge of length `1`, one leg of length `0+` at `s₁`; `∂Y = {s₁}` and `Γ_int = {s₂}` without edges; `H¹(Γ) = 0` and `H¹_c(Γ)^* = 0` (`∂^*` at `s₂` kills the edge coordinate, then `∂^*` at `s₁` kills the leg coordinate), the disc having no residue classes. Refining further does not change these groups. The Gauss point alone is not a fine triangulation: it gives one component.
- The closed annulus `|p^µ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1` with `S` its two boundary Gauss points: two vertices joined by one edge of length `µ`, each with one leg; `H¹(Γ) = 0` and `H¹_c(Γ)^*` has rank one.
- A Tate curve `𝔾_m/q^ℤ` with `S` three points of its skeleton: a circle subdivided into three edges of positive rational lengths summing to `v_p(q)`; each two of the three components meet in exactly one point, so `S` is fine, `H¹(Γ)` has rank one, and `N_µ` is multiplication by the total length `v_p(q)` (§0.2).
- Negative control: `S` two points of the skeleton of the Tate curve gives two components meeting in two points, a loop with two vertices, which is not fine; it must be refined before the reconstruction of §2.3 applies.

*Needs:* §0.1; §2.1; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.11; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.13; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.16.

### 2.3 Adoc geometry and the reconstruction of curves from patterns

Define, for a reduced Tate `O_C`-algebra `A` with reduced special fibre, the adoc spectrum `Spado(A)`: the topological space of `Spf A` enlarged by the points and opens attached to rank-two valuations at closed points of the special fibre, with the structure sheaf extended accordingly (CDN-CURVES §3.1, Appendix A.4). Shorts, legs and ghost circles have adoc spectra, and a curve pattern over a base `R` as in §2.1 — a finite marked bipartite graph with lengths `m: A_c → m_R` and no loop with one or two vertices, `R`-legs `R[[T_{a,s₁}, T_{a,s₂}]]/(T_{a,s₁}T_{a,s₂} − m(a))`, `R`-shorts base-changed from `O_C̆`, and isomorphisms of `R`-ghost circles (CDN-CURVES §§3.5.1 and 3.6.1) — glues to an adoc `R`-curve `Y^ado`. Prove that `Y^ado` is the adoc scheme of a formal `R`-curve of relative dimension one (CDN-CURVES Théorème 3.17), and, for `R = O_C` and lengths `µ(a) ∈ ℚ_{>0}`, that every curve pattern is the pattern of a unique pair `(Y, S)` of a quasi-compact curve and a triangulation (CDN-CURVES Théorème 3.19 = Théorème 0.9). Prove the adoc de Rham comparison in degree one: let `C_dR(Y_S)` be the Čech complex `[∏_{i∈I} Ω^•(Y_i) → ∏_{(i,j)∈I_{2,c}} Ω^•(Y_{i,j})]`; compute `H¹_dR(Y)` by Čech–de Rham cocycles for the covering of `Y` by the tubes `U_a`, `a ∈ A_c`, each the union of the leg `Y_a` and the two shorts at its ends. After choosing an edge `a(s) ∈ A(s)` at every vertex `s`, restricting a cocycle to the pieces of the pattern (the form on `U_a` to `Y_a`, the form on `U_{a(s)}` to `Y_s`, and the transition function between `U_a` and `U_{a(s)}` to the ghost circle `Y_{a,s}`) induces an isomorphism `H¹_dR(Y) ≅ H¹(C_dR(Y_S))` that does not depend on the choices `a(s)` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 3.20, lines 1900–1929). Degree one is what Layers 3–4 use; in degree zero both sides are the locally constant functions, `C` for connected `Y`, by the sheaf property, and no comparison in higher degrees is asserted here.

- `Spado` is functorial for continuous morphisms of reduced Tate `O_C`-algebras with reduced special fibre, and the spectra of a short, a leg and a ghost circle are the adoc spaces of CDN-CURVES §3.1; the ghost circles of a leg `Y_a` are open in `Spado(O(Y_a))`.
- A gluing isomorphism `ι_{a,s}: Y_{s,a} → Y_{a,s}` of `R`-ghost circles has source the ghost circle of the short `Y_s` and target that of the leg `Y_a`; it is given by a continuous `R`-algebra isomorphism `O(Y_{a,s}) → O(Y_{s,a})`, determined by the image `u` of `T_{a,s}`, a unit of `O(Y_{s,a})` with `Res(du/u) = ±1` (CDN-CURVES Lemme 4.2), the sign recording the orientation.
- `Y^ado` is functorial for isomorphisms of patterns and compatible with base change along `R → R′`; for `R = O_C` the pattern of §2.2 of `(Y, S)` returns `Y_S`.
- The degree-one comparison above is natural for refinements of `S` and compatible with the residues of §2.1 on the legs.

**Checks.**

- The pattern of §2.2 of the closed annulus, re-glued, returns the annulus with its two Gauss points.
- Composing a gluing isomorphism `ι_{a,s}` with the coordinate rescaling `T_{a,s} ↦ αT_{a,s}` of the ghost circle `Y_{a,s}`, `α ∈ O_C^×`, produces the curve obtained by replacing `Y_a` by `T₁T₂ = αp^µ` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.10(i)); patterns with different gluings can give non-isomorphic curves, so the gluing data are not redundant. Multiplying the algebra map itself by `α ≠ 1` is not a gluing: it sends `1` to `α`.
- On the closed annulus `Y = {|p^µ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1}`, the cocycle with `dT/T` on each of the two shorts and on the leg and `0` on the ghost circles corresponds to the class of `dT/T` in `H¹_dR(Y)`, which has residue `1` and spans the separated quotient `H¹_dR(Y)^sep = C·dT/T` (§3.1); an exact form `dg` with `g ∈ O(Y)` corresponds to a coboundary.
- Negative control: the comparison is between unseparated groups, and neither side is `C·dT/T`. On the same annulus the form `ω = Σ_{n≥1} p^n T^{p^n − 1} dT` converges (its coefficients `p^n` tend to `0`) and has residue `0`; a primitive `Σ_k a_k T^k` would need `a_{p^n} = 1` for every `n`, which does not converge on `|T| = 1`, so `[ω] ≠ 0` in `H¹_dR(Y)`, whereas a multiple of `[dT/T]` with residue `0` is `0`. The partial sums `Σ_{n≤m} p^n T^{p^n − 1} dT = d(Σ_{n≤m} T^{p^n})` are exact and converge to `ω`, so `[ω]` lies in the closure of `0` and vanishes in `H¹_dR(Y)^sep`. Thus `[ω] ≠ 0` before separation, `[ω] = 0` after it, and `[dT/T]` has residue `1`; reading the comparison as an isomorphism onto `C·dT/T` would contradict §3.1 and CDN-CURVES line 108.
- Over `R = O_C[[T_a]]` the family has constant special fibre and its fibre at `T_a = 0` is a nodal curve with the same dual graph (CDN-CURVES §3.6.3); the reconstruction is not asserted for singular fibres.

*Needs:* §2.1; §2.2; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §§2.16–2.17; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7 (proper GAGA for the compactification step).

### 2.4 Symbols and the Kummer regulator

For a quasi-compact curve `Y` and a prime `ℓ`, define `Symb_ℓ(Y)` as the group of sequences `(f_n)_{n∈ℕ}` of nonzero meromorphic functions with `Div(f_n) ∈ ℓ^n Div(Y)` and `f_{n+1}/f_n ∈ (C(Y)^×)^{ℓ^n}`, modulo the sequences `(h_n^{ℓ^n})` (CDN-CURVES §0.3.1, line 476; §5.1.3). Equivalently, writing `A_{ℓ,n}(Y) = {f ∈ C(Y)^× : Div(f) ∈ ℓ^n Div(Y)}`, `Symb_ℓ(Y) = lim_n A_{ℓ,n}(Y)/(C(Y)^×)^{ℓ^n}`.

- *Kummer regulator.* For `f ∈ A_{ℓ,n}(Y)` the `ℓ^n`-th roots of `f` form a `μ_{ℓ^n}`-torsor over `Y`, étale because `Div(f) ∈ ℓ^n Div(Y)`; its class `c_n(f) ∈ H¹_ét(Y, ℤ/ℓ^n(1))` induces an isomorphism `A_{ℓ,n}(Y)/(C(Y)^×)^{ℓ^n} ≅ H¹_ét(Y, ℤ/ℓ^n(1))`, compatible with the Kummer map on `O(Y)^×/(O(Y)^×)^{ℓ^n}` and with `f ↦ [ℓ^{−n}Div(f)] ∈ Pic(Y)[ℓ^n]` (CDN-CURVES Lemme 5.4, lines 2461–2477). Pushing the torsor of `ℓ^{n+1}`-th roots along `x ↦ x^ℓ` gives that of `ℓ^n`-th roots, so the Kummer regulator `Symb_ℓ(Y) → H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_ℓ(1)) = lim_n H¹_ét(Y, ℤ/ℓ^n(1))`, `(f_n) ↦ (c_n(f_n))_n`, is well defined (CDN-CURVES lines 2485–2488).
- *Symbols of the pieces.* For `Z` a leg or a ghost circle put `O(Z^gen) = O(Z)[1/p]` and `Symb_ℓ(Z^gen) = ℤ_ℓ ⊗̂ O(Z^gen)^× = lim_n O(Z^gen)^×/(O(Z^gen)^×)^{ℓ^n}` (CDN-CURVES §4.1.2, lines 2023–2024 and 2033–2035). For a leg `Z` let `C(Z^gen)` be the field of fractions of `O(Z^gen)`, a domain because it embeds in the ring of analytic functions on the connected open annulus `Z^gen`; for a ghost circle `Z` let `C(Z^gen)` be the total ring of fractions of `O(Z^gen)`. For a leg, `A_{ℓ,n}(Z^gen)` is the group of `f ∈ C(Z^gen)^×` whose divisor on the open annulus `Z^gen` has finite support and is divisible by `ℓ^n` (CDN-CURVES §4.1.2, lines 2036–2038), and the natural map `Symb_ℓ(Z^gen) → lim_n A_{ℓ,n}(Z^gen)/(C(Z^gen)^×)^{ℓ^n}` is an isomorphism, a divisor of finite support on a leg being principal (CDN-CURVES Lemme 4.4, lines 2041–2050). For a short `Y_s` the generic fibre `Y_s^gen` is a connected affinoid curve, `C(Y_s^gen)` is its field of meromorphic functions, and `A_{ℓ,n}(Y_s^gen)` and `Symb_ℓ(Y_s^gen)` are the groups defined above. Restriction gives maps `Symb_ℓ(Y) → Symb_ℓ(Y_i^gen) → Symb_ℓ(Y_{i,j}^gen)` for `i ∈ I`, `(i, j) ∈ I_{2,c}` (§2.2; CDN-CURVES §6.2.1, lines 3117–3120). To a leg `Y_a` the restriction is `(f_n) ↦ (f_n|_{Y_a})`, which lies in `lim_n A_{ℓ,n}(Y_a^gen)/(C(Y_a^gen)^×)^{ℓ^n}` because `f_n` has finitely many zeros and poles on the quasi-compact `Y`, followed by the inverse of the isomorphism of Lemme 4.4; to a short it is `(f_n) ↦ (f_n|_{Y_s})`; from a short or a leg to one of its ghost circles it is restriction to `O(Y_{i,j}^gen)`, in which an element of `O(Y_a^gen)^×`, and a nonzero meromorphic function on a short, become units (scaled to have nonzero reduction on the irreducible special fibre of the short, a function `h ∈ O(Y_s)` has on the ghost circle an expansion `Σ_j a_j T^j` with a least index `k` such that `a_k ∈ O_C^×`; then `ũ = Σ_{j≥k} a_j T^j` is `T^k` times a unit of `O_C[[T]]`, the `a_j` with `j < k` lie in `m_C` and tend to `0`, hence lie in `p^ε O_C` for one `ε > 0`, and `h = ũ(1 + p^ε z)` with `z ∈ O_C[[T, T^{−1}⟩` is a unit).
- *Localized symbols.* Let `A_{ℓ,∞}(Y_S)` be the group of families `(u_{i,n}, v_{i,n})_{i∈I, n∈ℕ}`, `(g_{i,j,n})_{(i,j)∈I_{2,c}, n∈ℕ}`, with `u_{i,n} ∈ A_{ℓ,n}(Y_i^gen)` (for a leg, in the sense above), `v_{i,n} ∈ C(Y_i^gen)^×` and `g_{i,j,n} ∈ C(Y_{i,j}^gen)^×`, satisfying `u_{i,n+1} = u_{i,n}v_{i,n}^{ℓ^n}`, `u_{i,n} = g_{i,j,n}^{ℓ^n}u_{j,n}` and `g_{i,j,n+1}^ℓ = g_{i,j,n}v_{i,n}/v_{j,n}`, and define `Symb_ℓ(Y_S)` as its quotient by the trivial families `(a_{i,n}^{ℓ^n}, a_{i,n+1}^ℓ/a_{i,n})_{i,n}`, `(a_{i,n}/a_{j,n})_{(i,j),n}` (CDN-CURVES §6.2.1, lines 3122–3129). The third relation is a quotient, not a product: the first two give `g_{i,j,n+1}^{ℓ^{n+1}}u_{j,n}v_{j,n}^{ℓ^n} = u_{i,n+1} = g_{i,j,n}^{ℓ^n}u_{j,n}v_{i,n}^{ℓ^n}`, that is `(g_{i,j,n+1}^ℓ)^{ℓ^n} = (g_{i,j,n}v_{i,n}/v_{j,n})^{ℓ^n}`, and the third relation is the identity before the `ℓ^n`-th power; the trivial families satisfy all three, `(a_{i,n+1}/a_{j,n+1})^ℓ = (a_{i,n}/a_{j,n})(a_{i,n+1}^ℓ/a_{i,n})(a_{j,n}/a_{j,n+1}^ℓ)`.

Prove: (a) the Kummer regulator is an isomorphism for every prime `ℓ`, including `ℓ = p` (CDN-CURVES Corollaire 5.5); (b) `Symb_ℓ(Y_S) ≅ Symb_ℓ(Y)` (CDN-CURVES Lemme 6.1); (c) there is an exact sequence `0 → H¹(Γ, ℤ_ℓ(1)) → Symb_ℓ(Y_S) → ker(∏_{i∈I} Symb_ℓ(Y_i^gen) → ∏_{(i,j)∈I_{2,c}} Symb_ℓ(Y_{i,j}^gen)) → 0` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.2).

**Checks.**

- For the closed unit disc and `ℓ ≠ p`, `Symb_ℓ = 0`; for `ℓ = p` it contains `ℤ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**}`, nonzero.
- For the leg `Y_a` with `O(Y_a) = O_C[[T₁, T₂]]/(T₁T₂ − p^µ)` and `ℓ ≠ p`, `Symb_ℓ(Y_a^gen) ≅ ℤ_ℓ` through the residue, generated by `T₁`, whose residue is `1` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 4.5, lines 2051–2053, with Lemme 4.2 for the residue of a unit): a unit is `cT₁^k u₊u₋` with `c ∈ C^×`, `u₊ ∈ 1 + T₁O_C[[T₁]]`, `u₋ ∈ 1 + T₂O_C[[T₂]]` (CDN-CURVES Corollaire 4.3), and `C^×`, `1 + T₁O_C[[T₁]]`, `1 + T₂O_C[[T₂]]` are `ℓ`-divisible, so they vanish in `ℤ_ℓ ⊗̂`. Without the completion `O(Y_a^gen)^×` would keep these factors; for `ℓ = p` the factors `1 + T_iO_C[[T_i]]` are not `p`-divisible and survive.
- The constant sequence `(c)` with `c ∈ C^×` is trivial: `C^×` is `ℓ`-divisible.
- Restriction to a leg is not restriction of units: on a Tate curve `E = 𝔾_m/q^ℤ` with `S` three points of its skeleton (§2.2), let `f ∈ A_{ℓ,n}(E)` have `Div(f) = ℓ^n(P − Q)` with `P ≠ Q`, `P − Q ∈ E[ℓ^n]` (so that the divisor is principal) and `P` in the leg `Y_a`, of coordinate `T₁(P) = α`, `|p^µ| < |α| < 1`. Then `f|_{Y_a}` vanishes at `P`, so it is not in `O(Y_a^gen)^×`, but its divisor has finite support divisible by `ℓ^n`, so it lies in `A_{ℓ,n}(Y_a^gen)`. With `h ∈ C(Y_a^gen)^×` of divisor `ℓ^{−n}Div(f|_{Y_a})`, the product of the factors `(T₁ − β)^{±1}` over the points of that divisor (`β` the coordinate of the point, the sign its multiplicity), `f h^{−ℓ^n}` is a unit of `O(Y_a^gen)`, and its class in `O(Y_a^gen)^×/(O(Y_a^gen)^×)^{ℓ^n}` is the image of `f` at level `n`; it is independent of the choice of `h`, two choices differing by a unit. A function on the leg with infinitely many zeros, such as a bounded function whose Newton polygon has infinitely many slopes, is not in `A_{ℓ,n}(Y_a^gen)`; the gluing in the proof of CDN-CURVES Lemme 6.1 (lines 3143–3152) needs this, the glued `u_n` being a meromorphic function on `Y`.
- Gluing data carry the graph: for a Tate curve with `S` three points of its skeleton (§2.2), the families with all `u_{i,n} = v_{i,n} = 1` force `g_{i,j,n} ∈ μ_{ℓ^n}` with `g_{i,j,n+1}^ℓ = g_{i,j,n}`, an element of `ℤ_ℓ(1)` on each edge of `Γ^{(2)}`, and modulo the trivial families with `a_{i,n} ∈ μ_{ℓ^n}` they form `coker(ℤ_ℓ(1)^I → ℤ_ℓ(1)^{I_{2,c}}) = H¹(Γ, ℤ_ℓ(1)) = ℤ_ℓ(1)`, the first term of (c), invisible on every piece; a definition without the functions `g_{i,j,n}` would lose it.

*Needs:* §§2.1–2.2; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4 (Kummer sequence); JacobianChallenge Layer D (Picard groups of the compactification).

### 2.5 ℓ-adic filtration and Picard–Lefschetz

Prove that for `ℓ ≠ p`, `H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_ℓ(1))` has a natural three-step filtration with graded pieces `H¹(Γ, ℤ_ℓ(1))`, `∏_{s∈Σ(Y)} H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℤ_ℓ(1))` and `H¹_c(Γ, ℤ_ℓ)^*`, the last map being the collection of residues (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.3 = Théorème 0.13(i)); that `H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℤ_ℓ(1)) ≅ T_ℓ J(Y_s^sp)` and `H¹_ét(Y_s, ℤ_ℓ(1))_0 ≅ H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℤ_ℓ(1))` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 5.10); that the choice of `r ↦ p^r` gives a section `s_{r↦p^r}` of the projection `H¹_ét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1)) → H¹_ét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))/H¹(Γ, ℚ_ℓ(1))`, and that changing it to `r ↦ ζ(r)p^r`, for `ζ` a homomorphism from `ℚ/ℤ` to the roots of unity, gives `s_{r↦ζ(r)p^r} = s_{r↦p^r} + t_ζ N` with `t_ζ = (ζ(ℓ^{−n}))_n ∈ ℤ_ℓ(1)` and `N` induced by `N_µ`, so the difference lands in `H¹(Γ, ℚ_ℓ(1))` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 6.4, the Picard–Lefschetz formula); and that when `Y_S` is defined over `O_K`, an element `σ` of inertia acts by `c ↦ c + t_σ N(c)` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 6.5).

**Checks.**

- For a Tate curve, the graded pieces are `ℤ_ℓ(1)`, `0`, `ℤ_ℓ`, and `N` is multiplication by `v_p(q)` from the top piece to the bottom one.
- For a good-reduction proper curve of genus `g`, only the middle piece survives, of rank `2g`.
- Components `s ∈ S ∖ Σ(Y)` have `Y_s^sp = ℙ¹` and contribute nothing, so the filtration does not depend on `S`.

*Needs:* §§0.1–0.2, 2.4; JacobianChallenge Layer D (Tate modules of Jacobians).

## Layer 3: de Rham, Hyodo–Kato and syntomic cohomology of quasi-compact curves

### 3.1 de Rham filtrations

Prove that `H¹_dR(Y_S)` has, up to `p^{N₁(S)}`, a filtration with pieces `H¹(Γ, O_C)`, `∏_{s∈S} H¹_dR(Y_s^♦)_0` and `H¹_c(Γ, O_C)^*`, where `Y_s^♦` is the partial compactification of `Y_s` along its compact edges and the subscript `0` imposes vanishing residues on its legs (CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.8); and that `H¹_dR(Y)^sep` is finite-dimensional with filtration `H¹(Γ_int, C)`, `∏_{s∈Σ(Y)} C ⊗ H¹_dR(Y_s^♦)^sep_0`, `H¹_c(Γ, C)^*` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.14, Lemme 6.15). Here `N₁(S)` is the least `N` with `p^N µ(a) ≥ 1` for all `a`.

**Checks.**

- For an affinoid, `H¹_dR(Y)` is infinite-dimensional and not Hausdorff, while `H¹_dR(Y)^sep` is finite-dimensional (CDN-CURVES lines 142–144).
- For the closed disc, `H¹_dR(Y)^sep = 0`.
- For the closed annulus, `H¹_dR(Y)^sep = C·dT/T`.

*Needs:* §0.1; §2.3.

### 3.2 Hyodo–Kato cohomology of a quasi-compact curve

Define the `A_cris`-thickening `Ỹ_S` of the pattern: `O(Ỹ_s) = A_cris ⊗̂_{O_C̆} O(Y̆_s)` with a Frobenius lift, `O(Ỹ_a) = A_cris[[T₁, T₂]]/(T₁T₂ − p̃_{µ(a)})` with `φ(T_i) = T_i^p`, and their ghost circles, and the Čech complexes `C_dR(Ỹ_S)` and `C_dR(Y̆_S) = O_C̆ ⊗_{θ₀} C_dR(Ỹ_S)` (CDN-CURVES §0.3.3–0.3.4, §6.3). Define `H^i_HK(Y) = C̆ ⊗_{O_C̆} H^i_dR(Y̆_S) = C̆ ⊗_{A_cris} H^i_dR(Ỹ_S)`, with its semilinear `φ` and the monodromy `N` induced by `N_µ` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 6.22(iii)), and `H¹_HK(Y)^sep` its maximal Hausdorff quotient.

Prove: (a) `H^i_HK(Y)` does not depend on `S` and is functorial; (b) `B_cris^+ ⊗_{C̆} H¹_HK(Y)^sep ≅ H¹_dR(Ỹ)^sep`, `φ`-equivariantly, with `H¹_dR(Ỹ)^sep` free of finite rank over `B_cris^+`, and `ι_HK: C ⊗_{C̆} H¹_HK(Y)^sep ≅ H¹_dR(Y)^sep` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.21, Lemme 6.19, Corollaire 6.20); (c) `N ∘ N = 0` and `Nφ = pφN`; (d) `H¹_HK(Y)^sep` has a `φ`-stable filtration with pieces `H¹(Γ_int, C̆)`, `(∏_{s∈Σ(Y)∖∂Y} H¹_rig(Y_s^sp)) ⊕ (∏_{s∈∂Y} H¹_rig(Y_s^sp)^{[1]})` and `H¹_c(Γ, C̆)^*(−1)`, where `^{[1]}` is the slope-one part (CDN-CURVES Théorème 0.13(ii), Remarque 6.22(ii), Proposition 5.20 via Cartier theory).

**Checks.**

- Closed unit disc: `H¹_HK(Y)^sep = 0` (CDN-CURVES line 160).
- Closed annulus: `H¹_HK(Y)^sep = C̆·dlog T` with `φ = p` and `N = 0`; it is one-dimensional.
- A proper curve with good reduction of genus `g`: `H¹_HK(Y) = H¹_rig(Y^sp)`, of dimension `2g`, with `N = 0`; a Tate curve gives a two-dimensional space with `N ≠ 0` of rank one.
- Dropping `^{[1]}` on boundary components would give the wrong dimension for an affinoid with good reduction of positive genus: its `H¹_HK^sep` is the slope-one part of `H¹_rig` of the proper component plus residues.

*Needs:* §§2.1–2.3, 3.1; PadicPeriodRings §4.1 with `gap:period-rings-general-C`; PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology §§RD.4.10, RD.4.18, RD.4.38; CrystallineCohomology §CR.5.1 (Cartier); CrystallineCohomology §CR.4.7 (*Slope decomposition on the existing isocrystal carrier*).

### 3.3 Syntomic cohomology and the syntomic regulator

Define `Syn(Y_S, 1)` as the mapping fibre of the map of complexes `1 − φ/p: F¹C_dR(Ỹ_S) → C_dR(Ỹ_S)`, with divided-power ghost-circle rings (CDN-CURVES §0.3.3; §6.4.1, lines 3591–3607): `Syn(Y_S, 1) = Cone(1 − φ/p)[−1]`, for the cochain mapping cone `Cone(f: A → B)^n = A^{n+1} ⊕ B^n` with `d(a, b) = (−da, f(a) + db)` and the shift `(K[m])^n = K^{n+m}` with differential `(−1)^m d` (Mathlib `CochainComplex.mappingCone` and `CochainComplex.shiftFunctor`). Thus `Syn^n(Y_S, 1) = F¹C^n_dR(Ỹ_S) ⊕ C^{n−1}_dR(Ỹ_S)` with `d(x, y) = (dx, −(1 − φ/p)x − dy)`: it vanishes in negative degrees, its degree-`0` term is `∏_i F¹O(Ỹ_i)`, and its degree-`1` term is `∏_i Ω¹(Ỹ_i) ⊕ ∏_{(i,j)} F¹O(Ỹ_{i,j})^PD`, from `F¹C¹_dR`, plus `∏_i O(Ỹ_i)`, from `C⁰_dR`. Define the syntomic regulator `δ_p̃: Symb_p(Y) → H¹_syn(Y_S, 1)` sending `(f_n)` to the class of the degree-one cocycle `lim (df̃_{n,i}/f̃_{n,i}, (1/p) log(φ(f̃_{n,i})/f̃_{n,i}^p), log(f̃_{n,i}/f̃_{n,j}))`, whose three components lie in `Ω¹(Ỹ_i)`, `O(Ỹ_i)` and `F¹O(Ỹ_{i,j})^PD` (CDN-CURVES lines 529–532 and 3608–3611). Here `f̃_{n,i}` lifts the restriction `f_n|_{Y_i}` to `Ỹ_i`; on a leg or a short this restriction may have zeros and poles, so need not be a unit (§2.4, the leg Check), but their multiplicities are divisible by `p^n`, and the lifts are chosen so that the limit is holomorphic (CDN-CURVES lines 532–535). Prove that `δ_p̃` is an isomorphism if `p > 2`, and is injective with cokernel killed by `2` if `p = 2` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.24, from Proposition 4.11 for legs and ghost circles and Théorème 5.34 for shorts; footnote 32, line 2195); hence `H¹_syn(Y_S, 1) ≅ Symb_p(Y) ≅ H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_p(1))` for `p > 2` (CDN-CURVES Corollaire 6.25). Prove that the sequence `0 → O(Y_S)/O_C → H¹_syn(Y_S, 1) --α--> H¹_dR(Ỹ_S)^{φ=p} → H¹(Y_S, O)` is `p²`-exact, its homology at each term being killed by `p²` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.26; `p^k`-exact as in §3.4). Let `A_st = A_cris[Log p̃]^PD` be the divided-power polynomial algebra over `A_cris` in a variable `Log p̃`, with `φ(Log p̃) = p·Log p̃` and `N = d/dLog p̃`, so `N(Log p̃) = 1`, the sign opposite to `N(u) = −1` on `B_st^+` (Conventions), and let `ι_p̃(x) = 1 ⊗ x − Log p̃ ⊗ Nx`, an isomorphism `H¹_dR(Ỹ_S) ≅ (A_st ⊗̂_{A_cris} H¹_dR(Ỹ_S))^{N=0}` commuting with `φ`; with this sign `N(ι_p̃(x)) = 1 ⊗ Nx − 1 ⊗ Nx − Log p̃ ⊗ N²x = 0` (CDN-CURVES §6.4.4, lines 3748–3762). Prove that the map `ι_p̃ ∘ α ∘ δ_p̃: Symb_p(Y) → (A_st ⊗̂_{A_cris} H¹_dR(Ỹ_S))^{N=0, φ=p}` does not depend on `r ↦ p^r` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.29 and its proof, lines 3766–3778). For `p > 2`, through the isomorphisms `δ_p̃` and `ι_p̃`, this gives the `p²`-exact sequence `0 → O(Y_S)/O_C → Symb_p(Y) → (A_st ⊗̂_{A_cris} H¹_dR(Ỹ_S))^{N=0, φ=p} → H¹(Y_S, O)`. CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.29 prints this sequence ending in `→ 0`, which fails: for a proper curve of positive genus its last map is nonzero (the elliptic-curve Check below).

- `Syn(Y_S, 1)` is functorial for morphisms of triangulated quasi-compact curves and, up to quasi-isomorphism, does not depend on the Frobenius lifts chosen on the shorts or on refinements of `S` (CDN-CURVES §6.4.1).
- `δ_p̃` is a homomorphism from the multiplicative group of symbols to the additive group `H¹_syn(Y_S, 1)`, natural in `Y`, killing the trivial symbols `(h_n^{p^n})`; the limit defining it does not depend on the lifts `f̃_{n,i}`.
- The `Ω¹`-component of `δ_p̃((f_n))` is `lim df̃_{n,i}/f̃_{n,i}`, so the composite with the projection to `Ω¹` and `θ` is the map `dlog` of §3.5.

**Checks.**

- `H⁰_syn(Y_S, 1) = (F¹A_cris)^{φ=p}` for connected `Y`, which is `ℤ_p t` for `p` odd (CDN-CURVES Proposition 6.26, proof); for `p = 2` it is strictly larger, since `t^{p−1} ∈ pA_cris` makes `t/2` an element of `F¹A_cris` with `φ(t/2) = 2·(t/2)`, so it is a rank-one `ℤ_2`-lattice of `ℚ_2 t` containing `t/2`. For two disjoint closed discs it is the square of that module, `(ℤ_p t)²` for `p` odd (Layer 2 preamble). With `1 − φ` in place of `1 − φ/p` it would be `(F¹A_cris)^{φ=1} = 0`, since `A_cris^{φ=1} = ℤ_p` meets `F¹A_cris` in `0`.
- Degrees, on the closed disc and `p` odd: `Syn^{−1} = 0`, so `H^{−1}_syn = 0`, and `H⁰_syn = {x ∈ F¹C⁰_dR : dx = 0, φ(x) = px} = ℤ_p t` sits in degree `0`, the regulator in degree `1`. The unshifted cone `Cone(1 − φ/p)`, with degree-`n` term `F¹C^{n+1}_dR ⊕ C^n_dR`, puts `∏_i F¹O(Ỹ_i)` in degree `−1`, has `H^{−1} = ℤ_p t` and would put the regulator classes in `H⁰`.
- Signs, on the closed disc with `φ(T) = T^p` and `f = 1 + pT`: `x = dlog f = p·dT/(1 + pT)` and `y = (1/p) log((1 + pT^p)/(1 + pT)^p)` have `dy = (φ/p)x − x`, so `−(1 − φ/p)x − dy = 0` and `(x, y)` is a cocycle. For the differential `(dx, (1 − φ/p)x − dy)` the same pair gives `2(1 − φ/p)x = 2p·dT/(1 + pT) − 2pT^{p−1}dT/(1 + pT^p)`, whose coefficient at `T = 0` is `2p ≠ 0`. That differential is the one of CDN-CURVES line 2093; it is isomorphic to the one used here through `y ↦ −y`, and with it the second component of the regulator is `(1/p) log(f̃^p/φ(f̃))`, the coboundary of `log ṽ` being `(dṽ/ṽ, (1/p) log(ṽ^p/φ(ṽ)))` (CDN-CURVES line 2109).
- On the closed disc, the constant symbol `(u)` of `u = 1 + pT ∈ O(Y)^{**}` has `Ω¹`-component `du/u = p·dT/(1 + pT) ≠ 0`; the zero map and a regulator forgetting the differential component are excluded.
- Trivial symbols map to zero: a constant `c ∈ C^×` has compatible `p^n`-th roots, so `(c)` is trivial; with the lifts `[c^♭]`, `d[c^♭]/[c^♭] = 0`, `log(φ([c^♭])/[c^♭]^p) = 0` and the ghost-circle terms vanish.
- Additivity and normalization: on the closed annulus `δ_p̃((T)·(T)) = 2δ_p̃((T))`, with differential component `2·dT/T`. A rescaled regulator with differential component `c·df/f` is additive as well, but gives residue `c` on `(T)`, whereas the Kummer class of `T` has residue `1` (§2.4, CDN-CURVES Proposition 4.5, lines 2051–2053, with Lemme 4.2 for the residue of a unit); so `c = 1`.
- Neither sequence ends in `0`. Take a good-reduction elliptic curve `Y`, with `S` the Gauss point and one point of a residue disc (two components, `Γ` one edge without legs, so `N = 0` and `ι_p̃` is `x ↦ 1 ⊗ x`). Then `O(Y_S)/O_C = 0`, `Symb_p(Y) ≅ H¹_ét(Y, ℤ_p(1)) ≅ ℤ_p²` (§2.4(a)), and `H¹_syn(Y_S, 1)`, which contains `δ_p̃(Symb_p(Y))` with quotient killed by `2`, is a finitely generated `ℤ_p`-module of rank `2`. After `⊗ ℚ_p` the third term `H¹_dR(Ỹ_S)^{φ=p}` becomes `(B_cris^+ ⊗_{C̆} H¹_HK(Y))^{φ=p} = X^1_st(H¹_HK(Y))` (§3.2(b), §1.3 with `N = 0`), of Dimension `(1, 2)` for slopes `0, 1` and for slopes `1/2, 1/2` (§4.7). Were the last map zero, `p²` times the third term would lie in the image of the second term, and after `⊗ ℚ_p` the third term would be at most two-dimensional over `ℚ_p`, contradicting its `C`-dimension `1`.

*Needs:* §2.4; §3.2; PadicPeriodRings §4.1; `gap:period-rings-general-C`.

### 3.4 The integral sequence

For `k ≥ 0`, call a sequence `p^k`-exact when its homology at each term is killed by `p^k`. Prove that for an affinoid `Y` there is a `p²`-exact sequence `0 → ℤ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**} → H¹_syn(Y_S, 1) → (H¹_dR(Ỹ_S)^sep)^{φ=p} → H¹(Y_S, O)` with `H¹(Y_S, O)` killed by a power of `p` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.30, Remarque 0.19), and that composed with §3.3 it gives integral versions of the first row of §3.5 with denominators depending only on the lengths of the legs.

**Checks.**

- For the closed disc the sequence reads `0 → ℤ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**} → H¹_syn → 0`, up to `p²`.
- For the closed annulus the third term contains the class of `dlog T`, fixed by `φ/p`, and the second map sends the symbol `(T)` to it.
- Over `ℚ_p` the sequence becomes exact (§3.5); integrally only `p²`-exactness is asserted, with denominators growing as the legs of `Y_S` get shorter (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.19).

*Needs:* §3.3.

### 3.5 The fundamental diagram of a quasi-compact curve

Prove that for a quasi-compact curve `Y` there is a functorial commutative diagram whose first row `0 → ℚ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**} → H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) → X^1_st(H¹_HK(Y)^sep) → H¹(Y, O) → 0` is exact and whose second row `0 → ℚ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**} → Ω¹(Y) → H¹_dR(Y)^sep → H¹(Y, O) → 0` is a complex, the vertical maps being `dlog` and `θ ⊗ ι_HK` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 6.32 with Corollaire 6.25 and §1.1(a)). For an affinoid, `H¹(Y, O) = 0` and this is a diagram of Banach spaces all of whose maps have closed image (CDN-CURVES Théorème 0.1). When `Y` is defined over a closed subfield `K ⊂ C` finite over `ℚ_p`, the diagram is `G_K`-equivariant.

**Checks.**

- Closed unit disc: `H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) ≅ ℚ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^{**}`, and `exp(O(Y))` is dense in it but different: `∏_{n≥1}(1 + p^{1/p^n}T)^{p^n}` converges in `O_C[[T]]` but not in `C⟨T⟩` (CDN-CURVES lines 160–164).
- Closed annulus: the third term is `X^1_st(C̆(−1)) = ℚ_p`, spanned by the image of the Kummer class of `T`.
- `ℓ ≠ p` gives a finite-dimensional `H¹_ét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))` for the same affinoids, whereas `H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1))` contains `ℚ_p ⊗ (O(Y)^{**}/(1 + m_C))`, a `C`-space of infinite dimension (CDN-CURVES Remarque 6.33(ii)).

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.3, 2.4, 3.2–3.4; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.4.

## Layer 4: Overconvergent affinoids, Stein curves, Weil–Deligne actions and Jacobians

### 4.1 Overconvergent affinoids

For an affinoid curve `Y` (connected, as throughout Layers 2–4) write `Y^† = lim_{δ>0} Y_δ` for its overconvergent structure, where `Y_δ ⊃ Y` are the affinoids obtained by shrinking the boundary discs, and set `H¹_proét(Y^†, ℚ_p(1)) = colim_δ H¹_proét(Y_δ, ℚ_p(1))` and `H¹_HK(Y^†) = colim_δ H¹_HK(Y_δ)` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.6(i), §7.1.4). Prove: (a) `H¹_HK(Y^†)` is finite-dimensional over `C̆` with a filtration of pieces `H¹(Γ, C̆)`, `∏_{s∈Σ(Y)} H¹_cris(Y_s^sp)`, `H¹_c(Γ, C̆)^*(−1)`, and `ι_HK: C ⊗ H¹_HK(Y^†) ≅ H¹_dR(Y^†)` (CDN-CURVES Proposition 7.4, Remarque 7.5); (b) there is a functorial diagram with exact rows `0 → O(Y^†)/C --exp--> H¹_proét(Y^†, ℚ_p(1)) → X^1_st(H¹_HK(Y^†)) → 0` and `0 → O(Y^†)/C --d--> Ω¹(Y^†) → H¹_dR(Y^†) → 0`, vertical maps `dlog` and `θ ⊗ ι_HK`, all maps with closed image (CDN-CURVES Théorème 7.6 = Théorème 0.5); (c) for `ℓ ≠ p`, `H¹_proét(Y^†, ℚ_ℓ(1)) ≅ H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 7.7).

**Checks.**

- Overconvergent closed disc: `H¹_HK(Y^†) = 0` and `exp: O(Y^†)/C ≅ H¹_proét(Y^†, ℚ_p(1))`; unlike §3.5, `log` identifies `ℚ_p ⊗ (O(Y^†)^{**}/(1 + m_C))` with `O(Y^†)/C`. The denominator is the group of constant principal units `O(Y^†)^{**} ∩ C^× = 1 + m_C`, not `C^×`: the constant `p` has `|p − 1| = 1`, so it is not in `O(Y^†)^{**}`, while `1 + p²` is, and its logarithm is a constant, zero in `O(Y^†)/C`.
- Overconvergent annulus: `H¹_HK(Y^†) = C̆(−1)` and `X^1_st = ℚ_p`.
- Connectedness: for `Y = D ⊔ D`, two closed discs, the function `f = (0, 1)` on `Y^†` is nonzero modulo the diagonal constants `C`, yet `df = 0` and `exp(f) = 0`, so with one copy of `C` neither row of (b) would be exact on the left. In the componentwise form (Layer 2 preamble) `C` is replaced by `H⁰_dR(Y^†) = C²`, in which `f` is zero, and `d` is injective on `O(Y^†)/C²`; the constant principal units become `(1 + m_C)²`.
- `(B_st^+ ⊗ H¹_HK(Y^†))^{N=0,φ=p}` and `H¹_dR(Y^†)` are Banach spaces while the other nonzero terms are inductive limits of Banach spaces (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.6(iii)).

*Needs:* §§3.2–3.5; AdicSpacesPartIV §§F1.5–F1.9; VectorBundlesAndIsocrystals Layer 5 (*Dimension and the abelian BC category*: closed images through finite Dimension).

### 4.2 Curves without boundary

Let `Y` be a non-proper curve without boundary, written as a strictly increasing union of affinoids `Y_n` or of overconvergent affinoids `Y_n^†`. Set `H¹_HK(Y) = lim_n H¹_HK(Y_n^†)`, a countable projective limit of finite-dimensional `C̆`-spaces with continuous `φ` and `N`. Prove that there is a functorial commutative diagram of Fréchet spaces with exact rows `0 → O(Y)/C --exp--> H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) → X^1_st(H¹_HK(Y)) → 0` and `0 → O(Y)/C --d--> Ω¹(Y) → H¹_dR(Y) → 0`, vertical maps `dlog` and `θ ⊗ ι_HK` with closed images, where `ι_HK: C ⊗̂_{C̆} H¹_HK(Y) ≅ H¹_dR(Y)` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 8.1 = Théorème 0.7, Remarque 8.2(iii)); that the kernel of `H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) → Ω¹(Y)` is `t·H¹_HK(Y)^{φ=1}` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.8(ii)); and that the diagram is `G_K`-equivariant when `Y` is defined over a closed subfield `K ⊂ C` finite over `ℚ_p`.

The proof passes to the limit in §3.5 or §4.1, using `R¹lim O(Y_n) = 0` (AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.4) and closedness of images in the limit. This is the curve case of CstConjectureForDaggerVarieties §1.5 (*the simplified Stein and affinoid diagram*), proved here without the model-free syntomic input of that roadmap and over every `C` with `v_p(C^×) = ℚ`; the two diagrams are compared on their common range in §4.3.

Extend the statement to a disjoint union `Y = ⊔_{i∈I} Y_i` of countably many curves without boundary, the case needed for the towers of Layer 7. Replace `C` by `H⁰_dR(Y) = LC(Y, C) = ∏_{i∈I} C`, the locally constant functions with the product topology, and set `H¹_HK(Y) = ∏_i H¹_HK(Y_i)`. Prove that the diagram of `Y`, with rows `0 → O(Y)/H⁰_dR(Y) --exp--> H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) → X^1_st(H¹_HK(Y)) → 0` and `0 → O(Y)/H⁰_dR(Y) --d--> Ω¹(Y) → H¹_dR(Y) → 0`, is the product of the diagrams of the `Y_i` (countable products of strictly exact sequences of Fréchet spaces are strictly exact, and `X^1_st` commutes with countable products by §1.2), and that it is equivariant for automorphisms and for `G_K` permuting the components. The statements of §§4.3–4.6 extend in the same way, as products over the components; when `Y` is defined over a finite `K/ℚ_p` and its components over finite extensions of `K`, the Weil–Deligne representations of §§4.4–4.6 on the products are induced from the stabilizers of the components, and similarity is preserved by induction.

**Checks.**

- Open unit disc: `H¹_HK(Y) = H¹_dR(Y) = 0` and `exp: O(Y)/C ≅ H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_p(1))`.
- Open annulus: `H¹_HK(Y) = C̆(−1)`, the Kummer class of the coordinate spans the image of `H¹_proét` in `X^1_st = ℚ_p`.
- A countable disjoint union of open annuli, through the product form above, has `H¹_HK = ∏_ℕ C̆(−1)`, and the completed tensor of §1.2 is needed: the ordinary tensor `C ⊗ ∏_ℕ C̆` is strictly smaller than `∏_ℕ C`.
- Disconnected spaces need `H⁰_dR(Y)` in place of `C`: on `Y = Ω ⊔ Ω` the function `f = (0, 1)` is nonzero modulo the diagonal constants `C`, yet `df = 0` and `exp(f) = 0`; it is zero in `O(Y)/H⁰_dR(Y)`, so the quotient by one copy of `C` would make neither row exact on the left. A function that is not locally constant has nonzero image in both.
- A comparison of de Rham spaces does not determine the Hyodo–Kato structure: `D₀ = (C̆, φ = σ, N = 0)` and `D₁ = (C̆, φ = pσ, N = 0)` both become `C` with an identity comparison, but have slopes `0` and `1`, `X^1_st(D₀) = (B_cris^+)^{φ=p}` and `X^1_st(D₁) = ℚ_p`. The comparison with CstConjectureForDaggerVarieties §1.5 in §4.3 therefore goes through the model complexes, compatibly with `φ`, and not through `ι_HK` alone.

*Needs:* §§4.1, 1.2–1.3; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2; LocallyAnalyticDistributions §§0.0, 0.2.

### 4.3 Filtrations for curves without boundary and overconvergent affinoids

Prove that for `Y` an overconvergent affinoid or a curve without boundary with a fine triangulation `S`: for `ℓ ≠ p`, `H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))` has a natural filtration with pieces `H¹(Γ, ℚ_ℓ(1))`, `∏_{s∈S} H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℚ_ℓ(1))`, `H¹_c(Γ, ℚ_ℓ)^*`; and `H¹_HK(Y)` has a `φ`-stable filtration with pieces `H¹(Γ, C̆)`, `∏_{s∈S} H¹_rig(Y_s^sp)`, `H¹_c(Γ, C̆)^*(−1)`, the monodromy being induced by `N_µ` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 0.15 = Théorème 8.9, Remarque 8.2(ii)). The terms do not depend on `S` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.16(i)). When `Y` has a semistable model over the integers `O_K` of a discretely valued subfield `K ⊂ C` of absolute ramification index `e`, the Hyodo–Kato structure of §4.2 agrees with that of LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.CR.6.6 after `C̆ ⊗̂_{K₀}`, compatibly with `φ`, with monodromy through the adapter `N_p = N_raw/e`, and with the Hyodo–Kato maps through the `p`-normalized map `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^{ϖ_K} ∘ exp(−log_K(ϖ_K^e/p)N_p)` of §1.4, not through `ι_HK^{ϖ_K}` itself (CDN20 §0.3, lines 194–198; CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.16(iii); §1.4 for the adapter, with CohomologyComparisons §4.5 for the transport). Here `N_p` is the monodromy of this section, induced by `N_µ` with the lengths `µ(a) = v_p(α)` of §2.2, and `N_raw` is the monodromy of the log rigid model complex, which counts lengths in powers of `ϖ_K`: both structures are computed from the same Čech complex of the pattern of the base-changed model, a node `xy = ϖ_K^h` having length `h/e` for the first and `h` for the second. The log structure of the model is normalized by `ϖ_K`, while the thickened legs of §3.2 use `p̃_{h/e} = [(p^{h/e})^♭]`, normalized by the chosen powers of `p`; they differ by the unit `ϖ_K^e/p`, whence the transport of CohomologyComparisons §4.5 in `ι_HK^{(p)}`. On the common range of CstConjectureForDaggerVarieties §1.5, namely `C` the completion of `K̄` for `K` with countable residue field and `Y` with such a model over a finite extension of `K` in `C`, prove that the diagram of §4.2 agrees with the diagram of that section. Route: the analytic Hyodo–Kato cohomology used there, whose period maps are normalized at `p` (CN-I line 448), agrees with the log rigid Hyodo–Kato cohomology of the model, compatibly with `φ`, `N_p` and the `p`-normalized map `ι_HK^{(p)}` (CohomologyComparisons §4.9a); composed with the identification above this matches the right-hand terms `X^1_st` and the right vertical maps; on each overconvergent affinoid of an exhaustion both middle vertical maps send the Kummer class of a symbol (§2.4) to its `dlog`, the normalization `p^{−1}` of that section being chosen for this, and these classes exhaust `H¹` (§2.4(a)), so the middle maps agree on the limit.

**Checks.**

- Drinfeld half-plane: `Γ` is the Bruhat–Tits tree, all components are `ℙ¹`, so `H¹_HK(Ω) = H¹_c(Γ, C̆)^*(−1)` has slope one, and `H¹_proét(Ω, ℚ_ℓ(1)) = H¹_c(Γ, ℚ_ℓ)^* = Sp_1(ℚ_ℓ)^*` by §0.1.
- For a curve without boundary the edge lengths of non-compact edges are infinite and `H¹_c(Γ)^*` is the full residue space; for an affinoid, `Γ_int` replaces `Γ` in §3.2(d), and the two statements differ exactly by the boundary vertices.
- A Mumford curve of genus `g`: pieces `C̆^g`, `0`, `C̆(−1)^g`.
- Ramified witness for the monodromy adapter: over `K` with `e = 2` and uniformizer `π`, the Tate curve with parameter `q = π²` has a semistable model whose special fibre is a cycle of two projective lines meeting in two nodes `xy = π`. On `H¹_HK = C̆e₀ ⊕ C̆e₁` the model monodromy is `N_raw e₁ = 2e₀`, while the graph has total length `v_p(q) = 1` and `N_p e₁ = e₀ = N_raw e₁/e`; identifying `N_raw` with `N_p` would double the monodromy. For `e = 1` the adapter is the identity.
- Unramified witness for the unit factor: over `K = ℚ_3` with the uniformizer `ϖ_K = 12` of a model, `e = 1` and `N_p = N_raw`, yet the Hyodo–Kato map of §4.2 corresponds to `ι_HK^{(p)} = ι_HK^{12} ∘ exp(−log_3(4)N)`, which on a module with `Ne₁ = e₀` differs from `ι_HK^{12}` on `e₁` by `−log_3(4)` times the image of `e₀`, a scalar of valuation `1` times a vector that is nonzero because the Hyodo–Kato map of the model is an isomorphism (the criterion of §1.4 for injective maps); matching the right vertical maps through the scaling alone would leave this term out (§1.4, the unit-factor Check).

### 4.4 The special fibre of a curve without boundary and its Weil–Deligne group

For a curve without boundary `Y`, define `Y^sp` as the stable marked curve obtained from the special fibre of the semistable model of any triangulation by contracting every contractible `ℙ¹` (CDN-CURVES §8.2.1–8.2.3), with the conventions of a point or a double point when the node set `Σ(Y)` is empty. Prove that a non-constant morphism `f: X → Y` induces a unique `f^sp: X^sp → Y^sp` compatible with the semistable models of triangulations `S ⊃ f^{−1}(T)` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 8.4), so that `Aut_C(Y)` acts on `Y^sp` (CDN-CURVES Corollaire 8.5); and that an automorphism acting trivially on `Y^sp` acts trivially on `H¹_dR(Y)` when `Σ(Y) ≠ ∅` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 8.7). For `Y` defined over a finite `K/ℚ_p` with `C = ℂ_p`, define `W_Y` as the subgroup of `Aut_K(Y) × φ^ℤ` generated by the elements of `Aut_K(Y) × φ^ℕ` acting trivially on `k_C`, with `deg: W_Y → ℤ` and `W_Y = W_K × Aut_C(Y)^{G_K}`, and `WD_Y = 𝔾_a ⋊ W_Y` as in the Conventions (CDN-CURVES §8.2.5, Remarque 8.6). A representation of `WD_Y` is a representation of `W_Y` with `N` satisfying `Ng = p^{deg g} gN`; it is smooth when every vector is fixed by an open subgroup of inertia.

**Checks.**

- The Drinfeld half-plane: `Y^sp` is the tree of `ℙ¹`'s with all `q + 1` rational points marked, acted on by `PGL₂(K)`.
- The open unit disc has `Σ = ∅` and `Y^sp` a point; a Tate curve has a double point, on which multiplication by `−1` must act non-trivially for Théorème 8.7 to persist (CDN-CURVES Remarque 8.8).
- Within `WD_K ⊂ WD_Y`, a geometric Frobenius `Φ` and `N` satisfy `NΦ = qΦN`; using arithmetic Frobenius would give `NΦ = q^{−1}ΦN`, contradicting the relation `N g = p^{deg g} g N` with `deg Φ = f`. On the Tate-curve module `ℚ_ℓ(1)e₀ ⊕ ℚ_ℓe₁` of `H¹_ét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))` the geometric Frobenius is `diag(q^{−1}, 1)` and `Ne₁ = µe₀`: `NΦe₁ = µe₀ = qΦNe₁`, and conjugation by `Φ` multiplies the additive parameter by `q^{−1} = p^{−deg Φ}`, as the group law of the Conventions says.

*Needs:* §2.2; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.16; TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.17.

### 4.5 ℓ-adic Weil–Deligne representations of curves

For a continuous `ℚ_ℓ`-representation `V` of `Aut_K(Y)`, `ℓ ≠ p`, define `WD(V) = colim_{[L:K]<∞} (ℚ_ℓ[u] ⊗ V)^{I_L}`, where `Aut_K(Y)` acts on `ℚ_ℓ[u]` through `G_K` by `g(u) = χ_ℓ(g)^{−1}(u + c_ℓ(g))` with `χ_ℓ` cyclotomic and `c_ℓ` the Kummer cocycle of `p`, `φ` acts trivially and `N = d/du`; extend to projective limits termwise (CDN-CURVES §8.3.1). Prove `V = (ℚ_ℓ[u] ⊗̂ WD(V))^{N=0}` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 8.10) and `WD(H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))) = H¹_proét(Y^sp, ℚ_ℓ(1))` as `WD_Y`-representations, the latter being `H¹(Γ, ℚ_ℓ)(1) ⊕ ∏_{s∈Σ(Y)} H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℚ_ℓ(1)) ⊕ H¹_c(Γ, ℚ_ℓ)^*` with `N` from `N_µ` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 8.11).

**Checks.**

- For a Tate curve over `K`, `WD(H¹(ℚ_ℓ(1)))` is two-dimensional with `N` of rank one; for a good-reduction curve `N = 0`.
- `N ∘ g = χ_ℓ(g)^{−1} g ∘ N` on `ℚ_ℓ[u]`, the twist that matches `p^{deg g}` on `W_Y`.
- For `V` unramified the colimit stabilizes at `L = K` and `WD(V) = V` with `N = 0`.

### 4.6 Similarity of ℓ-adic and Hyodo–Kato cohomology

Prove that for a curve without boundary `Y` defined over a finite `K/ℚ_p`, with `C = ℂ_p`, the `WD_Y`-representations `H¹_proét(Y^sp, ℚ_ℓ(1))` (`ℓ ≠ p`) and `H¹_HK(Y)(1)` are similar (CDN-CURVES Théorème 8.12, lines 4542–4543); together with §4.5, the `WD_K`-representations `WD(H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1)))` and `H¹_HK(Y)(1)` are similar, compatibly with `Aut_K(Y)`. For an overconvergent affinoid `Y^†` defined over `K` (§4.1), for which `Y^sp` and `WD_Y` are not defined (§4.4), prove the `WD_K`-statement: `WD(V)`, formed by the recipe of §4.5 with `G_K` in place of `Aut_K(Y)` from the `G_K`-representation `V = H¹_proét(Y^†, ℚ_ℓ(1)) = H¹_proét(Y, ℚ_ℓ(1))` (§4.1(c)), and `H¹_HK(Y^†)(1)`, on which `W_K` acts `C̆`-linearly through the pairs `(g, φ^n) ∈ G_K × φ^ℤ` acting trivially on `k_C` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 8.6(i), lines 4418–4423), `G_K` acting by transport of structure, and `N` is induced by the monodromy of §3.2 on the colimit of §4.1, are similar (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.16(ii), lines 412–415, where it is stated without proof). The proof, in both cases, splits both sides by weights, identifies the graph pieces from a common `ℚ`-structure with the same monodromy, and on a component `Y_s^sp` uses that `H¹_ét(Y_s^sp, ℚ_ℓ)` and `H¹_cris(Y_s^sp)` are similar representations of `End(J_s)` for the Jacobian `J_s` (CDN-CURVES proof of Théorème 8.12, lines 4548–4561); for `Y^†` it runs on the finite filtrations of §§4.1(a) and 4.3, with the inertia action of §2.5 (CDN-CURVES Remarque 6.5) on the ℓ-adic side, and needs no passage to the limit.

**Checks.**

- A good-reduction elliptic curve over a finite field `𝔽_q`: both twisted sides are two-dimensional with `N = 0`, and the geometric Frobenius has characteristic polynomial `X² − (a/q)X + 1/q` on both, where `X² − aX + q` is the polynomial on the untwisted `H¹_ét(−, ℚ_ℓ)` and on `H¹_cris` (for the linearized `φ^f`): the twist `(1)` divides the geometric Frobenius by `q`. For `y² + y = x³` over `𝔽₂`, `#E(𝔽₂) = 3`, so `a = 0`; the untwisted polynomial is `X² + 2` and the twisted one `X² + 1/2`, the same on both sides, and comparing a twisted side with an untwisted one would wrongly fail.
- The Drinfeld half-plane: both sides are the graph piece `H¹_c(Γ)^*`, with `N = 0`; on `H¹_HK(Ω) = H¹_c(Γ, C̆)^*(−1)` the Frobenius has slope one, and after the twist `(1)` it acts through the permutation action on `Γ`, as the geometric Frobenius does on `H¹_c(Γ, ℚ_ℓ)^*`.
- Similarity is not equality: the two sides live over `ℚ_ℓ` and `C̆`, and only become isomorphic over a common field.
- The overconvergent closed annulus `{|ϖ_K| ≤ |T| ≤ 1}^†` over `K`, with `T` defined over `K`: `Y^sp` is not defined for it, and only the `WD_K`-statement applies. `V = ℚ_ℓ`, spanned by the Kummer class of `T`, which `G_K` fixes because `T` is defined over `K`, so `WD(V) = ℚ_ℓ` with `N = 0` and every element of `W_K` acting by `1`. `H¹_HK(Y^†) = C̆·dT/T ≅ C̆(−1)` with `φ = pσ` (§4.1), so `H¹_HK(Y^†)(1)` has `φ = σ` on `dT/T`, which `G_K` fixes; a pair `(g, φ^n)` acting trivially on `k_C` acts by `1`, and `N = 0`: the two sides are similar. Without the twist `(1)` the geometric Frobenius would act by `q` on the Hyodo–Kato side against `1` on the ℓ-adic side. The overconvergent closed disc has both sides `0`.

### 4.8 The universal cover of the p-divisible group of a Jacobian

Let `X` be a smooth proper connected curve over `C` with Jacobian `J` (JacobianChallenge Layer D). Define `Ĵ` as the `ℚ_p`-space of sequences `(x_n)_{n∈ℤ}` in `J(C)` with `x_n = p·x_{n+1}` and `x_n → 0` as `n → −∞` (CDN-CURVES §7.1.5, lines 4017–4021, printed p. 96). Prove the exact sequence `0 → V_p(J) → Ĵ → C ⊗ Lie(J) → 0` given by `log_J` (CDN-CURVES §7.1.5, lines 4021–4028, printed p. 96); for `Y ⊂ X` the complement of an open disc, the exact sequence `0 → ℚ_p ⊗̂ O(Y)^× → H¹_ét(Y, ℚ_p(1)) → Ĵ → 0` (CDN-CURVES line 436, Remarque 7.8); and the natural isomorphism `ι_st: Ĵ ≅ X^1_st(H¹_HK(X))` obtained by comparing it with §4.1 (CDN-CURVES Théorème 7.9 = Théorème 0.17), `G_K`-equivariant when `X` is defined over `K`.

- `Ĵ` is functorial in `X`, contains `V_p(J)` as the sequences with `x_n = 0` for `n ≪ 0`, and carries a continuous `G_K`-action when `X` is defined over `K`.

Prove that if `X` has good reduction, so that `J` extends to an abelian scheme `𝒥` over `O_C` with `p`-divisible group `G = 𝒥[p^∞]`, then `Ĵ` is identified with the universal cover `G̃(O_C)`, compatibly with `V_p(J) = V_p(G)` and with `log_J`, and reduction modulo `p` identifies both with `G̃(O_C/p)`. The universal cover of FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory §R07.2.25 is a sheaf on `Nilp_{O_C}`, the `O_C`-algebras on which `p` is nilpotent, and `O_C` is not one of them (`p^m ≠ 0` in `O_C` for every `m`); define its value on the `p`-adically complete ring `O_C` as `G̃(O_C) = lim_m G̃(O_C/p^m)`. Each `O_C/p^m → O_C/p` has nilpotent kernel (its `m`-th power is `0`) and `p` is nilpotent in `O_C/p^m`, so §R07.2.26 makes the transition maps isomorphisms and `G̃(O_C) = G̃(O_C/p)`; since limits commute, `G̃(O_C) = lim_{×p} G(O_C)` with `G(O_C) = lim_m G(O_C/p^m)`, which contains the formal group `𝒥^∧(m_C)`. Route: a sequence of `Ĵ` lies in the formal group `𝒥^∧(m_C)` for `n ≪ 0`, where its terms are images of one another under iterates of `[p]`, so every reduction modulo `p^k` is `p`-power torsion; conversely a sequence `(y_n)` of `lim_{×p} G(O_C)` reduces to a chain `(ȳ_n)` in the torsion group `G(k_C)` with `p·ȳ_{n+1} = ȳ_n`, which vanishes for `n ≪ 0` because `ȳ_0` has finite order, and then `y_n = [p^m](y_{n+m})` tends to `0` in the formal group. The identification of `ι_st` with the Dieudonné-module description of `G̃(O_C)` is stated in §4.9; CDN-CURVES Remarque 7.10(i) records that description and says only that Théorème 7.9 could be deduced from it in the good-reduction case.

**Checks.**

- `g = 0`: `Ĵ = 0`.
- For a Tate curve `E = 𝔾_m/q^ℤ`, `V_p(E)` is an extension of `ℚ_p` by `ℚ_p(1)` and `Ĵ/V_p(E) ≅ C`.
- Sequences with `x_n = p·x_{n+1}` but `x_n` not tending to `0` would include lifts along non-torsion points of `J(C)`, and the quotient by `V_p(J)` would no longer be `C ⊗ Lie(J)`.
- Good ordinary reduction with `g = 1`: `G ⊗ k_C ≅ μ_{p^∞} × ℚ_p/ℤ_p`, so `G̃(O_C)` has Dimension `(1, 1) + (0, 1) = (1, 2)` by the Dieudonné-module description of §4.9, the Dimension of `Ĵ` read off `0 → V_p(J) → Ĵ → C → 0`; for supersingular reduction `D(G_s)` has slope `1/2` twice and the Dimension is again `(1, 2)`, as in §4.7.
- The Tate curve `E = 𝔾_m/q^ℤ`, `0 < |q| < 1`, has `|j(E)| = |q|^{−1} > 1`, so `E` itself extends to no abelian scheme over `O_C` and the good-reduction statement above does not apply to it. Its generic `p`-divisible group `E[p^∞] ≅ (ℚ_p/ℤ_p)²` (étale, `C` being algebraically closed) does extend over `O_C`, in non-isomorphic ways none of which comes from `E`: as the constant étale group, whose universal cover is `ℚ_p²`, of Dimension `(0, 2)`; and as `E₀[p^∞]` for an elliptic curve `E₀` with good reduction, for instance `E₀: y² = x³ − x` at `p = 5` (discriminant `64`, a unit), an abelian scheme whose generic `p`-divisible group is again `(ℚ_5/ℤ_5)²` and whose universal cover has Dimension `(1, 2)` (the good-reduction Check above). So extending the abstract generic group neither extends `E` nor determines a universal cover, and §4.8 identifies `Ĵ` with `G̃(O_C)` only for the `p`-divisible group of the abelian scheme extending `J` itself. The `p`-divisible group of the torus uniformizing `E` is `μ_{p^∞}`, of height one against height two for `E[p^∞]`, and its universal cover `(B_cris^+)^{φ=p}` has Dimension `(1, 1)`, smaller than `Ĵ` of Dimension `(1, 2)`.
- `O_C` lies outside the domain `Nilp_{O_C}` of §R07.2.26, since `(p)^m ≠ 0`; in `O_C/p²` and `O_C/p³` the kernel of the reduction to `O_C/p` has square, respectively cube, zero, so the finite-level values of `G̃` agree and so does their limit.

### 4.9 Integration and the Jacobian period map

Let `X` be defined over a finite `K/ℚ_p` and `C = ℂ_p`. Let `J̃` be the universal vector extension of `J` (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §A4.2). Define `ι_BdR: Ĵ → J̃(B_dR^+)` by sending `(x_n)` to `lim_{n→+∞} p^n·x̂_n` for a bounded sequence of lifts `x̂_n ∈ J̃(B_dR^+)` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.18), and `log_{J̃}: J̃(B_dR^+) → B_dR^+ ⊗_K H¹_dR(J)^*`. Prove that `log_{J̃} ∘ ι_BdR` factors through `(B_st^+ ⊗ H¹_HK(X)^*)^{N=0, φ=1}`, that this space equals `X^1_st(H¹_HK(X))` under the identification `H¹_dR(X)^* ≅ H¹_dR(X)` given by cup product into `H²_dR(X) = K`, and that `log_{J̃} ∘ ι_BdR = ι_st` (CDN-CURVES Théorème 7.12, Remarque 7.13).

Prove that if moreover `X` has good reduction, with `𝒥`, `G` and `Ĵ = G̃(O_C)` as in §4.8 and `G_s = G ⊗ k_C`, then `ι_st` corresponds to the period isomorphism of FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory §R07.2.28, under the identification `H¹_HK(X) = C̆ ⊗ H¹_cris(X_s/W(k_C))[1/p]` with `N = 0`. Three rational realizations of `G_s` enter and are kept apart: `D_contra = D(G_s)[1/p]`, the contravariant crystalline Dieudonné module of AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §A4.CR.7.2, equal to `H¹_cris(𝒥_s)[1/p] = H¹_cris(X_s)[1/p]` (for `μ_{p^∞}`, `F = pσ`, slope `1`); `D_raw = M_cov(G_s)[1/p] = D_contra^∨(−1)`, the covariant module of §R07.2.20 with its transposed Frobenius `F` (for `μ_{p^∞}`, `F = σ`, slope `0`); and `D_norm = D_raw(1) = D_contra^∨`, the same module with `φ = p^{−1}F` (slope `−1` for `μ_{p^∞}`). The period isomorphism of §R07.2.28 reads `G̃(O_C) ≅ (B_cris^+ ⊗ D_norm)^{φ=1} = (B_cris^+ ⊗ D_raw)^{F=p}`. On the other side `ι_st` lands in `(B_st^+ ⊗ H¹_HK(X)^*)^{N=0, φ=1} = (B_cris^+ ⊗ D_norm)^{φ=1}`, since `N = 0` and `H¹_HK(X)^* = D_contra^∨ = D_norm`, and the cup-product identification `H¹_HK(X) ≅ H¹_HK(X)^*(−1)` of Théorème 7.12(ii) turns this space into `X^1_st(H¹_HK(X))` (CDN-CURVES Remarque 7.10(ii)). Prove the logarithm comparison: the two maps `G̃(O_C) → (B_cris^+ ⊗ D_norm)^{φ=1}` coincide. Route: the Lie algebra of the universal vector extension is the dual of the contravariant realization (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §§A4.2 and A4.CR.7.2), so the logarithmic Lie realization of `G` is `D_norm`, and its condition `φ = 1` is the condition `F = p` on `D_raw`; the universal vector extension `E(G)` is the pullback of `J̃` along `G ⊂ 𝒥` and is a crystal, its Lie algebra giving the Hodge filtration of the Dieudonné crystal (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli §A4.CR.7.2, *Grothendieck–Messing deformation theory*); the isomorphism of §R07.2.28 is `log_{E(G)}` applied to lifts `x̂_n ∈ E(G)(A_cris)`, the value on the PD thickening `A_cris → O_C/p`, while `ι_st = log_{J̃} ∘ ι_BdR`; the images of the `x̂_n` along `A_cris ⊂ B_cris^+ ⊂ B_dR^+` form a bounded sequence of lifts in `J̃(B_dR^+)`, and `ι_BdR` does not depend on the choice of bounded lifts (second Check), so the two limits coincide. No claim is made that two independently chosen lifts are equal. The factorisation through the `N = 0` part is Théorème 7.12(i). CDN-CURVES Remarque 7.10(i) suggests this deduction without carrying it out.

**Checks.**

- For `x ∈ V_p(J)` the limit stabilizes and `ι_BdR(x)` is the Fontaine period of `x`; `log_{J̃}` lands in `Fil⁰`.
- `ι_BdR` does not depend on the choice of bounded lifts: two choices differ by a bounded sequence, killed by `p^n` in the limit.
- `log_{J̃} ∘ ι_BdR` is injective, by non-degeneracy of the period pairing (CDN-CURVES §7.2).
- Normalisation witness for the good-reduction comparison: for `μ_{p^∞}`, `D_raw` has `F = σ` (slope `0`), so `(B_cris^+ ⊗ D_raw)^{F=p} = (B_cris^+)^{φ=p}`, an extension of `C` by `ℚ_p t = V_p(μ_{p^∞})`; `D_norm` has `φ = p^{−1}σ` and gives the same space as `(B_cris^+ ⊗ D_norm)^{φ=1}`. Two wrong readings fail: the contravariant `D_contra` (slope `1`) with `F = p` gives `(B_cris^+)^{φ=1} = ℚ_p`, and the raw `F` used as the Lie Frobenius with the condition `φ = 1` gives `(B_cris^+ ⊗ D_raw)^{F=1} = ℚ_p` as well; both leave no room for the Lie algebra.
- Good ordinary reduction with `g = 1`: `H¹_HK(X)` has slopes `0, 1`, and so do `D_contra` and `D_raw` (the `μ_{p^∞}` and `ℚ_p/ℤ_p` parts exchange their slopes), so Dimensions alone do not detect the variance; the cup-product identification is what fixes it, and it is pinned by the `μ_{p^∞}` witness.

### 5.1 Stein semistable weak formal schemes

Define a semistable weak formal scheme over `O_K` as a weak formal scheme Zariski-locally étale over `Spwf(O_K[X₁, …, X_n]^†/(X₁⋯X_r − ϖ))`, `1 ≤ r ≤ n`, with the log structure of its special fibre, and call it Stein semistable when its special fibre `Y` has proper smooth irreducible components and there are closed subschemes `Y_s` (finite unions of components) and opens `U_s` with `Y_s ⊂ U_s ⊂ Y_{s+1}`, `∪ Y_s = Y`, whose tubes `]U_s[` form a Stein covering of the dagger generic fibre `X_K` (CDN-STEIN §3.1.1, lines 682–691; the same with formal schemes). The generic fibre is a smooth dagger Stein space, equivalently a smooth rigid Stein space (AdicSpacesPartIV §F1.8).

- The class is stable under base change along finite unramified extensions and under passage to the formal completion; the covering `U_s` can be refined by any cofinal choice satisfying the three conditions.
- Every Stein semistable weak formal scheme is semistable in the sense of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.6 after completion.

**Checks.**

- The standard model of the Drinfeld half-plane (HilbertModularVarietiesAndShimuraCurves §R18.5.3), with `Y_s` the components of the tree ball of radius `s`, is Stein semistable.
- The rigid affine line `𝔸¹_K` is the generic fibre of a Stein semistable weak formal scheme whose special fibre is an infinite chain of projective lines `C_0, C_1, …`, `C_m` the reduction at the Gauss point of `|T| ≤ |ϖ|^{−m}` and `C_m ∩ C_{m+1}` one node with local equation `X₁X₂ = ϖ`, `X₁ = ϖ^{m+1}T`, `X₂ = ϖ^{−m}T^{−1}`; take `Y_s = C_0 ∪ ⋯ ∪ C_s`. Each `C_m` is proper. The naive exhaustion by the dagger discs `|T| ≤ |ϖ|^{−m}` with models `Spwf O_K[ϖ^m T]^†` is not such a model: their special fibres `𝔸¹_k` are not proper, and the transition `ϖ^{m+1}T = ϖ·(ϖ^m T)` reduces to the zero map, not to an open immersion. For `d ≥ 2` the product of such chains is not semistable (a product of two nodes `X₁X₂ = ϖ = X₃X₄` is not étale-locally of the form `X₁⋯X_r = ϖ`), no Stein semistable model of `𝔸^d_K` is constructed in this roadmap, and its cohomology is taken from the model-free diagram of CstConjectureForDaggerVarieties §1.5.
- A proper semistable formal scheme is not Stein semistable unless its generic fibre is a finite set of points: its tubes cannot form a Stein exhaustion of a positive-dimensional proper space.

### 5.2 Overconvergent Hyodo–Kato cohomology

For a Stein semistable `X`, define `RΓ_HK(X₀) = Rlim_s RΓ_rig(U_s/O_F^0)`, the derived limit of the log rigid Hyodo–Kato complexes of the `U_s` over the log point `O_F^0 = (Spf W(k), 1 ↦ 0)` (CrystallineCohomology §CR.6.5; CDN-STEIN §3.1.2), with its Frobenius, its monodromy `N_raw` (normalized by `ϖ`, as in CDN-STEIN) and Fréchet topology, the `p`-normalized monodromy `N_p = N_raw/e` used whenever `RΓ_HK(X₀)` is compared with the `p`-based period spaces of §1.3 or with Layer 4 (§4.3), and the Hyodo–Kato map `ι_HK = ι_HK^ϖ: RΓ_HK(X₀) ⊗̂_F K ≅ RΓ_dR(X_K)` obtained from the finite-piece comparisons of LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.CR.6.6 (*Nonproper finite-piece Hyodo–Kato comparison*, which is stated in every dimension) by passage to the limit (GK05 Theorem 3.4 and Corollary 3.7; CDN-STEIN Proposition 3.12). **Checks.**

- The chain model of `𝔸¹_K` (§5.1): `H⁰_HK = F` and `H¹_HK = 0`, since the dual graph is a tree, the components are projective lines, and each `U_s` has a single boundary leg, which carries no residue class (§0.1); this agrees with `H¹_dR(𝔸¹_K) = 0` through `ι_HK` (CDN-STEIN Example 1.9 states the vanishing for `𝔸^d_K`).
- In dimension one, `H¹_HK(X₀)` is the Stein-curve Hyodo–Kato cohomology of LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.CR.6.6.
- Changing `ϖ` to `ϖw` with `N_raw ≠ 0` changes `ι_HK` on the two-dimensional Tate-curve module `φe₀ = e₀`, `φe₁ = pe₁`, `N_raw e₁ = e₀` by precomposition with `e₁ ↦ e₁ + log_K(w)e₀`, `e₀ ↦ e₀`, and the evaluation map `ρ` by `e₁ ↦ e₁ − log_K(w)e₀`. The sign is pinned by periods: for the Kummer representation of `ϖ` (basis `v₀`, `v₁`, `g v₀ = χ(g)v₀`, `g v₁ = v₁ + c(g)v₀` with `c` the Kummer cocycle of `ϖ`), `x₀ = t^{−1}v₀` and `x₁ = v₁ − t^{−1}u v₀`, `u` the variable of the `ϖ`-presentation, are Galois invariant in `B_st ⊗ V` with `Nx₁ = x₀`; in the `ϖw`-presentation `u` is `u_{ϖw} − ℓ_w` (§1.4), so the embedding attached to `ϖw` sends `u` to `log([ϖ^♭]/ϖ) + log([w^♭]/w) − ℓ_w = log([ϖ^♭]/ϖ) − log_K(w)`, so the de Rham image of `x₁` gains `+log_K(w)` times that of `x₀`. Reading the formula of CohomologyComparisons §4.5 as a formula for `ι_HK` would flip the sign and make `ι_HK ⊗ ι_ϖ` depend on `ϖ` (§5.4). With `N_raw = 0` the map is independent of `ϖ`.

### 5.3 Crystalline syntomic cohomology and the Fontaine–Messing period maps

Define the crystalline geometric syntomic complex `RΓ_syn(X_{O_C}, ℤ_p(r)) = [RΓ_cr(X_{O_C})^{φ=p^r} → RΓ_cr(X_{O_C})/F^r]` (mapping fibre; absolute crystalline cohomology over `W(k)`) on quasi-compact pieces and glue; define `RΓ_syn(X_{O_C}, ℚ_p(r))` by rationalizing on quasi-compact pieces and then gluing (CDN-STEIN lines 196–199 in the introduction, with §3.3.1, line 1360, for the definition). Prove that the Fontaine–Messing period maps `α_FM: RΓ_syn(X_{O_C}, ℚ_p(r)) → RΓ_proét(X_C, ℚ_p(r))` and `α_FM: RΓ_syn(X_{O_C}, ℤ_p(r))_{ℚ_p} → RΓ_ét(X_C, ℚ_p(r))` are strict quasi-isomorphisms after truncation `τ≤r` (CDN-STEIN Proposition 1.11 = Corollary 3.46 and Proposition 6.1). Route: let `U` be a quasi-compact semistable piece over `O_K`. Over each finite extension `K′ ⊂ C` of `K`, `U_{O_{K′}}` is a base change of a semistable model, admitted by the charts of CohomologyComparisonsPartII §4.4, and the integral period map into `Ẑ_p(r)′ = (p^a a!)^{−1}Ẑ_p(r)`, `r = (p−1)a + b`, has kernel and cokernel killed by `p^{A(e(K′),p,r)}` in degrees `≤ r`, with one exponent for all charts and all `n`, but growing with the ramification index `e(K′)`. Both sides over `O_C` are filtered colimits over `K′` of the corresponding objects over `O_{K′}` (crystalline cohomology of `U_{O_C}/p^n` and the nearby cycles commute with `O_C/p^n = colim_{K′} O_{K′}/p^n`), so the colimit needs a bound uniform in `K′`: for `K′` with enough roots in the sense of CohomologyComparisonsPartII §4.4 the exponent is `Nr + c_p`, with `N` universal and `c_p` depending only on `p` and, for `p = 2`, on the dimension of `U`, and such `K′` are cofinal (adjoin `p`-power roots of unity), so over `O_C` the period map is a `p^{Nr+c_p}`-quasi-isomorphism in degrees `≤ r`, uniformly in the piece. This is the uniform constant `N(r)` of CDN-STEIN line 2201; both exponents come from CohomologyComparisonsPartII §§4.1, 4.4–4.5, transferred to formal and weak formal schemes by `gap:formal-syntomic-period-comparison`, on the formal nearby cycles of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.6. The first comparison needs only some exponent on each piece, since `p` is inverted before gluing; the second glues integral complexes before inverting `p` and uses the uniformity over the pieces. Gluing over the Stein covering uses the Milnor sequence and strictness of zigzags of Banach complexes; on quasi-compact pieces étale and pro-étale cohomology agree (§1.1).

**Checks.**

- `r = 0`: `RΓ_syn(ℤ_p(0))` is `[RΓ_cr^{φ=1} → RΓ_cr/F⁰ = 0]`, and the comparison in degree `0` is `H⁰ = ℤ_p^{π₀}` on both sides.
- `p = 2`, `r = 2`: the lattice is `(1/8)ℤ_2(2)`, not `(1/4)ℤ_2(2)` (CohomologyComparisonsPartII §4.1, its factorial Check: `L_EN(r) = (p^a a!)^{−1}ℤ_p(r)` with `a = ⌊r/(p−1)⌋ = 2`, `p^a a! = 8`); the rational statement is insensitive to this.
- No statement is made above degree `r`: for the open disc and `r = 0`, `H¹_proét(ℚ_p) ≠ 0` while `τ≤0` sees only `H⁰`.
- Quantifiers: at fixed `K′`, `p` and `r`, the exponent `A(e(K′),p,r)` serves all charts and all `n`, but it is not uniform in `K′`, and a bound that grows with `e(K′)` gives no bound on the colimit over `O_C`; the enough-roots exponent `Nr + c_p` is the one that survives. At `p = 2`, `r = 1` the enough-roots bound keeps a dependence on the dimension through `c_2`.

### 5.4 Bloch–Kato syntomic cohomology

For a Stein semistable weak formal scheme `X` over `O_K` (§5.1) define the overconvergent Bloch–Kato syntomic complex `RΓ^BK_syn(X_C, ℚ_p(r))` as the mapping fibre of `ι_HK ⊗ ι_ϖ: [RΓ_HK(X₀) ⊗̂_F B_st^+]^{N=0, φ=p^r} → (RΓ_dR(X_K) ⊗̂_K B_dR^+)/F^r`, in the derived category of locally convex `ℚ_p`-spaces of LocallyAnalyticDistributions §0.0 (CDN-STEIN §1.3.1, line 206, which writes `RΓ^BK_syn(X_C, ℚ_p(r))`, and §3.2.2, line 964, which writes `RΓ_syn(X_C, ℚ_p(r))`: the two names denote this one object). ### 6.1 The standard semistable model

### 6.2 Schneider–Stuhler and Iovita–Spiess

### 6.8 `A_inf`-cohomology of the half-space

Let `𝔛 = X ⊗_{O_K} O_C`. Prove that for `i ≥ 0` there is a topological `φ^{−1}`-equivariant isomorphism of `G × G_K`-modules `H^i_ét(𝔛, AΩ_𝔛{i}) ≅ A_inf ⊗̂_{ℤ_p} Sp_i(ℤ_p)^*` (CDN-DUKE Theorem 1.3). ## Layer 7: The Drinfeld tower in dimension one

In this layer `F` is a finite extension of `ℚ_p` with uniformizer `ϖ`, residue cardinality `q`, `G = GL₂(F)`, `Ǧ = D^×`, `G′ = G/ϖ^ℤ`, `Ǧ_0 = O_D^×` and `Ǧ_n = 1 + ϖ_D^n O_D` for `n ≥ 1`, `ϖ_D` a uniformizer of `D`. The levels are indexed by powers of `ϖ_D` (CDN23 lines 181–197): `M_n` (`n ≥ 0`) is the Galois cover of `M_0` over `F̆` with group `Ǧ_0/Ǧ_n`. EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.4 indexes its Drinfeld levels `M_Dr,m` by powers of `ϖ`, with deck group `O_D^×/(1 + ϖ^m O_D) = Ǧ_0/Ǧ_{2m}` in rank two; the dictionary is `M_0 = M_Dr,0`, `M_{2m} = M_Dr,m` and, for odd `n = 2m − 1`, `M_n = M_Dr,m/(Ǧ_n/Ǧ_{2m})`, the quotient by a finite normal subgroup (each `Ǧ_k` is normal in `Ǧ`), to which the actions of `G × Ǧ × W_F`, the level maps and the period map descend. `M_n^ϖ` is the quotient of `M_n` by `ϖ^ℤ ⊂ G` (§12.14), descended to `F` with its `G′`-action (§12.19, which uses the indexing of CDN23), `M_∞` is the projective system of the `M_n`, and `H^•(M_∞) = colim_n H^•(M_n)`. `L` is a finite extension of `ℚ_p` with integers `O_L` and residue field `k_L`. For a `ℚ_p[Ǧ]`-module `Z` and an irreducible `L`-representation `ρ` of `Ǧ` write `Z[ρ] = Hom_{L[Ǧ]}(ρ, L ⊗_{ℚ_p} Z)`; for a rank-two `L-(φ, N, G_F)`-module `M` write `Z[M] = Z[JL(M)]` with `JL(M)`, `LL(M)` and `WD(M)` as in PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp §6.9, GL2AutomorphicRepresentationsAndTransfer R16.3 and PadicHodgeTheory §5.3, `M̆ = ℚ̆_p ⊗_{ℚ_p^nr} M` and `M_dR = (ℚ̄_p ⊗_{ℚ_p^nr} M)^{G_F}` (CDN20 §0.3). `M` is supercuspidal when `WD(M)` is absolutely irreducible and `φ` has slope `1/2` (CDN23 line 328, with absolute irreducibility as in PadicHodgeTheory §5.4; coefficients are extended to a finite extension of `L` when needed, which preserves the condition): absolute irreducibility gives `End_{(φ,N,G_F)}(M) = L` by Schur's lemma, and irreducibility alone forces `N = 0` and a single slope of `φ` (CDN20 lines 182–184), and the slope `1/2` is the hypothesis of every statement of CDN20 §2.1 (lines 644, 654, 702), under which the central character of `JL(M)` is unitary (CDN20 footnote 10, line 664). `M` has level at most `n` when `JL(M)` is trivial on `Ǧ_n` (CDN23 lines 181–182 and 330); since `Ǧ_n` acts trivially on `M_n`, only modules of level at most `n` contribute to the cohomology of `M_n`. Witness for the dictionary: for `q = 2`, `M_Dr,1` has deck group `O_D^×/(1 + ϖO_D)` of order `(q² − 1)q² = 12` over `M_0`, while `M_1` has deck group `Ǧ_0/Ǧ_1 = 𝔽_{q²}^×` of order `3`, the quotient of the first by `Ǧ_1/Ǧ_2` of order `q² = 4`; reading `M_1` as `M_Dr,1` would let level-two modules contribute at level one. Negative control for absolute irreducibility: for `F = L = ℚ_p`, let `M` have a basis `e₁, e₂` fixed by `G_F`, `N = 0`, `φ(e₁) = e₂` and `φ(e₂) = pe₁`. Then `φ² = p`, so both slopes are `1/2`, and `WD(M)` is unramified with Frobenius of characteristic polynomial `X² − p` (or `X² − p^{−1}` for the inverse Frobenius), irreducible over `ℚ_p`; but `End(M) = ℚ_p[φ] ≅ ℚ_p(√p)` and over `L(√p)` it splits into two unramified characters, so `M` is not supercuspidal.

### 7.1 Connected components of the Lubin–Tate and Drinfeld towers

This section is stated for every height `h ≥ 1`, the rank-two case `h = 2` being the one used below; `h` is the height, distinct from the Drinfeld level index `n` of the preamble. Let `D_h` be the central division algebra over `F` of invariant `1/h` (`D_2 = D`, `D_1 = F`) and `M_{LT,m}` the Lubin–Tate space of `GL_h(F)` with full level `m ≥ 1` (EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.3, whose `n` is `h` here). Prove that the height-zero part `M_{LT,m}^{(0)}` over `F̆` maps to `Sp(F̆_m)`, `F̆_m` the Lubin–Tate extension of level `m`, with geometrically connected fibres, so that `π₀(M_{LT,m}^{(0)} ×_{F̆} Sp(C))` is identified with `(O_F/ϖ^m)^×`, and that this bijection is `GL_h(O_F) × O_{D_h}^× × G_{F̆}`-equivariant when `(g, b, τ)` acts by `det(g) Nrd(b)^{−1} χ_LT(τ)^{−1}` modulo `1 + ϖ^m O_F` (STR08 Theorems 4.3 and 4.4). Deduce that `π₀` of the infinite-level Lubin–Tate and Drinfeld towers of `GL_h(F)` over `C` are principal homogeneous spaces under `F^×`, identified so that `g ∈ GL_h(F)` translates by `det(g)`, on which `(g, b, w) ∈ GL_h(F) × D_h^× × W_F` (`G × Ǧ × W_F` for `h = 2`) translates by `det(g)·Nrd(b)^{−1}·rec_F(w)`, where `rec_F: W_F^{ab} ≅ F^×` is the reciprocity isomorphism sending an arithmetic Frobenius to a uniformizer, so that `rec_F(τ) = χ_LT(τ)^{−1}` on `G_{F̆}` (STR08 Theorem 4.4, lines 381–390). This is the dictionary for the action conventions of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §§12.2 and 12.7, in which the diagonal `F^×` acts trivially and `(g, b, w)` preserves height zero exactly when `v(det g) − v(Nrd b) = v(w)`, `v` taking the value `1` on a geometric Frobenius; the Drinfeld tower is reached through the equivariant infinite-level duality of §12.11. Each factor therefore acts through the character `ν_H = det`, `Nrd`, `rec_F` of CDN20 §0.1 (lines 101–107), which names these characters without their orientation; the translation character of `Ǧ` is `Nrd^{−1}`, not `Nrd`. Consequently the action of `Ǧ` on `π₀(M_{n,C})` factors through `Nrd` for every `n`, and `H⁰(M_{n,C}, −)[ρ] = 0` for every irreducible `ρ` of dimension at least two.

**Checks.**

- `m = 1`, `h` arbitrary: `(q − 1)` geometric components of `M_{LT,1}^{(0)}`, and a scalar `b ∈ O_F^× ⊂ O_{D_h}^×` acts by `b^{−h}`; with `Nrd(b) = b^h` in place of `Nrd(b)^{−1}` the action of the centre on `π₀` would be inverted.
- `h = 1`: the tower is the Lubin–Tate tower of height one, `D_1 = F`, and `π₀` is the whole generic fibre `Sp(F̆_m)`.
- Central test at infinite level: `h = 2`, `F = ℚ_3`, `(g, b) = (3I₂, 3)`. Then `det(g) = 9` and `Nrd(3) = 9`, so the translation is `9·9^{−1} = 1` and the height changes by `2 − 2 = 0`, as the trivial action of the diagonal requires; the unsigned characters `det(g)·Nrd(b)` would translate by `81` and move the height by `4`. The finite-level formula keeps `b^{−h}` for scalar `b ∈ O_F^×` (first Check).
- Orientation of the Weil factor: `h = 1`, `(g, b, w) = (ϖ, 1, Φ)` with `Φ` a geometric Frobenius. `rec_F(Φ) ∈ ϖ^{−1}O_F^×`, so the translation `ϖ·rec_F(Φ)` is a unit and height zero is preserved, as EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.7 requires; with the geometric normalization `rec_F^{−1}` the translation would have valuation `2`.
- Sectors: at level `m` the `H⁰` decomposes into the characters `(ω∘det) ⊗ (ω∘Nrd)^{−1} ⊗ (ω∘rec_F)` (STR08 Theorem 4.4(ii), line 387), on which a diagonal `z ∈ O_F^×` acts by `ω(z^h)ω(z^h)^{−1} = 1`. Because the diagonal acts trivially, in `Z[ρ] = Hom_{L[Ǧ]}(ρ, L ⊗ Z)` an element `z` of the centre of `G` acts as `z^{−1} ∈ Ǧ`, that is by `ω_ρ(z)^{−1}` with `ω_ρ` the central character of `ρ`; for `ρ = JL(M)` this is the central character of the dual `LL(M)^*` occurring in §§7.4–7.5, since `LL(M)` and `JL(M)` have the same central character (CDN20 line 142).
- For `ρ` supercuspidal-type (`JL(M)` with `M` supercuspidal) `dim ρ ≥ 2`, while `JL(St ⊗ χ) = χ ∘ Nrd` is one-dimensional and can occur in `H⁰`.

### 7.2 Equivariant semistable models of the rank-two Drinfeld levels

Prove that for `n ≥ 0`, `M_n^ϖ` has, over the integers of a finite extension `E` of `F`, a `G × Ǧ × G_F`-equivariant semistable formal model `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)°` whose triangulation contains the node set `Σ_an(M_{n,E}^ϖ)` of the analytic skeleton and refines it, the further vertices being genus-zero points of valence two on the edges of the skeleton; that the irreducible components of its special fibre form finitely many `G`-orbits; and that each component is fixed by an open subgroup of `G` (CDN20 Proposition A.1; CDN23 Proposition 2.7). Route: for a cocompact `Γ ⊂ G/ϖ^ℤ` acting freely on the Bruhat–Tits tree, `X_n = Γ∖M_n^ϖ` is a proper smooth curve, algebraic by proper GAGA (AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7); over a finite extension `E` it has a stable model `X_{n,E}^+` (StableReduction Layer 7), whose triangulation is the node set `Σ_an(X_{n,E})` (CDN20 lines 2108–2109). Let `X^+ = Γ∖Ω^+` be the stable model of `Γ∖Ω`, which is also its minimal semistable model, `Ω^+` being the standard model of `Ω` (HilbertModularVarietiesAndShimuraCurves §R18.5.3). The fibre product `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+ = X_{n,E}^+ ×_{X^+} Ω^+` is étale over `X_{n,E}^+`, so it is a generalized semistable model, with nodes `xy = ϖ_E^h`, `h ≥ 1`. Since `Γ` acts freely, the projection to `X_{n,E}` is a local isomorphism and a point is a node exactly when its image is; the triangulation of `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+` contains `Σ_an(M_{n,E}^ϖ)` and both have the quotient `Σ_an(X_{n,E})` by `Γ`, so they are equal (CDN20 lines 2110–2120). This set is invariant under `Aut_F(M_n^ϖ)` (TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.16), so the action extends to the normal flat model `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+`, whose component-point set it is (TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.17). The semistable model `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)°` is obtained from `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+` by the functorial resolution that blows up the singular points and then the self-intersection points of the components (CDN23 lines 1206–1207), so the action extends to it as well (CDN20 lines 2121–2122). Each node of thickness `h` becomes a chain of `h` nodes of thickness one, and the resolution inserts the genus-zero vertices of valence two. Finiteness of orbits and openness of stabilizers come from the finiteness of `Γ`-orbits and the profiniteness of `GL₂(O_F)`.

**Checks.**

- `n = 0`: `M_0^ϖ` is the union of two copies of `Ω` (height classes modulo `2`), and the model is two copies of the standard model over `O_F` (`E = F`). Its nodes `xy = ϖ` all have thickness one and every vertex of the tree has valence `q + 1 ≥ 3`, so here the resolved triangulation is the node set.
- Thickness two: a node `xy = ϖ_E²` of `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+` is an edge of length `2` (in units of `v(ϖ_E)`) between two nodes. Resolving it inserts one exceptional projective line meeting the two strict transforms in nodes `xy = ϖ_E`, so the resolved vertex set has the two endpoints and one more vertex of genus zero and valence two, which is not a node (TropicalAndBerkovichArithmetic §2.16), and the edge becomes two edges of length `1`; the total length is `2 = 1 + 1` in both. Asserting that the triangulation of the semistable model is the node set fails here; node equality holds for the generalized model `(M_{n,E}^ϖ)^+`.
- The statement is for `GL₂(F)`; for `GL_n(F)`, `n ≥ 3`, the levels are not curves and no such model is asserted (CDN23 line 1172).
- The components of the model of `M_0^ϖ` are the pairs `(v, i)` of a vertex of the tree and a copy index `i ∈ ℤ/2`, with `g·(v, i) = (gv, i + v(det g))`. Since `g` changes the type of a vertex (the parity of the lattice index) by `v(det g)`, the class of type plus copy index modulo `2` is invariant, so the components form exactly two `G`-orbits, and one `G × Ǧ`-orbit, because `ϖ_D ∈ Ǧ` fixes the vertices and shifts the copy index. A stabilizer in `G` is `GL₂(O_F)ϖ^ℤ`, whose image in `G′` is open; finitely many orbits, not one, is what the statement asserts.

### 7.4 Hyodo–Kato and de Rham multiplicities

Prove that for a supercuspidal rank-two `L-(φ, N, G_F)`-module `M`, so `WD(M)` absolutely irreducible and `M` of slope `1/2`, there are natural isomorphisms of `G`-Fréchet spaces `H¹_HK(M_∞)[M] ≅ M̆ ⊗̂_L LL(M)^*` and `H¹_dR(M_∞)[M] ≅ (C ⊗_F M_dR) ⊗̂_L LL(M)^*`, compatible with `ι_HK` on the left and the identification `M_dR ⊗_F C = M̆ ⊗ C` on the right, all maps commuting with `W_F` and the first with `φ` (CDN20 Théorème 0.4, printed without the slope hypothesis, which CDN20 §2.1, lines 644 and 654, and CDN23 line 328 carry). Route (CDN-CURVES Remarque 0.16(iii)): at each level, §4.6, in the product form for disjoint unions of §4.2, applied to `M_n^ϖ`, which is defined over `F` and has finitely many connected components (§7.3), makes `H¹_HK(M_n^ϖ)(1)` similar to `WD(H¹_proét(M_n^ϖ, ℚ_ℓ(1)))` as representations of `WD_{M_n^ϖ} ⊃ W_F` with the actions of `G′ × Ǧ`; a module whose central character is nontrivial on `ϖ` is reduced to this case by the unramified twist of EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.14, after a finite extension of `L`; the ℓ-adic side is the dual, by curve duality (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§9.6–9.7), of the compactly supported cohomology whose `JL(M)`-part is computed by EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.20; Fontaine's equivalence between rank-two `(φ, N, G_F)`-modules and Weil–Deligne representations (PadicHodgeTheory §5.3), absolute irreducibility of `WD(M)` and the resulting `End_{(φ,N,G_F)}(M) = L` turn the similarity into the stated isomorphism on the `JL(M)`-part; the de Rham statement follows by `ι_HK`. The global route of CDN20 §5 is an alternative proof when `F = ℚ_p`.

**Checks.**

- `n = 0`: no supercuspidal `M` has level `0` (`JL(M)` would factor through `Ǧ/O_D^×`, hence be one-dimensional), so both sides vanish at level zero.
- `M` of slope `1/2`: `H¹_HK(M_∞)[M]` has slope `1/2`, not the slope one of `H¹_HK(Ω)`; transporting the half-plane structure to every sector would give the wrong slope.
- Negative control for the slope hypothesis: the unramified twist `M′ = M ⊗ E`, `E = ℚ_p^nr e` with `φe = pe` and `G_F` trivial on `e`, has `WD(M′)` absolutely irreducible, slope `3/2` and `JL(M′) = JL(M) ⊗ χ∘Nrd` with `χ` unramified. `Ǧ` permutes the geometric components of `M_n` transitively through `Nrd`, the stabilizer `S` of a component has `Nrd(S) ⊂ O_F^×`, and the Hyodo–Kato Frobenius fixes each component, so Frobenius reciprocity from `S` gives the same `φ`-module `H¹_HK(M_∞)[M′] = H¹_HK(M_∞)[M]`, of slope `1/2`, while `M̆′` has slope `3/2`: the isomorphism fails for `M′`, and absolute irreducibility of `WD(M)` alone does not suffice.
- Central characters: `LL(M)` and `JL(M)` have the same central character (CDN20 line 142); on `M_n^ϖ`, `ϖ` acts trivially, so for `M` whose central character is nontrivial on `ϖ` the multiplicity space on `M_n^ϖ` is `0` while that on `M_n` is not.

### 7.6 Cohomologically profinite sheaves of finite copresentation

Let `Y` be a scheme over `O_L` with an increasing cover by quasi-compact opens `{U_s}` and an action of a p-adic Lie group `G′` for which the smooth finitely presented `O_L`-representations form an abelian category closed under extensions, each `U_s` fixed by an open subgroup. Call a pro-system profinite if it is isomorphic in `Pro(Mod_{O_L})` to a system of finite-length modules, and a sheaf `ℱ` of `O_L`-modules on `Y_ét` cohomologically profinite if every `{H^i_ét(U_s, ℱ)}_s` is profinite; then `H^i_ét(Y, ℱ) = lim_s H^i_ét(U_s, ℱ)` is a profinite `O_L`-module. Call `ℱ` `G′`-continuous if each `H^i_ét(U_s, ℱ)` is killed by a power of `p` and fixed by an open compact subgroup, and cohomologically profinite of finite copresentation (cpcf) if it is cohomologically profinite, `G′`-continuous, and every `H^i_ét(Y, ℱ)^∨` is a smooth finitely presented `O_L[G′]`-module (CDN23 §2.1.3). Prove the two-out-of-three properties for short exact sequences of `G′`-equivariant sheaves (CDN23 Lemme 2.8). In the cpcf case the proof passes to the long exact sequence of Pontryagin duals and uses the five-term criterion: in an exact sequence `A → B → C → D → E` of smooth `O_L[G′]`-modules with `A, B, D, E` finitely presented, `C` is finitely presented. For `G′ = G/ϖ^ℤ` this criterion is ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §0.7 (CDN23 Lemme 2.9), imported; for a general `G′` under the standing hypothesis it is the elementary step of the proof, `0 → coker(A → B) → C → ker(D → E) → 0` with outer terms finitely presented because the category is abelian and `C` because it is closed under extensions, and it is not a separate target here.

**Checks.**

- A constant system of a finite module is profinite, and so is `{ℤ/p^n}` with surjective transitions, with limit `ℤ_p`; the constant system of `𝔽_p^{(ℕ)}` is not, since a pro-isomorphism would make its limit `𝔽_p^{(ℕ)}` a profinite module.
- A sheaf with `H⁰(U_s) = 𝔽_p^s`, surjective restrictions and no higher cohomology is cohomologically profinite with `H⁰(Y) = 𝔽_p^ℕ`.
- For `G′ = G/ϖ^ℤ` the standing hypothesis holds: kernels and cokernels by ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §0.5, extensions by §0.4; for a group without this coherence the five-term criterion, and with it the cpcf part of the two-out-of-three property, is not asserted.
- The elementary step in its extreme cases: with `A = B = 0` it is the closure of finitely presented modules under kernels, with `D = E = 0` their closure under cokernels; in the cpcf case of Lemme 2.8 for `0 → ℱ₁ → ℱ → ℱ₂ → 0` the middle term is `H^i(Y, ℱ)^∨`, flanked by `H^{i+1}(Y, ℱ₁)^∨ → H^i(Y, ℱ₂)^∨` and `H^i(Y, ℱ₁)^∨ → H^{i−1}(Y, ℱ₂)^∨`.

*Needs:* ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §§0.3–0.5 and 0.7; EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2.

### 7.7 Finite copresentation of mod `p^k` cohomology

Prove that for `K/F` finite, `k ≥ 1` and `q ≥ 0`, the `G′`-module `H^q_ét(M_{n,K}^ϖ, O_L/p^k)` is the Pontryagin dual of a smooth finitely presented representation of `G′`; in particular it is a profinite `O_L`-module (CDN23 Théorème 2.1 = Théorème 0.5). Route (CDN23 §2.2): over a finite Galois extension `K′ ⊃ K` containing `μ_p` and the field of definition of a model of §7.2, (1) the sheaves `Ω^t_{Y/k′}` of the special fibre are cpcf, by closed Mayer–Vietoris over the components and their intersections, whose cohomology is induced from open compact subgroups (§7.2); (2) the nearby cycle sheaves `R^jΨ𝔽_p` are cpcf, through the Bloch–Kato–Hyodo filtration (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.7), the Cartier sequences for `Ω_log`, `B`, `Z` (CrystallineCohomology §CR.5.1) and §7.6; (3) the pro-systems `{H^q_ét(U_{s,η}, ℤ/p^k)}_s` are profinite and continuous, by the spectral sequence `H^i(U_s, R^jΨ𝔽_p) ⇒ H^{i+j}(U_{s,η}, 𝔽_p)` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§4.1, 4.6) and dévissage in `k`; (4) the statement over `K′` follows; (5) descent to `K` by the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequences of the Stein covering for `Gal(K′/K)`. The argument shows more generally that a semistable formal scheme whose special fibre has such a stratification has profinite `H^q_ét(X_K, 𝔽_p)` (CDN23 Remarque 2.11).

**Checks.**

- `n = 0`, `q = 0`: over `F̆` the level-zero quotient is `ℤ/2 × Ω`, since `ϖ` in the centre of `G` shifts the height by `v(det ϖ) = 2`, and a Frobenius shifts it by `1` (CDN23 lines 196–208; EndoscopicTransferAndUnitaryTraceComparison §12.19), so the two geometric copies of `Ω` are conjugate under `Gal(F₂/F)`, `F₂` the unramified quadratic extension of `F`, and `π₀(M_{0,K}^ϖ) = π₀(Spec(K ⊗_F F₂))`. Hence `H⁰_ét(M_{0,K}^ϖ, 𝔽_p) = 𝔽_p` for `K = F` (or any `K` not containing `F₂`) and `𝔽_p²` for `K ⊇ F₂`; its dual is the permutation representation of `G′` on `π₀(Spec(K ⊗_F F₂))`, on which an element of odd determinant valuation acts as the nontrivial element of `Gal(F₂/F)`: trivial for `K = F`, two-dimensional for `K ⊇ F₂`. The value `𝔽_p²` for every `K` would be the geometric `H⁰`, which §7.7 does not compute.
- With `ℚ_p`-coefficients the corresponding `H¹` is a Fréchet space that is not compact; the statement concerns torsion coefficients only.
- Finite presentation is not admissibility: for `F ≠ ℚ_p` and `n > 0` the dual `H¹_ét(M^ϖ_{n,K}, k_L)^∨` is finitely presented by this section and, for `K` large, not admissible by §7.8(f); for `F = ℚ_p` and `p > 2` the duals of `H¹_ét(M^ϖ_{n,K}, O_L/p^k)` are admissible of finite length (CDN23 Théorème 4.1, which belongs to ScholzeFunctorAndModpCohomologyOfTheLubinTateTower by the scope above). A statement asserting admissibility here would contradict §7.8, and one asserting only that the module is profinite would not determine the smooth dual.

*Needs:* §7.2; §7.6; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.1; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.6; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.7; CrystallineCohomology §CR.5.1; ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §§0.3–0.5 and 0.7.

### 7.8 Non-admissibility for `F ≠ ℚ_p`

For `V` a finite-dimensional `L`-representation of `G_F` with `G_F`-stable lattice `V^+`, `V^+_k = V^+/m_L^k`, and `M` supercuspidal with a `Ǧ`-stable lattice `JL(M)^+`, define `Π_M(V) = Hom_{G_F×Ǧ}(V ⊗ JL(M), H¹_ét(M_{n,C}^ϖ, L(1)))^*`, `Π_M(V^+)` and `Π_M(V^+_k)` with `O_L`- and `O_L/m_L^k`-duals (CDN23 §2.3, lines 1465–1470). Prove: (a) `Π_M(V)` is a unitary Banach representation of `G` with `G`-stable lattice `Π_M(V^+)`, and `(O_L/m_L^k) ⊗ Π_M(V^+)` is a quotient of `Π_M(V^+_k)` (CDN23 Lemme 2.16, using §7.7); (b) if some `Π_M(V^+_k)` is admissible, then `k_L ⊗ Π_M(V^+)` lies in the category `C` of ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §2.4 (CDN23 Lemme 2.17); (c) for `F ≠ ℚ_p`, `π` supercuspidal with universal completion `π̂` and `Π` a unitary Banach representation with a `G`-stable lattice whose reduction modulo `m_L` lies in `C`, `Hom_G(π̂, Π) = 0` (CDN23 Proposition 2.18; the hypothesis `F ≠ ℚ_p` is that of ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §2.6 and of the standing hypothesis of CDN23 §1.5, line 955, under which Corollaire 1.34 is proved); (d) if `F ≠ ℚ_p` and `Π_M(V) ≠ 0`, then `Π_M(V^+_k)` is smooth and not admissible for every `k ≥ 1` (CDN23 Théorème 2.14), using the injection of §7.5; (e) for every supercuspidal `M` of level at most `n` on which `ϖ` acts trivially there is `V` with `Π_M(V) ≠ 0` (CDN23 Remarque 2.15(ii), line 1494), through a cocompact `Γ ⊂ G′`, the proper curves `X_n = M_{n,F}^ϖ/Γ` with `X₀` of genus `≥ 2`, the occurrence of every representation of `Gal(X_n/X₀)` in `Ω¹(X_n)` (NAK84 Theorem 4, as used in CDN23 Remarque 2.15(ii)) and the étale–de Rham comparison for proper curves (§4.7); (f) consequently, if `F ≠ ℚ_p` and `n > 0`, then `H¹_ét(M_{n,K}^ϖ, k_L)^∨` is not admissible for `K/F` finite and large enough (CDN23 Théorème 2.13).

**Checks.**

- `F = ℚ_p`: `Π_M(V)` and `Π_M(V^+_k)` are admissible (CDN23 Remarque 2.15(i)); the hypothesis `F ≠ ℚ_p` in (d) is necessary.
- `F = ℚ_p` in (c): take `M` supercuspidal of level at most `n` with `ϖ` trivial on `JL(M)` and `V` with `Π_M(V) ≠ 0` (e). Then `Π_M(V)` is a unitary Banach representation (a), `Π_M(V^+_k)` is admissible (CDN23 Remarque 2.15(i)), so `k_L ⊗ Π_M(V^+)` lies in `C` by (b), which holds for every `F`; and the bounded-vector injection of §7.5 dualizes to a map `H(V) ⊗ L̂L(M) → Π_M(V)` with dense image, `H(V) = Hom_{G_F}(V, X^+_st(M))` finite-dimensional with trivial `G`-action, so `Hom_G(L̂L(M), Π_M(V)) ≠ 0` with `π = LL(M)` supercuspidal: (c) fails for `F = ℚ_p`, and its hypothesis `F ≠ ℚ_p` cannot be dropped.
- `n = 0`: there is no supercuspidal `M` of level zero (§7.4), so (f) is stated for `n > 0`.
- `n = 1` and `M` of level `2` (`JL(M)` nontrivial on `1 + ϖ_D O_D`): `Ǧ_1` acts trivially on `M_1^ϖ`, so every constituent of `H¹_ét(M^ϖ_{1,C}, L(1))` is trivial on `Ǧ_1`, the `JL(M)`-isotypic part is `0` and `Π_M(V) = 0` for every `V`; the level hypothesis in (e) cannot be dropped. For `n = 1` the hypothesis is met by the two-dimensional `JL(M)` attached to a character of `𝔽_{q²}^×` that does not factor through the norm, so (f) has input.
- In (c), `St ⊗ χ ∘ det` and principal series receive no nonzero map from `π̂`, because `π` is dense in `π̂` and a cuspidal `π` has no nonzero map to a principal-series constituent; for `π = St` itself (c) fails, since `St` maps to `Ŝt`.

*Needs:* §4.7; §7.5; §7.7; ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §2.4; ModpRepresentationsOfGL2OverLocalFields §2.6; AdicSpacesPartIII §R3.7.
