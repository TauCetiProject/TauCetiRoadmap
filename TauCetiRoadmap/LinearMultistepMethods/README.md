# Roadmap: linear multistep methods

**Summary.** A linear multistep method approximates the solution of an initial value problem
`y' = f(t, y)` on a uniform mesh `t_n = t₀ + n h` by a recurrence that links `k + 1` consecutive
values: `∑ α_j Y_(n+j) = h ∑ β_j f(t_(n+j), Y_(n+j))`, with real coefficients and `j` running from
`0` to `k`. The Adams methods and the backward differentiation formulas are the families used in
practice. Everything here is a statement about the sequences that satisfy such a recurrence, in a
real normed space of any dimension; the solution of the differential equation is a hypothesis,
never constructed. Two polynomials carry all the information: `ρ(ζ) = ∑ α_j ζ^j` and
`σ(ζ) = ∑ β_j ζ^j`.

The main theorems are these.

- *Order.* A method has order `p`, meaning that the values of every smooth curve satisfy the
  recurrence up to an error `O(h^(p+1))`, exactly when it reproduces the polynomials of degree at
  most `p`: `∑ α_j j^q = q ∑ β_j j^(q−1)` for `q ≤ p`.
- *Zero-stability.* The solutions of `∑ α_j y_(n+j) = 0` are bounded by a fixed multiple of their
  starting values if and only if every root of `ρ` lies in the closed unit disc and the roots on
  the unit circle are simple (the root condition).
- *Dahlquist's equivalence theorem.* On a nonzero space, and with `α_k ≠ 0`, a method converges
  for every continuous field that is Lipschitz in the state, from all starting values that tend to
  the initial value, if and only if it satisfies the root condition and is consistent
  (`ρ(1) = 0` and `ρ′(1) = σ(1)`). If such a method also satisfies the order conditions up to `p`
  and the solution is of class `C^(p+1)`, the error is `O(h^p)` plus a multiple of the starting
  error.
- *Dahlquist's barriers.* A method with `α_k ≠ 0` that satisfies the root condition has order at
  most `k + 2`, and at most `k + 1` when `k` is odd. An A-stable method with `α_k ≠ 0`, one for
  which, whenever `Re μ < 0`, the polynomial `ρ − μ σ` keeps the degree `k` and has all its roots
  in the open unit disc, has order at most `2`, and no consistent explicit method is A-stable.

The `k`-step Adams–Bashforth and Adams–Moulton methods have orders `k` and `k + 1`; the `k`-step
backward differentiation formula has order `k` and satisfies the root condition for
`1 ≤ k ≤ 6`; the two-step formula is A-stable.

The roadmap builds on the one-step methods roadmap, whose setting for differential equations
(fields, solutions, Lipschitz conditions) it adopts and whose implicit Euler and trapezoidal
methods are its one-step members; on Mathlib's linear recurrences, polynomials and Lagrange
interpolation; and on Mathlib's Taylor theorem for the bound on the defect.

Below, Layer 1 treats methods and order, Layer 2 zero-stability and discrete stability, Layer 3
convergence and its rate, Layer 4 the Adams and backward differentiation families, and Layer 5
the barriers and A-stability.

## Conventions

- `E` is a real normed space (`[NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace ℝ E]`) of any dimension; no
  completeness is assumed anywhere, because no theorem solves an implicit equation. The setting
  for differential equations is that of OneStepMethodsForODEs (Conventions): a field is
  `f : ℝ → E → E`, time first; "`y` is a solution on `[a, b]`" means
  `IsIntegralCurveOn y f (Set.Icc a b)`; "`f` is `L`-Lipschitz in the state" means
  `∀ t, LipschitzWith L (f t)` with `L : ℝ≥0`.
- **Methods.** A linear `k`-step method is a pair of coefficient vectors `α β : Fin (k + 1) → ℝ`.
  No normalisation is built in: `α_k ≠ 0` (Süli's standing assumption) is a hypothesis exactly
  where it is needed, and Checks show it is needed there. Polynomials are Mathlib's `ℝ[X]`; roots
  are taken in `ℂ` after `Polynomial.map (algebraMap ℝ ℂ)`.
- **Meshes.** The mesh is uniform, `t_n = t₀ + n h`, with `h ≥ 0` (`h > 0` in convergence
  statements). A sequence `Y : ℕ → E` is generated by the method up to `N` when its residual
  vanishes at every `n` with `n + k ≤ N`. For an implicit method (`β_k ≠ 0`) the recurrence
  determines `Y_(n+k)` only implicitly; the theorems quantify over all sequences that satisfy it,
  so neither existence nor uniqueness of the next value is used.
- **Sign of the defect.** Residuals and defects are "left side minus right side" of the recurrence,
  with no division by `h` or by `σ(1)`; Süli's truncation error is the defect divided by `h σ(1)`.
- `0 ^ 0 = 1`, so the order condition with `q = 0` reads `∑ α_j = 0`; for `q = 0` the factor `q`
  makes the right side `0` whatever the junk value of `q − 1`.

## Layer 1: methods, consistency and order

### 1.1 Methods and the sequences they generate

Define the structure `LinearMultistepMethod k` with fields `α` and `β`, and for a method `m`:

- `m.rho = ∑ j, α_j X^j` and `m.sigma = ∑ j, β_j X^j`, the first and second characteristic
  polynomials;
- `m.IsExplicit`: `β_k = 0`;
- `m.residual f t₀ h Y n = ∑ j, α_j • Y (n + j) − h • ∑ j, β_j • f (t₀ + (n + j) h) (Y (n + j))`.

API to prove: `m.rho.eval 1 = ∑ j, α_j` and `(derivative m.rho).eval 1 = ∑ j, j α_j`; the degree of
`m.rho` is `k` when `α_k ≠ 0`; the residual is linear in `(α, β)` and, for a field that does not
depend on the state, affine in `Y`; for an explicit method with `α_k ≠ 0` and any starting values
`Y_0, …, Y_(k−1)` there is exactly one sequence with vanishing residual at every `n`, given by the
recurrence solved for `Y_(n+k)`. (Süli 2022, (37) and the definitions of `ρ` and `σ` in Section
3.2; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- The method `α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (1, 0)` generates `Y_(n+1) = Y_n + h f(t_n, Y_n)`, the numerical
  solution `oneStepSeq` of the explicit Euler method of OneStepMethodsForODEs 3.1 on the uniform
  mesh; `α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (0, 1)` and `β = (1/2, 1/2)` give the steps of its implicit Euler and
  trapezoidal methods (3.3). Reading the indices of `β` backwards would exchange explicit and
  implicit Euler.
- Simpson's method `α = (−1, 0, 1)`, `β = (1/3, 4/3, 1/3)`: `ρ = X² − 1`, `σ = (X² + 4X + 1)/3`.
- Reading `ρ` with reversed coefficients (`∑ α_j X^(k−j)`) would turn the root `1/3` of the
  backward differentiation formula of 4.2 with `k = 2` into `3`.

*Needs:* OneStepMethodsForODEs Conventions; Mathlib `Polynomial.eval`, `Polynomial.derivative`.

### 1.2 The order conditions and the defect

Define `m.OrderConditions p`: for every `q ≤ p`, `∑ j, α_j j^q = q ∑ j, β_j j^(q−1)`;
`m.IsConsistent`: `ρ(1) = 0` and `ρ′(1) = σ(1)`; and the defect of a curve `y` at `t` with step
`h`, `m.defect f y t h = ∑ j, α_j • y (t + j h) − h • ∑ j, β_j • f (t + j h) (y (t + j h))`, the
residual of the values of `y` on the mesh through `t`. Prove:

- `m.OrderConditions p` holds if and only if `∑ j, α_j P(j) = ∑ j, β_j P′(j)` for every real
  polynomial `P` of degree at most `p` (exactness on polynomials); `m.OrderConditions p` implies
  `m.OrderConditions q` for `q ≤ p`; the conditions are invariant under the shift that turns
  `m` into the `(k + 1)`-step method with coefficients `(0, α_0, …, α_k)` and `(0, β_0, …, β_k)`;
- `m.IsConsistent ↔ m.OrderConditions 1`;
- **the defect bound** (`LinearMultistepMethod.norm_defect_le`): let `0 < k`, `0 ≤ h`,
  `m.OrderConditions p`, and let `y` solve `y' = f(t, y)` and be of class `C^(p+1)` on
  `[t, t + k h]` with `‖iteratedDerivWithin (p + 1) y [t, t + k h] s‖ ≤ M` there. Then

      ‖m.defect f y t h‖ ≤ M h^(p+1) ∑ j, (|α_j| j^(p+1) / (p+1)! + |β_j| j^p / p!)

Route: for `h > 0`, write `y (t + j h)` by Taylor's formula of order `p` and
`f (t + j h) (y (t + j h)) = y′(t + j h)` by Taylor's formula of order `p − 1` at `t`. The
polynomial parts cancel coefficient by coefficient, the coefficient of `h^q y^(q)(t)` being
`(∑ α_j j^q − q ∑ β_j j^(q−1)) / q!`. The remainders are bounded through a norming functional
(`exists_dual_vector`), which reduces them to real functions, and the Lagrange form of the
remainder (`taylor_mean_remainder_lagrange`). For `h = 0` the defect is `ρ(1) y(t) = 0`.
(Süli 2022, (46) and (47) with the constants `C_q`, and Mirzaei 2024, Theorem 6.3, state the
conditions with unspecified remainders; the constant is proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `0 < k` is needed: for `k = 0`, `α = (0)`, `β = (1)`, `p = 0` the conditions hold, but on
  `[t, t]` every value is a derivative within the singleton, so `y` constant is a solution of
  `f = 1` with `M = 0`, while the defect is `−h`.
- The two-step Adams–Bashforth method `α = (0, −1, 1)`, `β = (−1/2, 3/2, 0)` satisfies the
  conditions for `p = 2` and not `p = 3`: `∑ α_j j³ − 3 ∑ β_j j² = 7 − 9/2 = 5/2`. For `y = e^t` at
  `t = 0`, `h = 1/10` the defect is `4.56… · 10⁻⁴` and the bound with `p = 2`, `M = e^(1/5)` is
  `2.75… · 10⁻³`.
- Simpson's method satisfies the conditions for `p = 4` and not `p = 5`:
  `∑ α_j j⁵ − 5 ∑ β_j j⁴ = 32 − 100/3 = −4/3`.
- Explicit Euler (`α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (1, 0)`) is consistent and has `∑ α_j j² − 2 ∑ β_j j = 1`;
  the method `α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (2, 0)` has `ρ′(1) = 1 ≠ 2 = σ(1)` and is not consistent.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `IsIntegralCurveOn`, `iteratedDerivWithin`,
`taylor_mean_remainder_lagrange`, `exists_dual_vector`.

### 1.3 Order

Define `m.HasOrder p`: for every `y : ℝ → ℝ` of class `C^∞` and all `a`, `b` there is `C` with
`|∑ j, α_j y (t + j h) − h ∑ j, β_j y′(t + j h)| ≤ C h^(p+1)` whenever `a ≤ t`, `0 ≤ h`,
`t + k h ≤ b`. Every smooth `y` solves `y' = f(t)` with `f = y′`, so this is order at least `p`
in the sense of Süli 2022, Definition 6, which names the largest such `p`. Prove
`m.HasOrder p ↔ m.OrderConditions p` (`LinearMultistepMethod.hasOrder_iff_orderConditions`).

Route: sufficiency is the defect bound of 1.2 on `[a, b]`, with `M` the maximum of `|y^(p+1)|`
there (for `k = 0` the conditions force `α = β = 0` when `p ≥ 1`, and `α_0 = 0` when `p = 0`).
Necessity: for `y (t) = t^q` with `q ≤ p`, the defect at `t = 0` is exactly
`h^q (∑ α_j j^q − q ∑ β_j j^(q−1))`, which is `O(h^(p+1))` as `h → 0⁺` only if it vanishes.
(Süli 2022, Definition 6 and (47); Mirzaei 2024, Theorem 6.3; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `y (t) = t^q` makes the defect a single monomial in `h`, so `HasOrder p` fails as soon as one
  condition with `q ≤ p` fails; for the two-step Adams–Bashforth method and `y = t³` the defect
  at `0` is `5h³/2`.
- The zero method `α = β = 0` has `HasOrder p` for every `p`, and the order conditions hold too;
  this degenerate method is excluded later by `α_k ≠ 0` or by the root condition (which fails for
  `ρ = 0`).

*Needs:* 1.2; Mathlib `ContDiff`, `deriv`.

Explicit and implicit Euler, the trapezoidal rule, Simpson's method, and the methods of Layer 4.

## Layer 2: zero-stability

### 2.1 The root condition

Define `m.RootCondition`: every complex root `z` of `ρ` has `‖z‖ ≤ 1`, and every root with
`‖z‖ = 1` has multiplicity `1`. Define `m.IsZeroStable`: there is `K` such that every real
solution of `∑ j, α_j y (n + j) = 0` (for all `n`) satisfies `|y n| ≤ K ∑ s < k, |y s|` for all
`n`. Prove `m.RootCondition ↔ m.IsZeroStable`
(`LinearMultistepMethod.rootCondition_iff_isZeroStable`), with no hypothesis on `α_k`.

API to prove: the root condition fails for `ρ = 0`; it implies `ρ ≠ 0`; with consistency it makes
`1` a simple root, so `σ(1) = ρ′(1) ≠ 0`; a zero-stability constant also bounds the solutions
with values in `E`, `‖y n‖ ≤ K ∑ s < k, ‖y s‖`, and `K ≥ 1` when `k ≥ 1` and `α_k ≠ 0` (the
solution with starting values `0, …, 0, 1` exists then; without `α_k ≠ 0` it may not:
`α = (1, 0, 0)` has only the zero solution, and `K = 0` is a zero-stability constant).

Route. Reduce to `α_k ≠ 0` by discarding vanishing leading coefficients: if `α_d` is the last
nonzero one, the solutions are those of the `d`-step recurrence and their values at
`d ≤ s < k` are fixed linear combinations of the first `d`. Then normalise to Mathlib's
`LinearRecurrence` over `ℂ`, whose characteristic polynomial is `ρ / α_k`. Necessity (Süli 2022,
Theorem 6): a root `z` with `|z| > 1` gives the solution `z^n`, and a multiple root with `|z| = 1`
gives `n z^n`; their real or imaginary parts are unbounded real solutions with bounded starting
values. Sufficiency: the sequences `n^i z^n`, `i` below the multiplicity of the root `z`, are
solutions for every root `z ≠ 0` (by `geom_sol_iff_root_charPoly` for `i = 0`, and by applying
`(ζ d/dζ)^i` to `ζ^n ρ(ζ)`), and for a root `0` of multiplicity `μ₀` the sequences supported at
one index below `μ₀` are solutions. These are `k` linearly independent solutions, so they span
the solution space of dimension `k` (`solSpace_rank`); under the root condition each is
bounded. Süli proves
sufficiency differently, through the boundedness of the coefficients of `1 / (ζ^k ρ(1/ζ))`
(Süli 2022, Lemma 1, and Lemma 2 with `β = 0` and `λ = 0`).

**Checks.**

- `ρ = X² − 1` (Simpson): solutions alternate between `y_0` and `y_1`, `K = 1`.
- The backward differentiation formula with `k = 2`, `ρ = (3/2)(X − 1)(X − 1/3)`: the solution
  with starting values `0, 1` is `3/2 − (3/2) 3^(−n)`, so the best constant is `K = 3/2`.
- Süli 2022, Example 4 c): `α = (−11, −27, 27, 11)` has the root `−3.1356…` and is not zero-stable,
  although its order is `6` (its `β = 3 (1, 9, 9, 1)` satisfies the conditions for `p = 6`).
- `ρ = (X − 1)²`: the solution `n` is unbounded; `ρ = X² + 1`: roots `±i`, simple, zero-stable.
- `ρ` with a zero leading coefficient: `α = (−1, 1, 0)` gives `y_(n+1) = y_n`, zero-stable with
  `K = 1`, and `ρ = X − 1` satisfies the root condition, as the theorem says without `α_k ≠ 0`.

*Needs:* 1.1; Mathlib `LinearRecurrence`, `LinearRecurrence.geom_sol_iff_root_charPoly`,
`LinearRecurrence.solSpace_rank`, `LinearRecurrence.eq_mk_of_is_sol_of_eq_init`,
`Polynomial.rootMultiplicity`, `Polynomial.map`.

### 2.2 Discrete stability for Lipschitz fields

Let `0 < k`, `α_k ≠ 0`, and let `K` be a zero-stability constant of `m`. Let `f` be `L`-Lipschitz
in the state, `0 ≤ h` with `2 h L |β_k| K ≤ |α_k|`, `Λ ≥ 0`, and let `Y`, `Z : ℕ → E` have
residuals with `‖m.residual f t₀ h Y n − m.residual f t₀ h Z n‖ ≤ Λ` for `n + k ≤ N`. With
`B = ∑ j, |β_j|`, prove for `n ≤ N` (`LinearMultistepMethod.norm_sub_le_of_isZeroStable`)

    ‖Y n − Z n‖ ≤ (2K ∑ s < k, ‖Y s − Z s‖ + 2K n Λ / |α_k|) · exp(2 K L B n h / |α_k|)

Route. The differences `e_n = Y_n − Z_n` satisfy `∑ α_j e_(n+j) = b_n`, where
`b_n = h ∑ β_j (f(t_(n+j), Y_(n+j)) − f(t_(n+j), Z_(n+j))) + λ_n` and `‖λ_n‖ ≤ Λ`. Write
`e_n = H_n + ∑_(m ≤ n−k) γ_(n−k−m) b_m` for `n ≥ k`, where `H` solves the homogeneous recurrence
with the starting values of `e`, and `γ_l` is the value at `l + k − 1` of the scalar solution
with starting values `0, …, 0, 1/α_k`; zero-stability gives `‖H_n‖ ≤ K ∑ s < k, ‖e_s‖` and
`|γ_l| ≤ K / |α_k|`. The only term of the sum containing `e_n` has coefficient at most
`h L |β_k| K / |α_k| ≤ 1/2`, and the others involve `e_i` with `i < n`, each with total weight at
most `B`. So `‖e_n‖ ≤ D_n + (2 K L B h / |α_k|) ∑ i < n, ‖e_i‖` with `D_n` the first factor above,
nondecreasing in `n` because `Λ ≥ 0`; also for `n < k`, as `K ≥ 1`. Induction gives the factor
`(1 + 2 K L B h / |α_k|)^n` and `Real.add_one_le_exp` the exponential. (Süli 2022, Lemma 2, for
`E = ℝ` and a constant `Γ` defined through `1/ρ̂`; the form with the zero-stability constant in a
normed space is proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `Λ ≥ 0` is needed: for `k = 2`, `N = 1` there is no residual condition, and with `Y = Z` and
  `Λ < 0` the right side at `n = 1` is negative.
- Explicit Euler, `K = 1`, `α_1 = 1`, `B = 1`, `Λ = 0`: the bound
  `2 ‖Y 0 − Z 0‖ e^(2 L n h)` is weaker by the factor `2` and the doubled rate than the bound
  `e^(L n h) ‖Y 0 − Z 0‖` of OneStepMethodsForODEs 1.3, the price of the general implicit case.
- The step-size condition is needed for implicit methods: implicit Euler (`α = (−1, 1)`,
  `β = (0, 1)`, `K = 1`) on `f t u = L u` with `h L = 1` has residual
  `Y_(n+1) − Y_n − Y_(n+1) = −Y_n`. With `N = 1`, `Y_0 = 0`, any `Y_1` and `Z = 0`, all residuals
  vanish and the right side is `0`, while `‖Y_1 − Z_1‖` is arbitrary; here `2 h L |β_1| K = 2`.

*Needs:* 2.1; Mathlib `LipschitzWith`, `Real.add_one_le_exp`.

The zero-stability constants of the Adams methods (`K = 1`, since `ρ = X^(k−1)(X − 1)` has the

## Layer 3: convergence

### 3.1 Dahlquist's equivalence theorem

Define `m.IsConvergent E`: for every `f : ℝ → E → E` with `Function.uncurry f` continuous and `f`
`L`-Lipschitz in the state for some `L`, every solution `y` on `[a, b]` and every `ε > 0`, there
is `δ > 0` such that for every `h` with `0 < h ≤ δ`, every `N` with `a + N h ≤ b` and every
`Y : ℕ → E` with `‖Y s − y a‖ ≤ δ` for `s < k` and vanishing residual (mesh `a + n h`) for
`n + k ≤ N`, `‖Y n − y (a + n h)‖ ≤ ε` for all `n ≤ N`. This strengthens Süli's convergence
(Süli 2022, Definition 7: the limit at each fixed `x` along the meshes with `n h = x − a`, from
starting values that tend to `y(a)`) to uniformity over the nodes and the steps; the theorem holds
for both. Prove, for `E` nontrivial and `α_k ≠ 0`,

    m.IsConvergent E ↔ m.IsConsistent ∧ m.RootCondition

(`LinearMultistepMethod.isConvergent_iff`).

Route. Fix `v ≠ 0` in `E`. *Root condition* (Süli 2022, Theorem 7): for `f = 0`, the solution
`y = 0` and `[a, b] = [0, 1]`, a root `z = r e^(iφ)` of `ρ` with `r > 1` gives the generated
sequence `Y_n = h r^n cos(nφ) v` (the real part of `h z^n`, a solution of the recurrence whatever
`ρ(1)` is), with starting values of norm at most `h r^k ‖v‖`, and a multiple root with `r = 1`
gives `h^(1/2) n cos(nφ) v`; for small `h` both leave the ball of radius `ε` about `0` at some
`n ≤ 1/h` (of two consecutive indices, one has `|cos(nφ)| ≥ |sin φ| / 2` when `sin φ ≠ 0`).
*Consistency* (Süli 2022, Theorem 8): with `f = 0`, `y = v` and
starting values `v`, the generated sequence does not depend on `h`, so it tends to `v`, and the
recurrence gives `ρ(1) v = 0`; with `f = v`, `y (t) = t v`, the sequence
`Y_n = (σ(1)/ρ′(1)) n h v` is generated (`ρ′(1) ≠ 0` by the root condition just proved) and
converges only if `σ(1) = ρ′(1)`.
*Sufficiency* (Süli 2022, Theorem 9): `t ↦ f t (y t)` is uniformly continuous on `[a, b]`
(`IsCompact.uniformContinuousOn_of_continuous`), and consistency with the mean value inequality
bounds the defect of 1.2 by `h (∑ j, j |α_j| + ∑ j, |β_j|) χ(k h)`, with `χ` the modulus of
continuity of `y′`; then 2.2 with `Z_n = y(a + n h)`, `Λ` that defect bound and `n h ≤ b − a`
gives the claim, because `‖Y s − y(a + s h)‖ ≤ δ + ‖y a − y(a + s h)‖`.
(Süli 2022, Definition 7 and Theorems 7–10, for `E = ℝ`; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `E` must be nontrivial: on the zero space every method is convergent, the inconsistent
  `α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (2, 0)` included.
- `α_k ≠ 0` is needed: `α = (−1, 1, 0)`, `β = (1, 0, 0)` is consistent and satisfies the root
  condition, but its residual condition for `n + 2 ≤ N` does not constrain `Y_N`, which is
  arbitrary.
- Consistency alone does not suffice: `α = (−11, −27, 27, 11)`, `β = 3 (1, 9, 9, 1)` (Süli 2022,
  Example 4 c)) is consistent; on `f = 0` the generated sequence with starting values
  `v, v, v + h v` has a component along the root `−3.1356…` of size a nonzero multiple of `h`,
  which grows geometrically.
- Explicit Euler: consistent and `ρ = X − 1`, so convergent; OneStepMethodsForODEs 1.5 gives
  this for exact starting values on meshes with variable steps.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1, 2.2; Mathlib `IsIntegralCurveOn`,
`IsCompact.uniformContinuousOn_of_continuous`,
`image_norm_le_of_norm_deriv_right_le_deriv_boundary`.

### 3.2 The rate of convergence

Let `α_k ≠ 0`, `m.RootCondition` and `m.OrderConditions p` (the condition with `q = 0`,
`∑ α_j = 0`, then forces `0 < k`), let `f` be `L`-Lipschitz in
the state and let `y` be a solution on `[a, b]` of class `C^(p+1)` there. Prove that there are
`h₀ > 0` and `C` such that for `0 < h ≤ h₀`, `a + N h ≤ b` and every `Y` generated up to `N` on the
mesh `a + n h`,

    ‖Y n − y (a + n h)‖ ≤ C (∑ s < k, ‖Y s − y (a + s h)‖ + h^p)      for n ≤ N

(`LinearMultistepMethod.exists_norm_sub_le_of_orderConditions`).

Route: 2.1 gives `K`; take `h₀` with `2 h₀ L |β_k| K ≤ |α_k|`; 1.2 bounds the defects by
`M h^(p+1) c_p`, with `M` the maximum of `‖iteratedDerivWithin (p + 1) y [a, b]‖` (for `a < b`;
for `a = b` only `N = 0` occurs); 2.2 with `Λ = M c_p h^(p+1)` and `n h ≤ b − a` gives
`C = 2K e^(2KLB(b−a)/|α_k|) max(1, M c_p (b − a)/|α_k|)`. (Süli 2022, Theorem 10, second part,
and Mirzaei 2024, Theorem 6.5 under `α_j ≥ 0`, state the rate; the constant is proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `y' = 1`, `y = t`: every consistent method generates `Y_n = n h` from exact starting values, as
  in the proof of 3.1, so the error is `0`.
- The exponent is attained for the two-step Adams–Bashforth method (`p = 2`): on `y' = 3t²`,
  `y = t³`, `a = 0`, its defect is `5h³/2` at every `t`, and from exact starting values the
  generated sequence has error `Y_n − y(n h) = −(5/2) h³ (n − 1)` for `n ≥ 1`, about `−5h²/2`
  at `n h = 1`.
- The starting error enters at first power: with starting values off by `δ` and `f = 0`, the
  error of explicit Euler is exactly `δ`.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1, 2.2.

## Layer 4: the Adams and backward differentiation families

Throughout, `ℓ_j^(s)` is Mathlib's Lagrange basis polynomial
`Lagrange.basis (Finset.range s) (fun i : ℕ ↦ (i : ℝ)) j` of the nodes `0, …, s − 1`.

### 4.1 Adams methods

Define `adamsBashforth k` by `α_k = 1`, `α_(k−1) = −1`, the other `α_j = 0`, and
`β_j = ∫_(k−1)^k ℓ_j^(k)` for `j < k`, `β_k = 0`; and `adamsMoulton k` with the same `α` and
`β_j = ∫_(k−1)^k ℓ_j^(k+1)` for `j ≤ k`. Both are used for `k ≥ 1`; their `ρ` is
`X^k − X^(k−1)`. The methods integrate the polynomial that interpolates the field values at
`k` (respectively `k + 1`) consecutive nodes over the last step (Süli 2022, Section 3.1, (40)–(42),
by backward differences; Mirzaei 2024, Sections 6.1 and 6.2, by interpolation). Prove, for
`0 < k`:

- `adamsBashforth k` has order exactly `k`: `OrderConditions k` and not `OrderConditions (k + 1)`
  (`adamsBashforth_orderConditions`);
- `adamsMoulton k` has order exactly `k + 1` (`adamsMoulton_orderConditions`);
- both satisfy the root condition and are consistent, hence convergent by 3.1; `adamsBashforth k`
  is explicit.

Route. For `P` of degree at most `k` (respectively `k + 1`), `∑ α_j P(j) = P(k) − P(k − 1)` is the
integral of `P′` over `[k − 1, k]`, and `∑ β_j P′(j)` is the integral of the interpolant of `P′`,
which is `P′` itself (`Lagrange.eq_interpolate`). For `P = X^(k+1)` (respectively `X^(k+2)`) the
difference is `(k + 1) ∫_(k−1)^k ω` (respectively `(k + 2) ∫_(k−1)^k ω`) with `ω` the node
polynomial `∏ (X − i)` over the nodes; on `(k − 1, k)` every factor of `ω` is positive for
Adams–Bashforth, and all but the last for Adams–Moulton, so the integral is not `0`.
(Proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `adamsBashforth 1` is explicit Euler, `β = (1, 0)`; `adamsBashforth 2` has
  `β = (−1/2, 3/2, 0)` and `adamsBashforth 3` has `β = (5/12, −4/3, 23/12, 0)`; the four-step
  method has `β = (−9, 37, −59, 55)/24`, as in Süli 2022, Example 3 c).
- `adamsMoulton 1` is the trapezoidal rule, `β = (1/2, 1/2)`; `adamsMoulton 2` has
  `β = (−1/12, 2/3, 5/12)` and `adamsMoulton 3` has `β = (1, −5, 19, 9)/24`. Süli 2022,
  Example 3 c), writes this formula with the indices shifted by one, as a four-step method, and
  prints `−9` for the coefficient of the oldest field value; consistency `σ(1) = ρ′(1) = 1`
  requires `1` there.
- The first failing condition: `∑ α_j j^(k+1) − (k+1) ∑ β_j j^k` is `1, 5/2, 9` for
  Adams–Bashforth with `k = 1, 2, 3`, and the corresponding value with `k + 2` is
  `−1/2, −1, −19/6` for Adams–Moulton.
- Mirzaei 2024, Section 6.1, indexes the Adams methods by the degree `q` of the interpolant: his
  formula with `q = 1`, (6.3), which he calls the two-step Adams–Bashforth formula, is
  `adamsBashforth 2` here.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1, 3.1; Mathlib `Lagrange.basis`, `Lagrange.eq_interpolate`,
`intervalIntegral`.

### 4.2 Backward differentiation formulas

Define `bdf k` by `α_j = (ℓ_j^(k+1))′(k)` and `β_k = 1`, `β_j = 0` for `j < k`: the derivative at
the last node of the interpolant of the values is set equal to the field there (Mirzaei 2024,
Section 6.3, (6.5)–(6.8); Süli 2022, Section 4.2 and Table 3, normalised by `α_k = 1`). Used for
`k ≥ 1`. Prove, for `0 < k`:

- `ρ = ∑_(1 ≤ j ≤ k) (1/j) X^(k−j) (X − 1)^j`; in particular `α_k = 1 + 1/2 + ⋯ + 1/k` and
  `α_0 = (−1)^k / k`;
- `bdf k` has order exactly `k` (`bdf_orderConditions`);
- `bdf k` satisfies the root condition for `1 ≤ k ≤ 6` (`bdf_rootCondition_of_le_six`).

The converse, that `bdf k` violates the root condition for every `k ≥ 7`, is the theorem of
Cryer (1972), with a short proof by Hairer and Wanner (1983); Süli 2022, Section 4.2, states it
without proof, and no openly readable proof was available for this roadmap, so it is not a target
here: the Checks verify the failure for `k = 7` and `k = 8` by computing the roots.

Route. Order: for `P` of degree at most `k`, `∑ α_j P(j)` is the derivative at `k` of the
interpolant of `P`, which is `P` (`Lagrange.eq_interpolate`), so it equals `P′(k) = ∑ β_j P′(j)`;
for `P = ω = ∏_(i ≤ k) (X − i)` the left side is `0` and `P′(k) = k!`. The formula for `ρ` follows
from the same exactness. Root condition: `α_0 = (−1)^k / k ≠ 0`, so `0` is not a root, and for
`ζ ≠ 0` and `u = 1/ζ` the formula gives `ρ(ζ) = ζ^k (1 − u) Q_k(u)` with
`Q_k(u) = ∑_(1 ≤ j ≤ k) (1 − u)^(j−1) / j`. Roots with `|ζ| ≥ 1` are points `|u| ≤ 1`; `u = 1` is
the simple root `ζ = 1` (as `Q_k(1) = 1`), so it suffices that `Q_k` has no zero in the closed
unit disc. Let `g_k = 1` for `k ≤ 5` and `g_6 = 5 + 4u`. On the unit circle, `u = e^(iθ)`, the
real part of `g_k Q_k` is a polynomial in `cos θ` of degree at most `6` with rational
coefficients that is positive on `[−1, 1]` (for `k = 5` it is
`(8/5)c⁴ − (21/5)c³ + (89/30)c² + (13/30)c + 1/5`; with `g_6 = 1 + u` it would vanish at
`c = −1`), a finite inequality of one real variable. The minimum principle, in the form of the
maximum modulus principle for `exp(−g_k Q_k)` on the closed disc
(`Complex.norm_le_of_forall_mem_frontier_norm_le`), makes the real part positive on the whole
closed disc, so `Q_k` does not vanish there. (Proved here.)

**Checks.**

- `bdf 1` is implicit Euler, `α = (−1, 1)`; `bdf 2` has `α = (1/2, −2, 3/2)`, that is
  `(1/3, −4/3, 1)` after division by `3/2`, with `β_2 = 2/3`; `bdf 3` divided by `11/6` is
  `(−2/11, 9/11, −18/11, 1)` with `β_3 = 6/11`, as in Süli 2022, Table 3.
- `bdf 6` has `ρ`-roots of modulus at most `0.8634` besides `1`; `bdf 7` has a pair of roots of
  modulus `1.0222…`, and `bdf 8` a pair of modulus `1.1839…`, so neither satisfies the root
  condition. Correspondingly the real part of `Q_6` alone is negative somewhere on the unit
  circle (its minimum is about `−1.37`), which is why `g_6` is needed in the route.
- `bdf 2`: the first failing condition is the one with `q = 3`: `∑ α_j j³ = −2 + 12 = 10` and
  `3 ∑ β_j j² = 12`, difference `−2`.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1; Mathlib `Lagrange.basis`, `Lagrange.eq_interpolate`,
`Polynomial.derivative`, `Polynomial.rootMultiplicity`,
`Complex.norm_le_of_forall_mem_frontier_norm_le`.

## Layer 5: the barriers and A-stability

### 5.1 The first barrier

Let `α_k ≠ 0` and `m.RootCondition`. Prove `¬ m.OrderConditions (k + 3)`, and
`¬ m.OrderConditions (k + 2)` when `k` is odd (`not_orderConditions_of_rootCondition`).

Route (Dahlquist 1956; Süli 2022, Lemma 3 and Theorem 12). The order conditions up to `p` say that
`ρ(e^h) − h σ(e^h) = O(h^(p+1))`, equivalently that `ρ(ζ)/log ζ − σ(ζ)` vanishes to order `p` at
`ζ = 1` (Lemma 3). The Cayley map `ζ = (1 + z)/(1 − z)` turns `ρ` and `σ` into polynomials
`r(z) = ((1 − z)/2)^k ρ(ζ)` and `s(z)` of degree at most `k`. The root condition makes `0` a simple
root of `r` (the assumed order conditions, up to `k + 2` or `k + 3`, contain consistency) and
puts every root of `r` in `Re z ≤ 0`, so the coefficients of `r` have one sign.
The coefficients `c_(2ν)` of `z / log((1 + z)/(1 − z)) = 1/2 − z²/6 − 2z⁴/45 − ⋯` are negative for
`ν ≥ 1`; Süli refers to Henrici for this, and here it is proved by Kaluza's lemma, since the
coefficients `a_ν = 1/(2ν + 1)` of `log((1 + z)/(1 − z)) / (2z)` in `z²` are positive and strictly
log-convex (`a_ν² < a_(ν−1) a_(ν+1)`): the reciprocal series `∑ b_ν z^(2ν)` satisfies
`b_(n+1) = ∑_(1 ≤ i ≤ n) (a_(n+1) a_(n−i) / a_n − a_(n+1−i)) b_i` with nonnegative weights, the
first one positive, and `b_1 = −1/3`, so every `b_ν` with `ν ≥ 1` is negative by induction.
Order `p` forces the coefficients of `z^j`, `k < j < p`, in `(z / log((1 + z)/(1 − z))) r(z) / z`
to vanish, since `s` has degree at most `k`. For odd `k` the sign information makes the
coefficient of `z^(k+1)` negative. For even `k` it makes the coefficient of `z^(k+1)` vanish only
when `r` is odd, which puts every root of `ρ` on the unit circle and `−1` among them (the degree
of `r` drops), and then the coefficient of `z^(k+2)` is negative. The hypothesis `α_k ≠ 0` is
used here: otherwise `r` has the factor `(1 − z)` and a root with positive real part.

**Checks.**

- The bound is attained: Simpson's method (`k = 2`, order `4`) and the trapezoidal rule (`k = 1`,
  order `2`).
- `α_k ≠ 0` is needed: the three-step coefficients `α = (−33, −24, 57, 0)`,
  `β = (10, 57, 24, −1)` satisfy the order conditions for `p = 5 = k + 2` with `k = 3` odd, and
  `ρ = 3(X − 1)(19X + 11)` satisfies the root condition.
- The root condition is needed: Süli's Example 4 c) has `k = 3` and order `6`.
- Süli 2022, Example 5: `ρ = (X − 1)(X − λ)` with `−1 ≤ λ < 1` has a unique `σ` of degree `2` with
  order at least `3`; the order is `4` exactly for `λ = −1` (Simpson), because the coefficient of
  the next condition is a nonzero multiple of `1 + λ`.

*Needs:* 1.2, 2.1; Mathlib `Polynomial.rootMultiplicity`.

### 5.2 Absolute stability and the second barrier

Define `m.stabilityRegion` as the set of `μ : ℂ` with `α_k − μ β_k ≠ 0` such that every root of
`ρ − μ σ` (in `ℂ`) has modulus `< 1`, and `m.IsAStable` as `{μ | Re μ < 0} ⊆ m.stabilityRegion`
(Süli 2022, Definitions 9–11). For `μ = h λ` in the region, every sequence generated for
`y' = λ y` on `ℂ` tends to `0`. Prove:

- API: if `μ` is in the region, every solution of `∑ (α_j − μ β_j) y_(n+j) = 0` tends to `0`; if
  `α_k − μ β_k ≠ 0` and some root has modulus `≥ 1`, some solution does not;
- no consistent explicit method is A-stable (`not_isAStable_of_isExplicit`);
- **the second barrier**: an A-stable method with `α_k ≠ 0` does not satisfy
  `OrderConditions 3` (`not_orderConditions_three_of_isAStable`).

Route. Explicit methods: if `α_k = 0` the leading coefficient `α_k − μ β_k` vanishes for every
`μ` and the region is empty. If `σ = 0`, consistency gives `ρ(1) = ρ′(1) = 0`, and the root `1`
of `ρ − μ σ = ρ` excludes every `μ`. Otherwise `σ` has some degree `d < k`, the polynomial
`ρ − μ σ` has leading coefficient `α_k ≠ 0` and coefficient `α_d − μ β_d` at `X^d`, so by Vieta's
formulas (`Polynomial.coeff_eq_esymm_roots_of_card`, over `ℂ`, where it splits) the elementary
symmetric function of degree `k − d` of its roots is unbounded as `μ → −∞` along the reals,
while it is at most a binomial coefficient when all roots lie in the unit disc.

Second barrier. A-stability gives, for `|ζ| > 1`: `ρ(ζ)` and `σ(ζ)` do not both vanish (else
`ζ` is a root for `μ = −1`), and `Re (ρ(ζ)/σ(ζ)) ≥ 0` when `σ(ζ) ≠ 0` (else `μ = ρ(ζ)/σ(ζ)` has
`Re μ < 0` and the root `ζ`). Hence `ρ` has no root in `|ζ| > 1`: at such a root `ρ/σ` is
analytic, vanishes and is not constant (`ρ ≠ 0` as `α_k ≠ 0`), so by the open mapping theorem
(`AnalyticAt.eventually_constant_or_nhds_le_map_nhds`) it takes values with negative real part
nearby. Also `σ(1) ≠ 0`, since `ρ(1) = σ(1) = 0` would make `1` a root for every `μ`. Map
`|ζ| > 1` onto `Re z > 0` by `ζ = (z + 1)/(z − 1)` and put `ψ(z) = σ(ζ)/ρ(ζ) − z/2`; the point
`z = 1` (`ζ = ∞`) is regular because `α_k ≠ 0`. Then `ψ` is a rational function, analytic on
`Re z > 0`, with `Re ψ(z) ≥ −Re z / 2` there. Order `3` means
`ρ(e^w) − w σ(e^w) = O(w^4)`, hence `σ(ζ)/ρ(ζ) = 1/log ζ + O((ζ − 1)^2)` near `ζ = 1`; on the
real axis `log ζ = 2 (1/x + 1/(3x³) + ⋯)` for `x > 1` (`Real.hasSum_log_sub_log_of_abs_lt_one`),
so `ψ(x) = −1/(6x) + O(x^(−2))` as `x → +∞`, and `ψ` tends to `0` at infinity. For `ε > 0` the
function `exp(−ψ(z + ε))` is bounded and continuous on `Re z ≥ 0` and has modulus at most
`e^(ε/2)` on the imaginary axis, so the Phragmén–Lindelöf principle
(`PhragmenLindelof.right_half_plane_of_bounded_on_real`) gives `Re ψ ≥ −ε/2` on `Re z ≥ ε`.
Letting `ε → 0`, `Re ψ ≥ 0` on `Re z > 0`, which contradicts `ψ(x) < 0` for large real `x`.
(Dahlquist 1963 proved the barrier through the Riesz–Herglotz representation, and order stars
give another proof, as Wanner 2006 recounts; Süli 2022, Theorem 16 (ii), states it without
proof. The route above is proved here.)

**Checks.**

- Explicit Euler: the region is the open disc `|1 + μ| < 1`; `μ = −2` gives the root `−1` and is
  not in it. The stability region of a Runge–Kutta method in OneStepMethodsForODEs 5.2 uses
  `‖R(μ)‖ ≤ 1` and contains `−2` for explicit Euler: the two notions differ on the boundary.
- Implicit Euler: the root is `1/(1 − μ)` and the region is `{μ | 1 < ‖1 − μ‖}`; `μ = 1` is
  excluded by `α_1 − μ β_1 = 0`.
- Consistency is needed in the second item: `α = (−1/2, 1)`, `β = (0, 0)` is explicit, and
  `ρ − μ σ = X − 1/2` for every `μ`, so it is A-stable.
- The two-step Adams–Bashforth method is not A-stable: for `μ = −1`,
  `ρ − μ σ = X² + X/2 − 1/2` has the root `−1`.
- Simpson's method, of order `4`, is not A-stable: for `μ = −1/2`, `ρ − μ σ` is a multiple of
  `7X² + 4X − 5`, with the root `(−2 − √39)/7 = −1.178…`.

*Needs:* 1.1, 1.2; Mathlib `Polynomial.roots`, `Polynomial.map`, `IsAlgClosed.splits`,
`Polynomial.coeff_eq_esymm_roots_of_card`, `AnalyticAt.eventually_constant_or_nhds_le_map_nhds`,
`PhragmenLindelof.right_half_plane_of_bounded_on_real`, `Real.hasSum_log_sub_log_of_abs_lt_one`.

### 5.3 A-stable methods of order two

Prove that `bdf 2` is A-stable (`isAStable_bdf_two`).

Route. For `bdf 2`, `σ = X²` and the leading coefficient `3/2 − μ` does not vanish when
`Re μ < 0`. A root `ζ` of `ρ − μ σ` with `|ζ| ≥ 1` would give `μ = ρ(ζ)/ζ² = 3/2 − 2w + w²/2` with
`w = 1/ζ`, `|w| ≤ 1`. Writing `w = x + iy`, the real part is
`3/2 − 2x + (x² − y²)/2 ≥ 3/2 − 2x + (2x² − 1)/2 = (1 − x)² ≥ 0`, using `y² ≤ 1 − x²`; this
contradicts `Re μ < 0`. (Mirzaei 2024, Section 6.5, states that the backward differentiation
formulas with `k = 1, 2` are A-stable; proved here.)

**Checks.**

- The trapezoidal rule `α = (−1, 1)`, `β = (1/2, 1/2)`: the single root of `ρ − μ σ` is
  `(1 + μ/2)/(1 − μ/2)`, the stability function of the trapezoidal Runge–Kutta method of
  OneStepMethodsForODEs 5.2, and the leading coefficient is `1 − μ/2`; so its region here is the
  open left half-plane, while the region there, defined with `≤`, is the closed one.

- `bdf 3` is not A-stable, as 5.2 predicts from its order `3`: at `ζ = e^(iπ/3)`,
  `ρ(ζ)/ζ³ = −1/12 + (3√3/4) i`, so `μ = −1/12 + (3√3/4) i` has a root on the unit circle.
- Implicit Euler (`bdf 1`) is A-stable; the trapezoidal rule and `bdf 2` attain the bound `2` of
  5.2.

*Needs:* 4.2, 5.2; Mathlib `Polynomial.roots`.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns linear multistep methods on uniform meshes in a real normed space: the
characteristic polynomials, the order conditions and the defect bound, the root condition and
its equivalence with zero-stability, the discrete stability theorem, Dahlquist's equivalence
theorem and the rate of convergence, the Adams and backward differentiation families with their
orders and zero-stability, the first barrier, the region of absolute stability and A-stability
of multistep methods, the second barrier and the A-stability of the two-step backward
differentiation formula.

Already in the libraries, and not targets here: linear recurrences with their solution space and
geometric solutions (Mathlib `LinearRecurrence`), Lagrange interpolation (`Lagrange.basis`,
`Lagrange.eq_interpolate`), Taylor's theorem (`taylor_mean_remainder_lagrange`). Every theorem
here takes the solution of the differential equation as a hypothesis; existence and uniqueness
are Mathlib's `IsPicardLindelof.exists_eq_forall_mem_Icc_hasDerivWithinAt` and
`ODE_solution_unique`, and, for fields measurable in time,
DiracScatteringAndNonlinearFourierTransform 1.7; none is restated.

Left to other roadmaps:

- OneStepMethodsForODEs owns meshes with variable steps, one-step methods and their defect, the
  discrete stability and convergence theorems for one-step methods (1.3–1.5), Runge–Kutta methods,
  and the stability function, stability region and A-stability of Runge–Kutta methods (5.1, 5.2).
  This roadmap adopts its setting for differential equations; its methods and theorems are used
  only in Checks.
  Its explicit and implicit Euler and trapezoidal methods are the linear one-step methods `k = 1`
  here, for which 2.2 and 3.2 give bounds of the same kind on uniform meshes with other constants;
  neither contains the other, since there the steps vary and the methods are Runge–Kutta methods,
  and here `k` is arbitrary. The stability region of 5.2 here is that of a multistep method, with
  strict inequality; theirs is that of a stability function, with `≤`. A-stability here asks
  for the open left half-plane `re μ < 0` (5.2), theirs (5.2) for the closed one `re z ≤ 0`.
- ExponentialIntegrators leaves exponential multistep methods out of its scope; they are not
  treated here either.
- The convergence to zero of the powers of a matrix with spectral radius below `1`, the subject
  of IterativeMethodsForLinearSystems 1.2, is not treated or used here. Zero-stability is the
  boundedness of the powers of the companion matrix of `ρ`, a different property, and the route
  of 2.1 uses solutions of the recurrence, not that matrix.
- The interpolation error formula, the subject of PolynomialInterpolationAndBestApproximation
  1.1, is not treated or used here. The Adams and backward differentiation coefficients of
  Layer 4 are defined through Mathlib's Lagrange basis, and their orders use only exactness of
  interpolation on polynomials.

Out of scope: variable step sizes and variable order, predictor–corrector pairs and their
iteration, the solvability of the implicit equation of an implicit step, general linear methods
and multistep Runge–Kutta methods, A(α)-stability and stiff stability, G-stability and one-leg
methods, Nyström and Milne–Simpson methods as families, the optimality of the error constant of
the trapezoidal rule, the Schur and Routh–Hurwitz criteria as general theorems, methods for
second-order equations, and floating-point arithmetic.
