# Roadmap: finite reflection invariants, degrees, and Weyl exponents

This roadmap supplies the invariant-theory results used by the Weyl/representation and compact-Lie topology roadmaps. It has two related but distinct branches: (I) finite complex pseudoreflection groups over characteristic zero, including both directions of the Shephard–Todd theorem; and (II) finite real reflection groups, with the crystallographic Weyl specialization that connects invariant degrees to Coxeter exponents and root heights. The second branch does not broaden to all complex reflection groups. In particular, Coxeter eigenvalue, coprime-exponent, and root-height statements require a finite crystallographic root system and are stated componentwise on its irreducible semisimple components.

The source uses “reflection” for a finite-order complex linear transformation whose fixed space has codimension one. In the complex branch use **pseudoreflection** for this notion; a real orthogonal reflection is the real codimension-one involution. Fix polynomial functions as `Sym(V*)`, with the contragredient action. “Polynomial invariant ring” means a graded polynomial algebra on homogeneous positive-degree generators. The generators are not canonical; the multiset of their degrees is. State the field, finite-dimensionality, faithfulness, and characteristic hypotheses at each theorem, rather than silently moving between integral, modular, real, and complex coefficients.

## 1. Finite-group invariant algebra used by both branches

Build a reusable finite-group action layer first. For a finite group acting linearly on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field, construct the induced graded action on `Sym(V*)`, its fixed graded subalgebra, and the Reynolds operator when `|G|` is invertible in the coefficient field. Prove that the Reynolds operator is a degree-preserving `K`-linear projection onto invariants, is linear over the invariant subalgebra, and commutes with scalar extension in the stated finite-free setting. It is generally not multiplicative and is not an algebra homomorphism; the fixed-subalgebra inclusion is the algebra homomorphism. This is a characteristic-not-dividing-order assertion; it does not give a modular averaging operator when the characteristic divides `|G|`.

State Molien's series with the actual field and representation hypotheses, and prove it from the graded trace formula for the Reynolds projector:

`Hilb(Sym(V*)^G,t) = |G|⁻¹ Σ_(g∈G) det(1 − t g|V*)⁻¹`.

The formula is an identity of formal power series over a characteristic-zero splitting field (or after a specified scalar extension where the eigenvalues exist). It is not, by itself, a proof of polynomiality. Give the Noether degree bound for generators of the invariant algebra for finite groups over the applicable nonmodular field, and distinguish that bound from a basic-invariant degree list. Prove finite generation and consume Mathlib's actual finite-action integral-extension theorem after identifying its fixed-subalgebra/invariant-extension hypotheses with this polynomial action; that theorem uses the monic orbit polynomial. Do not duplicate the generic orbit-polynomial proof. Record the Cohen–Macaulay/regular-sequence consequences only with their actual hypotheses and supplier, not as an unnamed consequence of averaging.

For a finite group in characteristic dividing its order, retain the invariant subalgebra and its grading, but do not claim Reynolds splitting, Molien averaging, polynomiality, a free-module decomposition, or the characteristic-zero coinvariant theorem without a separate modular hypothesis and proof.

## 2. Complex branch: Shephard–Todd in both directions

Let `K` be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, `V` a finite-dimensional `K`-vector space, and `G ≤ GL(V)` finite and faithful. A pseudoreflection is a nonidentity `g` with `codim_K (V^g)=1`. Construct the contragredient action on `Sym(V*)` and its graded fixed subalgebra.

Prove both implications:

1. If `G` is generated by pseudoreflections, then `Sym(V*)^G` is a polynomial algebra on `dim(V)` homogeneous algebraically independent invariants.
2. If the invariant algebra `Sym(V*)^G` is a polynomial algebra, then every element of `G` is generated by pseudoreflections.

The reverse implication is essential. Give the proof through the quotient map and its codimension-one ramification: show that the Jacobian of a basic invariant system has divisor supported exactly on reflecting hyperplanes with the correct ramification multiplicities; show that absence of nonreflection stabilizers in codimension one forces the quotient to be smooth only when the group is generated by those inertia groups. Do not replace this with “the quotient is smooth, hence a reflection group” without constructing the local quotient/ramification argument. Prove the inertia/stabilizer correspondence at codimension one and the no-divisorial-branch implication for the residual quotient. The cited purity-of-branch-locus lemma has hypotheses: finite dominant, generically separable, quasi-finite map from a normal locally Noetherian source to a regular target, equal local dimensions, and unramifiedness in codimension one. Prove these for the residual affine-space map. Then establish the connected finite étale affine-space cover is trivial over the algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Treat nonfaithful representations by first passing to the faithful image and explicitly identify the kernel action on the invariant algebra.

For the forward implication, first establish finite generation and finiteness: prove that each element of `Sym(V*)` is integral over the fixed algebra by its monic orbit polynomial, then prove the fixed algebra is finitely generated and the ambient algebra is finite as a graded module over it. These are construction targets before either direction of the theorem. Let `S=Sym_K(V*)`, `A=S^G`, and `k=A/A_+`. Use Mathlib’s `CategoryTheory.Tor'` construction in `ModuleCat`, which derives the first tensor factor, and set `T=Tor'_1^A(k,S)` using a projective resolution of `k`; do not silently identify this with `CategoryTheory.Tor`, which derives the second factor and has no supplied balancing isomorphism here. For each pseudoreflection `s`, choose a linear form `ℓ` cutting out its fixed hyperplane and construct the degree-minus-one operator `δ_s(f)=(f−s(f))/ℓ`; prove divisibility, `A`-linearity, and the operator identity `1−s = m_ℓ ∘ δ_s` on `S` and on the induced Tor groups. Use a bounded-below graded free `A`-resolution of `k` with trivial `G` action on its free bases to study `T=Tor'_1^A(k,S)`. The `A`-linear map `δ_s:S→S` induces a degree-minus-one map on `T`; multiplication by `ℓ` is also `A`-linear and induces the map needed for the identity. If `T` had a nonzero homogeneous element of least degree, every `δ_s` would kill it, so the identity would make it fixed by every generating pseudoreflection. Exact Reynolds invariants on the specified resolution identify `T^G` with `Tor'_1^A(k,A)=0`, a contradiction. Prove exactness and homology-commutation for this resolution; do not assert that invariants commute with arbitrary Tor. Then prove from `T=0` that a minimal finite graded free presentation of `S` over `A` has zero kernel, so `S` is free and faithfully flat over `A`. Prove the Noetherian regularity-descent lemma for this faithfully flat extension and apply it to the regular polynomial algebra `S`; do not mistake `RingTheory.Regular.Flat` for this descent theorem, since it concerns regular-sequence base change. At the local vertex, use `IsRegularLocalRing.iff_finrank_cotangentSpace` after constructing the local-ring instance and the cotangent-space comparison. To compute the local dimension at the positive graded vertex, first prove that the finite quotient map has a unique point over the vertex: invariants separate distinct finite `G`-orbits, so the orbit of a point in that fiber is the origin. Prove this orbit-separation statement from finite-orbit interpolation and averaging. Equivalently, establish `rad(A_+S)=S_+`; the quotient `S/A_+S` is finite-dimensional graded and its positive-degree elements are nilpotent, while orbit separation excludes any other maximal ideal over `A_+`. Finite integral local dimension comparison then gives `dim A_(A_+) = dim S_(S_+) = dim(V)`. Prove the vertex cotangent dimension is `dim(V)` using regularity. Lift a homogeneous basis of `A_+/(A_+)²`; graded Nakayama gives a polynomial-algebra epimorphism on exactly `dim(V)` variables, and dimension plus the prime kernel forces it to be an isomorphism. Handle dimension zero separately. This route constructs polynomiality without assuming a basic system and without a local-stabilizer induction at the origin. The divisibility operator is `m_ℓ∘δ_s` (multiplication by `ℓ` after `δ_s`); no order-two hypothesis is used for complex pseudoreflections. After both directions of the theorem are established, construct the point-stabilizer generation theorem and the completed-local quotient comparison as reusable APIs; if the stabilizer proof uses the converse, it follows that converse and is not one of its premises.

For a chosen homogeneous basic system, prove the precise Jacobian formula J=c·∏_H ℓ_H^(e_H−1), where c≠0, H ranges over the reflecting hyperplanes, ℓ_H is a nonzero defining linear form, and e_H is the order of its pointwise stabilizer. Prove the cyclic normal-coordinate model z↦z^(e_H), the codimension-one ramification comparison and the chain-rule multiplicities; changes of the defining forms alter only the nonzero scalar c. This calculation follows polynomiality and does not assume a basic system in order to prove its existence.

For any chosen homogeneous basic invariants `f₁,…,f_r`, prove `r=dim(V)`, positive degrees, algebraic independence, and that the degrees are intrinsic as a multiset. Give the examples from the source: `S_n` on the full permutation representation has elementary-symmetric degrees `1,…,n`; on its trace-zero reflection representation the degrees are `2,…,n`. State the exact distinction between the two representations, including the trivial-line factor; do not call the degree-one invariant a convention artifact. Include the rank-zero and rank-one cases.

For the converse, let `H` be generated by the pseudoreflections in `G`. Construct the finite quotient maps `V → V/H → V/G` and prove the stabilizer/inertia correspondence: the height-one inertia groups of `V → V/G` are precisely the cyclic pointwise stabilizers of reflecting hyperplanes, hence lie in `H`. Apply the already established forward implication to `H`, so `V/H ≃ A^n`; the point-stabilizer and completed-local APIs are subsequent consumers, not premises of the forward proof. The assumed polynomiality of `Sym(V*)^G` identifies `V/G ≃ A^n` as well. Prove that the induced `G/H` action on `V/H` is faithful: for the faithful finite linear action, the rational-function-field extension `K(V)/K(V)^G` is Galois with group `G`, and the subgroup fixing `K(V)^H` is exactly `H`. Then prove the residual finite map `A^n ≃ V/H → V/G ≃ A^n` is generically separable and unramified at every codimension-one point. Apply purity in the precise form for a finite dominant generically separable map from a normal locally Noetherian source to a regular target, quasi-finite with equal local dimensions and unramified in codimension one; these hypotheses imply it is étale. Finally prove/apply that a connected finite étale cover of affine space over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field is trivial. The source is connected and normal, and the generic degree is `|G/H|`, so `G/H=1`. Handle `n=0` separately (faithfulness forces `G=1`). The purity statement alone does not provide the stabilizer correspondence or the affine-space cover theorem; those are explicit targets with these hypotheses.

The complex Shephard–Todd theorem does not supply a Coxeter element, a Coxeter number, a crystallographic root system, or the Weyl coprime-exponent theorem. Noncrystallographic real groups may be treated as real finite reflection groups in the invariant-ring branch, but root-height and Lie-group conclusions remain outside their scope.

## 3. Real reflection and Weyl invariant package

Let `W` be a finite real reflection group acting faithfully and orthogonally on a finite-dimensional real inner-product space `V`. Establish the real invariant theorem and then extend scalars to `ℂ` for the graded algebra statements. Choose basic homogeneous invariants with degrees `d₁,…,d_r`; put `eᵢ=dᵢ−1`. Make clear that a different basic system changes generators but not this multiset.

For the actual finite orthogonal group W generated by reflections, put E=(V^W)⊥. Construct the reflecting-hyperplane arrangement on E and choose a chamber C. Prove its closure is a simplicial cone, its wall reflections belong to W, and W acts simply transitively on chambers. With these wall reflections as generators and m(s,t)=order(st), construct a native `CoxeterSystem` on the same group W, proving generation and the Coxeter presentation. Reuse RootSystems Layer3's abstract exchange/reduced-word APIs. Define ℓ_C(w) through this system and prove it equals the number of hyperplanes separating C and wC. Prove compatibility under conjugating the chosen chamber; use this length in the Hilbert identity below. Fixed directions contribute degree-one polynomial generators and cancel from the coinvariant Hilbert series. Treat E=0 by the trivial group and empty generating set. In the crystallographic specialization, identify this construction with RootSystems' existing Weyl/Coxeter system.

Construct the positive-degree ideal `I = Sym(V*)^W_+ · Sym(V*)` and the coinvariant algebra `C_W=Sym(V*)/I`. Prove, rather than merely cite, the following package or name and pin the exact theorem used at each step:

- `Hilb(Sym(V*)^W,t)=∏ᵢ(1−t^{dᵢ})⁻¹`;
- `Hilb(C_W,t)=∏ᵢ(1−t^{dᵢ})/(1−t)^r = Σ_(w∈W)t^{ℓ(w)}`;
- `Sym(V*)` is a free module over `Sym(V*)^W` of rank `|W|`, with a homogeneous harmonic/coinvariant complement and the multiplication comparison that gives the free basis;
- as an ungraded `W`-module, the coinvariant algebra is the regular representation; separately compute its graded character (the fake-degree refinement), rather than calling the graded module itself the ungraded regular representation;
- `∏ᵢ dᵢ=|W|` and `Σᵢ dᵢ=r+|Ref(W)|`, where `Ref(W)` is the set of distinct reflections, not reflecting hyperplanes counted with an unstated multiplicity.

The proof route must expose the difficult algebra. Identify the Jacobian/discriminant as the product of linear forms of reflecting hyperplanes with the correct multiplicities; prove the invariant quotient is finite and regular exactly in the reflection-generated case; prove the basic invariants form a homogeneous system of parameters and a regular sequence in the polynomial ring; build the harmonic complement and show its multiplication map over the invariant ring is an isomorphism; then establish the ungraded regular-representation theorem and the graded fake-degree character formula as distinct statements. A Noether bound or Molien series is useful input but is not a substitute for these steps. If a general finite-reflection theorem supplies a step, identify its exact characteristic-zero statement and prove the Weyl specialization rather than hiding all of the coinvariant argument behind the theorem name.

For a compact connected Lie group with maximal torus of dimension `n`, include one degree-one invariant for each central fixed direction. The flag Poincaré polynomial is

`P_(G/B)(q) = [∏ᵢ(1−q^(2dᵢ))]/(1−q²)^n = ∏ᵢ[(1−q^(2dᵢ))/(1−q²)]`.

The denominator is raised to the total torus rank `n`, and the two expressions are equivalent. If one omits the central fixed directions, the formula reduces to the semisimple rank `r` and only semisimple degrees remain. Each central direction contributes a degree-one invariant which cancels one factor. This is a separate central factor, not a positive root or an exponent of an irreducible Coxeter system.

### Character-lattice bridge (before topology)

For a compact connected `G` and maximal torus `T`, consume LieGroups Layer5A/Layer6's geometric Weyl action and root/character pairing, and identify the rational character space `X*(T) ⊗ ℚ` with that same Weyl representation used by the RootSystems/LHW root datum. Extend scalars to `ℝ` for the orthogonal reflection geometry, and record the central fixed subspace separately. This roadmap does not define a second geometric Weyl action or root/character identification; T1 supplies the later integral character-period and global-form refinement.

Construct the fixed-subalgebra inclusion `Sym_ℚ(X*(T) ⊗ ℚ)^W ↪ Sym_ℚ(X*(T) ⊗ ℚ)` and the presentation equivalence `Sym_ℚ(ℚ^n) ≃ Sym_ℚ(X*(T) ⊗ ℚ)^W` given by the chosen homogeneous basic invariants. The presentation has `n=rank(T)` polynomial variables, including all `dim(Z(G)^0)` fixed central directions; only the semisimple factor uses `r=rank([G,G])`. Thus for `S¹`, `W` is trivial and the full invariant algebra is `ℚ[t]`, not `Sym(ℚ⁰)`. Give the polynomial source the weighted grading with generator degrees `dᵢ`; the ordinary symmetric-algebra grading assigning each basis vector degree one is not the grading for which this evaluation is graded. Prove the presentation is a graded algebra equivalence and transport its degree multiset under rational-to-real scalar extension using the finite-group Reynolds/base-change comparison. The corresponding map on `H*(BT;ℚ)` sends each character to its degree-two class. CharacteristicClasses C5.1 consumes this invariant restriction, while C5.2–C5.3 own the rational Borel/classifying-space comparison. These are late consumers, not inputs to the algebraic construction.

## 4. Crystallographic Weyl/Coxeter dictionary

Consume `RootPairing`, its finite reduced crystallographic hypotheses, `RootPairing.Base`, positive roots and heights, the Weyl group, the finite-type classification, and the Coxeter system/presentation from [RootSystems](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md). This roadmap owns the invariant degrees and the bridge from those degrees to the already-owned Coxeter/root data; it does not duplicate the Coxeter presentation or root-system classification. In particular, RootSystems' length Poincaré polynomial is `Σ_w t^{ℓ(w)}`; the product identity above is a new degree-dependent theorem and must be proved as such.

For an irreducible finite crystallographic root system of semisimple rank `r`, prove that the degrees give exponents `eᵢ=dᵢ−1`, and establish the following mutually compatible statements:

- the Coxeter number is `h=|Φ|/r` (equivalently `|Φ⁺|=rh/2`), and a Coxeter element has order `h`;
- its eigenvalues on the reflection representation are `exp(2πi eᵢ/h)` with multiplicity;
- for every `j≥1`, `#{α∈Φ⁺ : ht(α)=j}=#{i:eᵢ≥j}`;
- the height of the highest root is `h−1`, derived from the height-partition identity and the maximal exponent, and checked against the actual highest-root coefficient model;
- if `1≤e<h` and `gcd(e,h)=1`, then `e` occurs among the exponents.

The classification examples should be stated as multisets, with coincidences retained (not silently deduplicated):

| Type | Basic degrees `dᵢ` | Exponents `eᵢ` | `h` |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `A_r` | `2,3,…,r+1` | `1,2,…,r` | `r+1` |
| `B_r`, `C_r` | `2,4,…,2r` | `1,3,…,2r−1` | `2r` |
| `D_r` (`r≥4`) | `2,4,…,2r−2,r` | `1,3,…,2r−3,r−1` | `2r−2` |
| `E₆` | `2,5,6,8,9,12` | `1,4,5,7,8,11` | `12` |
| `E₇` | `2,6,8,10,12,14,18` | `1,5,7,9,11,13,17` | `18` |
| `E₈` | `2,8,12,14,18,20,24,30` | `1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29` | `30` |
| `F₄` | `2,6,8,12` | `1,5,7,11` | `12` |
| `G₂` | `2,6` | `1,5` | `6` |

For `A_r`, the full permutation representation adds the fixed degree `1`; for `B_r` and `C_r`, the Weyl groups agree, while the root data and long/short root bookkeeping remain distinct in the RootSystems owner. These lists are acceptance examples and useful checks of the general construction, not a substitute for the stated general proof route.

These are irreducible crystallographic statements. For a reducible semisimple root system, apply them separately to each irreducible component. The order of the product Coxeter element is the least common multiple of component Coxeter numbers; do not invent one aggregate Coxeter number from total roots divided by total rank. A reductive central subspace contributes fixed degree-one directions and exponent zero only in the invariant/topological polynomial bookkeeping; it has no Coxeter number, positive-root height, or contribution to `|Φ|/r` for a semisimple component.

The degree-to-Coxeter comparison has two acceptable proof routes, and each must be completed rather than used as a citation stub. The uniform route proves the regular-eigenvector theorem: construct a primitive `h`-eigenvector off the reflecting hyperplanes; prove the invariant quotient map is unramified there; use homogeneous basic invariants and their differentials at that point to identify the Coxeter eigenvalues as `exp(2πi(dᵢ−1)/h)`. The alternative is an explicit irreducible-type classification using the actual root/Coxeter matrices, basic invariant systems, and characteristic polynomials, with the rank-two and exceptional cases checked. For the height partition, derive the equality from the Weyl denominator/graded coinvariant identity or from the same explicit type tables; do not infer a whole distribution from equality of total root count. Derive `h=1+ht(θ)` for the actual highest root `θ`. For the coprime property, show the Coxeter matrix is integral in the crystallographic root basis, has a primitive `h`-th eigenvalue, and its characteristic polynomial contains all Galois conjugates; then connect those eigenvalues to the invariant degrees by the proved degree/Coxeter theorem. This last implication is not available for `H₃` or other noncrystallographic groups.

The coprime-exponent theorem is **not** stated for arbitrary real reflection groups. The H₃ system has `h=10` and exponents `1,5,9`, so the coprime residues `3,7` do not occur. For the Weyl theorem, give the integral crystallographic argument: select a primitive `h`-th Coxeter eigenvalue, use the integral reflection/Coxeter matrix to transport its cyclotomic conjugates, and prove that the degree/exponent multiset obtained from the invariant ring matches the characteristic roots with multiplicities. The existence of a primitive eigenvalue alone does not prove the matching of the degree multiset or the entire coprime assertion.

For reducible crystallographic systems, state the coprime assertion componentwise with that component's `h`; do not apply it to the lcm of component Coxeter numbers. For the noncrystallographic groups `H₃`, `H₄`, and `I₂(m)`, retain invariant degrees and Coxeter eigenvalue data only when separately proved; omit the crystallographic coprime and root-height statements.

- the Weyl coprime-exponent assertion in crystallographic scope;
- the root-count/rank Coxeter number and positive-root-height partition;
- the highest-root height consequence and the `SU(n)` checks;

Make rational topology a late consumer only. This roadmap proves no integral torsion-freeness and does not replace compact-group/classifying-space, transfer, Borel-fibration, or transgression arguments. The flag cohomology product must be connected to the existing flag-cell/cohomology owner with its exact compact connected hypotheses.

## Dependency order

1. Finite-group invariant algebra: induced graded action, fixed subalgebra, Reynolds/Molien, finite generation and the bounded Noether result.
2. Complex pseudoreflection branch: codimension-one local quotient/ramification and the two directions of Shephard–Todd; basic-invariant degrees/examples.
3. Real finite reflection specialization: Jacobian/discriminant, homogeneous parameters, regular sequence, harmonic complement, free-module and regular-representation proof; Hilbert/product identities.
4. Weyl specialization: identify the real reflection representation and positive roots from the RootSystems owner, prove degrees/exponents and component bookkeeping.
5. Coxeter/eigenvalue/height package and the crystallographic coprime theorem, with `E₈`, type-A, `U(n)`, `SU(n)`, and affine-colouring examples.
6. Separate consumer adapters to LHW7 Chevalley/Harish–Chandra, RootSystems' Coxeter length polynomial, flag cohomology, and compact-Lie rational topology. No consumer is a premise for the invariant algebra proof.

## Boundaries

There is no integral or modular analogue asserted here without separate hypotheses and theorems. There is no assertion that every finite group has a polynomial invariant ring, no claim that a nonreflection finite group satisfies Shephard–Todd, and no use of “polynomial” without a graded algebra/degree statement. No generic complex-reflection Coxeter number or root-height package is defined. Rational classifying-space/exterior-cohomology statements remain in the topology owners. Each displayed theorem is a construction target; a signature, compiled declaration, or admitted proof does not discharge it.
