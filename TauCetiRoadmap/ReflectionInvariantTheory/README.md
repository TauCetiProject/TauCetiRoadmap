# Roadmap: finite reflection invariants, degrees, and Weyl exponents

**Summary.** A finite group `G` acting linearly on a finite-dimensional vector space `V` over a
field `K` acts on the polynomial functions `Sym(V*)`, and the invariant polynomial functions form a
graded subalgebra `Sym(V*)^G`. This roadmap asks when that algebra is a polynomial algebra and what
the degrees of its generators say about the group. An element of `G` is a *pseudoreflection* when
its fixed space is a hyperplane; for a finite real group these are the orthogonal reflections. When
`Sym(V*)^G` is polynomial on homogeneous generators, the generators are not canonical but the
multiset of their degrees `d₁, …, dₙ` is; the `eᵢ = dᵢ − 1` are the exponents.

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero the roadmap proves the **Shephard–Todd
theorem**: for `G` acting faithfully, `Sym(V*)^G` is a polynomial algebra if and only if `G` is
generated by pseudoreflections. For a finite real reflection group `W` acting on `ℝⁿ` it proves
`d₁ ⋯ dₙ = |W|` and `d₁ + ⋯ + dₙ = n + |Ref(W)|`, `Ref(W)` the set of reflections; that the
coinvariant algebra, the quotient of `Sym(V*)` by the invariants of positive degree, has Hilbert
series `∏ᵢ (1 + t + ⋯ + t^(dᵢ−1)) = Σ_w t^ℓ(w)`, `ℓ` the length for the walls of a chamber; and that
it is the regular representation of `W`. For the Weyl group of an irreducible crystallographic root
system of rank `r`, with Coxeter number `h = |Φ|/r`, a Coxeter element has order `h` and eigenvalues
`exp(2πi eᵢ/h)`; `#{i : eᵢ ≥ j}` positive roots have height `j`; the highest root has height
`h − 1`; and every `e` with `1 ≤ e < h` prime to `h` is an exponent. None of these is claimed for a
reducible system as a whole or for the noncrystallographic types.

The Tau Ceti library supplies the starting point: the Reynolds operator, Molien's formula,
Noether's finiteness theorem and the forward direction of the Shephard–Todd theorem.
[RootSystems](../RepresentationTheory/RootSystems/README.md) and the library supply bases, heights,
the highest root and the Coxeter presentation of Weyl groups;
[PurityOfBranchLocus](../PurityOfBranchLocus/README.md) the purity theorem used in the converse;
[LieGroups](../RepresentationTheory/LieGroups/README.md) the Weyl group of a compact connected Lie
group and the Bruhat cells of its flag manifold, whose Poincaré polynomial the degrees evaluate.
[CharacteristicClasses](../CharacteristicClasses/README.md) turns the degrees into the rational
cohomology of classifying spaces and compact Lie groups.

Layer 1 is the invariant algebra of any finite linear group; Layer 2 the Shephard–Todd theorem, the
Jacobian of basic invariants and the stabilizers of points; Layer 3 finite real reflection groups,
from chambers to the coinvariant algebra, the degree identities and compact Lie groups; Layer 4 the
dictionary between degrees and the root data of Weyl groups; Layer 5 classical examples.

## Conventions

1. **The standing data.** `K` is a field, `V` a finite-dimensional `K`-vector space and `G` a group
   acting by `ρ : Representation K G V`. Finiteness of `G`, invertibility of `|G|` in `K`, the
   characteristic, algebraic closedness and faithfulness are hypotheses of the statements that use
   them, never folded into a definition.
2. **Polynomial functions.** `S = Sym(V*)` is Mathlib's `SymmetricAlgebra K (Module.Dual K V)`,
   graded by Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.homogeneousSubmodule`. `G` acts on it through the
   contragredient representation `ρ.dual` (Mathlib's `Representation.dual`) extended
   multiplicatively, `ρ.dual.symmetricAlgebra`: `(g · f)(v) = f(g⁻¹ v)`. The invariant ring is
   `A = S^G = ρ.dual.symmetricAlgebraInvariants`, the subalgebra cut out by the equations
   `g · f = f` for all `g`. `A₊` is the ideal of invariants with zero constant term; the *Hilbert
   ideal* is `I = A₊ · S` (`ρ.dual.symmetricAlgebraHilbertIdeal`) and the *coinvariant algebra* is
   `C = S/I`, graded, with the induced action of `G`.
3. **Hilbert series.** For a graded vector space `X = ⊕ₖ Xₖ` with finite-dimensional pieces,
   `Hilb(X, t) = Σₖ dim Xₖ tᵏ ∈ ℤ⟦t⟧`. The degree-`k` part of `C` is the image of `Sₖ`.
4. **Pseudoreflections.** `g ∈ G` is a pseudoreflection when its fixed space
   `(ρ g).fixedSubmodule` (Mathlib's `LinearMap.fixedSubmodule`) has codimension one; it can have
   any order. `ρ.pseudoreflectionSubgroup` is the subgroup they generate, and "`G` is generated by
   pseudoreflections" means `ρ.pseudoreflectionSubgroup = ⊤`. A *reflecting hyperplane* is the fixed
   space `H` of a pseudoreflection; `ℓ_H` is a nonzero linear form with kernel `H`, and `e_H` is the
   order of the pointwise stabilizer of `H`.
5. **Polynomial invariant rings.** `A` is a *polynomial algebra* when some family
   `f : Fin m → A` makes `MvPolynomial.aeval f : K[X₁, …, Xₘ] → A` bijective. A *system of basic
   invariants* with degrees `d₁, …, dₙ` is such a family of homogeneous invariants, `fᵢ` of degree
   `dᵢ` (`IsBasicInvariants`, §2.1). The *exponents* are `eᵢ = dᵢ − 1`. Degrees and exponents are
   multisets: coincidences are kept, as in type `D₄`, whose degree `4` occurs twice.
6. **Real reflection groups.** In Layer 3, `W` is a finite group acting faithfully on a
   finite-dimensional real vector space `V`, `n = dim V`, by `ρ : Representation ℝ W V`, and is
   generated by pseudoreflections. Averaging an inner product over `W` gives a `W`-invariant one,
   fixed once and for all; for it every pseudoreflection of `W` is the orthogonal reflection in its
   hyperplane, of order two. `Ref(W)` is the set of these reflections, counted as group elements.
   `V^W` is the fixed space and `E = (V^W)^⊥` its orthogonal complement. Basic invariants of `W`
   include one of degree `1` for each fixed direction; no statement drops them silently.
7. **Root systems.** In Layer 4, `P : RootPairing ι K M N` is a finite (`[Finite ι]`), reduced,
   crystallographic root pairing over a field `K` of characteristic zero, in Mathlib's sense, with
   a base `b : P.Base`; `Φ` is the set of roots, `Φ⁺` the positive roots (`b.IsPos`), `ht` the
   height `b.height`, `r = b.support.card` the rank and `θ = b.highestRoot` the highest root. `W` is
   `P.weylGroup`, acting on `M` by `P.weylGroupRootRep`; its simple reflections are
   `RootPairing.weylGroup.ofIdx P i` for `i ∈ b.support`, and its Coxeter system is
   `TauCeti.weylCoxeterSystem P b`. The degrees of `P` are those of `W` acting on `M`. "Irreducible"
   is `P.IsIrreducible`; the Coxeter number, Coxeter eigenvalues, root heights and the coprime
   statement are stated for irreducible `P` only.
8. **Coefficients.** Degrees do not change under extension of scalars between fields of
   characteristic zero (§3.3); statements over `ℚ`, `ℝ` and `ℂ` are moved between these fields
   only through that theorem. In characteristic dividing `|G|`, this roadmap asserts the invariant
   ring, its grading, integrality and finite generation (Layer 1), and nothing else: no Reynolds
   operator, Molien formula, polynomiality, freeness or coinvariant theorem.
9. **The roadmap and its acceptance test.** This README is the roadmap; every target and Check is
   required. `Suggested.lean` is its acceptance test: it states, each admitted, the headline
   theorems statable at the pins, in `TauCetiRoadmap.ReflectionInvariantTheory`, and its module
   docstring names those that are not statable and what each lacks. Targets without a declaration
   there are named in prose. A target is discharged by a proof; a declaration with an admitted
   proof states it and does not discharge it.

## Layer 1: the invariant algebra of a finite linear group

Let `G` act on `V` through `ρ`. The action on `S` is by graded algebra automorphisms
(`Representation.symmetricAlgebra_mem_homogeneousSubmodule`); as an action by semiring
automorphisms it is `Representation.symmetricAlgebraMulSemiringAction`, and
`Representation.symmetricAlgebraInvariants_eq_fixedPoints` identifies `A` with Mathlib's
`FixedPoints.subalgebra` for it. Mathlib's `IsInvariantSubring`, which says that a subring is stable
under the action, is a different notion and is not used for `A`. `A` is a homogeneous subalgebra
(`Representation.isHomogeneous_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`), so it is graded.

For finite `G`, every `f ∈ S` is a root of the monic orbit polynomial `∏_g (X − g · f)` with
coefficients in `A`, so `S` is integral over `A` (Mathlib's `Algebra.IsInvariant.isIntegral`, through
`Representation.isIntegral_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`), `S` is a finite `A`-module
(`Representation.moduleFinite_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`) and `A` is a finitely generated
`K`-algebra (`Representation.finiteType_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`), in every characteristic.

When `|G|` is invertible in `K`, the Reynolds operator `R(f) = |G|⁻¹ Σ_g g · f` is
`Representation.symmetricAlgebraReynolds`: an `A`-linear, degree-preserving projection of `S` onto
`A`, equal to the averaging projection `Representation.averageMap` of the representation on `S`,
and compatible with extension of scalars
(`Representation.coe_symmetricAlgebraReynolds_scalarTensorBialgEquiv_tmul`). It is not
multiplicative; the algebra map is the inclusion of `A`. Molien's formula
`Hilb(A, t) = |G|⁻¹ Σ_g det(1 − t g | V*)⁻¹` holds in `K⟦t⟧` for the dimensions read in `K`
(`Representation.mk_finrank_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_inf_homogeneousSubmodule_eq_sum_inv_reverse_charpoly`
applied to `ρ.dual`), with `det(1 − t g)` the reverse characteristic polynomial, so no splitting
field is needed; in characteristic zero it is the identity of Hilbert series. It does not by itself
decide whether `A` is a polynomial algebra. In characteristic zero, Noether's bound
(`TauCeti.FixedPoints.subalgebra_eq_adjoin_sum_smul_prod_pow`) generates `A` by the orbit sums of the
monomials of degree at most `|G|` in a basis of `V*`. It bounds the degrees of one generating family;
it is not the degree multiset of a system of basic invariants, which exists only when `A` is a
polynomial algebra (Layer 2).

### 1.1 Noether's bound in nonmodular characteristic

Let `G` be finite and `|G|` invertible in `K`. Prove that `A` is generated as a `K`-algebra by
homogeneous invariants of degrees between `1` and `|G|`. In characteristic zero this is Noether's
bound above; the target is the case of positive characteristic not dividing `|G|`, where Noether's
argument, which divides by multinomial coefficients, fails once the characteristic is at most
`|G|`. Route (Fleischmann; Fogarty): show that every product of `|G|` linear forms lies in the ideal
of `S` generated by the homogeneous invariants of degrees `1, …, |G|`; then a homogeneous invariant
`f` of larger degree is `Σ aᵢ sᵢ` with `aᵢ` among those invariants, and applying the Reynolds
operator gives `f = Σ aᵢ R(sᵢ)`, so induction on the degree concludes.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `Representation.symmetricAlgebraReynolds`,
`Representation.isHomogeneous_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`,
`TauCeti.FixedPoints.subalgebra_eq_adjoin_sum_smul_prod_pow`.

**Checks.**
- `G = μₘ` acting on `K` by multiplication, `m` invertible in `K` and `K` containing the `m`-th roots
  of unity: `A = K[xᵐ]`, so the bound `|G| = m` is attained. A bound `|G| − 1` would be false.
- `G = {±1}` acting on `K²`, `char K ≠ 2`: `A = K[x², xy, y²]`, generated in degree `2 ≤ |G|`,
  although no system of basic invariants exists (Layer 2): the bound says nothing about the number
  of generators.

### 1.2 Cohen–Macaulay invariant rings

Let `G` be finite and `|G|` invertible in `K`, and let `θ₁, …, θₘ` be homogeneous invariants of
positive degree such that `A` is a finite module over `K[θ₁, …, θₘ]` (a homogeneous system of
parameters of `A`). Prove that `m = dim V`, that `θ₁, …, θₘ` is a regular sequence on `A`, and that
`A` is a free graded `K[θ₁, …, θₘ]`-module of finite rank (Hochster–Eagon). Route: `S` is finite over
`A`, hence over `K[θ]`, so `θ` is also a homogeneous system of parameters of the polynomial ring
`S`, which is therefore free over `K[θ]`; the Reynolds operator is `A`-linear, hence `K[θ]`-linear,
and splits the inclusion of `A`, so `A` is a graded direct summand of a free graded `K[θ]`-module of
finite rank, hence free by the graded Nakayama lemma; freeness gives the regular sequence. The
count `m = dim V` is the Krull dimension of `A`
(`Representation.ringKrullDim_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`). For groups generated by
pseudoreflections, the regular-sequence property of basic invariants in `S` itself is Tau Ceti's
`Representation.isRegular_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_of_flat`; it is not a consequence of averaging
alone.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `Representation.symmetricAlgebraReynolds`,
`Representation.moduleFinite_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`,
`Representation.ringKrullDim_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`,
`TauCeti.GradedAlgebra.adjoin_eq_top_of_irrelevant_le`; Mathlib `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular`,
`Module.Free`.

**Checks.**
- `G = {±1}` on `K²`, `char K ≠ 2`: `θ = (x², y²)` is a homogeneous system of parameters and
  `A = K[x², y²] · 1 ⊕ K[x², y²] · xy`, free of rank `2`; its Hilbert series is
  `(1 + t²)/(1 − t²)²`. The pair `(x², xy)` is not a system of parameters: at `x = 0` the invariant
  `y²` is unconstrained, so `A` is not finite over `K[x², xy]`.

### Examples

- `G = {±1}` acting on `K`, `char K ≠ 2`: `R(x) = 0` but `R(x · x) = x²`, so the Reynolds operator
  is not multiplicative; Molien's formula gives `½(1/(1 − t) + 1/(1 + t)) = 1/(1 − t²)`, the Hilbert
  series of `K[x²]`.
- `G = {±1}` acting on `K²`: Molien's formula `½(1/(1 − t)² + 1/(1 + t)²) = (1 + t²)/(1 − t²)²`; the
  coefficient of `t²ᵏ` is `2k + 1`, the number of monomials of degree `2k`.
- Modular case: `S₂` swapping the coordinates of `𝔽₂²`. Averaging is impossible since `|G| = 0`
  in `𝔽₂`; the invariants are still `𝔽₂[x + y, xy]`, but the coinvariant algebra is
  `𝔽₂[x]/(x²)`, on which the swap acts trivially (`y ≡ x` modulo `x + y`). It is not the regular
  representation `𝔽₂[S₂]`, so the coinvariant theorem of §3.6 has no modular analogue.

### Dependencies

Layer 1 rests on the Tau Ceti invariant algebra, Reynolds operator, Molien formula and Noether's
bound named above, and on Mathlib. §1.1 and §1.2 are independent of each other.

## Layer 2: pseudoreflection groups and the Shephard–Todd theorem

In this layer `G` is finite and `V` finite-dimensional. The pseudoreflection subgroup is normal
(`Representation.normal_pseudoreflectionSubgroup`). The forward direction of the Shephard–Todd
theorem holds whenever `|G|` is invertible in `K`, with neither faithfulness nor algebraic
closedness: if `G` is generated by pseudoreflections, `A` is a polynomial algebra on `dim V`
homogeneous invariants of positive degree
(`Representation.exists_bijective_aeval_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_dual_of_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_top`).
Its proof runs through the divided-difference operator `δ_s(f) = (f − s · f)/ℓ` of a
pseudoreflection `s` with hyperplane `ker ℓ`, which is `A`-linear of degree `−1` and satisfies
`1 − s = m_ℓ ∘ δ_s` with no hypothesis on the order of `s`
(`Representation.symmetricAlgebraDividedDifference`); the vanishing of `Tor'ₖ^A(N, S)` for `k ≥ 1`,
with Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.Tor'`, which derives the first variable
(`Representation.isZero_Tor'_succ_symmetricAlgebra`); the faithful flatness of `S` over `A`
(`Representation.faithfullyFlat_symmetricAlgebra_dual_of_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_top`); descent
of regularity along it (`TauCeti.IsRegularRing.of_faithfullyFlat`); and lifting a homogeneous basis
of `A₊/A₊²` at the vertex (`Representation.exists_bijective_aeval_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_of_isRegularLocalRing`).
`S` is then free over `A` (`Representation.free_symmetricAlgebra_dual_of_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_top`).
§2.1 holds over every field; §§2.2–2.5 work over an algebraically closed field `K` of
characteristic zero, where `|G|` is invertible.

### 2.1 Systems of basic invariants and their degrees

Define `IsBasicInvariants ρ f d`, for `f : Fin n → A` and `d : Fin n → ℕ`: each `f i` is homogeneous
of degree `d i` and `MvPolynomial.aeval f : K[X₁, …, Xₙ] → A` is bijective. API, for finite `G`:
- the `fᵢ` are algebraically independent, each `dᵢ ≥ 1`, and `n = dim V`, the Krull dimension of
  `A` (`Representation.ringKrullDim_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`);
- `Hilb(A, t) = ∏ᵢ (1 − t^dᵢ)⁻¹`;
- the degree multiset is intrinsic: for two systems of basic invariants of the same `ρ`, the
  multisets of degrees are equal, since the number of `i` with `dᵢ = k` is the dimension of the
  degree-`k` part of `A₊/A₊²`;
- if `|G|` is invertible in `K` and `G` is generated by pseudoreflections, a system of basic
  invariants exists, by the forward theorem above;
- the invariant ring of `ρ` is that of its image `ρ(G)`, since the kernel of `ρ` acts trivially on
  `S`; so a system of basic invariants of `ρ` is one of the faithful action of `ρ(G)`.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `Representation.ringKrullDim_symmetricAlgebraInvariants`,
`Representation.exists_bijective_aeval_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_dual_of_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_top`;
Mathlib `MvPolynomial.aeval`.

**Checks.**
- `S₃` permuting the coordinates of `K³`, `char K = 0`: `(e₁, e₂, e₃)` and the power sums
  `(p₁, p₂, p₃)` are two different systems of basic invariants with the same degrees `1, 2, 3`. A
  definition of "the" basic invariants as specific polynomials would be ill posed.
- `{±1}²` acting on `K²` by sign changes of the coordinates: `(x², y²)` is a system of basic
  invariants with degree multiset `{2, 2}`; recording only the set `{2}` would lose the count
  `n = 2`.
- Rank zero: `V = 0`, a faithful `G` is trivial, `A = K` and the empty family is a system of basic
  invariants.
- Rank one: `G = μₘ` acting on `K`: `A = K[xᵐ]`, one basic invariant of degree `m`.

### 2.2 The Jacobian of basic invariants

Let `K` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, `G` act faithfully, and `f` be a system of
basic invariants. Fix a basis of `V*` and let `J = det(∂fᵢ/∂xⱼ)` be the Jacobian
(`SymmetricAlgebra.jacobian`). Prove that `J = c · ∏_H ℓ_H^(e_H − 1)`, the product over the
reflecting hyperplanes, for some `c ∈ K` nonzero; changing the forms `ℓ_H` or the basis changes
only `c`. Consequently `Σᵢ (dᵢ − 1) = Σ_H (e_H − 1)`, which is the number of pseudoreflections of
`G`, since the pointwise stabilizer of `H` is cyclic of order `e_H` and its nonidentity elements
are exactly the pseudoreflections with hyperplane `H`. Route: `J ≠ 0`
(`Representation.jacobian_ne_zero_of_surjective_aeval`) and `J` is a relative invariant
(`Representation.dual_symmetricAlgebra_apply_jacobian`). At a height-one prime of `S` whose inertia
group is trivial, `S` is unramified over `A`, so `J` is a unit there; by
`Representation.exists_eq_span_singleton_ι_and_ker_eq_of_mem_inertia` the other height-one primes
are the `(ℓ_H)`, with cyclic inertia group the pointwise stabilizer of `H`
(`Representation.isCyclic_inertia_of_height_eq_one`). Near a generic point of `H` the quotient map
is, in suitable coordinates, `z ↦ z^(e_H)` in the direction normal to `H` and the identity along
`H`, so the ramification index of `(ℓ_H)` over `A` is `e_H`, and the chain rule gives
`ord_(ℓ_H)(J) = e_H − 1`. Unique factorization in `S` concludes. The formula needs a system of
basic invariants and so follows the forward theorem; it is not used to construct one.
*Needs:* §2.1; Tau Ceti `SymmetricAlgebra.jacobian`, `Representation.jacobian_ne_zero_of_surjective_aeval`,
`Representation.dual_symmetricAlgebra_apply_jacobian`,
`Representation.exists_eq_span_singleton_ι_and_ker_eq_of_mem_inertia`,
`Representation.isCyclic_inertia_of_height_eq_one`; Mathlib `Ideal.inertia`.

**Checks.**
- `μₘ` acting on `K`: `f = xᵐ`, `J = m xᵐ⁻¹`, one hyperplane `{0}` with `e = m`. The exponent
  `e_H` in place of `e_H − 1` would give degree `m`, not `m − 1`.
- `S₃` on `K³` with `(e₁, e₂, e₃)`: `J = (x − y)(y − z)(z − x)`, of degree `3 = 0 + 1 + 2`; the
  hyperplanes are `xᵢ = xⱼ` with `e_H = 2`. The three-cycles fix only the line `x = y = z` and
  contribute no factor.
- `μ₃` acting on `K`: one reflecting hyperplane but two pseudoreflections, and
  `d − 1 = 2` counts the pseudoreflections, not the hyperplanes.

### 2.3 Finite étale covers of affine space

Let `K` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and `n ≥ 0`. Prove that a nonzero finite
étale `K[y₁, …, yₙ]`-algebra `B` whose only idempotents are `0` and `1` is isomorphic to
`K[y₁, …, yₙ]` through its structure map: a connected finite étale cover of `𝔸ⁿ_K` is an
isomorphism. Route: for `n = 1`, `B` is a domain whose fraction field has constant field `K`, and
the Hurwitz genus formula (`TauCeti.hurwitz_genus_formula`) for the cover of the projective line,
unramified over the affine line and tamely ramified over `∞` in characteristic zero, gives
`2g − 2 = −d − m` with `m ≥ 1` points over `∞`, so the degree `d` is `1`. For `n ≥ 2`, base change
to the affine line over an algebraic closure of `K(y₂, …, yₙ)` and the case `n = 1` show that `B` is
`C[y₁]` for the integral closure `C` of `K[y₂, …, yₙ]` in a finite extension of `K(y₂, …, yₙ)`;
`C` is finite étale over `K[y₂, …, yₙ]` by faithfully flat descent along `C → C[y₁]`, and induction
on `n` concludes.
*Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.hurwitz_genus_formula`; Mathlib `Algebra.Etale`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

**Checks.**
- In characteristic `p`, `y ↦ yᵖ − y` makes `𝔽̄ₚ[x, y]/(yᵖ − y − x)` a connected finite étale cover
  of `𝔸¹` of degree `p`: characteristic zero is needed.
- `𝔸ⁿ_ℂ → 𝔸ⁿ_ℝ` is a connected finite étale cover of degree `2`: algebraic closedness is needed.
- `z ↦ z²` on `𝔾_m` over `ℂ` is a connected finite étale cover of degree `2`; the theorem is about
  affine space, not about every smooth affine variety.

### 2.4 The converse of the Shephard–Todd theorem

Let `K` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and `G` act faithfully. Prove that if `A` is
a polynomial algebra (any bijective `MvPolynomial.aeval f`, homogeneous or not), then `G` is
generated by pseudoreflections. With the forward direction this is `shephard_todd_target`: for
faithful `ρ`, `ρ.pseudoreflectionSubgroup = ⊤` if and only if `A` is a polynomial algebra. For `ρ`
not faithful, prove the form that results by passing to the image: `A` is a polynomial algebra if
and only if `ρ.pseudoreflectionSubgroup ⊔ ρ.ker = ⊤`.
Route, through the quotient maps `V → V/H → V/G` for `H = ρ.pseudoreflectionSubgroup`: the inertia
group of every height-one prime of `S` lies in `H`
(`Representation.inertia_le_pseudoreflectionSubgroup`), so `G/H` acts on `S^H` with trivial inertia
at every height-one prime (`Representation.inertia_quotient_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_bot`) and
`S^H` is unramified over `A` there
(`Representation.isUnramifiedAt_quotient_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_of_height_eq_one`). `G/H` acts
faithfully on `S^H`, since the elements of `G` fixing `S^H` are exactly those of `H`
(`Representation.fixingSubgroup_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_comp_subtype`,
`Representation.faithfulSMul_quotient_symmetricAlgebra`). The forward theorem for `H` makes `S^H`
a polynomial ring, and the hypothesis makes `A` one, so `Spec S^H → Spec A ≅ 𝔸ⁿ` is a finite
dominant morphism of integral schemes, with normal source and regular target, separable on function
fields in characteristic zero, and unramified at every point of codimension one; by purity of the
branch locus (PurityOfBranchLocus §2.2) it is étale. By §2.3 it is an isomorphism, so `G/H` acts
trivially on `S^H`, and faithfulness gives `G = H`. For `n = 0` faithfulness forces `G = 1`. The
codimension-one ramification of `V → V/G` is the one §2.2 makes explicit.
*Needs:* §2.3; PurityOfBranchLocus §2.2; Tau Ceti
`Representation.exists_bijective_aeval_symmetricAlgebraInvariants_dual_of_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_top`,
`Representation.inertia_le_pseudoreflectionSubgroup`,
`Representation.inertia_quotient_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_eq_bot`,
`Representation.isUnramifiedAt_quotient_pseudoreflectionSubgroup_of_height_eq_one`,

### 3.1 Chambers and the Coxeter presentation

The reflecting hyperplanes all contain `V^W` and meet `E` in hyperplanes of `E`; the *chambers* are
the connected components of the complement in `E` of their union. Fix a chamber `C`. Prove:
- the closure of `C` is a simplicial cone in `E`: there are linearly independent unit vectors
  `α₁, …, α_r` in `E`, `r = dim E`, with `C = {x ∈ E : ⟨αᵢ, x⟩ > 0 for all i}`;
- the reflections `s₁, …, s_r` in the walls `αᵢ^⊥` lie in `W` and generate it, and `W` acts simply
  transitively on the chambers;
- with `m(sᵢ, sⱼ)` the order of `sᵢ sⱼ`, the homomorphism from the Coxeter group of this matrix onto
  `W` is an isomorphism, which gives a Mathlib `CoxeterSystem` on `W` itself;
- its length `ℓ_C(w)` (`CoxeterSystem.length`) is the number of reflecting hyperplanes separating
  `C` and `w C`;
- for the chamber `u C`, `u ∈ W`, the Coxeter system is the conjugate one, and
  `ℓ_(u C)(w) = ℓ_C(u⁻¹ w u)`;

### 4.3 Eigenvalues of a Coxeter element

unramified at `v` (§2.2, `J(v) ≠ 0`); differentiating `fᵢ(c x) = fᵢ(x)` at `v` and using
`fᵢ(ζ v) = ζ^(dᵢ) fᵢ(v)` shows that the differentials of the basic invariants at `v` are
eigenvectors of the transpose of `c` with eigenvalues `ζ^(−eᵢ)`, which gives the eigenvalues of
`c`. The alternative: the classification (RootSystems §§3.3–3.5), with for each irreducible type the
actual Coxeter and root matrices, a system of basic invariants and the characteristic polynomial,

### 4.4 The Coxeter number

For irreducible `P`, the *Coxeter number* is `h = |Φ|/r`. Prove that every Coxeter element has order
`h`: `|Φ| = r · ord(c)` (`card_eq_card_support_mul_orderOf`), equivalently `|Φ⁺| = r h/2`. Prove
that the exponents lie in `[1, h − 1]` and that their multiset is stable under `e ↦ h − e`. Route
(Steinberg's orbit count): for `c = s₁ ⋯ s_r`, the positive roots that `c` makes negative are the
`r` roots `s_r ⋯ s_(i+1)(αᵢ)`; through the Coxeter plane of the bipartite Coxeter element, on which
`c` acts by a rotation of order `ord(c)`, every `⟨c⟩`-orbit in `Φ` has `ord(c)` elements and
contains exactly one of these roots, so there are `r` orbits. For the exponents: by §4.3 their
residues modulo `h` give the eigenvalues of `c`, none is `0` since `c` fixes no nonzero vector
(§4.2), and they are stable under `e ↦ h − e` since `c` is real; so the residues sum to
`r h/2 = |Φ⁺|`, which is `Σ eᵢ = |Ref(W)|` by §3.6, and each `eᵢ`, at least its residue, equals it.
*Needs:* §3.6, §4.2, §4.3; Tau Ceti `CoxeterSystem.bipartiteCoxeterElement`.

**Checks.**
- `E₈`: `|Φ| = 240`, `r = 8`, `h = 30`, and `240 = 8 · 30`.
- `A_r`: `|Φ| = r(r + 1)`, `h = r + 1`.

### 4.5 Heights of roots

For irreducible `P` and every `j ≥ 1`, prove that `#{α ∈ Φ⁺ : ht(α) = j} = #{i : eᵢ ≥ j}`
(`card_height_eq_card_degrees`), and that the highest root has height `h − 1`
(`height_highestRoot_add_one_eq_orderOf`). Route for the partition: compare §4.1 with Macdonald's
product formula `Σ_w t^ℓ(w) = ∏_(α ∈ Φ⁺) (1 − t^(ht(α)+1))/(1 − t^ht(α))`, a form of the Weyl
denominator identity, or verify it type by type from the tables in Examples. Equality of the total
number of roots alone does not determine the distribution. For the highest root: the largest height
is the largest `j` with `#{i : eᵢ ≥ j} > 0`, the largest exponent, which is `h − 1` by §4.4's
symmetry since `1` is an exponent (the invariant quadratic form is a basic invariant of degree `2`,
there being no invariant linear form); it is the height of `b.highestRoot`, the root of greatest
height, whose coefficients RootSystems Layer 4 computes.
*Needs:* §4.1, §4.4; Mathlib `RootPairing.Base.height`; Tau Ceti `RootPairing.Base.highestRoot`.

**Checks.**
- `G₂`: positive roots of heights `1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5`, exponents `1, 5`: two roots of height `1` and
  one of each height `2, …, 5`, and the highest root `3α + 2β` has height `5 = 6 − 1`.
- `B₂`: heights `1, 1, 2, 3`, exponents `1, 3`, `h = 4`.
- `A_(n−1)`, the root system of `SU(n)` with Weyl group `Sₙ`: `n − j` positive roots of height `j`,
  exponents `1, …, n − 1`, `h = n`, and the highest root `α₁ + ⋯ + α_(n−1)` has height
  `n − 1 = h − 1`.

### 4.6 The coprime exponents

For irreducible `P` with Coxeter element of order `h`, prove that every `e` with `1 ≤ e < h` and
`gcd(e, h) = 1` is an exponent (`exists_degree_eq_of_coprime`). Route: in the basis of simple roots
the matrix of `c` has integer entries, so its characteristic polynomial lies in `ℤ[X]`;
`exp(2πi/h)` is an eigenvalue, since `1` is an exponent, so every primitive `h`-th root of unity is
one; by §4.3 these are the `exp(2πi eᵢ/h)` with `1 ≤ eᵢ ≤ h − 1`, so `e` occurs among the
`eᵢ`. A primitive eigenvalue alone does not identify the exponents; §4.3 does. The statement is not
made for arbitrary finite real reflection groups.
*Needs:* §4.3, §4.4.

**Checks.**
- `E₈`: the integers in `[1, 29]` prime to `30` are `1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29`, eight of them,
  and they are all the exponents.
- `H₃`, not crystallographic: a Coxeter element has order `10` and the exponents are `1, 5, 9`; `3`
  and `7` are prime to `10` and are not exponents. For `H₄` (exponents `1, 11, 19, 29`, order `30`)
  `7, 13, 17, 23` are missing, and for the dihedral group of order `10` (exponents `1, 4`) `2, 3`
  are.

### 4.7 Reducible root systems and central directions

Let `P` be reducible, with irreducible components as in RootSystems §6.1. Prove that the degrees of
`P` are the union, as multisets, of the degrees of the components together with one `1` for each
dimension of the fixed space of `W` on `M`; that a Coxeter element of `P` is a product of commuting
Coxeter elements of the components, of order the least common multiple of their Coxeter numbers; and
that §§4.3–4.6 hold for each component with its own Coxeter number. No aggregate Coxeter number
`|Φ|/r` is defined for a reducible `P`, and the coprime statement is not made with the least common
multiple. Central fixed directions contribute degree `1` and exponent `0` to the invariant and
topological bookkeeping of §3.3 and §3.8 only: no Coxeter number, height or term of `|Φ|/r`.
*Needs:* §4.2–§4.6; RootSystems §6.1.

**Checks.**
- `A₁ × A₂`: `|Φ| = 8`, `r = 3`, and `8/3` is not an integer; a Coxeter element has order
  `lcm(2, 3) = 6`. The exponents are `1` and `1, 2`; `5` is prime to `6` but is not an exponent.

### 4.8 Invariant polynomials of a semisimple Lie algebra

Let `K` be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, `L` a finite-dimensional Lie algebra over
`K` with nondegenerate Killing form (`LieAlgebra.IsKilling`) and `H` a Cartan subalgebra, with root
system `LieAlgebra.IsKilling.rootSystem H`. Prove that the invariant polynomials
`TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.lieInvariants K L L` form a polynomial algebra on homogeneous generators
whose degrees are the degrees of that root system, and that a system of basic invariants of its
Weyl group acting on `H` gives one of `L` through the Chevalley restriction isomorphism
`LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso`, which preserves degrees
(`LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso_mem_homogeneousSubmodule_iff`). Route: identify its
target `TauCeti.linearInvariants H`, the polynomials on the dual of `H` invariant under the Weyl
group, with the invariant ring of §2.1 for the Weyl group acting on `H`, and apply the forward
theorem and §2.1. The Chevalley restriction isomorphism and the Harish-Chandra isomorphism are not
targets here.
*Needs:* §2.1; Tau Ceti `LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso`,
`LieSubalgebra.lieChevalleyRestrictionIso_mem_homogeneousSubmodule_iff`, `TauCeti.linearInvariants`,
`TauCeti.SymmetricAlgebra.lieInvariants`; Mathlib `LieAlgebra.IsKilling`,
`LieAlgebra.IsKilling.rootSystem`.

**Checks.**
- `L = 𝔰𝔩₂`: the invariant polynomials are generated by the Killing quadratic form, of degree `2`,
  the degree of `A₁`; restricted to the diagonal Cartan subalgebra it is `x²` up to a scalar, the
  basic invariant of `{±1}` acting on `K`.

## Layer 5: classical examples

### 5.1 Unitary and special linear groups

Prove that the Weyl group of `U(n)`, and of `GL_n(ℂ)`, acting on the Lie algebra of the diagonal

`Representation.esymmSymmetricAlgebraInvariantsEquiv` and `TauCeti.esymmStandardInvariantsEquiv`

*Needs:* §3.7; LieGroups Layer 6; RootSystems §3.2; Tau Ceti

### 5.2 Coxeter elements of Dynkin trees

Prove that in the Weyl group `Sₙ` of `A_(n−1)`, with `sᵢ` the transposition `(i i+1)`, the Coxeter
element `s₁ s₂ ⋯ s_(n−1)` is the `n`-cycle `(1 2 ⋯ n)`, of order `n`, the Coxeter number of
`A_(n−1)`. Every Dynkin diagram is a tree and has a two-colouring
(`RootPairing.nonempty_coloring_graph_coxeterMatrixOfBase`); the black-then-white Coxeter word
spells `CoxeterSystem.bipartiteCoxeterElement`, whatever the order inside each colour class
(`CoxeterSystem.exists_wordProd_eq_bipartiteCoxeterElement`); swapping the colours inverts it
(`CoxeterSystem.isConj_bipartiteCoxeterElement_inv`); and all Coxeter elements are conjugate by
moving word endpoints (§4.2).
*Needs:* §4.2; RootSystems §3.2; Tau Ceti `CoxeterSystem.bipartiteCoxeterElement`,
`CoxeterSystem.exists_wordProd_eq_bipartiteCoxeterElement`,
`CoxeterSystem.isConj_bipartiteCoxeterElement_inv`,
`RootPairing.nonempty_coloring_graph_coxeterMatrixOfBase`.

**Checks.**
- `S₃`: `(1 2)(2 3) = (1 2 3)`, of order `3`; the other order gives `(2 3)(1 2) = (1 3 2)`, its
  inverse and conjugate.
- `D₄`: colour the central node black and the three outer nodes white; the black-then-white word
  is `s₂ s₁ s₃ s₄`, of order `6 = h`.

### 5.3 Affine Dynkin diagrams and two-colourings

For irreducible `P` with base `b`, the *affine diagram* is the graph on `Option b.support` with an
edge between distinct nodes whose entry of `RootPairing.Base.affineCartanMatrix` is nonzero, the
node `none` standing for `α₀ = −θ`; its *automorphisms* are the permutations of the nodes that
preserve the affine Cartan matrix, and each acts on the affine Weyl group
(`RootPairing.affineWeylGroup`) by permuting the simple affine reflections. Prove:
- the affine diagram is bipartite unless `P` has type `A_(2m)`, `m ≥ 1`, whose affine diagram is a
  cycle of odd length `2m + 1`: no two-colouring exists and no black or white class is assigned;
- for a bipartite affine diagram, every automorphism preserves or exchanges the colour classes,
  since a connected bipartite graph has a unique two-colouring up to exchange; the classification is:
  type `A₁` (two nodes), the swap exchanges; type `A_(2m−1)`, `m ≥ 2` (a cycle of `2m` nodes
  numbered modulo `2m`), `i ↦ ±i + k` preserves the classes exactly when `k` is even; types `B_r`,
  `E₆` and `E₇`, every automorphism preserves them; type `C_r`, the reversal of the path exchanges
  them exactly when `r` is odd; type `D_r`, an exchanging automorphism exists exactly when `r` is
  odd, the one interchanging the two ends; types `E₈`, `F₄` and `G₂`, the only automorphism is the
  identity;
- a preserving automorphism fixes the black-then-white element `c` and the white-then-black element
  `c⁻¹`; an exchanging one interchanges them;
- for the odd cycles of type `A_(2m)` the automorphism group is dihedral of order `2(2m + 1)`; the
  question of graph symmetry stays a question about graph automorphisms, with no two-colouring and
  no extra Coxeter relation.

*Needs:* Tau Ceti `RootPairing.Base.affineCartanMatrix`, `RootPairing.affineWeylGroup`,
`TauCeti.AffineDynkinType`.

**Checks.**
- Type `A₂`: the affine diagram is a triangle, not bipartite. Type `A₃`: a square; the rotation by
  one step exchanges the colours, the rotation by two steps preserves them, and four of the eight
  automorphisms do each.
- Type `D₄`: all `24` automorphisms permute the four outer nodes and fix the centre, so they
  preserve the colours. Type `D₅`: four of the eight automorphisms exchange them.
- Type `C₃`: the reversal of the four-node path exchanges the colours; for `C₂` and `C₄` it
  preserves them.
