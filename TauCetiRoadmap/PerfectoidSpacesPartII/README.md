# Roadmap: perfectoid rings and spaces, part II — ramification and Riemann extension

**Summary.** [Perfectoid rings and spaces](../PerfectoidSpaces/README.md) (PerfectoidSpaces) builds
almost mathematics, perfectoid Tate rings and their tilts, perfectoid rational localisation and the
almost purity theorem for finite étale covers: everything there concerns covers that are étale
wherever `p` is invertible. This Part II studies a perfectoid algebra `A` together with an element
`g` that has a compatible system of `p`-power roots, and covers of `Spec A[1/g]` that may ramify
along `g = 0`. It proves two families of theorems. The first is the **Riemann extension
theorem**: bounded functions on `{g ≠ 0}` extend across `g = 0` up to an almost zero error. In
Bhatt's quantitative form (Layers 0–1) the comparison of `A/tᵐ` with the tower of bounded functions
on the rational annuli `{|tⁿ| ≤ |g|}` is an almost-pro-isomorphism whose constants do not depend on
`n`, which makes it survive derived tensor products; in André's form (Layers 2–3) the limit is
identified exactly with the complete integral closure `g^{-1/p^∞}A°` of `A°` in `A[1/g]`, on top of
a calculus of uniform Banach algebras (closures, Laurent and Weierstrass localisations, uniform
limits and colimits, root algebras) and of almost perfectoid algebras. The second is **André's
perfectoid Abhyankar lemma** (Layer 4): for a finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebra `B′` the largest
perfectoid `A`-algebra `B` inside `B′` has `B°/pᵐ` almost finite étale over `A°/pᵐ` in the almost
setup generated by the roots of `g` and of a pseudouniformizer, which extends almost purity from
`g = 1` to arbitrary ramification. The last layer (Layer 5) applies both to the normalized Kummer
tower over `W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧`: pure and almost pure maps, André's flatness theorem (the completed
tower is almost faithfully flat over the coordinate tower), purity of all Kummer extensions, and
almost faithful flatness modulo `pᵐ` of finite extensions étale away from `pg`. These are the
inputs of André's and Bhatt's proofs of the direct summand theorem, which belong to
DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay.

The end theorems are Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem with its derived and limit
forms (§1.2–§1.5), André's perfectoid Riemann extension theorem (§3.6) and nested-systems
theorem (§3.11), ramified almost purity (§4.5), the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma (§4.7),
André's flatness theorem (§5.8), the purity of Kummer extensions (§5.9) and ramified almost
flatness modulo `pᵐ` (§5.12). `Suggested.lean` states those of them that the pinned libraries
can express.

## Conventions

- `p` is a fixed prime. A **root system** of an element `x` of a ring is a sequence
  `(x^{1/p^k})_{k≥0}` with `x^{1/p^0} = x` and `(x^{1/p^{k+1}})^p = x^{1/p^k}`; it is part of the
  data, and products of root systems are root systems, `(tg)^{1/p^k} = t^{1/p^k}g^{1/p^k}`. For
  such an `x`, almost mathematics "with respect to `x^{1/p^∞}`" uses the root setup
  `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`, whose ideal `m_x` is generated by all `x^{1/p^k}`; in Layers 0–1 almost
  statements are elementwise (killed by every `x^{1/p^k}`) and use no flatness of `m_x`.
- `K` is a perfectoid field (Layer 1, Layers 3–5) or a field complete for a nontrivial nonarchimedean
  absolute value (Layer 2), `K°` its valuation ring, `K°°` its maximal ideal, and `ϖ ∈ K°°` a
  pseudouniformizer with `|p| ≤ |ϖ| < 1` and a root system; in characteristic `0` it is chosen as
  `ϖ = (ϖ♭)^♯`. Bhatt's `t` is a pseudouniformizer, `0 < |t| < 1`, with a root system. Bhatt
  normalises `|t| = |p|` in characteristic `0`; the statements of Layer 1 hold for every such `t`
  and do not use this normalisation (§1.2).
- An **integral perfectoid `K°`-algebra** in Bhatt's sense is a `K°`-flat, `t`-adically complete
  `K°`-algebra `A` equal to its almost elements (if `f ∈ A[1/t]` and `t^{1/p^k}f ∈ A` for all `k`
  then `f ∈ A`), with Frobenius inducing a bijection `A/t^{1/p} → A/t`, for `t` with `|t| = |p|`
  in characteristic `0`; `A ↦ A[1/t]` and `R ↦ R°` are inverse equivalences with perfectoid
  `K`-algebras (PerfectoidSpaces P1.25). These are therefore the rings `R°` of perfectoid
  `K`-algebras `R`, a description that involves no choice of `t`.
- **Uniform Banach `K`-algebras** are complete Tate `K`-algebras whose power-bounded subring `A°` is
  bounded, with continuous homomorphisms; equivalently their norm is equivalent to the spectral
  seminorm, whose unit ball is `A°` (AdicSpacesPartII R0.4, AdicSpacesPartIII R3.5). `K-uBan`
  denotes this category. `B{f/λ}` and `A{λ/g}` are the Weierstrass and Laurent localisations; the
  superscript `u` is the uniformization of AdicSpacesPartII R0.4 (separated completion for the
  spectral seminorm). `A_{≤1}` is the unit ball of a given norm.
- The almost setups `(K°, K°°)`, `(K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°)` and `(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞})`
  occur side by side. "Almost" is always relative to a stated setup, and an almost statement is
  never upgraded to an exact one. For a `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra, `g` denotes the image of `T` and
  `g^{1/p^k}` that of `T^{1/p^k}`.
- In Layer 5, `k` is a perfect field of characteristic `p` (the root index of the Kummer tower is
  written `l` and is always a subscript), `A = W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧`, `K_j = W(k)[1/p](ζ_{p^j})`, infinite
  indices mean directed unions, and a hat means `p`-adic completion.
- Generic Banach completions use the Gauss topology: complete an integral unit ball `p`-adically and
  then invert `p`; they are never algebraic `(p)`-adic completions of rings in which `p` is already a
  unit (those are zero). Kummer root algebras retain all branches of the roots and their integral
  closures are taken inside the specified ambient algebras; these models can have zero divisors.
- Towers are indexed by `n ≥ 1` with transition maps `ρ_{n′,n} : M_{n′} → M_n` for `n′ ≥ n`,
  `ρ_{n,n} = id`, `ρ_{n′,n} ∘ ρ_{n″,n′} = ρ_{n″,n}`.
- Universes: all rings and modules of a statement live in one universe; `Suggested.lean` quantifies
  the test modules of purity and almost flatness over the universe of the base ring.
- Source abbreviations: **Bhatt** is Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived
  variant* (arXiv:1608.08882v2; pages are the preprint's, theorem numbers agree with Invent. Math.
  212 (2018)); **André A** is André, *Le lemme d'Abhyankar perfectoïde*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 127
  (2018), 1–70; **André B** is André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 127
  (2018), 71–93; **GR** is Gabber–Ramero, *Almost ring theory* (arXiv:math/0201175v3); **Scholze**
  is Scholze, *Perfectoid spaces*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 116 (2012). Page numbers of André A and André B
  are those of the published articles. The statements below incorporate the corrections listed in
  the section *Corrections to the sources*.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 → Layer 1 and Layer 2 → Layer 3 → Layer 4 can be developed in parallel; Layer 5 uses Layer 2, Layer 3 and Layer 4.
Layer 0 is pure commutative algebra of towers and delivers the almost-pro calculus and its passage
through derived tensor products. Layer 1 builds the annular tower of an integral perfectoid algebra
from PerfectoidSpaces P2 and proves Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem, then its
derived form, its form without regularity of `g` and its limit form. Layer 2 is André's calculus of
uniform Banach algebras: closures, the unit-ball dictionary, root saturation, Banach localisations,
uniform limits and colimits and root algebras. Layer 3 adds perfectoid and almost perfectoid algebras
and proves André's perfectoid Riemann extension theorem and the nested-systems theorem. Layer 4
restricts finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebras to the annuli, where almost purity applies, takes uniform
limits and controls finite étaleness through almost Galois theory; it proves ramified almost purity
and the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma. Layer 5 develops purity and almost purity and applies Layers 2–4 to
the normalized Kummer tower. Layer 1 and Layer 3 prove overlapping Riemann extension statements by
different methods; both are kept because the direct summand proofs use them in incompatible forms:
Bhatt needs the uniform pro-statement before limits, André the exact identification of the limit.

## Layer 0: Almost-pro-zero towers and uniform annihilation

Almost-pro-zero towers are Bhatt's device for statements that hold "almost, after passing far
enough in a tower", where how far may depend on the precision `t^{1/p^k}`. They are weaker than
being zero as a pro-object of almost modules, but strong enough to make derived limits almost zero
and to be preserved by linear functors. The uniform variant fixes the distance `c(k)` independently
of the tower index; it is the form in which Riemann extension enters the direct summand theorems,
because it survives derived tensor products. Throughout the layer `A` is a commutative ring and
`t ∈ A` carries a root system, with root ideal `m_t` (Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`).

### 0.1 almost-pro-zero towers and almost-pro-isomorphisms

Define a tower `M = (M_n)_{n≥1}` of `A`-modules to be **almost-pro-zero** (with respect to
`t^{1/p^∞}`) if for every `k ≥ 0` and every `n ≥ 1` there is `n′ ≥ n` such that `t^{1/p^k}` kills
the image of `ρ_{n′,n} : M_{n′} → M_n` (Bhatt, Definition 3.2, p. 6). The index `n′` may depend on
`k` as well as on `n`; this is strictly weaker than asking that one `n′` serve every `k`, which is
the condition that `M` be zero as a pro-object of almost modules (Bhatt, Example 3.3, p. 6, and the
paragraph before Definition 3.2, p. 5). A morphism of towers of `A`-modules is an
**almost-pro-isomorphism** if its kernel and cokernel towers are almost-pro-zero; a morphism of
towers in `D^b(A)` is an almost-pro-isomorphism if for every `i` the tower of `i`-th cohomology
modules of its cone is almost-pro-zero (Bhatt, Definition 3.2). No hypothesis on `t` enters the
definition. *Needs:* Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower`,
`CategoryTheory.Functor.IsProZero`, `CategoryTheory.Functor.proIsomorphisms`,
`CategoryTheory.Functor.isProZero_monotone_comp_iff`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- A tower is almost-pro-zero iff for all `k` and `n` there is `n′ ≥ n` with
  `t^{1/p^k} · ρ_{n′,n}(x) = 0` for every `x ∈ M_{n′}`.
- Equivalently, for every `k` the inclusion of the `t^{1/p^k}`-torsion subtower
  `(M_n[t^{1/p^k}])_n → (M_n)_n` is a pro-isomorphism, a morphism in Tau Ceti's
  `CategoryTheory.Functor.proIsomorphisms` (Bhatt, Definition 3.2): if the image of `ρ_{n′,n}` is killed by `t^{1/p^k}` the
  transition factors through the torsion submodule, which gives the inverse pro-morphism.
- An ordinary pro-zero tower is almost-pro-zero; if `t` is a unit of `A` the two notions agree.
- A tower whose terms are almost zero (killed by `m_t`) is almost-pro-zero, with `n′ = n`.
- Subtowers and quotient towers of an almost-pro-zero tower are almost-pro-zero; in a short exact
  sequence of towers `0 → M′ → M → M″ → 0` with `M′`, `M″` almost-pro-zero, `M` is almost-pro-zero:
  choose `n′ ≥ n` with `t^{1/p^{k+1}}` killing the image of `M′_{n′} → M′_n`, then `n″ ≥ n′` with
  `t^{1/p^{k+1}}` killing the image of `M″_{n″} → M″_{n′}`; for `x ∈ M_{n″}` the element
  `t^{1/p^{k+1}}ρ_{n″,n′}(x)` has zero image in `M″_{n′}`, so lies in `M′_{n′}`, and its image in
  `M_n` is killed by `t^{1/p^{k+1}}`, so `t^{2/p^{k+1}}` kills `ρ_{n″,n}(x)`; and `t^{2/p^{k+1}}`
  divides `t^{1/p^k}` because `p ≥ 2`. The order matters: choosing the index for `M″` first
  produces an element of `M′_n` that need not come from a higher level of `M′`.
- The property is invariant under cofinal monotone reindexing of the tower.
- Almost-pro-isomorphisms of module towers are closed under composition and satisfy two-out-of-three;
  so do almost-pro-isomorphisms of towers in `D^b(A)`, through the long exact cohomology sequences of
  cones.

**Checks.**

- For `A = K°` the valuation ring of a perfectoid field and `t = ϖ`, the tower `M_n = A/ϖ^{1/p^n}`
  with transitions multiplication by `ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n+1}}` (Bhatt, Example 3.3) is almost-pro-zero:
  the image of `M_{n′} → M_n` is generated by the class of `ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n′}}`, which `ϖ^{1/p^k}`
  kills as soon as `n′ ≥ k`.
- For the same tower no single `n′` works for all `k`: the image of `M_{n+r} → M_n` contains the
  class of `ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n+r}}`, which is killed by `ϖ^{1/p^{n+r}}` but not by `ϖ^{1/p^{n+r+1}}`.
  A definition in which `n′` does not depend on `k` declares this tower not almost-pro-zero.
- The zero tower, and any tower of almost zero modules with arbitrary transitions, is
  almost-pro-zero.
- For `A = K°` and `t = ϖ ≠ 0`, the constant tower `(A, id)` is not almost-pro-zero: every image is
  `A`, which `ϖ^{1/p^k}` does not kill.
- For `t = 1` with all roots equal to `1`, almost-pro-zero coincides with Tau Ceti's
  `CategoryTheory.Functor.IsProZero`: a definition that forgot the torsion condition would accept the constant tower `(ℤ, id)`
  over `A = ℤ`, which `IsProZero` rejects.

### 0.2 uniformly almost-pro-zero towers relative to a second root system

Let `g ∈ A` carry a root system as well. Define a tower `(N_n)_{n≥1}` of `A`-modules to be
**uniformly almost-pro-zero** with respect to `g^{1/p^∞}`, in `t`-almost modules, with constants
`c = (c(k))_{k≥0}`, if for every `k ≥ 0`, every `n ≥ 1` and every `l ≥ 0` the image of the
transition `N_{n+c(k)} → N_n` is killed by `g^{1/p^k}·t^{1/p^l}` (Bhatt, Theorem 4.2(2), p. 6, and
Remark 4.3, p. 7). The constant `c(k)` does not depend on `n`, and annihilation by `g^{1/p^k}` is
measured in `t`-almost modules (by `g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l}` for every `l`, not by `g^{1/p^k}` alone). A
morphism `(f_n : L → L_n)_n` from a constant module to a tower, compatible with the transitions, is a
**uniform almost-pro-isomorphism** with constants `c` if every `ker f_n` is killed by `m_t` and the
cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with constants `c`. *Needs:* §0.1; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- A uniformly almost-pro-zero tower is almost-pro-zero for the root system of `tg`: the image of
  `N_{n+c(k)} → N_n` is killed by `g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^k} = (tg)^{1/p^k}`.
- If `c ≤ c′` pointwise, constants `c` may be replaced by `c′`.
- Subtowers and quotient towers of a uniformly almost-pro-zero tower are uniformly almost-pro-zero
  with the same constants.
- A morphism from a constant module is a uniform almost-pro-isomorphism iff its kernels are almost
  zero and its cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero.

**Checks.**

- The zero tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with all constants `0`.
- A tower with zero transition maps is uniformly almost-pro-zero with `c(k) = 1` for every `k`,
  whatever its terms; with `c(k) = 0` the transition is the identity, and for `N_n = K°/ϖ` over
  `A = K°`, `t = g = ϖ`, `p = 2` the condition fails at `k = l = 2`, since `ϖ^{1/4 + 1/4}` does not
  kill `K°/ϖ`.
- For `g = 1`, the tower `A/ϖ^{1/p^n}` of Bhatt Example 3.3 over `A = K°` is almost-pro-zero but not
  uniformly almost-pro-zero: the `c`-step image into `M_n` is generated by `ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n+c}}`,
  which `ϖ^{1/p^l}` kills only for `l ≤ n + c`; no constant independent of `n` works.
- Every uniformly almost-pro-zero tower is almost-pro-zero in the sense of §0.1 for the root
  system of `tg`; the converse fails by the previous Check.

### 0.3 derived limits of almost-pro-zero towers are almost zero

Let `t` be a nonzerodivisor of `A` with a root system (Bhatt, Notation 3.1, p. 5). Prove: (1) if
`(M_n)_{n≥1}` is an almost-pro-zero tower of `A`-modules then `lim_n M_n` and `lim¹_n M_n` are killed
by every `t^{1/p^k}`, so `R lim_n M_n` has almost zero cohomology; (2) if `(K_n) → (L_n)` is an
almost-pro-isomorphism of towers in `D^b(A)` given as coherent towers, then `R lim K_n → R lim L_n`
is an almost isomorphism: the cohomology of its cone is killed by every `t^{1/p^k}` (Bhatt, Lemmas
3.4–3.5, p. 6). Towers are indexed by `n ≥ 1`, so `R lim` has amplitude `[0, 1]` on module towers.
The nonzerodivisor hypothesis is Bhatt's standing one; part (1) does not use it.

*Proof sketch.* Fix `k`. The torsion subtower is pro-isomorphic to `M`, pro-isomorphic towers have
isomorphic `lim` and `lim¹` (the six-term sequence of `TauCeti.ModuleTower.connecting` applied to
kernel and cokernel, which have vanishing `lim` and `lim¹` by
`TauCeti.ModuleTower.lim_eq_bot_of_isProZero` and
`TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isProZero`), and the torsion subtower has `lim` and
`lim¹` killed by `t^{1/p^k}`. For (2), `R lim` commutes with cones, and the Milnor sequence expresses each
cohomology of `R lim` of the cone through `lim` and `lim¹` of its cohomology towers, which are almost
zero by (1).

*Needs:* §0.1; DerivedModuleAlgebra §1.1; PerfectoidSpaces P0.27; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ModuleTower.lim`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.limOne`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.connecting`,
`TauCeti.ModuleTower.lim_eq_bot_of_isProZero`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isProZero`,
`TauCeti.ComplexTower.exact_limOneToHomology_homologyToLim`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.derivedLimit`.

**Checks.**

- For Bhatt's Example 3.3 the limit is zero and `lim¹` is almost zero.
- If `t` is a unit the statement reduces to the vanishing of `lim` and `lim¹` of pro-zero towers.
- The tower `ℤ ←p ℤ ←p ⋯` over `A = ℤ` with `t = 1` is not almost-pro-zero and has
  `lim¹ = ℤ_p/ℤ ≠ 0`, so the hypothesis cannot be dropped.

### 0.4 linear functors preserve almost-pro-zero towers

Let `(M_n)` be an almost-pro-zero tower of `A`-modules and `F : Mod_A → Mod_A` an additive
`A`-linear functor (`F(a·φ) = a·F(φ)` for `a ∈ A` and every `A`-linear `φ`). Prove that
`(F(M_n))_n`, with transitions `F(ρ_{n′,n})`, is almost-pro-zero (Bhatt, Lemma 3.6, p. 6). In
particular this holds for `Hom_A(N, −)`, `Ext^i_A(N, −)`, `N ⊗_A −` and `Tor_i^A(N, −)` for any
`A`-module `N`, and a tower almost-pro-isomorphic to a constant tower stays so after applying such a
functor termwise. `F` need not be exact.

*Proof sketch.* Choose `n′` with `ρ_{n′,n}` factoring through `M_n[t^{1/p^k}]`. Then `F(ρ_{n′,n})`
factors through `F(M_n[t^{1/p^k}])`, on which `t^{1/p^k}` acts as `F(t^{1/p^k}·id) = F(0) = 0` by
linearity.

*Needs:* §0.1; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Functor.Linear`, `CategoryTheory.Abelian.Ext`,
`CategoryTheory.Tor`.

**Checks.**

- Applied to `Hom_A(Q, −)` for a finitely presented `Q` it gives the almost vanishing of `lim¹` of
  the Hom towers used in Bhatt's proof of Theorem 5.4.
- The functor `M ↦ M ⊗_ℤ ℚ` over `A = ℤ`, `t = 1`, sends the pro-zero tower with zero transitions to
  a pro-zero tower, as it must.
- Linearity is needed: over `A = K°`, `t = ϖ`, the additive functor `M ↦ M ⊗_ℤ A`, with `A`
  acting on the second factor, is not `A`-linear, and it sends Bhatt's tower `A/ϖ^{1/p^n}` to a tower
  that is not almost-pro-zero. Each `A/ϖ^{1/p^n}` is an `𝔽_p`-vector space (as `|p| ≤ |ϖ|`), so
  `(A/ϖ^{1/p^n}) ⊗_ℤ A = (A/ϖ^{1/p^n}) ⊗_{𝔽_p} (A/p)`. Take `k = 1`. The images
  `ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n′}}A/ϖ^{1/p^n}` of the transitions are nonzero for every `n′ ≥ n`, and
  `|ϖ^{1/p}| > |ϖ| ≥ |p|` puts `ϖ^{1/p}` outside `pA`; a simple tensor of nonzero vectors over
  `𝔽_p` is nonzero, so `x ⊗ ϖ^{1/p}` is nonzero for every nonzero class `x` in an image. The
  witness needs `k ≥ 1`: at `k = 0` with `ϖ = p` (for instance `K = ℂ₂`, `ϖ = 2`), `ϖ` lies in
  `pA` and every `x ⊗ ϖ` is zero.

### 0.5 uniform annihilation survives derived tensor products

Let `R` be a commutative ring with elements `t` and `g` carrying root systems, and
`(f_n : R → R_n)_{n≥1}` maps of `R`-modules into a tower `(R_n)` compatible with the transitions.
Suppose every `ker f_n` is killed by `m_t` and the cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with
respect to `g^{1/p^∞}` with constants `c(k)` (§0.2). Prove that for every complex `K ∈ D(R)` and
every `i ∈ ℤ`, the kernel tower and the cokernel tower of `H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L_R R_n)` have
`c(k)`-step transitions killed by `g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l}` for every `l`, in particular by
`(tg)^{1/p^k}`, for all `n`; the constants do not depend on `n`, `K` or `i` (the argument behind
Bhatt, Remark 4.3, p. 7, which Bhatt calls formal). The `R_n` need only be `R`-modules; in the
application they are the `R`-algebras `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ` over `R = A/tᵐ`.

*Proof sketch.* Let `C_n` be the mapping cone of `f_n`, formed at the level of complexes so that the
transitions `τ : C_{n+c} → C_n` are honest chain maps; `H^{−1}(C_n) = ker f_n` and
`H^0(C_n) = coker f_n`. With `γ = g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l}`, the composite of `γτ` with `C_n → H^0(C_n)[0]`
vanishes, so by the truncation triangle `γτ` factors through `H^{−1}(C_n)[1] → C_n`. Every
`t^{1/p^{l′}}` kills `H^{−1}(C_n)`, so `t^{1/p^{l′}}γτ = 0` in `D(R)`; since
`t^{1/p^l} = t^{1/p^{l+1}}·t^{(p−1)/p^{l+1}}`, every element of `g^{1/p^k}m_t` annihilates `τ`. Tensor
with `K`; in the long exact sequence of `K → K ⊗^L R_n → K ⊗^L C_n` the cokernel of
`H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L R_n)` embeds into `H^i(K ⊗^L C_n)` and the kernel is a quotient of
`H^{i−1}(K ⊗^L C_n)`, compatibly with the transitions.

*Needs:* §0.2; DerivedModuleAlgebra §0.2; Mathlib `DerivedCategory`.

**Checks.**

- For `K = R` the statement returns the hypothesis, with the same constants.
- For `K` the Koszul complex of an `R`-regular sequence it gives the uniform almost vanishing of the
  higher Koszul homology of the `R_n` (Bhatt, Remark 4.3).
- If the cones are taken only in `D(R)`, without chain-level transitions, the maps `τ` are not
  determined; the chain-level mapping cone is what makes the towers of `H^i(K ⊗^L C_n)` well defined.

### Examples

- Bhatt's tower `K°/ϖ^{1/p^n}` separates the three notions of this layer: it is almost-pro-zero, it
  is not zero as a pro-object of almost modules, and it is not uniformly almost-pro-zero for `g = 1`.
- A tower of almost zero modules is uniformly almost-pro-zero with `c(k) = 0` for every root system
  of `g`, since `m_t` kills every term.

## Layer 1: The quantitative Riemann extension theorem

Throughout, `K` is a perfectoid field, `t ∈ K°` a pseudouniformizer with a root system (no
normalisation of `|t|` is imposed), `A` an integral perfectoid `K°`-algebra with perfectoid space
`X = Spa(A[1/t], A)`, and `g ∈ A` an element with a root system. The annular tower consists of the
bounded functions on the rational subsets `{|tⁿ| ≤ |g|}`, which exhaust `{g ≠ 0}`. The layer proves
that, modulo `tᵐ`, `A` is almost-pro-isomorphic to this tower with uniform constants
`c(k) = pᵏm`, by a short computation on Scholze's explicit almost presentation of the annuli.

### 1.1 the annular tower `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩`

For `n ≥ 0` put `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩ = {x ∈ X : |tⁿ(x)| ≤ |g(x)|}`, a rational subset, and
`A⟨tⁿ/g⟩ = O⁺_X(X⟨tⁿ/g⟩)` (Bhatt, Notation 4.1, p. 6, and §1.2, p. 2). For `n = 0` this is the
locus `{1 ≤ |g|} = {|g| = 1}`, since `|g| ≤ 1` on `X`; the tower of §0.1 is indexed by `n ≥ 1`,
and the term `n = 0` changes no pro-statement. Since
`X⟨tⁿ/g⟩ ⊆ X⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩`, restriction gives maps `A⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩ → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩`, and with the structure
maps `f_n : A → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` they form a tower under `A`. With `A = R°` for the perfectoid Tate ring
`R = A[1/t]`, the tower is Tau Ceti's plus presheaf `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.presentationLimitPlusPresheaf` of
`Spa(R, R°)` composed with the increasing sequence of opens `n ↦ X⟨tⁿ/g⟩`: in `Suggested.lean`,
`annularOpen t g n` is the open `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` (`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spaBasicOpen` of the presentation `({tⁿ, g}, g)`),
`annularTower P t g h` is the functor `ℕᵒᵖ ⥤ CommRingCat` (`h` the monotonicity of the opens), and
`coeffToSections P R° V` is the canonical map `R → 𝒪_X(V)` (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.toPresentationLimit`);
for `V = X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` its restriction to `R°` is `f_n` followed by the inclusion `𝒪⁺ ⊆ 𝒪`. Since
`tⁿ` is a unit of `R`, the
numerator ideal of `({tⁿ, g}, g)` is the unit ideal, so each term is identified with
`TauCeti.Huber.PairOfDefinition.completedPlusSubring` of that presentation, the carrier on which
§1.2 is stated in `Suggested.lean` with the restriction maps
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.pairHomOfRationalSubsetSubset`. PerfectoidSpaces P2.3–P2.4 supply the
perfectoid structure of the terms. In `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` the
functions `u_n^{1/p^k} := (tⁿ/g)^{1/p^k}` are bounded, `g^{1/p^k}u_n^{1/p^k} = t^{n/p^k}`, and `g`
divides `tⁿ`. For every `m ≥ 0` the map
`A[u_n^{1/p^∞}]/(tᵐ, (u_ng)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k} for all k) → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ` is an almost isomorphism
with respect to `t^{1/p^∞}`, and under these presentations the transition sends `u_{n+1}^e` to
`t^e u_n^e` for `e ∈ ℤ[1/p]_{≥0}` (Bhatt, proof of Theorem 4.2, p. 7, from Scholze, Lemma 6.4). The
pseudouniformizer condition `0 < |t| < 1` matters in characteristic `p`.

*Construction.* Rational subsets of affinoid perfectoid spaces are affinoid perfectoid with an
explicit almost integral model (PerfectoidSpaces P2.3); since `g` has a root system, the generators
`tⁿ` and `g` of `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` are already of the form required there and no approximation is needed. The
model is the `t`-adic completion of `A[u^{1/p^∞}]/((ug)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k})_k` up to almost
isomorphism, and reduction modulo `tᵐ` gives the presentation. Restriction from `X⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩` to
`X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` sends `t^{n+1}/g` to `t·(tⁿ/g)`.

*Needs:* PerfectoidSpaces P1.25, P2.3, P2.4; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`,
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.presentationLimitPlusPresheaf`,
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.toPresentationLimit`,
`TauCeti.Huber.PairOfDefinition.completedPlusSubring`,
`TauCeti.Huber.PairOfDefinition.toCompletionLoc`,
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.pairHomOfRationalSubsetSubset`; Mathlib `Monotone.functor`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- For `n′ ≥ n` there is a restriction map `A⟨t^{n′}/g⟩ → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩`; restriction from `n` to `n` is the
  identity and restrictions compose.
- The structure maps `f_n : A → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` are compatible with restriction.
- In `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩`, `g^{1/p^k}·u_n^{1/p^k} = t^{n/p^k}` for every `k`; in particular `g` divides `tⁿ`.
- For every `m ≥ 0`, the presentation map above is an almost isomorphism for `t^{1/p^∞}`.
- Under the presentations, restriction from `n + c` to `n` sends `u_{n+c}^e` to `t^{ce}u_n^e`.
- `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩[1/t]` is the rational localisation `O_X(X⟨tⁿ/g⟩)` of PerfectoidSpaces P2.3.

**Checks.**

- If `g` is a unit of `A` then `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩ = X`, `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩ = A` and every `f_n` is the identity.
- If `g = 0` then `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` is empty, since `tⁿ` is invertible on `X`, so `A⟨tⁿ/0⟩ = 0`.
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T`, `A⟨tⁿ/T⟩` is almost the `t`-adic completion of
  `K°[T^{1/p^∞}, U^{1/p^∞}]/((TU)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k})_k`, the closed perfectoid annulus
  `|tⁿ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1` (compare André A (3.12), p. 49).
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ × K°` and `g = (T, 0)` (with roots `(T^{1/p^k}, 0)`), `X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` lies in the
  first component, so `(0, 1)` lies in the kernel of every `f_n` and is not almost zero: injectivity
  of the `f_n` needs a hypothesis on `g`.
- `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩[1/t]` agrees with PerfectoidSpaces P2.3's rational localisation of `A[1/t]` at
  `({tⁿ, g}, g)`; a definition through the uncompleted localisation `A[1/g]` would miss the completion.

### 1.2 Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem

In the setting of §1.1 fix `m ≥ 0` and assume that `g` is an almost nonzerodivisor modulo `tᵐ`
(the kernel of multiplication by `g` on `A/tᵐ` is killed by `m_t`). Let
`f_n : A/tᵐ → A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ`. Prove, in almost mathematics with respect to `t^{1/p^∞}` (Bhatt,
Theorem 4.2 and proof, pp. 6–7; Theorem 1.6, p. 3):

1. every `ker f_n` (`n ≥ 0`) is almost zero;
2. for every `k ≥ 0`, with `c = pᵏm`, and every `n ≥ 0`, the image of the transition
   `coker f_{n+c} → coker f_n` is killed by `g^{1/p^k}`: the cokernel tower is uniformly
   almost-pro-zero with respect to `g^{1/p^∞}` with constants `c(k) = pᵏm`;
3. consequently `(f_n)_n` is an almost-pro-isomorphism from the constant tower `A/tᵐ` with respect to
   `(tg)^{1/p^∞}`.

The almost regularity of `g` modulo `tᵐ` is used only for (1); §1.4 removes it for (3). The
constant `pᵏm` depends on `k` and `m` only.

`Suggested.lean` states (1) as `bhatt_riemann_extension_ker_almostZero` and (2) as
`bhatt_quantitative_riemann_extension`, on the presentationwise carriers of §1.1: `R = A[1/t]` is a
perfectoid Tate ring (`TauCeti.Perfectoid.IsPerfectoidTateRing p R`) which is a topological algebra over a field `K`
satisfying the perfectoid-field criterion of PerfectoidSpaces P1.11, `t` is given by its compatible
`p`-power roots in `K` with `0 < ‖t‖ < 1`, `A = R°` is Tau Ceti's `powerBoundedSubring R` (Bhatt's
integral perfectoid `K°`-algebras are exactly the rings `R°`, since they satisfy `A = A_*`; Bhatt,
§1.4, p. 3), `g` is given by its roots in `R°`, `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` is `completedPlusSubring` of the
presentation `({tⁿ, g}, g)` with localisation `R[1/g]`, `f_n` is `toCompletionLoc`, and the
restriction from `n + c` to `n` is `pairHomOfRationalSubsetSubset` of the inclusion
`X⟨tⁿ/g⟩ ⊆ X⟨t^{n+c}/g⟩`. The pair of definition of `R`, the containment of its ideal of
definition in `R°`, the denominator condition `HasDenominatorPower` of each presentation and the
inclusions of the annuli are hypotheses of the statements; each holds for every pair of definition
(the ideal of definition is topologically nilpotent, `tⁿ` is a unit, `|t| ≤ 1` on `Spa(R, R°)`), so
they weaken nothing. Almost zero is written out: (1) says that `f_n(a) ∈ tᵐA⟨tⁿ/g⟩` for `a ∈ A`
forces `t^{1/p^l}a ∈ tᵐA` for every `l`, under the hypothesis that `ga ∈ tᵐA` forces the same; (2)
says that for every `x ∈ A⟨t^{n+c}/g⟩` with `c = pᵏm` and every `l`, `g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l}ρ_{n+c,n}(x)`
lies in `f_n(A) + tᵐA⟨tⁿ/g⟩`. Neither statement uses the normalisation `|t| = |p|`. (3) unfolds,
by the definition of §0.1, to the conjunction of (2) with a kernel clause for the same offset; for
almost regular `g` the kernel clause is (1), and without regularity it is
`bhatt_kernel_without_regularity` (§1.4). Raising the two offsets to their maximum gives (3), so it is
not stated separately. In `Suggested.lean` the index `n` of all three statements runs over all
natural numbers: `n = 0` is the locus `{1 ≤ |g|}`, where the same presentation and proof apply
(Bhatt states (2) for `n ≥ 0`, Theorem 4.2(2), p. 6).

*Proof sketch.* Replace `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ` by its almost presentation `M_n` (§1.1). Kernels: every
`g^{1/p^k}` is an almost nonzerodivisor on `A/tᵐ`, and comparing coefficients of the monomials `u_n^e`
shows that `A/tᵐ → M_n` is almost injective. Cokernels: the `c`-fold transition sends
`g^{1/p^k}u_{n+c}^e` to `g^{1/p^k}t^{ce}u_n^e`. If `e ≥ 1/pᵏ` then `ce ≥ m` and the term vanishes
modulo `tᵐ`; if `e < 1/pᵏ` it equals `g^{1/p^k−e}t^{ce}(gu_n)^e = g^{1/p^k−e}t^{(n+c)e}`, which lies in
the image of `A/tᵐ`. (3) follows from (1), (2) and §0.2.

*Needs:* §0.1, §0.2, §1.1; PerfectoidSpaces P2.3.

**Checks.**

- Every `c ≥ pᵏm` works for every `n`, and no optimality is claimed: for `e ≥ 1/pᵏ` the term
  `g^{1/p^k}t^{ce}u_n^e` equals `t^{ce + n/p^k}u_n^{e − 1/p^k}`, which already vanishes once
  `ce + n/pᵏ ≥ m`; a statement asserting that `pᵏm` is the least constant would be false.
- For `m = 0` all rings are zero and the statement is empty.
- At `n = 0`, for `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T`, the presentation is
  `M_0 = (K°/tᵐ)[T^{±1/p^∞}]`, the localisation of `A/tᵐ` at `T`, so `ker f_0 = 0`; with `c = pᵏm`
  the transition sends `T^{1/p^k}u_c^e` to `T^{1/p^k}t^{ce}u_0^e`, which is `0` for `e ≥ 1/pᵏ` and
  `T^{1/p^k − e}t^{ce}` in the image of `A/tᵐ` otherwise. A statement restricted to `n ≥ 1` would
  omit a case that holds.
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T`, (3) is the integral almost form of the statement that bounded
  functions on the punctured perfectoid disc extend.
- The non-example of §1.1 (`g = (T, 0)`) shows that (1) fails without the hypothesis on `g`.

### 1.3 derived form of the quantitative Riemann extension theorem

In the setting of §1.2 (with `g` almost regular modulo `tᵐ`, `m ≥ 1`), prove that for every
complex `K ∈ D(A/tᵐ)` and every `i ∈ ℤ` the kernel and cokernel towers of
`H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L_{A/tᵐ} A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ)` have `pᵏm`-step transitions killed by `g^{1/p^k}` in
`t`-almost modules, hence by `(tg)^{1/p^k}`, for every `k` and `n`; the constants do not depend on
`n`, `K` or `i`. In particular, if `x₁, …, x_r` is a regular sequence in `A/tᵐ`, then for `j ≠ 0` the
Koszul homology towers `H_j(Kos(A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ; x))` are uniformly almost-pro-zero with respect to
`g^{1/p^∞}` in `t`-almost modules (Bhatt, Remark 4.3, p. 7; Remark 1.8, p. 3).

*Proof sketch.* Apply §0.5 with `R = A/tᵐ`, `R_n = A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ` and the constants of §1.2. For the
Koszul statement take `K` the Koszul complex `K(x)` over `A/tᵐ`, quasi-isomorphic to
`(A/tᵐ)/(x)` by regularity, so that `K ⊗^L R_n` is the Koszul complex over `R_n` and `H^i(K) = 0` for
`i ≠ 0`.

*Needs:* §0.5, §1.2; DerivedModuleAlgebra §§0.K.1, 0.2.

**Checks.**

- For `K = A/tᵐ` this is §1.2.
- The Koszul consequence is the case recorded in Bhatt, Remark 4.3.
- Regularity of the sequence is needed only for the vanishing of `H^i(K)` in nonzero degrees; for
  `x = (0)` the source Koszul homology `H_1 = A/tᵐ` is not zero, and the comparison then concerns a
  nonzero `H_1` tower.

### 1.4 quantitative Riemann extension without regularity of `g`

Let `A` be an integral perfectoid `K°`-algebra and `g ∈ A` with a root system, with no regularity
hypothesis on `g`. Prove that for every `m ≥ 0` the map of towers `(A/tᵐ)_n → (A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ)_n` is an
almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to `(tg)^{1/p^∞}` (Bhatt, Remark 4.4, p. 7). Only conclusion (3)
of §1.2 survives; almost injectivity of each `f_n` can fail (§1.1).

`bhatt_kernel_without_regularity` states the kernel half on the same presentationwise carriers
as §1.2: for every `m, k, n` there exists `c` such that every `a ∈ R°` with
`f_{n+c}(a) ∈ tᵐA⟨t^{n+c}/g⟩` satisfies
`g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^k}a ∈ tᵐR°`. The offset is chosen before `a`. The cokernel half is
`bhatt_quantitative_riemann_extension`, which has no regularity binder and uses `c = pᵏm`.
Increasing the two offsets to their maximum gives the combined almost-pro-isomorphism.


*Proof sketch.* In the universal case `R = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `g = T`, the element `T` is a nonzerodivisor
on `R/tᵐ = (K°/tᵐ)[T^{1/p^∞}]` and §1.2 applies. The universal property of perfected Tate algebras
(PerfectoidSpaces P1.30) gives a unique continuous `K°`-algebra map `R → A` with
`T^{1/p^k} ↦ g^{1/p^k}`. Base change along `R/tᵐ → A/tᵐ` preserves the almost-pro-isomorphism by
§1.3 applied to `K = A/tᵐ` over `R/tᵐ`, and the almost presentations of §1.1 are compatible with
base change, so `R⟨tⁿ/T⟩ ⊗_R A/tᵐ` is almost `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ`; take `H^0`.

*Needs:* §1.1, §1.3; PerfectoidSpaces P1.30.

**Checks.**

- For `g` a nonzerodivisor modulo `tᵐ` this recovers §1.2(3).
- For `g = 0` the root ideal of `tg` is zero, every module is almost zero for it, and the statement is
  empty, matching `A⟨tⁿ/0⟩ = 0`.
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ × K°` and `g = (T, 0)` the conclusion holds although §1.2(1) fails: the
  kernel `(0) × K°` is killed by `(tg)^{1/p^k} = (t^{1/p^k}T^{1/p^k}, 0)`.

### 1.5 Riemann extension after limits

Let `A` be an integral perfectoid `K°`-algebra and `g ∈ A` with a root system. Prove that the
canonical map `A → lim_n A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` is an almost isomorphism with respect to `(tg)^{1/p^∞}`: its kernel
and cokernel are killed by every `(tg)^{1/p^k}` (Bhatt, Remark 1.7, p. 3). Geometrically, a bounded
function on the Zariski open `{g ≠ 0} = ⋃_n X⟨tⁿ/g⟩` of `X` almost extends to `X`. This is the
principal case of Scholze's Hebbarkeitssatz in mixed characteristic.

`bhatt_riemann_extension_limit` states it on the tower of §1.1, with the hypotheses on `K`, `R`, `t`
and `g` of §1.2 and no regularity hypothesis on `g`. The limit is `CategoryTheory.Limits.limit` of
`annularTower` in commutative rings, and the canonical map is read componentwise: the image of
`a ∈ R°` in `𝒪_X(X⟨tⁿ/g⟩)` is `coeffToSections`, and a component of an element of the limit is
compared through the inclusion `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.presentationLimitPlusPresheafι : 𝒪⁺ ⟶ 𝒪`. Kernel: if the image of
`a ∈ R°` vanishes on every annulus, then `(tg)^{1/p^k}a = 0` for every `k`. Cokernel: for every
element `x` of the limit and every `k` there is `a ∈ R°` whose image on every annulus is
`(tg)^{1/p^k}xₙ`, with the same `a` for all `n`. The monotonicity of `n ↦ X⟨tⁿ/g⟩`, needed to form
the tower, is a hypothesis; it always holds, since `t ∈ R°` gives `|t| ≤ 1` on `Spa(R, R°)` and hence
`|t^{n+1}| ≤ |tⁿ| ≤ |g|`. The index runs over `n ≥ 0`; the term at `n = 0` does not change the
limit of a tower.

*Proof sketch.* For each `m`, §1.4 and §0.3 give that `A/tᵐ → R lim_n A⟨tⁿ/g⟩/tᵐ` is an almost
isomorphism. Then take `R lim` over `m`: `A` and every `A⟨tⁿ/g⟩` are `t`-adically complete and the
towers over `m` have surjective transitions, so limits over `m` and `n` commute and no `lim¹` in `m`
appears; the almost isomorphism passes to the limit (PerfectoidSpaces P0.27).

The kernel statement is exact, not only almost modulo `tᵐ`: if `a` dies on every annulus, then for
every `m` the kernel half of §1.4 puts `(tg)^{1/p^k}a` in `tᵐA`, and `⋂_m tᵐA = 0` because `A` is
bounded in the Hausdorff ring `R` and `tᵐ → 0`.

*Needs:* §0.3, §1.1, §1.4; PerfectoidSpaces P0.27; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.presentationLimitPlusPresheaf`,
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.toPresentationLimit`; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.Limits.limit`,
`CommRingCat.hasLimits`.

**Checks.**

- For `g` a unit both sides equal `A`.
- For `g = 0` (all roots `0`) every annulus `{|tⁿ| ≤ |0| ≠ 0}` is empty and the limit is the zero
  ring, so the kernel is all of `A`; it is killed by `(tg)^{1/p^k} = 0`, and the cokernel clause
  holds with `a = 0`. A version asserting injectivity, or almost injectivity for `t^{1/p^∞}` alone,
  fails here for `A ≠ 0`.
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ × K°` and `g = (T, 0)` the kernel is `(0) × K°`: the annuli lie in the
  first component. It is killed by `(tg)^{1/p^k} = (t^{1/p^k}T^{1/p^k}, 0)` but not by `t^{1/p^k}`, so
  the almost setup of the limit form is that of `(tg)^{1/p^∞}`, as stated.
- When `g` is a nonzerodivisor, André's theorem (§3.6) identifies the limit exactly with
  `g^{-1/p^∞}A`, consistently with this almost statement.
- For `A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T` the map is an isomorphism onto `lim_n A⟨tⁿ/T⟩ = A` (§2.3's
  computation of the root saturation), so the almost statement is sharp there.

### Examples

## Layer 2: Uniform Banach algebras: closures, localisations, uniform limits and colimits

This layer is André's dictionary between uniform Banach algebras and their unit balls, with the
constructions that the perfectoid Riemann extension and Abhyankar arguments manipulate. Mathlib
supplies the integral and complete integral closures, AdicSpacesPartII the spectral seminorm,
uniformization and uniform completed tensor products. Added here are the `p`-root closure, root
saturation, Banach localisations with their unit balls, uniform limits and colimits, root algebras,
and the description of the root algebra as a colimit of tubes around `T = g`. `K` is a field
complete for a nontrivial nonarchimedean absolute value, with `|K^×|` dense in `ℝ_{>0}` where stated.

### 2.1 the `p`-root closure and the localised complete integral closure

Let `R → S` be an injective map of commutative rings. Define an element `s ∈ S` to be
**`p`-radicial** over `R` if `s^{pⁿ} ∈ R` for some `n ≥ 0`, `R` to be **`p`-root closed** in `S` if
`s ∈ S` and `s^p ∈ R` imply `s ∈ R`, and the **`p`-root closure** `R^†_S` as the smallest subring of
`S` containing `R` and `p`-root closed in `S`; it is the increasing union of `R_0 = R` and
`R_{i+1}` = the subring generated by the elements `p`-radicial over `R_i` (André A, §2.3.1, p. 15).
For `g ∈ R` a nonzerodivisor of `S` with `S = R[1/g]`, define the **localised complete integral
closure** `R^*_S = {s ∈ S : ∃ m, ∀ n ≥ 0, g^m sⁿ ∈ R}`, the set of `s` whose powers lie in one finitely
generated `R`-submodule of `S` (André A, §2.5.5, (2.18)–(2.19), p. 26). Then
`R ⊆ R^†_S ⊆ R^+_S ⊆ R^*_S`, with `R^+_S` the integral closure. The intended signatures are a
subalgebra of `S` over `R` for each closure. André's complete integral closure uses finitely generated
submodules; Mathlib's `completeIntegralClosure` uses a multiplier from `R⁰`, and for `S = R[1/g]`
with `g ∈ R⁰` André's is contained in Mathlib's. *Needs:* Mathlib `integralClosure`,
`completeIntegralClosure`, `IsAlmostIntegral`, `Localization.Away`, `Submodule.FG`.

*Construction.* The union of the `R_i` is `p`-root closed (if `s^p ∈ R_i` then `s ∈ R_{i+1}`), and
every `p`-root closed intermediate ring contains every `R_i` by induction. In characteristic `p`,
`(a + b)^{pⁿ} = a^{pⁿ} + b^{pⁿ}`, so the `p`-radicial elements already form a subring; for `S = R[1/p]`
André cites Roberts for the same conclusion. A `p`-radicial element is integral, and an integral
element of `R[1/g]` has its powers in the module spanned by its first `d` powers. A finitely generated
`R`-submodule of `R[1/g]` lies in `g^{−m}R` for some `m`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The `p`-root closure is `p`-root closed in `S` and is contained in every `p`-root closed
  intermediate subring.
- If `S` has characteristic `p`, `s` lies in the `p`-root closure iff `s^{pⁿ} ∈ R` for some `n`.
- The `p`-root closure is contained in the integral closure, which is contained in the localised
  complete integral closure.
- For `g ∈ R⁰`, the localised complete integral closure is contained in Mathlib's
  `completeIntegralClosure R R[1/g]`.
- A commutative square `R → S`, `R′ → S′` induces maps of `p`-root closures, of integral closures
  and of complete integral closures.
- For a ring `R` with a root system of a nonzerodivisor `t`, the complete integral closure of `R` in
  `R[1/t]` is the root saturation `t^{-1/p^∞}` of the `p`-root closure (André A, Remarque 2.5.6,
  p. 27).

**Checks.**

- The `p`-root closure of `R` in itself is `R`.
- For `R = 𝔽_p[T]` inside `S = 𝔽_p[T^{1/p}]` the `p`-root closure is `S`: `T^{1/p}` has `p`-th power
  `T ∈ R`.
- For `p = 3`, `R = ℤ` and `S = ℤ[i]`, the `p`-root closure is `ℤ` (if `(a + bi)³ ∈ ℤ` then
  `b(3a² − b²) = 0`, so `b = 0`; no Gaussian integer outside `ℤ` has a power `3ⁿ` in `ℤ` either, since
  its argument is not a rational multiple of `π/3ⁿ`), while `i` is integral: `R^†_S ≠ R^+_S`, and a
  definition of the `p`-root closure as the integral closure fails here.
- For `R = ℤ`, `g = 2`, the localised complete integral closure in `ℤ[1/2]` is `ℤ`: if
  `s = a/2^e` in lowest terms with `e > 0` and `2^m sⁿ ∈ ℤ` for all `n`, then `en ≤ m` for all `n`,
  which forces `e = 0`; this agrees with the integral closure of `ℤ` in `ℤ[1/2]`.

### 2.2 the norm–unit-ball dictionary for uniform Banach algebras

Let `ϖ ∈ K°°` be nonzero and `Γ ⊆ ℝ` with `|ϖ|^Γ = |K^×|`. Prove (André A, Sorite 2.3.1 and proof,
pp. 16–19; Corollaire 2.3.2, p. 19; Remarques 2.3.3, p. 19):

1. For a `K°`-flat `K°`-algebra `A`, the gauge `|a| = |ϖ|^r`, `r = sup{s ∈ Γ : ϖ^{−s}a ∈ A}`, is a
   seminorm on `A[1/ϖ]`, a norm iff `A` has no nonzero infinitely `ϖ`-divisible element; its unit
   ball is `A` when `Γ` is discrete and `A_* = ⋂_{s ∈ Γ_{>0}} ϖ^{−s}A` otherwise. A ring map
   `φ : A[1/ϖ] → A′[1/ϖ]` with `φ(A) ⊆ A′` is continuous; it is isometric iff
   `φ^{−1}(A′_{≤1}) = A_{≤1}` for the gauge unit balls, and `φ^{−1}(A′) = A` suffices.
2. If `|K|` is dense in the norm values of a normed `K`-algebra `A` with unit ball `A_{≤1}`, then the
   closures of `A_{≤1}` in `A` satisfy `A^†_A ⊆ A^+_A ⊆ A^*_A = A°`, and `(A^†_A)_*` is the unit ball of
   the spectral seminorm.
3. Under (2) the following are equivalent: the norm is spectral (power-multiplicative);
   `A_{≤1} = A°`; `A_{≤1}` is completely integrally closed in `A`; it is integrally closed in `A`; it
   is `p`-root closed in `A`; and, when `|ϖ| ≥ |p|` and `1/p ∈ Γ`, Frobenius
   `A_{≤1}/ϖ^{1/p} → A_{≤1}/ϖ` is injective.
4. If `|K|` is dense in `ℝ_{>0}`, `B ↦ B°` is an equivalence from `K-uBan` to the category of
   `K°`-algebras that are `K°`-flat, `ϖ`-adically complete, completely integrally closed
   (equivalently integrally closed, or `p`-root closed) in `B[1/ϖ]` and equal to
   `Hom_{K°}(K°°, B)`; a quasi-inverse is `B ↦ (B[1/ϖ], gauge norm)`. The same holds relative to a
   fixed uniform Banach `K`-algebra.

Uniform means that `A°` is bounded, equivalently that the norm is equivalent to the spectral
seminorm. Density of `|K|` in `ℝ_{>0}` holds when the valuation is not discrete, for instance for
perfectoid `K`.

*Proof sketch.* Construct the gauge using flatness; a linear map is an isometry exactly when it
preserves and reflects every norm bound, and `φ^{−1}(A′) = A` gives this after taking the
intersections that define `A_*`. An element is power-bounded iff its powers lie in a finitely
generated `A_{≤1}`-submodule, so `A^*_A = A°`; the gauge of the `p`-root closure is
`lim |a^{pⁿ}|^{1/pⁿ}`, the spectral seminorm (Fekete's lemma, as in Mathlib `smoothingSeminorm`).
Frobenius injectivity modulo `ϖ` is `p`-root closedness read on reductions; (4) follows from (1) and
(3), since continuous maps of uniform algebras preserve power-bounded elements.

*Needs:* §2.1; AdicSpacesPartII R0.4; AdicSpacesPartIII R3.5; PerfectoidSpaces P0.6; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofValuationRing`, `TauCeti.Huber.spectralSeminorm`;
Mathlib `smoothingSeminorm`.

**Checks.**

- André's example `K°[ϖT, …, ϖ^{i+1}T^{p^i}, …] ⊆ K°[T]` (André A, Remarques 2.3.3(2), p. 19) shows
  that `A^+ ⊆ A^* = A° ⊆` spectral unit ball can all be strict, and that a complete integral closure
  need not be completely integrally closed.
- For densely valued `K`, take `A = K° + K°°T·K°[T] ⊆ A′ = K°[T]`. Both generic fibres are `K[T]` and
  both gauges are the Gauss norm: for every `λ ∈ K` with `|λ| > 1`, `λ^{−1}T ∈ K°°T ⊆ A`, so `|T|_A = 1`.
  The identity is isometric although `T ∈ A′ ∖ A`; the unit ball of the gauge of `A` is `A_* = A′`,
  not `A`.
- For `K` densely valued with `ϖ^{1/2} ∈ K` and the lattice `A = K°[T, ϖ^{−1}T²] ⊆ K[T]` with its
  gauge norm: the coefficient of `T` in an element of `A` lies in `K°`, so `|T| = 1`, while
  `T² = ϖ·(ϖ^{−1}T²)` gives `|T²| ≤ |ϖ| < 1`; the gauge is not power-multiplicative. The element
  `ϖ^{−1/2}T` is integral over `A` (its square lies in `A`) and power-bounded, but has gauge
  `|ϖ|^{−1/2} > 1`. So `A_{≤1} ≠ A°` and `A_{≤1}` is not integrally closed, as (3) predicts.
- For discrete `Γ` (`K = ℚ_p`, `ϖ = p`) and `A = ℤ_p + pT·ℤ_p[T] ⊆ ℚ_p[T]`, the gauge of `T` is `p`,
  attained, and the unit ball is `A` itself; for densely valued `K` the analogous lattice of the
  second Check has unit ball `A_* = K°[T] ≠ A`. A definition using `A_*` in the discrete case would
  give `⋂_{s ≥ 1} p^{−s}A = p^{−1}A ≠ A` and fail here.

### 2.3 the root saturation `g^{-1/p^∞}B°`

Let `|K|` be dense in `ℝ_{>0}` and `B` a uniform Banach `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra, with `g^{1/p^m}` the
image of `T^{1/p^m}`; equivalently a uniform Banach `K`-algebra with a root system of `g ∈ B°`.
Define the **root saturation**
`g^{-1/p^∞}B° = ⋂_m g^{−1/p^m}B° = {b ∈ B°[1/g] : g^{1/p^m}b ∈ B° for all m}` (André A, §2.5.2,
Lemme 2.5.1 and (2.12)–(2.16), pp. 24–25). In the almost setup
`(V, m) = (K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°V)` it is the module of almost elements: `(B°^a)_*`, the
limit of `B°` along multiplication by `g^{1/p^m − 1/p^{m+1}}`, maps injectively to `B°[1/g]` with
image `g^{-1/p^∞}B°`; this image is `ϖ`-adically complete, and `B° → g^{-1/p^∞}B°` is injective iff
`g` is a nonzerodivisor of `B`. Prove that the following are equivalent (André A, Lemme 2.5.3 with
proof, pp. 26–27): `g^{-1/p^∞}B°` is `p`-root closed in `B°[1/g]`; it is `p`-root closed in
`B[1/g]`; it equals the complete integral closure of `B°` in `B°[1/g]`; it equals the complete
integral closure of `B°` in `B[1/g]`; it is completely integrally closed in `B°[1/g]`; it is
completely integrally closed in `B[1/g]`. Root saturation commutes with projective limits:
`g^{-1/p^∞}(lim B_α°) = lim g^{-1/p^∞}B_α°` (André A, Lemme 2.8.2, p. 32). `Suggested.lean`
defines the root saturation for every nonarchimedean topological ring `R` and every compatible
system `g` of `p`-power roots in `R°` (`g (m + 1) ^ p = g m`, proof `hg`) as `rootSaturation p g hg`,
the `R°`-subalgebra `{b ∈ R[1/g] : g^{1/p^m}b ∈ R° for all m}` of `R[1/g]`. It is closed under
products because `g^{1/p^m} = g^{1/p^{m+1}}·(g^{1/p^{m+1}})^{p−2}·g^{1/p^{m+1}}` and `p ≥ 2`. Taken
in `R[1/g]` instead of `R°[1/g]` it is the same set: `R°[1/g] → R[1/g]` is injective because
localisation is exact, and `gb ∈ R°` puts `b` in its image. The facts below concern uniform Banach
algebras. *Needs:* §2.1, §2.2; PerfectoidSpaces P0.6, P0.16; Tau Ceti
`TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`.

*Construction.* Since `B` is uniform, `Hom_V(V°°, B°) = B°`; as `m` is the colimit of `V°°` along
multiplication by `T^{1/p^m − 1/p^{m+1}}`, `(B°^a)_*` is the stated limit, a reduced ring on which `g`
is almost a nonzerodivisor, so it embeds in `B°[1/g]` with image `g^{-1/p^∞}B°`; completeness comes from
André A, Lemme 2.3.4. For the closedness conditions,
`g^{-1/p^∞}B° ⊆ B°^*_{B°[1/g]} ⊆ B°^*_{B[1/g]} ⊆ (g^{-1/p^∞}B°)^*_{B[1/g]}`, so the last condition implies
the others; the first implies the second by §2.2, and the second implies the last because
`ϖ^{m/pⁿ}g^{m/pⁿ}b` lies in `g^{-1/p^∞}B°` for all `n`. Almostification and almost elements preserve
limits.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The image of `B°` lies in the root saturation; the map is injective iff `g` is a nonzerodivisor of
  `B`.
- The root saturation is the image of the almost elements `(B°^a)_*` in the setup
  `(K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°)`.
- If `g` is a unit of `B` the root saturation is `B°` (André A, Lemme 2.5.2, p. 25).
- The root saturation is `ϖ`-adically complete.
- The six closedness conditions above are equivalent.
- Root saturation commutes with projective limits of uniform Banach `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebras.
- For a ring `R` with a root system of a nonzerodivisor `t`, the complete integral closure of `R` in
  `R[1/t]` equals the root saturation of the `p`-root closure of `R` in `R[1/t]`.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 1` the root saturation is `B°`.
- For `B = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T` the root saturation is `K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`: if `b = c/T^N` with
  `c ∈ K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `T^{1/p^m}b ∈ K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` for every `m`, then `T^{N − 1/p^m}` divides `c`
  for every `m`; divisibility by a monomial is read on exponents, so `T^N` divides `c` and `b ∈ B°`.
- For `B = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ × K` and `g = (T, 0)`, `B° → B°[1/g]` kills `(0, 1)`: the map to the root
  saturation is not injective (André A, Lemme 2.5.1(2)).
- Every root of `g` is needed, not only `g` itself: for `B = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T`, the element
  `b = T^{−(1 − 1/p)}` of `B[1/T]` has `gb = T^{1/p} ∈ B°`, but `g^{1/p^m}b = T^{1/p^m + 1/p − 1}` has a
  negative exponent once `1/p^m + 1/p < 1` (`m = 1` for `p ≥ 3`, `m = 2` for `p = 2`), so `b` is not
  in the root saturation `B°`. A definition testing only `m = 0` would contain `b`.
- In characteristic `p`, for `P = K⟨T₁^{1/p^∞}, T₂^{1/p^∞}⟩` and the closed subalgebra
  `B = K + (Σ_m T₁^{1/p^m}P)^−` with `g = T₁`, the root saturation of `B°` is `P°`, strictly larger than
  `B°` although `B` is perfectoid (André A, §3.5.3, pp. 45–46): `T₂ ∉ B` but `T₁^{1/p^m}T₂ ∈ B` for every
  `m`. A definition of root saturation that returned `B°` for perfectoid `B` fails here.

### 2.4 the Laurent localisation `A{λ/g}`

Let `A` be a uniform Banach `K`-algebra. For `f₁, …, f_n, f ∈ A` generating the unit ideal define
`A{f₁, …, f_n/f} = A⟨U₁, …, U_n⟩/(fU_i − f_i)^−`, the quotient by the closure of the ideal, the
universal Banach `A`-algebra in which `f` is invertible and the `f_i/f` are power-bounded (André A,
§2.6.2, (2.21)–(2.24), pp. 27–28). For `0 ≠ λ ∈ K°` and `g = λf ∈ A°` define the **Laurent
localisation** `A{λ/g} := A{1/f} = A⟨U⟩/(fU − 1)^−`, a `K⟨T⟩`-algebra through `T ↦ g`; it is
AdicSpaces' rational localisation of the Huber pair `(A, A°)` at `({λ, g}, g)` with its quotient norm.
The intended signature is a Banach `K`-algebra attached to `A`, `λ` and `g`, with its universal map
from `A`. Prove (André A, Lemme 2.6.1, p. 28; Lemmes 2.6.2–2.6.3, p. 29; Exemples 2.6.3 and
Remarques 2.6.4, pp. 29–30):

1. for uniform `A` the ideal `(fU − 1)` is closed;
2. `A{λ/g} ≅ A ⊗̂_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T⟩{λ/T}` isometrically; `A{λ/g} = 0` when `g ∈ λA°°`; for `|λ| ≤ |μ|`
   there are canonical maps `A{λ/g} → A{μ/g}` and `A{μ/g} ≅ A{λ/g}{μ/g}`; if `|g − h| < |λ|` then
   `A{λ/g} ≅ A{λ/h}`;
3. the image of `a ∈ A` has quotient norm `inf_{h ∈ A⟨U⟩} ‖a + (fU − 1)h‖_Gauss`, which in general
   differs from `inf_m |(g/λ)^m a|`;
4. the uniformization `A{λ/g}^u` is left adjoint, on uniform `K⟨T⟩`-algebras, to the inclusion of
   those in which `T/λ` is invertible with power-bounded inverse;
5. (Gabber's invariance) if `g` is a nonzerodivisor and `A ⊆ A′ ⊆ A[1/g]` is a Banach algebra
   extension, then `A{λ/g} → A′{λ/g}` is an isomorphism of Banach algebras;
6. if `A` is uniform and `g` a nonzerodivisor, `A° → lim_{λ→0} A{λ/g}°` is injective.

`A{λ/g}` need not be uniform; only its uniformization is used in `K-uBan`. Restriction maps between
individual annuli need not be injective.

*Proof sketch.* (1) after Mihara: replace the norm by the equivalent spectral norm; at each bounded
multiplicative point `x` the Gauss extension satisfies `‖(fU − 1)h‖_x = max(1, |f(x)|)‖h‖_x ≥ ‖h‖_x`,
and taking the supremum over `x` bounds multiplication by `fU − 1` below, so its image is complete and
closed. (2) from the presentations and universal properties; for `|g − h| < |λ|` use the convergent
expansion `1/h = Σ_{m≥0}(g − h)^m/g^{m+1}` in `A{λ/g}`. (5) by Baire and the open mapping theorem,
multiplication by a power `g^N` maps `A′` continuously into `A`. (6) for nonzero `a` and a
nonzerodivisor `g`, `ag ≠ 0`; the spectral seminorm of a uniform Banach algebra is a norm, so some
bounded multiplicative seminorm `x` has `|a(x)g(x)| > 0` (AdicSpacesPartIII R3.5, R3.9); for nonzero
`λ ∈ K°` with `|λ| ≤ |g(x)|` the point `x` extends to `A{λ/g}`, where it detects the image of `a`.

*Needs:* §2.2; AdicSpaces §3.1; AdicSpacesPartII R0.3, R0.4; AdicSpacesPartIII R3.5, R3.9.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- A continuous map `φ : A → B` of Banach algebras with `φ(g)/λ` invertible with power-bounded inverse
  factors uniquely through `A{λ/g}`; two maps out of `A{λ/g}` that agree on `A` are equal.
- For uniform `A` the ideal `(fU − 1)` of `A⟨U⟩` is closed.
- `A{λ/g} ≅ A ⊗̂_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T⟩{λ/T}` isometrically.
- For `|λ| ≤ |μ|` there is a map `A{λ/g} → A{μ/g}` and `A{μ/g} ≅ A{λ/g}{μ/g}`.
- If `|g − h| < |λ|` then `A{λ/g} ≅ A{λ/h}`.
- The quotient norm of the image of `a` is `inf_{h ∈ A⟨U⟩} ‖a + ((g/λ)U − 1)h‖_Gauss`.
- Gabber's invariance under Banach extensions `A ⊆ A′ ⊆ A[1/g]` for a nonzerodivisor `g`.
- For uniform `A` and a nonzerodivisor `g`, `A° → lim_λ A{λ/g}°` is injective.
- A bounded multiplicative seminorm `x` on `A` with `|g(x)| ≥ |λ|` extends to `A{λ/g}`; consequently
  the norm of the image of `a` is at least `|a(x)|`.

**Checks.**

- For `0 < |λ| ≤ 1` and `A = K⟨T⟩`, `A{λ/T} = K⟨T, λ/T⟩` is the closed annulus `|λ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1`, with
  unit ball `K°⟨T, λ/T⟩`; its quotient norm is spectral, and multiplicative exactly when `|λ| = 1`.
- If `g ∈ λA°°` then `A{λ/g} = 0`, since `fU − 1` is a unit; if `|λ| = 1` and `g = 1` then
  `A{λ/g} = A`.
- For `0 < |λ| < 1`, `K⟨T⟩{λ/T}` and `K⟨T⟩{λ²/T²}` are the same rational localisation (both describe
  `|λ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1`), but their presentation norms give `λ/T` the norms `1` and `|λ|^{−1}`: in the second
  presentation a representative `Σ a_{ij}T^iU^j` of `λ/T` has `Σ_{j≥1} a_{2j−1,j}λ^{2j} = λ`, so some
  `|a_{2j−1,j}| ≥ |λ|^{1−2j} ≥ |λ|^{−1}`, attained by `λ^{−1}TU`. The second norm is not spectral.
- For `A = K × K` with the max norm, `q = |ϖ|`, `λ = ϖ`, `g = (ϖ², 1)` and `a = (1, ϖ²)`: the first
  factor of `A{λ/g}` is zero and the second is `K` with `U = ϖ`, so the image of `a` has norm `q²`,
  while `inf_{m≥0} ‖(g/λ)^m a‖ = inf_m max(q^m, q^{2−m}) = q`. The one-parameter infimum is not the
  quotient norm, even for uniform `A` and regular `g`.
- For the same `A` and `g`, the restriction `A{ϖ³/g} → A{ϖ/g}` kills the first factor although `g` is
  a nonzerodivisor: limit injectivity (6) cannot be proved by asking every restriction to be injective.

### 2.5 the Weierstrass localisation `B{f/λ}` and its unit ball

Let `0 ≠ λ ∈ K°`, `B` a uniform Banach `K`-algebra and `f ∈ B_{≤1}`. Prove: `B{f/λ} = B⟨U⟩/(λU − f)`,
the ideal being closed; if `f` is a nonzerodivisor of `B` so is `λU − f` in `B⟨U⟩` (André B, §1.2,
formula (1), p. 76). Suppose further that `|K|` is dense in `|B|` and multiplication by `f` is
isometric (for instance the norm is multiplicative and `|f| = 1`). Then `B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f)` is
torsion-free over `K°`, `(λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩ = ((λU − f)B⟨U⟩)_{≤1}`, and the unit ball of `B{f/λ}` is
`B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f)` if the valuation is discrete and `(B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f))_*` otherwise (André B,
§1.2.1, formulas (2)–(3), pp. 76–77). Moreover `(λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩` is closed, and
`B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f)` is `ϖ`-adically separated and equals the `ϖ`-adic completion of
`B_{≤1}[f/λ] ⊆ B`, through the exact sequence
`0 → (λU − f)B_{≤1}[U] → B_{≤1}[U] → B_{≤1}[f/λ] → 0` and its completion (André B, §1.2.2, formulas
(4)–(6), p. 77). Finally `B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f)` does not change, up to isomorphism, when `f` is replaced
by `f′` with `|f − f′| ≤ |λ|` or `λ` by `λ′` with `|λ′| = |λ|`; the map from the new presentation to
the old one sends `U_new ↦ (λ/λ′)U_old + (f′ − f)/λ′` (André B, §1.2.3, p. 77). The torsion-freeness
is over `K°` (the computation with an arbitrary nonzero `μ ∈ K°` in place of `λ`).

*Proof sketch.* Closedness is the Weierstrass case of §2.4(1). If
`μ·Σ b_mU^m = (λU − f)Σ a_mU^m` with `a_m ∈ B_{≤1}`, comparing coefficients and using that
multiplication by `f` is injective modulo `μ` (it is isometric) shows that `μ` divides every `a_m`; this
gives torsion-freeness and the equality of unit balls, then (2)–(3) by §2.2. The polynomial sequence
is exact by the same torsion-freeness; its completion is right exact with dense image of
`(λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩` in the kernel, and closedness gives exactness. The substitution
`U_new ↦ (λ/λ′)U_old + (f′ − f)/λ′` is bounded on integral Tate algebras and sends `λ′U_new − f′` to
`λU_old − f`; its inverse is `U_old ↦ (λ′/λ)U_new + (f − f′)/λ`.

*Needs:* §2.2, §2.4; Mathlib `IsAdicComplete`.

**Checks.**

- For `B = K⟨T⟩` with the Gauss norm, `f = T` and `λ = ϖ`, `B{T/ϖ} = K⟨T/ϖ⟩` is the disc of radius
  `|ϖ|` and its unit ball is `K°⟨U⟩` with `T = ϖU`.
- The invariance under `|f − f′| ≤ |λ|` is what allows André B to replace `(T − g)^{1/p^k}` by an
  element of a Noetherian stage (André B, formula (14), p. 80).
- Isometry of multiplication by `f` is needed: for `B = K⟨T⟩`, `f = ϖT` and `λ = ϖ²`, multiplication
  by `f` is not isometric and `B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(ϖ²U − ϖT)` has the nonzero `ϖ`-torsion class of `ϖU − T`.

### 2.6 uniform limits

The category `K-uBan` has all small limits. Define, for a projective system `(A_α)` indexed by a (not
necessarily filtered) ordered set, the **uniform limit**
`ulim A_α = {a = (a_α) ∈ lim A_α : sup_α |a_α|_sp < ∞}` with the norm `sup_α |a_α|_sp`; it is a
spectral Banach `K`-algebra with `(ulim A_α)° = lim A_α°`, so the conditions of §2.2(3) pass to
limits, and `(ulim A_α)°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ)` is injective (André A, §2.8.1, (2.27)–(2.28), p. 31;
Exemples 2.8.2 and Lemme 2.8.1, pp. 31–32). It is computed with spectral norms and differs in general
from the limit in perfectoid algebras (§3.8). The intended signature is a functor from projective
systems in `K-uBan` to `K-uBan` with its universal cone. *Needs:* §2.2; Mathlib
`CategoryTheory.Limits.limit`.

*Construction.* Equalizers are closed subalgebras and products are the bounded families, so the
category is complete. The unit ball of the supremum of spectral norms is the set of families with every
`a_α` power-bounded. Each `A_α°` is `ϖ`-torsion-free, so `0 → lim A_α° → lim A_α° → lim(A_α°/ϖ)`, the
first map multiplication by `ϖ`, is exact.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The projections `ulim A_α → A_α` are continuous homomorphisms compatible with the system.
- Compatible continuous maps `B → A_α` from a uniform Banach algebra factor uniquely through the
  uniform limit.
- The norm of `a` is `sup_α |a_α|_sp`.
- `(ulim A_α)° = lim A_α°` as subrings of `lim A_α`.
- `(ulim A_α)°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ)` is injective.
- A morphism of projective systems induces a map of uniform limits, functorially.

**Checks.**

- The uniform limit of a constant diagram `A` on a nonempty connected category is `A` with its
  spectral norm.
- `ulim_j K⟨T, ϖ^j/T⟩ = K⟨T⟩` (André A, Exemple 2.8.2(2)): a Laurent series `Σ_{n∈ℤ} a_nT^n` with
  `|a_{−n}||ϖ|^{−jn} ≤ C` for all `j` has `a_{−n} = 0`.
- The uniform limit of the closed discs of radius `r_i ↑ 1` (`r_i ∈ |K|`) is `K°⟦T⟧ ⊗_{K°} K`, the
  bounded functions on the open unit disc.
- For the same discs the algebraic limit contains `Σ T^n/ϖ^{⌊√n⌋}`, which converges on each disc of
  radius `< 1` but has unbounded spectral norms: the boundedness condition cannot be dropped.

### 2.7 uniform filtered colimits

Let `(B_α)` be a filtered inductive system in `K-uBan`. The colimit seminorm `|(b_α)| = lim_α |b_α|_sp`
on `colim B_α` is well defined; define the **uniform colimit** `ucolim B_α` as the separated
completion of `colim B_α` for it, a spectral Banach `K`-algebra, equivalently the uniformization
(AdicSpacesPartII R0.4) of the colimit Tate ring (André A, §§2.9.1–2.9.2 and Lemme 2.9.1, p. 32;
Exemples 2.9.3, p. 33). Prove that it is the filtered colimit in `K-uBan`, that
`(ucolim B_α)° = (completed colim B_α°)_*`, and that, if the norms are spectral and the transitions
isometric, the maps `B_β → ucolim B_α` are isometric and `(ucolim B_α)°` is the completed colimit of the
unit balls; `K-uBan` is cocomplete with pushouts the uniform completed tensor products, and uniform
colimits commute with the uniform localisations `(·){λ/g}^u`. The colimit in `K-uBan` is not the
colimit of underlying rings. For perfectoid `B_α` it is PerfectoidSpaces P1.29's completed filtered
colimit. *Needs:* §2.2, §2.4; AdicSpacesPartII R0.4; PerfectoidSpaces P1.29.

*Construction.* Spectral seminorms decrease along continuous maps, so the limit seminorm is well
defined and power-multiplicative; its separated completion receives compatible maps and has the
universal property among uniform algebras. The unit-ball formula is §2.2. Filtered colimits,
pushouts `⊗̂^u` and the initial object `K` give all colimits; `ucolim` commutes with `⊗̂^u`, and
`A{λ/g}^u ≅ A ⊗̂^u_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T⟩{λ/T}`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The canonical maps `B_β → ucolim B_α` are continuous.
- Compatible continuous maps `B_α → C` to a uniform Banach algebra factor uniquely through `ucolim`.
- `(ucolim B_α)° = (completed colim B_α°)_*`.
- If the norms are spectral and the transitions isometric, each `B_β → ucolim B_α` is isometric.
- `ucolim B_α` is the uniformization of the colimit Tate ring.
- `ucolim` commutes with `(·){λ/g}^u`.

**Checks.**

- The uniform colimit of a constant system `B` is `B` with its spectral norm.
- For the shrinking discs, `ucolim_i K⟨T/ϖ^i⟩ = K` (André A, Exemple 2.9.3(4)); the transitions
  restrict functions to smaller discs and the universal cocone to `K` is evaluation at `T = 0`.
- For the same system the algebraic colimit is the ring of overconvergent germs at `0`, which is not
  `K`: the separated completion for the spectral seminorm cannot be omitted.
- If `char K = p`, `ucolim K^{1/p^i}` is the completion of the perfect closure of `K` (André A,
  Exemple 2.9.3(1), p. 33), agreeing with the completion of Mathlib's `PerfectClosure K p`, the
  direct limit along Frobenius. Mathlib's `Perfection K p` is the inverse limit along Frobenius,
  which for `K = 𝔽_p(T)` is `⋂_n K^{p^n} = 𝔽_p`, not the perfect closure `𝔽_p(T^{1/p^∞})`.
- Every uniform Banach `K`-algebra is a filtered uniform colimit of reduced affinoid algebras with
  bijective algebraic colimit map (André A, Exemple 2.9.3(3), p. 33).

### 2.8 the root algebras `A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩` and `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩`

Let `A` be a spectral Banach `K`-algebra and `g ∈ A°`, a `K⟨T⟩`-algebra through `T ↦ g`. Define
`A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ = A ⊗̂^u_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T^{1/p^i}⟩` and the **root algebra**
`A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ = ucolim_i A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ = A ⊗̂^u_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` (André A, Exemple 2.9.3(2),
formulas (2.29)–(2.30), p. 33). Prove that `A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ → A⟨g^{1/p^{i+1}}⟩` and
`A → A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` are isometric, that `A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ = (A⟨T^{1/p^i}⟩/(T − g))^u`, and that the images
of the `T^{1/p^i}` form a root system of `g`, universal among uniform Banach `A`-algebras with a root
system of `g`. The isometry uses the orthogonal basis `1, T^{1/p^{i+1}}, …, T^{(p−1)/p^{i+1}}` of
`K⟨T^{1/p^{i+1}}⟩` over `K⟨T^{1/p^i}⟩` (André A, Remarque 2.2.7). For perfectoid `A`, the unit ball of
`A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` is almost the root extension of PerfectoidQuotients §1.3 built on `A°`; that roadmap's
construction is integral and specific to perfectoid `A`, this one is the uniform Banach construction
for every spectral `A`. *Needs:* §2.7; AdicSpacesPartII R0.4; PerfectoidSpaces P1.30.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The root algebra carries the root system `g^{1/p^i}` with `(g^{1/p^{i+1}})^p = g^{1/p^i}` and
  `g^{1/p^0} = g`.
- A root system of `φ(g)` in `B°`, for `φ : A → B`, determines a unique map `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ → B`
  extending `φ`.
- `A → A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` is isometric for spectral norms.
- `A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ = (A⟨T^{1/p^i}⟩/(T − g))^u`.

**Checks.**

- For `A = K`, `g = 1` at level `1`: if `char K = 0`, `K⟨1^{1/p}⟩ = K[S]/(S^p − 1)`, a reduced
  `K`-algebra of dimension `p`, so the root of `1` is adjoined freely; if `char K = p` it is `K`,
  because `S − 1` is nilpotent and dies in the uniformization. A definition omitting the
  uniformization fails the characteristic-`p` case.
- For `A = K⟨T⟩` and `g = T`, the root algebra is `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`; when `K` is perfectoid this is
  PerfectoidSpaces P1.30's perfected Tate algebra, and for `K = ℚ_p` it is not perfectoid.
- `A → A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` is isometric but in general not surjective: for `A = K⟨T⟩`, `T^{1/p} ∉ A`.

### 2.9 the root algebra as a uniform colimit of tubes

Let `B` be a uniform Banach `K`-algebra, `f ∈ B°` and `0 ≠ ϖ ∈ K°°`. Prove that
`ucolim_i B{f/ϖ^i}^u ≅ (B/fB)^u` (André A, Proposition 2.9.2, formula (2.31), pp. 33–34). Consequently,
for a uniform Banach `K`-algebra `A` and `g ∈ A°`, there are canonical isomorphisms
`ucolim_i A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{(T − g)/ϖ^i}^u ≅ A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` and
`(completed colim_i A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{(T − g)/ϖ^i}°)_* ≅ A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩°`, with `ϖ`-adic completion and
`(·)_*` the almost elements in `(K°, K°°)` when the valuation is not discrete (the identity when it is)
(André A, Corollaire 2.9.3, formula (2.32), p. 34). Here `A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ = A ⊗̂_K K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` is
the completion of `A[T^{1/p^∞}]` for the Gauss norm built on the spectral norm of `A`, and the tubes
are its Weierstrass localisations `{|T − g| ≤ |ϖ|^i}`. This completed algebra is perfectoid when `K`
and `A` are perfectoid (PerfectoidSpaces P1.30; André A, Exemples 3.2.3(1), p. 37), and not in
general; the colimit theorem uses only uniformity of `A`.

*Proof sketch.* The image of `f` in `B{f/ϖ^i}^u` has norm at most `|ϖ|^i`, so it vanishes in the
uniform colimit, and `B{f/ϖ^i}/f = B⟨U⟩/(f, ϖ^iU − f) = B/f`. Both `B → ucolim B{f/ϖ^i}^u` and
`B → (B/fB)^u` are epimorphisms in `K-uBan`, and colimits preserve epimorphisms, so the two triangles
commute and give inverse isomorphisms. Apply this to `B = A⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩` and `f = T − g` using
`A⟨g^{1/p^j}⟩ = (A⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩/(T − g))^u` and interchange the colimits over `i` and `j`; the unit-ball
statement is §2.7.

*Needs:* §2.5, §2.7, §2.8.

**Checks.**

- For `f = T` in `K⟨T⟩` this is the shrinking-disc colimit, equal to `K` (§2.7).
- For `f = 0` every tube is `B` and the statement says `ucolim B = B`.
- André B uses the second isomorphism as its Lemme 2.5.1 for the Kummer tower (§5.6).
- The base algebra need not be perfectoid, and then neither is `A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`: for `K` perfectoid of
  characteristic `p` and `A = K⟨X⟩`, every `p`-th power in `K⟨X, T^{1/p^∞}⟩` has all its
  `X`-exponents divisible by `p`, so `X` is not a `p`-th power (for `p = 2` and `p = 3` alike), the
  algebra is not perfect and hence not perfectoid (André A, Remarques 3.2.2(2), p. 36). For `A = K`
  every exponent has its roots and the algebra is P1.30's perfected Tate algebra.

### Examples

- For `A = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `g = T`: the root saturation is `K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, the annuli `A{ϖ^j/T}`
  have uniform limit `A`. The free root algebra has an evaluation retraction to `A` using the
  chosen roots, but retains other branches. Over `ℂ₂` the two images of a new square root are
  `T^{1/2}` and `−T^{1/2}`. Specialising the old roots to `1` gives at level one
  `ℂ₂[S]/(S² − 1) ≅ ℂ₂ × ℂ₂`, with four idempotents, whereas `ℂ₂` has two
  (André A, Exemple 2.9.3(2), formula (2.29), p. 33).
- For `A = K × K`, `g = (ϖ², 1)`, the Laurent localisations separate the quotient norm from the
  one-parameter infimum and show that restriction maps need not be injective.

## Layer 3: Almost perfectoid algebras and the perfectoid Riemann extension theorem

The perfectoid coreflection `♮ = ♯ ∘ ♭` makes perfectoid algebras a coreflective subcategory of
uniform Banach algebras. Almost perfectoid algebras are the objects that become perfectoid in the
almost category generated by the roots of `T`; they are the natural output of limits. The central
theorem is André's perfectoid Riemann extension: the limit of the unit balls of the annular
localisations `A{ϖʲ/g}` is the root saturation `g^{-1/p^∞}A°`, which is the complete integral closure
of `A°` in `A[1/g]`. With Scholze's limit lemma and a `lim¹` estimate it shows that nested systems of
perfectoid algebras on the annuli are equivalent to a coreflective part of almost perfectoid
algebras. `K` is a perfectoid field of residue characteristic `p` and `ϖ ∈ K°°` has `pK° ⊆ ϖK°` and a
root system. Perfectoid Tate rings are Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Perfectoid.IsPerfectoidTateRing`, under the
complete Hausdorff Tate-ring conventions of the supplier contracts.

### 3.1 the perfectoid coreflection `A ↦ A^♮`

For a uniform Banach `K`-algebra `A`, `A♭ = lim_{x↦x^p} A` is a perfectoid `K♭`-algebra
(PerfectoidSpaces P1.7); define `A^♮ := (A♭)^♯`, the perfectoid `K`-algebra corresponding to it under
the tilting equivalence (PerfectoidSpaces P1.27) (André A, §3.3.6, formula (3.10) and
Proposition 3.3.3, p. 40). The intended signature is a functor from `K-uBan` to perfectoid
`K`-algebras with a counit `A^♮ → A`. Prove: the counit is isometric for spectral norms and an
isomorphism iff `A` is perfectoid; `♮` is right adjoint to the inclusion of perfectoid `K`-algebras into
`K-uBan`, so perfectoid `K`-algebras form a coreflective full subcategory, and `♭` is right adjoint to
`ι ∘ ♯`; in characteristic `p`, `A^♮ = ulim_F A`, and limits of perfectoid algebras exist and are
computed as `(ulim)^♮` (André A, Proposition 3.2.1, p. 36, and §3.8.1, pp. 50–51). The counit need not
be surjective, and `A^♮°/ϖ → A°/ϖ` is injective (André A, Proposition 3.3.1, p. 38). In characteristic
`p`, `ulim_F A` is §2.6's uniform limit of the tower `⋯ → A_2 → A_1 → A_0` with Frobenius
transitions `a ↦ a^p`, where `A_n` is the ring `A` with `K` acting by `λ·a = λ^{1/p^n}a` (`K` is
perfect) and with the spectral norm of this `K`-structure, `|a|_n = |a|_sp^{p^n}`; Frobenius
`A_{n+1} → A_n` is then `K`-linear and isometric. A compatible sequence `x = (x_n)`,
`x_{n+1}^p = x_n`, has `|x_n|_n = |x_0|_sp` for every `n`, so `ulim_F A` is the whole inverse
perfection `lim_F A` with the norm `x ↦ |x_0|_sp`, and the counit is `x ↦ x_0`. *Needs:* §2.6;
PerfectoidSpaces P1.1, P1.4, P1.7, P1.27.

*Construction.* `A^♮°/ϖ ≅ A♭°/ϖ♭ → A°/ϖ` is injective, which gives the isometry of the counit; `♮`
restricted to perfectoid algebras is the identity, which gives the adjunction. `♭` restricted to
perfectoid algebras has `♯` as quasi-inverse, so `♭ = ♭| ∘ ♮` is right adjoint to `ι ∘ ♯`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The counit `A^♮ → A` is a continuous `K`-algebra map, isometric for spectral norms.
- The counit is an isomorphism iff `A` is perfectoid.
- `Hom_{Perf}(P, A^♮) ≅ Hom_{K-uBan}(P, A)` naturally in perfectoid `P`.
- `(A^♮)♭ = A♭`, and in characteristic `p`, `A^♮ = ulim_F A`, the inverse perfection of `A` in
  which a compatible sequence has the spectral norm of its zeroth term.

**Checks.**

- For a perfectoid `K`-algebra `A`, `A^♮ ≅ A` through the counit.
- `K⟨T⟩^♮ = K`: elements of `K⟨T⟩♭` are compatible `p`-power root systems, and only constants have
  them (`(1 + T)^{1/p}` has coefficients `binom(1/p, n)` of unbounded absolute value in characteristic
  `0`, and `T^{1/p} ∉ K⟨T⟩` in characteristic `p`).
- The counit `K → K⟨T⟩` is not surjective: a definition of `A^♮` as a quotient of `A` fails.
- In characteristic `p` the stage norms must be rescaled. Let `K` be the completed perfect closure
  of `𝔽_p((t))` with `|t| = q < 1`, `A = K` and `x = (t^{1/p^n})_n`. The rescaled norms
  `|t^{1/p^n}|^{p^n}` all equal `q`, the norm of the counit image `t` (so `1/4` for `p = 2` with
  `q = 1/4`, and `1/8` for `p = 3` with `q = 1/8`), and the counit is isometric. The supremum of the
  unrescaled spectral norms, `sup_n q^{1/p^n} = 1`, gives every positive power of `x` the value
  `1` and is not `K`-homogeneous; with it the counit would not be isometric.

### 3.2 perfectoid root algebras

Let `A` be a perfectoid `K`-algebra and `g ∈ A°`. The perfected Tate algebra
`A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ = A ⊗̂_K K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, its unit ball `A°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and its universal property
among uniform `A`-algebras with a root system are PerfectoidSpaces P1.30 with `R = A` and one
variable. Let `(B_g, B_g⁺)` be the monic-root pair of PerfectoidQuotients §1.4 for the pair `(A, A°)`
and the monic polynomial `T − g ∈ A°[T]`; that roadmap proves it perfectoid. Prove (André A,
Exemples 3.2.3(1), p. 37; §3.6.1, pp. 48–49):

1. the map `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ → B_g` given by the universal property of §2.8 is an isomorphism of uniform
   Banach `A`-algebras carrying the root system of §2.8 to that of `B_g`; hence the root algebra
   `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` of a perfectoid `A` is perfectoid;
2. the canonical map `A⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ → A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `T^{1/p^i} ↦ g^{1/p^i}`, is an extremal
   epimorphism of uniform Banach algebras;
3. the root system of `g` defines `g♭ ∈ A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩♭` with `(g♭)^♯ = g`.

Perfectoidness is PerfectoidQuotients' theorem. What is proved here is the comparison (1) of its
integral-closure construction with the uniform completed tensor product of §2.8, which exists over
every spectral base, together with (2) and (3).

*Proof sketch.* (1) `B_g` is perfectoid, hence a uniform Banach `A`-algebra, and the images `h_n` of
the `T^{1/p^n}` form a root system of `g` in `B_g⁺ ⊆ B_g°`, so §2.8 gives `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ → B_g`. In a
uniform Banach `A`-algebra `C` every root system of `g` lies in `C°`, its terms having spectral norm
`|g|_sp^{1/p^n} ≤ 1`; so PerfectoidQuotients' universal property, applied to `(C, C°)` with
`C = A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩`, gives a map back. Both composites fix the root systems, so the two uniqueness
clauses make them identities. (2) The map is an extremal epimorphism by the quotient description
`A⟨g^{1/p^i}⟩ = (A⟨T^{1/p^i}⟩/(T − g))^u` of §2.8, passed to the uniform colimit. (3) `g♭` is the
inverse system of the `g^{1/p^k}` modulo `p`, and `(g♭)^♯ = lim_k (g^{1/p^k})^{p^k} = g`.

*Needs:* §2.8; PerfectoidSpaces P1.4, P1.5, P1.30; PerfectoidQuotients §1.4.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 0` and `A = K` the root algebra is `K`, with `g♭ = 0`: in `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩/(T)` every
  `T^{1/p^i}` is nilpotent (`(T^{1/p^i})^{p^i} = 0`) and dies in the uniformization. Without the
  uniformization the quotient is not reduced, hence not perfectoid. PerfectoidQuotients'
  construction also returns `K`: the integral closure of `K°` in `K[T^{1/p^∞}]/(T)` is `K° ⊕ N`,
  with `N` the nilradical, a `K`-vector space, so `N/ϖⁿN = 0` and the `ϖ`-adic completion is `K°`.
- The comparison retains every branch. For `K` perfectoid of characteristic `0` with `p = 2`, `A = K`
  and `g = 1`, both constructions have first stage `K[S]/(S² − 1) ≅ K × K`, with four idempotents and
  the two evaluations `S ↦ 1` and `S ↦ −1`; the isomorphism (1) matches both, and the evaluation
  retraction `A⟨1^{1/2^∞}⟩ → A` choosing the branch `1` is not an isomorphism.
- For `A = Â_∞0` and `g` as in Layer 5, `A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ = Â_∞∞` is perfectoid (§5.6).
- For `A = K` and `g = 1` in characteristic `0`, `K⟨1^{1/p^∞}⟩` is the completed colimit of the
  `K[S]/(S^{p^i} − 1)`, a perfectoid algebra with many idempotents (at level `i`, one for each subset
  of the irreducible factors of `S^{p^i} − 1` over `K`, so `2^{p^i}` when `K` contains the `p^i`-th
  roots of unity, as `K = K̂_∞` of Layer 5 does; in the completed
  colimit, one for each clopen subset of the profinite set of Galois orbits of compatible systems
  of `p`-power roots of unity, since two idempotents at distance `< 1` in a uniform Banach algebra
  coincide and every idempotent of the completed colimit comes from a finite level); the
  universal property does not choose the root `1`.

### 3.3 almost perfectoid `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebras

Work in the almost setup `(V, m) = (K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°V)`. For a uniform Banach
`K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra `A` let `A^{ˆa}-uBan` be the category whose objects are uniform Banach
`A`-algebras and whose morphisms `B → C` are morphisms `B°^a → C°^a` of almost algebras. Define a
uniform Banach `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra `B` to be **almost perfectoid** if `B^{ˆa}` is isomorphic in this
category to a perfectoid `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra (André A, §3.5.4, Définition 3.5.2 and Lemme 3.5.3,
p. 46). Prove that the following are equivalent (André A, Proposition 3.5.4, p. 47): `B` is almost
perfectoid; Frobenius is almost surjective on `B°/ϖ`; `B^♮° → B°` is an almost isomorphism; `B^♮ → B`
is an almost isomorphism; some perfectoid `B′` maps to `B` by an almost isomorphism; `(B^{ˆa})_!!` is
perfectoid. Prove that `B ↦ B°^a` identifies almost perfectoid algebras up to almost isomorphism with
`K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩^a`-algebras that are `K°`-flat, complete and on which `x ↦ x^p` induces a bijection
modulo `ϖ^{1/p} → ϖ`, and that over an almost perfectoid `A`, `(·)°^a` is an equivalence from almost
perfectoid, almost finite étale `A`-algebras to finite étale `A°^a`-algebras (André A, Lemme 3.5.5 and
Proposition 3.5.6, p. 48). Perfectoid implies almost perfectoid, not conversely, even when `g` is a
nonzerodivisor. The intended signature is a predicate on uniform Banach `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebras.
*Needs:* §2.2, §3.1; PerfectoidSpaces P0.9, P0.14, P0.16, P3.11; PerfectoidSpaces P0.26,
gap:complete-almost-nakayama; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`.

*Construction.* The coreflection `♮` descends to the almost category (André A, Lemme 3.5.3), so the
perfectoid objects are equivalent to the almost perfectoid ones. Put `L = B°/ϖ`, so that
`B♭° = lim_F L`. Frobenius almost surjectivity makes `F` surjective on `mL`, so `lim_F L → L` is
almost surjective by Mittag-Leffler for `𝔽_p`-vector spaces; hence the reduction
`B^♮°/ϖ ≅ B♭°/ϖ♭ → B°/ϖ` is almost surjective, the almost Nakayama lemma for `ϖ`-adically
complete modules (PerfectoidSpaces P0.26, gap:complete-almost-nakayama) makes `B^♮° → B°` almost
surjective, and it is injective by §3.1: the third condition (André A, Proposition 3.5.4, proof of
(2) ⇒ (3), p. 47). The fifth gives the first by André A, Lemme 2.3.5; the third gives the sixth by
adjunction. The integral description is the almost analogue of PerfectoidSpaces P1.25, and
the finite étale equivalence the almost analogue of PerfectoidSpaces P3.11.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- `B` is almost perfectoid iff `B^{ˆa} ≅ P^{ˆa}` for a perfectoid `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra `P`.
- The six conditions of André A, Proposition 3.5.4 are equivalent.
- Perfectoid algebras are almost perfectoid.
- `B` is almost perfectoid iff Frobenius on `B°/ϖ` is almost surjective in `(V, m)`.
- `B ↦ B°^a` is an equivalence onto flat complete almost algebras with bijective Frobenius
  `ϖ^{1/p} → ϖ`.
- Over an almost perfectoid `A`, `(·)°^a` is an equivalence from almost perfectoid almost finite étale
  `A`-algebras to finite étale `A°^a`-algebras.

**Checks.**

- `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` itself is almost perfectoid.
- The subalgebra `B = K + (T₁^{1/p^∞}P)^− + (T₂P)^−` of `P = K⟨T₁^{1/p^∞}, T₂^{1/p^∞}⟩`, over
  `K⟨T₁^{1/p^∞}⟩`, is almost perfectoid, since `T₁^{1/p^k}P ⊆ B ⊆ P` makes `B°^a = P°^a` (André A,
  Remarques 3.5.5(2), pp. 47–48).
- The same `B` is not perfectoid: `T₂ ∈ B` but `T₂^{1/p} ∉ B` (the monomial `T₂^{1/p}` is neither
  constant nor divisible by a root of `T₁` nor by `T₂`), and modulo the roots of `T₁` the image of `B` is
  not perfect. A definition equating almost perfectoid with perfectoid fails here.
- `B` is almost perfectoid iff the counit `B^♮ → B` of §3.1 is an almost isomorphism in `(V, m)`.

### 3.4 annular localisations of perfectoid algebras

Let `ϖ = (ϖ♭)^♯` with roots `ϖ^{1/p^i} = ((ϖ♭)^{1/p^i})^♯`. Prove (André A, §3.6.3, formulas
(3.12)–(3.15), p. 49; Proposition 3.6.1, formula (3.16), p. 50): for the standard algebra,
`K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{ϖʲ/T}° = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}, (ϖʲ/T)^{1/p^∞}⟩`, the completion of
`K°[T^{1/p^∞}, U^{1/p^∞}]/(T^{1/p^k}U^{1/p^k} − ϖ^{j/p^k})_k`, while its unit ball for the quotient
norm is `K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}, ϖʲ/T⟩`; for a perfectoid `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra `A` with spectral norm and
`g = T·1`, every `A{ϖʲ/g}` is perfectoid, `A{ϖʲ/g} ≅ A ⊗̂_{K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩} K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{ϖʲ/T}`, the canonical
map `(A° ⊗̂_{K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩} K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}, (ϖʲ/T)^{1/p^∞}⟩)_* → A{ϖʲ/g}°` is an isomorphism, and if
`char K = 0`, `A{ϖʲ/g}♭ = A♭{(ϖ♭)ʲ/g♭}`. This is the case of PerfectoidSpaces P2.3 in which the
generators already have compatible roots, written over `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`.

*Proof sketch.* Compute the standard case at finite level and pass to the completed colimit, then base
change along `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ → A` using §2.4(2) and PerfectoidSpaces P2.13 and P2.16 (completed tensor
products of perfectoid algebras are perfectoid, with almost integral model the completed tensor product
of unit balls).

*Needs:* §2.4; PerfectoidSpaces P1.30, P2.3, P2.13, P2.16.

**Checks.**

- For `A = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` this is the closed perfectoid annulus `|ϖʲ| ≤ |T| ≤ 1`.
- The almost presentation agrees with that of §1.1 for `t = ϖ`, `n = j`.
- For `j ≥ 1`, the power-bounded subring strictly contains the quotient-norm unit ball:
  `(ϖʲ/T)^{1/p}` has spectral norm `1` and quotient norm
  `|ϖ|^{−j(1−1/p)} > 1`. At `j = 0`, writing `U = 1/T` gives
  `T^{−1/p} = T^{1−1/p}U` in the quotient-norm unit ball; both norms are `1`. The annular
  theorem includes this boundary (André A, formulas (3.12)–(3.16), pp. 49–50).

### 3.5 Scholze's limit lemma for root localisations

Let `r ∈ ℕ[1/p]` and `R` a `(K°/ϖ^r)[T^{1/p^∞}]`-algebra. Put
`R[j] = R ⊗_{K°[T^{1/p^∞}]} K°[T^{1/p^∞}, (ϖʲ/T)^{1/p^∞}]` for `j ∈ ℕ`, with transition maps sending
`u^s_{[j+1]} = 1 ⊗ (ϖ^{j+1}/T)^s` to `ϖ^s u^s_{[j]}`; note `T^s u^s_{[j]} = ϖ^{js}`. Prove that the
canonical map `R → lim_j R[j]` is an almost isomorphism in the setup
`(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}K°[T^{1/p^∞}])` (André A, §4.2.1, formula (4.3), and Proposition 4.2.1
with proof, pp. 54–55; Remarque 4.2.2, p. 55). The statement is algebraic (no completion); `lim` and
`lim¹` over an ordered set may be computed on a cofinal subset. André presents it as a simplified form of a proposition of Scholze on the
Hebbarkeitssatz (André A, §4.2.1, p. 54).

*Proof sketch.* Let `R[j]_k = Σ_{s ∈ ℕ[1/p] ∩ [0, 1/pᵏ]} R·u^s_{[j]}`. Since `u^s_{[j′]}` maps to `0` in
`R[j]` when `s(j′ − j) ≥ r`, the transition `R[j′] → R[j]` factors through `R[j]_k` for
`j′ ≥ j + rpᵏ`, so `lim_j R[j] = lim_{j,k} R[j]_k`. `T^{1/p^k}` maps `R[j]_k` into the image `S_j` of
`R`, so `(S_j)^a = lim_k (R[j]_k)^a`. The kernel of `R → S_j` is killed by `T^{r/j}` for `j = p^ℓ`, by
comparing coefficients; hence `R^a = lim_j (S_j)^a = lim_j R[j]^a`.

*Needs:* PerfectoidSpaces P0.27; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup.ofCompatibleRoots`.

**Checks.**

- For `r = 0` every ring is zero and the statement is empty.
- For `R = K°/ϖ^r` with `T` acting by `1`, every `R[j]` is `R` and the statement is trivial.
- For `R = K°/ϖ^r` (`r ≥ 1`) with every root `T^s` of `T` acting by `0`: at `j = 0` the relation
  `T·u_{[0]} = ϖ⁰ = 1` forces `0 = 1`, so `R[0] = 0`. For `j ≥ 1` the relations `T^su^s = ϖ^{js}` force
  every `ϖ^{js}` with `s > 0`, hence `K°°`, to vanish, so `R[j] = k_K[U^{1/p^∞}]` with `k_K` the
  residue field, and the transitions `R[j+1] → R[j]` kill every `u^s` with `s > 0`. The stages
  `j ≥ 1` are cofinal, so `lim_j R[j] = k_K`, the constant `1` surviving at every positive stage,
  and `R → k_K` has kernel `K°°/ϖ^r`. This is an almost isomorphism in the setup of `(ϖT)^{1/p^∞}`, which acts as `0` on `R`, but
  not in the setup of `ϖ^{1/p^∞}` alone: `ϖ^{1/p}` does not kill the class of `ϖ^{1/p²}` in `K°°/ϖ`.
  The roots of `T` in the almost setup are therefore needed.

### 3.6 André's perfectoid Riemann extension theorem

Let `A` be a perfectoid `K`-algebra and `g ∈ A°` an element admitting a root system in `A` (its
roots lie in `A°`, since `A°` is integrally closed). Clauses (1) and (3) need no further hypothesis
on `g`; (2) assumes that `g` is a nonzerodivisor of `A`, so that `A° ⊆ A[1/g]`, and in (4) it is one
because multiplication by `g` is isometric. Prove (André A, Théorème 4.2.2 with proof, pp. 55–56;
Lemme 4.2.3 and Remarque 4.2.4, p. 56):

1. `lim_j A{ϖʲ/g}° = g^{-1/p^∞}A°`;
2. this ring is the complete integral closure of `A°` in `A[1/g]`: an element `b ∈ A[1/g]` lies in
   `g^{-1/p^∞}A°` iff its powers lie in one finitely generated `A°`-submodule of `A[1/g]`, iff
   `(ϖg)^m bⁿ ∈ A°` for some `m` and all `n` (André A, (2.19), p. 26);
3. for every perfectoid `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra `B`, with `g` the image of `T` (a zero divisor
   allowed), `g^{-1/p^∞}B°` is completely integrally closed in `B[1/g]`: an element of `B[1/g]` whose
   powers lie in one finitely generated `g^{-1/p^∞}B°`-submodule of `B[1/g]` lies in `g^{-1/p^∞}B°`;
   so the six conditions of §2.3 hold (André A, Lemme 4.2.3, p. 56);
4. if multiplication by `g` is isometric, `ulim_j A{ϖʲ/g} = g^{-1/p^∞}A`, the complete integral
   closure of `A` in `A[1/g]`;
5. (1) and (2) hold for almost perfectoid `A` (§3.3) as well, under the same hypotheses on `g`,
   since `A^♮` still contains the roots of `g` and has the same root saturation.

The roots of `g` are part of the hypothesis: without them André only shows, under an Ax–Sen–Tate type
invariance, that the limit is contained in the complete integral closure (André A, Remarque 4.2.5,
pp. 56–57). The formula is the perfectoid analogue of Bartenwerfer's rigid-analytic Riemann extension
theorem (André A, §4, formula (4.1), p. 53).

### 3.8 uniform limits of perfectoid algebras

Let `K` have characteristic `0` and `ϖ_{1/p}` an element of norm `|ϖ|^{1/p}`. For a projective system
`(A_α)` of perfectoid `K`-algebras with uniform limit `A` (§2.6) and perfectoid limit `A^♮`, consider:
(1) `A` is perfectoid; (2) `A^♮ → A` is an isomorphism; (3) `A^♮°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ)` is almost surjective;
(4) the Frobenius-induced map `lim(A_α°/ϖ) ⊗_{K°/ϖ, F} K°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ)` is almost surjective; (5)
`lim(A_α°/ϖ) → lim(A_α°/ϖ_{1/p})` is almost surjective. Prove `(1) ⇔ (2) ⇐ (3) ⇔ (4) ⇔ (5)` (André A,
Proposition 4.3.1 with proof, pp. 58–59). The same argument in the setup
`(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], T^{1/p^∞}K°°K°[T^{1/p^∞}])` shows: if the `A_α` are perfectoid
`K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebras and `lim(A_α°/ϖ) → lim(A_α°/ϖ_{1/p})` is almost surjective, then `A` is almost
perfectoid and `A°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ)` is an almost isomorphism (André A, Remarque 4.3.1, p. 59).

*Proof sketch.* `(A^♮)♭° = A♭° = lim_F L` with `L = lim(A_α°/ϖ)` (André A, formula (3.17)). The
Frobenius map of (4) factors through `L/ϖ_{1/p} → L_{1/p}` and the Frobenius isomorphisms of the
`A_α`, which gives (4) ⇔ (5). Almost surjectivity of Frobenius makes `F` surjective on `K°°L`, hence,
by Mittag-Leffler for `𝔽_p`-vector spaces, `lim_F L → L` almost surjective: (4) ⇒ (3). With (3) and
(4), `A°/ϖ → L` is injective (§2.6), hence an almost isomorphism, and Frobenius on `A°/ϖ` is almost
surjective: (1). (1) ⇔ (2) is §3.1.

*Needs:* §2.6, §3.1, §3.3.

**Checks.**

- For a constant system all five conditions hold.
- A uniform limit of perfectoid algebras need not be perfectoid (André A, Exemple prophylactique
  3.8.5): condition (1) can fail while every `A_α` is perfectoid.
- For a system of two perfectoid algebras with an isomorphism `A₂ → A₁` as transition, the uniform
  limit is `A₂ ≅ A₁`, and the map of (5) is the reduction `A₂°/ϖ → A₂°/ϖ_{1/p}`, which is surjective.

### 3.9 vanishing of `lim¹` for nested root-localisation systems

In the setting of §3.5, work in `(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}K°[T^{1/p^∞}])`. Let `(R_i)_{i∈ℕ}` be a
projective system of `(K°/ϖ^r)[T^{1/p^∞}]`-algebras such that for all `i ≥ j` the canonical maps
`R_i[j] → R_j[j] ← R_j` are almost isomorphisms. Prove `lim¹_j (R_j)^a = 0` (André A, Proposition 4.4.1
with proof, pp. 60–61).

*Proof sketch.* Introduce the triple system `R_i[j]_k` of submodules spanned by the `u^s` with
`s ≤ 1/pᵏ`; as in §3.5, `lim¹_j (R_j)^a = lim¹_{i,j,k}(R_i[j]_k)^a`. The transition cokernels are
bounded: `R_i[j+1]_k → R_i[j]_k` by `ϖ^{1/p^k}`, `R_i[j]_{k+1} → R_i[j]_k` by `T^{1/p^k}`, and for
`i′ ≥ i ≥ j + rp^{k+1}/(p−1)` an image comparison gives almost containment. On the cofinal diagonal
`j = k`, `i_k ≥ k + rp^{k+1}/(p−1)`, the transition cokernels in the almost category are killed by
`s^{3/p^k}`, `s = ϖT`. Passing to almost elements doubles the exponent, `s^{6/p^k}` on an ordinary
representative tower `N_k`, and `(lim¹ N_k)^a ≅ lim¹ (N_k^a)` is the module-tower comparison of GR,
Lemma 2.4.2(iii), pp. 22–23. To see that every `s^ε`, `ε = 1/p^l`, kills the ordinary `lim¹`, fix a tail
`k ≥ N` with `b_k = 12/pᵏ ≤ ε` and solve `x_k − ψ_k(x_{k+1}) = s^ε y_k` by `x_k = s^{b_k}z_k`: take
`z_N = 0` and lift `s^{b_k − b_{k+1}}(z_k − s^{ε − b_k}y_k)` through `ψ_k`, which is possible because
`b_k − b_{k+1} ≥ 6/pᵏ`; extend `x` backwards through the finite head. All exponents lie in `ℤ[1/p]_{≥0}`.

*Needs:* §3.5; PerfectoidSpaces P0.6, P0.27; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ModuleTower.limOne`.

**Checks.**

- For a constant nested system `lim¹` is zero on the nose.
- For a nested system with surjective transitions `R_{i+1} → R_i`, Mittag-Leffler gives `lim¹ = 0`
  before almostification.
- If every `R_j` is almost zero, the nested condition holds trivially and `lim¹_j (R_j)^a = 0`
  follows from PerfectoidSpaces P0.27.
- For a non-nested algebra system set `R_i = (K°/ϖ)[X]`, let every root of `T` act as `1`, and
  let each successor transition send `X` to `0`. These are unital algebra maps and `R_i[j] = R_i`.
  For `i > j` their image is the constants, so the cokernel contains `[X]` with
  `ϖ^{1/p}[X] ≠ 0`; it is not almost zero. The images stabilise after one step, hence
  Mittag-Leffler gives `lim¹ = 0`: nestedness is sufficient, not necessary.

## Layer 4: The perfectoid Abhyankar lemma

The Abhyankar lemma is proved by restricting a finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebra `B′` to the annuli
`A{ϖʲ/g}`, where almost purity applies, and taking the uniform limit. Finite étaleness is not an
equational condition, but being Galois is: the layer therefore develops almost Galois extensions,
imports the symmetric-group splitting torsor of AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3, proves the classical
stabiliser and rigidity lemmas, and reduces every statement to the Galois case. The
output is ramified almost purity and the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma. In §4.3–§4.6, `K` is a
perfectoid field, `ϖ ∈ K°°` has `|p| ≤ |ϖ|` and a root system, `(V, m) = (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}V)`,
`A` is a perfectoid (or almost perfectoid) `K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`-algebra, `g = T·1 ∈ A°` and `A[j] = A{ϖʲ/g}`.

### 4.1 almost Galois extensions of almost algebras

Let `(V, m)` be a basic setup (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup`), `A → B` a monomorphism of commutative almost
`V`-algebras and `G` a finite group acting on `B` by `A`-algebra automorphisms. For a set `X` of
`A`-homomorphisms `B → C` into a further extension `C` there is a canonical `C`-algebra map
`B ⊗_A C → ∏_{χ∈X} C`, `b ⊗ c ↦ (χ(b)c)_χ`; for `X = G` and `C = B` this is
`B ⊗_A B → ∏_G B`, `b ⊗ b′ ↦ (γ(b)b′)_γ`. Define `A → B` to be **`G`-Galois** if `B^G = A` (the invariants
being the kernel of `B → ∏_G B`, `b ↦ (γb − b)_γ`) and `B ⊗_A B → ∏_G B` is an isomorphism; equivalently
`(B_*)^G = A_*` and `(B ⊗_A B)_* → ∏_G B_*` is an isomorphism (André A, §§1.9.1–1.9.2, formula (1.1) and
footnote 6, pp. 8–9). Prove (André A, Proposition 1.9.1 with proof, pp. 9–10): a `G`-Galois extension is
finite étale of rank `|G|` with trace the sum of conjugates; `G`-Galois extensions are stable under base
change and descend along faithfully flat maps; for an intermediate extension `C` with `C → B`
`H`-Galois for a subgroup `H`, `C ⊗_A B ≅ ∏_{G/H} B` and `C` is finite étale of rank `[G : H]` over `A`.
Galois for almost algebras implies neither that `B_*` is Galois over `A_*` nor that `B_!!` is Galois
over `A_!!`: almost elements do not commute with tensor products and `(·)_!!` not with finite products.
For `m = V` it is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor` (AdicEtaleGeometry A4.2), whose
Galois map `TauCeti.MulSemiringAction.tensorToPi` is André's map composed with the swap of the two
tensor factors. The
intended signature is a predicate on a finite group acting on an extension of almost algebras.
*Needs:* PerfectoidSpaces P0.8, P0.9, P0.11, P0.12, P0.14, P0.25; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup`,
`TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor`, `TauCeti.MulSemiringAction.tensorToPi`.

*Construction.* Finite projectivity: for `η ∈ m` choose `e_η ∈ B_* ⊗ B_*` with `e_η² = ηe_η`, killing
the kernel of multiplication and mapping to `η`; writing `e_η = Σ b_i ⊗ b′_i` gives `Σ γ(b_i)b′_i = 0`
for `γ ≠ 1` and `Σ b_ib′_i = η`, so `ηb = Σ t(bb_i)b′_i`, a factorisation of `η·id` through a free module
(PerfectoidSpaces P0.11). Rank and trace follow by faithfully flat descent from `B` to `A`. Base
change: `(B ⊗_A A′) ⊗_{A′} (B ⊗_A A′) ≅ (B ⊗_A B) ⊗_A A′ ≅ ∏_G B ⊗_A A′`, and invariants are computed
after faithfully flat base change; the converse is faithfully flat descent (PerfectoidSpaces P0.25).
For intermediate extensions the canonical map becomes, after `⊗_C B`, a composite of isomorphisms;
descend from `B` to `C`.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- `A → B` is `G`-Galois iff `B^G = A` and `B ⊗_A B → ∏_G B` is an isomorphism of almost algebras.
- The canonical map `B ⊗_A B → ∏_G B`, `b ⊗ b′ ↦ (γ(b)b′)_γ`, and its generalisation
  `B ⊗_A C → ∏_{χ ∈ X} C`.
- A `G`-Galois extension is finite étale of rank `|G|` (PerfectoidSpaces P0.14), and
  `Tr_{B/A}(b) = Σ_{γ ∈ G} γ(b)`.
- If `A → B` is `G`-Galois so is `A′ → B ⊗_A A′`; conversely for faithfully flat `A → A′`.
- If `C → B` is `H`-Galois for `H ≤ G` then `C ⊗_A B ≅ ∏_{G/H} B` and `C` is finite étale of rank
  `[G : H]` over `A`.
- For `m = V`, `G`-Galois is the classical notion `TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor`.

**Checks.**

- `A → A` is Galois for the trivial group.
- `A → ∏_G A`, with `G` permuting the factors by translation, is `G`-Galois (the trivial torsor).
- For `m = V = ℤ` and `G = ℤ/2` acting on `ℤ[i]` by conjugation, `ℤ → ℤ[i]` is not Galois: the
  canonical map sends the basis `1⊗1, 1⊗i, i⊗1, i⊗i` to `(1,1), (i,i), (i,−i), (−1,1)`, whose span has
  index `4` in `ℤ[i]²`, with cokernel `(ℤ/2)²`; over `V = ℤ[1/2]` it becomes Galois.
- Convention witness: for `G = ℤ/2` acting on `B = A × A` by swapping, André's map sends
  `(1,0) ⊗ (1,0)` to `((1,0), (0,0))` (the component at the swap is `γ(1,0)·(1,0) = (0,1)(1,0) = 0`) and
  `tensorToPi` sends it to the same element, while `(1,0) ⊗ (0,1)` goes to `((0,0),(0,1))` under André's
  map (`γ(1,0)·(0,1) = (0,1)`) and to `((0,0),(1,0))` under `tensorToPi` (`(1,0)·γ(0,1) = (1,0)`): the two
  maps differ by the swap of tensor factors.

### 4.2 Galois closures of finite étale extensions and Galois rigidity

Let `R → S` be a finite étale extension of rings of constant rank `r ≥ 1` (faithful, so `r ≥ 1`),
and let `T = ε·S^{⊗r}` be its frame algebra, the finite étale `S_r`-torsor over `R` of
AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3 (*splitting-torsor-of-finite-etale-algebra*), with `S_r` permuting the tensor
factors; for `m = V` it is `S_r`-Galois over `R` in the sense of §4.1. Prove (André A, §1.9.3,
Lemmes 1.9.2–1.9.3 with proofs, p. 10): (1) the first frame coordinate
`ι : S → T`, `s ↦ ε·(s ⊗ 1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ 1)`, is a map of `R`-algebras, so `R → T` factors through `S`; the
stabiliser `S_{r−1} ⊆ S_r` of the first factor acts on `T` by `S`-algebra automorphisms; and through
`ι`, `T` is a finite étale `S_{r−1}`-torsor over `S`, so that `S = T^{S_{r−1}}`; (2) if `G` is a
finite group of automorphisms of a ring `S`, `R = S^G`, and `R ⊆ S′ ⊆ S` is a `G`-stable
intermediate ring that is `G`-Galois over `R`, then `S′ = S`. Both are classical; connectedness of
`Spec R` is not required. A general finite étale algebra is first split along its open and closed
constant-rank loci, and the rank-zero part is treated separately.

*Proof sketch.* (1) The permutations fixing the first factor fix `ε`, which is symmetric, and every
`ι(s)`. The diagonal idempotent splits `S ⊗_R S ≅ S × S″` as `S`-algebras through the first factor,
with `S″ = (1 − δ)(S ⊗_R S)` finite étale of constant rank `r − 1` over `S`. Writing
`S^{⊗r} = (S ⊗_R S) ⊗_S ⋯ ⊗_S (S ⊗_R S)` (`r − 1` factors, each an `S`-algebra through its first
factor), the idempotents `1 − δ_{1b}` cut each factor down to `S″` and the remaining `1 − δ_{ab}`,
`2 ≤ a < b`, are the diagonal idempotents of `S″` over `S`; so `T`, as an `S`-algebra through `ι`,
is the frame algebra of `S″` over `S`, with `S_{r−1}` permuting its factors, and A4.3 over the base
`S` makes it an `S_{r−1}`-torsor; `TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor.isInvariant` gives `T^{S_{r−1}} = S`. On geometric
fibres, where `S` is a product of `r` copies of the residue field, this says that the frames with a
prescribed first point form a torsor under the permutations of the other `r − 1` points. (2) The
idempotents splitting `S′ ⊗_R S′ ≅ ∏_G S′` also split `S ⊗_R S′ ≅ ∏_G e_γ(S ⊗_R S′)`; faithful
flatness of `S′` over `R` and `G × 1`-invariants give `S ⊗_R S′ = S′ ⊗_R S′`, hence `S = S′`.

*Needs:* §4.1; AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor`,
`TauCeti.Algebra.IsFiniteEtaleTorsor.isInvariant`; Mathlib `Algebra.Etale`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

**Checks.**

- For `r = 1`, `ε = 1` (an empty product), `T = S = R`, `ι` is the identity, and `S_1` and `S_0` are
  trivial.
- For `r = 2` and `S = R[x]/(x² − a)` with `2a` invertible, the diagonal idempotent is
  `δ = (1 + (x ⊗ x)/a)/2`, the frame algebra is `T = (1 − δ)(S ⊗_R S)`, in which `1 ⊗ x = −(x ⊗ 1)`,
  and `ι` identifies `T` with `S`: the `S_1`-torsor is trivial, while the transposition of `S_2`
  acts on `T = S` by `x ↦ −x`.
- Rank zero is excluded: over a nonzero ring `R`, `R → 0` cannot factor a faithfully flat Galois
  extension `R → T` through `0`.
- For `R = ℚ` and `S = ℚ(2^{1/3})` (`r = 3`), `T` is the Galois closure `ℚ(2^{1/3}, ζ₃)`, of degree
  `6 = 3!` over `ℚ` and `2` over `S`, and `S_2` is the Galois group of `T` over `S`. Only the
  stabiliser acts over `S`: the transposition `(1 2)` of the nonabelian `S_3` sends
  `ι(2^{1/3}) = ε·(2^{1/3} ⊗ 1 ⊗ 1)` to `ε·(1 ⊗ 2^{1/3} ⊗ 1)`, a different cube root of `2` in `T`, so
  a statement making all of `S_r` act by `S`-algebra automorphisms fails here.
- The frame torsor need not be a field when `S` is: for a cyclic cubic field `S` over `ℚ` (`r = 3`),
  the Galois group `ℤ/3` of `S` acts freely on the six frames of its three embeddings, with two
  orbits (the two cyclic orientations), so `T ≅ S × S`, of rank `6` over `ℚ`, and the transposition
  `(2 3)` reverses orientations and exchanges the two factors: `T` is the trivial `S_2`-torsor over
  `S`. Taking `T` to be the normal closure of `S` would give `S` itself, of rank `3`, which is not an
  `S_3`-torsor.

### 4.3 annular envelopes of finite étale algebras over `A[1/g]`

Let `B′` be a finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebra. Prove (André A, Proposition 4.4.4 with proof, pp. 62–63):
(1) the algebras `B_j = B′ ⊗_{A[1/g]} A[j]` form an object of `2-lim A[j]-Perf` (§3.10); let
`B = ulim B_j`; (2) the counits `B[j] = B{ϖʲ/g} → B_j` are isomorphisms; (3) they come from an
isomorphism `B[1/g] ≅ B′`. `A` may be replaced by `g^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ]` without changing the almost
category, so `A` may be assumed completely integrally closed in `A[1/g]`.

*Proof sketch.* (1) Each `B_j` is finite étale over the perfectoid `A[j]`, hence perfectoid
(PerfectoidSpaces P3.10); `B_i ⊗_{A[i]} A[j] ≅ B_j`, and `B_i` being finite projective over `A[i]` the
tensor product is already complete, equal to `B_i{ϖʲ/g}`. (2) The integral closure of `A°` in `B′` maps
to the integral closure of `A[j]°` in `B_j`, which is almost `B_j°` (PerfectoidSpaces P3.12); in the
limit this gives `δ : B′ → B[1/g]`, and `δ_j` is a right inverse of the counit `η_j`; the adjunction of
§3.11 forces `δ_jη_j = id`. (3) For injectivity, use §2.4(6) to embed `A[1/g]` into the algebraic
projective limit `lim_j A[j]`, where `g` is invertible (it does not embed into the bounded uniform limit:
`1/T` has unbounded annular norms). If `I = ker(B′ → lim_j B_j)` then `tr(xy)` maps to zero for `x ∈ I`
and every `y ∈ B′`, so `tr(xy) = 0`, and nondegeneracy of the finite étale trace pairing gives `I = 0`.
For surjectivity: for `B′` Galois with group `G`, `B[1/g]^G = A[1/g]` and Galois rigidity (§4.2(2))
concludes; in general, on each constant-rank component of positive rank `r`, embed `B′` through its
first frame coordinate in its `S_r`-Galois frame torsor (AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3), apply the Galois
case to the torsor and take `S_{r−1}`-invariants (§4.2(1)).

*Needs:* §2.4, §3.10, §3.11, §4.2; AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3; PerfectoidSpaces P0.12, P0.14, P3.10,
P3.12.

**Checks.**

- For `B′ = A[1/g]^n` the envelope is the product of `n` copies of the Riemann extension of `A`.
- For `B′ = 0` all `B_j` vanish and `B = 0`.
- For `A = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `g = T`, the element `1/T ∈ A[1/T]` has norms `|ϖ|^{−j}` in the `A[j]`, so it lies
  in the algebraic limit `lim_j A[j]` but not in the uniform limit; the injectivity argument must use the
  algebraic limit.

### 4.4 integral almost étaleness of extensions finite étale away from `g`

Let `B` be a perfectoid `A`-algebra in which `g` is a nonzerodivisor and such that `B[1/g]` is finite
étale over `A[1/g]`. Prove that for every `m ≥ 1`, `B°^a/p^m` is a finite étale `A°^a/p^m`-algebra in the
setup `(V, m)`, and `B°[1/g]` is almost finite étale over `A°[1/g]` in `(K°, K°°)`; if `B[1/g]` is Galois
over `A[1/g]` with group `G`, then `B°^a/p^m` is `G`-Galois over `A°^a/p^m` in `(V, m)` (André A,
Proposition 5.2.3 with proof, pp. 66–67). In characteristic `0` it is not claimed that `B°^a` is
finite étale over `A°^a`: the missing ingredient is almost finiteness, because the ideal generated by
`ϖ` is not tight (André A, Remarque 5.2.1, p. 67). In characteristic `p`, `p^m = 0` for `m ≥ 1`, so
the statement is that `B°^a` itself is finite étale over `A°^a`; this is GR, Theorem 3.5.28, with
`V = A°`, `ε = ϖT`, and it is PerfectoidSpaces P3.3 applied to the complete Tate ring `A[1/g]` with
ring of definition `A°` and pseudouniformizer `ϖg`, which is perfect because `A` is and `g` has a root
system (`A°` is `ϖg`-adically complete because it is `ϖ`-adically complete). There the power-bounded
subrings of `A[1/g]` and of `B[1/g]` (the latter with its topology as a finite `A[1/g]`-module) are
`(ϖg)^{-1/p^∞}A°` and `(ϖg)^{-1/p^∞}B°`, almost equal to `A°` and `B°`.

*Proof sketch.* Characteristic `0`, Galois case: put `B₂ = B ⊗_A B` and
`B₂^{[j]} = B[j] ⊗_{A[j]} B[j]`; their completions are
perfectoid with `(B̂₂)°^a = B°^a ⊗̂ B°^a` (PerfectoidSpaces P2.16) and `B̂₂[j] = B₂^{[j]}`. By §3.6,
`lim A[j]° = A°_*`, `lim B[j]° = B°_*`, `lim B₂^{[j]°} = g^{-1/p^∞}B̂₂°`. Each `B[j]°^a` is `G`-Galois over
`A[j]°^a` by almost purity (PerfectoidSpaces P3.13), so in the limit `(B°_*)^G = A°_*` and
`(B̂₂°)_* ≅ ∏_G B°_*`; reducing modulo `p^m` gives the Galois property, hence finite étaleness by §4.1.
After inverting `g`, `(B°/p)[1/g]` is almost flat over `(A°/p)[1/g]` in `(K°, K°°)`, `B[1/g]` is
finite étale, hence flat, over `A[1/g]`, and `p` is a nonzerodivisor on the `K°`-torsion-free rings
`A°[1/g]` and `B°[1/g]`; the principal almost-flatness criterion (PerfectoidSpaces P0.11,
gap:principal-almost-flatness, with `C = A°[1/g]^a`, `M = B°[1/g]^a`, `f = p`; GR, Lemma 5.2.1,
pp. 106–107) gives almost flatness of `B°[1/g]` over `A°[1/g]` in `(K°, K°°)`, and separatedness and
isometry place the Galois idempotents in `ϖ^{-1/p^∞}B₂°[1/g]`. General case: reduce to the Galois case
through the `S_r`-Galois frame torsor of `B[1/g]` (AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3) and its `S_{r−1}`-invariants
(§4.2(1)). In characteristic `p` the statement is PerfectoidSpaces P3.3
as above, transported to the setup `(V, m)` by PerfectoidSpaces P0.16; the reduction modulo `p` is the
algebra itself, so no lifting from `p` is needed.

*Needs:* §3.6, §4.1, §4.2, §4.3; AdicEtaleGeometry A4.3; PerfectoidSpaces P0.14, P0.16, P2.16, P3.3,
P3.13; PerfectoidSpaces P0.11, gap:principal-almost-flatness.

**Checks.**

- If `g` is a unit this is the almost purity theorem of PerfectoidSpaces P3.13 modulo `p^m`.
- For `B = A × A` the conclusion holds trivially: `B°^a/p^m = (A°^a/p^m)²` is the trivial
  `ℤ/2`-torsor.
- For `B = A`, `m = 1`, the statement says `A°^a/p` is finite étale of rank `1` over itself.

### 4.5 ramified almost purity

Prove that inverting `ϖ` and then `g` induces equivalences between (i) complete `A°^a`-algebras that are
finite étale modulo every power of `p` and finite étale after inverting `g`, (ii) almost perfectoid
`A`-algebras that are finite étale after inverting `g`, and (iii) finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebras (André A,
§5.1 and Théorème 5.2.1 with proof, pp. 63–65). For `g = 1` this is the almost purity theorem of
PerfectoidSpaces P3.11. In characteristic `0`, whether these categories also agree with finite étale
`A°^a`-algebras is open (André A, Question 5.2.2, p. 64); in characteristic `p`, `p^m = 0` for `m ≥ 1`,
so being finite étale modulo `p^m` already means being finite étale over `A°^a`.

*Proof sketch.* Replace `A` by `g^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ]`; by §3.6 `A = ulim A[j]` and `g` is a nonzerodivisor.
In the square formed by localisation `ς`, forgetting norms, and `B′ ↦ (B′ ⊗ A[j])_j`, almost purity makes
the bottom functor on finite étale systems an equivalence; `σ(B′) = ulim(B′ ⊗ A[j])` is a quasi-inverse
by §4.3 and §3.11, so (ii) → (iii) is an equivalence. (i) → (ii) is fully faithful, and essential
surjectivity reduces, since (ii) → (iii) is essentially surjective, to §4.4.

*Needs:* §3.3, §3.6, §3.11, §4.3, §4.4; PerfectoidSpaces P3.11.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 1`, (i) is the category of finite étale `A°^a`-algebras and the statement is PerfectoidSpaces
  P3.11.
- The zero algebra corresponds to itself in all three categories.
- For `A = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `g = T` and `B′ = A[1/T][Y]/(Y^ℓ − T)` with `ℓ ≠ p` prime, the object of (ii) is
  `K⟨T^{1/ℓp^∞}⟩` (§4.7), which is ramified along `T = 0` and is not finite étale over `A`.

### 4.6 the perfectoid Abhyankar envelope

Let `A` be perfectoid and `B′` finite étale over `A[1/g]`. Define `B̃°` as the integral closure of
`g^{-1/p^∞}A°` in `B′`, `B̃` as the integral closure of `g^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ]` in `B′` with its spectral norm,
and the **Abhyankar envelope** as `B̃^♮` (André A, §5.3 and Proposition 5.3.1(1)–(2) with proof,
pp. 67–68). Prove: with `B = ulim_j B′ ⊗_{A[1/g]} A[j]` (§4.3), `B = B̃`;
`B̃° = (g^{-1/p^∞}A°)^+_{B′} = (ϖg)^{-1/p^∞}B̃°` is `ϖ`-adically complete; `B̃°` is almost isomorphic, in
`(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], T^{1/p^∞}K°°K°[T^{1/p^∞}])`, to the integral closure `(A°)^+_{B′}` of `A°` in `B′`
(Mathlib `integralClosure`); and `B̃^♮` is the largest perfectoid `A`-algebra contained in `B′`. Integral
closure commutes with localisation, so `B̃[1/g] = B′ = B[1/g]`. The intended signature is a functor from
finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebras to perfectoid `A`-algebras with an identification of its localisation at
`g` with the identity. *Needs:* §2.3, §3.1, §3.7, §4.3, §4.4; Mathlib `integralClosure`.

*Construction.* Replace `A°` by `g^{-1/p^∞}A°`. The integral closure of `A°` in `B′` maps into every
`B_j°` (André A, Lemme 2.4.1(1)), hence into `B°`; this map of subrings of `B′` is injective, and
surjective because `B°` is integral over `A°`, by the Galois invariants formula (André A, (5.6)) after
reducing to the Galois case. For the almost comparison, let `x` satisfy a monic equation of degree `n`
with coefficients `b_i ∈ g^{-1/p^∞}A°`; for `ε = g^{1/p^l}`, multiplying by `εⁿ` gives a monic equation
for `εx` with coefficients `ε^{n−i}b_i ∈ A°`, so every root of `g` sends `B̃°` into `(A°)^+_{B′}`, and
the reverse inclusion is immediate. Maximality of `B̃^♮` follows from §3.7 and §3.1.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- The envelope is a perfectoid `A`-algebra with an isomorphism between its localisation at `g` and
  `B′`.
- Its integral model is `(B̃^♮)°`. The natural inclusion `(B̃^♮)° → B̃°` is an almost
  isomorphism in the stated ramified setup; `B̃°` is the integral model of `B̃`
  (André A, Théorème 0.3.1(1), p. 2; Proposition 5.3.1, pp. 67–68).
- `B̃ = ulim_j B′ ⊗ A[j]`.
- Every perfectoid `A`-algebra contained in `B′` with continuous structure map lies in the envelope.
- `B̃°` is almost isomorphic to the integral closure of `A°` in `B′` in the ramified setup.
- A map `B′ → B″` of finite étale `A[1/g]`-algebras induces a map of envelopes, functorially.

**Checks.**

- For `B′ = A[1/g]` the envelope is `(g^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ])^♮`, and for `g = 1` it is `A`.
- The envelope of `B′ × B″` is the product of the envelopes.
- For `A = K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `g = T` and `B′ = A[1/T]`, the envelope is `(T^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ])^♮ = A`, a
  proper subalgebra of `B′`: a definition taking the envelope to be `B′` itself fails.
- If `B′ = C[1/g]` for a finite étale `A`-algebra `C`, the envelope is `(g^{-1/p^∞}C°[1/ϖ])^♮`, equal to
  `C` iff `C°` is root-saturated along `g`; finite étaleness alone does not ensure this. In
  characteristic `p`, for `A = K + (Σ_m T₁^{1/p^m}P)^−` inside `P = K⟨T₁^{1/p^∞}, T₂^{1/p^∞}⟩`, `g = T₁` and
  `C = A`, the envelope is `P`, strictly larger than `A` (André A, §3.5.3, pp. 45–46).

### 4.7 André's perfectoid Abhyankar lemma

Let `A` be a perfectoid algebra over a perfectoid field `K` of residue characteristic `p` containing a
root system of a nonzerodivisor `g ∈ A°`; view `A°` as a `K°[T^{1/p^∞}]`-algebra through
`T^{1/p^i} ↦ g^{1/p^i}` and work in `(K°[T^{1/p^∞}], T^{1/p^∞}K°°[T^{1/p^∞}])`. Let `B′` be a finite étale
`A[1/g]`-algebra. Prove (André A, Théorème 0.3.1, p. 2, and §0.4, pp. 2–3; §5.3 and Proposition 5.3.1(3)
with proof, pp. 67–68):

1. there is a largest perfectoid `A`-algebra `B` contained in `B′` with continuous structure map
   `A → B`; `B[1/g] = B′`; `B°` is contained in the integral closure of `g^{-1/p^∞}A°` in `B′` and
   almost isomorphic to it, and almost isomorphic to the integral closure of `A°` in `B′`;
2. for every `m ∈ ℕ`, `B°/p^m` is almost finite étale over `A°/p^m`, and `B°[1/g]` is almost finite
   étale over `A°[1/g]` in `(K°, K°°)`;
3. if `B′` is a faithful `A[1/g]`-module, the trace `tr_{B′/A[1/g]}` of the finite projective
   `A[1/g]`-module `B′` maps `B°` into `g^{-1/p^∞}A°`, almost surjectively.

The structure map `A → B` is injective when `B′` is faithful over `A[1/g]`, which (3) assumes; for
`B′ = 0` it is not. In characteristic `0`, whether `B°` is almost finite étale over `A°` itself (rather
than modulo every `p^m`) is not established (André A, §0.3; Remarque 5.2.1); in characteristic `p`,
(2) for `m = 1` is that statement, since `p = 0`.

`abhyankar_trace_almost_surjective` states (3) in the integral-closure model `B̃°` of §4.6 (André A,
Proposition 5.3.1(3), p. 68), on the carriers of §3.6. Here `A = R` is a perfectoid Tate ring over a
field `K` satisfying the perfectoid-field criterion, `A° = powerBoundedSubring R`, `g` is given by its
roots in `R°` and is a nonzerodivisor of `R`, and `A[1/g]` is
`Localization.Away ((g 0 : powerBoundedSubring R) : R)`; `rootSaturation p g hg` is the
`R°`-subalgebra `g^{-1/p^∞}A°` of §2.3, and `B̃°` is Mathlib's `integralClosure` of it in `B′`. The trace is Tau Ceti's
`LinearMap.projectiveTrace` of multiplication by an element. The statement says that the trace maps
`B̃°` into the root saturation, and that `c·g^{1/p^l}·a` is the trace of an element of `B̃°` for every
`a` in the root saturation, every `c ∈ K` with `|c| < 1` and every `l`. These elements `c·g^{1/p^l}`
generate the ideal `T^{1/p^∞}K°°[T^{1/p^∞}]` of the setup, and the image of `B̃°` under the trace is a
`g^{-1/p^∞}A°`-submodule, so the second half is almost surjectivity. Faithfulness is the injectivity
of `R[1/g] → B′`. Finite étale is written as `Module.Finite`, `Module.Projective` and
`Algebra.Etale`, and every finite étale algebra is projective. By (1), `B° ⊆ B̃°` with almost zero
quotient, and the ideal of the setup is idempotent (`c·g^{1/p^l} = (c′g^{1/p^{l+1}})·(c″g^{(p−1)/p^{l+1}})`
with `c = c′c″` and `|c′|, |c″| < 1`), so almost surjectivity on `B̃°` and on `B°` are equivalent.

*Proof sketch.* Take `B` to be the envelope of §4.6, which gives (1) and maximality. (2) is §4.4
applied to `B`, with `B[1/g] = B′`, transferred along the almost isomorphism with `B̃°` when the
integral-closure model is used. (3), containment, which needs no faithfulness: embed `B′` as a direct
summand of a free `A[1/g]`-module and extend multiplication by `b ∈ B̃°` by `0`; if `f` is a monic
polynomial over `S = g^{-1/p^∞}A°` with `f(b) = 0`, the extension is killed by `X·f(X)`, so over a
splitting algebra of its characteristic polynomial every root `α` has `α·f(α)` in every prime, hence
nilpotent, and `α` is integral over `S`; the trace, the sum of the roots, lies in `A[1/g]` and is
integral over `S`. Finally `S` is integrally closed in `A[1/g]`: if `x` is integral over `S` of degree
`d`, its powers lie in `Σ_{i<d} g^{-1}A°·xⁱ`, a finitely generated `A°`-module, because `gS ⊆ A°`, so
`x ∈ S` by §3.6(2). (3), almost surjectivity: if `B′` is faithful, `B̃°` is a faithful `A°`-module, and since
`A → B̃` is isometric, `B̃°/p` is faithful over `A°/p`; `B̃°^a/p` is finite étale over `A°^a/p`, so the
trace is almost surjective modulo `p` onto `(A°^a)_* = g^{-1/p^∞}A°`. In characteristic `0`, both
`B̃°` and `A°` are `ϖ`-adically complete (§4.6), hence `p`-adically complete, because `p ∈ ϖK°` and
`ϖ^N ∈ pK°` for large `N` (`0 < |p| ≤ |ϖ| < 1`), so the trace is almost surjective by the almost
Nakayama lemma for complete modules (PerfectoidSpaces P0.26, gap:complete-almost-nakayama, with
`I = pV`). In characteristic `p`, `pK° = 0` while `ϖ^N ≠ 0`, so the two topologies differ; but the
reduction modulo `p` is the trace itself, and its almost surjectivity modulo `p` is the claim.

*Needs:* §2.3, §3.6, §3.7, §4.4, §4.5, §4.6; PerfectoidSpaces P0.12, P0.14, P3.13; PerfectoidSpaces

### 4.8 almost perfectoid envelopes of reduced affinoid algebras

## Layer 5: Purity and André's flatness theorem for Kummer towers

Purity (universal injectivity) is the commutative-algebra content of the direct summand theorem, and
almost purity its almost analogue. The normalized Kummer tower adjoins to `A = W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧` the
`p`-power roots of unity, the roots of the coordinates and the roots of `g`, then takes integral
closures. André's flatness theorem thickens the completed tower into a colimit of tubes around `T = g`,
descends each tube to a Noetherian stage and proves faithful flatness there; the Noetherian step uses
Bhatt's complete flatness criterion. The theorem gives purity of all Kummer extensions and, with the
Abhyankar lemma, almost faithful flatness modulo `p^m` of finite extensions ramified along `pg`. Fix a
perfect field `k` of characteristic `p`, `A = W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧` and `0 ≠ g ∈ A`.

### 5.1 pure (universally injective) module and ring maps

Let `R` be a commutative ring. Define an `R`-linear map `u : M → N` to be **pure** if
`P ⊗_R u : P ⊗_R M → P ⊗_R N` is injective for every `R`-module `P`; it suffices to test finitely
presented `P`. Define a ring map `R → S` to be **pure** if it is pure as a map of `R`-modules,
equivalently `R′ → S ⊗_R R′` is injective for every `R`-algebra `R′` (André B, Annexe A.1, Lemme A.1.1,
p. 89; Annexe A.2, Lemme A.2.1, pp. 89–90). `Suggested.lean` defines the ring-map form as
`IsPureHom f`: with `S` an `R`-algebra through `f`, the map `P → P ⊗_R S` (Mathlib `LinearMap.lTensor` of
the structure map) is injective for every `R`-module `P` in the universe of `R`. Prove, after Lazard:
`M ⊆ N` is pure iff `Hom(P, N) → Hom(P, N/M)` is surjective for every finitely presented `P`; if `N/M`
is finitely presented, `M ⊆ N` is pure iff `M` is a direct summand; pure maps are the filtered colimits
of split injections and are `Tor`-injective; if `N` is flat, `M ⊆ N` is pure iff `N/M` is flat; pure maps
of `S`-modules are pure over `R`. Pure ring maps are stable under composition, products, base change
and filtered colimits; if `R → S → T` is pure so is `R → S`; if `R → S → T` is flat and `S → T` pure then
`R → S` is flat; `R → S` is pure iff base change `Mod_R → Mod_S` is faithful; it is faithfully flat iff
pure and flat; if `S` is finitely presented as an `R`-module, `R → S` is pure iff it has an `R`-linear
retraction iff `S` is a generator, the dual in the generator criterion being `Hom_R(S, R)`. Module purity
is distinct from the spectral universal injectivity of Mathlib's
`AlgebraicGeometry.UniversallyInjective` (injectivity on points after base change), which the finite
free extension `ℤ → ℤ[i]` fails (two primes of `ℤ[i]` lie over `5`) although it is pure. *Needs:* Mathlib `LinearMap.lTensor`,
`Module.Flat`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat.lTensor_injective_iff_injective`.

*Construction.* Lazard's characterisation through finitely presented modules and filtered colimits of
split monomorphisms; `Tor`-injectivity because filtered colimits commute with `Tor`. Ring-level
statements follow from module-level ones and the faithfulness criterion (André B, Lemme A.2.1). For the
generator criterion use the trace ideal generated by the `f(s)`, `f ∈ Hom_R(S, R)`: if it is `R`, choose
finitely many pairs with `Σ_i f_i(s_i) = 1`; then `t ↦ Σ_i f_i(s_it)` is an `R`-linear retraction. A
single evaluation `f(s) = 1` is not required.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- A linear map is pure iff `u.lTensor P` is injective for every `P`; finitely presented `P` suffice.
- If `coker u` is finitely presented, `u` is pure iff it is a split injection.
- If `N` is flat, an inclusion `M ⊆ N` is pure iff `N/M` is flat.
- A pure map is injective (take `P = R`).
- Pure maps are closed under composition, and if `v ∘ u` is pure then `u` is pure.
- Purity is stable under base change and filtered colimits.
- A ring map is pure iff it is pure as a map of `R`-modules, iff `R′ → S ⊗_R R′` is injective for every
  `R`-algebra `R′`.
- A faithfully flat ring map (Mathlib `Module.FaithfullyFlat`) is pure; faithfully flat iff pure and
  flat.
- For `S` finitely presented as an `R`-module, purity is equivalent to the existence of an `R`-linear
  retraction `S → R` and to `S` being a generator.

**Checks.**

- The identity of any module is pure, and so is `0 → N`; `IsPureHom` holds for the identity ring map.
- The inclusion of the first factor `ℤ → ℤ × ℤ` is pure (split), and `ℤ → ℤ[i]` is pure (free with basis
  `1, i`).
- Multiplication by `2` on `ℤ` is injective but not pure: tensoring with `ℤ/2` gives the zero map
  `ℤ/2 → ℤ/2`. A definition of purity as injectivity fails this.
- `ℤ → ℤ[1/2]` is injective and flat but not pure: `ℤ/2 → ℤ/2 ⊗ ℤ[1/2] = 0`.
- For `R = K[x, y]/(xy)` and its normalisation `S = K[x] × K[y]`, `R → S` is injective and finite but not
  pure: `x` lies in `(x + y)S ∩ R` (as `(x + y)·(1, 0) = (x, 0)`) but not in `(x + y)R` (André B, §0.2,
  p. 72).

### 5.2 almost pure maps and almost flatness

Let `(V, m)` be a basic setup, `R` a `V`-algebra and `u : M → N` a map of `R`-modules. Define `u` to be
**almost pure** if `P ⊗_R u` is almost injective (its kernel is killed by `m`) for every `R`-module `P`
(equivalently every finitely presented `P`), equivalently `M^a_! → N^a_!` is pure; a map of `V`-algebras
`R → S` is almost pure if it is almost pure as a map of `R`-modules, equivalently `R^a_!! → S^a_!!` is pure
(André B, Annexe A.4 and Lemme A.4.1, p. 92). Prove: if `M ⊆ N` is almost pure then
`Hom_R(P, N) → Hom_R(P, N/M)` is almost surjective for every finitely presented `P`; in particular, if
`N/M` is finitely presented, `M` is an almost direct summand of `N` and the class of `N` in
`Ext¹_R(N/M, M)` is almost zero; if `R → S → T` is almost pure so is `R → S`; for a factorisation
`M → M′ → N` with `M → N` almost pure, `M → M′` is almost pure; an almost faithfully flat map of almost
algebras is almost pure (André B, Corollaire 2.6.1 proof and Théorème 3.2.1, pp. 82–83).

To state André's flatness theorem, `Suggested.lean` specialises PerfectoidSpaces P0.11's
module-level notions for a commutative ring `V` with an ideal `m` and `V`-modules: annihilation is
Mathlib `Module.IsTorsionBySet V N (m : Set V)` (every element of `m` kills `N`), `IsAlmostFlat m M` (for every injective `V`-linear `f : N → N′` between
modules in the universe of `V`, the kernel of `f.lTensor M` is almost zero) and
`IsAlmostFaithfullyFlat m M` (almost flat, and `M ⊗_V N` almost zero forces `N` almost zero): `M^a ⊗ −`
is exact, respectively exact and faithful, on almost modules (GR, Definition 2.4.4(i)). For an idempotent `m` these agree with the almost flatness and almost faithful
flatness of `M^a` (an almost injective `f` factors as a surjection with almost zero kernel followed by an
injection, and an extension of `m`-killed modules is killed by `m² = m`).

*Needs:* §5.1; PerfectoidSpaces P0.6, P0.9, P0.11, P0.25; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Almost.BasicSetup`.

*Construction.* `(·)_!` is exact and commutes with `⊗` (GR, §2.2 and Proposition 2.4.35; PerfectoidSpaces
P0.6), so almost injectivity of `P ⊗ u` is injectivity of its `(·)_!`. Lemme A.4.1 is Lazard's lifting criterion applied to `N^a_!`: with
`P = N/M`, for each `η ∈ m` there is `f : N/M → N` projecting to `η·id`. For algebras `(·)_!!` commutes
with `⊗` and a map is a monomorphism iff its `(·)_!!` is injective (GR, Remark 3.1.3; PerfectoidSpaces
P0.25). Almost
faithfully flat implies almost pure because `(·)_!!` preserves faithful flatness (PerfectoidSpaces P0.25)
and faithfully flat maps are pure.

Its API consists of the following facts.

- `u` is almost pure iff for every `P` the kernel of `u.lTensor P` is killed by every element of `m`,
  iff `u^a_!` is pure.
- Pure maps are almost pure for every setup.
- If `M ⊆ N` is almost pure and `N/M` finitely presented, then for every `η ∈ m` there is
  `f : N/M → N` with `π ∘ f = η·id`, and the extension class in `Ext¹(N/M, M)` is almost zero.
- If `v ∘ u` is almost pure then `u` is almost pure.
- An almost faithfully flat map of almost algebras (PerfectoidSpaces P0.11) is almost pure.
- For `m = V`, `Module.IsTorsionBySet V N (m : Set V)` means `N` is zero, `IsAlmostFlat` is flatness (Mathlib
  `Module.Flat.iff_lTensor_injective`) and `IsAlmostFaithfullyFlat` is faithful flatness.

**Checks.**

- For the setup `(V, V)`, almost purity is purity.
- For `V = K°` with `K` perfectoid and `m = K°°`, the inclusion `K°° ⊆ K°` is almost pure (an almost
  isomorphism) but not pure: after tensoring with `K°/ϖ` its kernel is `ϖK°/ϖK°° ≅ K°/K°°`, the residue
  field, nonzero but almost zero.
- Multiplication by `p` on `ℤ_p^cycl` (setup `(ℤ_p^cycl, m)`, `m` the maximal ideal) is injective but not
  almost pure: tensoring with `ℤ_p^cycl/p` gives the zero map on a module that is not almost zero.
- Any map with almost zero kernel into or out of an almost zero module is almost pure.
- For `m = 0` every module is almost zero, so `IsAlmostFlat 0 M` and `IsAlmostFaithfullyFlat 0 M` hold for
  every `M`; for `m = V` and `V = ℤ`, `IsAlmostFaithfullyFlat V ℚ` fails since `ℚ ⊗ ℤ/2 = 0` while
  `ℤ/2 ≠ 0`.

### 5.3 Krull detection along faithfully flat algebras with an idempotent ideal

Let `R` be a Noetherian ring, `M` a finitely generated `R`-module, `S` a faithfully flat `R`-algebra and
`K` an idempotent ideal of `S` with `K·(M ⊗_R S) = 0`. Prove that for every `s ∈ R` whose image lies in
`K` there is `r ∈ Ann_R(M)` with `(1 − r)s = 0`; in particular, if `R` is local and the preimage of `K` in
`R` is nonzero, then `M = 0` (André B, Lemme 1.1.2 with proof, p. 76). The preprint arXiv:1609.00345v1
states a different version, with an ideal `J` and `K ⊆ JS`; both are proved the same way.

*Proof sketch.* Let `J = Ann_R M`. By flatness `JS = Ann_S(M ⊗ S)`, so `K ⊆ JS` and `K = Kⁿ ⊆ JⁿS` for all
`n`. By purity of `R → S` (§5.1), `R ∩ JⁿS = Jⁿ`, so the preimage of `K` lies in `⋂_n Jⁿ`. Krull's
intersection theorem (Mathlib `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff`) gives `r ∈ J` with `(1 − r)s = 0`; in
the local case `1 − r` is a unit unless `J = R`, and then `M = 0`.

*Needs:* §5.1; Mathlib `Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff`, `Module.FaithfullyFlat`,
`Module.annihilator`, `IsNoetherianRing`.

**Checks.**

- If `K = 0` the statement is empty.
- Noetherianity of `R` is necessary: for `R = S = K°` (`K` perfectoid), `M = K°/K°°` and `K = K°°`
  (idempotent), `K` kills `M ⊗ S = M` and `R ∩ K ≠ 0`, yet `M ≠ 0`.
- For `R = ℤ_(p)`, `S = ℤ_(p)`, `K = S` and `M = ℤ/p`: `K` does not kill `M`, so the hypothesis fails, and
  indeed `M ≠ 0` although the preimage of `K` is `R`.

### 5.4 the normalized two-index Kummer tower over `W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧`

Let `K₀ = W(k)[1/p]`, `K_j = K₀(ζ_{p^j})` with `(ζ_{p^j})_j` a compatible system of primitive `p^j`-th roots
of unity, `K_∞ = ⋃ K_j` and `K̂_∞` its `p`-adic completion, a perfectoid field. For `(j, l) ∈ ℕ²` put
`K_j°⟦T^{1/p^j}⟧[g^{1/p^l}] = K_j°⟦T₁^{1/p^j}, …, T_n^{1/p^j}⟧[X]/(X^{p^l} − g)` and
`A_jl = K_j°⟦T^{1/p^j}⟧[g^{1/p^l}][1/p]`, a double inductive system of `K₀`-algebras with the natural
inclusions; infinite indices mean directed unions. Let `A°_jl` be the integral closure of `A` in `A_jl`
(André B, §§2.1–2.3, formulas (7)–(8), p. 78). The symbol `[g^{1/p^l}]` adjoins the root freely and keeps
all branches; it does not choose a root in a field, and integral closedness is relative to the specified
ambient algebra. Prove: `A°_jl` contains `K_j°⟦T^{1/p^j}⟧[g^{1/p^l}]` and `A_jl = A°_jl[1/p]`; for finite
`(j, l)` it is Noetherian and `p`-adically complete; `A°_{j0} = K_j°⟦T^{1/p^j}⟧` and `A°_{00} = A`;
`A°_{j′l′} = A°_jl ∩ A_{j′l′}` for `j′ ≤ j`, `l′ ≤ l`; for `j = ∞`, `A°_{∞l}` is the increasing union of
Noetherian rings finite over one another, each integrally closed in its generic algebra, and `A°_{∞l}`
is completely integrally closed in `A_{∞l}`. These algebras may have zero divisors. The maps
`A°_{j0} → A°_{j′0}` are finite free for `0 ≤ j ≤ j′ < ∞`; `A°_{∞0}` is their filtered union,
faithfully flat over every finite `A°_{j0}`, and so is its
`p`-adic completion `Â°_{∞0} ≅ W(k⟦T^{1/p^∞}⟧) ⊗̂_{W(k)} K̂_∞°` (André A, Exemple 3.2.3(2), formula
(3.1), p. 37). `K̂_∞` is perfectoid for every perfect `k` (André A, Exemple 3.1.2(1), p. 35).

`Suggested.lean` encodes the tower by generators and relations, in one ambient ring:

- `KummerBase p k n` is `A = W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧` (Mathlib `MvPowerSeries (Fin n) (WittVector p k)`, with its
  ring structure), and `kummerCoord p k n i` is the coordinate `T_i`.
- `KummerVar n` indexes the variables `ζ_j` (`KummerVar.zeta j`, `j ∈ ℕ`), `T_i^{1/p^j}`
  (`KummerVar.coord i j`, `i < n`, `j ∈ ℕ`) and `g^{1/p^l}` (`KummerVar.root l`, `l ∈ ℕ`).
- `kummerRelations p k n g` is the set of relations `ζ_j^{p^j} = 1`, `ζ_{j+1}^p = ζ_j`, `Φ_p(ζ₁) = 0`
  (`Φ_p = 1 + X + ⋯ + X^{p−1}`), `(T_i^{1/p^j})^{p^j} = T_i`, `(T_i^{1/p^{j+1}})^p = T_i^{1/p^j}`,
  `(g^{1/p^l})^{p^l} = g`, `(g^{1/p^{l+1}})^p = g^{1/p^l}`. Their instances at index `0` say `ζ₀ = 1`,
  `T_i^{1/p^0} = T_i` and `g^{1/p^0} = g`; for positive indices the power relations follow from these
  and the chain relations, and are listed so that integrality is immediate.
- `KummerQuotient p k n g` is the quotient of the polynomial ring over `A` in these variables by these
  relations, and `KummerAlgebra p k n g` is its localisation away from `p`: the algebra `A_∞∞`.
  `kummerVar p k n g v` is the image of the variable `v`.
- `kummerGenerators p k n g j l` (for `j, l ∈ ℕ ∪ {∞}`) is the set of `ζ_{j′}`, `T_i^{1/p^{j′}}` with
  `j′ ≤ j`, `g^{1/p^{l′}}` with `l′ ≤ l`, and `1/p`; `kummerStage p k n g j l` is the `A`-subalgebra of
  `A_∞∞` they generate (the image of `A_jl`), and `kummerIntegralStage p k n g j l` is

### 5.5 the spectral norm of the Kummer tower and isometric transitions

Prove that there is a unique `K_∞`-algebra norm on `A_{∞l}` with unit ball `A°_{∞l}`, that it is
spectral, that with the induced norms the unit ball of `A_jl` is `A°_jl` and all transition maps of the
system `(A°_jl)` are isometric, that the algebras `A_{j0}` are multiplicatively normed (this can fail for
`l ≠ 0`), and that the ideal `A°°_{∞0}` of topologically nilpotent elements of `A°_{∞0}` is an idempotent
prime ideal equal to `K°°_∞A°_{∞0}` (André B, §2.4, p. 79). Density of `|K_∞|` in `ℝ_{>0}` is used for
uniqueness and spectrality.

*Proof sketch.* `A°_{∞l}` is completely integrally closed (§5.4), so by §2.2(3)–(4) its gauge is the
unique spectral norm with this unit ball; isometry of transitions follows from
`A°_{j′l′} = A°_jl ∩ A_{j′l′}` and §2.2(1). For `l = 0` the norm on `A_{j0}` is the Gauss-type norm of
coefficients, multiplicative because the reduction `k⟦T^{1/p^j}⟧` is a domain; `A°°_{∞0} = K°°_∞A°_{∞0}`
is prime because `A°_{∞0}/K°°_∞ = k⟦T^{1/p^∞}⟧` is a domain (André A, 2.2.1–2.2.2).

*Needs:* §2.2, §5.4.

**Checks.**

- For `n = 0` the norm on `A_{∞0} = K_∞` is the absolute value.
- For `n = 1`, `g = T₁`, `j = l = 1`, the norm on `A_{11}` is not multiplicative: the two nonzero factors
  of §5.4's zero-divisor Check have product `0`.
- For `n = 0` the topologically nilpotent ideal is `K°°_∞`, and it is idempotent: for `|x| < 1` choose
  `j` with `|ζ_{p^j} − 1|² ≥ |x|` (possible as `|ζ_{p^j} − 1| → 1`); then `u = x/(ζ_{p^j} − 1)² ∈ K°_∞` and
  `x = (ζ_{p^j} − 1)·((ζ_{p^j} − 1)u)` is a product of two elements of `K°°_∞`. For a discretely valued
  field the maximal ideal is not idempotent, which is why the cyclotomic tower is needed.

### 5.6 the completed Kummer tower as a colimit of tubes; perfectoidness

Let `Â_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` be the completion of `A_∞0[T^{1/p^∞}]` for the multiplicative Gauss norm (complete
the integral Gauss unit ball `p`-adically, then invert `p`), so that
`Â_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩° = Â°_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `T − g` has norm `1`; let `Â_∞∞` be the completed uniform colimit
of the `A_jl` with the spectral norms of §5.5. Prove that in `(K°_∞, K°°_∞)` there is a canonical
isomorphism `Â°_∞∞^a ≅ completed colim_i (Â_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{(T − g)/ϖ^i}°)^a`, with `p`-adic completion,
and that `Â_∞0` is perfectoid, hence so are `Â_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` and `Â_∞∞ = Â_∞0⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` (André B, §2.5,
Lemme 2.5.1 and formulas (9)–(10), p. 79; §3.1, p. 83). The completed tower is the completed directed
union of the `A°_jl` including the coordinate roots `T_i^{1/p^j}`. An algebraic `(p)`-adic completion
after inverting `p` would be the zero ring and is not meant; the root algebra freely adjoins a root
system even when `g = 1`.

*Proof sketch.* `Â_∞0` is perfectoid: Frobenius is surjective modulo `p` on `⋃ A°_{i0}`, since
`A°_{i0} ≡ (A°_{i+1,0})^p` modulo `p` (André A, Exemple 3.2.3(2)); the perfected Tate algebra over it is
perfectoid (PerfectoidSpaces P1.30). `Â_∞∞` is the root algebra `Â_∞0⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩` (the `A_jl`
adjoin a root system of `g`, and the spectral norms agree by isometry), so it is perfectoid by
§3.2(1), that is, by PerfectoidQuotients §1.4 for `T − g` over `(Â_∞0, Â°_∞0)`;
the colimit formula is the unit-ball form of §2.9 for `A = Â_∞0`.

*Needs:* §2.9, §3.2, §5.4, §5.5; PerfectoidSpaces P1.2, P1.30; PerfectoidQuotients §1.4.

**Checks.**

- For `g = 1` the tubes around `T = 1` recover `Â_∞0⟨1^{1/p^∞}⟩`, which keeps all cyclotomic branches.
- For `n = 0` and `g = p`, `Â_∞∞` is the completed full free-root algebra over `K_∞`; it is
  perfectoid and retains the two branches exhibited at `p = 2` in §5.4. The idempotent has
  norm `1`, so completion cannot turn this algebra into a field.
- Coordinate roots cannot be omitted in general: for `g = 1` and `n ≥ 1`, `T₁` has no `p`-th
  root modulo `p`, since exponents in a `p`-th power are multiples of `p`, even after adjoining
  the constant roots. For `n = 1`, `g = T₁`, the `g`-roots themselves supply all coordinate
  roots: `T₁^{1/p^l}` is the `p`-th power of `T₁^{1/p^{l+1}}`.

### 5.7 faithful flatness of the Noetherian tube models

Let `j ≥ k′` be finite (`k′` the root index of the tube), `ϖ_j` a uniformizer of `K_j°`, `0 ≠ ϖ_{ik′} ∈ K_j`
with `|ϖ_{ik′}| < |ϖ_j|`, `f_{ik′} ∈ A°_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩` and `g_{k′} ∈ A°_{j0}` with
`f_{ik′} ≡ T^{1/p^{k′}} − g_{k′}` modulo `ϖ_j`. Prove that
`C = A°_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}, U⟩/(ϖ_{ik′}U − f_{ik′}) = A_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩{f_{ik′}/ϖ_{ik′}}_{≤1}` is a Noetherian,
`ϖ_j`-torsion-free, `ϖ_j`-adically complete `A°_{j0}`-algebra with
`C/ϖ_j ≅ (A°_{j0}/ϖ_j)[T^{1/p^j}, U]/(T^{1/p^{k′}} − g_{k′})` free over `A°_{j0}/ϖ_j`, hence faithfully flat
over `A°_{j0}`. This repairs André B's step (a) of the proof of Théorème 2.5.2, p. 81, which derives the
flatness of the generic fibre `A_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩{f_{ik′}/ϖ_{ik′}}` over `A_{j0}` from an unargued description
of the analytic spectrum of `A_{j0}` as an increasing union of affinoid polydiscs; generic-fibre flatness
follows here by inverting `p`.

*Proof sketch.* Since `g_{k′}` lies in the base and `j ≥ k′`, the reduction of `f_{ik′}` in
`Z = T^{1/p^j}` is the monic polynomial `Z^{p^{j−k′}} − g_{k′}`, so `f_{ik′}` has Gauss norm `1`; the base
Gauss norm is multiplicative, so multiplication by `f_{ik′}` is isometric, and §2.5 gives the
torsion-free unit-ball quotient `C`. The Noetherian restricted power series quotient is `ϖ_j`-adically
complete; its reduction `(A°_{j0}/ϖ_j)[Z, U]/(Z^{p^{j−k′}} − g_{k′})` is free on
`{Z^aU^b : 0 ≤ a < p^{j−k′}, b ≥ 0}`, a basis containing `1`, hence faithfully flat. Bhatt's criterion
(DerivedDeRhamCohomology §1.10) with `π = ϖ_j` gives faithful flatness of `A°_{j0} → C`.

*Needs:* §2.5, §5.4, §5.5; DerivedDeRhamCohomology §1.10; Mathlib `IsNoetherianRing`,
`IsAdicComplete`.

**Checks.**

- For `f_{ik′} = T^{1/p^{k′}} − g_{k′}` exactly, `C` is the unit ball of the tube
  `{|T^{1/p^{k′}} − g_{k′}| ≤ |ϖ_{ik′}|}`.
- The hypothesis `g_{k′} ∈ A°_{j0}` is essential: for `f = 0` (the "approximation" `g_{k′} = T^{1/p^{k′}}`
  taken in the auxiliary algebra) the quotient `A°_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}, U⟩/(ϖ_{ik′}U)` has nonzero `ϖ_j`-torsion
  and is not flat; if `j < k′` the monomial `T^{1/p^{k′}}` is not even in the stage.
- For a fixed `k′`, `g_{k′}` is obtained by Frobenius surjectivity on `Â°_∞0` modulo a small
  pseudouniformizer, approximated in a finite stage, and `j` is enlarged to `j ≥ k′`; no dependence on
  the auxiliary `T` is needed.

### 5.8 André's flatness theorem for the Kummer tower

Prove that, in the almost setup `(K°_∞, K°°_∞)` (equivalently `(A°_∞0, K°°_∞A°_∞0)`), the `p`-adically
completed Kummer tower `Â°_∞∞` is almost faithfully flat over the decompleted coordinate tower `A°_∞0`;
and that for each `i` the unit ball of the tube `Â_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩{(T − g)/ϖ^i}` is almost faithfully flat
over `A°_∞0`, with `(·)_!!` of it a faithfully flat `A°_∞0`-algebra (André B, Théorème 2.5.2 with proof,
pp. 79–82; the preprint arXiv:1609.00345v1 numbers it Théorème 2.5.1). Only the statement modulo powers
of `p` is needed by the applications, and the method extends to finitely many elements
`g^{(1)}, …, g^{(m)}` and to a ramified complete regular local base of mixed characteristic with perfect
residue field (André B, Remarques 2.6.1(4), pp. 82–83).

`kummer_almost_faithfullyFlat` states the first assertion: for `g ≠ 0`,
`IsAlmostFaithfullyFlat (kummerAlmostIdeal p k n g) (AdicCompletion (span {p}) A°_∞∞)`, with `A°_∞∞` a
module over `A°_∞0 = kummerIntegralStage p k n g ⊤ 0` through the inclusion of normalized stages.

*Proof sketch.* Fix `ϖ ∈ K̂°_∞` with `|p| ≤ |ϖ| < 1` and a root system, and `ϖ′ ∈ K°_∞` with
`|ϖ| < |ϖ′| < 1`. By the approximation lemma (PerfectoidSpaces P2.2) choose `f_i ∈ Â°_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` with a
root system, `f_i ≡ T − g` modulo `ϖ′`, and the same tube (André B, formula (11)). By Scholze's almost
integral model (PerfectoidSpaces P2.3) and §2.5, the almost unit ball of the tube is the completed
colimit over `k′` of `Â°_∞0⟨T^{1/p^∞}, U⟩/(ϖ^{i/p^{k′}}U − f_i^{1/p^{k′}})` (formulas (12)–(13)). Each term does
not change when `f_i^{1/p^{k′}}` is replaced by `f_{ik′} ∈ A°_{j0}⟨T^{1/p^j}⟩` congruent modulo
`ϖ^{i/p^{k′}}`, and `ϖ^{i/p^{k′}}` by `ϖ_{ik′} ∈ K_j` of the same norm (§2.5's invariance); it is the
completed colimit over `j` of the Noetherian tube models (formulas (14)–(17)), with `g_{k′}` chosen as in
§5.7. Fix a finite coordinate stage `A°_{a0}`: the filtered colimits of tube models over stages
`j ≥ a, k′` are faithfully flat over `A°_{a0}` (§5.7, `A°_{j0}` being finite free over `A°_{a0}`), and their

then gives flatness over `A°_∞0`, since a finite relation descends to one stage, and faithfulness, since a
finite expression `1 ∈ IC` for a proper ideal `I` also descends. Finally `(·)_!!` respects faithful
flatness (PerfectoidSpaces P0.25), the completed colimit over `i` is faithfully flat, and §5.6 identifies
it almost with `Â°_∞∞`.

*Needs:* §2.5, §5.2, §5.4, §5.6, §5.7; PerfectoidSpaces P0.16, P0.25, P2.2, P2.3;

- Consequence: `Â_∞∞` is faithfully flat over `A[1/p]` and `g` is a nonzerodivisor of `Â_∞∞` (§5.10).
- For `n = 0`, `k = 𝔽_p`, `g = p`, `Â°_∞∞` is the unit ball of the full free-root algebra, with

  almost faithfulness: `ℂ_p°` is faithfully flat over `K°_∞`, so annihilation of a tensor
  product by `K°°_∞` forces the same annihilation of the original module.

### 5.9 purity of normalized Kummer extensions

Prove that for all `j, l ∈ ℕ ∪ {∞}` the inclusion `A°_{j0} ⊆ A°_jl` is pure (André B, Corollaire 2.6.1
with proof, p. 82). In particular, for `j = 0` and `l` finite, `A = W(k)⟦T₁, …, Tₙ⟧` is a direct summand, as
an `A`-module, of the integral closure of `A` in `A[g^{1/p^l}][1/p]`, and likewise after adjoining roots of
unity and of the coordinates. This proves the direct summand theorem for Kummer extensions of
`W(k)⟦T⟧` without the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma (André B, §0.6, p. 74). Infinite indices mean directed
unions, not completions.

`kummer_isPureHom` states it: for `g ≠ 0` and `j, l ∈ ℕ∞`, `IsPureHom` holds for the inclusion
`kummerIntegralStage p k n g j 0 → kummerIntegralStage p k n g j l`.

*Proof sketch.* It suffices that `A°_∞∞` is pure over `A°_∞0`, since `A°_∞0` is faithfully flat over each
`A°_{j0}` and purity of a composite gives purity of the first map (§5.1). `A°_∞0` is coherent, a
filtered union of Noetherian rings finite flat over one another. Let `P` be a finitely presented
`A°_∞0`-module and `N ⊆ ker(P → P ⊗ A°_∞∞)` finitely generated, hence finitely presented. By §5.8 and
§5.2, `Â°_∞∞`, and so `A°_∞∞`, is almost pure over `A°_∞0`; thus `N` is almost zero and its annihilator
`I` contains `K°°_∞`. `I` is finitely presented, so `I = I_j ⊗ A°_∞0` for an ideal `I_j` of some `A°_{j0}`;
§5.3 with `R = A°_{j0}` (local), `M = A°_{j0}/I_j`, `S = A°_∞0` and `K = A°°_∞0` (idempotent, §5.5) gives
`I_j = A°_{j0}`, so `N = 0`. For finite `j, l`, the finite extension `A°_{j0} → A°_jl` of Noetherian rings is
pure with finitely presented cokernel, hence split (§5.1).

*Needs:* §5.1, §5.2, §5.3, §5.4, §5.5, §5.8.

**Checks.**

- For `l = 0` the map is the identity; for `j′ ≤ j` the coordinate inclusions `A°_{j′0} ⊆ A°_{j0}` are
  faithfully flat, hence pure.
- For `n = 0`, `g = p`, `j = 0`, `l = 1`: `ℤ_p → ℤ_p[p^{1/p}]` (for `k = 𝔽_p`) is split by the projection onto
  the coefficient of `1` in the basis `1, p^{1/p}, …, p^{(p−1)/p}`.
- For `n = 0`, `p = 2`, `g = 1`, `(j, l) = (0, 1)`: `ℤ₂ → ℤ₂ × ℤ₂` (diagonal) is split by the first
  projection, as purity predicts although the extension is not a domain.

  its unit ball is almost finite étale of rank `ℓ` over `K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩` in the setup of `T^{1/p^∞}K°°`
  (§4.3–§4.7).
- **The arithmetic Kummer tower in dimension zero.** `n = 0`, `k = 𝔽_p`, `g = p`: the normalized stages are
  the integral closures of `ℤ_p` in `ℚ_p(ζ_{p^j})[X]/(X^{p^l} − p)`. The completed full algebra
  is perfectoid, with nontrivial idempotents already at `p = 2`, `j = 3`, `l = 1` (§5.4).
  Torsion-freeness over the valuation ring `K°_∞` gives flatness; the compatible evaluations
  detect almost faithfulness (§5.8). At finite stages purity gives module splittings, including
  for the product integral models (§5.9), while `S` can be strictly smaller than the completed
  integral ring (§5.11).
- **A non-domain stage.** `n = 1`, `g = T₁`, `j = l = 1`: `X^p − T₁` splits over `K_1°⟦T₁^{1/p}⟧`, the stage
  `A°_{11}` has zero divisors, and the inclusion `A°_{10} ⊆ A°_{11}` is still pure (§5.4, §5.9).
