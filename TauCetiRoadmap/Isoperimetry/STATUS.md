## Where this roadmap stands

**At a glance.** I1 and I3 are done, and the sharp Euclidean isoperimetric inequality now holds for every finite-volume measurable set. The planar Jordan-curve theorem is proved with its circle equality case. Still open are general finite-perimeter rigidity, convergence of Steiner symmetrizations to the ball, the equality cases of sharp BV Sobolev and the `W¹,¹` Pólya–Szegő theory.

### Named results

- **The sharp isoperimetric inequality** — every Lebesgue-measurable set of finite positive volume satisfies `P(E)^n ≥ n^n ω_n |E|^(n−1)`, with no boundedness or regularity assumed and with extended and real-valued forms ([`MeasureTheory.NullMeasurableSet.finrank_pow_mul_volume_ball_mul_volume_pow_le_perimeter_pow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/BoundedVariation/Isoperimetric.html#MeasureTheory.NullMeasurableSet.finrank_pow_mul_volume_ball_mul_volume_pow_le_perimeter_pow)).
- **The isoperimetric theorem for rectifiable Jordan curves** — the inside of a Jordan curve of Hausdorff length `L` has area `A` with `4πA ≤ L²`, and equality holds exactly when the curve is a circle. A raw-curve version covers continuous bounded-variation parameters that need not be absolutely continuous ([`TauCeti.IsJordanCurve.four_pi_mul_volume_real_filledHull_sdiff_eq_iff`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/PlaneSeparation/Isoperimetric.html#TauCeti.IsJordanCurve.four_pi_mul_volume_real_filledHull_sdiff_eq_iff)).
- **Isoperimetry for convex bodies with equality** — a convex body of positive volume satisfies the sharp inequality, with equality exactly for closed balls. The proof combines Brunn–Minkowski with the identification of perimeter as the first variation of parallel volume ([`ConvexBody.perimeter_pow_eq_iff_exists_eq_closedBall`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/BrunnMinkowski/Isoperimetric.html#ConvexBody.perimeter_pow_eq_iff_exists_eq_closedBall)).
- **The Brunn–Minkowski inequality** — `|A|^(1/n) + |B|^(1/n) ≤ |A + B|^(1/n)` for nonempty measurable sets, derived from Prékopa–Leindler and completed by the homothety equality case for convex bodies ([`TauCeti.volume_rpow_add_volume_rpow_le`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/BrunnMinkowski/Basic.html#TauCeti.volume_rpow_add_volume_rpow_le)).
- **The sharp BV Sobolev inequality** — in dimension `n ≥ 2`, `‖u‖_{n/(n−1)} ≤ (n ω_n^(1/n))⁻¹ |Du|`, deduced through BV Pólya–Szegő, which says that Schwarz rearrangement does not increase total variation. Its equality cases are not yet proved ([`TauCeti.eLpNorm_le_inv_mul_totalVariationOn`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/BoundedVariation/Sobolev/Sharp.html#TauCeti.eLpNorm_le_inv_mul_totalVariationOn)).

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Steiner symmetrization** now [does not increase perimeter](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Steiner/Perimeter.html#TauCeti.perimeter_steinerSymmetrization_le), is continuous in its direction, and [fixes, in every direction, exactly the centered balls](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Steiner/Ball.html#TauCeti.forall_steinerSymmetrization_ae_eq_iff_exists_ae_eq_ball). Together with second-moment monotonicity, these are the ingredients for convergence to the ball. The nonincrease is proved by approximation, so it does not give the slicing equality that rigidity needs.
- [**Geometric signed area**](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Green/Examples/CurveArea/RectifiableSignedArea.html#TauCeti.rectifiableSignedArea) of a rectifiable curve, computed along its arclength parametrization, is invariant under increasing reparametrization and agrees with `½∫(xy′ − yx′)` for absolutely continuous curves. For a simple curve it is the orientation sign times the enclosed area. This is what moves area–energy beyond absolutely continuous parameters.
- **The perimeter slicing inequality** — the perimeters of line sections integrate to at most the perimeter ([`TauCeti.lintegral_perimeter_lineSection_le`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/BoundedVariation/Slicing.html#TauCeti.lintegral_perimeter_lineSection_le)). It is the starting point for the localized Steiner comparison in I4.

## The frontier

- **Steiner equality forces interval sections** — prove that `P(S_e E) = P(E)` makes almost every section along `e` an interval. This uses the localized slicing comparison with its equality implication. Combined with the conditional rigidity theorem, it completes I4's equality case.
- **Convergence of Steiner symmetrizations to the ball** — first for bounded finite-perimeter sets and then for all finite-volume sets. Continuity in the direction, the fixed-point theorem and second-moment monotonicity are already in place.
- **Pólya–Szegő in `W¹,¹`** — show that the rearranged gradient is absolutely continuous, with the Lipschitz convex-integrand estimate and uniform integrability. Jensen bounds for mollified gradients and De la Vallée Poussin are ready. After this come the radial-profile comparison and the BV Sobolev equality cases.
- **Frontier and domain corollaries** — the `Hⁿ⁻¹(frontier E)` form with its a.e. rigidity, exact rigidity for regular open sets, and Lipschitz and `C¹` domains. These depend on GMT G5–G6 boundary comparisons.
- **Planar finite-perimeter compatibility and acceptance examples** — identify the Jordan theorem with the finite-perimeter specialization through `P(filledHull C \ C) = H¹(C)`, and supply the examples, including the non-AC Cantor-parametrized circle.
