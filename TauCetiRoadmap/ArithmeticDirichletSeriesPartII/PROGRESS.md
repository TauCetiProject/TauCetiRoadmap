## ArithmeticDirichletSeriesPartII: 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-25 (`eec695d` to `7433fc3`)

The roadmap's two standing hypotheses now exist as predicates with working APIs. [`HasLaplace`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/LaplaceTransform/Basic.html#TauCeti.HasLaplace) says that the ordinary one-sided Laplace integral converges absolutely to a given value. Around it come linearity, holomorphy of a transform on its half-plane, the model transform `k!/(s − a)^(k+1)` of `e^(at) t^k` ([`hasLaplace_exp_mul_pow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/LaplaceTransform/Elementary.html#TauCeti.hasLaplace_exp_mul_pow)) and the rule for the transform of a derivative ([`hasLaplace_deriv`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/LaplaceTransform/Basic.html#TauCeti.hasLaplace_deriv)). [`PoleBoundary`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/PoleBoundary.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.PoleBoundary) is Delange's boundary condition: a pole of order `k + 1` at `a` and holomorphic germs everywhere else on the line. It comes with uniqueness of the order and leading coefficient, a product rule under which pole orders add, and the fact that `ζ^(n+1)` has a pole of order `n + 1` at `1` ([`poleBoundary_riemannZeta_pow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/PoleBoundary.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.poleBoundary_riemannZeta_pow)).

Both auxiliary functions of Delange's proof are in place. The squared-sinc kernel has mass one and the triangle as its Fourier transform ([`squaredSinc_fourier`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/SquaredSinc.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.squaredSinc_fourier)), and the regularising weight has a proved closed form ([`delangeWeight_mul_pow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/DelangeWeight.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.delangeWeight_mul_pow)) and uniform slow variation. The first analytic step is done too: [`regularizedBoundaryL1`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/Regularized.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.regularizedBoundaryL1) gives the regularised remainder locally integrable boundary values. Separately, the smoothed and interval forms of Wiener–Ikehara for complex coefficients landed ([`wienerIkehara_smoothed`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/WienerIkehara/Smoothed.html#TauCeti.LSeries.wienerIkehara_smoothed), [`wienerIkehara_interval`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/WienerIkehara/Smoothed.html#TauCeti.LSeries.wienerIkehara_interval)), with an example showing that a Chebyshev bound alone does not give the interval form.

The Dirichlet series of the `k`-fold divisor function is shown to sum to `ζ(s)^k` ([`LSeriesHasSum_zeta_pow`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/Zeta/Pow.html#ArithmeticFunction.LSeriesHasSum_zeta_pow)). The dyadic function `1/(1 − 2^(1−s))` has the right local pole at `1` but [fails the boundary condition](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/NumberTheory/LSeries/HigherPole/PoleBoundary.html#TauCeti.HigherPoleTauberian.not_poleBoundary_inv_one_sub_cexp_log_two). The smoothed limit, Delange's theorem and every counting theorem are still missing, so no asymptotic for a higher-order pole has been proved yet.
