# Roadmap: sequential analysis

**Summary.** In a sequential test the number of observations is not fixed in advance: after
each draw the statistician either decides or asks for another. The sample size is then a
stopping time, and the basic tools are identities for a random walk observed at a stopping time.
This roadmap proves those identities for walks with independent, identically distributed real
increments and applies them to Wald's sequential probability ratio test for two probability
measures `P` and `Q`. Its main results are the following.

- *Wald's equation.* If the increments are integrable and the stopping time `τ` has finite mean,
  then `E[S_τ] = E[τ] · E[X]`. If they are square integrable with mean `μ`, then also
  `E[(S_τ − μτ)²] = E[τ] · Var(X)`.
- *Exit times of an interval.* If the increments are not almost surely zero, the time at which
  the walk leaves a bounded interval has an exponentially small tail. For such increments and every
  `θ` with `φ(θ) = E[exp(θX)]` finite, the walk stopped at that time satisfies
  `E[exp(θ S_τ) φ(θ)^(−τ)] = 1`.
- *The threshold inequalities.* Let `P` and `Q` be mutually absolutely continuous. The test
  continues while the sum of the log-likelihood ratios `log(dQ/dP)` lies in `(a, b)`, rejects
  `P` when the sum reaches `b` or more, and accepts `P` when it reaches `a` or less. Let `α` be
  the probability of rejecting `P` under `P`, and `β` the probability of accepting `P` under `Q`.
  Then `e^b α ≤ 1 − β` and `β ≤ e^a (1 − α)`. These hold for any thresholds `a < b` and also
  when an error probability is `0`. If `P ≠ Q`, the test stops almost surely under both laws.
- *Expected sample size.* Let `τ` be a stopping time of the observations and `E` an event
  decided by time `τ`. For every pair of laws, the binary divergence between the probabilities
  of `E` under `P` and `Q` is at most `E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q)`. For the test above with
  thresholds `a < 0 < b` and a log-likelihood ratio bounded by `c`,
  `E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q) ≤ c − a` and `E_Q[τ] · D(Q ‖ P) ≤ c + b`.

The roadmap builds on Mathlib's stopping times, optional stopping at bounded times, infinite
product measures and Kullback–Leibler divergence. It uses the exponential martingale of a walk
from RuinTheory, and the binary divergence with its data-processing bound for an event from
MathematicalStatisticsNonparametric. HypothesisTestingExponents states the error exponents of
tests with a fixed number of observations, and OptimalStopping states finite-horizon optimal
stopping; neither is used here.

Layer 1 below proves Wald's equation and the second identity for a general filtration. Layer 2
defines the exit time of an interval and proves its exponential tail and the exponential identity.
Layer 3 builds the observation filtration on sequences, proves the likelihood ratio identity at a
stopping time, and derives termination, the threshold inequalities and the bounds on the expected
sample size.

## Conventions

- **Walks.** In Layers 1 and 2 the increments are real random variables `X i : Ω → ℝ`, `i : ℕ`,
  on a probability space `(Ω, P)`, and the walk is `Sₙ = ∑ i ∈ Finset.range n, X i`, written out
  in every statement, so `S₀ = 0` and `X i` moves the walk from time `i` to time `i + 1`.
  Identically distributed means Mathlib's `IdentDistrib (X i) (X 0) P P` for every `i`.
- **Filtrations.** Layer 1 allows any filtration `ℱ` with `X i` strongly measurable for
  `ℱ (i + 1)` and independent of `ℱ i` (Mathlib's `Indep` of `MeasurableSpace.comap (X i)` and
  `ℱ i`), the convention of RuinTheory 2.2. The natural filtration of the walk,
  `σ(X 0, …, X (n − 1))` at time `n`, satisfies it for independent increments; Mathlib's natural
  filtration of the increments themselves, `σ(X 0, …, X n)`, does not (Check of 1.1). Layer 2
  states its results for independent increments (`iIndepFun X P`) and uses the natural
  filtration of the walk in its routes.
- **Stopping times.** A stopping time is `τ : Ω → WithTop ℕ` with Mathlib's `IsStoppingTime`,
  `⊤` meaning that the walk never stops. Its mean is `∫⁻ ω, ENat.toENNReal (τ ω) ∂P` in
  `[0, ∞]`; a finite mean forces `τ < ⊤` almost surely. `stoppedValue u τ` is `u` read at `τ`;
  on `{τ = ⊤}` Mathlib gives it a junk value (the process at an unspecified time, through
  `WithTop.untopA`), so every statement either has `τ < ⊤` almost surely or integrates over
  `{τ ≠ ⊤}` only. The σ-algebra of events decided by time `τ` is Mathlib's
  `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace`; its events are measurable.
- **Observations.** In Layer 3, `𝓧` is a measurable space, `P` (the null hypothesis) and `Q`
  (the alternative) are probability measures on it, and an outcome is a sequence `ω : ℕ → 𝓧`
  with law `P^∞ = Measure.infinitePi fun _ ↦ P` or `Q^∞`. The observation filtration has at
  time `n` the σ-algebra generated by `ω 0, …, ω (n − 1)`; at time `0` it is trivial.
- **Likelihood ratios.** `dQ/dP` is Mathlib's `Q.rnDeriv P`, a function to `[0, ∞]`, and the
  log-likelihood ratio is Mathlib's `llr Q P x = log (dQ/dP)(x)`, computed through `toReal`, so
  that it is `0` where the density is `0` or `∞` (`Real.log 0 = 0`). For mutually absolutely
  continuous `P` and `Q` the density lies in `(0, ∞)` almost surely under both laws and the
  logarithm is the true one. The threshold inequalities (3.3) and the bounds of 3.5 are stated for
  such pairs, and termination (3.2) for `Q ≪ P`. Where `P ≪ Q` fails, the true log-likelihood
  ratio is `−∞` on a set of positive `P`-probability and the Lean value there is `0`; where
  `Q ≪ P` fails, it is `+∞` on a set of positive `Q`-probability, where `P` has no mass; there
  `Q.rnDeriv P` takes some unspecified value in `[0, ∞]`, and the Lean value is the logarithm of
  its `toReal`, an unspecified real number. Either way the Lean test differs from the true one
  (Checks of 3.3). `D(P ‖ Q)` is Mathlib's `klDiv P Q` in `[0, ∞]`.
- **Arithmetic in `[0, ∞]`.** Products of an expected stopping time and a divergence are taken in
  `ℝ≥0∞`, where `∞ · 0 = 0`. Logarithms are natural.

## Layer 1: Wald's identities

### 1.1 Wald's equation

Prove `wald_equation`: let `P` be a probability measure, `ℱ` a filtration, and let the
increments `X i` be strongly measurable for `ℱ (i + 1)`, independent of `ℱ i`, identically
distributed and integrable. For every stopping time `τ` of `ℱ` with finite mean, `S_τ` is
integrable and `E[S_τ] = E[τ] · E[X 0]` (Durrett, Theorem 2.6.2, p. 92, and Theorem 4.8.6,
p. 257; Ferguson, §3.3, Theorem 4, p. 3.7. Both state it for the natural filtration of the
increments; the route below uses only the independence of `X i` from `ℱ i`, and is complete).

Route (Durrett's proof of Theorem 2.6.2). On `{τ < ⊤}`, `S_τ = ∑ i, X i · 1{τ > i}`. The event
`{τ > i}` is the complement of `{τ ≤ i} ∈ ℱ i`, so it is independent of `X i`, and
`E[|X i| 1{τ > i}] = E|X 0| · P(τ > i)`. Summing over `i`, `∑ i, E[|X i| 1{τ > i}] = E|X 0| · E[τ]`
is finite (`E[τ] = ∑ i, P(τ > i)`), so the series converges in `L¹` and may be integrated term by
term (`integral_tsum`); the terms are `E[X 0] · P(τ > i)`, with sum `E[X 0] · E[τ]`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `IsStoppingTime`, `stoppedValue`, `IndepFun.integral_mul_eq_mul_integral`
(through `Indep`), `integral_tsum`, `lintegral_tsum`, `IdentDistrib.integral_eq`.

**Checks.**

- Steps `1` with probability `p` and `0` otherwise, and `τ` the first `n` with `Sₙ = r` for an
  integer `r ≥ 1`: `S_τ = r`, so `E[τ] = r/p`, the mean of the negative binomial law (Ferguson,
  Example 3, pp. 3.8–3.9); for `p = 1/2`, `r = 2` it is `4`.
- A finite mean is needed: for steps `±1` with probability `1/2` and `τ` the first `n` with
  `Sₙ = 1`, `τ < ⊤` almost surely and `S_τ = 1`, while `E[X 0] = 0`; here `E[τ] = ∞`, and the
  right side with `(∞).toReal = 0` would be `0` (Ferguson, Remark 1, p. 3.8).
- Independence from `ℱ i` is needed: with `ℱ i = σ(X 0, …, X i)`, steps `±1` with probability
  `1/2`, and `τ = 1` if `X 0 = 1` and `τ = 0` otherwise (a stopping time, since
  `{τ ≤ 0} = {X 0 = −1} ∈ ℱ 0`), `E[S_τ] = P(X 0 = 1) = 1/2` while `E[τ] · E[X 0] = 0`.
- `τ = 0`: both sides are `0`. A constant `τ = n` gives `E[Sₙ] = n E[X 0]`.

### 1.2 Wald's second identity

Prove `wald_second_identity`: under the hypotheses of 1.1 with `X 0` square integrable and
`μ = E[X 0]`, for every stopping time `τ` with finite mean, `(S_τ − μτ)²` is integrable and
`E[(S_τ − μτ)²] = E[τ] · Var(X 0)` (Durrett, Exercise 4.8.4, p. 261, for mean zero; proved
here, for every mean).

Route (proved here). Put `Y i = X i − μ`, which satisfies the hypotheses of 1.1 with mean `0`,
and `Tₙ = ∑ i < n, Y i · 1{τ > i}`, so that `Tₙ = S_(τ∧n) − μ (τ∧n)`. For `i < j` the product
`Y i 1{τ > i} 1{τ > j}` is `ℱ j`-measurable and square integrable, and `Y j` is independent of
`ℱ j` with mean `0`, so the cross terms of `E[(Tₙ − Tₘ)²]` vanish; the diagonal terms are
`E[Y i² 1{τ > i}] = Var(X 0) · P(τ > i)` by independence. Hence
`E[(Tₙ − Tₘ)²] = Var(X 0) ∑ m ≤ i < n, P(τ > i)`, which tends to `0` because
`E[τ] = ∑ i, P(τ > i) < ∞`. So `Tₙ` converges in `L²`; it converges almost surely to
`S_τ − μτ` (it is constant from time `τ` on, and `τ < ⊤` almost surely), and the two limits
agree. Therefore `E[(S_τ − μτ)²] = lim E[Tₙ²] = Var(X 0) · lim E[τ∧n] = Var(X 0) · E[τ]`.

*Needs:* 1.1 (its hypotheses, not its conclusion); Mathlib `MemLp`, `MemLp.integrable_sq`,
`variance`, `IndepFun.integral_mul_eq_mul_integral`, `Lp` completeness
(`MeasureTheory.Lp.instCompleteSpace`), `tendstoInMeasure_of_tendsto_eLpNorm`.

**Checks.**

- Steps `±1` with probability `1/2` and `τ` the exit time from `(−2, 3)` (2.1): `S_τ` is `−2`
  with probability `3/5` and `3` with probability `2/5`, so `E[S_τ²] = 12/5 + 18/5 = 6`, and
  `E[τ] = 6`, computed exactly from the walk killed outside `{−1, 0, 1, 2}`.
- Centring is needed: for `X i = 1` and `τ = 2`, `E[S_τ²] = 4`, while `Var(X 0) = 0` and
  `E[X 0²] · E[τ] = 2`; the centred form gives `0 = 0`.
- A finite mean is needed: for the first passage of the fair `±1` walk to `1`, `S_τ² = 1`, while
  the right side with `E[τ] = ∞` read as `(∞).toReal = 0` is `0`.

### Examples

The negative binomial waiting time, the fair walk on an interval, and the first passage of the
fair walk to `1`, which has `S_τ = 1` with mean-zero increments.

### Dependencies

Mathlib only (`IsStoppingTime`, `stoppedValue`, `integral_tsum`, `lintegral_tsum`,
`IndepFun.integral_mul_eq_mul_integral`, `IdentDistrib.integral_eq`, `MemLp`, `variance`, `Lp`
completeness). 1.2 uses the hypotheses of 1.1, not its conclusion.

## Layer 2: exit times of an interval

### 2.1 The exit time and its exponential tail

Define `exitTime X a b` as Mathlib's `hittingAfter` of the walk in the complement of the open
interval `(a, b)` from time `0`: the first `n ≥ 0` with `Sₙ ≤ a` or `Sₙ ≥ b`, and `⊤` if there is
none. For measurable increments it is a stopping time of the natural filtration of the walk
(`Adapted.isStoppingTime_hittingAfter`). It is `0` when `0 ∉ (a, b)`, in particular when `b ≤ a`.

Prove `exists_measureReal_lt_exitTime_le`: for measurable, independent, identically distributed
increments that are not almost surely `0` and all real `a`, `b`, there are `C` and `0 ≤ ρ < 1`
with `P(τ > n) ≤ C ρⁿ` for every `n`, where `τ = exitTime X a b`. Hence `τ < ⊤` almost surely and
`E[τ^r]` is finite for every `r` (proved here; the block argument is the one Durrett uses for the
simple random walk in the proof of Theorem 4.8.7, p. 258, and for renewal counts in the proof of
Theorem 2.6.3, p. 93).

Route (proved here). If `b ≤ a` then `τ = 0`. Otherwise, since `X 0` is not almost surely `0`
there is `ε > 0` with `P(X 0 > ε) > 0` or `P(X 0 < −ε) > 0`; take the first case (the second is
symmetric) and put `p = P(X 0 > ε)` and `k = ⌈(b − a)/ε⌉`. If `τ > jk` then `S_(jk) ∈ (a, b)`,
and if moreover the `k` increments `X (jk), …, X (jk + k − 1)` all exceed `ε`, the walk is above
`b` by time `(j + 1)k`, so `τ ≤ (j + 1)k`. The event `{τ > jk}` depends on `X 0, …, X (jk − 1)`
only, and the block is independent of it with probability `pᵏ` of all increments exceeding `ε`.
Hence `P(τ > (j + 1)k) ≤ (1 − pᵏ) P(τ > jk)` and `P(τ > jk) ≤ (1 − pᵏ)ʲ`. Take
`ρ = (1 − pᵏ)^(1/k)` and `C = 1/ρᵏ`, using `k⌊n/k⌋ > n − k`; if `pᵏ = 1`, then
`P(τ > k) = 0`, and `ρ = 1/2`, `C = 2ᵏ` work.

*Needs:* Mathlib `hittingAfter`, `Adapted.isStoppingTime_hittingAfter`, `Filtration.natural`,
`iIndepFun`, `iIndepFun.indep_comap_natural_of_lt`, `IdentDistrib.measure_mem_eq`.

**Checks.**

- Steps `±1` with probability `1/2` and `(a, b) = (−2, 2)`: from `0` two steps either exit or
  return to `0`, each with probability `1/2`, so `τ` is even and `P(τ > 2k) = 2^(−k)`; the bound
  holds with `ρ = 2^(−1/2)` and `C = 2^(1/2)`.
- The hypothesis is needed: for `X i = 0` and `a < 0 < b`, `τ = ⊤` everywhere and
  `P(τ > n) = 1` for every `n`.
- `0 ∉ (a, b)`: `τ = 0` and `P(τ > n) = 0`.

### 2.2 The exponential identity

Prove `wald_exponential_identity`: for measurable, independent, identically distributed
increments that are not almost surely `0`, all real `a`, `b`, and every real `θ` with
`exp(θ X 0)` integrable, put `φ(θ) = E[exp(θ X 0)]` (Mathlib's `mgf (X 0) P θ`, which is then
positive) and `τ = exitTime X a b`. Then
`E[exp(θ S_τ) / φ(θ)^τ ; τ < ⊤] = 1` (Durrett, Exercise 4.8.8, p. 262, for `θ` with
`φ(θ) = 1`; proved here for every `θ` with `φ(θ)` finite).

Route (proved here). Let `ℱ` be the natural filtration of the walk. By RuinTheory 2.2
(`martingale_exp_mul_sum_div_prod_mgf`), `Mₙ = exp(θ Sₙ) / φ(θ)ⁿ` is a martingale for `ℱ`; the
product of the moment generating functions of the `X i` is `φ(θ)ⁿ` because they are identically
distributed (`IdentDistrib.comp`). Optional stopping at the bounded time `τ ∧ n`
(`Martingale.stoppedValue_min_ae_eq_condExp`) gives
`E[M_τ ; τ ≤ n] + E[Mₙ ; τ > n] = 1`. The first term increases with `n` (`M ≥ 0`) to
`E[M_τ ; τ < ⊤]` (`lintegral_iSup`), so it suffices that `E[Mₙ ; τ > n] → 0`. This reduction is
the optional sampling under a vanishing tail of StochasticCalculus §2.3 (an item of its README
without a Lean declaration, stated there with time starting at `1`, `τ < ∞` almost surely and the
stopped value assumed integrable); here `M ≥ 0` and monotone convergence give that integrability,
and the short argument is kept. Take `ε`, `k` as in 2.1 with `P(X 0 > ε) > 0` (the other case
is symmetric) and put `q = E[exp(θ X 0) ; X 0 > ε] / φ(θ) > 0`. With `B_j` the event that
`X (jk), …, X (jk + k − 1)` all exceed `ε`, the route of 2.1 gives
`{τ > (j + 1)k} ⊆ {τ > jk} ∖ B_j`. Write `M_((j+1)k) = M_(jk) · W` with
`W = ∏ exp(θ X i)/φ(θ)` over the block. `M_(jk) 1{τ > jk}` is `ℱ (jk)`-measurable and the
block is independent of `ℱ (jk)`, so
`E[M_((j+1)k) ; τ > (j + 1)k] ≤ E[M_(jk) ; τ > jk] · E[W ; B_jᶜ] = (1 − qᵏ) E[M_(jk) ; τ > jk]`,
since `E[W] = 1` and `E[W ; B_j] = qᵏ`. Hence `E[M_(jk) ; τ > jk] ≤ (1 − qᵏ)ʲ`. For
`jk ≤ n`, `{τ > n} ⊆ {τ > jk}` and `M ≥ 0`, so `E[Mₙ ; τ > n] ≤ E[Mₙ ; τ > jk]`, which equals
`E[M_(jk) ; τ > jk]` by the martingale property, since `{τ > jk} ∈ ℱ (jk)`. Letting `n → ∞`
with `j = ⌊n/k⌋` gives the limit `0`.

*Needs:* 2.1 (the definition); RuinTheory 2.2 (`martingale_exp_mul_sum_div_prod_mgf`); Mathlib
`Martingale.stoppedValue_min_ae_eq_condExp`, `Martingale.setIntegral_eq`, `lintegral_iSup`,
`IdentDistrib.comp`, `mgf_pos`, `iIndepFun.indep_comap_natural_of_lt`, `Filtration.natural`.

**Checks.**

- Steps `+1` with probability `2/3` and `−1` with probability `1/3`, `(a, b) = (−2, 2)`: summing
  `exp(θ S_τ) φ(θ)^(−τ)` over the paths of the walk killed outside `{−1, 0, 1}`, in exact
  rational arithmetic, gives `1` for `e^θ = 1/5, 1/2, 3/4, 2`, where `φ(θ) = 9/5, 1, 17/18, 3/2`.
  For `e^θ = 3/4`, where `φ(θ) < 1`, the contributions of the exits at `2` and at `−2` are
  `81/145` and `64/145`.
- `θ = 0` gives `P(τ < ⊤) = 1`, the conclusion of 2.1 on termination.
- Nondegeneracy is needed: for `X i = 0` and `a < 0 < b`, `τ = ⊤` everywhere and the left side is
  `0`.
- Integrability of `exp(θ X 0)` is needed: for standard Cauchy increments and `θ = 1`, Mathlib's
  `mgf` is `0`, so for `a < 0 < b` (where `τ ≥ 1`) the integrand is `exp(θ S_τ)/0 = 0` and the left
  side is `0`.

### Examples

The fair walk on `(−2, 2)`; the walk with steps `+1` and `−1` of probabilities `2/3` and `1/3`,
for several values of `θ` on both sides of the minimum of `φ`.

### Dependencies

2.2 uses the definition of 2.1 and RuinTheory 2.2. Mathlib `hittingAfter`,
`Adapted.isStoppingTime_hittingAfter`, `Filtration.natural`, `iIndepFun.indep_comap_natural_of_lt`,
`IdentDistrib.measure_mem_eq`, `IdentDistrib.comp`, `mgf_pos`,
`Martingale.stoppedValue_min_ae_eq_condExp`, `Martingale.setIntegral_eq`, `lintegral_iSup`.
Layer 2 does not use Layer 1.

## Layer 3: the sequential probability ratio test

### 3.1 The likelihood ratio identity at a stopping time

Define `obsFiltration 𝓧`, the filtration on `ℕ → 𝓧` whose σ-algebra at time `n` is the supremum
of the σ-algebras generated by the coordinates `ω i` with `i < n`. It differs from Mathlib's
`Filtration.piLE`, which at time `n` also contains `ω n`: with this one, the walk of
log-likelihood ratios of the first `n` observations is measurable at time `n` and the `n`-th
observation is independent of time `n` under `P^∞` and `Q^∞`.

Prove `infinitePi_inter_ne_top_eq_setLIntegral`: for probability measures with `Q ≪ P` and
`f = dQ/dP`, every stopping time `τ` of `obsFiltration 𝓧` and every event `E` decided by time `τ`,

    Q^∞(E ∩ {τ < ⊤}) = ∫⁻_{E ∩ {τ < ⊤}} f(ω 0) ⋯ f(ω (τ − 1)) dP^∞

(Polyanskiy–Wu, (16.13) and (16.14), p. 323, in the form `dP|F_τ / dQ|F_τ = exp S_τ` under the
bounded log-likelihood ratio of their Theorem 16.4; here with the density product, for every
`Q ≪ P`, and with the event `{τ = ⊤}` excluded).

Route (the source's). On the σ-algebra of the first `n` observations, `Q^∞` has density
`Lₙ = f(ω 0) ⋯ f(ω (n − 1))` with respect to `P^∞`: both laws restrict to the finite products
`Qⁿ` and `Pⁿ` (`Measure.infinitePi_map_restrict`), and `Qⁿ = (Pⁿ).withDensity (∏ f(xᵢ))` by
`Measure.withDensity_rnDeriv_eq` and Tau Ceti's `pi_withDensity`. For `E` decided by `τ`,
`E ∩ {τ = n}` lies in that σ-algebra, so `Q^∞(E ∩ {τ = n}) = ∫⁻_{E ∩ {τ = n}} Lₙ dP^∞`. Sum over
`n` (countable additivity on both sides).

*Needs:* Mathlib `Measure.infinitePi`, `Measure.infinitePi_map_restrict`, `Measure.rnDeriv`,
`Measure.withDensity_rnDeriv_eq`, `IsStoppingTime.measurableSpace`,
`IsStoppingTime.measurableSet_eq_of_countable`, `lintegral_iUnion`; Tau Ceti `pi_withDensity`.

**Checks.**

- `𝓧 = {0, 1}`, `P(1) = 1/2`, `Q(1) = 1/4`, `τ` the number of observations up to and including
  the first `1`, `E` everything: `Q^∞(τ < ⊤) = 1`, and the right side is
  `∑ n ≥ 1, 2^(−n) (3/2)^(n−1) (1/2) = (1/4) ∑ n ≥ 1, (3/4)^(n−1) = 1`.
- The event `{τ = ⊤}` must be excluded: for `Q` the point mass at `0` (so `Q ≪ P`) and the same
  `τ`, `Q^∞(τ < ⊤) = 0` and the right side is `0` because `f(1) = 0`, while `Q^∞` of the whole
  space is `1`.
- `Q ≪ P` is needed: for `P` the point mass at `0`, `Q` the point mass at `1`, `τ = 1` and `E`
  everything, the left side is `1`, while `dQ/dP = 0` `P`-almost everywhere
  (`Measure.rnDeriv_eq_zero`) and the right side is `0`.

### 3.2 The test and its termination

Define `sprtTime P Q a b` as the exit time (2.1) from `(a, b)` of the walk with increments
`llr Q P (ω i)`, `sprtReject P Q a b` as the event that this time is finite and the walk at it
is at least `b` (the test rejects `P`), and `sprtAccept P Q a b` as the event that the time is
finite and the walk at it is at most `a` (the test accepts `P`). For `a < b` the two events are
disjoint, and their union is `{τ < ⊤}`. In terms of the likelihood ratio, the test continues
while it lies in `(e^a, e^b)`.

Prove `exists_measureReal_lt_sprtTime_le`: for probability measures with `Q ≪ P` and `P ≠ Q`
and all real `a`, `b`, there are `C` and `0 ≤ ρ < 1` with `P^∞(τ > n) ≤ C ρⁿ` and
`Q^∞(τ > n) ≤ C ρⁿ` for every `n`. In particular the test stops almost surely under both laws, so
`P^∞(accept) = 1 − P^∞(reject)` and `Q^∞(reject) = 1 − Q^∞(accept)` when `a < b` (proved
here).

Route (proved here). Under `P^∞` and under `Q^∞` the increments `llr Q P (ω i)` are measurable,
independent and identically distributed (`iIndepFun_infinitePi`, `measurable_llr`), so 2.1
applies under each law once the increment is shown not to be almost surely `0`. Let `f = dQ/dP`;
then `Q = P.withDensity f` and `∫ f dP = 1`. The Lean increment is `0` exactly where `f` is `0`,
`1` or `∞`, and `f < ∞` `P`-almost everywhere. If `f ∈ {0, 1}` `P`-almost everywhere, then
`1 = ∫ f dP = P(f = 1)`, so `f = 1` `P`-almost everywhere and `Q = P`. Hence `P(f ∉ {0, 1, ∞}) > 0`.
Under `Q`: `Q(f = 0) = ∫_{f=0} f dP = 0` and `Q(f = ∞) = 0` (since `Q ≪ P`); if `Q(f ≠ 1) = 0`
then `f = 0` `P`-almost everywhere on `{f ≠ 1}`, which was excluded above. Take the larger of the
two constants `C` and of the two rates `ρ`.

*Needs:* 2.1; Mathlib `iIndepFun_infinitePi`, `measurable_llr`, `Measure.withDensity_rnDeriv_eq`,
`Measure.rnDeriv_lt_top`.

**Checks.**

- `𝓧 = {0, 1}`, `P = (2/3, 1/3)`, `Q = (1/3, 2/3)` (the masses at `0` and `1`): the increments
  are `log 2` at `1` and `−log 2` at `0`, a walk on the lattice `log 2 · ℤ` that moves up with
  probability `1/3` under `P` and `2/3` under `Q`. For `a = −log 2` and `b = 2 log 2` the walk
  lives on `{0, log 2}` before stopping, and `P^∞(τ > 2j) = Q^∞(τ > 2j) = (2/9)ʲ`.
- `P ≠ Q` is needed: for `P = Q`, `llr P P = 0` `P`-almost everywhere (`llr_self`), and for
  `a < 0 < b` the test never stops.
- `Q ≪ P` is needed: for `P` the point mass at `0` and `Q` the point mass at `1`,
  `dQ/dP = 0` `P`-almost everywhere, so the Lean increment is `0` `P`-almost surely and for
  `a < 0 < b` the test never stops under `P^∞`.

### 3.3 The threshold inequalities

Prove `exp_mul_measureReal_sprtReject_le`: for mutually absolutely continuous probability
measures `P`, `Q` and `a < b`, with `α = P^∞(reject)` and `β = Q^∞(accept)`,

    e^b · α ≤ 1 − β   and   β ≤ e^a · (1 − α).

In particular `α ≤ e^(−b)` and `β ≤ e^a`. On the likelihood-ratio scale with `A = e^b` and
`B = e^a` these are `A ≤ (1 − β)/α` and `B ≥ β/(1 − α)` whenever the denominators are positive;
the products above hold also when `α` or `1 − α` is `0` (Polyanskiy–Wu, achievability part of
the proof of Theorem 16.4, p. 323, proves `α ≤ e^(−b)` and `β ≤ e^a` in its notation; the forms
with `1 − β` and `1 − α` are proved here). No termination hypothesis is needed.

Route (proved here). Since `P ≪ Q`, the density `f = dQ/dP` lies in `(0, ∞)` `P`-almost
everywhere, so `exp(llr Q P) = f` `P`-almost everywhere (`exp_llr_of_ac'`), and on `{τ < ⊤}`
the product `f(ω 0) ⋯ f(ω (τ − 1))` equals `exp S_τ` `P^∞`-almost surely. The events of rejection
and acceptance are decided by time `τ` (the walk is adapted, and its stopped value is measurable
for that σ-algebra by Tau Ceti's `Adapted.measurable_stoppedValue`). By 3.1 (which uses
`Q ≪ P`), `Q^∞(reject) = ∫_{reject} exp S_τ dP^∞ ≥ e^b α`, and
`Q^∞(accept) = ∫_{accept} exp S_τ dP^∞ ≤ e^a P^∞(accept)`. Finally `Q^∞(reject) ≤ 1 − β` and
`P^∞(accept) ≤ 1 − α`, since the events are disjoint for `a < b`.

*Needs:* 3.1, 3.2 (definitions); Mathlib `exp_llr_of_ac'`, `Adapted.isStoppingTime_hittingAfter`;
Tau Ceti `Adapted.measurable_stoppedValue`.

**Checks.**

- The laws of the Check of 3.2 with `a = −log 2`, `b = 2 log 2`: by the gambler's-ruin formula
  (computed exactly from the killed walk) `α = 1/7` and `β = 3/7`, and both inequalities are
  equalities: `4 · 1/7 = 4/7 = 1 − 3/7` and `3/7 = (1/2)(6/7)`. The walk stops exactly on the
  thresholds, so there is no overshoot.
- Overshoot makes the inequalities strict: with `b = log 3` instead, the walk still rejects only
  at `2 log 2`, the events are unchanged, and `3 · 1/7 = 3/7 < 4/7`. The reverse inequality
  `α ≥ e^(−b)(1 − β)` therefore fails: `1/7 < (1/3)(4/7) = 4/21`.
- `P ≪ Q` is needed: for `𝓧 = {0, 1}`, `P = (1/2, 1/2)`, `Q` the point mass at `0`, `a = −1` and
  `b = 1/2`, `dQ/dP` is `2` at `0` and `0` at `1`, so the Lean increment is `log 2` at `0` and `0`
  at `1`. Under `P^∞` the test rejects at the first `0`, so `α = 1`; under `Q^∞` it rejects at
  once, so `β = 0`; and `e^(1/2) · 1 ≤ 1 − 0` fails.
- `Q ≪ P` is needed, whatever value the Lean increment takes where `P` has no mass: for
  `𝓧 = {0, 1}`, `P` the point mass at `0` and `Q = (1/2, 1/2)`, the increment is `−log 2` at `0`
  and some real `L` at `1`. Take `a = −2 log 2` and `b > |L|`. Under `P^∞` the test accepts at
  time `2`, so `α = 0`. Under `Q^∞` it accepts when the first two observations are `0`, and also
  on the paths that start with `1` (where the walk is at `L < b`) followed by enough `0`s, so
  `β > 1/4 = e^a (1 − α)`. For `L = 0`, `log 2`, `2 log 2` (with `b = log 2`, `2 log 2`,
  `3 log 2`) the killed walk gives `β = 1`, `1/2`, `1/3`.
- `a < b` is needed: for `a = b = 0`, `τ = 0` and both events are the whole space, so
  `α = β = 1` and `e^0 · 1 ≤ 1 − 1` fails.

### 3.4 A lower bound on the expected sample size

Copy verbatim from MathematicalStatisticsNonparametric 1.3 the binary divergence `klBin`,
`klBin x y = x log(x/y) + (1 − x) log((1 − x)/(1 − y))`.

Prove `ofReal_klBin_le_lintegral_mul_klDiv`: for all probability measures `P`, `Q` on `𝓧`, every
stopping time `τ` of `obsFiltration 𝓧` and every event `E` decided by time `τ`,

    klBin (P^∞ E) (Q^∞ E) ≤ E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q)   in [0, ∞]

(Polyanskiy–Wu, converse part of the proof of Theorem 16.4, pp. 323–324, under a bounded
log-likelihood ratio and with `E[τ]` finite; proved here for all laws and stopping times). For a
test that stops almost surely under both laws, with acceptance event `E`, it reads
`E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q) ≥ klBin (1 − α) β`, and with `P` and `Q` exchanged and the rejection event,
`E_Q[τ] · D(Q ‖ P) ≥ klBin (1 − β) α`.

Route (proved here). If `D(P ‖ Q) = 0` then `P = Q` (`klDiv_eq_zero_iff`) and the left side is
`klBin x x = 0`. If `E_P[τ] = ∞` and `D(P ‖ Q) > 0` the right side is `∞`. If `E_P[τ] = 0`, then
`τ = 0` `P^∞`-almost surely; `{τ = 0}` lies in the trivial σ-algebra at time `0`, so `τ = 0`
everywhere, `E` is empty or everything, and the left side is `0`. If `D(P ‖ Q) = ∞` and
`E_P[τ] > 0` the right side is `∞`. There remains `0 < E_P[τ] < ∞` and `D(P ‖ Q) < ∞`, so
`P ≪ Q` and `llr P Q` is `P`-integrable (`klDiv_ne_top_iff`). Let `𝒢` be the σ-algebra of events
decided by `τ`. By 3.1 with `P` and `Q` exchanged and `g = dP/dQ`, for `A ∈ 𝒢`,
`P^∞(A) = P^∞(A ∩ {τ < ⊤}) = ∫_A G 1{τ < ⊤} dQ^∞` with `G = g(ω 0) ⋯ g(ω (τ − 1))`, which is
`𝒢`-measurable; so the restriction of `P^∞` to `𝒢` is absolutely continuous with respect to that
of `Q^∞`, with this density. Its divergence is `∫ log G dP^∞ = E_P[∑ i < τ, llr P Q (ω i)]`
(`g ∈ (0, ∞)` `P`-almost everywhere, `exp_llr_of_ac`), and by 1.1, applied under `P^∞` to the
increments `llr P Q (ω i)` and `obsFiltration 𝓧`, this is `E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q)`.
MathematicalStatisticsNonparametric 1.3 (`ofReal_klBin_le_klDiv`), applied to the two
restrictions (`Measure.trim`) and the event `E ∈ 𝒢`, gives the claim.

*Needs:* 1.1, 3.1; MathematicalStatisticsNonparametric 1.3 (`klBin`, `ofReal_klBin_le_klDiv`);
Mathlib `klDiv_eq_zero_iff`, `klDiv_ne_top_iff`, `toReal_klDiv_of_measure_eq`, `exp_llr_of_ac`,
`Measure.trim`, `iIndepFun_infinitePi`, `Adapted.measurable_stoppedValue` (Tau Ceti).

**Checks.**

- The test of the Check of 3.3 (`α = 1/7`, `β = 3/7`): `D(P ‖ Q) = (2/3) log 2 − (1/3) log 2 =
  (1/3) log 2` and, by 1.1 with `E_P[S_τ] = 2 log 2 · 1/7 − log 2 · 6/7 = −(4/7) log 2`,
  `E_P[τ] = 12/7`. With `E` the acceptance event, `klBin (6/7) (3/7) = (6/7) log 2 +
  (1/7) log(1/4) = (4/7) log 2 = (12/7)(1/3) log 2`: equality. Exchanging the laws,
  `E_Q[τ] = 15/7` and `klBin (4/7) (1/7) = (5/7) log 2 = (15/7)(1/3) log 2`: equality again.
- A fixed sample size `τ = n` gives `klBin (Pⁿ E) (Qⁿ E) ≤ n D(P ‖ Q)` for every event of the
  first `n` observations.
- `P = Q`: both sides are `0`, also for the test of 3.2 with `a < 0 < b`, whose mean is then `∞`
  (`∞ · 0 = 0`).

### 3.5 The expected sample size of the test

Prove `lintegral_sprtTime_mul_klDiv_le`: for mutually absolutely continuous probability measures
`P`, `Q` and `c` with `|llr Q P| ≤ c` `P`-almost everywhere, for all real `a`, `b`,

    E_P[τ] · D(P ‖ Q) ≤ c − a   and   E_Q[τ] · D(Q ‖ P) ≤ c + b   in [0, ∞]

(the right sides read as `0` when negative), where `τ = sprtTime P Q a b` (Polyanskiy–Wu,
achievability part of the proof of Theorem 16.4, p. 323, in its notation with thresholds `−A` and
`B`, through the one-sided time `τ₀ ≥ τ`; here by a direct route). With 3.3 and 3.4 these are the
non-asymptotic ingredients of the exponent trade-off of Polyanskiy–Wu, Theorem 16.4, which is not
stated here.

Route (proved here). If `0 ∉ (a, b)` then `τ = 0` and both left sides are `0`. Otherwise
`a < 0 < b`. Under `P^∞` the increments `llr Q P (ω i)` are independent, identically distributed,
bounded by `c` almost surely, with mean `−D(P ‖ Q)` (`llr Q P = −llr P Q` `P`-almost everywhere,
`neg_llr`; the divergence is finite because `|llr P Q| ≤ c` almost everywhere). 1.1 at the bounded
stopping time `τ ∧ n` gives `E[S_(τ∧n)] = −E[τ ∧ n] · D(P ‖ Q)`. Before `τ` the walk lies in
`(a, b)`, and at `τ ≥ 1` it is the value before plus one increment, so `S_(τ∧n) ≥ a − c` almost
surely. Hence `E[τ ∧ n] · D(P ‖ Q) ≤ c − a`; let `n → ∞` (monotone convergence). Under `Q^∞` the
mean of the increment is `D(Q ‖ P)`, the bound holds `Q`-almost everywhere since `Q ≪ P`, and
`S_(τ∧n) ≤ b + c` gives the second inequality.

*Needs:* 1.1, 3.2 (definition); Mathlib `neg_llr`, `klDiv_ne_top_iff`,
`toReal_klDiv_of_measure_eq`, `iIndepFun_infinitePi`, `lintegral_iSup`.

**Checks.**

- The test of the Check of 3.3, with `c = log 2`: `(12/7)(1/3) log 2 = (4/7) log 2 ≤ 2 log 2`
  and `(15/7)(1/3) log 2 = (5/7) log 2 ≤ 3 log 2`.
- The bound `c` is needed: for the same laws, `c = 0`, `a = −1/100` and `b = 2 log 2`, the test
  takes at least one observation, so the first left side is at least `(1/3) log 2`, which exceeds
  `1/100`.
- `P = Q`: `D = 0`, and both left sides are `0` although the test never stops when `a < 0 < b`.

The two-point laws `(2/3, 1/3)` and `(1/3, 2/3)`, whose test is a gambler's ruin on a lattice
with no overshoot, so that 3.3 and 3.4 hold with equality; the point masses, which show the role
of absolute continuity.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns Wald's equation and the second identity for walks adapted to a general
filtration, the exit time of an interval with its exponential tail and the exponential identity,
the observation filtration on sequences, the likelihood ratio identity at a stopping time, the
sequential probability ratio test with its termination and threshold inequalities, and the
bounds on the expected sample size of 3.4 and 3.5.

Stated by other roadmaps and cited above, not restated: the exponential martingale of a walk with
independent increments (RuinTheory 2.2); the binary divergence and its data-processing bound for
an event (MathematicalStatisticsNonparametric 1.3). Already in the libraries: stopping times,
hitting times, optional stopping at bounded stopping times, infinite product measures and their
finite-dimensional marginals, independence of coordinates, Radon–Nikodym derivatives,
log-likelihood ratios and the Kullback–Leibler divergence (Mathlib); the density of a finite
product of measures with densities and the measurability of stopped values for the σ-algebra of
a stopping time (Tau Ceti).

Left to other roadmaps and stated differently here:

- RuinTheory 3.2 states the overshoot identity for the one-sided ruin time and an exponent `R > 0`
  with `φ(R) = 1`. 2.2 above is the two-sided exit time of a bounded interval and every `θ` with
  `φ(θ)` finite; of RuinTheory it uses only the martingale of 2.2.
- StochasticCalculus §2.3 states the gambler's-ruin exit probabilities of the nearest-neighbour
  walk, which appear here only in Checks, and, in its README, optional sampling of a discrete
  martingale under a vanishing tail condition, whose short argument the route of 2.2 keeps.
- HypothesisTestingExponents states the error exponents of tests with a fixed number of
  observations: Stein's lemma, the Hellinger bound and Chernoff's theorem. Nothing of it is
  restated or used; the fixed-size case of 3.4 is a Check. This roadmap does not depend on that
  one.
- OptimalStopping states finite-horizon optimal stopping and names the sequential probability
  ratio test outside its scope. The optimality of the test among all sequential tests with the
  same error probabilities (the Wald–Wolfowitz theorem), which rests on infinite-horizon Bayes
  stopping, is not treated here. This roadmap does not depend on that one.
- BestArmIdentification 5.1 states a change-of-measure bound for bandit histories of a fixed
  number of rounds, with the expected numbers of pulls of the arms; 3.1 and 3.4 above concern
  independent observations at a stopping time. This roadmap does not depend on that one.
- RuntimeAnalysisOfEvolutionaryAlgorithms states the additive drift theorems; 3.5 uses Wald's
  equation, not a drift theorem. This roadmap does not depend on that one.

Not treated here: the asymptotic exponent trade-off of Polyanskiy–Wu, Theorem 16.4; Wald's
approximations of the error probabilities and of the expected sample size by neglecting the
overshoot; composite hypotheses; renewal-theoretic corrections for the overshoot.
