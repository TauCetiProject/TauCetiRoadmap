# Gromov–Witten theory for closed symplectic manifolds

This roadmap constructs cohomology-valued Gromov–Witten operations for closed symplectic manifolds, proves their identities from stable-map virtual data, and then constructs the unital CohFT, genus-zero quantum product, and descendants. Listing formal axioms does not construct the classes or prove the identities.

For a closed symplectic manifold $(X^{2m},\omega)$, a smooth tame almost-complex structure $J$, and a class $A\in H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$, the principal operations have the form

\[
 I_{g,n,A}:H^*(X;\mathbb Q)^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow
 H^*(\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n};\mathbb Q),\qquad 2g-2+n>0.
\]

The target is the coarse space of stable pointed curves, equipped with a specified finite-isotropy weighted pairing. The construction passes through the actual domain groupoid and stable-map virtual functional and proves the comparison to that coarse carrier. A finite-group atlas on a singular map space does not make it a smooth orbifold.

Stable maps have connected nodal domains of arithmetic genus $g$, $n$ ordered distinct smooth marks, componentwise $J$-holomorphic maps in class $A$, and finite automorphism group of the full marked map. A pointed domain can be unstable: nonconstant unmarked spheres and tori occur when the map makes the automorphism group finite. Constant components unstable as maps are contracted. The condition $2g-2+n>0$ is needed when stabilizing the pointed domain to $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$; it is not a restriction excluding map-stable domains. The pointed signature $(0,3)$ is stable: $2g-2+n=1$, and $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,3}$ is a point. It is the basic three-point exception in the unit axiom. Pointed-domain signatures $(0,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(0,2)$ and $(1,0)$ are unstable; map-stable operations with those signatures have no $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ target and are defined numerically from the virtual functional where needed.

## Conventions and target

Use rational cohomology, cohomological grading, and Koszul signs. Set $c_1(A)=\langle c_1(TX,J),A\rangle$ and $K_J=-c_1(TX,J)$. The real virtual dimension and operation degree are

\[
 d_{g,n,A}=2\big((1-g)(m-3)+c_1(A)+n\big),\qquad
 \deg I_{g,n,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)
 =\sum_i\deg\gamma_i+2(g-1)m-2c_1(A).
\]

Equivalently, the degree shift is $2(K_J\cdot A)+(2g-2)m$. Keep the real virtual dimension separate from the complex cohomological degree. In splitting identities, order the node branches and inverse Poincaré pairing explicitly; permutations of odd insertions carry the induced Koszul sign.

Targets are compact, boundaryless, Hausdorff, second-countable, and finite-dimensional. Parameter families of tame structures carry uniform taming bounds and specified collars/faces. No integrability or generic-$J$ assumption is built into the goal. Work componentwise for disconnected targets.

For a stable map, $\operatorname{ev}_i$ is evaluation at the $i$th mark. The domain stabilization $\operatorname{st}$ forgets the map and contracts components unstable as pointed curves; it exists only for stable $(g,n)$. Forgetting a mark from a stable map is a separate map: it contracts newly unstable constant components but retains nonconstant components even when their pointed domains become unstable.

## G0. Coefficients, effective classes, and finiteness

Use the integral homology class of each stable map. For fixed $(g,n,J)$, define the realized effective set to contain $0$ and the classes represented by stable maps of that type. Classes outside it have empty moduli and zero operation. Tameness gives positive area to each nonconstant component; a zero-area map is constant and has $A=0$. Retain the area homomorphism even if its image is nondiscrete.

Consume MorseNovikov N1 for the generic completed Novikov ring on the additive group $H=H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$ with period homomorphism $N=\omega:H\to\mathbb R$. Write $\Lambda_\omega=\operatorname{Nov}(\mathbb Q,H,\omega)$ for this commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra; it need not be a field. For a finite-dimensional rational vector space $V$, define $V\widehat\otimes_\mathbb Q\Lambda_\omega$ as finite-below functions $H\to V$ with the N1 support condition. The canonical map $V\otimes_\mathbb Q\Lambda_\omega\to V\widehat\otimes_\mathbb Q\Lambda_\omega$ is an isomorphism for finite-dimensional $V$: prove it by a finite basis and the finite union of the coordinate supports, not as a general completed-base-change theorem. Give this module its support filtration and prove the Cauchy product is continuous and bilinear; for a finite free graded state module, prove the induced pairing is perfect degreewise in the completed grading. Specialize the monomial degree to $\deg q^A=2c_1(A)$ and prove compatibility with that completed cohomological grading. N1’s finite-below support condition proves convolution finiteness for arbitrary Novikov supports; do not infer that arbitrary support classes are represented by curves. G0 separately proves that the geometric supports of the stable-map operations satisfy that condition and that, for a fixed exponent, decompositions of realized classes from two bounded-below geometric supports have summand energies bounded above. Prove that only finitely many integral curve classes occur in those energy ranges as follows. First obtain a finite CW witness for the compact smooth target from GeometricTopology's compact-smooth finite-CW target. Use the finite-CW chain comparison to obtain finitely generated $H_2(X;\mathbb Z)$ (hence finite torsion and a finite-rank free quotient); use AT 6.1’s UCT exact sequence under its stated coefficient hypotheses for the cohomology-to-homology duality step, not as the source of finite generation; this GW item does not reprove generic UCT or chain comparison. Choose closed real two-forms whose de Rham classes pair as a dual basis to the free quotient. For each such form, bound its pairing with a represented class by its comass (uniform on compact $X$) times the total area of the $J$-holomorphic stable map. Fix the auxiliary metric $g_J(v,w)=\tfrac12(\omega(v,Jw)+\omega(w,Jv))$ for the chosen tame $J$; compactness of the target and a compact tame-$J$ parameter family gives a uniform comparison of $g_J$-area and symplectic area on every $J$-complex tangent plane, which supplies the area bound needed here. Bounded area therefore bounds every integer coordinate in the free lattice; finitely many lattice points and the finite torsion subgroup give finitely many classes. Prove the comass bound uniformly over compact tame-$J$ parameter families. DifferentialGeometry supplies the de Rham comparison and closed-form representatives; this GW item proves the curve-pairing/comass estimate and combines the finite list of suppliers rather than treating “finite basis” as a proof. CharacteristicClasses supplies the integral $c_1(TX,J)$ class used in $c_1(A)$ and its natural pairing; it does not prove the area estimate. Do not assume the area image itself is discrete or infer finiteness from the word “effective.”

## G2. From the virtual functional to a cohomology-valued operation

For homogeneous insertions put $s=\sum_i\deg\gamma_i$, $t=6g-6+2n$, $d=d_{g,n,A}$, and $r=t-d+s$. Define $I_{g,n,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)\in H^r(\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n};\mathbb Q)$ by the unique equation

\[
 \int_{\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}}^{\rm wt}\psi\smile I_{g,n,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)
 =\Phi_{g,n,A}\!\left(\operatorname{st}^*\psi\smile
       \prod_{i=1}^n\operatorname{ev}_i^*\gamma_i\right),
 \qquad \deg\psi=t-r.
\]

Here $\Phi$ is the virtual functional on the pullback by the actual combined map. Fix the displayed order of insertions; reorder only with its Koszul sign. Existence and uniqueness follow from that pairing, not Poincaré duality on singular map moduli. Do not identify dual-Čech homology with singular homology without the finite-CW comparison and compatible product maps.

## G6. Properties 6–7: separating and nonseparating gluing

Prove the class-valued identities in KM 2.2.6–2.2.7 using compatible **joint** virtual reductions. Fix a homogeneous basis $\{\Delta_a\}$ of $H^*(X;\mathbb Q)$ and set
\[
 g_{ab}=\int_X\Delta_a\smile\Delta_b,\qquad (g^{ab})=(g_{ab})^{-1}.
\]
Use the ordered Casimir $\sum_{a,b}g^{ab}\Delta_a\otimes\Delta_b$ representing the diagonal, with the standard graded tensor product. For any reordering of external odd insertions, include the Koszul sign of the permutation; no additional unverified sign is inserted into the ordered inverse-pairing contraction.

For separating gluing, take $g=g_1+g_2$, $n=n_1+n_2$, disjoint ordered subsets $S_1\sqcup S_2=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $|S_i|=n_i$, and $n_i+2g_i-2\ge0$ so each factor after adding its node mark is stable. Let $\phi_S:\overline{\mathcal M}_{g_1,n_1+1}\times\overline{\mathcal M}_{g_2,n_2+1}\to\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ be the clutching map. The target formula is
\[
 \phi_S^* I_{g,n,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)
 =\sum_{A_1+A_2=A}\epsilon(S)\sum_{a,b}g^{ab}
 I_{g_1,n_1+1,A_1}((\gamma_j)_{j\in S_1},\Delta_a)
 \otimes I_{g_2,n_2+1,A_2}(\Delta_b,(\gamma_j)_{j\in S_2}),
\]
where each subset retains original relative order and $\epsilon(S)$ is precisely the Koszul sign of moving the original ordered insertions into the displayed $S_1,S_2$ order. The sum is over effective class decompositions and is finite by G0.

For nonseparating gluing, let $\xi:\overline{\mathcal M}_{g-1,n+2}\to\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,n}$ glue the last two marks, with $g-1,n+2$ stable. Then
\[
 \xi^*I_{g,n,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n)
 =\sum_{a,b}g^{ab}I_{g-1,n+2,A}(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n,\Delta_a,\Delta_b),
\]
with branches ordered as displayed and the corresponding node-branch automorphism retained in the clutching map. The inverse pairing is the same ordered diagonal contraction as above.

Before applying the joint map-node comparison, construct the domain-boundary Gysin maps on the compact weighted target. For a separating clutching map $\phi_S$ and the self-clutching map $\xi$, define $\phi_{S*}$ and $\xi_*$ as adjoints to pullback under the specified weighted perfect pairing. First define the normalized geometric boundary Thom class $\tau_D$ once for each unordered local node branch as the supported Thom class of its normal smoothing line. Its pullback under stabilization is computed by the smoothing product; it is not the clutching adjoint. Separately compute the weighted-adjoint clutching transfer $\xi_*\chi$ (and $\phi_{S*}\chi$) from ordered branch lifts, source and target stabilizers, and the quotient map; do not identify $\xi_*\chi$ with $\tau_D$ by convention. For the nonseparating clutching map, the weighted stack transfer has degree two over the normalized unordered-node Thom class, from the ordered-branch S₂ cover. For a separating clutching map, the transfer degree is two exactly in the symmetric unmarked case $n=0$ and $g_1=g_2$; otherwise it is one.
