# Roadmap: inert anticyclotomic CM main conjecture

**Summary.** This roadmap derives the global arithmetic of a CM elliptic curve at a prime p≥5 inert in its CM field from Rubin's integral signed decomposition of anticyclotomic local units, which AnticyclotomicLocalUnits proves. Starting from a Gross Q-curve E over the Hilbert class field H of an imaginary quadratic field K, with p∤h_K and coefficients in the ring of integers R of a finite extension of K_p, it transports the signed formal-point Kummer images to the anticyclotomic tower and cuts out signed, strict and relaxed Selmer groups. It then defines Rubin's integral anticyclotomic p-adic L-function as the coordinate of the primitive elliptic-unit class in a signed basis, and compares the compact and discrete Selmer modules through global duality. The end theorems are BKO21 Theorem 6.1, that the Pontryagin dual of the signed Selmer group whose sign is opposite to the root number is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module with characteristic ideal generated by Rubin's p-adic L-function, integrally over Λ_R, and BKO21 Theorem 6.2, the finite-character bound for the χ-part of the Mordell–Weil and Selmer ranks by the order of vanishing of that L-function at χ, with one extra dimension in the opposite parity, when E is defined over K.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the following arithmetic of the inert CM datum:

- the primitive elliptic-unit class at trivial auxiliary character, its special values, sign and nonvanishing, Rubin's integral p-adic L-function as a signed coordinate over Λ_R, and its interpolation formula (Layer 2);

## Conventions

Fix a prime p≥5 and an imaginary quadratic field K in which p is inert and p∤h_K; H is the Hilbert class field of K. Fix embeddings ι_∞:ℚ̄→ℂ and ι_p:ℚ̄→ℂ_p. Since (p) is principal its Artin symbol in Gal(H/K) is trivial, so p splits completely in H; let 𝔭 be the place of H above p selected by ι_p, so that H_𝔭=K_p.

A *Gross datum* consists of an algebraic Hecke character φ of K of infinity type (1,0), with conductor f prime to p, φ((α))=α for α≡1 modulo f, and φ(ā)=conjugate(φ(a)); a CM elliptic curve E over H with End_H(E)=O_K whose Hecke character is φ∘N_(H/K) (a Q-curve in the sense of Gross), with good reduction at every place above p; a Weierstrass model of E over the integers of H smooth at 𝔭 with invariant differential ω; and Ω∈ℂ× whose multiple ΩO_K is the period lattice of (E,ω) (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §0.4; ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §4.3). Put ε=W(φ)∈{+1,−1}, the root number of L(φ,s), identified with the sign ε∈{+,−}.

Let Φ=K_p, the unramified quadratic extension of ℚ_p, O its ring of integers, π=−p, and F the Lubin–Tate formal O-module for π, with normalised logarithm λ, as in AnticyclotomicLocalUnits (Conventions). Let L/Φ be a finite extension inside ℂ_p containing the values of the p-adic avatar φ_p:G_K→L×, R its ring of integers, ϖ_R a uniformiser of R and e=e(R/O) the ramification index of R over O, so that πR=ϖ_R^eR; T=R(φ_p) the free rank-one R-module on which G_K acts by φ_p, V_T=T⊗_R L and W=V_T/T=T⊗_O Φ/O. Write T′=Hom_(ℤ_p)(W,μ_(p^∞)) for the Cartier-dual lattice; under the coefficient trace it is the inverse-different twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1), and it is never identified with T without a stated comparison (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.13).

Let K∞/K be the anticyclotomic ℤ_p-extension, K_n its layer of degree pⁿ, Γ⁻=Gal(K∞/K) with topological generator γ, Γ_n=Γ⁻^(pⁿ), and K̃∞/K the ℤ_p²-extension with Γ=Gal(K̃∞/K)=Γ⁺×Γ⁻, the eigenspaces of complex conjugation. Put Λ_R=R⟦Γ⁻⟧, identified with R⟦T⟧ by γ↦1+T through the isomorphism ℤ_p≃Γ⁻ sending 1 to γ and Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Iwasawa.completedCoordinateEquiv`, and Λ_(2)=R⟦Γ⟧. Λ_R is a two-dimensional regular local ring, hence a noetherian normal unique factorisation domain, and characteristic ideals and divisors over it are Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Iwasawa.charIdeal` and `TauCeti.Iwasawa.charDivisor`. The only height-one prime of Λ_R containing p is the vertical prime (ϖ_R), and (π)=(ϖ_R)^e is prime exactly when e=1. Every characteristic-ideal identity in this roadmap is integral and retains its p-primary part, the power of (ϖ_R) dividing it. The μ-invariant of a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module is the exponent of (ϖ_R) in its characteristic ideal, so Λ_R/(ϖ_R) has μ-invariant 1 and Λ_R/(π) has μ-invariant e.

For a finite continuous character χ:Γ⁻→ℚ̄_p×, its conductor exponent is c(χ)=0 if χ=1 and c(χ)=r+1 if χ has exact order p^r, r≥1; Ξ⁺ and Ξ⁻ are the characters of even and odd conductor exponent (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §0.1). A character of exact order p^r factors through Gal(K_n/K) for every n≥r; the source's indexing by the conductor uses the deeper layer n=c(χ). Unit signs kill the opposite parity and point signs kill the same parity:

| Character at p=5 | c(χ) | Sign | Signed unit module killing χ | Signed point module killing χ |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| trivial | 0 | + | minus | plus |
| exact order 5 | 2 | + | minus | plus |
| exact order 25 | 3 | − | plus | minus |

Agboola–Howard index characters by the parity of their order and place the trivial character in their minus family (AHII05 §2, p.613, and Remark 3.2, p.614): their set of sign s is Ξ^(−s) here. Their point spaces, signed local conditions and signed Selmer groups of label s kill their character set of sign −s, which is Ξ^s here, so these objects carry the same label in both papers. Only character-set labels are translated; the label of X_(−ε) is not.

The derivatives δ and δ_χ on V∞* are those of AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§1.2–1.4, with δ_χ(r·x)=r(χ⁻¹)δ_χ(x) for r∈Λ_O and δ_1=(1+p⁻¹)δ. They extend R-linearly to V∞*⊗_O R with values in ℂ_p through ι_p. The Pontryagin dual of a discrete module carries the contragredient action (γf)(x)=f(γ⁻¹x) and (rf)(x)=f(rx) (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.10): a coefficient scalar acts on a functional through its argument, never on its value in ℚ_p/ℤ_p, which is only a ℤ_p-module. So, when R contains the values of a finite character χ, the dual of the χ-eigen-submodule of a discrete module is the χ⁻¹-specialisation of its dual, and the dual of its χ-eigenquotient is the χ⁻¹-eigen-submodule of its dual (§1.5). For f∈Λ_R and a finite character ρ with values in a finite extension L_ρ/L, ord_ρ f is the order of vanishing of f(T) at T=ρ(γ)−1 in the open unit disc of ℂ_p, equivalently the multiplicity of the height-one prime P_ρ=ker(ρ:Λ_R→L_ρ) in (f).

The finite-level rank statements and global control statements of Layers 5–6, §§5.3, 5.4, 5.6 and 6.2–6.4, make one further assumption, displayed at each target: E is defined over K, and V_T is the CM component of V_pE in the fixed embedding. Then End_K(E)=O_K forces K(j(E))=H to equal K, so h_K=1, H=K, φ is the Hecke character of E/K and R=O may be taken, so that V_pE=V_T and the rational Selmer groups Sel_a(K_n,V_pE) are those of §1.4. Character values lie in a finite extension L_χ/L and every dimension is over L_χ. The main-conjecture statements of Layers 0–4 do not assume this.

Native carriers use their library universes; fields, towers and character fields are taken inside the fixed closures, and every inverse or direct limit is over the countable cofinal system of layers. Natural-number subtraction is never evaluated below zero: the least layer of the trivial character is K_0 by definition, not c−1.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 fixes the arithmetic objects: the Gross datum, the identification of global and local towers, the conductor and sign dictionary, the local discrete module with its dual lattice, and the R-extended signed unit modules. Layer 1 transports the signed formal-point Kummer images to the global tower and cuts out the strict, signed, ordinary and relaxed Selmer groups with their compact and discrete limits. Layer 2 specialises the early CM class at trivial auxiliary character, places it in its signed line and defines Rubin's integral p-adic L-function as its coordinate. Layer 3 builds the all-auxiliary global elliptic-unit module, identifies compact local cohomology with the signed local-unit module and proves the global ranks and the strict comparison from Rubin's two-variable theorem. Layer 4 runs global duality to opposite signed torsion and the signed characteristic formula, and compares the localised global lattice with the primitive coordinate, proving the main conjecture. Layers 5–6 prove signed and strict control at finite level and the finite-character rank bounds. Each *Needs:* line lists what its statement and its indicated route use, so an implementer who proves a target another way is not blocked behind an unrelated layer; in particular the main conjecture does not depend on §4.3.

## Layer 0: The inert CM datum and the tower dictionary

All targets use the standing hypotheses of the Conventions: p≥5 inert in K, p∤h_K, a Gross datum over H and the coefficient ring R. Nothing in this layer assumes that E descends to K or that 2 splits in K.

### 0.1 The inert Gross CM datum

Define an inert CM datum as a Gross datum (φ,E,model,ω,Ω) together with the place 𝔭 of H, the coefficient ring R containing the values of φ_p, the avatar lattice T=R(φ_p) with a fixed G_H-equivariant identification of T with T_πE⊗_O R in the chosen CM embedding, W=T⊗_O Φ/O, Λ_R and ε=W(φ). It is a record of data on the supplied carriers, not a new theory of CM curves or Hecke characters. Coefficient extension along a finite local map R→R′ replaces T by T⊗_R R′, W by T⊗_R R′⊗_O Φ/O and Λ_R by Λ_(R′)=Λ_R⊗̂_R R′ (BKO21 §§3.0.1–3.0.2, pp.950–952, and §6, pp.960–961).

A route is as follows. Take the character, curve, model, differential and period from NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §0.4 and ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §§4.1, 4.3, recording them rather than inferring a descent of E to K. Form R(φ_p) on the supplied rank-one Tate component of §4.1 and compare its quotient with T⊗_O Φ/O by flatness of R over O.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- An inert CM datum is assembled from a supplied Gross realization together with the explicit smooth model, differential and period comparison; no splitting hypothesis on p or on 2 is an argument.
- Restricted to G_H, T is T_πE⊗_O R in the chosen CM embedding.
- Coefficient extension along R→R′ preserves the CM representation; the identity extension is the identity and the extension along a composite R→R′→R″ is the composite of the two extensions.
- Replacing ω by aω with a∈H× replaces Ω by aΩ; an integral comparison treats a as a unit only when both differentials are bases of the same smooth integral model at 𝔭.

**Checks.**

- For K=ℚ(√−23), the reduced forms of discriminant −23 are (1,1,6),(2,1,3),(2,−1,3), so h_K=3, and −23≡2 modulo 5 is not a square modulo 5, so p=5 is inert and 5∤3. A datum over this K, with E defined over H and not over K, is admitted; here H is a cubic extension of K, so [H:ℚ]=6, not a cubic field; an extra class-number-one condition would wrongly reject it.
- An ordinary-reduction hypothesis (p split in K) cannot be an argument: for p inert the CM curve has supersingular reduction at 𝔭 and Frobenius φ((p))=−p (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §3.4). The residue field at 𝔭 has p² elements, and the characteristic polynomial of this Frobenius on the Tate module is (X−φ((p)))(X−conjugate(φ((p))))=(X+p)², of trace −2p, which is −10 at p=5. The trace 0 is that of the Frobenius at p of a model over ℚ (a_p=0), a different Frobenius over the field with p elements.
- After R→R′ the discrete module is T⊗_R R′⊗_O Φ/O, of R′-corank one. The quotient (T⊗_R L′)/T of the larger vector space by the old lattice is not torsion when L′≠L, since it surjects onto the nonzero L-vector space (T⊗_R L′)/(T⊗_R L), so it cannot be the discrete module.

### 0.2 Global and local anticyclotomic towers

The completion of K_n at the unique place above 𝔭 is identified with Ψ_n over Φ, compatibly with the inclusions K_n⊂K_(n+1) and Ψ_n⊂Ψ_(n+1), with the Galois actions, with norms and traces, and hence with restriction and corestriction of cohomology. ### 0.3 Conductor and sign dictionary

A character χ of Γ⁻ of exact order p^r, r≥1, factors exactly through Gal(K_r/K) and has conductor exponent c(χ)=r+1; the trivial character has c=0 and factors through Gal(K_0/K). For every n≥r the character factors through Gal(K_n/K). Agboola–Howard's sign-s set is Ξ^(−s), and their signed point spaces, signed local conditions and signed Selmer groups of label s coincide with the objects of label s defined here (AHII05 §2, p.613, and Remark 3.2, p.614; BKO21 §2, p.948, and §6.0.2, p.962).

A route is as follows. Use the conductor filtration of AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §0.1 transported by §0.2. Agboola–Howard's minus set consists of the trivial character and the characters of exact order p^k with k odd, that is c=k+1 even or c=0; their plus set consists of exact orders p^k with k≥2 even, that is c odd. Their point space of label s kills their set of sign −s, which is Ξ^s, as does A^s. Inversion preserves the order and the conductor.

**Checks.**

- The trivial character, a character of exact order p and one of exact order p² have conductor exponents 0, 2 and 3 and signs +, + and −. Using the parity of the order instead gives +, −, + for the order exponents 0, 1, 2 and the wrong sign at order p.
- The trivial character lies in Agboola–Howard's minus set and in Ξ⁺ here; their nontrivial root-number formula (3.1) is not applied at r=0.
- A character of exact order p² factors through Gal(K_2/K) and through Gal(K_3/K), the source's indexing by the conductor; it does not factor through Gal(K_1/K). Requiring the degree-p^c layer to be the least field of definition would be false.

*Needs:* §0.2; AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§0.1, 3.2.

### 0.4 The local discrete CM module and its dual lattice

The chosen CM comparison gives an R[G_Φ]-isomorphism W|_(G_Φ)≃F[π^∞]⊗_O R, natural under finite coefficient extension. The Cartier-dual lattice T′=Hom_(ℤ_p)(W,μ_(p^∞)) is identified, through the coefficient trace Tr_(L/ℚ_p), with the twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1) by the inverse different of R over ℤ_p; changing the trivialisation of the inverse different changes this identification by a unit of R. The local Tate pairing H¹(Ψ_n,T′)×H¹(Ψ_n,W)→ℚ_p/ℤ_p is perfect (BKO21 §6.0.2, pp.961–962; AHII05 §2, p.613, and Proposition 3.4 with (3.2), p.615).

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- W restricted to G_Φ is F[π^∞]⊗_O R, compatibly with coefficient extension.
- T′ is the Cartier dual of W; under the trace it is the inverse-different twist of Hom_R(T,R)(1).
- A change of trace-dual basis changes the pairing maps by a unit of R.

**Checks.**

- For R=O unramified over ℤ_p the inverse different is O itself and T′≃Hom_O(T,O)(1). For R=O[√π], ramified of index 2 over O, the different over ℤ_p is generated by 2√π, the inverse different is (√π)⁻¹R, and (√π)⁻¹R/R≃R/√πR has p² elements; identifying T′ with Hom_R(T,R)(1) without the twist is off by this module, the obstruction recorded in SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.13.
- The coefficient trace Tr_(O/ℤ_p)(1)=2, so the pairing of the unit vectors is 2/p^k and not 1/p^k modulo ℤ_p at level k; an identity trace gives the wrong normalisation.
- Replacing a trace-dual basis element b by ub, u∈R×, multiplies the pairing map by u; replacing it by πb multiplies it by a nonunit, which changes the dual lattice and is excluded.

### 0.5 Signed decomposition over the CM coefficient ring

Put V_R=V∞*⊗_O R and V_R^s=V∞*,s⊗_O R. Then V_R=V_R⁺⊕V_R⁻ with each V_R^s free of rank one over Λ_R. Moreover, for x∈V_R, x∈V_R^s if and only if the R-linear extension of δ_χ, evaluated in ℂ_p through ι_p, vanishes at x for every χ∈Ξ^(−s). For every Λ_R-basis v_s of V_R^s and every χ∈Ξ^s, δ_χ(v_s)≠0 (BKO21 Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2, p.949, and §6.0.1, p.961).

A route is as follows. Tensor AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §8.2's integral direct sum with the finite free O-module R, identifying Λ_O⊗_O R with Λ_R (PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §1.7). For the converse inclusion write x=a_+v_++a_−v_−. At χ∈Ξ^(−s) the summand of sign −s is not killed, and δ_χ(a_(−s)v_(−s))=a_(−s)(χ⁻¹)δ_χ(v_(−s)) with δ_χ(v_(−s))≠0 by AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §8.4 after base change. Vanishing at every χ∈Ξ^(−s) makes a_(−s)(χ⁻¹) vanish at infinitely many points of the open unit disc, so a_(−s)=0, since a nonzero element of R⟦T⟧ has only finitely many zeros there (`PowerSeries.exists_isWeierstrassFactorization` after removing the power of ϖ_R, as in the Mathlib contract). The nonvanishing for a basis over Λ_R follows from that over Λ_O because two bases differ by a unit of Λ_R, whose specialisation at χ⁻¹ is a unit.

**Checks.**

- A nonzero coefficient divisible by p is detected: a_s=p has a_s(χ⁻¹)=p≠0 at every χ. A criterion by reduction modulo p would declare pv_s to lie in the opposite module.
- The series a=T has a single zero, at T=0, which is the trivial character; vanishing of the specialisations at the trivial character alone does not force a=0, while vanishing at the infinitely many characters of Ξ^(−s) does.
- On a Λ_R-basis v_+ of V_R⁺ at the trivial character, δ(v_+) is a unit of R. For a basis extended from a Λ_O-basis of V∞*,+ it is a unit of O by AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §8.4. Two Λ_R-bases differ by u∈Λ_R×, and δ(uv_+)=u(1)δ(v_+) with u(1)∈R×. So the value need not lie in O: for R=O[√π] and the unit u=1+√π, δ(uv_+)=(1+√π)δ(v_+). ## Layer 1: Signed Kummer images and global Selmer kernels

Use the tower identification of §0.2 and the local module of §0.4. Every local condition below is the transport of a supplied Kummer image or its exact annihilator; no new parity condition is introduced.

### 1.1 Transported signed Kummer conditions

For n≥0 and s∈{+,−}, define L_n^s⊂H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W) as the image of A_n^s⊗_O Φ/O⊗_O R under AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §8.5's Kummer map, transported through (K_n)_𝔭=Ψ_n and W≃F[π^∞]⊗_O R. Define L_∞^s as the direct limit of the L_n^s under restriction. Define the compact condition L_n^(s,⊥)⊂H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T′) as the exact annihilator of L_n^s under the local Tate pairing of §0.4, and L_∞^(s,⊥) as the inverse limit under corestriction (BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962; AHII05 §2, p.613).

A route is as follows. The Kummer map on A_n^s⊗Φ/O is injective because A_n^s is saturated in F(Ψ_n) (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §8.5); tensoring with the flat O-module R preserves injectivity. Restriction and corestriction correspond to inclusion and formal trace of points by the same section.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- A class lies in L_n^s exactly when it is the Kummer image of an element of A_n^s⊗_O Φ/O⊗_O R.
- For n≤m, restriction carries L_n^s into L_m^s, and the composite of two restrictions is the restriction along the composite inclusion.
- L_n^(s,⊥) is the full annihilator of L_n^s under the local pairing of §0.4, not an arbitrary complement of the same corank.
- Finite free coefficient extension R→R′ carries L_n^s to the corresponding condition over R′ and, through the trace-dual comparison of §0.4, carries the annihilators to the annihilators.

**Checks.**

- At n=0, A_0⁺=0 and A_0⁻=F(Φ) (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §3.2), so L_0⁺=0 and L_0⁻ is the full ordinary Kummer image of F(Φ)⊗Φ/O⊗R, of R-corank one; reversing the point signs reverses these values.
- A Kummer class is attached to a tensor y⊗a with a∈Φ/O, not to a point alone. Take R=O and n=0, and let Q generate F(Φ), which is free of rank one over O. Then Q⊗π⁻¹ is nonzero in F(Φ)⊗Φ/O, so its Kummer image is nonzero and lies outside L_0⁺=0. By contrast [π]Q⊗π⁻¹=Q⊗1=0 although λ([π]Q)=πλ(Q)≠0, so a nonzero logarithm does not by itself give a nonzero class. In general F(Ψ_n)/A_n^s is a free O-module because A_n^s is saturated, and the class of y⊗π^(−j), j≥1, lies in L_n^s exactly when the image of y in F(Ψ_n)/A_n^s is divisible by π^j. A point with λ_χ(y)≠0 for some χ∈Ξ^s has nonzero image there, so y⊗π^(−j) leaves L_n^s once j exceeds the π-adic valuation of that image. A point with λ_χ(y)=0 for every χ∈Ξ^s lies in A_n^s, and every y⊗a then lies in L_n^s.
- For a point y∈A_n^s and n≤m, restriction of its Kummer class is the class of y viewed in F(Ψ_m), whereas corestriction from m to n corresponds to the formal trace, which multiplies λ_1 of a base point by p^(m−n); interchanging the two maps sends a base point of logarithm a at level 1 to pa rather than a.

### 1.2 Independence of the local CM comparison

L_n^s and L_∞^s do not depend on the choice of R[G_Φ]-isomorphism W≃F[π^∞]⊗_O R compatible with the fixed CM embedding. They are unchanged when ι_p is replaced by ι_p∘g with g∈G_K, which keeps the embedding of K and the unique place of K_n above p, conjugates its decomposition group by g and changes the identification (K_n)_𝔭=Ψ_n of §0.2 by the image of g in Gal(K_n/K)≃Gal(Ψ_n/Φ), when the localisation map is conjugated together with its target condition. They are compatible with finite coefficient extension (BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962).

A route is as follows. Two such isomorphisms differ by an R[G_Φ]-automorphism of F[π^∞]⊗_O R. The Lubin–Tate character has image O× in End(F[π^∞]), so the centraliser of G_Φ in End_R(F[π^∞]⊗_O R) is R acting by scalars and the automorphism is multiplication by a unit u∈R×. The Kummer image of A_n^s⊗Φ/O⊗R is an R-submodule, hence stable under u. For a change of ι_p by g∈G_K, the Kummer map is equivariant for Gal(Ψ_n/Φ) and A_n^s is stable under it, since λ_χ(hy)=χ(h)λ_χ(y) (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§3.1–3.2); so L_n^s is stable under Gal(Ψ_n/Φ). An embedding that changes the embedding of K changes the CM embedding and is not covered (last Check).

**Checks.**

- Multiplication by a unit u∈R× fixes L_n^s as a submodule, not merely up to finite index.
- At n=2 and p=5, replacing ι_p by ι_p∘g with g mapping to a generator γ of Gal(K_2/K) changes the identification by a generator of Gal(Ψ_2/Φ), cyclic of order 25. A_2^− is cut out by λ_χ=0 for the twenty characters of order 25 and is stable under γ. A single O-line O·y with λ_χ(y)≠0 for a character χ of order 5 is not: γy=uy with u∈O would give u=χ(γ), a primitive fifth root of unity, which O=ℤ_25 does not contain. So the independence uses the Gal(Ψ_n/Φ)-stability of A_n^s.
- Multiplication by π is not an automorphism of F[π^∞]⊗R: it has kernel F[π]⊗R of order p² when R=O, so it is not a comparison and the statement does not cover it.
- The conjugate CM embedding is not covered, and it is not a semilinear self-map of R. It replaces the O-algebra structure of R by its composite with the conjugation c of Φ, so it compares W with F[π^∞]⊗_(O,c)R (R viewed as an O-algebra through c), on which G_Φ acts by c composed with the Lubin–Tate character. Equivalently, an extension c̃ of c to ℚ̄_p carries R to the conjugate ring c̃(R), which need not be R. At p=5 let a∈O be the Teichmüller lift of a generator of 𝔽₂₅×, so c(a)=a⁵, and let L=Φ(β) with β³=aπ, totally ramified of degree 3 over Φ because X³−aπ is Eisenstein. This L is an admitted coefficient field, for instance for K=ℚ(√−2), where 5 is inert, h_K=1 and φ_p takes values in O. An automorphism of L extending c would send β to a cube root of a⁵π, and the quotient would be a cube root of a⁴ in L. The cubes in μ_24 form μ_8, which does not contain a⁴, so such a cube root has order 18. But the roots of unity of order prime to 5 in L lie in μ_24, because L has residue field 𝔽₂₅. So c does not extend to L.

### 1.3 Rational local vanishing away from p

For every n≥0 and every finite place v of K_n not above p, H¹((K_n)_v,V_T)=0. Consequently the finite condition H¹_f((K_n)_v,W), the image of H¹_f((K_n)_v,V_T)=0, is zero. The group H¹((K_n)_v,W) itself is finite and need not vanish (AHI06 Lemma 1.1.4 and the sentence after it, p.1006; BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962).

A route is as follows. E has integral j-invariant and so potentially good reduction at v; pass to a finite extension of (K_n)_v over which it has good reduction, where restriction is injective on rational cohomology because corestriction after restriction is multiplication by the degree (ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.2.3). There inertia acts trivially on V_T and the arithmetic Frobenius acts by an eigenvalue of complex absolute value √q (ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §4.1, Frobenius and infinity-type identities), so neither V_T nor its Tate dual V_T^∨(1) has invariants. Local duality gives H²=0 and the local Euler characteristic formula for residue characteristic different from p gives H¹=0.

**Checks.**

- At a good place v with q=Nv, the invariants of V_T are zero because the Frobenius eigenvalue α has |α|=√q≠1, and those of V_T^∨(1) are zero because q/α has absolute value √q≠1; at q=4, for instance, √q=2.
- The integral group need not vanish. At a good place v, W is unramified and divisible, so H¹ of the unramified quotient vanishes and H¹((K_n)_v,W) is the group of Frobenius invariants of W(−1); it is nonzero exactly when α≡q modulo the maximal ideal of R, where α is the Frobenius eigenvalue on T and q=Nv. The statement asserts vanishing only rationally.
- At a bad place where E acquires good reduction over an extension of degree d_v, the argument uses injectivity of restriction on V_T-coefficients, valid because d_v is invertible in ℚ_p; on W-coefficients restriction can have kernel of exponent dividing d_v, so no integral vanishing is claimed.

*Needs:* §0.1; ComplexMultiplicationAndExplicitReciprocity §4.1; TauCetiRoadmap.EllipticCurves Layers 3, 4; ArithmeticGaloisDuality §§R02.2.3, R02.4.2, R02.4.4.

### 1.4 The four global Selmer conditions

Each group below is the Selmer group of an instance of Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.ContCohomology.SelmerStructure` on the discrete G_(K_n)-module W. It is indexed by the finite places v of K_n and localises along the decomposition groups G_((K_n)_v)→G_(K_n) given by the fixed embeddings, with the identity of W as coefficient map. It carries the R-module structure of the discrete Selmer data of SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.1. For s∈{+,−} define

    Sel_s(K_n,W) = ker[ H¹(K_n,W) → H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W)/L_n^s × ∏_(v∤p) H¹((K_n)_v,W) ].

The ordinary Selmer group Sel(K_n,W) uses the full ordinary Kummer image of the formal points at 𝔭, the strict Selmer group Sel_str(K_n,W) uses 0 at 𝔭, and the relaxed Selmer group Sel_rel(K_n,W) uses all of H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W); all four use 0 at every finite v∤p, and the complex place contributes nothing. The same kernels are computed in H¹(G_(K_n,S),W) for any finite S containing the places above pf and ∞. Put Sel_a(K∞,W)=lim_res Sel_a(K_n,W) for a∈{str,+,−,ord,rel} (BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962; AHII05 §2, pp.612–613).

For a∈{str,+,−,ord} and n≥0 let Sel_a(K_n,V_T), with Sel_ord(K_n,V_T) written Sel(K_n,V_T), be the Selmer group of the instance of `TauCeti.ContCohomology.SelmerStructure` on the rational module V_T, with the rational Selmer data of SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.1, whose condition at 𝔭 is 0, the image of A_n^s⊗_O L, or the image of F(Ψ_n)⊗_O L under the rational Kummer map F(Ψ_n)⊗_O L→H¹((K_n)_𝔭,V_T), and whose condition at every finite v∤p is H¹((K_n)_v,V_T), which is 0 by §1.3. The rational Kummer map is the inverse limit of the Kummer maps of §1.1 on the F(Ψ_n)/π^kF(Ψ_n), tensored with L and transported by §0.4. The conditions of Sel_a(K_n,W) are the images of these, that is, they are propagated from V_T (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§2.5–2.6; BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962). When E is defined over K and R=O (Conventions), V_pE=V_T and Sel_a(K_n,V_pE) denotes Sel_a(K_n,V_T); for a finite character χ of Gal(K_n/K), Sel_a(K_n,V_pE)^χ is the χ-eigenspace of Sel_a(K_n,V_pE)⊗_L L_χ.

A route is as follows. Take the local condition L_n^s, the ordinary Kummer image, 0 or H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W) at 𝔭 and 0 at every finite v∤p in `TauCeti.ContCohomology.SelmerStructure`, whose `TauCeti.ContCohomology.SelmerStructure.selmerGroup` is the kernel displayed above by `TauCeti.ContCohomology.SelmerStructure.mem_selmerGroup_iff`. The localisation maps are R-linear and every local condition is an R-submodule, so the four groups are R-submodules, and they are Selmer data in the sense of SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.1. For the restricted-ramification comparison, a class unramified at v∉S has trivial localisation in H¹((K_n)_v,W) because Frobenius minus one acts surjectively on the divisible module W, whose invariants are finite by §1.3 (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.2). Restriction preserves every condition by §1.1.

For the rational groups, the rational and finite Kummer maps are compatible with V_T→W, so the image of A_n^s⊗_O L is the Kummer image of A_n^s⊗_O L/R=A_n^s⊗_O Φ/O⊗_O R, which is L_n^s, and likewise for the ordinary image; away from p the image of 0 is 0. Hence the image of Sel_a(K_n,V_T) lies in Sel_a(K_n,W), and it is divisible. Conversely, the cokernel of H¹(G_(K_n,S),V_T)→H¹(G_(K_n,S),W) injects into the torsion of the finitely generated R-module H²(G_(K_n,S),T), so it is finite (ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.4.4), and every divisible class of Sel_a(K_n,W) lifts to H¹(G_(K_n,S),V_T). The lift is unique up to the image of H¹(G_(K_n,S),T), and its failure to satisfy the condition at 𝔭 lies in the finitely generated R-module H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T)/L_T, L_T the saturated preimage of the condition on V_T (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.6), modulo the image of H¹(G_(K_n,S),T), a finitely generated R-module, which has no nonzero divisible subgroup. That failure is additive in the class, so it vanishes on the divisible subgroup, and the lift can be chosen in Sel_a(K_n,V_T). Since Sel_a(K_n,W) is cofinitely generated (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.18), its corank is that of its divisible subgroup.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- A class lies in Sel_s(K_n,W) exactly when its localisation at 𝔭 lies in L_n^s and its localisation at every finite v∤p is zero.
- Sel_str(K_n,W)⊂Sel_s(K_n,W)⊂Sel(K_n,W)⊂Sel_rel(K_n,W) for both signs.
- For n≤m restriction carries each of the four groups at level n into the group of the same type at level m, with the identity and composition laws.
- Enlarging S beyond the places above pf and ∞ does not change any of the four groups.
- For a∈{str,+,−,ord}, the image of Sel_a(K_n,V_T) in H¹(K_n,W) is the maximal divisible subgroup of Sel_a(K_n,W). Hence corank_R Sel_a(K_n,W)=dim_L Sel_a(K_n,V_T), and Sel_a(K_n,W)^∨⊗_R L, with the contragredient action and the coefficient trace of §0.4, is Gal(K_n/K)-equivariantly the L-linear dual of Sel_a(K_n,V_T).

**Checks.**

- A global class whose localisation at 𝔭 lies in L_n^s but whose localisation at some v∤p is nonzero lies in none of the four groups: it fails the away-from-p condition even for the relaxed group.
- A class with nonzero localisation in L_n^s lies in Sel_s and in Sel_rel and not in Sel_str; a class with zero localisation at 𝔭 and at every v∤p lies in all four.
- Adding an auxiliary prime q∤pf to S, with the condition 0 retained at q, leaves each group unchanged; relaxing the condition at q to all of H¹((K_n)_q,W) defines a different, possibly larger, kernel and is not this definition.
- At n=0, A_0⁺=0 and A_0⁻=F(Φ) give Sel_+(K,V_T)=Sel_str(K,V_T) and Sel_−(K,V_T)=Sel(K,V_T), matching the integral groups of the Examples of this layer. Away from p the rational condition is the whole group H¹((K_n)_v,V_T)=0, and its image in H¹((K_n)_v,W) is 0. At a good place v with α≡q modulo the maximal ideal of R, H¹((K_n)_v,W) is nonzero (§1.3), so the relaxed integral condition there is not the propagated one and would give a different, possibly larger, Sel_a(K_n,W).

### 1.5 Infinite Selmer duals and compact Selmer modules

For a∈{str,+,−,ord,rel} put X_a=Hom_(ℤ_p,cts)(Sel_a(K∞,W),ℚ_p/ℤ_p), compact, with (γf)(x)=f(γ⁻¹x) and (rf)(x)=f(rx) for r∈R, so that X_a is a Λ_R-module. For s∈{+,−} let S_s=lim_cor Sel_s(K_n,T′), where Sel_s(K_n,T′) uses L_n^(s,⊥) at 𝔭 and the full group H¹((K_n)_v,T′) at every finite v∤p, the annihilator of the discrete condition 0. Let S_str and S_rel use 0 and all of H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T′) at 𝔭 respectively, with the same full condition away from p; thus compact strict is dual to discrete relaxed and compact relaxed to discrete strict. Let ℋ=lim_cor H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T′) and ℋ^s=lim_cor L_n^(s,⊥) (AHII05 §2, pp.612–613, for the Selmer groups and their limits; §3, p.614, before Proposition 3.1, for ℋ and ℋ^s; §3, p.615, before Proposition 3.3, for X_a). Finite generation over Λ_R is not part of these definitions (§3.4).

A route is as follows. Use the direct and inverse limit interfaces of SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§2.10 and 3.1, keeping every finite local condition and the trace-dual comparison of §0.4 in the limit. The localisation maps induced by the pairings are Λ_R-linear with the involution on the dual side.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- X_a is the continuous ℤ_p-dual of Sel_a(K∞,W) with the displayed actions, which satisfy (γf)(x)=f(γ⁻¹x) and (rf)(x)=f(rx).
- Duality identifies X_a/(γ^(pⁿ)−1)X_a with the dual of Sel_a(K∞,W)^(Γ_n), without asserting a comparison with Sel_a(K_n,W). When R contains the values of a finite character χ, it identifies X_a/(γ−χ(γ)⁻¹)X_a with the dual of the χ-eigen-submodule of Sel_a(K∞,W).
- S_a is the corestriction limit of the compact Selmer groups over T′, and ℋ and ℋ^s are the corresponding local limits.
- Under finite free coefficient extension the trace and inverse-different comparison of §0.4 transports X_a and S_a; the ℤ_p-dual is not identified with Hom_R(−,L/R) without that comparison.

**Checks.**

- A coefficient scalar moves onto the functional, not onto its value. When R contains the values of χ and γx=χ(γ)x, (γf)(x)=f(γ⁻¹x)=f(χ(γ)⁻¹x)=(χ(γ)⁻¹·f)(x). The dual of multiplication by γ−χ(γ) is −χ(γ)γ⁻¹(γ−χ(γ)⁻¹) on X_a, which gives the χ⁻¹-specialisation of the API. Model at p=5: L=Φ(ζ₅), of degree 8 over ℚ₅, R its integers, M=L/R with γ acting by ζ₅, and f=Tr_(L/ℚ₅) modulo ℤ₅, well defined because the trace is integral on R. For x the class of (ζ₅−ζ₅²)/25, f(x)=(−2−(−2))/25=0, but f(γ⁻¹x)=Tr((1−ζ₅)/25)=(8+2)/25=2/5≠0 in ℚ₅/ℤ₅. A scalar on the value would give χ(γ)⁻¹f(x)=0, whereas (ζ₅⁻¹·f)(x)=2/5. Under the trace M^∨ is the inverse different of R, γ acts on it by ζ₅⁻¹, and its χ⁻¹-specialisation is all of M^∨. With (γf)(x)=f(γx), γ would act by ζ₅ and that specialisation would be M^∨/(ζ₅−ζ₅⁻¹)M^∨≃R/(ζ₅−1)R=𝔽₂₅.
- A finite control kernel is retained in X_a integrally and disappears after tensoring with L; X_a is not defined modulo finite modules.
- The limits run in opposite directions on the two sides. For R=O, W≃Φ/O is the increasing union of W[π^k]≃D_k=π^(−k)O/O along inclusions. The trace pairing (y,x)↦Tr_(Φ/ℚ_p)(yx) modulo ℤ_p is perfect because O is unramified over ℤ_p (§0.4), and it identifies D_k^∨ with O/π^kO, which has 25 and 625 elements for k=1,2 at p=5. The dual of D_1⊂D_2 is reduction O/π²O→O/πO, sending the class of 1 to the class of 1 and the class of π to 0. So the dual of the direct limit is the inverse limit of the duals, W^∨≃lim O/π^kO=O, compact and torsion-free. The direct limit of the same finite groups along the injections O/π^kO→O/π^(k+1)O given by multiplication by π is Φ/O, which is π-power torsion. Accordingly X_a is the inverse limit of the duals of the Sel_a(K_n,W) along the duals of restriction, and S_a is the inverse limit along corestriction.

## Layer 2: The primitive class and Rubin's integral p-adic L-function

Use the R-extended signed decomposition of §0.5 and the supplied projected CM class. All coordinates are integral, in Λ_R.

### 2.1 The primitive elliptic-unit class

Define ξ=ξ(E,Ω)∈V_R as the image in V_R=V∞*⊗_O R of the norm-compatible family of normalised elliptic-unit classes of moduli f p^(n+1), n≥0, with no auxiliary prime (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.7), under the composite of the arrows of AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §4.2 with its auxiliary ring class field L_r replaced by K, so that no character evaluation occurs: the CM coefficient untwist, corestriction to H(E[p^(n+1)]), localisation at the primes above p, augmentation of R[Gal(H/K)], projection to Δ-invariants and the Kummer comparison. The composite lands in U∞*⊗_O R; write ξ̃ for its value there, so that ξ is the image of ξ̃ under the quotient map U∞*⊗_O R→V_R, whose kernel is (σ−1)(U∞*⊗_O R) for a topological generator σ of Γ⁺ (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §0.5). This is the trivial-auxiliary-character member of the family of projected classes, which AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §4.2 states for nontrivial ν only; it is defined here for every inert CM datum of the Conventions. Retain the norm-compatible global class whose localisation it is (BKO21 §3.0.2, pp.952–953, the composite defining ξ_ν read with ν trivial, Proposition 3.3, p.953, and §6.0.1, p.961).

A route is as follows. Each modulus f p^(n+1) is admissible for NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.7, whose moduli are supported on f, p and at most one auxiliary split prime. That section makes the classes integral on every component other than that of the residual character of T_φ^∨(1), and the component used here, the augmentation over Gal(H/K) followed by the projection to Δ-invariants, is one of them; so ξ lies in V_R and not only in V_R⊗L. ### 2.2 Special values of the primitive class

The primitive class satisfies δ(ξ)=L(φ̄,1)/Ω and δ_χ(ξ)=L(φ̄χ,1)/Ω for every nontrivial finite character χ of Γ⁻, with the fixed embeddings ι_∞ and ι_p identifying both sides as algebraic numbers. Since χ is ramified at 𝔭 and unramified at the primes dividing f, no Euler factor is omitted on the right; at the trivial character δ_1(ξ)=(1+p⁻¹)L(φ̄,1)/Ω is the value with the Euler factor at p deleted (BKO21 Proposition 3.3, p.953, and §6.0.1, p.961).

A route is as follows. Follow AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §4.4, which states these values for the projected classes at nontrivial ν, as the template. NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.8 at the ray level K(f p^(n+1)), with g=f, gives the χ-weighted sum of the localised dual exponentials of the classes of §2.1 as Ω⁻¹L_(pf)(φ̄χ,1)·ω, and Wiles reciprocity in dual-exponential form for an arbitrary element of U∞*⊗_O R (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §4.3 in the form requested as gap:wiles-reciprocity-every-local-unit, since that section states it only for its own projected class), applied to the element ξ̃ of U∞*⊗_O R of which ξ is the image (§2.1), through the CM comparison of §0.4, which matches ω with the invariant differential of F, identifies the χ-weighted semilocal sum with δ_χ(ξ̃)·ω. For χ of Γ⁻, δ_χ kills (σ−1)U∞* and so descends to V∞* (AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§0.5, 1.4), hence δ_χ(ξ̃)=δ_χ(ξ), and likewise for δ. The deleted primes are those dividing f and p; the local factor of φ̄χ at each prime dividing f is 1 because φ̄χ is ramified there, and so is the factor at p for nontrivial χ. At χ=1 use δ_1=(1+p⁻¹)δ from AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §1.4, with φ̄((p))=−p, so that L_(pf)(φ̄,1)=(1+p⁻¹)L(φ̄,1).

**Checks.**

- At χ=1 the deleted Euler factor is 1−φ̄((p))/N(p)=1−(−p)/p²=1+p⁻¹, which is 6/5 at p=5. Since 1+p⁻¹=(p+1)/p is nonzero, δ and δ_1 determine each other on ξ; the factor is never used to cancel a nonunit in an integral identity.
- The character appearing is φ̄χ, not the complex conjugate of φχ; the two agree only after replacing χ by χ⁻¹, as in §2.5.
- For χ of conductor exponent 2 the local L-factor of φ̄χ at p is 1, so a formula retaining the factor 1+p⁻¹ at nontrivial χ is wrong by 6/5 at p=5.

*Needs:* §2.1; §0.4; AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§0.5, 1.4, 4.3 (gap:wiles-reciprocity-every-local-unit), 4.4; NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.8.

### 2.3 Sign and nonvanishing of the primitive class

ξ∈V_R^ε and ξ≠0, so Λ_Rξ is free of rank one. More precisely δ_χ(ξ)=0 for every χ∈Ξ^(−ε), and δ_χ(ξ)≠0 for all but finitely many χ∈Ξ^ε. ξ need not be a basis of V_R^ε (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961; AHII05 Proposition 3.1 and Remark 3.2, p.614).

A route is as follows. For nontrivial χ of conductor exponent c, W(φχ)=(−1)^c ε (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §1.4); for χ∈Ξ^(−ε) this is −1 and L(φ̄χ,1)=0 (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §1.5). At χ=1, which lies in Ξ⁺, the vanishing is required only when ε=−, where L(φ̄,1)=0 by the functional equation. Hence ξ∈V_R^ε by §0.5. For χ∈Ξ^ε the root number is +1 and NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §6.4 gives L(φ̄χ,1)≠0 for all but finitely many χ, so ξ≠0 by §2.2; Λ_R is a domain and V_R^ε is free of rank one.

**Checks.**

- For ε=− the trivial derivative δ(ξ)=L(φ̄,1)/Ω vanishes by the sign of the functional equation; the nontrivial formula (−1)^c ε would give (−1)^0·(−1)=−1 as well, but it is not used at c=0.
- For ε=+ and χ of exact order p², c=3 and W(φχ)=(−1)³·1=−1, so δ_χ(ξ)=0; for χ of exact order p, c=2 and W(φχ)=+1.
- ξ=πv_ε is nonzero and generates a free submodule of index (π) in V_R^ε; nonvanishing does not make ξ a basis.

*Needs:* §2.2; §0.5; NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §§1.4, 1.5, 6.4.

### 2.4 Rubin's integral anticyclotomic p-adic L-function

Choose a Λ_R-basis v_ε of V_R^ε. Define C_∞=Λ_Rξ⊂V_R^ε and Rubin's p-adic L-function L_p=L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)∈Λ_R by the unique equality ξ=L_p·v_ε. Then C_∞ is free of rank one and V_R^ε/C_∞≃Λ_R/(L_p). For u∈Λ_R× the basis uv_ε gives the coordinate u⁻¹L_p; the principal ideal (L_p) does not depend on the basis (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961).

A route is as follows. Take the coordinate of ξ in the basis v_ε (`Module.Basis`). The quotient isomorphism is the basis isomorphism V_R^ε≃Λ_R followed by the quotient by L_p. Unit changes of basis and of differential preserve (L_p) by `Ideal.span_singleton_mul_left_unit`. Integrality comes from ξ∈V_R and v_ε a basis of V_R^ε, not from interpolation.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- L_p is the coordinate of ξ in the basis v_ε, and ξ=L_p·v_ε is the unique expression of ξ in that basis.
- For u∈Λ_R×, the coordinate of ξ in the basis uv_ε is u⁻¹L_p, and both generate the same principal ideal.
- The basis induces an isomorphism V_R^ε/C_∞≃Λ_R/(L_p) of Λ_R-modules.
- Under coefficient extension R→R′ with compatible bases, L_p for R maps to L_p for R′, with identity and composition laws.

**Checks.**

- The coordinate of v_ε is 1 and that of πv_ε is π, so the definition detects a nonunit lattice index; the coordinate of 0 is 0, which §2.3 excludes for ξ.
- For ξ=a·v and the basis v′=u·v, the coordinate is u⁻¹a; with a=T and u=1+T this is T/(1+T), which generates (T).
- If a∈R⟦T⟧ does not have coefficients in O, the coordinate lies in Λ_R and is not replaced by an element of Λ_O; for R/O of ramification index e, the coordinate ϖ_R of ϖ_R·v generates (ϖ_R), with (ϖ_R)^e=(π), so for e≥2 the coordinate ideal is strictly larger than (π).

*Needs:* §2.3; §0.5; `Module.Basis`; `Ideal.span_singleton_mul_left_unit`.

### 2.5 Interpolation

For every nontrivial χ∈Ξ^ε, δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)≠0 and

    L_p(χ) = L(conjugate(φχ),1) / (Ω·δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)),

where L_p(χ) is the specialisation of L_p at γ↦χ(γ). Hence L_p(χ)≠0 exactly when L(conjugate(φχ),1)≠0. At χ=1 and ε=+, δ(ξ)=L_p(1)δ(v_+) with δ(v_+) a unit of R. At χ=1 and ε=−, δ(ξ)=δ(v_−)=0 and this relation does not compute L_p(1) (BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961, and Corollary 2.2, p.949).

A route is as follows. Apply δ_(χ⁻¹) to ξ=L_p·v_ε, using δ_(χ⁻¹)(a·v)=a(χ)δ_(χ⁻¹)(v) from the inverse covariance of the derivatives. Use χ⁻¹=χ̄ for finite characters to rewrite φ̄χ⁻¹ as conjugate(φχ) in §2.2. The character χ⁻¹ lies in Ξ^ε because it has the same conductor, so §0.5 gives δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε)≠0.

**Checks.**

- With a=γ, δ_(χ⁻¹)(γ·v)=χ(γ)δ_(χ⁻¹)(v)=ζδ_(χ⁻¹)(v) for χ(γ)=ζ; locating the specialisation at χ⁻¹ instead gives ζ⁻¹, which differs from ζ for χ nontrivial (ζ⁴≠ζ for ζ of order 5).
- At ε=− no quotient by δ(v_−)=0 is formed, and nothing follows about L_p(1): in the lattice model ξ=πv_− of the worked examples, L_p=π has L_p(1)=π≠0 although δ(ξ)=0.
- For χ∈Ξ^(−ε) the formula is not asserted: both δ_(χ⁻¹)(ξ) and δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε) vanish.

*Needs:* §2.4; §2.2; §0.5; AnticyclotomicLocalUnits §§1.3, 8.4.

### 3.2 Descent from the Gross datum over H

Let M be H or K₀=H(E[p]) and G=Gal(M/K). M is abelian over K, being the compositum of H with the field cut out by φ_p modulo p, and #G is h_K or h_K(p²−1), prime to p; hence M∩K∞=K and Gal(MK_n/K_n)≃G for every n≥0. G acts on the cohomology of W over MK_n through the G_K-module structure of W, whose restriction to G_M is E[π^∞]⊗_O R (§0.1). For every n≥0:

- restriction is an isomorphism of H¹(K_n,W) onto the G-invariants of H¹(MK_n,W), and it carries Sel_str(K_n,W) and Sel_rel(K_n,W) onto the G-invariants of the strict and relaxed Selmer groups of W over MK_n, defined with 0, respectively no condition, at every place above p and 0 at every other finite place;
- corestriction identifies the G-coinvariants of the compact strict and relaxed Selmer groups of T′ over MK_n, defined with 0, respectively no condition, at every place above p and the full local group at every other finite place, and of the sum of the H¹((MK_n)_w,T′) over the places w above p, with Sel_str(K_n,T′), Sel_rel(K_n,T′) and H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T′).

These identifications commute with restriction and corestriction in n, with localisation and with the local pairings of §0.4, so a rank or characteristic-ideal statement proved for the G-invariant part over MK∞ is the same statement over Λ_R. Through them the constructions of AHII05 for an elliptic curve over ℚ, namely the relaxed and strict Selmer modules, the global elliptic-unit module and the two-variable comparisons, apply to the inert CM datum; every coefficient trace uses the inverse different of §0.4, and the specialisation diagrams use Λ_R and the differential of §0.1 (BKO21 Theorem 6.1 and the paragraph before it, p.962; AHII05 §2, pp.612–613, Proposition 3.3, p.615, Theorems 4.1–4.3, pp.616–617).

### 3.3 Compact local cohomology and signed local units

There is a Λ_R-linear isomorphism ℋ≃V_R, compatible with localisation, with the Cartier-dual trace pairing of §0.4 and with the Γ-actions, the pairing pairing χ with χ⁻¹. Its inverse is the Kummer map, which sends x∈V_R to the corestriction-compatible family of the Kummer classes of x at the layers Ψ_n, transported through the comparison of §0.4; so an element of ℋ and its image in V_R have the same Kummer classes. It carries ℋ^s onto V_R^s, with the same label s, and the localisation of each c_a onto its local elliptic-unit projection y_a=c·d_a·ξ, where d_a=N(a)−φ(a)γ_a∈Λ_R is the twisted smoothing factor, γ_a the image in Γ⁻ of the Artin symbol σ_a, and c∈R× does not depend on a. Hence ℋ=ℋ⁺⊕ℋ⁻ is free of rank two over Λ_R and ℋ^s and ℋ/ℋ^s are free of rank one (AHII05 Proposition 3.4 and (3.2), p.615; BKO21 Theorem 2.1, p.949, and §§6.0.1–6.0.2, pp.961–962). A route is as follows. For the elliptic-unit classes, let y_a∈V_R be the image of loc(c_a). The standard units of auxiliary ideal a are N(a)−σ_a applied to the zeta element of NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.3, which does not depend on a. Tensoring with the basis t_0 of §3.1 turns this factor into N(a)−φ(a)σ_a, by the diagonal action of NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.9 (§§2.6–2.7 there), and turns the zeta element into the normalised class of NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.7 from which ξ̃ is formed (§2.1), up to the comparison of t_0 with the R-basis γ=per_φ(ω)/Ω of Hom_R(T,R) used there (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.4). The constant c is a unit of R independent of a and of every character: t_0 and γ correspond under the trivialisation of the inverse different of §0.4, which is a unit, and the remaining comparison is the unit just described. ### 3.4 Relaxed and strict global ranks

S_rel is a finitely generated torsion-free Λ_R-module of rank one, C has rank one, X_rel has rank one and X_str is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module. The two-variable relaxed Selmer dual over Λ_(2) has rank one, and its specialisation along γ⁺−1 maps onto X_rel with kernel killed by a power of ϖ_R, so it has rank one over Λ_R (AHII05 Proposition 3.3(i), p.615, and the proof of Theorem 4.1, p.616; AHI06 Lemma 1.1.9, pp.1009–1010).

A route is as follows. Nonzero localisation of an elliptic-unit class (§3.3) gives rank at least one for C and S_rel, and shows that the anticyclotomic class c_a of §3.1 is not Λ_R-torsion, ℋ being torsion-free. Torsion of X_str follows from weak Leopoldt (EulerSystemsAndKolyvaginSystems §8.25) for the Euler system of §3.1 on the anticyclotomic tower K∞ itself: X_str is the module X∞ there, the dual of the Selmer group of Hom(T′,μ_(p^∞))=W that is strict at p, its finite conditions away from p being 0 by §1.3. For V=T′⊗_R L the record Hyp(K∞,V) of §8.2 holds with τ=1: its two clauses do not involve the admissibility under which that section introduces it, τ=1 lies in G_(K∞) and acts trivially on μ_(p^∞), on (O_K^×)^(1/p^∞) and on K(1), V/(τ−1)V=V is one-dimensional over L, and a one-dimensional V is irreducible over G_(K∞). K∞/K is not admissible in the sense of §8.1: complex conjugation inverts Γ⁻, so a prime of K prime to p that is inert or ramified over ℚ, being fixed by conjugation, has Frobenius of order at most 2 in the torsion-free Γ⁻, hence trivial, and splits completely in K∞. Admissibility is replaced by Rubin's condition (*) of §8.11, which holds here in the stronger form that at every prime 𝔮∤p of K and every finite subextension of 𝒦_a the class restricts to zero on the inertia group at 𝔮. Modulo p^k, the class over K(fgpⁿ) is the corestriction, from a layer K(fgp^m) over which T_0/p^k is trivial, of cup products of Kummer classes of the standard units _az_(fgp^m) with elements of T_0/p^k (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.9). These are global units by NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §2.2, the modulus being divisible by p and by a prime dividing f, so their Kummer classes vanish on inertia at 𝔮; by the double-coset formula, the restriction to inertia of a corestriction is a sum of corestrictions of restrictions to inertia groups of the larger field, so corestriction preserves the vanishing. The argument does not ask whether T′ is unramified at 𝔮, so it covers the primes dividing f that are inert or ramified over ℚ, at which T′ is ramified. The rank of S_rel is then one. S_rel is the corestriction limit of the H¹(G_(K_n,S),T′), S the places above pf∞: at a finite place w∉S every class of H¹((K_n)_w,T′) is unramified, its restriction to inertia lying in the Frobenius invariants of T′(−1)=T_0, which vanish because Frobenius acts on T_0 by φ_p⁻¹ of a Frobenius, of complex absolute value different from 1. By the global Euler characteristic (second Check) this limit has rank one more than the second Iwasawa cohomology, and by Poitou–Tate (ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.4.3) the second Iwasawa cohomology contains X_str with cokernel inside the local second Iwasawa cohomology at the places of S, which is torsion: at 𝔭 it is dual to H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 (§3.3), and at v∤p it is finitely generated over R where the decomposition group of v in Γ⁻ is infinite, and Λ_R tensor a finite module where v splits completely. X_rel then has rank one. For the two-variable statement apply §8.25 on the ℤ_p²-extension K̃∞, which contains the cyclotomic ℤ_p-extension and so is admissible (§8.1), to the two-variable class of §3.1. ## Layer 4: Global duality and the integral main conjecture

### 4.1 The signed global-duality sequences

For s∈{+,−} there are two exact sequences of Λ_R-modules

    0 → S_s → S_rel → ℋ/ℋ^s → X_s → X_str → 0,
    0 → S_str → S_s → ℋ^s → X_rel → X_s → 0.

In the first, the middle map is localisation at 𝔭 followed by the quotient by ℋ^s, and the last map is dual to the inclusion Sel_str(K∞,W)⊂Sel_s(K∞,W). In the second, the map S_s→ℋ^s is localisation at 𝔭, which lands in ℋ^s because the classes of S_s satisfy the condition L_∞^(s,⊥) at 𝔭; the map ℋ^s→X_rel is dual to the localisation Sel_rel(K∞,W)→lim_res H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W)/L_∞^s, ℋ^s being the Pontryagin dual of that quotient by the local duality of §0.4; and the last map is dual to the inclusion Sel_s(K∞,W)⊂Sel_rel(K∞,W). The maps are those induced by the Cartier-dual local conditions of §1.1 and the contragredient action (AHII05 equation (3.3) and the proof of Theorem 3.6, p.616, for the first sequence; AHII05 equation (4.3) in the proof of Theorem 4.2, p.617, where it is written with S_str=0, for the second).

A route is as follows. Apply Poitou–Tate for Selmer groups (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §2.19, from ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.4.3) at each finite level to two pairs of Selmer structures on T′ that differ only at 𝔭, using the exact annihilators of §1.1. For the first sequence the pair is (signed, relaxed), whose dual conditions on W are (signed, strict), and the local term H¹((K_n)_𝔭,T′)/L_n^(s,⊥) is dual to L_n^s. For the second the pair is (strict, signed), whose dual conditions on W are (relaxed, signed), and the local term L_n^(s,⊥) is dual to H¹((K_n)_𝔭,W)/L_n^s; this is the Pontryagin dual of §2.19 for the pair (signed, relaxed) on W, the roles of W and T′ being exchanged. Pass to the corestriction and restriction limits; the finite-level groups are finite or of finite type, so the inverse limits are exact on compact modules, and H⁰ terms vanish because H⁰(K∞,W)⊆H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 (§3.3). Do not assume localisation surjective.

### 4.2 Opposite signed torsion

S_ε has rank one, S_(−ε)=S_str=0, X_ε has rank one and X_(−ε) is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module. Moreover S_ε=S_rel. The vanishing assertions are equalities of compact modules (AHII05 Theorem 3.6, p.616, and the proof of Theorem 4.2, p.617; BKO21 Theorem 2.1, p.949).

A route is as follows. On S_rel, localisation at 𝔭 has kernel S_str, the two modules having the same conditions away from p, so S_rel/S_str is isomorphic to the image loc(S_rel) in the free module ℋ (§3.3). That image contains loc(C)≠0 (§3.3), so its rank is at least one, and it is a quotient of S_rel, of rank one (§3.4), so its rank is exactly one; hence S_str has rank zero and, lying in the torsion-free S_rel, is zero, and localisation is injective on S_rel. Next, C and S_rel both have rank one (§3.4), so S_rel/C is torsion: each x∈S_rel has a multiple λx∈C with 0≠λ∈Λ_R, and loc(λx) lies in ℋ^ε (§3.3). The class of loc(x) in ℋ/ℋ^ε is therefore killed by λ, and ℋ/ℋ^ε is free (§3.3), so loc(x)∈ℋ^ε. Since S_ε is the preimage of ℋ^ε in S_rel (first sequence of §4.1), S_ε=S_rel, of rank one. A class of S_(−ε) then lies in S_ε as well, so its localisation lies in ℋ^ε∩ℋ^(−ε)=0, and the class lies in S_str=0. The first sequence of §4.1 gives the ranks of the duals: for s=ε the map S_rel→ℋ/ℋ^ε is zero and 0→ℋ/ℋ^ε→X_ε→X_str→0 makes X_ε of rank one; for s=−ε the alternating sum of ranks in 0→S_rel→ℋ/ℋ^(−ε)→X_(−ε)→X_str→0 is 1−1+rank X_(−ε)−0=0, and X_(−ε) is finitely generated as an extension of X_str (§3.4) by a quotient of ℋ/ℋ^(−ε).

**Checks.**

- The nonzero localised elliptic-unit class excludes a strict submodule of rank one: if S_str had rank one, S_rel/S_str would be a torsion module injecting into ℋ, hence zero, and every class of S_rel would localise to zero.
- The sign is that of the primitive class: for ε=+ the torsion dual is X_−, and for ε=− it is X_+; interchanging them contradicts the rank one of X_ε given by §4.1.
- Without the integral splitting of §3.3 one only knows that S_rel/S_ε injects into ℋ/ℋ^ε, which need not be torsion-free, so S_ε=S_rel would not follow.

### 4.3 Torsion in the relaxed dual

char((X_rel)_tors)=char(X_str) over Λ_R. The two-variable relaxed dual has rank one, its specialisation along γ⁺−1 has rank one, and γ⁺−1 is prime to the characteristic ideal of the torsion of the two-variable dual (AHII05 Theorem 4.1 and its proof, p.616; AHI06 Lemma 1.2.1, p.1010).

The comparison passes through a two-variable strict–relaxed identity, which this section also proves: Billot's theorem for E over K₀=H(E[p]), on the parts that the inflation–restriction argument of §3.2 identifies with the modules over K∞ and K̃∞. Put W′=T′⊗_R L/R, the discrete module of the Cartier-dual avatar, and let X′_rel and X′^(2)_rel be the Pontryagin duals of the relaxed Selmer groups of W′ over K∞ and over K̃∞, with the conditions of §1.4 at every finite layer, as Λ_R- and Λ_(2)-modules. Assume Leopoldt's conjecture at p for every finite subextension of K₀(E[p^∞])/K₀, and that γ⁺−1 is prime to the characteristic ideal over Λ_(2) of the torsion submodule of X′^(2)_rel. Then char_(Λ_R)(X_str) is the image under the involution γ↦γ⁻¹ of char_(Λ_R)((X′_rel)_tors) (BIL86 §3, standing hypotheses (1)–(4), p.350, Proposition 3.8 and the Leopoldt hypothesis, p.353, and Theorem 3.24 with the remark after it, pp.364–365, which runs the argument on the parts cut out by a group of order prime to p).

A route is as follows. From the two-variable rank one and the rank one of its specialisation (§3.4), γ⁺−1 does not divide the characteristic ideal of the torsion of the two-variable relaxed dual of W; the symmetry below, applied over K̃∞, where it fixes γ⁺ and inverts γ⁻, carries this to X′^(2)_rel. The Leopoldt hypothesis concerns fields abelian over K: G_H acts on E[p^∞] through the restriction of φ_p, so H(E[p^∞])=K₀(E[p^∞]) is the compositum of H with the field cut out by φ_p. So the supplied Ax–Brumer theorem gives δ(M,p)=0 for each finite subextension M (IntegralIwasawaTheory §4.4, gap:leopoldt-abelian-over-imaginary-quadratic; BRU67, pp.121–124). The two-variable identity then equates char(X_str) with the involution of char((X′_rel)_tors).

For the two-variable identity follow Billot's proof for E over K₀, which satisfies his standing hypotheses: K⊆K₀, p inert in K, good reduction above p and E[p] rational over K₀ (BIL86 §3, p.350). Then E has good reduction at every place of K₀, so away from p the condition 0 of §1.4 is Billot's unramified condition (§1.4), and K₀(E[p^∞])=K₀K̃∞, because K₀(E[p^∞]) is abelian over K, its group over K₀ is ℤ_p² and p∤[K₀:K]. The steps of that proof are proved here. Over K₀K̃∞, compare the strict dual with the Iwasawa adjoint, the first Ext into the two-variable Iwasawa algebra, of the relaxed dual, composed with the involution (BIL86 Proposition 3.17, p.361, from class field theory under the Leopoldt hypothesis and local duality over the Iwasawa algebra; the Galois-cohomological form of this comparison is the Iwasawa duality with the involution of SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.12). Reduce this comparison modulo γ⁺−1. Its error terms are built from the lattices of W and W′ and from the duals of their points at the places above p. They are finitely generated R-modules on which γ⁺ acts by scalars different from 1, the places of K₀ above p being totally ramified in K₀K̃∞, so γ⁺−1 has finite kernel and finite cokernel on every subquotient of them, and the reduced map is a pseudo-isomorphism over Λ_R (BIL86, proof of Theorem 3.24, p.364, where these finiteness statements come from p being inert). The relaxed dual over K₀K̃∞ has no nonzero pseudo-null submodule, a property of the Galois group of its maximal abelian pro-p extension unramified outside p (BIL86 p.365 and the proof of Proposition 3.26, p.366), so BIL86 Theorem 3.22, pp.363–364, with Lemmas 3.18–3.21, shows that the quotient of the adjoint by γ⁺−1 and the torsion submodule of the quotient of the relaxed dual by γ⁺−1 have the same characteristic ideal over Λ_R. That theorem carries no involution: the one involution of the identity is the one in the comparison with the adjoint. Restriction identifies the quotients by γ⁺−1 of the duals over K₀K̃∞ with the duals over K₀K∞, because γ⁺ acts on W and on W′ by a nontrivial scalar, so that H¹ of Gal(K₀K̃∞/K₀K∞) and of its decomposition groups with these coefficients vanish (BIL86 pp.364–365; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11). Every step is natural for G=Gal(K₀/K), of order prime to p, acting through the G_K-structures of W and W′, so it holds on the parts cut out by G with the coprimality needed only there, as in the remark after BIL86 Theorem 3.24. The inflation–restriction argument of §3.2 identifies those parts with the modules over K∞ and K̃∞, for W′ as for W and at the finite layers of K̃∞ as at the K_n: it uses only that #G is prime to p, which also gives K₀∩L=K and Gal(K₀L/L)≃G for every finite layer L of the pro-p extension K̃∞/K (ArithmeticGaloisDuality §R02.2.2). Coefficients R enter by tensoring with the flat O-module R, after splitting E[p^∞]⊗_(ℤ_p)O into its components through the two embeddings of O; check every pseudo-isomorphism and finite error.

Remove the involution with the complex-conjugation symmetry of AHI06 Lemma 1.2.1, stated with its source and target. Choose τ∈G_ℚ in the decomposition group of the place selected by ι_p, restricting to the nontrivial automorphism of K. It normalises G_Φ and every G_(K_n), fixes the place of K_n above p and acts on Γ⁻ by inversion. Conjugation by τ is an R-linear isomorphism from the cohomology of W onto that of its τ-conjugate W^τ, the same R-module on which g∈G_K acts as τ⁻¹gτ. It preserves the strict and relaxed conditions and inverts γ. By φ(ā)=conjugate(φ(a)) and φ(a)·conjugate(φ(a))=Na, the character g↦φ_p(τ⁻¹gτ) of W^τ is φ_p⁻¹χ_cyc, where χ_cyc is the cyclotomic character. That is the character of the Cartier-dual avatar of §0.4, so W^τ≃W′ as R[G_K]-modules. It is the analogue over R of the conjugate component of BIL86 Lemma 3.6, whose character is the conjugation of O applied to that of the Tate module, and which equals the inverse of that character times χ_cyc. The two-variable identity therefore pairs the strict module of W with the involution of the relaxed torsion of W^τ. The symmetry carries the latter to the relaxed torsion of W composed with the involution, so the two involutions cancel. The conjugation c of Φ enters only through the O-algebra structure of R: on G_Φ, W^τ is F[π^∞]⊗_(O,c)R. No step uses an automorphism of R, and none need exist (§1.2). Each comparison is a semilinear equivalence along an explicit ring isomorphism, and `LinearEquiv.charIdeal_eq_comap` expresses the characteristic ideal of its target through that of its source. Invariance of an ideal is never read off from a single transport.

### 4.4 The opposite signed characteristic ideal

The following two identities hold over Λ_R.

1. char((X_ε)_tors)·char(ℋ^ε/S_ε)=char(S_rel/C).
2. char(X_(−ε))=char(ℋ^ε/loc(C)), where loc is localisation at 𝔭, injective on S_ε because S_str=0.

(AHII05 Theorem 4.2 with (4.1)–(4.4), pp.616–617.)

A route is as follows. For the second identity, which is the one the main conjecture uses, the first sequence of §4.1 with s=−ε, S_(−ε)=0, S_ε=S_rel and ℋ/ℋ^(−ε)≃ℋ^ε (§§3.3, 4.2) gives 0→ℋ^ε/S_ε→X_(−ε)→X_str→0, and there is an exact sequence 0→S_ε/C→ℋ^ε/loc(C)→ℋ^ε/S_ε→0. For the first identity, the second sequence of §4.1 with s=ε and S_str=0 (§4.2) gives 0→S_ε→ℋ^ε→X_rel→X_ε→0; since ℋ^ε/S_ε is torsion, it yields 0→ℋ^ε/S_ε→(X_rel)_tors→(X_ε)_tors→0, and §§4.3 and 3.5 conclude.

### 4.5 The localised global lattice and the primitive class

Under ℋ^ε≃V_R^ε of §3.3, char(ℋ^ε/loc(C))=(L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)); equivalently, the coordinates of loc(C) in the basis v_ε generate the ideal (L_p). The comparison accounts for every auxiliary ideal, for the primitive and imprimitive normalisations, for the CM period and the trace-dual bases, and for the inversion of characters; an equality after tensoring with L is not sufficient (AHII05 Theorem 4.3 and the paragraph after it, p.617; BKO21 §6.0.1, p.961, and the proof of Theorem 6.1, p.962). In particular, for a=(α) with α≡1 modulo 6f and α≡2 modulo p, the twisted smoothing factor N(a)−φ(a)γ_a, γ_a the image of the Artin symbol σ_a in Γ⁻, is a unit of Λ_R, and ξ is c⁻¹ times the image under §3.3 of loc((N(a)−φ(a)γ_a)⁻¹·c_a), the localisation of an element of C, where c∈R× is the unit of §3.3.

A route is as follows. Transport the localised classes loc(c_a) through §3.3, which sends loc(c_a) to c·d_a·ξ=c·d_a·L_p·v_ε with c∈R× independent of a: the coordinate of loc(c_a) in the basis v_ε is L_p times the distribution factor d_a of a, up to the unit c, so the coordinates of loc(C) form the ideal L_p·I, where I is the ideal generated by the d_a. These distribution factors generate the unit ideal of Λ_R. The factor of a is the twisted smoothing factor N(a)−φ(a)σ_a (NonordinaryCMSpecialValues §§2.6–2.7), whose image in Λ_R is N(a)−φ(a)γ_a with γ_a the image of σ_a in Γ⁻; since γ_a−1 lies in the maximal ideal (ϖ_R,γ−1), it is congruent to N(a)−φ(a) modulo that ideal. Take a=(α) with α≡1 modulo 6f and α≡2 modulo p, which exists by the Chinese remainder theorem because p∤6f. Then a is prime to 6pf, φ(a)=α, and N(a)−φ(a)=α(ᾱ−1)≡2·(2−1)=2 modulo p, a unit of R because p≥5; so that factor is a unit of Λ_R. Compare the primitive ν=1 normalisation of §2.1 without dividing by a nonunit. Translate Agboola–Howard's direct-character formula to the inverse-character convention of §2.5. Only after this integral lattice comparison may infinitely many matching characters be used, since an equality in the fraction field could hide a power of π.

**Checks.**

- If loc(C)=Λ_R·L_p·v_ε then ℋ^ε/loc(C)≃Λ_R/(L_p); if instead the distribution factors generated only (π), the ideal would be (πL_p), and the identity would fail by exactly the factor the integral statement detects.
- The auxiliary ideal decides the unit. At p=5, α≡2 modulo 5 gives the residue 2·(2−1)=2≠0 of N(a)−φ(a); a choice α≡1 modulo 5 gives ᾱ−1≡0, a factor in the maximal ideal, which alone would not show that the factors generate the unit ideal.
- Restriction of scalars from Λ_R to Λ_O multiplies degrees: for R/O of degree d and f with coefficients in O, the Λ_O-characteristic ideal of Λ_R/(f) is (f^d), not (f).
- Agboola–Howard's interpolation pairs χ with δ_χ(v_ε), the one used here pairs χ with δ_(χ⁻¹)(v_ε); for a character of order 5 these evaluate the generator at ζ and at ζ⁴, which differ unless the generator is invariant under the involution.

### 4.6 The inert anticyclotomic CM main conjecture

For an inert CM datum, X_(−ε) is a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module and char_(Λ_R)(X_(−ε))=(L_p(φ,Ω,v_ε)) as an integral ideal of Λ_R. The theorem allows E to be defined only over H, with p≥5 inert in K, p∤h_K and the standing CM and good-reduction conditions; it does not assume that E descends to K or that 2 splits in K. The ideal is unchanged by unit changes of v_ε and of the integral differential, and compatible finite coefficient extension R→R′ carries the identity over Λ_R to the identity over Λ_(R′). Restriction of scalars to Λ_O replaces (L_p) by its norm (BKO21 Theorem 6.1 and its proof, p.962).

A route is as follows. Combine §4.4(2) with §4.5. Keep Λ_R fixed through both identifications, including at the vertical prime (ϖ_R). Base change uses PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6's base change along finite free O→O′, transported to R→R′, and restriction of scalars uses the supplier contract below.

**Checks.**

- The torsion module is X_− when ε=+ and X_+ when ε=−; the module X_ε has rank one by §4.2 and has no characteristic ideal in this sense.
- For R/O of degree d and L_p with coefficients in O, restriction of scalars gives the ideal (L_p^d), which at d=2 and L_p=T is (T²); an unchanged ideal (T) would be wrong.
- The ideal (L_p) contains the factor (π) whenever ξ∈πV_R^ε, as in the lattice model ξ=πv_ε of the worked examples, where X_(−ε) then has characteristic ideal divisible by (π) and positive μ-invariant.

### 5.1 Signed control elements

For n≥0 define

    ω_n⁺ = ∏_(1≤k≤n, k even) Φ_(p^k)(γ),    ω_n⁻ = (γ−1)·∏_(1≤k≤n, k odd) Φ_(p^k)(γ)

in Λ_R, where Φ_m is the m-th cyclotomic polynomial (`Polynomial.cyclotomic`). Then ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1. For a character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Γ⁻/Γ_n, χ(ω_n^s)=0 exactly when χ∈Ξ^(−s). Consequently ω_n^s annihilates A_n^s, and ω_n^(−s) carries the ordinary formal points F(Ψ_n) into A_n^s and the ordinary Selmer group Sel(K_n,W) into Sel_s(K_n,W) (AHII05 §5, p.618, definitions and (5.1)).

A route is as follows. The product formula is the factorisation X^(pⁿ)−1=∏_(0≤k≤n)Φ_(p^k)(X) (`Polynomial.prod_cyclotomic_eq_X_pow_sub_one` over the divisors of pⁿ, or `Polynomial.cyclotomic_prime_pow_mul_X_pow_sub_one` and induction), with Φ_1(X)=X−1, evaluated at γ. A character of exact order p^k, k≥1, sends γ to a root of Φ_(p^k) and has c=k+1, so the roots of ω_n⁺ are the characters with c odd, Ξ⁻, and those of ω_n⁻ are the trivial character and c even, Ξ⁺. On points use λ_χ(f·y)=f(χ)λ_χ(y) and injectivity of λ on the torsion-free F(Ψ_n); transfer to Selmer groups by equivariance of localisation.

Its API consists of the following facts on these carriers and with these hypotheses.

- ω_n^s is the displayed product in Λ_R for sign s and level n.
- ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1 for every n≥0.
- For χ factoring through Γ⁻/Γ_n, χ(ω_n^s)=0 if and only if χ∈Ξ^(−s).
- ω_n^s·A_n^s=0, ω_n^(−s)·F(Ψ_n)⊆A_n^s and ω_n^(−s)·Sel(K_n,W)⊆Sel_s(K_n,W).

**Checks.**

- At p=5 and n=2, ω_2⁺=Φ_25(γ) and ω_2⁻=(γ−1)Φ_5(γ), with degrees 20 and 5 adding to 25; a character of order 5 kills ω_2⁻ only, and one of order 25 kills ω_2⁺ only.

### 5.2 Signed local control

For every inert CM datum, s∈{+,−} and n≥0, the restriction map L_n^s→L_∞^s[ω_n^s] is an isomorphism. All statements use the Kummer images of §1.1 and the coefficient ring R (AHII05 Lemma 5.1 and its proof, p.618, which give injectivity and, under Rubin's conjecture in the form of AHII05 Conjecture 3.5, p.615, a finite cokernel; that conjecture is the integral splitting ℋ=ℋ⁺⊕ℋ⁻ of §3.3).

A route is as follows. H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 by the tame argument of §3.3; coefficient extension creates no invariants. Inflation–restriction gives injectivity. The R-corank of L_n^s is the R-rank of Λ_R/ω_n^s, by the logarithm and the normal basis theorem (`IsGalois.normalBasis`) applied to F(Ψ_n)⊗Φ≃Φ[Gal(Ψ_n/Φ)]. The Pontryagin dual of L_∞^s is ℋ/ℋ^s, free of rank one by §3.3, so the dual of L_∞^s[ω_n^s] is Λ_R/(ι(ω_n^s)); here ι(ω_n^s) is a unit multiple of ω_n^s, because Φ_(p^k)(γ⁻¹)=γ^(−φ(p^k))Φ_(p^k)(γ) and γ⁻¹−1=−γ⁻¹(γ−1). As ω_n^s(1+T) is a distinguished polynomial in T, each factor Φ_(p^k)(1+T) being monic and congruent to T^(φ(p^k)) modulo p, the isomorphism R[T]/(ω_n^s)≃R⟦T⟧/(ω_n^s) (`Polynomial.IsDistinguishedAt.algEquivQuotient`) and the power basis of R[T]/(ω_n^s) (`AdjoinRoot.powerBasis'`) make Λ_R/(ω_n^s) free over R of rank deg ω_n^s. Hence L_∞^s[ω_n^s] is R-cofree of the same corank as L_n^s, and in particular divisible. Equal coranks and injectivity make the cokernel finite; it is a quotient of a divisible module, hence divisible, and a finite divisible module is zero.

**Checks.**

- The proof needs the tame field: the pro-p property of Gal(Ψ∞/Φ) alone says nothing about W[π], whereas G_Φ acts on W[π]≃F[π]⊗R through the cyclic group Gal(Φ(F[π])/Φ) of order p²−1, which is 24 at p=5, without nonzero fixed vectors, and that group meets Gal(Ψ∞/Φ) trivially.
- At n=1 and s=+, ω_1⁺=1, so L_∞⁺[1]=0 and L_1⁺=0: the plus condition at level 1 is zero because every character through level 1 has even conductor.
- The cokernel vanishes. At p=5 and n=2 both sides have R-corank 20 for s=+ and 5 for s=−, the degrees of Φ_25(γ) and (γ−1)Φ_5(γ). A finite quotient Q of the divisible target killed by ϖ_R^k satisfies Q=ϖ_R^kQ=0.
- The integral freeness of ℋ/ℋ^s in §3.3 is what excludes a nonzero cokernel. If the dual of L_∞^− were the rank-one ideal 𝔪=(ϖ_R,T), which is not free, then at n=0, where ω_0⁻=T, the dual of L_∞^−[T] would be 𝔪/T𝔪≃R⊕R/ϖ_R, the class of T being nonzero and killed by ϖ_R. The divisible image of L_0^− would miss the finite part, so the cokernel would be finite and nonzero. This is the bounded error that AHII05 Lemma 5.1 allows without Rubin's conjecture.

### 5.3 Signed Selmer control

Assume E is defined over K. For s∈{+,−} and n≥0, let X_(n,s) be the Pontryagin dual of Sel_s(K_n,W) with the trace convention of §0.4. The restriction map induces a surjection X_s/ω_n^sX_s→X_(n,s)/ω_n^sX_(n,s) with finite kernel; equivalently, restriction Sel_s(K_n,W)[ω_n^s]→Sel_s(K∞,W)[ω_n^s] is injective with finite cokernel. After tensoring with L_χ and projecting to a character χ with χ(ω_n^s)=0, it identifies X_s⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹)L_χ, the χ⁻¹-specialisation of X_s, with the L_χ-linear dual of Sel_s(K_n,V_pE)^χ (§1.4), through the coefficient trace of §0.4 and the duality of §1.5; in particular the two have the same L_χ-dimension (AHII05 Theorem 5.2 and its proof, pp.619–620).

A route is as follows. Compare global localisation kernels by inflation–restriction and the snake lemma (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11), using the image of localisation rather than assuming it surjective. At 𝔭 use §5.2. Away from p only places above f matter, since the comparison is in cohomology unramified outside fp; at w|v|f the kernel of restriction is a quotient of W^(G_(K∞,w)), which lies in W^(I_v), a proper R-submodule of W≃L/R because φ is ramified at v, hence finite. Global injectivity uses H⁰(K∞,W)=0 from §5.2. Dualise with the involution to obtain the stated direction.

**Checks.**

- The kernel on the dual side corresponds to the cokernel of the injective discrete map, so the dual map is surjective; the reverse direction would claim injectivity of a map with a possibly nonzero finite kernel.
- At a place v|f the local error W^(I_v) is finite because every proper R-submodule of L/R is ϖ_R^(−k)R/R for some k≥0; rational vanishing of H¹ away from p (§1.3) is a different fact and does not bound these integral errors.
- At n=0 and s=+, ω_0⁺=1, so both sides are zero; at n=0 and s=−, ω_0⁻=γ−1 and the statement compares the Γ⁻-coinvariants of X_− with the dual of Sel_−(K,W)=Sel(K,W).

*Needs:* §5.2; §1.4; §1.5; §0.4; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §§3.3, 3.11.

### 5.4 Finite-level decomposition into the two signed pieces

Assume E is defined over K. Let X_n be the dual of Sel(K_n,W). The map X_n→X_(n,+)/ω_n⁺X_(n,+)⊕X_(n,−)/ω_n⁻X_(n,−), dual to the sum of the inclusions Sel_+(K_n,W)[ω_n⁺]⊕Sel_−(K_n,W)[ω_n⁻]→Sel(K_n,W), has finite kernel and cokernel. After tensoring with L it is an isomorphism compatible with every character projector after extension to L_χ. For χ∈Ξ^ε factoring through Gal(K_n/K), Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ=Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ (AHII05 Proposition 5.3 and its proof, p.620, with the misprint recorded in the References; BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, p.963).

A route is as follows. Use ω_n⁺ω_n⁻=γ^(pⁿ)−1, which kills Sel(K_n,W) and X_n, and the inclusions ω_n^(−s)Sel(K_n,W)⊆Sel_s(K_n,W) of §5.1 to build the comparison maps. Their kernels and cokernels are modules over the finite ring Λ_R/(ω_n⁺,ω_n⁻), finite but not zero in general. After inverting p the character projectors split the disjoint root sets of ω_n⁺ and ω_n⁻. A character χ∈Ξ^ε survives the point condition of sign −ε, which kills Ξ^(−ε), so the χ-parts of the local conditions of sign −ε and of the ordinary condition agree.

**Checks.**

- At χ=1 and ε=+, the trivial character lies in Ξ⁺, and the minus point condition A_n⁻ retains it, so Sel_−(K_n,V_pE)^1=Sel(K_n,V_pE)^1; the plus condition kills it.
- Integrally only finite kernel and cokernel are asserted: Λ_R/(ω_2⁺,ω_2⁻) is a nonzero finite ring, so the integral map need not be an isomorphism.
- For χ of order 25 at p=5 and ε=+, χ∈Ξ⁻=Ξ^(−ε), and the equality of χ-parts is not asserted for the minus sign; there the plus condition is the one that retains χ.

*Needs:* §5.1; §5.3; §1.4; `Polynomial.IsDistinguishedAt.algEquivQuotient`; `AdjoinRoot.powerBasis'`.

### 5.5 Specialisation dimension and local length

Let A be a commutative local ring with residue field κ and M an A-module. Then dim_κ(κ⊗_A M)≤length_A(M) in ℕ∞. In particular, for a finitely generated torsion Λ_R-module X and a finite character ρ of Γ⁻ with values in L_ρ, applied to the discrete valuation ring A=(Λ_R)_(P_ρ), whose residue field is the field L(ρ) generated by the values of ρ, dim_(L(ρ))(X⊗_(Λ_R,ρ)L(ρ))≤length_A(X_(P_ρ))=ord_ρ char(X). If a generator f of char(X) is a unit multiple of an element of Λ_O, then ord_ρ f=ord_(ρ⁻¹) f. BKO21, proof of Theorem 6.2, p.963, uses the inequality without stating it, among the arguments it calls standard, and writes ord_χ throughout without comparing ρ with ρ⁻¹; both assertions are proved here by the route below.

A route is as follows. κ⊗_A M is an A-module quotient of M, so its A-length is at most that of M (`Module.length_le_of_surjective`). Its A-length equals its κ-length (`IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars` with B=κ), which is its κ-dimension (`Module.length_eq_finrank`, or ∞ when infinite). Λ_R is regular local of dimension two, so its localisation at the height-one prime P_ρ is a discrete valuation ring with residue field Frac(Λ_R/P_ρ)=L(ρ), and X⊗κ(P_ρ) is the specialisation at ρ. The length of X_(P_ρ) is the coefficient of P_ρ in `TauCeti.Iwasawa.charDivisor` of X, which is ord_ρ char(X) by the supplier contract with PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras (gap:character-prime-multiplicity). For the last assertion, an automorphism of Φ(μ_(p^r)) over Φ sends ρ(γ) to ρ(γ)⁻¹, because Φ/ℚ_p is unramified and so Gal(Φ(μ_(p^r))/Φ)≃(ℤ/p^r)×, and it fixes the coefficients of f.

**Checks.**

- For A a discrete valuation ring and M=A/𝔪^k, κ⊗M=κ has dimension 1 and M has length k, so the inequality is strict for k≥2.
- For M=κ², dimension and length are both 2.
- For X=Λ_R/(g²) with g the minimal polynomial of ρ(γ)−1, the specialisation has dimension 1 and ord_ρ char(X)=2. For R ramified with μ_p⊂L, the coefficient automorphism need not invert ρ(γ), so ord_ρ and ord_(ρ⁻¹) are kept apart for a general R-coefficient generator.

*Needs:* `Module.length`; `Module.length_le_of_surjective`; `IsLocalRing.length_restrictScalars`; `Module.length_eq_finrank`; `TauCeti.Iwasawa.charDivisor`; PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras §4.6 (gap:character-prime-multiplicity).

### 5.6 Strict specialisation inequality

Assume E is defined over K. For every finite character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Gal(K_n/K),

    dim_(L_χ) Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ dim_(L_χ)(X_str ⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹) L_χ) ≤ ord_(χ⁻¹) char(X_str).

The middle term is the specialisation of X_str at χ⁻¹, because the dual action inverts characters. Only injectivity of restriction is used; no finite-cokernel control is asserted (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, pp.962–963; AHII05 Lemma 5.1 and its proof, p.618).

A route is as follows. Restriction Sel_str(K_n,W)→Sel_str(K∞,W)^(Γ_n) is injective: its kernel lies in H¹(Γ_n,W^(G_(K∞))) (SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11), and W^(G_(K∞)) lies in H⁰(Ψ∞,W)=0 (§5.2); restriction preserves the zero local conditions. After tensoring with L_χ the corank of the χ-part of Sel_str(K_n,W) is dim Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ, the strict condition on W being propagated from that on V_pE (§1.4). The dual of Sel_str(K∞,W)^(Γ_n) is X_str/(γ^(pⁿ)−1)X_str, whose χ⁻¹-part after tensoring with L_χ is X_str⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹)L_χ. Apply §5.5 at the prime of χ⁻¹ to the finitely generated torsion module X_str (§3.4).

**Checks.**

- Repeated factors raise the order without raising the dimension: X_str=Λ_R/(g²) gives dimension 1 at χ⁻¹ and order 2.
- The argument covers χ=1: injectivity uses only H⁰(K∞,W)=0, and the trivial character equals its inverse.
- No surjectivity of restriction is claimed: a finite cokernel would not affect the inequality, and an infinite one would only make the left side smaller.

*Needs:* §1.4; §1.5; §0.4; §3.4; §5.2; §5.5; SelmerIwasawaCohomology §3.11.

## Layer 6: Finite-character CM rank bounds

Sections 6.2–6.4 assume in addition that E is defined over K and that V_T is the CM component of V_pE (Conventions); then h_K=1 and R=O. Section 6.1 holds for every inert CM datum.

### 6.1 The strict characteristic divisor contains L_p

The surjection X_(−ε)→X_str of the first sequence of §4.1 has torsion kernel ℋ^ε/S_ε, so char(X_(−ε))=char(ℋ^ε/S_ε)·char(X_str); hence (L_p)⊆char(X_str) and ord_ρ char(X_str)≤ord_ρ L_p at every finite character ρ (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, p.962). When R=O, ξ and a generator of V∞*,ε are O-integral, so (L_p) is extended from Λ_O and ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p=ord_χ L_p (§5.5); for a general inert CM datum the two orders are kept apart.

A route is as follows. Use the first sequence of §4.1 with s=−ε and the torsion of X_(−ε) (§4.2), multiplicativity of characteristic ideals and §4.6. Translate the product of integral ideals into containment and then into orders at height-one primes; apply §5.5 for the comparison of ρ and ρ⁻¹.

**Checks.**

- In the model char(X_(−ε))=(fg) and char(X_str)=(f), the containment is (fg)⊆(f); the reverse containment (f)⊆(fg) fails for g a nonunit such as g=T.
- At a character ρ with ord_ρ f=1 and ord_ρ g=2, the orders are 1≤3.
- For R ramified with μ_p⊂L and a generator not extended from Λ_O, ord_ρ and ord_(ρ⁻¹) can differ, so the equality is asserted only when R=O.

*Needs:* §4.1; §4.2; §4.6; §5.5; `TauCeti.Iwasawa.charIdeal_eq_mul_of_exact`.

### 6.2 The one-dimensional CM local correction

Assume E is defined over K. For every finite character χ of Γ⁻ factoring through Gal(K_n/K), dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ−dim Sel_str(K_n,V_pE)^χ≤1. Indeed the quotient embeds into the χ-part of the ordinary local Kummer image at 𝔭, which is one-dimensional over L_χ for the CM component (BKO21 proof of Theorem 6.2, p.963).

A route is as follows. Localisation at 𝔭 on the ordinary Selmer group has kernel the strict Selmer group, the conditions away from p being equal. The formal logarithm identifies E((K_n)_𝔭)⊗Φ with Ψ_n≃Φ[Gal(Ψ_n/Φ)] by the normal basis theorem, in which each character occurs once. Extend to L_χ before applying the character idempotent and conclude from the injection.

**Checks.**

- The local image can have dimension 0 or 1; the statement is an inequality and does not assert that global localisation is onto the local line.
- Over ℚ_p rather than over the CM coefficient field, E((K_n)_𝔭)⊗ℚ_p has dimension 2pⁿ, and its χ-part over L_χ⊗ℚ_p would count both CM embeddings, giving 2 instead of 1.
- At n=0 and χ=1, E(Φ)⊗Φ≃Φ is one-dimensional, so the ordinary and strict rational Selmer groups over K differ by at most one dimension.

*Needs:* §1.4; §0.3; `IsGalois.normalBasis`.

### 6.3 The matching-parity bound

Assume E is defined over K. If χ∈Ξ^ε factors through Gal(K_n/K), then dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ=dim Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ≤ord_χ L_p. The trivial character is covered exactly when ε=+ (BKO21 Theorem 6.2 and the last paragraph of its proof, pp.962–963; AHII05 Theorem 5.2 and Proposition 5.3, pp.619–620).

A route is as follows. Since χ∈Ξ^ε, χ(ω_n^(−ε))=0 by §5.1. Signed control for the sign −ε at χ (§5.3) gives dim Sel_(−ε)(K_n,V_pE)^χ=dim_(L_χ)(X_(−ε)⊗_(Λ_R,χ⁻¹)L_χ), the χ⁻¹-specialisation of X_(−ε) being the dual of that χ-part, and this dimension is at most ord_(χ⁻¹)char(X_(−ε))=ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p by §§5.5 and 4.6, which equals ord_χ L_p because R=O (§6.1). The equality with the ordinary χ-part is §5.4.

**Checks.**

- A character of exact order p has conductor exponent 2 and tests the Ξ⁺ branch; one of exact order p² has exponent 3 and tests Ξ⁻.
- At χ=1 and ε=+, the ordinary component equals the minus signed one by §5.4, and the bound reads dim Sel(K,V_pE)≤ord_1 L_p, the order of vanishing of L_p at T=0.
- If ord_χ L_p=0, the χ-part of the rational Selmer group vanishes.

*Needs:* §4.6; §5.1; §5.3; §5.4; §5.5; §6.1.

### 6.4 Finite-character CM rank bounds

Assume E is defined over K. Let χ be a finite character of Γ⁻ of exact order p^r, r≥0, and let n≥r, so that χ factors through Gal(K_n/K); this includes the source's layer n=c(χ). With rank_CM E(K_n)^χ=dim_(L_χ)(E(K_n)⊗_(O_K)L_χ)^χ, through the fixed embedding O_K→Φ⊂L_χ,

    rank_CM E(K_n)^χ ≤ dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ ord_χ L_p        if χ ∈ Ξ^ε,
    rank_CM E(K_n)^χ ≤ dim Sel(K_n,V_pE)^χ ≤ ord_χ L_p + 1    if χ ∈ Ξ^(−ε).

Neither equality nor a leading-term formula is asserted (BKO21 Theorem 6.2 and its proof, pp.962–963).

A route is as follows. For every k the Kummer map E(K_n)/p^kE(K_n)→Sel_(p^k)(E/K_n) is injective (`WeierstrassCurve.kummerSelmerMap`, `WeierstrassCurve.kummerSelmerMap_injective`, since p^k is invertible in K_n). Since E(K_n) is finitely generated (`WeierstrassCurve.Affine.fg_point_of_numberField`), E(K_n)⊗ℤ_p is the inverse limit of the E(K_n)/p^kE(K_n), and the inverse limit of these injections, tensored with ℚ_p, embeds E(K_n)⊗ℚ_p into Sel(K_n,V_pE) of §1.4: the localisation at 𝔭 of the class of a point lies in the image of E((K_n)_𝔭)⊗ℚ_p=F(Ψ_n)⊗ℚ_p, the formal group having finite index in the local points, and the localisations away from p lie in H¹((K_n)_v,V_pE)=0. After extending scalars to L_χ through O_K→Φ this gives an injection of E(K_n)⊗_(O_K)L_χ into Sel(K_n,V_pE)⊗L_χ, equivariant for Gal(K_n/K), with torsion killed by the tensor product. For χ∈Ξ^ε use §6.3. For χ∈Ξ^(−ε) combine §§6.2, 5.6 and 6.1: dim Sel^χ≤dim Sel_str^χ+1≤ord_(χ⁻¹)char(X_str)+1≤ord_(χ⁻¹)L_p+1=ord_χ L_p+1.

**Checks.**

- For ε=+, χ=1 lies in Ξ^ε and receives no extra one; for ε=−, χ=1 lies in Ξ^(−ε) and receives the bound ord_1 L_p+1.
- If ord_χ L_p=0, the matching χ-part of the Mordell–Weil group has rank 0, and the opposite one has rank at most 1.
- The main conjecture of §4.6 for E over H is not turned into this assertion: for K=ℚ(√−23), h_K=3 and no maximal-CM curve with Hecke character over K is defined over K, so §6.4 says nothing there while §4.6 applies.

*Needs:* §6.1; §5.6; §6.2; §6.3; §1.4; `WeierstrassCurve.kummerSelmerMap`; `WeierstrassCurve.kummerSelmerMap_injective`; `WeierstrassCurve.Affine.fg_point_of_numberField`.

## Worked examples

Take a character χ of order p. Its conductor exponent is 2, so χ∈Ξ⁺. If ε=+, the primitive class lies in V_R⁺ and the torsion dual is X_−; the minus point condition kills Ξ⁻ and so retains χ, and the finite Selmer bound at χ has no extra dimension. If ε=−, the same χ lies in the opposite parity and only the strict route of §§5.6, 6.1–6.2 bounds it, with the extra one. For a character of order p² the conductor exponent is 3 and both branches reverse. Translating Agboola–Howard's order-indexed sets by a uniform sign reversal would put the order-p character in the wrong branch (BKO21 §6.0.2, p.962, footnote 3, p.960; AHII05 Remark 3.2, p.614).

At χ=1, ω_n⁻ has a zero and ω_n⁺ has none. The minus formal-point condition retains the ordinary component at the base layer, so ε=+ again uses X_− with no extra one, while for ε=− the bound allows the extra one. In the latter case δ vanishes on V_R⁻, and no interpolation formula divides by δ(v_−); this does not force L_p(1)=0.

For a lattice test let ξ=πv_ε in the free line V_R^ε with basis v_ε. Then L_p=π, V_R^ε/C_∞=Λ_R/(π), and the characteristic ideal retains (π). Replacing v_ε by uv_ε for a unit u replaces the coordinate by u⁻¹π, with the same ideal. After inverting p the quotient disappears, so an equality proved only after inverting p loses exactly the information the integral main conjecture records.

For a coefficient test let R/O have degree d and let f have coefficients in O. The Λ_R-module Λ_R/(f) has characteristic ideal (f) over Λ_R, while as a Λ_O-module it is (Λ_O/(f))^d with characteristic ideal (f^d). For general f use its norm. This rejects an identity that keeps the same element over both rings.

For a specialisation test, at a characteristic-zero character prime with local parameter t, the localised module A/(t^k) has specialisation dimension 1 and characteristic multiplicity k. The rank inequality is therefore dimension at most order, with strict inequality for k>1. A finite R-module can be a nonzero integral control error while contributing nothing to a rational character dimension; the control arguments therefore track finite errors and characteristic lengths separately.
