# Roadmap: connected symplectic gauge field theory and quilted sewing

**Summary.** Fix a rank `r ≥ 1` and a degree `d` coprime to `r`. To a closed connected oriented surface `X` of genus `g`, carrying a rank-`r` Hermitian bundle `P` of degree `d` and a unitary connection `δ` on its determinant line, gauge theory attaches the moduli space `M(P,δ)` of unitary connections on `P` with central curvature and determinant connection exactly `δ`, modulo gauge transformations of determinant one. Holonomy identifies it with the tuples `(A₁,B₁,…,A_g,B_g)` in `SU(r)` satisfying `[A₁,B₁]⋯[A_g,B_g] = ζ·1`, `ζ = exp(2πi d/r)`, modulo simultaneous conjugation. Following Wehrheim and Woodward, this roadmap builds from them a (2+1)-dimensional field theory: a surface goes to an integral `A∞` category of generalized Lagrangian branes in `M(P,δ)`, and a connected bordism, cut into elementary pieces, to the composite of the quilted Floer functors of their Lagrangian correspondences. Throughout, `(r,d)` is fixed, bordisms and their intermediate surfaces are connected, bundles carry determinant data, coefficients are integral and gradings are `ℤ/4`.

The headline theorems are these. If `ζ` is a primitive `r`-th root of unity, the relation `[A,B] = ζ·1` has a solution in `SU(r)`, unique up to simultaneous conjugation; for every genus `g ≥ 1` the solutions of `[A₁,B₁]⋯[A_g,B_g] = ζ·1` modulo conjugation form a simply connected space; and an element of `SU(r)` commuting with every entry of a solution is central. For `r ≥ 2` and `g ≥ 2`, `M(P,δ)` is a compact, connected, simply connected symplectic manifold of dimension `(2g−2)(r²−1)` with even Chern numbers and `c₁ = 2r[Ω]`, for the form `Ω` built from the pairing `−tr(ξη)/(4π²)`; it is a point on the torus and empty on the sphere. If `Y` is an elementary bordism between surfaces of positive genus, a cylinder or a single one- or two-handle, restricting connections to the two ends embeds the moduli of `Y` as a compact, simply connected, relatively spin Lagrangian correspondence, which need not be spin: for `r = 2`, `d = 1` and the handle from genus two to genus three it is not. Finally, the quilted functors of these correspondences assemble into a functor from the bundle-decorated connected (2+1)-bordism category to a category `A∞(4)/S` of integral `ℤ/4`-graded cohomologically unital `A∞` categories with shift, whose morphisms are classes of shift-equivariant unital functors up to shifts. This is Wehrheim–Woodward's connected Floer field theory, here for every coprime degree and with coherent rather than strict shift equivariance.

In Tau Ceti, the roadmap specializes the surface gauge theory of SymplecticGeometry/HamiltonianReduction to fixed determinant and coprime degree, and takes bundles, holonomy and spin lifts from SmoothBundlesConnections and SpinGeometry. HeegaardFloer and FukayaTheory provide the Fredholm and pseudoholomorphic analysis and the ordinary Floer theory of branes, on which the quilts are built here. Tau Ceti's `ℤ`-graded `A∞` categories and DGAInfinity's functor categories carry the algebra. InstantonFloerTheory takes the rank-two odd-degree surface moduli of Layer 1, and nothing else, for its Atiyah–Floer comparison.

Below, Layer 0 defines the decorated bordism category; Layer 1 builds the surface moduli, their relation model and normalizations; Layer 2 the elementary correspondences with their spin, orientation, grading and monotonicity data; Layer 3 the connected Cerf relations; Layer 4 the quilt categories, their zero extensions, the quilted analysis and the target `A∞(4)/S`; Layer 5 assembles the field theory.

## Conventions

1. **Rank, degree and twist.** `r ≥ 1` and `d ∈ ℤ` with `gcd(r,d) = 1` are fixed; `ζ = exp(2πi d/r)` and `z_(r,d) = ζ·I_r`. For `r ≥ 1`, `ζ` is a primitive `r`-th root of unity exactly when `gcd(r,d) = 1`, and `z_(r,d)` depends only on `d mod r`. The statements on the relation model are made for every primitive `r`-th root `ζ` (Mathlib's `IsPrimitiveRoot ζ r`), which covers every coprime degree. Surfaces are closed, connected, oriented and smooth, of genus `g`.
2. **Matrices and gauge action.** `u(r)` is the Lie algebra of anti-Hermitian matrices and `su(r)` its trace-free part. The covariant derivative is `d + A`, and gauge transformations act on the left, `g·A = gAg⁻¹ − (dg)g⁻¹`, with infinitesimal generator `−d_Aξ`, as in HamiltonianReduction R5.
3. **Normalization.** On `su(r)`, `B(ξ,η) = −tr(ξη)/(4π²)`; on `u(1) = iℝ` the same formula reads `B(ξ,η) = −ξη/(4π²)`. The surface form is `Ω(a,b) = ∫_X B(a∧b)` on trace-free one-forms. The determinant convention is `c₁(det P) = [iF_δ/(2π)]`, so the fixed-determinant equation is `F_A = (F_δ/r)·I_r`. The monotonicity constant is `τ_WW = 1/(2r)` in `[Ω] = τ_WW·c₁`. Wehrheim–Woodward's form, built from the basic inner product `−Tr(ξη)` (Remark 3.2.2(b) and eq. (26)), is `ω_WW = 4π²Ω`, and the unnormalized pairing `−∫_X tr(a∧b)` equals `4π²Ω`. Rescaling a form by a positive constant keeps its Lagrangian submanifolds, its compatible almost complex structures and monotonicity, and multiplies `τ` by that constant. In FukayaTheory's monotone convention, `ω(B) = 2τc₁(B)` on spherical classes and `ω(β) = τμ(β)` on relative classes, the constant is `τ_FK = τ_WW/2 = 1/(4r)` (§2.6).
4. **Negative factors and correspondences.** `M⁻` is `M` with the form `−ω` and the almost complex structure `−J`. A Lagrangian correspondence from `M_-` to `M_+` is a Lagrangian submanifold of `M_-⁻ × M_+`, whose form is `−pr₁*ω_- + pr₂*ω_+`. Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.SmoothTwoForm.twistedProd σ τ` is `pr₁*σ − pr₂*τ` on `M × N`; with `σ = ω_-` and `τ = ω_+` it is the negative of the form used here, and has the same Lagrangian submanifolds. The geometric composite of `L_01 ⊂ M_0⁻ × M_1` and `L_12 ⊂ M_1⁻ × M_2` is the image `L_02` of the fiber product `L_01 ×_(M_1) L_12` in `M_0⁻ × M_2`. A bordism from `X_0` to `X_1` followed by one from `X_1` to `X_2` composes to a bordism from `X_0` to `X_2`; functors compose as maps, `GF = G∘F` applying `F` first.
5. **Gradings and signs.** `A∞` operations are cohomological: the differential has degree one and `|m_k| = 2 − k`, read modulo four on the `ℤ/4`-graded categories of Layer 4. Inputs are in Keller's order `(a_k,…,a₁)`, with `m₂(g,f) = g∘f`, as for `TauCeti.AInfinityCategory`. Tensor products of maps carry the Koszul sign `(f⊗g)(x⊗y) = (−1)^(|g||x|) f(x)⊗g(y)` (DGAInfinity, Standing conventions). A grading of a Lagrangian is a lift to a chosen fourfold Maslov cover.
6. **Determinant lines.** The determinant line of a Fredholm operator `D` is `det(coker D)*⊗det(ker D)`, of parity `ind D mod 2`; exchanging two entire determinant blocks costs `(−1)^(ind D₁·ind D₂)`. HeegaardFloer F0.0 orders its fibers `det(ker D)⊗det(coker D)*`; §2.5 constructs the comparison of the two orders.
7. **Bordisms.** A bordism is compact, connected, oriented and smooth, with its boundary parametrized by `X_-⁻ ⊔ X_+`. Elementary bordisms are those of Wehrheim–Woodward Definition 2.2.1(b): bordisms carrying a Morse function adapted to the two ends, with connected level sets and at most one critical point; with none it is a cylinder, and a single critical point has index one or two (Remark 2.2.4). Compression bodies and Heegaard splittings are those of Definition 2.2.6. Cerf decompositions have connected intermediate surfaces; no zero- or three-handle and no disconnected surface occurs.
8. **Labels and names.** Other roadmaps cite Layer `k` of this roadmap as GQk; §2.2 and §2.3 are GQ2a and GQ2b, and §§2.4–2.6 are GQ2c. `Suggested.lean` declares, in `TauCetiRoadmap.GaugeQuiltFieldTheory`, the definitions `relationSet` and `relationModuli` and the theorems `relationModuli_genus_one`, `relationSet_stabilizer_central` and `relationModuli_simplyConnected`. Every other target is named in prose.

## Layer 0: the decorated connected bordism category

### 0.1 Decorated surfaces and decorated bordisms

Define the decorated connected bordism category for the fixed `(r,d)`. An object is a decorated surface `(X, P, Λ, δ, θ)`: a closed connected oriented smooth surface `X`, a rank-`r` Hermitian bundle `P → X`, a Hermitian line bundle `Λ` of degree `d` (the determinant line) with a unitary connection `δ`, and the selected determinant identification `θ : det P ≅ Λ`. A morphism from `(X_-, P_-, Λ_-, δ_-, θ_-)` to `(X_+, P_+, Λ_+, δ_+, θ_+)` is an equivalence class of decorated bordisms: a compact connected oriented smooth three-manifold `Y` with an orientation-preserving parametrization `∂Y ≅ X_-⁻ ⊔ X_+` of its incoming and outgoing boundary, a rank-`r` Hermitian bundle `Q → Y`, a Hermitian line bundle `Λ_Q` with a unitary connection `δ_Q` and an identification `θ_Q : det Q ≅ Λ_Q`, together with isomorphisms over the boundary parametrization identifying the restriction of `(Q, Λ_Q, δ_Q, θ_Q)` with the exact boundary data `(P_±, Λ_±, δ_±, θ_±)`. Two decorated bordisms are equivalent when a diffeomorphism compatible with the boundary parametrizations is covered by bundle isomorphisms preserving the determinant lines, their connections and identifications, and commuting with the boundary identifications. Equal boundary degrees alone do not identify the bundles or their relative boundary identifications. Construct composition by gluing along collars, the bundles, determinant lines and connections being glued through the boundary identifications, and the identity of a decorated surface as the cylinder `[0,1] × X` with the pulled-back data. Equivalence, collars and gluing preserve all these data. Prove that composition is well defined on equivalence classes, independent of the collars, associative and unital, and construct the diffeomorphism laws (Wehrheim–Woodward, *Floer field theory for coprime rank and degree*, Definitions 2.2.1 and 2.2.6 for the elementary and Heegaard bordisms; the bundle decorations are added here).

- Composition of equivalence classes of decorated bordisms is associative, and the product cylinders `[0,1] × X` with the pulled-back data are two-sided identities.
- A determinant-preserving diffeomorphism of decorated surfaces, that is, an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism covered by a bundle isomorphism compatible with `Λ`, `δ` and `θ`, has a mapping cylinder, which is an invertible morphism; the mapping cylinder of the identity is the identity, and mapping cylinders compose as the diffeomorphisms do.
- Reversing the orientation of a decorated bordism and exchanging its ends gives a decorated bordism in the opposite direction; reversing twice gives back the original class.

*Needs:* GeometricTopology §1.1 (smooth collars) and §1.3 (gluing along the boundary); SmoothBundlesConnections B1–B3 (bundles, the determinant line, connections); Wehrheim–Woodward Definitions 2.2.1 and 2.2.6.

### Examples

- The two ends of a decorated bordism have the same degree: `⟨c₁(Λ_Q), [X_+]⟩ − ⟨c₁(Λ_Q), [X_-]⟩` is the pairing of `c₁(Λ_Q)` with the image of `[∂Y] = [X_+] − [X_-]` in `H₂(Y)`, which vanishes because `[∂Y]` is the boundary of the relative fundamental class of `Y`. So the category with fixed `d` is closed under composition, and a bordism between surfaces of different degrees carries no decoration.
- The identity of `(X, P, Λ, δ, θ)` is `[0,1] × X` with `pr*P`, `pr*Λ`, `pr*δ` and `pr*θ`, and the identity identifications at both ends.
- A compression body from a surface of genus `g` to one of genus `g + 1`, obtained by attaching a single one-handle, is an elementary bordism; a Heegaard splitting cuts a connected bordism into two compression bodies along a connected Heegaard surface (Wehrheim–Woodward Definition 2.2.6).

### Dependencies

GeometricTopology §§1.1 and 1.3 (collars, gluing along the boundary). SmoothBundlesConnections B1–B3 (Hermitian bundles, determinant lines, unitary connections). Mathlib's smooth manifolds with corners.

## Layer 1: fixed determinant and coprime surface moduli

### 1.1 The fixed-determinant moduli and the adapter from R5

Consume HamiltonianReduction R5 for closed-surface connections, Sobolev gauges, central curvature, the smooth and rough quotient topologies and holonomy. Use the conventions above: anti-Hermitian matrices, covariant derivative `d + A`, the left action `g·A = gAg⁻¹ − (dg)g⁻¹`, on trace-free matrices the pairing `B(ξ,η) = −tr(ξη)/(4π²)` and the reduced form induced by `Ω(a,b) = ∫_X B(a∧b)`, and the determinant convention `c₁(det P) = [iF_δ/(2π)]`, so that the fixed-determinant equation is `F_A = (F_δ/r)·I_r`. R5 is stated for a fixed but arbitrary positive definite `Ad`-invariant inner product; construct the adapter that applies it with `B`, including its moment-map sign, curvature, Chern–Weil and integral-class normalizations.

Define `M(P,δ)` as the space of central-curvature unitary connections on `P` whose induced connection on the determinant line is exactly `δ` (through `θ`), modulo determinant-one unitary gauge transformations. Keep three objects distinct: the ineffective finite center (the constant gauge transformations in `μ_r·I_r`, which act trivially), the effective quotient group, and the stabilizers of individual connections.

- The constant central gauge transformations `μ_r·I_r` act trivially on the fixed-determinant central-curvature connections; for `g ≥ 1`, where these connections exist and have central stabilizers, they form the global ineffective kernel of the determinant-one gauge group.
- At a connection whose trace-free stabilizer Lie algebra vanishes, the tangent space of `M(P,δ)` is the first cohomology `H¹_A` of the trace-free deformation complex `Ω⁰(su(P)) → Ω¹(su(P)) → Ω²(su(P))` with both maps `d_A`, and `Ω` is nondegenerate on it.

*Needs:* HamiltonianReduction R5 (the surface quotient, moment map, central-curvature level and stabilizers); SmoothBundlesConnections B3–B4 (connections, curvature, the gauge group).

### 1.2 The twisted relation model

Prove the comparison of `M(P,δ)` with the twisted `SU(r)` relation model

    z_(r,d) = exp(2πi d/r)·I_r,    [A_1,B_1]⋯[A_g,B_g] = z_(r,d).

Choose the punctured-surface generators so that the product of commutators represents the positively oriented loop enclosing the removed disc, and prove the sign dictionary with parallel transport for `d + A`. Include the determinant transport and the lift choices in the comparison. Do not replace it by an ordinary flat `U(r)` character variety when `d ≠ 0`, and do not assert that an action by `H¹(X;μ_r)` is free on every coarse quotient. Construct the finite-quotient and groupoid comparisons with their isotropy.

The representation model is stated on native carriers. For a complex number `ζ` define `relationSet r ζ g` as the set of tuples `(A_1,B_1,…,A_g,B_g)` in Mathlib's `Matrix.specialUnitaryGroup (Fin r) ℂ` whose ordered product of group commutators (`⁅a,b⁆ = a*b*a⁻¹*b⁻¹`, Mathlib's `commutatorElement`) is the scalar matrix `ζ·1`, and `relationModuli r ζ g` as its quotient by simultaneous conjugation: the orbit relation `MulAction.orbitRel` of `ConjAct` on the ambient tuples, pulled back to the subtype, with the quotient topology. With `ζ = exp(2πi d/r)` the hypothesis `IsPrimitiveRoot ζ r` is exactly `gcd(r,d) = 1`, so primitive `r`-th roots cover every coprime degree. Prove that `M(P,δ)` is homeomorphic to `relationModuli r ζ g` for this `ζ`, through the holonomies of a determinant connection whose curvature is concentrated near a base point (Wehrheim–Woodward Remark 3.3.7, eq. (31)).

- `relationSet r ζ g` is a closed, hence compact, subset of `(SU(r) × SU(r))^g` and is invariant under simultaneous conjugation; `relationModuli r ζ g` is compact and Hausdorff.
- For `r ≥ 1` and `g = 0`, `relationSet r ζ 0` is the one-point space when `ζ = 1` and is empty otherwise.
- The map `(A_j,B_j)_j ↦ (B_(g+1−j), A_(g+1−j))_j` inverts the ordered product of commutators, so it is a conjugation-equivariant homeomorphism from `relationSet r ζ g` onto `relationSet r ζ⁻¹ g`. It relates the two models that the sign dictionary distinguishes; reversing the orientation of `X` replaces `d` by `−d`, hence `ζ` by `ζ⁻¹`.
- For central elements `c_j, c'_j ∈ μ_r·1`, `(A_j,B_j) ↦ (c_jA_j, c'_jB_j)` preserves `relationSet r ζ g` and commutes with conjugation; this is the action of `H¹(X;μ_r)` on the model, which need not be free on `relationModuli r ζ g` (fourth Check below).
- For `r = 1` the group `SU(1)` is trivial, the only primitive first root of unity is `1`, and `relationModuli 1 1 g` is a point for every `g`.

For `r ≥ 1` and `IsPrimitiveRoot ζ r`, prove:

- `relationModuli_genus_one`: the genus-one relation `[A,B] = ζ·1` has a solution, unique up to simultaneous `SU(r)`-conjugation (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 3.3.3(c)). A route: `A^r` and `B^r` are scalars, the eigenvalues of `A` form one coset of `μ_r` fixed by `det A = 1`, and the irreducible representation of the finite Heisenberg group with central character `ζ` is unique; clock and shift matrices rescaled into `SU(r)` give existence.
- `relationSet_stabilizer_central`: for every genus `g` (including `g = 0`, where the relation set is empty when `r ≥ 2`), an element `u` of `SU(r)` commuting with every entry of a solution lies in the center. Route: an eigenspace `V` of `u`, of dimension `m`, is preserved by all `A_j, B_j`; the determinant of the restricted commutator product is `1 = ζ^m`, so `r ∣ m` and `V = ℂ^r`. This is the finite-group form of the parallel-subbundle exclusion of Wehrheim–Woodward, proof of Theorem 3.3.3, and gives freeness of the effective `PSU(r)` action of §2.2.
- `relationModuli_simplyConnected`: for every genus `g ≥ 1`, `relationModuli r ζ g` is simply connected (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 3.3.3, final clause, whose proof cites Atiyah–Bott Theorem 9.12). Genus one and rank one give a point. A route passes through the comparison with `M(P,δ)` and the Atiyah–Bott stratification.

**Checks.**

- `r = 2`, `ζ = −1`: `A = diag(i,−i)` and `B` the rotation matrix with rows `(0,1)`, `(−1,0)` lie in `SU(2)` and satisfy `ABA⁻¹B⁻¹ = −1`; the untwisted relation `[A,B] = 1` would instead admit the non-free point `(1,1)`, whose stabilizer is all of `SU(2)`.
- `g = 0`, `r = 2`, `ζ = −1`: the empty product is `1 ≠ −1`, so `relationSet 2 (−1) 0` is empty and the sphere moduli is empty; `r = 1`, `ζ = 1` gives a point for every `g`.
- Non-coprime `r = 2`, `ζ = 1`, `g = 1`: commuting pairs of diagonal matrices are fixed by the non-central torus, and the quotient is the pillowcase `(T×T)/(ℤ/2)`, a 2-sphere, not a point; this is why the primitive-root hypothesis is carried by all three statements.
- `r = 2`, `ζ = −1`, `g = 1`, the pair of the first Check: `BAB⁻¹ = −A` and `ABA⁻¹ = −B`, so every sign change `(A,B) ↦ (±A,±B)` stays in the conjugation orbit. The residual `H¹(X;ℤ/2)` action on the rank-two odd moduli of §1.6 is therefore not free.
- Existence for every `r`: the clock matrix `diag(1, ζ, …, ζ^(r−1))` and the cyclic shift satisfy `CSC⁻¹S⁻¹ = ζ·1` (or `ζ⁻¹·1` with the other cyclic direction), and dividing each by an `r`-th root of its determinant puts them in `SU(r)` without changing the commutator; for `r = 2` this recovers the pair of the first Check up to conjugation. A version requiring `A` and `B` to be the unscaled clock and shift would fail for even `r`, where their determinants are `−1`.

*Needs:* Mathlib `Matrix.specialUnitaryGroup`, `commutatorElement`, `ConjAct`, `MulAction.orbitRel`, `IsPrimitiveRoot`, `Subgroup.center`, `SimplyConnectedSpace` and the quotient topology; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.Matrix.isCompact_specialUnitaryGroup`; for the comparison and the simple connectivity, §1.1, HamiltonianReduction R5 (continuous holonomy) and SmoothBundlesConnections B5 (parallel transport and the flat-holonomy correspondence); Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 3.3.3 and Remark 3.3.7.

### 1.3 The smooth symplectic quotient in rank at least two

For `r ≥ 2`, prove that coprimality excludes proper parallel subbundles and gives trace-free stabilizer Lie algebra zero: a parallel splitting into summands of ranks `k` and `r − k` with `0 < k < r` would give the first summand the non-integral degree `kd/r` (Wehrheim–Woodward, proof of Theorem 3.3.3). Use surface duality to obtain the vanishing of the trace-free obstruction space, then construct the compact smooth symplectic quotient and its comparison with the representation model of §1.2. Include all genera: on `S²` the moduli is empty; on `T²` it is a point; for `g ≥ 2` prove compactness, connectedness, simple connectivity and dimension `(2g−2)(r²−1)` (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 3.3.3(a)–(c) and its final clause). The symplectic form is a smooth two-form satisfying Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.SmoothTwoForm.IsSymplectic` on the smooth quotient. Establish even Chern evaluations, `⟨c₁(M(P,δ)), H₂(M(P,δ))⟩ ⊂ 2ℤ`, and

    c₁(M(P,δ)) = 2r[Ω],    [Ω] = τ_WW·c₁,    τ_WW = 1/(2r).

Wehrheim–Woodward display their form with the basic inner product `⟨ξ,ζ⟩ = −Tr(ξζ)` and no `1/(4π²)` (Remark 3.2.2(b) and eq. (26)); so `Ω = ω_WW/(4π²)`, and `c₁ = 2r[Ω]` is the statement that holds, for the Atiyah–Bott normalization `Ω` (Theorem 3.3.3(a), whose monotonicity clause its proof takes from Proposition 3.3.2 with the dual Coxeter number `c = r` of `SU(r)`; the proof of Proposition 3.3.2 cites the Drezet–Narasimhan theorem for `c₁ = 2c[ω]`). The Checks below witness the constant.

**Checks.**

- Rank one, the normalization witness: for flat `U(1)` connections `A = iα` on a surface of genus `g`, with the same formula `B(ξ,η) = −ξη/(4π²)` on `u(1) = iℝ`, the gauge action is `α ↦ α − dθ` with `θ : X → ℝ/2πℤ`, so the flat moduli is the Jacobian `H¹(X;ℝ)/2πH¹(X;ℤ)`. On lattice generators `2πe_j`, `2πf_j` with `∫_X e_j∧f_j = 1` the form `Ω` takes the value `(1/(4π²))·(2π)²·1 = 1`, an integral class. The form `∫_X(−tr)(a∧b)` without the `1/(4π²)` takes the value `4π²` there, so it is `4π²` times an integral class and no relation `c₁ = 2r[ω]` with integral `c₁` can hold for it.
- `r = 2`: `α`, the generator of `H²(N_g;ℤ)`, is twice the symplectic class and `c₁(TN_g) = 2α` (Scaduto–Stoffregen, *The cohomology of rank two stable bundle moduli*, introduction and §4.2), so `c₁ = 4[Ω] = 2r[Ω]` and `τ_WW = 1/4`; the wrong normalization `B = −tr` would read `c₁ = (1/π²)[ω]`.
- Dimension by counting the relation model: `2g` matrices in `SU(r)` give `2g(r²−1)` parameters, the relation in `SU(r)` removes `r²−1` and the free effective `PSU(r)` action removes `r²−1`, leaving `(2g−2)(r²−1)`; for `r = 3`, `g = 2` this is `16`. With `U(r)` holonomies the relation still imposes only `r²−1` conditions, a product of commutators having determinant one, and the count gives `2g·r² − 2(r²−1) = (2g−2)(r²−1) + 2g`; the extra `2g` is the torus of determinant connections, which fixing `δ` removes, so a definition without the determinant condition has the wrong dimension.

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.2; HamiltonianReduction R5 (surface duality `H²_A ≅ (H⁰_A)*`, the Kuranishi thickening at the central-curvature level, the splitting `k = z ⊕ [k,k]`, finite residual stabilizers); CharacteristicClasses C3.2 (Chern classes); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.SmoothTwoForm.IsSymplectic`; Wehrheim–Woodward Remark 3.2.2(b), eq. (26), Proposition 3.3.2 and Theorem 3.3.3; Scaduto–Stoffregen, *The cohomology of rank two stable bundle moduli*, §4.2.

For `r = 1`, prove directly that the fixed-determinant moduli is a point for every genus and every integer `d`, including zero and negative degrees. The point has no nonconstant disc classes; Maslov divisibility, rather than existence of a class of Maslov number four, is the uniform grading statement.

### 1.5 Naturality and line tensoring

Construct naturality under determinant-preserving surface diffeomorphisms and bundle identifications: such a diffeomorphism, covered by a bundle isomorphism compatible with the determinant data, induces a symplectomorphism of the moduli by pullback, and two bundle isomorphisms covering the same diffeomorphism induce the same one (Wehrheim–Woodward Lemma 3.3.6 for the undecorated form). For a line bundle with connection `(L,a)` of degree `ℓ`, construct the tensoring comparison `P ↦ P⊗L`, `d ↦ d + rℓ`, `δ ↦ δ + r a`. Prove preservation of the trace-free connection, the deformation complex, the projective holonomy, the central relation and the symplectic form. This reduces the rank-`r ≥ 2` surface results to a positive coprime residue `1 ≤ d′ ≤ r−1`. To transport the decorated bordism data, construct the actual line-bundle extensions over `Q`, the relative determinant identifications and their compatibility with gluing (Layer 0); choices on boundary surfaces alone do not establish that comparison. Their coherence with the Cerf moves is part of §5.1.

**Checks.**

- The twist `z_(r,d) = exp(2πi d/r)·I_r` depends only on `d mod r`, and tensoring with a line of degree `ℓ` shifts `d` by `rℓ`, so the relation model is unchanged: for `r = 3`, `d = 5`, `ℓ = −1` gives `d′ = 2` with the same `ζ = exp(4πi/3)`. A rule shifting the degree by `ℓ` instead would change `ζ` (`d = 4` gives `exp(2πi/3)`) and could leave the coprime range (`d = 3`).

## Layer 3: connected Cerf comparison

### 3.1 Connected Cerf decompositions and moves

Construct connected Cerf decompositions of decorated connected bordisms, with connected intermediate surfaces, elementary one- and two-handle pieces and cylinders (Wehrheim–Woodward Definitions 2.2.1 and 2.2.2; existence through Morse data, Lemma 2.2.5). Enumerate the allowed critical-point cancellation, cylinder insertion/removal and critical-point reordering moves (Wehrheim–Woodward Definition 2.2.9). Prove the connected Cerf theorem in the decorated setting: any two connected Cerf decompositions of a decorated connected bordism are related, up to equivalence, by a finite sequence of these moves (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 2.2.11 and the remarks on its proof).

The comparison of two decompositions rests on two theorems about a compact triad `(W; V₀, V₁)` of dimension `m` and the smooth functions `f : W → [0,1]` with `f⁻¹(0) = V₀`, `f⁻¹(1) = V₁` and no critical point on `∂W`, as in GeometricTopology §1.3.1. Both are targets here.

- *One-parameter Cerf theory.* The excellent functions, Morse with pairwise distinct critical values, form an open dense set. The functions of codimension one are the birth functions, with one birth point (a critical point of normal form `−x₁² − ⋯ − x_k² + x_(k+1)³ + x_(k+2)² + ⋯ + x_m²`) and otherwise Morse critical points at distinct values, and the crossing functions, Morse with exactly two critical values equal; together they form a submanifold of codimension one of the open set of functions of codimension at most one, the excellent functions together with these. Paths that are excellent except at finitely many times, where they cross this submanifold transversally, are dense among all paths in the `C⁰` topology, so any two excellent functions are joined by such a generic path; at each exceptional time a cancelling pair of critical points of indices `k` and `k + 1` is born or dies, or two critical values cross (Cerf, *La stratification naturelle des espaces de fonctions différentiables réelles et le théorème de la pseudo-isotopie*, Chapter I, §3.1, properties 1° and 2°, p. 24; Gay–Kirby, *Indefinite Morse 2-functions; broken fibrations and generalizations*, Definition 2.3, for the local models of the two events). A route: apply in the interior of `W`, where every critical point lies, the birth–death analysis of HeegaardFloer §1.5 (M.5), made there on closed manifolds by localized perturbations in jet charts, and add transversality of pairs of critical values for the crossings.
- *Connected level sets.* If `W` is connected and oriented, `m ≥ 3`, and `V₀`, `V₁` are nonempty, any two excellent functions on the triad whose level sets are all connected are joined by a generic path along which every function has connected level sets (Gay–Kirby, Theorem 1.4, the case of maps to `[0,1]`). Here connected level sets exclude local extrema, so these functions are indefinite in Gay–Kirby's sense; a generic path of the first theorem between two such functions need not keep the level sets connected (Wehrheim–Woodward, remarks on the proof of Theorem 2.2.11). A route, Gay–Kirby's for this case: homotope each function, keeping its level sets connected, to an ordered one, whose critical values increase with the index (Lemma 4.10, Definition 2.12), then join the two ordered functions by an ordered indefinite generic homotopy (Theorem 4.5), whose level sets are connected because the ends are connected and `m ≥ 3` (Lemma 2.13 and the remark before Theorem 4.5).

A route to the connected Cerf theorem (Wehrheim–Woodward, remarks on the proof of Theorem 2.2.11): every Cerf decomposition comes from a Morse datum (Remark 2.2.3); join the functions of two Morse data by a path as in the second theorem; between consecutive exceptional times the decompositions are equivalent, by the flow of a vector field tangent to smoothly moving separating levels; across a crossing they differ by a critical-point switch, and across a birth or death by a critical-point cancellation or creation; with the function fixed, moving the separating levels inserts or removes cylinders.

Lift the moves to comparisons of the decorated bundles and determinant connections, preserving the boundary parametrizations and the central relation. Prove the extension principle for the decorated category: an assignment of objects to decorated surfaces and of morphisms to elementary decorated bordisms, sending the product cylinder `[0,1] × X` with the pulled-back data, the identity of §0.1, to the identity and satisfying the Cerf relations for cylindrical composites, critical-point switches and composites with a cylinder, extends uniquely to a functor on the decorated connected bordism category (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 2.2.13).

*Needs:* §0.1; GeometricTopology §1.3 (handle attachment) and §1.3.1 (Morse functions on compact manifolds with boundary); HeegaardFloer §1.5 (M.5: the one-parameter Cerf theorem and the birth–death normal form on closed manifolds); Wehrheim–Woodward Definitions 2.2.1, 2.2.2 and 2.2.9, Remark 2.2.3, Lemma 2.2.5 and Theorems 2.2.11 and 2.2.13; Cerf, Chapter I, §3.1; Gay–Kirby, Definitions 2.2, 2.3 and 2.12, Lemmas 2.13 and 4.10 and Theorems 1.4 and 4.5.

## Layer 5: the connected field theory

### 5.1 The connected field theory

The theorem target of Layers 1–4 is a connected functor for the fixed-`(r,d)`, determinant-preserving bundle-decorated (2+1)-bordism category of Layer 0, valued in the integral uncurved `A∞(4)/S` of §4.4 (Wehrheim–Woodward Theorem 4.2.1, stated there for coprime positive `r` and `d` and for functors commuting strictly with the shifts). It sends a decorated surface to the zero-extended generalized quilt category of `M(P,δ)` and an elementary decorated bordism to the class of the functor of its correspondence. All integer coprime degrees, rank-one point values and rank-`r ≥ 2` empty-sphere values are included. Descent between coherently tensor-related determinant decorations uses the line extensions of §1.5; prove their coherence with gluing and with the Cerf moves of §3.1, so that tensoring by a line bundle with connection over the bordism intertwines the two field theories. A disconnected symmetric-monoidal extension or point-level fully extended field theory is not part of this connected theorem.

*Needs:* Layer 0; §1.5 (line extensions); Layers 1–4 in full, in particular §3.1 (the extension principle) and §4.5 (the two functor levels); Wehrheim–Woodward Theorems 3.5.1 and 4.2.1.

### Examples

- Rank one: every surface value is a point, every elementary correspondence is the point correspondence, and the field theory sends every decorated bordism to the class of the identity functor of the zero-extended point-quilt category.
- For `r ≥ 2`, every decorated bordism with a sphere end goes to the zero functor, and a bordism from the sphere to itself goes to the identity of the category with a single formal zero object.
- For `r = 2`, `d = 1` and a compression body from genus one to genus two, the field theory sends the bordism to the class of the functor of `L_1`, which takes the length-zero object of `C(pt)` to the Lagrangian three-sphere `L_1 ⊂ N_2`.

### Dependencies

Layers 0–4.

## Scope and ownership

**This roadmap owns** the decorated connected bordism category (Layer 0); the fixed-determinant coprime specialization of the surface gauge theory and its exact normalizations for every coprime rank and degree, with the twisted relation model, the rank-one point values, naturality and line tensoring, and the rank-two odd-degree moduli with its `H¹(X;ℤ/2)` action and its `4π²` comparison with the unnormalized pairing (Layer 1); the three-dimensional projective/determinant lift, the boundary restriction application and the elementary correspondences with their relative-spin, orientation, grading and monotonicity data (Layer 2); the connected Cerf comparison, including one-parameter Cerf theory on compact triads with its connected-level-set form, the connected Cerf theorem and the extension principle for the decorated category (Layer 3); generalized quilts, their analytic and coherent applications, the zero extension and the shift target `A∞(4)/S` (Layer 4); and the connected field theory (Layer 5).

**Owned elsewhere and consumed here.**

- *The generic surface gauge construction*: HamiltonianReduction R5, which Layer 1 specializes and does not restate.
- *Smooth transport, cochains and duality*: SmoothBundlesConnections, DifferentialGeometry and AlgebraicTopology own the generic smooth-transport, de Rham, cochain and duality floors; Layer 2 owns the three-dimensional projective/determinant lift and the boundary restriction application.
- *Characteristic classes and spin lifts*: CharacteristicClasses C3.2 and SpinGeometry G4.
- *Collars, gluing and Morse functions on manifolds with boundary*: GeometricTopology Layer 1. The handle calculus there is not a target, and the connected Cerf theory of decorated bordisms is §3.1 here.
- *`A∞` carriers*: Tau Ceti has the `ℤ`-graded `A∞` categories, functors, their composition, strict and cohomological unit predicates, the `H⁰` homotopy category and unital functors on it; it has no transformations between `A∞` functors (only reduced transformations between morphisms of `A∞` algebras), no `A∞` category of functors and no `ℤ/4`-grading. DGAInfinity owns the remaining generic `A∞` algebra.
- *Symplectic carriers*: Tau Ceti has smooth symplectic two-forms, their twisted products and Lagrangian maps; the smooth structure on the moduli spaces is built here.
- *One-parameter Cerf theory on closed manifolds*: HeegaardFloer §1.5 (M.5); its version on compact triads, with crossings and connected level sets, is §3.1 here.
- *Ordinary elliptic, pseudoholomorphic and polygon theory*: HeegaardFloer and FukayaTheory; every quilted domain, seam condition and generalized-object construction is Layer 4's.
- *Matrix groups and quotients*: Mathlib has the special unitary group, commutators, the conjugation action, orbit relations, primitive roots and the quotient topology; Tau Ceti has the compactness of `SU(r)`.

**The instanton boundary.** InstantonFloerTheory owns the admissible integral and framed three-dimensional instanton theories: their Chern–Simons critical connections, finite-energy ASD trajectories, perturbations, reducible treatment, Fredholm, compactification, gluing and orientation theory, differential, continuation, naturality and four-dimensional cobordism maps. Its admissible Atiyah–Floer comparison, IF9–IF10, owns the mixed ASD/Cauchy–Riemann bridge with its splitting, bundle and embeddedness hypotheses, the perturbed flat moduli of its three-manifolds with boundary and their restriction maps, and the coherently oriented pair-strip complex. That comparison takes from this roadmap only the rank-two odd-degree surface value of §1.6, with the normalization `−∫_X tr₂(a∧b) = 4π²·Ω`; InstantonFloerTheory constructs its `SO(3)` adapter and Hodge-star complex structure on it. The elementary correspondences, generalized-quilt categories and connected sewing theorem of Layers 2–5 are neither a prerequisite nor a conclusion of that comparison, and none of them identifies the quilt theory with instanton Floer homology. No instanton construction is a prerequisite for the connected sewing theorem.

**Not in scope.** A disconnected symmetric-monoidal extension, a point-level fully extended field theory, disconnected intermediate surfaces and zero- or three-handle pieces.
