# Roadmap: the Galois action on étale cohomology of geometric fibres

**Summary.** For a field `k` and a scheme `X/k`, and for a complete non-archimedean field `K` and a rigid `K`-space `X`, the étale cohomology of the geometric fibre carries an action of the absolute Galois group. Every arithmetic roadmap that writes `H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` "with its Galois action" uses this action, and most leave it implicit. This roadmap constructs the action explicitly, as `ρ(σ) = c_σ ∘ σ_X^*`, and supplies the rules that make it usable. It starts from Mathlib's absolute Galois group, spectral norm and uniform completion, the analytic étale sites of AdicEtaleGeometry, the analytic sheaf theory of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology and the scheme sheaf theory of SchemeAndStackFoundations and EtaleCohomologyFoundations. Its end theorems are:

- the identification of `G_K` with the continuous `K`-automorphisms of the completed algebraic closure `C`, with a jointly continuous action, for every complete non-archimedean `K` of every characteristic;
- the action itself, as a left action by `Λ`-linear automorphisms, on étale and pro-étale cohomology, with its functoriality (pullbacks, sheaf maps, connecting maps, cup products for commutative coefficients, restriction to open subgroups, change of algebraic closure);
- discreteness, the dictionary between higher direct images to the base point and Galois modules, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence with its `E_2` term identified, and agreement with the scheme-side adic action and its Frobenius conventions;
- the `ℓ`-adic and rational groups as continuous representations;
- equivariant twisting, the equivariant comparison with analytifications (for proper schemes with torsion coefficients, and for schemes of finite type with constructible coefficients of order invertible in `K`), and the passage from finiteness to finitely generated and finite-dimensional continuous representations.

`Suggested.lean` is the acceptance test. It states the first end theorem, which is statable at the pins: it needs only the completed algebraic closure, defined there as `CompletedAlgClosure K`, the uniform completion of `SpectralAlgClosure K` (Mathlib's `AlgebraicClosure K` carrying the spectral norm). Its module docstring lists the end theorems that are not statable at the pins and the carrier each lacks.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns:

## Conventions

- `K` is a field complete for a nontrivial non-archimedean absolute value `|·|` of rank one, of any characteristic and with any value group: a Mathlib `NontriviallyNormedField` with `CompleteSpace K` and `IsUltrametricDist K`. `𝒪_K` is its closed unit ball. Archimedean and trivially valued fields are excluded.
- `K̄` is Mathlib's `AlgebraicClosure K` with the spectral norm `spectralNorm.normedField K K̄`; by `spectralNorm_unique` this is the unique power-multiplicative extension of `|·|`. In `Suggested.lean` this normed field is `SpectralAlgClosure K`, a type synonym of `AlgebraicClosure K` whose normed-field and normed-algebra structures are `spectralNorm.normedField` and `spectralNorm.normedAlgebra`; the synonym keeps the spectral norm from becoming an instance on `AlgebraicClosure K` itself. `K^sep` is Mathlib's `SeparableClosure K ⊆ K̄`. `C` is `UniformSpace.Completion K̄`, written `CompletedAlgClosure K` in `Suggested.lean`, with closed unit ball `𝒪_C` and maximal ideal `𝔪_C`.
- `G_K` is Mathlib's `Field.absoluteGaloisGroup K = K̄ ≃ₐ[K] K̄` with the Krull topology. Restriction to `K^sep` is an isomorphism of topological groups onto `Gal(K^sep/K)`, because `K̄/K^sep` is purely inseparable (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.absoluteGaloisGroupRestrictEquiv`), and `G_K` is compact (the Tau Ceti instance of Exact supplier contracts). For a finite extension `L ⊆ K̄` of `K`, `G_L ⊆ G_K` is the open subgroup fixing `L`; `L` is complete, and `K̄` is an algebraic closure of `L` with the same norm, so `C` is also the completed algebraic closure of `L`.
- A rigid `K`-space is an adic space locally of finite type over `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (huber1994, §§1–3; AdicSpacesPartII §R0.6); Tate's rigid spaces, AdicSpacesPartII §R1.1 (*rigid-analytic-space*), are carried to these by Huber's functor `r_K` of §R1.7 (*rigid-adic-comparison-functor*), with `r_K(Sp A) = Spa(A, A°)`. It is locally strongly noetherian, hence locally noetherian and locally strongly sheafy (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.1). For a complete extension `L` of `K` inside `C` (a finite `L ⊆ K̄`, or `L = C`) write `X_L := X ×_{Spa(K,𝒪_K)} Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` (AdicSpacesPartII §R0.7, *fibre-products-existence*, case (a)), `pr_L : X_L → X`, and `ℱ_L := pr_L^{-1} ℱ`, the inverse image of abelian sheaves (never the module pullback `𝒪_{X_L} ⊗ pr_L^{-1}(−)`).
- Sheaves of `Λ`-modules on `X_ét`, `RΓ`, `R^q f_*` and `D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` are those of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1, for an arbitrary ring `Λ`. Torsion and invertibility hypotheses appear only where a statement needs them. On schemes the corresponding objects are those of SchemeAndStackFoundations §§2.1–2.2.
- The unbounded derived category `D(X_ét, Λ)`, with its derived global sections and inverse images, is that of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.1–0.2 and §0.4, for an arbitrary ring `Λ`. It contains `D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` as the full subcategory of cohomologically bounded-below objects, on which its derived functors are those of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1 (resp. SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.1) (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4). Derived tensor products `⊗^L_Λ` and cup products are formed only for commutative `Λ` (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3). For `M, N ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` the tensor `M ⊗^L_Λ N` lies in `D(X_ét, Λ)` and need not lie in `D⁺`; it lies in `D⁺` when one factor has finite Tor amplitude (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3).
- **Direction of the action.** For `σ ∈ G_K` let `σ̂ : C → C` be its continuous extension (§0.2) and `σ_X := id_X × Spa(σ̂) : X_C → X_C`. `Spa` is contravariant, so `(στ)_X = τ_X ∘ σ_X`: `σ ↦ σ_X` is a right action on `X_C`. Pullback is contravariant again, so `ρ(σ) := c_σ ∘ σ_X^*` is a left action on cohomology. The Galois action on the stalk of a sheaf on `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét` at the geometric point `ξ_C : Spa(C, 𝒪_C) → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` is the rule of stacks03N1, Tag 03QW (stacks03N1 line 3966): `σ` sends an étale neighbourhood `(V, v̄)` to `(V, v̄ ∘ Spa(σ̂))`. This is the only left action that the automorphisms `Spa(σ̂)` induce. Witness: on `H⁰(Spa(C, 𝒪_C), μ_n) = μ_n(C)` and on the stalk `(μ_n)_{ξ_C} = μ_n(C)`, `σ` acts by `ζ ↦ σ(ζ)` (stacks03N1, Example 59.3, line 4241). For `K = ℚ_p`, `ℓ ≠ p` and an arithmetic Frobenius lift `φ`, `ρ(φ)` sends `ζ ∈ μ_ℓ(C)` to `ζ^p`.
- **Twists.** For `n` invertible in `K` and a `ℤ/n`-algebra `Λ`, `Λ(j)` is the twist of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4 (on schemes, EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.3), for all `j ∈ ℤ`. On representations, `ℤ_ℓ(j)` is the Tate twist of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.2, with `σ` acting on `ℤ_ℓ(1) = lim μ_{ℓ^n}(K̄)` by `ζ ↦ σ(ζ)`. An arithmetic Frobenius lift acts on `ℤ_ℓ(1)` by `q`, the cardinality of the residue field, and a geometric Frobenius by `q⁻¹` (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.IsArithFrobeniusLift.coe_localCyclotomicCharacter`, for a local field and `ℓ` prime to the residue characteristic). This is the normalisation of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.3, F.10.2 and F.4.6.
- **Adic coefficients.** `ℓ` is a prime. Twists `ℤ/ℓ^n(j)` with `j ≠ 0` require `ℓ` invertible in `K`; when `ℓ = char K` only `j = 0` occurs. When `char K = 0`, `ℓ` may equal the residue characteristic.
- **Schemes.** `k` is an arbitrary field, `k̄ = AlgebraicClosure k`, `k^sep = SeparableClosure k ⊆ k̄`, and `G_k = Field.absoluteGaloisGroup k` acts on `k^sep` by restriction. For a `k`-scheme `X`, `X_{k^sep} := X ×_k Spec k^sep` and `X_{k̄} := X ×_k Spec k̄`.
- Cohomological indexing: `H^i(M[r]) = H^{i+r}(M)`. Negative cohomological degrees of a sheaf vanish.
- Complexes of sheaves are written `M`, `N` (and `P` on a pro-étale site); the letters `K`, `L` denote fields only.

## How to read the build

Layer 0 is field theory close to Mathlib: the completed algebraic closure, the extension of Galois automorphisms with the end theorem `G_K ≅ Aut_cont(C/K)`, and the automorphisms of the geometric point. Layer 1 first constructs, for any automorphism `a` of a space `Y` over `X`, the induced automorphism of `H^i(Y, p^{-1}M)`, and then specialises it to `X_C → X` and to `X_{k^sep} → X`, with functoriality. Layer 2 proves that the action is discrete, identifies it with the module structure of higher direct images to the base point, identifies the Hochschild–Serre `E_2` term, and compares the scheme action with the scheme-side adic action. Layer 3 passes to `ℓ`-adic and rational coefficients and proves continuity. Layer 4 proves twisting, the comparison with analytifications of schemes, and the passage from finiteness to representations. Each layer uses only the layers before it and the suppliers named in its Dependencies.

## Layer 0: The Galois group and the completed algebraic closure

### 0.1 The completed algebraic closure

Define `SpectralAlgClosure K` as `AlgebraicClosure K` with the normed-field structure `spectralNorm.normedField K K̄` and the normed-algebra structure `spectralNorm.normedAlgebra K K̄`; the norm extends `|·|` (`spectralNorm_extends`) and is the unique power-multiplicative `K`-algebra norm on `K̄` (`spectralNorm_unique`). Define `CompletedAlgClosure K`, the field `C`, as `UniformSpace.Completion (SpectralAlgClosure K)`, a complete non-archimedean normed field and normed `K`-algebra, with `𝒪_C = {|x| ≤ 1}` and `𝔪_C = {|x| < 1}`. Prove:

- `K^sep` is dense in `K̄` and in `C`. For `a ∈ K̄` with `a^{p^e} = b ∈ K^sep` (`p = char K > 0`, `e ≥ 1`), the polynomials `X^{p^e} − tX − b` with `t ∈ K^×` have constant derivative `−t ≠ 0`, so they are separable and their roots lie in `K^sep`; as `|t| → 0` they have roots tending to `a` by the continuity of roots over `K̄` (`Polynomial.exists_roots_norm_sub_lt_of_norm_coeff_sub_lt`, applied to `X^{p^e} − b` and `X^{p^e} − tX − b`, which split in `K̄`). Hence `K^sep` is dense in `K̄`, and `K̄` is dense in `C`. In characteristic zero `K^sep = K̄`. (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6, line 1477, where `C` is introduced as the completion of `K^sep`.)
- `C` is algebraically closed (PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology §RD.0.33; in characteristic zero Mathlib's `IsAlgClosed.of_denseRange`). Consequently `(C, 𝒪_C)` is a geometric field pair in the sense of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §7.1, and the structure map `ξ_C : Spa(C, 𝒪_C) → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` is an `AdicSpace.GeometricPoint` (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.7).
- `Spa(C, 𝒪_C) ∼ lim_L Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` over the finite subextensions `L` of `K^sep/K`, as a colimit-presented tilde-limit (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.5): `colim_L 𝒪_L = 𝒪_{K^sep}`, its completion is `𝒪_C`, and finite-stage detection holds because `𝒪_L ∩ ϖ^n 𝒪_{K^sep} = ϖ^n 𝒪_L` for a pseudouniformiser `ϖ ∈ K`.

**Checks.**

- For `K = ℚ_p`, `C` is Mathlib's `PadicComplex p`: `PadicAlgCl p` is by definition `AlgebraicClosure ℚ_[p]` with the normed-field structure `spectralNorm.normedField`, so `CompletedAlgClosure ℚ_[p]` and `PadicComplex p` are the same type with the same normed-field structure, and algebraic closedness is Mathlib's `PadicComplex.isAlgClosed`.
- For `K = 𝔽_p((t))`, `K^sep ≠ K̄` (the root of `X^p − t` is not separable), `K̄` is not complete, and `C` is algebraically closed although `IsAlgClosed.of_denseRange` does not apply (`CharZero` fails). The root `t^{1/p}` is the limit of roots of `X^p − sX − t` as `s → 0`: a root `b_s` satisfies `(b_s − t^{1/p})^p = s b_s`, so `|b_s − t^{1/p}| = |s b_s|^{1/p} → 0` for bounded `b_s`.
- If `K` is algebraically closed then `K̄ = K = C` and `ξ_C` is the identity. A definition taking `C := K̄` without completing gives an incomplete field for `K = ℚ_p` (`[K̄ : K]` is infinite and `K̄` is not complete), on which `Spa` is not the geometric point of AdicEtaleGeometry A1.7.
- For `K = ℚ_p` the norm on `ℚ_p(√p) ⊆ K̄` gives `|√p| = p^{-1/2}`; a norm extending `|·|` that is not power-multiplicative (for instance `|a + b√p| = max(|a|, |b|)` on `ℚ_p(√p)`, which gives `|√p| = 1`) is excluded by uniqueness.

### 0.2 Galois automorphisms extend to C

For `σ ∈ G_K`, `σ` is an isometry of `K̄` (`spectralNorm_eq_of_equiv`), hence uniformly continuous; define `σ̂ := UniformSpace.Completion.map σ : C → C`. Prove (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6, page 36, line 1479):

- `σ̂` is a continuous `K`-algebra automorphism of `C` with continuous inverse `(σ⁻¹)^`, isometric, and the unique continuous map `C → C` extending `σ` (two continuous maps that agree on the dense subset `K̄` of the Hausdorff space `C` are equal, `UniformSpace.Completion.ext`); it preserves `𝒪_C` and `𝔪_C`.
- `(στ)^ = σ̂ ∘ τ̂` and `1̂ = id` (`map_comp`, `map_id`).
- **End theorem.** `σ ↦ σ̂` is a bijection from `G_K` onto the continuous `K`-algebra automorphisms of `C` (Mathlib's `C ≃A[K] C`), compatible with composition, so `G_K ≅ Aut_cont(C/K)` as groups; and the action map `G_K × C → C`, `(σ, x) ↦ σ̂(x)`, is jointly continuous for the Krull topology. This is `exists_galois_continuousAut_equiv` of `Suggested.lean`, stated for every `K` of Conventions. Injectivity is restriction to `K̄`. For surjectivity, a continuous `K`-algebra endomorphism `τ` of `C` sends each `a ∈ K̄` to a root of its minimal polynomial over `K`, which splits in the algebraically closed `K̄`, so `τ(K̄) ⊆ K̄`; the restriction is a `K`-endomorphism of an algebraic extension, hence an element `σ` of `G_K`, and `τ = σ̂` by uniqueness. In particular every continuous `K`-algebra endomorphism of `C` is an automorphism. Joint continuity: for `x ∈ C`, `ε > 0` and `σ₀`, choose `a ∈ K̄` with `|x − a| < ε`; for `σ` in the open coset `σ₀ · Fix(K(a))` (`IntermediateField.fixingSubgroup_isOpen`) and `|y − x| < ε`, the ultrametric inequality gives `|σ̂(y) − σ̂₀(x)| ≤ max(|y − a|, |σ(a) − σ₀(a)|, |a − x|) < ε`, since `σ(a) = σ₀(a)`.
- For a finite extension `L ⊆ K̄` of `K`, the extension of `σ ∈ G_L` computed over `L` is the extension computed over `K` (both are the unique continuous extension).
- For a second algebraic closure `K̄′` with completion `C′` and a `K`-isomorphism `τ : K̄ → K̄′`, `τ` is isometric (uniqueness of the spectral norm), `τ̂ : C → C′` is an isometric isomorphism, and `τ̂ ∘ σ̂ ∘ τ̂⁻¹ = (τστ⁻¹)^` for `σ ∈ G_K`.

**Checks.**

- `σ = 1` extends to `id_C`; every `σ̂` fixes the image of `K`.
- For `K = ℚ_3` and `σ` with `σ(√3) = −√3`, `σ̂` is not the identity although it is isometric; restricted to `ℚ_3(√3)` it is the nontrivial automorphism.
- For finite `K/ℚ_p` inside `PadicAlgCl p`, `σ̂` is PadicPeriodRings §1.1's `completedGaloisAction`, transported along a `K`-isomorphism `AlgebraicClosure K ≅ PadicAlgCl p` (both are algebraic closures of `K`), which is an isometry by the uniqueness of the spectral norm, as in the last bullet before these Checks; joint continuity recovers that section's joint continuity statement.
- Continuity cannot be dropped from surjectivity: choose algebraically independent `t₁, t₂ ∈ ℂ_p` over `PadicAlgCl p` with `|t₁| = 1` and `|t₂| = p⁻¹`, extend `{t₁, t₂}` to a transcendence basis of `ℂ_p` over `PadicAlgCl p`, and extend the permutation swapping `t₁` and `t₂` to a `PadicAlgCl p`-automorphism of the algebraically closed field `ℂ_p`. It is a `ℚ_p`-algebra automorphism that changes `|t₁|`, so it is not isometric, hence not of the form `σ̂`, hence not continuous.
- For `K = 𝔽_p((t))` and `σ ∈ G_K`, `σ̂` fixes `t^{1/p}` (the only `p`-th root of `t`), so `σ̂` is determined by `σ|_{K^sep}`.
- Composition order: for `K = ℚ_2`, `L` the splitting field of `T³ − 2` and `σ, τ ∈ G_K` restricting to an element of order two and an element of order three of `Gal(L/K) ≅ S₃`, `(στ)^` and `τ̂ ∘ σ̂` differ on `2^{1/3}`, while `(στ)^ = σ̂ ∘ τ̂`; the map `σ ↦ σ̂` is a homomorphism for the product `(στ)(x) = σ(τ(x))` of `G_K`, not an anti-homomorphism.

### 0.3 Automorphisms of the geometric point

Prove that every morphism of adic spaces `a : Spa(C, 𝒪_C) → Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` with `ξ_C ∘ a = ξ_C` equals `Spa(σ̂)` for a unique `σ ∈ G_K`, and that `σ ↦ Spa(σ̂)` is an anti-isomorphism from `G_K` onto the group of such automorphisms: `Spa((στ)^) = Spa(τ̂) ∘ Spa(σ̂)`. The morphism `a` is `Spa` of a unique continuous `K`-algebra endomorphism of `C` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §7.1), which is some `σ̂` by §0.2 (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6, line 1479).

Let `N(ξ_C)` be the cofiltered category of pointed étale neighbourhoods `(V, v̄)` of `ξ_C` in `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét`, a morphism `(V′, v̄′) → (V, v̄)` being a morphism `V′ → V` over `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` carrying `v̄′` to `v̄`; its terminal object is `(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ξ_C)`, and the stalk of a sheaf `F` is `F_{ξ_C} = colim_{(V, v̄) ∈ N(ξ_C)^op} F(V)` (stacks03N1, proof of Theorem 53.1, Tag 03Q9, line 3745, where the colimit runs over the opposite of the category of étale neighbourhoods). Prove also that the connected pointed neighbourhoods `(Spa(L, 𝒪_L), v̄)`, with `L/K` finite separable, form a full subcategory of `N(ξ_C)` that is initial (every object of `N(ξ_C)` receives a morphism from one of them; compare the initial subcategory of affine neighbourhoods at stacks03N1 line 3746), and that this subcategory is equivalent to the opposite `P^op` of the poset `P` of finite subextensions of `K^sep/K` ordered by inclusion, by `(Spa(L, 𝒪_L), v̄) ↦ ι(L)` for the embedding `ι` below. Consequently `F_{ξ_C} = colim_{L ∈ P} F(Spa(L, 𝒪_L))`, the inclusion-filtered colimit whose transition maps are the restrictions `F(Spa(L, 𝒪_L)) → F(Spa(L′, 𝒪_{L′}))` for `L ⊆ L′`.

An étale `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`-space `V` need not be connected. Since the affinoid étale objects over `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` are `Spa` of finite étale `K`-algebras `L_1 × ⋯ × L_r` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1), `V` is the disjoint union of one-point open subspaces `Spa(L_s, 𝒪_{L_s})` with `L_s/K` finite separable, `v̄` factors through the component `V₀` containing its image, and `(V₀, v̄) → (V, v̄)` is a morphism of pointed neighbourhoods. On a connected neighbourhood, `v̄` corresponds to a `K`-embedding `ι : L → K^sep ⊆ C` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §7.1); a morphism `(Spa(L′, 𝒪_{L′}), v̄′) → (Spa(L, 𝒪_L), v̄)` exists exactly when `ι(L) ⊆ ι′(L′)`, and it is then unique, so arrows go from the larger field to the smaller. Precomposition of `v̄` with `Spa(σ̂)` corresponds to postcomposition of `ι` with `σ`. This is the action on stalks fixed in Conventions.

**Checks.**

- For a separable quadratic `L/K`, namely `L = K(√a)` with `a ∉ K^{×2}` when `char K ≠ 2`, or the Artin–Schreier extension `L = K(α)`, `α² + α = t⁻¹`, when `K = 𝔽_2((t))`, `Hom_K(L, C)` has two elements (`±√a`, resp. `α` and `α + 1`) and the nontrivial class of `G_K/G_L` swaps them.
- Negative control: for `K = 𝔽_2((t))` and the purely inseparable `L = K(t^{1/2})`, `[L : K] = 2` but `T² − t = (T − t^{1/2})²` over `C`, so `Hom_K(L, C)` has one element, `G_L = G_K` acts trivially on it, and `Spa(L, 𝒪_L) → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` is not étale, so it is not a neighbourhood of `ξ_C`. Counting embeddings by the degree fails here.
- For `V = Spa(K, 𝒪_K) ⊔ Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` pointed through the first component, `V` has two components and is not `Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` for any field `L`. The inclusion of the first component is a morphism of pointed neighbourhoods from `(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ξ_C)`, and for a sheaf `F` a section `(s_1, s_2) ∈ F(V) = F(Spa(K, 𝒪_K))²` has the germ of `s_1` at `ξ_C`.
- Direction of the equivalence: for `K = ℚ_3`, `L = K(√3)` and an embedding `ι : L → C`, there is exactly one morphism `(Spa(L, 𝒪_L), v̄) → (Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ξ_C)` in `N(ξ_C)` and none in the other direction (it would come from a `K`-algebra map `L → K`, impossible as `[L : K] = 2`), whereas in `P` there is one arrow `K → L` (from `K ⊆ L`) and none `L → K`. So the neighbourhoods match `P^op`, not `P`: `N(ξ_C)` has the terminal object `(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ξ_C)`, while `P` has no largest element, since no finite extension of `ℚ_3` contains all of them. Taking opposites once more, the stalk colimit runs over `P` with the restriction `F(Spa(K, 𝒪_K)) → F(Spa(L, 𝒪_L))` as its transition map. For `F = μ_4` and `K = ℚ_3` the inclusion-filtered colimit is `μ_4(K^sep)`, of order 4, whereas a colimit indexed so that `K` is terminal would return `μ_4(ℚ_3)`, of order 2.
- For `L/K` cyclic of degree three with generator `g` and an embedding `τ₀`, the element `σ` restricting to `g` sends `τ₀` to `τ₀ ∘ g`, that is, it postcomposes with `σ`: `σ ∘ τ₀ = τ₀ ∘ g`. The opposite convention would send `τ₀` to `τ₀ ∘ g⁻¹`, which differs, and composing in that convention gives a right action.
- For `K = C`, the only automorphism of `ξ_C` over `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` is the identity.

*Needs:* §§0.1–0.2; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.1, 7.1; AdicEtaleGeometry A1.7.

### Examples

For `K = ℚ_2` and `σ ∈ G_K` with `σ(i) = −i` and `σ(√2) = √2`: `σ̂` acts on `μ_4(C) = {±1, ±i}` by `i ↦ −i`, and on `Hom_{ℚ_2}(ℚ_2(i), C)` by the swap. For `K = 𝔽_2((t))` and the Artin–Schreier extension `L = K(α)`, `α² + α = t⁻¹`, the nontrivial element of `Gal(L/K)` is `α ↦ α + 1`, and its extension to `C` fixes `t^{1/2}`.

## Layer 1: The action on cohomology

### 1.1 Automorphisms of a cover act on cohomology

Work in one of the following settings, with the étale site written `Y_τ`: (a) locally strongly sheafy analytic adic spaces with their étale sites (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.6, ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1); (b) locally noetherian analytic adic spaces with their pro-étale sites (AdicSpacesPartIV R4, *proetale-site-functoriality*; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1); (c) schemes with their small étale sites (SchemeAndStackFoundations §§1.10, 2.1). Let `p : Y → X` be a morphism, `a : Y → Y` an automorphism with `p ∘ a = p`, `Λ` a ring and `M ∈ D(X_τ, Λ)`, the unbounded derived category of Conventions (on the pro-étale site, that of the essentially small site of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1); `M ∈ D⁺(X_τ, Λ)` is the bounded-below case.

Define `c_a : a^{-1} p^{-1} M ≅ p^{-1} M` as the composite of the coherence isomorphism `a^{-1} p^{-1} ≅ (p ∘ a)^{-1}` (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.6 `AdicSpace.smallEtale.mapComp`; AdicSpacesPartIV R4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §1.10 `smallEtale_map_comp`) with the identity `(p ∘ a)^{-1} = p^{-1}`. Define the induced automorphism `a^♮ := H^i(c_a) ∘ a^* : H^i(Y, p^{-1}M) → H^i(Y, p^{-1}M)`, where `a^* : H^i(Y, W) → H^i(Y, a^{-1}W)`, for `W ∈ D(Y_τ, Λ)`, is induced by the unit `W → Ra_* a^{-1} W` of the enhanced adjunction `a^{-1} ⊣ Ra_*` and the composition isomorphism `RΓ(Y, Ra_*(−)) ≅ RΓ(Y, −)` (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4). For `W ∈ D⁺(Y_τ, Λ)` it is the cohomology pullback along the morphism of sites of `a` (on schemes Tau Ceti's `Sheaf.H.pullback` applied as in SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.1; on analytic sites the unit `W → Ra_* a^{-1} W` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1 composed with `RΓ ∘ Ra_* ≅ RΓ` of §1.3), because the enhanced direct image agrees on `D⁺` with the bounded-below right derived functor (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4). The same formula defines `a^♮` on `RΓ(Y, p^{-1}M)` and on `H^i(U ×_X Y, p^{-1}M)` for every `U` étale over `X`. (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6, lines 1481–1488.) Prove:

- **Composition.** `(a ∘ b)^♮ = b^♮ ∘ a^♮` and `id^♮ = id`: the coherence isomorphisms satisfy the cocycle identity `c_{a∘b} = c_b ∘ b^{-1}(c_a)` under `(a ∘ b)^{-1} ≅ b^{-1} a^{-1}`, and `(a ∘ b)^* = b^* ∘ a^*` (the composition coherence of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4; on `D⁺` over schemes, `pullback_comp_apply`).
- **Degree zero.** For a sheaf of sets `ℱ = h_V` represented by `V` étale over `X`, `H⁰(Y, p^{-1}h_V) = Hom_X(Y, V)` and `a^♮(s) = s ∘ a`. For a sheaf `ℱ` of `Λ`-modules, `a^♮` on `H⁰` is `Λ`-linear and natural in `ℱ`.
- **Linearity and naturality.** `a^♮` is `Λ`-linear and natural in `M` for morphisms in `D(X_τ, Λ)`; it commutes with the connecting maps of distinguished triangles in `D(X_τ, Λ)` (on `D⁺` over schemes, `pullback_δ`); it commutes with change of coefficients along a ring map `Λ → Λ′`.
- **Morphisms of covers.** Let `p′ : Y′ → X′` be a second morphism and `g : X′ → X`, `h : Y′ → Y` morphisms with `p ∘ h = g ∘ p′`, all in the same setting. Define `h^♯ := H^i(e_h) ∘ h^* : H^i(Y, p^{-1}M) → H^i(Y′, p′^{-1} g^{-1} M)`, where `e_h : h^{-1} p^{-1} M ≅ (p ∘ h)^{-1} M = (g ∘ p′)^{-1} M ≅ p′^{-1} g^{-1} M` is built from coherence isomorphisms as `c_a` is; for `X′ = X`, `g = id_X`, `Y′ = Y` and `h = a` it is `a^♮`. Then `id^♯ = id` and `(h ∘ h′)^♯ = h′^♯ ∘ h^♯` for a second square `p′ ∘ h′ = g′ ∘ p″` with `h′ : Y″ → Y′` and `g′ : X″ → X′`, up to the coherence isomorphism `g′^{-1} g^{-1} ≅ (g ∘ g′)^{-1}` on the target. In particular, if `a′` is an automorphism of `Y′` with `p′ ∘ a′ = p′` and `h ∘ a′ = a ∘ h`, then `a′^♮ ∘ h^♯ = h^♯ ∘ a^♮` on `H^i(Y, p^{-1}M) → H^i(Y′, p′^{-1} g^{-1} M)`. Route: both sides of each identity are the pullback along one morphism (`h ∘ h′`, resp. `h ∘ a′ = a ∘ h`), by the composition coherence of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4 (on `D⁺` over schemes, `pullback_comp_apply`), followed by two isomorphisms between the same composite inverse images, both built from coherence isomorphisms, which agree by the coherence of the pseudofunctor of inverse images (the cocycle identity of the composition bullet). No fibre product is assumed. When `Y ×_X X′` exists and lies in the setting, its projection `h` to `Y`, with `a′ := a × id`, is the base change of the cover: the fibre product always exists in setting (c), and in settings (a) and (b) under the hypotheses of AdicSpacesPartII §R0.7 (*fibre-products-existence*), for instance for `Y = X_C` and `g` a morphism of rigid `K`-spaces, where `X_C ×_X X′ = X′_C`. The route uses only that the inverse images along `g`, `h`, `p`, `p′`, `a`, `a′` are exact and compose up to coherent isomorphism, so the statement holds verbatim for a commutative square of morphisms of sites with exact inverse images from one setting to another; §4.2 applies it to analytification morphisms.
- **Inverse image and tensor.** Let `Λ` be commutative and `g : Y′ → Y` a morphism in one of the settings (a)–(c). For `M, N ∈ D(Y_τ, Λ)` there is an isomorphism `g^{-1}(M ⊗^L_Λ N) ≅ g^{-1}M ⊗^L_Λ g^{-1}N`, natural in `M` and `N`, compatible with the unit `g^{-1}Λ = Λ`, with the associativity isomorphisms of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3, and with the composition isomorphisms `(g ∘ h)^{-1} ≅ h^{-1} g^{-1}`. Route: the inverse image of sheaves of `Λ`-modules is exact, commutes with the tensor product of sheaves, and carries K-flat complexes with flat terms to K-flat complexes with flat terms, so the isomorphism is computed termwise on the K-flat replacements of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.2 (stacks01FQ, Lemma 21.18.1, Tag 0G7E, page 33, line 1451, and Lemma 21.18.4, Tag 07A4, page 35, line 1525; Lemma 21.18.3, Tag 0D6D, line 1515, for composites).
- **Cup products.** Let `Λ` be commutative and `M, N ∈ D(X_τ, Λ)`. Identify `H^i(Y, W)` with `Hom_{D(Y_τ, Λ)}(Λ, W[i])` (K-injective replacement, EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.1–0.2). For `x : Λ → p^{-1}M[i]` and `y : Λ → p^{-1}N[j]` let `x ∪ y` be the composite `Λ ≅ Λ ⊗^L_Λ Λ → p^{-1}M[i] ⊗^L_Λ p^{-1}N[j] ≅ (p^{-1}M ⊗^L_Λ p^{-1}N)[i+j] ≅ p^{-1}(M ⊗^L_Λ N)[i+j]`, built from the unit, the bifunctoriality and the shift sign of the derived tensor of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3 (`ClassicalDerived.tensorHom`) and the inverse of the isomorphism of the previous bullet; on schemes with finite coefficients it is the cup product of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.1. Then `a^♮(x ∪ y) = a^♮(x) ∪ a^♮(y)`: in these terms `a^*` sends `x` to `a^{-1}(x)`, using `a^{-1}Λ = Λ` and the adjunction `a^{-1} ⊣ Ra_*`, and `c_a` is compatible with the tensor isomorphisms by the compatibility of the previous bullet with composition. The target `M ⊗^L_Λ N` is an object of `D(X_τ, Λ)`, in general not of `D⁺` even when `M` and `N` are sheaves; it lies in `D⁺(X_τ, Λ)` when `M, N ∈ D⁺(X_τ, Λ)` and one of them has finite Tor amplitude (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.1 on schemes). For a noncommutative `Λ` the action `a^♮` is defined but no cup product is formed.
- **Finite Galois covers.** For a finite étale Galois cover `p : U → X` with group `G` (settings (a), (c)), `g ↦ (g^{-1})^♮` is the left `G`-action on `H^q(U, p^{-1}F)` whose group cohomology is the `E_2` term of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3 (a) and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15.

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `Y = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `a = Spa(σ̂)` and `ℱ = μ_n = h_V` with `V = Spa(K[T]/(T^n − 1))` (`n` invertible in `K`), `Hom_X(Y, V) = μ_n(C)` and `a^♮(ζ) = σ(ζ)`: the degree-zero formula computes the convention witness. Replacing `a^♮` by `(a^{-1})^♮` gives `ζ ↦ σ⁻¹(ζ)`.
- For `X` a point, `Y = X ⊔ X` and `a` the swap, `H⁰(Y, ℤ) = ℤ²` and `a^♮` swaps the coordinates; for `a = id`, `a^♮ = id`.
- For `Y = ∅` every cohomology group is zero and `a^♮` is the unique map.
- Unbounded tensor target: for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `Y = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `a = Spa(σ̂)`, `Λ = ℤ/4` and `M = N` the constant sheaf `ℤ/2` in degree zero, the periodic free resolution of `ℤ/2` with differentials multiplication by `2` has zero differentials after tensoring with `ℤ/2`, so `H^{−n}(Y, p^{-1}(M ⊗^L_Λ N)) = Tor_n^{ℤ/4}(ℤ/2, ℤ/2) = ℤ/2` for every `n ≥ 0`. The target is not in `D⁺`, `a^♮` is the identity on each of these groups, and the cup product of the two unit classes is the generator in degree zero. The other side of the inverse-image isomorphism, `p^{-1}M ⊗^L_Λ p^{-1}N`, has the same stalks `Tor_n^{ℤ/4}(ℤ/2, ℤ/2) = ℤ/2`, as it must, because `p^{-1}` is exact and keeps the periodic resolution flat; the underived tensor `p^{-1}M ⊗_Λ p^{-1}N` keeps only degree zero, so the isomorphism of the inverse-image bullet is one of derived tensors. With `M = Λ` in degree zero, `M ⊗^L_Λ N = N`, the inverse-image isomorphism is the unit isomorphism, and the cup product with the unit class is the identity.
- Noncommutative coefficients: for `Λ = M_2(𝔽_2)` the action on `H^i(Y, p^{-1}M)` is defined, but a left module made into a right one by the same matrices is not a right module: for `v = e_1`, `A = E_{12}` and `B = E_{21}`, acting by `A` and then by `B` gives `BAv = 0`, whereas acting by the product `AB` gives `ABv = e_1`. Two left `Λ`-modules have no balanced tensor product over `Λ`, so no cup product is formed.
- The composition law reverses the order: for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `Y = Spa(L ⊗_K C)` with `L/K` Galois with group `S₃` (for `K = ℚ_2`, the splitting field of `T³ − 2`, in which `ζ_3` generates the unramified quadratic subextension), let `σ, τ ∈ G_K` restrict to an element `s` of order two and an element `r` of order three of `Gal(L/K)`, and let `a = Spa(id_L ⊗ σ̂)`, `b = Spa(id_L ⊗ τ̂)`. On `H⁰(Y, ℤ) = ℤ^{Gal(L/K)}` the rule of §1.2 gives `a^♮(δ_g) = δ_{sg}` and `b^♮(δ_g) = δ_{rg}`, so `(a ∘ b)^♮ = b^♮ ∘ a^♮` sends `δ_1` to `δ_{rs}`, whereas the wrong order `a^♮ ∘ b^♮` sends it to `δ_{sr} ≠ δ_{rs}`; this agrees with `a ∘ b = σ_Y ∘ τ_Y = (τσ)_Y` and `ρ(τσ)(δ_1) = δ_{rs}`. A cyclic extension does not separate the two orders, since its Galois group is abelian.
- Morphisms of covers, in the base-change case: for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `Y = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `a = Spa(σ̂)`, `X′ = Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` with `L/K` finite separable and `M = μ_n` (`n` invertible in `K`), `Y′ = Y ×_X X′ = ⊔_{τ ∈ Hom_K(L, C)} Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `h^♯ : μ_n(C) → μ_n(C)^{Hom_K(L, C)}` is the diagonal map, and for `a′ = a × id = σ_{X′}` the map `a′^♮` sends `(ζ_τ)_τ` to `(σ(ζ_{σ⁻¹∘τ}))_τ` (§1.2). So `a′^♮ ∘ h^♯` and `h^♯ ∘ a^♮` both send `ζ` to the constant family `σ(ζ)`. For `K = ℚ_2`, `n = 5` and `σ` an arithmetic Frobenius lift (`σ(ζ) = ζ²`), an intertwining of `a′^♮` with `(a^{-1})^♮` instead would give the constant family `ζ³ ≠ ζ²`.
- No fibre product is presupposed: for `X′ = Y = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` over `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` neither structure map is locally of finite type, and AdicSpacesPartII §R0.7 provides no `Y ×_X X′`; a base-change statement quantified over every `g : X′ → X` would assert nothing meaningful there, whereas the morphism-of-covers statement concerns only the given `h`.

### 1.2 The rigid action

Let `X` be a rigid `K`-space. For `σ ∈ G_K` define `σ_X := id_X × Spa(σ̂) : X_C → X_C`, the morphism induced by the universal property of `X_C = X ×_{Spa(K,𝒪_K)} Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` from `pr_C` and `Spa(σ̂) ∘ q` (`q : X_C → Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` the second projection). Prove (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6, lines 1481–1488):

- `pr_C ∘ σ_X = pr_C`, `(στ)_X = τ_X ∘ σ_X`, `1_X = id`; hence `σ_X` is an automorphism of `X_C` over `X` with inverse `(σ⁻¹)_X`.
- `q ∘ σ_X = Spa(σ̂) ∘ q`; if `X ≠ ∅` and `σ̂ ≠ id`, `σ_X` is not a morphism over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` (on global sections `C → 𝒪(X_C)` is injective, and `q ∘ σ_X = q` would force `σ̂ = id`).
- For `M ∈ D(X_ét, Λ)`, in particular for `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)`, define `ρ(σ) := (σ_X)^♮` on `H^i(X_C, M_C)` and on `RΓ(X_C, M_C)` (§1.1 with `p = pr_C`). Then `ρ(στ) = ρ(σ) ρ(τ)`, `ρ(1) = id`, and each `ρ(σ)` is `Λ`-linear: `ρ` is a left action of `G_K` by `Λ`-linear automorphisms.
- The same definition on the pro-étale site (§1.1 (b)) gives a left action on `H^i(X_{C,proét}, pr_C^{-1} P)` for `P ∈ D⁺(X_proét, Λ)`, and for `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` the identification `H^i(X_{C,ét}, M_C) ≅ H^i(X_{C,proét}, ν^* M_C)` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3 is `ρ`-equivariant (§1.1, Morphisms of covers, for the square of morphisms of sites from the pro-étale to the étale setting formed by `ν` on `X_C` and on `X`; AdicSpacesPartIV R4: `ν ∘ f_proét ≅ f_ét ∘ ν`).

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `ℱ = μ_n` with `n` invertible in `K`, `H⁰(X_C, μ_n) = μ_n(C)` and `ρ(σ)(ζ) = σ(ζ)` (§1.1, first Check).
- For `X = Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` with `L/K` finite separable, `X_C = ⊔_{τ ∈ Hom_K(L, C)} Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `σ_X` maps the component `τ` to the component `σ⁻¹ ∘ τ`, and `ρ(σ)` maps the indicator of `τ` to the indicator of `σ ∘ τ` on `H⁰(X_C, ℤ) = ℤ^{Hom_K(L, C)}`. This is the permutation representation on `ℤ[Hom_K(L, C)] ≅ ℤ[G_K/G_L]`, the representation of `G_K` induced from the trivial representation of `G_L`; only when `L/K` is Galois is it the regular representation of `Gal(L/K)`, inflated to `G_K`. For the non-Galois cubic `L = ℚ_2(2^{1/3})` over `K = ℚ_2` it has rank `3`, and an element of `G_K` acting as a transposition on the three embeddings has trace `1`, whereas the regular representation of the splitting group `S₃` has rank `6` and trace `0` off the identity. The assignment `σ ↦ ρ(σ⁻¹)` reverses products and is not a homomorphism when `L/K` is Galois with group `S₃`.
- For `K = C`, `G_K` is trivial and `ρ` is the identity; for `X = ∅` all groups vanish.
- With the module pullback `𝒪_{X_C} ⊗ pr^{-1}ℱ` in place of `pr^{-1}ℱ`, `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `ℱ = 𝒪_{X_ét}` would give `H⁰ = C`, whereas the definition here gives `H⁰(X_C, pr^{-1}𝒪_{X_ét}) = K^sep` (the stalk of `𝒪_{X_ét}` at `ξ_C`), a discrete `G_K`-module.

*Needs:* §§0.2, 1.1; AdicSpacesPartII §R0.7 (*fibre-products-existence*); AdicEtaleGeometry A1.6; AdicSpacesPartIV R4 (*proetale-site-functoriality*); ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.1, 1.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.1.

### 1.3 Functoriality of the rigid action

Let `X` be a rigid `K`-space, `Λ` a ring and `M, N ∈ D(X_ét, Λ)` (in particular `M, N ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)`). Prove that the following maps commute with `ρ(σ)` for every `σ ∈ G_K` (dejongvanderput1996, §3.6; zavyalov2025, §5.4, line 3798, for the use of these compatibilities):

- (a) pullback `g_C^* : H^i(X_C, M_C) → H^i(X′_C, (g^{-1}M)_C)` along a morphism `g : X′ → X` of rigid `K`-spaces, `g_C := g × id`;
- (b) the map induced by a morphism `M → N` in `D(X_ét, Λ)`, in particular by a morphism of sheaves;
- (c) the connecting map `H^i(X_C, ℱ″_C) → H^{i+1}(X_C, ℱ′_C)` of a short exact sequence `0 → ℱ′ → ℱ → ℱ″ → 0` of sheaves of `Λ`-modules on `X_ét` (`pr_C^{-1}` is exact);
- (d) for `Λ` commutative, the cup product `H^i(X_C, M_C) ⊗_Λ H^j(X_C, N_C) → H^{i+j}(X_C, (M ⊗^L_Λ N)_C)`, where `M ⊗^L_Λ N ∈ D(X_ét, Λ)` lies in `D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` when `M, N` are bounded below and one of them has finite Tor amplitude, and not in general (Conventions);
- (e) restriction: for a finite extension `L ⊆ K̄` of `K` (not necessarily separable), `(X_L)_C = X_C` canonically, `(ℱ_L)_C = ℱ_C`, and the action of `G_L` on `H^i((X_L)_C, ℱ_C)` defined over `L` is the restriction of `ρ` to `G_L`;
- (f) change of algebraic closure: for `τ : K̄ → K̄′` as in §0.2, `id_X × Spa(τ̂⁻¹) : X_C → X_{C′}` induces an isomorphism `H^i(X_{C′}, M_{C′}) → H^i(X_C, M_C)` that intertwines `ρ′(τστ⁻¹)` with `ρ(σ)`.

All six follow from §1.1. (a), (e) and (f) are morphisms of covers: (a) with `h = g_C` over `g`, the base change of the cover `X_C → X` along `g` (`X_C ×_X X′ = X′_C`, AdicSpacesPartII §R0.7, case (a)), since `g_C ∘ σ_{X′} = σ_X ∘ g_C`; (e) with `h` the identification `(X_L)_C = X_C` over `pr_L : X_L → X`, since for `σ ∈ G_L` the two definitions of `σ_X` coincide (§0.2); (f) with `g = id_X` and `h = id_X × Spa(τ̂⁻¹)`, since `(id × Spa τ̂⁻¹) ∘ σ_X = (τστ⁻¹)_X ∘ (id × Spa τ̂⁻¹)`. (b)–(d) are naturality, connecting maps and cup products.

**Checks.**

- In (f), if `K̄′ = K̄` and `τ ∈ G_K`, then `id_X × Spa(τ̂⁻¹) = (τ⁻¹)_X`, so the isomorphism is `ρ(τ⁻¹) = ρ(τ)⁻¹` and the statement reduces to `ρ(τ)⁻¹ ρ(τστ⁻¹) ρ(τ) = ρ(σ)`; with `ρ(τ)` in its place the statement would read `ρ(σ) = ρ(τ²στ⁻²)`, which fails for non-commuting `σ, τ`.
- For (c), the Kummer sequence `0 → μ_n → 𝔾_m → 𝔾_m → 0` on `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4, `n` invertible in `K`) pulls back to `0 → μ_n(C) → (K^sep)^× → (K^sep)^× → 0` on `H⁰(X_C, −)`, a sequence of discrete `G_K`-modules (`H⁰(X_C, pr^{-1}𝔾_m)` is the stalk `(K^sep)^×`), and its `G_K`-cohomology recovers the Kummer isomorphism `K^×/K^{×n} ≅ H¹(G_K, μ_n(C))`. Working with `𝔾_{m,X_C}` instead (global sections `C^×`) gives a module that is not discrete.
- For (e) with `L/K` purely inseparable of degree `p` (`K = 𝔽_p((t))`, `L = K(t^{1/p})`), `G_L = G_K` and the two actions agree.
- For (d) with `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `M = N` the constant sheaf `ℤ/2`: over `Λ = ℤ/2` the target `(M ⊗^L_Λ N)_C` is `ℤ/2` in degree zero, while over `Λ = ℤ/4` it has `H^{−n}(X_C, −) = ℤ/2` for every `n ≥ 0` (§1.1, Checks), with trivial action in each degree; the second target is outside `D⁺`.

*Needs:* §§0.2, 1.1, 1.2; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.1–0.4; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.1, 1.4.

### 1.4 The scheme action

Let `X` be a scheme over a field `k`. For `σ ∈ G_k` let `σ_X := id_X × Spec(σ|_{k^sep}) : X_{k^sep} → X_{k^sep}` and, for `M ∈ D(X_ét, Λ)`, `ρ(σ) := (σ_X)^♮` on `H^i(X_{k^sep}, M)` (§1.1 (c) with `p` the projection; stacks03N1, Section 56, Tag 03QW, line 3966, for the action through `Spec(σ)` at a geometric point; milneLEC Theorem 14.9 and Remark 14.10, pages 96–97, lines 3834–3866, where the Galois group of a Galois covering, and of a tower of Galois coverings, acts on the cohomology of the cover, and §14, page 99, line 3940, for its instance over a finite field; sga4half, Arcata). Prove:

- `(στ)_X = τ_X ∘ σ_X`, so `ρ` is a left action by `Λ`-linear automorphisms; the analogues of §1.3 (a)–(f) hold, with rigid spaces replaced by `k`-schemes, `K̄` by `k^sep` and, for commutative `Λ`, the cup product of §1.1 (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.1 for finite coefficients).
- The same construction on `X_{k̄}`, with `σ_X = id_X × Spec(σ)`, gives an action for which the pullback `H^i(X_{k^sep}, M) → H^i(X_{k̄}, M)` is a `G_k`-equivariant isomorphism: equivariant by §1.1 (Morphisms of covers), the projection `X_{k̄} → X_{k^sep}` intertwining the two `σ_X`, and an isomorphism because `X_{k̄} → X_{k^sep}` is a universal homeomorphism (SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.23a, EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.4).
- For a field extension `k ⊆ k′` with compatible separable closures, the pullback along `X_{k′^sep} → X_{k^sep}` is equivariant for `G_{k′} → G_k` (§1.1, Morphisms of covers: the projection intertwines `σ′_X` with `(σ′|_{k^sep})_X`).

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spec k`, `H⁰(X_{k^sep}, μ_n) = μ_n(k^sep)` with `ρ(σ)(ζ) = σ(ζ)` for `n` invertible in `k`; for `k = ℝ` and `n = 4`, complex conjugation sends `i` to `−i`.
- For `k = 𝔽_q`, `X = Spec k` and `ℱ = μ_{ℓ^n}` with `ℓ ∤ q`, the arithmetic Frobenius `x ↦ x^q` acts on `H⁰(X_{k^sep}, μ_{ℓ^n})` by `ζ ↦ ζ^q`, not by `ζ ↦ ζ^{q⁻¹}`.
- For `X = Spec k′` with `k′/k` finite separable, `H⁰(X_{k^sep}, ℤ) = ℤ^{Hom_k(k′, k^sep)}` with `ρ(σ)` sending the indicator of `τ` to that of `σ ∘ τ` (stacks03N1, Tag 03QW, line 3969).
- For `k = 𝔽_p(u)` and `X = Spec k(u^{1/p})`, `X_{k^sep} = Spec k^sep(u^{1/p})` is the spectrum of a separably closed field, while `X_{k̄} = Spec k̄[T]/(T − u^{1/p})^p` is a nonreduced one-point scheme; the map `X_{k̄} → X_{k^sep}` is a universal homeomorphism, and both have `H⁰(−, ℤ) = ℤ` with trivial action and no higher cohomology, as the second bullet requires. Counting points of `X_{k̄}` with multiplicity, `p` instead of `1`, would break this.

*Needs:* §1.1; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.23a, 1.10, 2.1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.4, F.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§0.1, 0.3–0.4; Mathlib `SeparableClosure`, `AlgebraicClosure`.

### Examples

For `X = ℙ¹_K` and `n` invertible in `K`, `H²(X_C, μ_n) ≅ ℤ/n` by the first Chern class of `𝒪(1)`, which is defined over `K`; `ρ` is trivial on it. Hence `H²(X_C, ℤ/n) ≅ ℤ/n(−1)`, on which an arithmetic Frobenius lift of a local field with residue field `𝔽_q` (`n` prime to `q`) acts by `q⁻¹`.

## Layer 2: Discreteness, the dictionary and Hochschild–Serre

### 2.1 Finite levels and discreteness for rigid spaces

Let `X` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid `K`-space, `Λ` a ring and `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` (in particular any abelian sheaf, with no torsion hypothesis). Let `L` run over the finite subextensions of `K^sep/K`, ordered by inclusion. For `σ ∈ G_K` write `σ_{X,L} := id_X × Spa(σ|_L) : X_{σL} → X_L` and `σ_L^♮ := H^i(c) ∘ σ_{X,L}^* : H^i(X_L, M_L) → H^i(X_{σL}, M_{σL})`. Prove (dejongvanderput1996, Corollary 3.7.5 and its proof, page 39, lines 1594–1605, where the proof makes the identification for overconvergent sheaves, line 1602; for every abelian sheaf on the adic étale site, huber1996, §2.4, Corollary 2.4.6, and the route below):

- the pullbacks along `X_C → X_L` induce an isomorphism `colim_L H^i(X_L, M_L) → H^i(X_C, M_C)` for every `i`;
- `ρ(σ) ∘ pr_{C/L}^* = pr_{C/σL}^* ∘ σ_L^♮` (§1.1, Morphisms of covers, for `pr_{C/L} ∘ σ_X = σ_{X,L} ∘ pr_{C/σL}`, all three covers lying over `X`), so the isomorphism intertwines `ρ` with the action on the colimit given by the `σ_L^♮`;
- `G_L` fixes the image of `H^i(X_L, M_L)`, since `σ_{X,L} = id` for `σ ∈ G_L`;
- consequently every element of `H^i(X_C, M_C)` has open stabiliser, `H^i(X_C, M_C)` with the discrete topology is a discrete `G_K`-module (`continuousSMul_iff_stabilizer_isOpen`), and `H^i(X_C, M_C) = ⋃_L H^i(X_C, M_C)^{G_L}`.

The isomorphism is the colimit theorem of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.5 for the system `(X_L)_L` with final object `X`: `X_C ∼ lim_L X_L` is the base change of `Spa(C, 𝒪_C) ∼ lim_L Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` (§0.1) along the qcqs morphism `X → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.5), and its qcqs étale site is the 2-colimit of those of the `X_L` (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.14, *etale-descent-along-algebraic-extension*, with `L = K^sep` and `L̂ = C`).

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `ℱ` a sheaf on `X_ét`, `colim_L ℱ(Spa(L, 𝒪_L)) = ℱ_{ξ_C}` and the statement is the description of the stalk as a discrete module (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1).
- Quasi-compactness is needed: for `K = ℚ_p`, `ℓ ≠ p`, `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `ℱ` equal to `μ_{ℓ^m}` on the `m`-th component, `H⁰(X_C, ℱ_C) = ∏_m μ_{ℓ^m}(C)`, and a compatible system of primitive roots has stabiliser `ker(χ_ℓ)`, which is not open because `ℚ_p(μ_{ℓ^∞})/ℚ_p` is infinite; this element lies in no `H⁰(X_L, ℱ_L)`.
- For `ℱ = 𝒪_{X_ét}` as an abelian sheaf on `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, the colimit is `K^sep`; the statement does not compute `H⁰(X_C, 𝒪_{X_C}) = C`, which is not a discrete `G_K`-module.
- For `K = 𝔽_p((t))` the index set consists of the finite separable subextensions only; this suffices because `C` is the completion of `K^sep` (§0.1), and AdicEtaleGeometry A1.14 is stated for separable algebraic extensions.

*Needs:* §§0.1–0.2, 1.1–1.2; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.5 (colimit theorem, base change of colimit-presented tilde-limits); AdicEtaleGeometry A1.14 (*etale-descent-along-algebraic-extension*); Mathlib `continuousSMul_iff_stabilizer_isOpen`; ProfiniteCohomology Layer 0.

### 2.2 Finite levels and discreteness for schemes

Let `X` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme over `k` and `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)`. Prove that `colim_L H^i(X_L, M) → H^i(X_{k^sep}, M)` is an isomorphism over the finite subextensions `L` of `k^sep/k`, compatibly with `ρ` as in §2.1, that `G_L` fixes the image of `H^i(X_L, M)`, and that `H^i(X_{k^sep}, M)` is a discrete `G_k`-module (stacks03N1, Theorem 53.1, Tag 03Q9, line 3733, and its proof; milneLEC Corollary 12.2, page 81, line 3237, for the stalk of a higher direct image as a colimit over étale neighbourhoods). The isomorphism is SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_cohomology_limits`) for `X_{k^sep} = lim_L X_L` with affine transition maps; the case of bounded-below complexes follows by the convergent hypercohomology spectral sequence.

**Checks.**

- `X = Spec k` and `M = ℱ`: `H⁰(X_{k^sep}, ℱ) = ℱ_{k^sep}` and higher groups vanish (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15, its Check on the separable closure of a field, through `etale_cohomology_limits`).
- For `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spec ℚ` (not quasi-compact) and `ℱ = μ_{ℓ^m}` on the `m`-th component, the compatible system of roots has non-open stabiliser `ker χ_ℓ`.
- No finite level suffices in general: for `k = ℚ` and `X = Spec ℚ(2^{1/3})`, `H⁰(X_{k^sep}, ℤ) = ℤ³`, while `H⁰(X_L, ℤ)` is `ℤ` for `L = ℚ` and `ℤ²` for `L = ℚ(2^{1/3})` (since `ℚ(2^{1/3}) ⊗_ℚ ℚ(2^{1/3}) ≅ ℚ(2^{1/3}) × ℚ(2^{1/3}, ζ_3)`); the map from `H⁰(X_L, ℤ)` is onto only once `L` contains the Galois closure `ℚ(2^{1/3}, ζ_3)`, and a statement claiming an isomorphism at each finite level is false.

*Needs:* §§1.1, 1.4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_cohomology_limits`); ProfiniteCohomology Layer 0.

### 2.3 The dictionary for rigid spaces

Let `f : X → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid `K`-space, `Λ` a ring and `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)`. Under the equivalence of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1 between sheaves of `Λ`-modules on `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét` and discrete `Λ[G_K]`-modules (stalk at `ξ_C`, with the action fixed in Conventions), the isomorphism `(R^i f_* M)_{ξ_C} ≅ H^i(X_C, M_C)` of `Λ`-modules, natural in `M` and compatible with the connecting maps of short exact sequences of sheaves, is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.5 (the strict-localisation stalk theorem with its clause (b): `f` is quasi-compact, quasi-separated and locally of finite type, `ξ_C` is built from the completed algebraic closure `C` of `K`, so `Y(ξ_C) = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` and `X ×_{Spa K} Y(ξ_C) = X_C`; for `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` its complex form). Prove that this isomorphism carries the stalk action of §0.3 to `ρ`, so that it is an isomorphism of `Λ[G_K]`-modules and `R^i f_* M` corresponds to `(H^i(X_C, M_C), ρ)` (dejongvanderput1996, Theorem 3.7.3, page 38, lines 1571–1587; zavyalov2025, §5.4, page 78, line 3798, citing huber1996, Proposition 2.3.10).

Route: the supplier's isomorphism is its neighbourhood stalk formula `(R^i f_* M)_{ξ_C} = colim_{(V, v̄) ∈ N(ξ_C)^op} H^i(V ×_{Spa K} X, M)` followed by its colimit theorem along `Spa(C, 𝒪_C) ∼ lim V`, that is, it is induced by the pullbacks along `X_C → V ×_{Spa K} X`. For `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` the neighbourhood formula holds because `R^i f_* M` is the sheafification of `V ↦ H^i(V ×_{Spa K} X, M)` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1), and the colimit theorem by its clause for bounded-below complexes. On the neighbourhood side, the connected neighbourhoods `(Spa(L, 𝒪_L), v̄)` with `L ⊆ K^sep` finite form an initial subcategory of `N(ξ_C)` equivalent to `P^op` (§0.3), so the stalk is `colim_{L ∈ P} H^i(X_L, M_L)`, over the finite subextensions ordered by inclusion with the pullbacks along `X_{L′} → X_L` for `L ⊆ L′` as transition maps; the stalk action of `σ` (§0.3) is the system `σ_L^♮`; and §2.1 identifies this with `(H^i(X_C, M_C), ρ)`.

Deduce:

- For `ℱ, 𝒢 ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)` and `i, j`, the map `Hom(R^i f_* ℱ, R^j f_* 𝒢) → Hom_{Λ[G_K]}(H^i(X_C, ℱ_C), H^j(X_C, 𝒢_C))` sending a sheaf morphism to its stalk at `ξ_C` is a bijection; in particular every morphism of sheaves on `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét` between higher direct images induces a `ρ`-equivariant map, and every `ρ`-equivariant map arises from a unique such morphism.
- A morphism `R^i f_* ℱ → Λ` to the constant sheaf induces a `G_K`-invariant linear form `H^i(X_C, ℱ_C) → Λ`.
- For a morphism `g : X′ → X` of quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid `K`-spaces, with `f′ := f ∘ g`, the morphism `R^i f_* M → R^i f′_* g^{-1} M` induced by the unit `M → Rg_* g^{-1} M` and the composition isomorphism `Rf′_* ≅ Rf_* ∘ Rg_*` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3) corresponds to the pullback `g_C^* : H^i(X_C, M_C) → H^i(X′_C, (g^{-1}M)_C)` of §1.3 (a). Route: on the stalk formula it is the colimit over `L` of the pullbacks along `X′_L → X_L`, and §2.1, applied to `X` and to `X′`, carries that colimit to `g_C^*`. Without quasi-compactness of `X′` the sheaf `R^i f′_* g^{-1} M` need not correspond to `H^i(X′_C, (g^{-1}M)_C)` at all (dejongvanderput1996, Theorem 3.7.3, line 1571, assumes the morphism quasi-compact; Checks).

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` with `L/K` finite separable and `M = ℤ`, `R⁰f_*ℤ = f_*ℤ` corresponds to the permutation module `ℤ[G_K/G_L]`, induced from the trivial representation of `G_L`, matching §1.2's computation of `H⁰(X_C, ℤ)`; it is the regular representation of `Gal(L/K)`, inflated to `G_K`, only when `L/K` is Galois.
- The trace `t_X : H^{2d}(X_C, ℤ/n(d)) → ℤ/n` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §9.7 (`n` invertible in `K`, `X` smooth proper of pure dimension `d`) comes from a morphism `R^{2d} f_* ℤ/n(d) → ℤ/n`, so `t_X ∘ ρ(σ) = t_X`. For `X = ℙ¹_K`, `t_X(c₁(𝒪(1))) = 1` and `c₁(𝒪(1))` is `ρ`-invariant, consistent with the equivariance. The mod-`p` trace of zavyalov2025, Theorem 5.4.2 and Remark 5.4.1 (line 3800), is a statement of the same kind.
- Convention test: for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `M = μ_n`, both sides are `μ_n(C)` with `ζ ↦ σ(ζ)`; the inverse convention on stalks (`v̄ ↦ v̄ ∘ Spa(σ̂⁻¹)`) is a right action and is not a `Λ[G_K]`-module structure when `G_K` acts through a nonabelian quotient.
- The source `X′` of `g` in the third consequence must be quasi-compact. Take `K = ℚ_p`, `ℓ ≠ p`, `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (so `f = id`), `X′ = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spa(L_m, 𝒪_{L_m})` with `L_m = K(μ_{ℓ^m})`, `g` the structure map and `M = ℤ/ℓ`. Then `H⁰(X′_C, ℤ/ℓ) = ∏_m (ℤ/ℓ)^{Hom_K(L_m, C)}`, whereas the stalk of `f′_*ℤ/ℓ = g_*ℤ/ℓ` at `ξ_C` is `colim_L H⁰(X′_L, ℤ/ℓ)`, the union over finite `L` of the `G_L`-invariants of that product, that is, its elements with open stabiliser. The element equal in each factor to the indicator of the inclusion `L_m ⊆ C` has stabiliser `G_{K(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`, which is not open: `[L_m : K]` is the order of `p` modulo `ℓ^m`, unbounded in `m` (for `p = 2`, `ℓ = 3` it is `2, 6, 18` for `m = 1, 2, 3`). So `g_*ℤ/ℓ` does not correspond to `H⁰(X′_C, ℤ/ℓ)`: its stalk at `ξ_C` is a proper subgroup, and the stalk of the morphism `ℤ/ℓ → g_*ℤ/ℓ` is `g_C^*` only after its target is cut down to that subgroup. For the single quasi-compact component `X′ = Spa(L_1, 𝒪_{L_1})` both sides are `(ℤ/ℓ)^{Hom_K(L_1, C)}` (of order `9` for `p = 2`, `ℓ = 3`), and the morphism is the diagonal map from `ℤ/ℓ`, which is `g_C^*`.

*Needs:* §§0.3, 1.3 (a), 2.1; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.1 (equivalence over `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `R^i f_*` as a sheafification), 1.3 (composition of direct images), 1.5 (strict-localisation stalk theorem, neighbourhood stalk formula, colimit theorem), 9.7 (for the Check).

### 2.4 The dictionary for schemes

Let `f : X → Spec k` be quasi-compact quasi-separated and `M ∈ D⁺(X_ét, Λ)`. Under SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_galois_comparison`: the stalk functor at `Spec k^sep → Spec k`, with the action of stacks03N1, Tag 03QW), prove that `R^i f_* M` corresponds to `(H^i(X_{k^sep}, M), ρ)`, with the same consequences as in §2.3, the morphism `g : X′ → X` of the third being one of quasi-compact quasi-separated `k`-schemes and the composition isomorphism `Rf′_* ≅ Rf_* ∘ Rg_*` that of SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.1 (`derivedPushforward_comp`) (stacks03N1, Theorem 53.1, Tag 03Q9, line 3733; Section 56, Tag 03QW, line 3963; Section 59, Tag 03QQ, line 4216). The route is SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.1 (`higherDirectImage_iso_sheafify`: the stalk is the colimit over the opposite of the category of étale neighbourhoods of `Spec k^sep → Spec k`, in which the neighbourhoods `Spec L`, `L ⊆ k^sep` finite separable, form an initial subcategory equivalent to the opposite of the inclusion poset, as in §0.3) followed by §2.2.

**Checks.**

- `X = Spec k′` with `k′/k` finite separable: `R⁰f_*ℤ ↦ ℤ[G_k/G_{k′}]`.
- For `k = ℝ` and `X = Spec ℂ`, `R⁰f_*μ_4 ↦ μ_4(ℂ)^{Hom_ℝ(ℂ,ℂ)} = μ_4(ℂ)²`, with complex conjugation acting by the swap composed with `ζ ↦ ζ̄` in each coordinate: it sends `(i, 1)` to `(1, −i)`, whereas the swap alone gives `(1, i)`.
- Quasi-compactness is needed: for `k = ℚ`, `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spec ℚ` and `ℱ = μ_{ℓ^m}` on the `m`-th component, the stalk of `f_*ℱ` at `Spec ℚ̄` is `colim_L ∏_m μ_{ℓ^m}(L)`, the elements of `∏_m μ_{ℓ^m}(ℚ̄)` with open stabiliser; it does not contain a compatible system of primitive roots, which lies in `H⁰(X_{ℚ̄}, ℱ) = ∏_m μ_{ℓ^m}(ℚ̄)`.
- The source of `g` in the third consequence must be quasi-compact as well: for `k = ℚ`, `X = Spec ℚ`, `X′ = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spec ℚ(μ_{ℓ^m})` with `g` the structure map and `M = ℤ/ℓ`, `H⁰(X′_{ℚ̄}, ℤ/ℓ) = ∏_m (ℤ/ℓ)^{Hom(ℚ(μ_{ℓ^m}), ℚ̄)}`, the `m`-th factor having `[ℚ(μ_{ℓ^m}) : ℚ] = ℓ^{m−1}(ℓ − 1)` coordinates. The element equal in each factor to the indicator of one embedding has stabiliser `G_{ℚ(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`, which is not open, so it does not lie in the stalk of `g_*ℤ/ℓ` at `Spec ℚ̄`, and `g_*ℤ/ℓ` does not correspond to `H⁰(X′_{ℚ̄}, ℤ/ℓ)`. A constant family, `X′ = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spec ℚ` with `M = ℤ/ℓ`, does not detect this: there `H⁰(X′_{ℚ̄}, ℤ/ℓ) = ∏_m ℤ/ℓ` has trivial action and equals the stalk.

### 2.5 The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence

Let `Λ` be a ring and `𝒢` a sheaf of `Λ`-modules on `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét`. Prove first that there are isomorphisms of `Λ`-modules `H^n(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), 𝒢) ≅ H^n_cont(G_K, 𝒢_{ξ_C})` for all `n ≥ 0`, where `𝒢_{ξ_C}` carries the stalk action of §0.3 and `H^n_cont` is the continuous cohomology of the discrete module `𝒢_{ξ_C}` (ProfiniteCohomology Layer 10); they are natural in `𝒢`, compatible with the connecting maps of short exact sequences of sheaves, and in degree zero they are the equality `𝒢(Spa(K, 𝒪_K)) = (𝒢_{ξ_C})^{G_K}` (dejongvanderput1996, Lemma 3.3.1 (2) and Corollary 3.3.2, pages 28–29, lines 1159–1204, applied to `Sp(K)` as in the proof of Corollary 3.7.5, line 1606; stacks03N1, Lemma 59.2, Tag 03QU, line 4229, for `Spec K`). Route: the equivalence of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1 between sheaves of `Λ`-modules on `Spa(K, 𝒪_K)_ét` and discrete `Λ[G_K]`-modules carries the constant sheaf `Λ` to `Λ` with the trivial action, hence global sections `Hom(Λ, 𝒢)` to invariants, and injective sheaves to injective discrete modules; so `H^n(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), −)` is the derived functor of invariants on discrete `Λ[G_K]`-modules. The functors `H^n_cont(G_K, (−)_{ξ_C})` form a δ-functor (the stalk functor is exact; ProfiniteCohomology §10.3) that is effaceable in positive degrees, since every discrete module `A` embeds in the coinduced module `Coind_1^{G_K} A`, which is acyclic (ProfiniteCohomology §10.4); two universal δ-functors that agree in degree zero agree. The cohomology of a `Λ`-module sheaf is that of its underlying abelian sheaf (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1), and continuous cohomology over `Λ` is that of the underlying abelian group (ProfiniteCohomology §10.4), so the comparison may be made over `ℤ` and is `Λ`-linear by naturality. On the scheme side the same statement is SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_galois_comparison`).

Let `X` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid `K`-space (resp. a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme over `k`) and `ℱ` a sheaf of `Λ`-modules on `X_ét`. Prove that the Leray spectral sequence of `f : X → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (resp. `Spec k`) has, after the identifications `H^p(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), 𝒢) ≅ H^p_cont(G_K, 𝒢_{ξ_C})` of the first statement of this section (resp. SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15, `etale_galois_comparison`) and §2.3 (resp. §2.4), the form

    E_2^{p,q} = H^p_cont(G_K, (H^q(X_C, ℱ_C), ρ)) ⇒ H^{p+q}(X, ℱ),

natural in `ℱ`, with continuous cohomology of the discrete module of §2.1 in the sense of ProfiniteCohomology Layer 10; and that the edge map `H^n(X, ℱ) → H^n(X_C, ℱ_C)^{G_K}` is `pr_C^*` (dejongvanderput1996, Corollary 3.7.5, page 39, whose proof treats overconvergent sheaves; serreGC1997, Chapter I, §2). For rigid `X`, prove that this spectral sequence agrees from `E_2` on with the spectral sequence of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3 (b) for the pro-finite-étale `G_K`-torsor `X_{K^sep} = lim_L X_L → X` (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.14, *proetale-field-extension-slice*), under `H^q(X_{K^sep}, ν^*ℱ) ≅ colim_L H^q(X_L, ℱ_L) ≅ H^q(X_C, ℱ_C)` (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3 colimit formula and §2.1); in particular the `G_K`-module structure on `H^q(X_{K^sep}, ν^*ℱ)` given by the torsor is `ρ`. For a scheme `X`, prove likewise that this spectral sequence agrees from `E_2` on with the continuous case of SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`hochschildSerre_galois_covering`) for the profinite Galois covering `X_{k^sep} = lim_L X_L → X` with group `Gal(k^sep/k) ≅ G_k` (restriction, Conventions), on which `σ` acts on the left by `(σ⁻¹)_X`, so that the induced left action on `H^q(X_{k^sep}, ℱ)`, as in §1.1 (Finite Galois covers), is `ρ`; its continuous cohomology is that of the discrete module of §2.2, through `colim_L H^q(X_L, ℱ) ≅ H^q(X_{k^sep}, ℱ)`.

**Checks.**

- For `K = C` algebraically closed, `G_K = 1` and the right side of the first statement vanishes in positive degrees; so does the left side, because global sections on `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)_ét` are a fibre functor and hence exact (AdicEtaleGeometry A1.7, *geometric-point-etale-split*).
- Degree zero sees the action: for `K = ℚ_3` and `𝒢 = μ_4`, `−1` is not a square in `ℚ_3`, so `H⁰(Spa(ℚ_3, ℤ_3), μ_4) = μ_4(ℚ_3) = {±1}` has order 2 and equals `μ_4(C)^{G_K}`. A version without invariants (the stalk `μ_4(C)`) or with the trivial action on the stalk gives order 4.
- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `f = id`, `R^q f_*ℱ = 0` for `q > 0`, and the spectral sequence collapses to the first statement, `H^p(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ℱ) = H^p_cont(G_K, ℱ_{ξ_C})`.
- For `X = Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` with `L/K` finite separable, `E_2^{p,q}` is concentrated in `q = 0`, where the module is induced from `G_L`, and Shapiro's lemma gives `H^p(G_L, ℱ_{ξ_C})`.
- Scheme side: for `X = Spec k′` with `k′/k` finite separable and `ℱ = ℤ/n`, `X_{k^sep} = ⊔_{Hom_k(k′, k^sep)} Spec k^sep`, so in the Leray form and in the Galois-covering form of SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 alike `E_2^{p,q} = 0` for `q > 0` and `E_2^{p,0} = H^p(G_k, (ℤ/n)^{Hom_k(k′, k^sep)}) = H^p(G_{k′}, ℤ/n)` (Shapiro's lemma), the abutment `H^p(Spec k′, ℤ/n)`. For `X = Spec k`, `ℱ = μ_n` and `k = ℚ_2`, `n = 5`, `E_2^{2,0}` is `H²(G_k, μ_5) ≅ ℤ/5` for the module `ρ`, and `0` for the module `σ ↦ ρ(σ⁻¹)`, as in the direction test below.
- Direction test: for `K = ℚ_2` and `ℱ = μ_5`, `H²(Spa(ℚ_2, ℤ_2), μ_5) ≅ Br(ℚ_2)[5] ≅ ℤ/5` (Kummer), and `E_2^{2,0} = H²(G_{ℚ_2}, μ_5(C))` with `ζ ↦ σ(ζ)` is `ℤ/5` by local duality. With the module `ζ ↦ σ⁻¹(ζ)`, which is `ℤ/5(−1)`, local duality gives `H²(G_{ℚ_2}, ℤ/5(−1)) ≅ H⁰(G_{ℚ_2}, ℤ/5(2))^∨ = 0`, because the cyclotomic character of `ℚ_2` mod `5` is onto `(ℤ/5)^×` and `2² = 4 ≢ 1 (mod 5)`.

### 2.6 Agreement with the scheme adic action and the Frobenius normalisation

Let `X` be a locally noetherian scheme over `k`, `ℓ` a prime invertible in `k`, and `(M_n)_n` coefficient descent data over `k` in the normalised adic-system category of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.1, so that each `M_n ∈ D^b_ctf(X_ét, ℤ/ℓ^n)`. ## Layer 3: Adic and rational coefficients

### 3.1 The ℓ-adic cohomology groups of the geometric fibre

Let `X` be a rigid `K`-space, `ℓ` a prime and `j ∈ ℤ`, with `j = 0` if `ℓ = char K`. Let `T_j = (ℤ/ℓ^n(j))_{n ≥ 1}` be the tower of sheaves on `X_ét` with the reduction transition maps (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4; for `j = 0` the constant tower). Define (hansenzavyalov2023, proof of Theorem 3.3.2, page 39, line 1706; milneLEC §19, page 123):

- the naive group `H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) := lim_n H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))`, with the inverse-limit topology of the discrete groups `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))`, the `ℤ_ℓ`-module structure through the quotients `ℤ/ℓ^n`, and the action `lim_n ρ`;
- the continuous group `H^i_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) := H^i(X_C, Rlim_n (T_j)_C) = H^i(Rlim_n RΓ(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j)))` (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3 `ClassicalDerived.inverseLimits`; derived global sections commute with derived limits), with the action `(σ_X)^♮` of §1.1 applied to `Rlim_n (T_j)_C` and the isomorphisms `σ_X^{-1} Rlim_n (T_j)_C ≅ Rlim_n σ_X^{-1}(T_j)_C ≅ Rlim_n (T_j)_C` (`σ_X^{-1}` is an equivalence of topoi, so commutes with derived limits; the second map is `Rlim` of the levelwise `c_σ`).

Prove the following.

- `lim_n ρ` and the action on `H^i_cont` are left actions by `ℤ_ℓ`-linear automorphisms.
- The Milnor sequence `0 → lim¹_n H^{i−1}(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j)) → H^i_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) → H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) → 0` is exact and `G_K`-equivariant: it is the Milnor sequence of EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3 (`ClassicalDerived.inverseLimits`, with its module comparison and its compatibility with group actions through compatible tower maps) for the tower `RΓ(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))`, on which `σ` acts levelwise by `ρ(σ)`. The two groups agree when the system `H^{i−1}(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` is Mittag–Leffler (Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isMittagLeffler`), in particular when its terms are finite (`TauCeti.ModuleTower.isMittagLeffler_of_isArtinian`).
- Derived reduction: `RΓ_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) ⊗^L_{ℤ_ℓ} ℤ/ℓ^m ≅ RΓ(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^m(j))` equivariantly, giving the equivariant short exact sequences `0 → H^i_cont/ℓ^m → H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^m(j)) → H^{i+1}_cont[ℓ^m] → 0`.
- For `K ⊇ ℚ_p`, `ℓ = p` and `X` locally noetherian, `H^i_cont(X_C, ℤ_p(j)) ≅ H^i(X_{C,proét}, Ẑ_p(j))` with `Ẑ_p = lim_r ν^*ℤ/p^r` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3, `ρ`-equivariantly for the pro-étale action of §1.2.
- For a finite extension `L ⊆ K̄`, the groups for `X_L` are those for `X` with the restricted action (§1.3 (e)).

**Checks.**

- `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, `ℓ` invertible in `K`, `j = 1`: `H⁰(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(1)) = lim μ_{ℓ^n}(C) = ℤ_ℓ(1)` along the `ℓ`-th power maps `μ_{ℓ^{n+1}} → μ_{ℓ^n}`, free of rank one, with `σ` acting through the cyclotomic character.
- `X = ∅`: all groups vanish. For `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` the naive `H⁰(X_C, ℤ_ℓ) = ∏_m ℤ_ℓ` carries the inverse-limit topology of the discrete groups `H⁰(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n) = ∏_m ℤ/ℓ^n`, for which the kernels `ℓ^n ∏_m ℤ_ℓ` of the projections form a base of open subgroups: this is the `ℓ`-adic topology, strictly finer than the product topology (every product-open subgroup contains some `∏_{m > M} ℤ_ℓ`, which `ℓ ∏_m ℤ_ℓ` does not), and it is not compact, because `∏_m ℤ_ℓ / ℓ ∏_m ℤ_ℓ = ∏_m ℤ/ℓ` is an infinite discrete quotient. The group is not finitely generated.
- The Milnor term is not zero in general: the tower of groups `⋯ → ℤ → ℤ → ℤ` with multiplication-by-`ℓ` maps has `lim = 0` and `lim¹ = ℤ_ℓ/ℤ ≠ 0`, so the naive and continuous constructions differ for general towers; for towers of finite groups (the case of §4.3) `lim¹ = 0`.
- For `ℓ = char K = p` and `X` the closed unit disc, `H¹(X_C, ℤ/p) = C⟨T⟩/{g^p − g}` (Artin–Schreier) is defined and infinite; no twist `ℤ/p(1)` is defined.

*Needs:* §§1.1–1.3; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.3, 1.4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3 (`ClassicalDerived.inverseLimits` and its Milnor sequence); Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ModuleTower.lim`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.limOne`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.isMittagLeffler_of_isArtinian`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isMittagLeffler`.

### 3.2 Continuity of the ℓ-adic action

With the notation of §3.1 and `X` quasi-compact quasi-separated, prove (serreGC1997, Chapter I, §2; dejongvanderput1996, Corollary 3.7.5, page 39, whose proof gives the discreteness for overconvergent sheaves, lines 1602–1605; for every sheaf it is §2.1):

- the action map `G_K × H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) → H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` is jointly continuous: each component `G_K × lim_m H^i_m → H^i_n` factors through the continuous action on the discrete module `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` of §2.1;
- `H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` with this topology and action is an object of Mathlib's `TopRep ℤ_ℓ G_K` (a topological additive group with `ContinuousSMul ℤ_ℓ` and a `ContRepresentation`), hence a coefficient object for ProfiniteCohomology Layer 1's continuous cohomology;
- the representation `ρ` on the whole group `H := H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))`, with its inverse-limit topology and the joint continuity of the first bullet, is a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_ℓ H`, torsion included, and `TauCeti.ContinuousRep.toContRepresentation` of it is the `ContRepresentation` of the `TopRep` object of the second bullet; no finiteness hypothesis enters. When `H` is moreover finite free over `ℤ_ℓ` and its inverse-limit topology is the `ℓ`-adic topology (both hold when the finite levels are finite and `H` is torsion free, §4.3), it lies in the finite projective subcategory used by EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0 and ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.1;
- pullbacks, sheaf maps, connecting maps of short exact sequences of towers and cup products (§1.3) are continuous equivariant maps in the limit.

**Checks.**

- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` and `j = 1`, the action on `ℤ_ℓ(1)` is the continuous cyclotomic character `TauCeti.continuousLocalCyclotomicCharacter ℓ K`.
- The `ℤ_ℓ^×`-action on `ℚ_ℓ` with the discrete topology has continuous operators but is not jointly continuous (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0 Check): joint continuity is the content of the first bullet, not a consequence of the continuity of each `ρ(σ)`.
- Quasi-compactness is used: for `K = ℚ_p`, `ℓ ≠ p` and `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spa(L_m, 𝒪_{L_m})` with `L_m = K(μ_{ℓ^m})`, the element of `H⁰(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` equal to the indicator of one point over each `m` has stabiliser `G_{K(μ_{ℓ^∞})}`, which is not open, so the finite level is not a discrete `G_K`-module and the first bullet's argument does not apply.
- The carrier admits torsion: `ℤ_ℓ/ℓ` with the discrete topology and the trivial action of `G_K` (the finite level `H⁰(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, viewed as a `ℤ_ℓ`-module) has action map `(σ, x) ↦ x`, which is continuous, so it is a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_ℓ (ℤ_ℓ/ℓ)`, although `ℓ · 1 = 0` and it is not projective over `ℤ_ℓ`. A carrier restricted to finite projective modules would exclude it, and the integral group `H²(X_C, ℤ_2)` of the Enriques example of §4.3.

*Needs:* §§2.1, 3.1; Mathlib `TopRep`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ContinuousRep`, `TauCeti.ContinuousRep.toContRepresentation`; ProfiniteCohomology Layers 0–1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0 (finite projective representations); ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.1.

### 3.3 Rational coefficients

Let `A` be a topological `ℤ_ℓ`-module (a topological additive group with continuous scalar multiplication) with a `ℤ_ℓ`-linear action of `G_K` whose action map `G_K × A → A` is jointly continuous, that is, with a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_ℓ A`; the case in view is `A = H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` of §3.2, for `X` quasi-compact quasi-separated, with its inverse-limit topology. Write `V := A ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ = A[1/ℓ]`, the algebraic localisation, and `j_A : A → V` for the canonical map. Give `V` the topology whose basic neighbourhoods of zero are the `ℤ_ℓ`-submodules `Ω ⊆ V` with `⋃_{n ≥ 0} ℓ^{−n} Ω = V` and `j_A^{-1}(ℓ^r Ω)` open in `A` for every `r ≥ 0`. It is the finest topology making `V` a topological `ℚ_ℓ`-vector space with a basis of open `ℤ_ℓ`-submodules (a non-archimedean locally convex space) for which `j_A` is continuous. Define `H^i(X_C, ℚ_ℓ(j)) := H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ` with this topology and the action `σ ↦ ρ(σ) ⊗ id` (hansenzavyalov2023, line 1706, for the algebraic localisation). Prove:

- `V` is a topological `ℚ_ℓ`-vector space and `j_A` is continuous; every continuous `ℤ_ℓ`-linear map from `A` to a topological `ℚ_ℓ`-vector space with a basis of open `ℤ_ℓ`-submodules extends uniquely to a continuous `ℚ_ℓ`-linear map from `V`. Hence the construction is functorial for the continuous equivariant maps of §3.2.
- The action of `G_K` on `V` is by continuous `ℚ_ℓ`-linear automorphisms and is jointly continuous, so `V` is an object of `TopRep ℚ_ℓ G_K` and the action is a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℚ_ℓ V`, for every such `A`, Hausdorff or not. Route: `G_K` is compact. For a basic `Ω`, each open subgroup `j_A^{-1}(ℓ^r Ω)` contains a `G_K`-stable open subgroup, by joint continuity at `(σ, 0)` for each `σ` and a finite subcover, and each orbit `G_K · a` is compact and so lies in one of the increasing open subgroups `j_A^{-1}(ℓ^{−s} Ω)`. Hence `⋂_{σ ∈ G_K} σ(Ω)` is a `G_K`-stable basic neighbourhood. Continuity of the orbit maps of `V` follows from that on `A`.
- `j_A(A)` need not be open in `V`, and `V` need not be Hausdorff. If `A` is finitely generated over `ℤ_ℓ` and carries its `ℓ`-adic topology, then `j_A(A)` is an open lattice, `V` is finite-dimensional with its unique Hausdorff vector-space topology, and `V` lies in the finite projective subcategory of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0. For `A = H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` finitely generated the topology is automatically the `ℓ`-adic one: the inverse-limit topology is Hausdorff, and it is coarser than the compact `ℓ`-adic topology because each `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` is killed by `ℓ^n`. §4.3 gives conditions for finite generation.

**Checks.**

- `A = ℤ_ℓ/ℓ` gives `V = 0`. `A = ℤ_ℓ(1)` gives `ℚ_ℓ(1)` with its `ℓ`-adic topology, since the proper basic neighbourhoods are the `ℓ^k ℤ_ℓ(1)`; it is a one-dimensional representation with character `χ_ℓ`, and `j_A(A)` is open.
- Negative control for an open-image prescription: `A = ∏_{m ≥ 1} ℤ_ℓ` with the product topology and trivial action is the inverse limit of the finite discrete groups `(ℤ/ℓ^n)^n` under truncation and reduction. Every open subgroup of `A` contains some unit vector `e_m ∉ ℓA`, so `ℓA` is not open; a vector-space topology in which `j_A(A)` is open with its own topology would make multiplication by `ℓ⁻¹` continuous and `ℓA` open. In the topology defined here the lattices `{v : v_1, …, v_M ∈ ℓ^k ℤ_ℓ}` are basic, `V` is Hausdorff, and every basic `Ω` contains some `ℓ⁻¹ e_m ∉ j_A(A)`, so `j_A(A)` is not open.
- For `A = ∏_ℕ ℤ_ℓ`, `V` (sequences with a common denominator) is a proper subspace of `∏_ℕ ℚ_ℓ`, containing no `(ℓ^{−m})_m`: the definition is the tensor product, not the product of rationalisations.
- For `A = ℤ_ℓ` the topology on `V = ℚ_ℓ` is the `ℓ`-adic one; with the discrete topology instead, scalar multiplication `ℤ_ℓ × ℚ_ℓ → ℚ_ℓ` is not continuous at `(0, 1)`, since `U · 1 = U ≠ {0}` for every open `U ∋ 0` of `ℤ_ℓ`.
- For `A = ℚ_ℓ` with the indiscrete topology (a topological `ℤ_ℓ`-module), the only basic neighbourhood is `V` itself, so `V = ℚ_ℓ` is indiscrete: Hausdorffness is not automatic for general `A`.

## Layer 4: Twists, comparison and finiteness

### 4.1 Equivariant twisting

Let `X` be a rigid `K`-space, `n` invertible in `K`, `Λ` a `ℤ/n`-algebra, `ℱ` a sheaf of `Λ`-modules on `X_ét` and `j ∈ ℤ`. Let `Λ(j)(C) := Λ ⊗_{ℤ/n} μ_n(C)^{⊗j}` (dual powers for `j < 0`), a free `Λ`-module of rank one with `σ` acting by `ζ ↦ σ(ζ)` on `μ_n(C)`. Prove (stacks03N1, Example 59.3, line 4241; zavyalov2025, §5.4, line 3800):

- on `X_C`, the inclusion of global sections induces a canonical isomorphism of sheaves `(μ_n(C))_{X_C} ≅ μ_{n,X_C} = (μ_n)_C`, where the left side is the constant sheaf; it intertwines `c_σ` with `σ` acting on `μ_n(C)`. No primitive root of unity is chosen.
- the resulting isomorphism `H^i(X_C, ℱ(j)_C) ≅ H^i(X_C, ℱ_C) ⊗_{ℤ/n} μ_n(C)^{⊗j}`, with `Λ` acting through the first factor (cup product over the commutative ring `ℤ/n` with the constant sheaf, which is free of rank one over `ℤ/n`; for commutative `Λ` the right side is `H^i(X_C, ℱ_C) ⊗_Λ Λ(j)(C)`), is `G_K`-equivariant, with `ρ ⊗ (ζ ↦ σ(ζ))` on the right. No tensor product over a noncommutative `Λ` is used;
- for `ℓ` invertible in `K`, the limit isomorphisms `H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) ≅ H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ) ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℤ_ℓ(j)`, the same for `H^i_cont`, and `H^i(X_C, ℚ_ℓ(j)) ≅ H^i(X_C, ℚ_ℓ) ⊗_{ℚ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ(j)` are `G_K`-equivariant, where `ℤ_ℓ(j)` is the Tate twist of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.2 (*TateTwist.zlOne*, identified with `TauCeti.PadicTateTwist ℓ K̄`, using `μ_{ℓ^m}(K̄) = μ_{ℓ^m}(C)` for `ℓ` invertible in `K`);
- the same statements on schemes, with `C` replaced by `k^sep` and the twists of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.3;
- convention: an arithmetic Frobenius lift of a local field `K` with residue field `𝔽_q`, `ℓ ∤ q`, acts on `H⁰(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(1)) = ℤ_ℓ(1)` for `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` by `q` (`TauCeti.IsArithFrobeniusLift.coe_localCyclotomicCharacter`), and a geometric Frobenius by `q⁻¹`, matching EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.6.

**Checks.**

- Replacing a primitive `ζ ∈ μ_p(C)` by `ζ^u` multiplies the coordinate of a fixed element of `𝔽_p(1)` by `u⁻¹` (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.0.3); the canonical isomorphism of the first bullet involves no such choice.
- For `X = ℙ¹_K`, `H²(X_C, ℤ/n(1)) ≅ ℤ/n` with trivial action (first Chern class of `𝒪(1)`) and `H²(X_C, ℤ/n) ≅ ℤ/n(−1)`; the twisting isomorphism with `j = 1` identifies the first with the second tensored with `μ_n(C)`. For `n = 7` and an arithmetic Frobenius lift of `ℚ_2`, the scalars are `1 = 4 · 2`.
- For `j = 0` the isomorphism is the identity; for `n = 1` all groups vanish.
- For an elliptic curve `E/K` and `n` invertible in `K`, `H¹(E_C, μ_n) ≅ E[n](K̄)` has determinant the mod-`n` cyclotomic character `χ_n` (Weil pairing), so `H¹(E_C, ℤ/n) ≅ H¹(E_C, μ_n) ⊗ μ_n^{⊗(−1)}` has determinant `χ_n · χ_n^{−2} = χ_n^{−1}`. Twisting in the wrong direction (`⊗ μ_n` instead of `⊗ μ_n^{⊗(−1)}`) would give `χ_n³`; for `n = 7` and `K = ℚ_2` the two values at an arithmetic Frobenius lift are `2^{−1} ≡ 4` and `2³ ≡ 1 (mod 7)`.

### 4.2 Equivariant comparison with analytifications

Let `Y` be a proper `K`-scheme, `f : Y → Spec K` its structure map, `Λ` a torsion ring and `M ∈ D⁺(Y_ét, Λ)`. Apply Huber's comparison (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.2) to the locally ringed morphism `φ : R := Spa(C, 𝒪_C) → S := Spec K`, with `φ_Y : Y ×_S R → Y` the induced morphism: since `Y ×_{Spec K} Spa(C, 𝒪_C) = (Y^an)_C` (AdicSpacesPartII §R1.2, *scheme-fibre-product-analytification*, applied twice) and `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)_ét` has global sections equal to the stalk at the geometric point `Spec C → Spec K`, it gives

    H^i(Y_{K^sep}, M) ≅ (R^i f_* M)_{Spec C} ≅ H^i((Y^an)_C, M^an),

the first isomorphism being §2.4 (with `C ⊇ K^sep`). Prove that this composite is `G_K`-equivariant for the scheme action of §1.4 on the left and `ρ` on the right. Route: for `σ ∈ G_K`, `s := Spa(σ̂)` is an automorphism of `R` over `S`, and `σ_{Y^an} = id_Y × s` is the automorphism of `(Y^an)_C = Y ×_S R` over `Y` of §1.2. The square formed by `σ_{Y^an}`, `s` and two copies of `f^ad` is cartesian with invertible horizontal maps, so its base-change map `s^{-1} R⁺f^ad_* → R⁺f^ad_* σ_{Y^an}^{-1}` is an isomorphism. Pasting this square with Huber's square gives Huber's square again, because `φ ∘ s = φ` and `φ_Y ∘ σ_{Y^an} = φ_Y`, the inverse images being matched by the coherence isomorphisms `s^{-1} φ^{-1} ≅ (φ ∘ s)^{-1}` and `σ_{Y^an}^{-1} φ_Y^{-1} ≅ (φ_Y ∘ σ_{Y^an})^{-1}`. The base-change map of a pasted square is the pasting of the base-change maps, a formal property of mates (Mathlib `CategoryTheory.mateEquiv_vcomp` and `CategoryTheory.mateEquiv_hcomp`, applied to the adjunctions `g^{-1} ⊣ R⁺g_*` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.1 and SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.1), so Huber's map commutes with the two `s`-transports. On global sections over `R`, the transport on the left is the stalk action of §2.4 (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15; stacks03N1, Tag 03QW, line 3966) on `(R^i f_* M)_{Spec C}`, a stalk of a sheaf on `(Spec K)_ét`: `σ` precomposes the geometric point `Spec C → Spec K` with `Spec(σ̂)`, and the stalks at `Spec C` and at `Spec K^sep` are identified along `K^sep ⊆ C`, compatibly because `σ̂` restricts to `σ` on `K^sep`. On the right it is `c_σ ∘ σ_{Y^an}^* = ρ(σ)`. Prove also that the composite factors through `H^i(Y_C, M)`, with `G_K` acting by `(id_Y × Spec σ̂)^♮` (§1.1): `H^i(Y_{K̄}, M) → H^i(Y_C, M)` is equivariant by §1.1 (Morphisms of covers) and an isomorphism by proper base change for `Spec C → Spec K̄` (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.2.1; milneLEC Corollary 17.8 (b), page 116, line 4534, is the case of constructible sheaves on complete varieties), and `H^i(Y_C, M) ≅ H^i((Y^an)_C, M^an)` is §8.2 for `Spa(C, 𝒪_C) → Spec C`. (huber1996, Theorem 3.7.2, page 226, through ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.2; berkovich1993, Theorem 7.1.1, page 128, line 4584, compares compactly supported cohomology of a compactifiable scheme and of its analytification for every abelian torsion sheaf, which for proper `Y` is ordinary cohomology.)

Without properness, let `Y` be a `K`-scheme of finite type, `n` an integer invertible in `K` and `F` a constructible sheaf of `ℤ/n`-modules on `Y_ét`. Prove that the composite

    H^i(Y_{K^sep}, F) → H^i(Y_C, F) → H^i((Y^an)_C, F^an)

of the pullback along `pr : Y_C → Y_{K^sep}` and the comparison of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.1 for `Y_C → Spec C` (with `k = C`, and `(Y_C)^an = (Y^an)_C` by AdicSpacesPartII §R1.2) is a `G_K`-equivariant isomorphism, for the scheme action of §1.4 on the left, `(id_Y × Spec σ̂)^♮` (§1.1) in the middle and `ρ` on the right. The first map is an isomorphism by the invariance of étale cohomology under extension of separably closed fields (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.2.2, which needs the torsion orders invertible in `K`), and the second by §11.1 (huber1996, Theorem 3.8.1, as restated by ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.1, for every `n` invertible in `K`; berkovich1993, Corollary 7.5.4, page 150, line 5339, is the case of torsion orders prime to the residue characteristic (berkovich1993, lines 113 and 119), so it does not cover `n = p` when `char K = 0`). The stalk route of the proper case is not available: `Y^an` is not quasi-compact when `Y` is not proper (for `Y = 𝔸¹_K`), so §2.3 does not identify the stalk of `R^i f^an_* F^an` at `ξ_C`, for `f^an : Y^an → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`, with `H^i((Y^an)_C, F^an)`. Route: on global sections over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, the base-change map of §11.1 for `Y_C → Spec C` is the pullback along the analytification morphism of étale sites `φ_C : ((Y^an)_C)_ét → (Y_C)_ét`, because inverse image from `(Spec C)_ét` to `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)_ét` is an equivalence (every étale covering of either splits; AdicEtaleGeometry A1.7, *geometric-point-etale-split*). The squares `pr ∘ (id_Y × Spec σ̂) = (id_Y × Spec(σ|_{K^sep})) ∘ pr` and `φ_C ∘ σ_{Y^an} = (id_Y × Spec σ̂) ∘ φ_C` commute, both composites in each being induced by `σ̂` on the second factor, so the two pullbacks intertwine the three actions by §1.1 (Morphisms of covers): the first square is one of schemes over `Y`, and the second is a square of morphisms of sites from setting (a) to setting (c), over the analytification morphism `(Y^an)_ét → Y_ét`; the analytification morphisms of étale sites of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§8.2 and 11.1 are morphisms of sites, so their inverse images are exact and compose with those of settings (a) and (c) up to coherent isomorphism, and EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.4 gives the enhanced adjunction for each of them.

**Checks.**

- `Y = Spec L` with `L/K` finite separable: all groups are `Λ^{Hom_K(L, K̄)}` in degree zero, with the permutation action of §1.2 on the embeddings (the representation induced from the trivial representation of `G_L`).
- For `Y` a smooth proper geometrically connected curve and `n` invertible in `K`, the groups `H¹(−, μ_n)` are `J(K̄)[n] = J(C)[n] = Pic⁰((Y^an)_C)[n]` by Kummer theory on each side (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.16, `curve_Gm_cohomology`; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.4) and rigid GAGA, with `G_K` acting on points; for an elliptic curve the determinant is `χ_n` on each side.
- `Y = ℙ¹_K`, `M = ℤ/n` and `n` invertible in `K`: the three groups `H²` are `ℤ/n(−1)`, and an arithmetic Frobenius lift of a local field with residue field `𝔽_q`, `n` prime to `q`, acts by `q⁻¹` on each; `p`-torsion coefficients with `char K = 0` are included, as §8.2 imposes no condition on the torsion.
- `Y = ∅`: all groups vanish.
- Nonproper convention witness: for `Y = 𝔾_{m,K} = Spec K[T, T⁻¹]` and `F = μ_n`, the units of `K^sep[T, T⁻¹]` are the `c T^k`, `(K^sep)^×` is `n`-divisible and the Picard group vanishes, so Kummer theory gives `H¹(𝔾_{m,K^sep}, μ_n) ≅ ℤ/n`, generated by the class of `T`. As `T` is defined over `K`, `G_K` acts trivially, and by the comparison `H¹((𝔾_m^an)_C, μ_n) ≅ ℤ/n` with trivial action. Twisting by `μ_n^{⊗(−1)}`, `H¹((𝔾_m^an)_C, ℤ/n) ≅ ℤ/n(−1)`, on which an arithmetic Frobenius lift of a local field with residue field `𝔽_q`, `n` prime to `q`, acts by `q⁻¹`; the inverse convention `σ ↦ ρ(σ⁻¹)` would give `q`.
- For `Y = 𝔸¹_K` and `F = ℤ/n`, `n` invertible in `K` (so `n = p` is allowed when `char K = 0`), the left side is `ℤ/n` in degree zero and vanishes in degree one (Kummer theory, as `ℤ/n ≅ μ_n` over `K^sep` up to a twist: the units of `K^sep[T]` are the `n`-divisible constants and the Picard group vanishes), so the same holds for `(𝔸¹)^an_C`, the union of the closed discs of all radii. The closed unit disc alone, which is not an analytification, has infinite `H¹(−, ℤ/p)` for `n = p` and `char K = 0` (§4.3, Checks): the nonproper comparison is a statement about analytifications of schemes, not about their affinoid pieces.
- Negative control for the invertibility of `n`: for `K = 𝔽_p((t))`, `n = p` and `Y = 𝔸¹_K`, Artin–Schreier theory gives `H¹(𝔸¹_{K^sep}, ℤ/p) = K^sep[T]/{g^p − g}` and `H¹(𝔸¹_C, ℤ/p) = C[T]/{g^p − g}`. Sending `a T^{m p^k}` (`m` prime to `p`) to `a^{1/p^k} T^m` and constants to `0` identifies the second with `⊕_{m prime to p} C · T^m`, and the image of the first consists of the sums with coefficients in the perfect closure `K̄` of `K^sep`. Since `C ≠ K̄`, the class of `c T` for `c ∈ C ∖ K̄` is not in the image: the first map of the composite is not surjective, and invariance under extension of separably closed fields fails without the hypothesis.

### 4.3 From finiteness to representations

Let `X` be a quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid `K`-space, `ℓ` a prime, `j ∈ ℤ` (with `j = 0` if `ℓ = char K`) and `i ≥ 0`. Prove the following; a route uses dévissage, the Milnor sequence and derived reduction of §3.1, and the topological Nakayama lemma `Module.Finite.of_quotient_smul_top` of Tau Ceti (milneLEC, Theorem 19.2, page 125, line 4860, is the corresponding finite-generation statement for flat `ℤ_ℓ`-sheaves on a variety `X` over a separably closed field `k`, assuming `X` is complete or `ℓ ≠ char k`; hansenzavyalov2023, page 39, line 1706, uses the definitions `H^i(−, ℤ_ℓ) = lim_n H^i(−, ℤ/ℓ^n)` and `H^i(−, ℚ_ℓ) = H^i(−, ℤ_ℓ)[1/ℓ]` of §§3.1 and 3.3 for qcqs rigid spaces over a discretely valued `p`-adic field with `ℓ ≠ p`):

- (a) if `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` is finite, then every `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` is finite: dévissage along `0 → ℤ/ℓ^{n−1}(j) → ℤ/ℓ^n(j) → ℤ/ℓ(j) → 0`, with `ℤ/ℓ(j)_C ≅ ℤ/ℓ_C` as sheaves of groups (§4.1);
- (b) if `H^{i−1}(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` and `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` are finite, then `H^i_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) = H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` (Mittag–Leffler), and it is a finitely generated `ℤ_ℓ`-module whose inverse-limit topology is the `ℓ`-adic topology. Route: `H := H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` is a closed subgroup of the product of the finite discrete groups `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` (finite by (a)), hence compact and Hausdorff, with continuous `ℤ_ℓ`-scalar multiplication; `ℤ_ℓ` is compact and the ideal `(ℓ)` is generated by one topologically nilpotent element; and `H/ℓH` embeds in the finite group `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ(j))` by the derived reduction of §3.1. So `Module.Finite.of_quotient_smul_top`, applied to the ring `ℤ_ℓ`, the ideal `(ℓ)` and the module `H`, gives finite generation; the inverse-limit topology is then the `ℓ`-adic one, being Hausdorff and coarser than the compact `ℓ`-adic topology;
- (c) under (b), `H := H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` with its `ℓ`-adic topology is a finitely generated `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_ℓ H` (§3.2), torsion included. Its torsion submodule `H_tors` is finite and `G_K`-stable; the quotient `H/H_tors` is finite free over `ℤ_ℓ` and, with the quotient topology (which is its `ℓ`-adic topology), a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_ℓ (H/H_tors)` in the finite projective subcategory of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0 and ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.1; the action on the quotient is jointly continuous because `id × (H → H/H_tors)` is an open surjection. Finally `H^i(X_C, ℚ_ℓ(j)) = H ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ = (H/H_tors) ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ` is a finite-dimensional `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℚ_ℓ` with its unique Hausdorff topology (§3.3);
- (d) if `H^q(X_C, ℤ/ℓ) = 0` for every `q > N`, then `H^q(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` (every `n ≥ 1`), `H^q(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))`, `H^q_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` and `H^q(X_C, ℚ_ℓ(j))` vanish for every `q > N`, with no finiteness hypothesis in any degree. Route: the finite levels vanish by the dévissage of (a), hence so do the naive and the rational groups. The sequence `0 → ℤ/ℓ(j) → ℤ/ℓ^{n+1}(j) → ℤ/ℓ^n(j) → 0`, whose first map is multiplication by `ℓ^n` and whose second is the reduction of §3.1, gives the exact sequence `H^N(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^{n+1}(j)) → H^N(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j)) → H^{N+1}(X_C, ℤ/ℓ(j)) = 0` (the last group vanishes as in (a)), so every transition map of the tower `(H^N(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j)))_n` is surjective. The tower is therefore Mittag–Leffler and its `lim¹` vanishes (`TauCeti.ModuleTower.isMittagLeffler_of_surjective`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isMittagLeffler`), so the Milnor sequence of §3.1 gives `H^{N+1}_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) = 0`; for `q ≥ N + 2` both outer terms of the Milnor sequence vanish.

**Checks.**

- `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`: `H⁰ = ℤ_ℓ(j)`, a free rank-one representation, and higher groups vanish.
- A finitely generated group with torsion: for `char K = 0`, `ℓ = 2` and `X` the analytification of an Enriques surface over `K`, `H := H²(X_C, ℤ_2) ≅ ℤ_2^{10} ⊕ ℤ/2` as a `ℤ_2`-module. By §3.2 and (c) it is a `TauCeti.ContinuousRep G_K ℤ_2 H` whose torsion `ℤ/2` is nonzero, so it lies outside the finite projective subcategory of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0, while `H/H_tors ≅ ℤ_2^{10}` lies in it. The torsion survives in the integral representation and dies in `H²(X_C, ℚ_2)`, of dimension `10`. A carrier restricted to finite projective modules would exclude the integral representation `H` itself.
- In (d) no finiteness is needed in degree `N`. For `K = 𝔽_2((t))`, `ℓ = 2 = char K`, `j = 0` and `X` the closed unit disc, the Artin–Schreier sequence `0 → 𝔽_2 → 𝒪 → 𝒪 → 0` (`g ↦ g² − g`) on `X_{C,ét}` and the vanishing of `H^q(X_{C,ét}, 𝒪)` for `q ≥ 1` on the affinoid `X_C` give `H^q(X_C, 𝔽_2) = 0` for `q ≥ 2`, so `N = 1`, and `H¹(X_C, 𝔽_2) = C⟨T⟩/{g² − g}`. This `H¹` is infinite: if `Σ_m c_m T^{2m+1}` (finitely many `c_m ∈ 𝔽_2`) were `g² − g` with `g = Σ_r a_r T^r`, comparing coefficients would give `a_{2^k(2m+1)} = c_m` for every `k ≥ 0`, and `a_r → 0` forces every `c_m = 0`, so the classes of `T, T³, T⁵, …` are independent. Still, `H²(X_C, 𝔽_2) = 0` makes the transitions `H¹(X_C, ℤ/2^{n+1}) → H¹(X_C, ℤ/2^n)` surjective, and `H²_cont(X_C, ℤ_2) = 0`; a version of (d) that asked for `H^N(X_C, ℤ/ℓ)` to be finite would say nothing in this degree.
- For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (`N = 0`) the reductions `ℤ/ℓ^{n+1}(j)(C) → ℤ/ℓ^n(j)(C)` are surjective and `H¹_cont(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j)) = 0`. The multiplication-by-`ℓ` tower `⋯ → ℤ → ℤ` of §3.1, with `lim¹ = ℤ_ℓ/ℤ ≠ 0`, has non-surjective transitions, so it is not the degree-`N` tower of any `X` satisfying the hypothesis of (d).
- For `X` the closed unit disc, `char K = 0` and `ℓ = p` the residue characteristic, the hypothesis of (a) fails in degree one. Kummer theory embeds `𝒪(X_C)^×/𝒪(X_C)^{×p}` in `H¹(X_C, μ_p)`, and for a fixed `a ∈ C` with `|p|^{p/(p−1)} < |a| < 1` the classes of the units `1 + aT^m`, `m` prime to `p`, are independent: if a nontrivial product `u` of them with exponents in `{1, …, p − 1}` were `c(1 + h)^p` with `c ∈ C^×`, `h(0) = 0` and sup norm `|h| < 1` (so `c = 1`), then `|u − 1| = |a|` would rule out `|h| ≤ |p|^{1/(p−1)}` (then `|(1 + h)^p − 1| ≤ |p|^{p/(p−1)}`), and for larger `|h|` the reduction of `(u − 1)/a` would be a `p`-th power in the residue polynomial ring, hence a polynomial in `T^p`, whereas it is a nonzero combination of monomials `T^m` with `m` prime to `p`. So `H¹(X_C, ℤ/p) ≅ H¹(X_C, μ_p)` is infinite. And `H¹(X_C, ℤ_p) ≅ H¹(X_C, ℤ_p(1))` (§4.1) is not finitely generated: the compatible Kummer classes of these units lie in it and map to infinitely many independent classes of `H¹(X_C, μ_p)`, whereas a finitely generated `H` maps to a group killed by `p` through the finite `H/pH`.

*Needs:* §§3.1–3.3, 4.1; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §0.3 (`ClassicalDerived.inverseLimits` and its Milnor sequence); Tau Ceti `Module.Finite.of_quotient_smul_top`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.isMittagLeffler_of_surjective`, `TauCeti.ModuleTower.subsingleton_limOne_of_isMittagLeffler`, `TauCeti.ContinuousRep`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.0; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations §1.1.

### 4.4 Finiteness instances

Let `X` be a rigid `K`-space of dimension `d` and `ℓ` a prime. Prove that the hypotheses of §4.3 (a)–(d), with `N = 2d`, hold in the following cases; hence `H^i(X_C, ℤ/ℓ^n(j))` is finite, `H^i(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(j))` finitely generated and `H^i(X_C, ℚ_ℓ(j))` a finite-dimensional continuous `G_K`-representation, vanishing for `i > 2d`:

- (a) `X` smooth proper and `ℓ` invertible in `𝒪_K`: finiteness is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §9.7 (berkovich1993, Corollary 7.4.4, page 143, line 5110); vanishing above `2d` is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.4 (huber1996, Corollary 2.8.3, as attested by hansenzavyalov2023, line 1708);
- (b) `X` smooth proper, `char K = 0` and `ℓ = p` the residue characteristic: AInfCohomology §AI.3.1 (`AInf.primitive_mod_p_comparison`) for `X_C` over the complete algebraically closed extension `C` of `ℚ_p` (scholze2013, Theorem 5.1, page 28, line 1279);
- (c) `X = Y^an` for a proper `K`-scheme `Y`, with any prime `ℓ` and `j = 0` when `ℓ = char K`: for `ℓ ≠ char K`, finiteness is §4.2 and EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.3 (`VanishingCycles.etaleFiniteness`) for `Y_{K̄}` (milneLEC Theorem 19.1 (a), page 123, line 4783). For `ℓ = char K`, derive finiteness from the Artin–Schreier sequence and finite-dimensional coherent cohomology of the proper scheme `Y_{K̄}` (SchemeAndStackFoundations §§2.6, 2.10–2.11), then apply §4.2 and §4.3 (a). The linear-algebra step to prove is that, for an algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic `p > 0`, a finite-dimensional `k`-vector space `V` and an additive map `F` satisfying `F(av) = a^p F(v)`, `F − 1` is surjective and its kernel has `𝔽_p`-dimension at most `dim_k V`; the Artin–Schreier sequence therefore identifies `H^q(Y_{K̄}, 𝔽_p)` with this finite kernel on `H^q(Y_{K̄}, 𝒪)` (stacks03N1, Lemmas 63.2 and 63.6, Tags 0A3L and 0A3P, lines 4455 and 4569). Vanishing above `2d` is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.4 applied to `Y^an → Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` with the stalk identification of §2.3, which imposes no condition on the torsion, or, when `ℓ ≠ char K`, on the scheme side EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.3.0 (`VanishingCycles.etaleCohomologicalDimension`) for `Y_{K̄}` with §4.2 (milneLEC Theorem 15.1, page 105, line 4133);
- (d) `X` quasi-compact quasi-separated and `ℓ` invertible in `𝒪_K`, proper or not (this contains (a)): `X_C` is then quasi-compact, quasi-separated and of finite type over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`. When `X_C` is locally algebraically of finite type over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, in particular when `X` is smooth, finiteness is ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.1 (huber1996, Proposition 6.1.1, applied to the constant sheaf `ℤ/ℓ`, constructible, with `B = ℤ/ℓ`); for the remaining `X`, finiteness is `gap:proper-rigid-finiteness` (huber2007, Corollary 5.6, page 996, line 963, for every quasi-separated adic space of finite type over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)` and every characteristic of `C`). Vanishing above `2d` is the bound `cd_ℓ(X_C) ≤ 2 dim.tr(X_C/Spa(C, 𝒪_C)) = 2d` of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.2 (huber1996, Corollary 2.8.3, applied to quasi-compact quasi-separated rigid spaces of dimension `d` in hansenzavyalov2023, line 1708); for proper `X` it is also ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.4.

**Checks.**

- `X = ℙ^d_K` and `ℓ ≠ char K`: `H^{2m}(X_C, ℤ_ℓ) = ℤ_ℓ(−m)` for `0 ≤ m ≤ d` and the odd groups vanish, consistent with the bound `2d`. For `d = 0` only `H⁰ = ℤ_ℓ` survives.
- When `ℓ = char K`, case (c) applies with `j = 0`; cases (a), (b) and (d) do not. For `K = 𝔽_2((t))`, `Y = ℙ¹_K` and `ℓ = 2`, Artin–Schreier gives `H⁰(Y_C, 𝔽_2) = 𝔽_2` and `H^{q>0}(Y_C, 𝔽_2) = 0`; §4.2 and dévissage give `H⁰((Y^an)_C, ℤ/2^n) = ℤ/2^n` and zero in higher degrees. Thus the integral groups are `H⁰ = ℤ_2`, `H^{q>0} = 0`, and the rational groups are `H⁰ = ℚ_2`, `H^{q>0} = 0`. The prime-to-characteristic projective-space formula would instead give a nonzero `H²`.
- The quasi-unipotence of inertia on these groups (hansenzavyalov2023, Theorems 3.3.1 and 3.3.2, line 1684) is a statement about `ρ` that this roadmap does not make.
- Properness is used only when `ℓ` is the residue characteristic, in (b) and in (c) with `ℓ = p`. For the closed unit disc (smooth and quasi-compact, not proper), `char K = 0` and `ℓ = p`, finiteness fails in degree one (§4.3, last Check), so properness cannot be dropped from (b). For `ℓ` invertible in `𝒪_K` the same disc is covered by (d): `H⁰(X_C, ℤ/ℓ) = ℤ/ℓ` and the groups vanish in positive degrees (ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §10.4, for a closed disc of radius in `|C^×|` over `Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `C` algebraically closed, and `ℤ/n` with `n` prime to the residue characteristic; Layer 3, Examples, for `ℤ_ℓ`), finite and zero above `2d = 2`. A version of (d) restricted to proper `X` would miss it.
- Quasi-compactness cannot be dropped from (d): for `X = ⊔_{m ≥ 1} Spa(K, 𝒪_K)` (`d = 0`, quasi-separated, not quasi-compact) and `ℓ` invertible in `𝒪_K`, `H⁰(X_C, ℤ/ℓ) = ∏_m ℤ/ℓ` is infinite.

*Needs:* §§2.3, 4.2–4.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§2.6, 2.10–2.11; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§8.4, 9.7, 10.4 (for a Check), 11.1, 11.2; AInfCohomology §AI.3.1; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.3.0, F.3; `gap:proper-rigid-finiteness`.

## Worked examples

- **The point.** For `X = Spa(K, 𝒪_K)`: `X_C = Spa(C, 𝒪_C)`, `ρ` on `H⁰(X_C, ℱ_C) = ℱ_{ξ_C}` is the stalk action, higher groups vanish, and the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence is the identity on `H^p_cont(G_K, ℱ_{ξ_C})`, which is `H^p(Spa(K, 𝒪_K), ℱ)` by the first statement of §2.5.
- **The Tate twist.** For `K = ℚ_p`, `ℓ ≠ p` and `φ` an arithmetic Frobenius lift, `ρ(φ)` acts on `H⁰(X_C, ℤ_ℓ(1)) = ℤ_ℓ(1)` by `p`, and on `H²(ℙ¹_C, ℤ_ℓ) = ℤ_ℓ(−1)` by `p⁻¹`.
- **A permutation module.** For `X = Spa(L, 𝒪_L)` with `L/K` Galois with group `S₃`, `H⁰(X_C, ℚ_ℓ)` is the regular representation of `S₃` inflated to `G_K`; its character takes the value `6` at the identity and `0` elsewhere. For the non-Galois cubic subfield the character of `H⁰` is `3` at the identity, `1` at a transposition and `0` at a three-cycle.
