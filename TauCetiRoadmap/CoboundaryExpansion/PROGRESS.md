## CoboundaryExpansion: 2026-11-05 to 2026-11-09 (`419b078` to `7ca783d`)

The coefficient layer of the roadmap is now complete: every group norm the later layers need is in place, though no cochain has been defined yet. The centrepiece is the [moved-point norm](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/MovedNorm.html#Equiv.Perm.movedNorm) on a finite permutation group, which counts the points a permutation moves. It is [conjugation invariant](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/MovedNorm.html#Equiv.Perm.isConjInvariant_movedNorm), and the [Hamming distance between two permutations is the moved-point norm of their quotient](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/MovedNorm.html#Equiv.Perm.hammingDist_eq_movedNorm). Alongside it comes the [parity retraction](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/ParityRetract.html#Equiv.Perm.parityRetract), the homomorphism sending even permutations to the identity and odd ones to a fixed transposition, which [does not increase the moved-point norm](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/ParityRetract.html#Equiv.Perm.movedNorm_parityRetract_le). This is the lemma the headline will use to show that `Sym(3)` gauges do no better than `{e, t}` gauges on Kozlov's binary witnesses.

Underneath sits a general theory of [conjugation-invariant seminorms](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Group/ConjInvariant.html#GroupSeminorm.IsConjInvariant). Conjugation invariance is exactly [right invariance of the associated metric](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/Analysis/Normed/Group/ConjInvariant.html#GroupNorm.isConjInvariant_iff_dist_mul_right). Conversely, every bi-invariant metric comes from such a norm, and the two constructions are mutually inverse. The property survives cyclic rotation of a product, pullback along injective homomorphisms (which gives restriction to subgroups), positive scaling, sums and maxima (TauCeti#27680, TauCeti#29436).

Looking ahead to the cross-degree convention, Chapman and Lubotzky's [distance with errors](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/GroupTheory/Perm/DistErr.html#Equiv.Perm.distErr) between permutations of different degrees is now defined. It is symmetric, it reduces to the normalized Hamming distance in equal degree, and it is bounded below by the share of points outside the smaller degree. The cross-degree cost and constant built from it are still to come.
