# Roadmap: the Schneider–Lang criterion and Baker's theorem

Mathlib has Liouville numbers, the house of an algebraic number with Siegel's lemma over number
fields, and the analytic part of Hermite's method for Lindemann–Weierstrass. It does not prove
that `e` or `π` is transcendental, it has no Gelfond–Schneider theorem (open Mathlib PR
[#42911](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/42911) proves it by the
one-variable method), and it has no analysis of
holomorphic functions of several variables beyond what holds on an arbitrary normed space. This
roadmap builds the transcendence theory of the exponential function through the criterion of
Schneider–Lang for `ℂ^{d₀} × (ℂˣ)^{d₁}`, and derives from it Hermite–Lindemann, Gelfond–Schneider
and **Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms**: if `α₁, …, αₙ` are nonzero algebraic numbers
whose logarithms are linearly independent over `ℚ`, then `1, log α₁, …, log αₙ` are linearly
independent over the field of algebraic numbers.

The route is Waldschmidt, *Diophantine Approximation on Linear Algebraic Groups*, Chapter 4:
Corollary 4.2 (Schneider–Lang for `ℂ^{d₀} × (ℂˣ)^{d₁}`), its special cases Corollaries 4.3 and 4.4,
Theorem 4.5, and Theorem 1.6 (Baker's theorem), with the direct proof of Corollary 4.2 in §4.6. The
analytic engine is a Schwarz lemma for Cartesian products (Proposition 4.7) and a Thue–Siegel
construction of an auxiliary function (Proposition 4.10), and it needs a working theory of power
series in several complex variables that this roadmap also builds.

Suggested homes:
- `TauCeti/Analysis/Analytic/MultiIndex/`: multi-index power series and Taylor coefficients
  (Layer 0);
- `TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/Polydisc/`: estimates on polydiscs (Layer 1);
- `TauCeti/Analysis/Complex/ExpPolynomial/`: exponential polynomials (Layer 2);
- `TauCeti/NumberTheory/NumberField/House/`: Liouville's inequality and sizes of algebraic numbers
  (Layer 3);
- `TauCeti/NumberTheory/Transcendental/`: the Thue–Siegel lemmas, the auxiliary function, the
  criterion, Baker's theorem and the corollaries (Layers 3–6).

## Scope

In scope: everything needed to prove Corollary 4.2 and Theorem 1.6 of Waldschmidt, with the full
API of each object introduced along the way, and the classical corollaries listed in Layers 5
and 6.

Not in this roadmap:
- the Schneider–Lang criterion for meromorphic functions of finite order satisfying algebraic
  differential equations (Waldschmidt, Theorem 4.1), of which Corollary 4.2 is a special case;
- effective lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms (Baker–Wüstholz, Matveev) and the
  Diophantine applications that need them, in particular Siegel's theorem on integral points;
- Hermite's method and the Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem, whose analytic part is Mathlib's
  `Mathlib/NumberTheory/Transcendental/Lindemann/AnalyticalPart.lean`;
- algebraic independence (Schanuel's conjecture, Nesterenko), the six exponentials theorem, and
  `p`-adic and elliptic analogues.

## Conventions

These are fixed for every target below.

- **Several complex variables.** The ambient space is `ι → ℂ` for `[Fintype ι]`, with Mathlib's sup
  norm. A polydisc about `0` is `Metric.closedBall (0 : ι → ℂ) R` or `Metric.ball 0 R`; there is no
  polydisc definition. Layer 0 is stated over an arbitrary nontrivially normed field `𝕜`, on
  `ι → 𝕜`; the estimates of Layers 1 and 2 are over `ℂ`. Values lie in a normed space `F`, complete
  where sums are formed.
- **Multi-indices** are `α : ι → ℕ`; the monomial is `∏ i, z i ^ α i`; the total degree is
  `∑ i, α i`; the multi-indices of total degree `k` are `Finset.univ.piAntidiag k`. "Order `< m` in
  each coordinate" is `∀ i, κ i < m`; "total degree `< M`" is `∑ i, κ i < M`. Waldschmidt writes
  `|σ|` for `maxᵢ σᵢ` and `‖σ‖` for `∑ᵢ σᵢ`; the roadmap never uses his notation.
- **Taylor coefficients.** `taylorCoeff f x α : F` is the coefficient of `(z - x) ^ α` in the
  expansion of `f` about `x`, defined from any `FormalMultilinearSeries` of `f` at `x` (Layer 0.4),
  and `0` where `f` is not analytic at `x`. Waldschmidt's `D^σ f (ξ)` is `σ! • taylorCoeff f ξ σ`
  with `σ! = ∏ i, (σ i)!`; every statement is made in terms of `taylorCoeff`, not of iterated
  derivatives, and Layer 0.4 proves the two agree.
- **Analyticity and bounds on a polydisc** are hypotheses `∀ y, ‖y‖ ≤ R → AnalyticAt ℂ f y` and
  `∀ y, ‖y‖ ≤ R → ‖f y‖ ≤ M` (or `‖y‖ < R` for open polydiscs), never a new predicate. Power series
  on a polydisc are Mathlib's `HasFPowerSeriesOnBall f p 0 R`.
- **Algebraic and transcendental numbers** are `IsAlgebraic ℚ z` and `Transcendental ℚ z` for
  `z : ℂ`. Number fields generated by finitely many algebraic numbers are
  `IntermediateField.adjoin ℚ S` with its `FiniteDimensional` instance; their embeddings are
  `K →+* ℂ`; the size of an algebraic integer is `NumberField.house`.
- **Logarithms of algebraic numbers carry no branch.** "`x` is a logarithm of an algebraic number"
  is `IsAlgebraic ℚ (Complex.exp x)`; nonvanishing of `exp x` is automatic. No definition of the
  `ℚ`-vector space of such logarithms is introduced. Complex powers `a ^ b` appear only in the
  corollaries phrased with Mathlib's principal branch `Complex.cpow`, and each such corollary is
  derived from its branch-free form.
- **Exponential polynomials** are written out,
  `fun z => (∏ v, z v ^ τ v) * Complex.exp (∑ v, w v * z v)`, with no named definition; the
  pairing `w · z` is `∑ v, w v * z v`.
- **Sizes of algebraic numbers** are the pair of hypotheses `IsIntegral ℤ ((δ : K) ^ a * α)` and
  `house ((δ : K) ^ a * α) ≤ H` for a denominator `δ : ℕ`, an exponent `a` and a bound `H`, carried
  explicitly; there is no predicate bundling them.

## What is missing (build here)

A theory of power series in several complex variables at the level of multi-indices: the
correspondence between analytic functions on `ι → 𝕜` and normally convergent multi-index series,
Taylor coefficients at a point, Cauchy's inequality on a polydisc, and Schwarz lemmas for zeros on
Cartesian products. Linear independence of exponential polynomials. Liouville's inequality for
algebraic integers and the size calculus for expressions in algebraic numbers. The Thue–Siegel
lemma for small linear forms. The auxiliary function, the criterion, and the transcendence theorems
it yields. None of this is in Mathlib or Tau Ceti.

## The build, in layers

Every statement below is expressible against Mathlib alone once `coeff` and `taylorCoeff` are
defined; `Suggested.lean` gives both definitions and suggested forms for milestones of every
layer.

### Layer 0: multi-index power series

Over a nontrivially normed field `𝕜`, values in a normed space `F`, complete where sums are formed.

- **0.1 Products of absolutely convergent series.** For finitely many absolutely summable families
  `f i : ℕ → R` in a complete normed commutative ring, `σ ↦ ∏ i, f i (σ i)` over `σ : ι → ℕ` is
  absolutely summable with sum `∏ i, ∑' k, f i k`.
- **0.2 Sums of power series.** If each `g n` (over an arbitrary index type) has the power series
  `p n` on the ball of radius `r > 0` about `x`, and `(k, n) ↦ ‖p n k‖ r ^ k` is summable, then
  `∑' n, g n` has the power series `k ↦ ∑' n, p n k` on that ball, and so is analytic at `x`.
- **0.3 Multi-index coefficients of a formal series.** The multi-index coefficient
  `coeff p α` of `p : FormalMultilinearSeries 𝕜 (ι → 𝕜) F` is the sum of `p (∑ i, α i)` over the
  tuples of basis vectors whose coordinate counts are `α`. Milestones: the degree-`k` term is
  `p k (fun _ => z) = ∑ α ∈ univ.piAntidiag k, (∏ i, z i ^ α i) • coeff p α`; the coefficients are
  normally summable on every polydisc where `∑ (card ι) ^ k ‖p k‖ ρ ^ k` converges; **an analytic
  function is the sum of its multi-index series** there; conversely **a normally convergent
  multi-index series `z ↦ ∑' α, (∏ i, z i ^ α i) • c α` has a power series on the polydisc, with
  multi-index coefficients `c`**; and the identity theorem in coefficient form: a function vanishing
  near a point has all coefficients zero there, and a normally convergent series vanishing on a
  polydisc has all coefficients zero. The identity theorem comes from
  `HasFPowerSeriesAt.apply_eq_zero` and the fact that a polynomial map `ι → 𝕜` to `F` vanishing
  everywhere has zero coefficients (take coordinates in a basis of the span of the coefficients
  and apply `MvPolynomial.funext`; `𝕜` is infinite).
- **0.4 Taylor coefficients at a point.** `taylorCoeff f x α` as in the conventions,
  with: independence of the chosen series; `taylorCoeff_eq` for any `HasFPowerSeriesAt f p x`;
  agreement with iterated derivatives, `(∏ i, (α i)!) • taylorCoeff f x α =
  iteratedFDeriv 𝕜 (∑ i, α i) f x v` for `v` any tuple of basis vectors with counts `α` (from
  `HasFPowerSeriesOnBall.iteratedFDeriv_eq_sum`);
  additivity, subtraction and finite sums for functions analytic at `x`; locality (functions equal
  near `x` have the same coefficients); **the local expansion**: an analytic `f` satisfies
  `f (x + y) = ∑' α, (∏ i, y i ^ α i) • taylorCoeff f x α` on some polydisc, normally convergently;
  **coefficients from an expansion**: any normally convergent expansion of `f (x + ·)` on a polydisc
  has the Taylor coefficients as coefficients; and **linear change of variables**: if the Taylor
  coefficients of `F` at `A x` of total degree `k` all vanish, then so do those of `F ∘ A` at `x`,
  for `A` a continuous linear map.
- **0.5 Division by a coordinate.** If the coefficients of `f` at `x` vanish whenever `α i < m`,
  then `f (x + z) = z i ^ m • g z` with `g` analytic, on every polydisc about `0` on which the
  expansion of `f` about `x` converges normally. The divided difference
  `z ↦ dslope (fun w => f (Function.update z i w)) ζ (z i)`, which is
  `(f z - f (update z i ζ)) / (z i - ζ)` off the hyperplane `z i = ζ`, is analytic at every `x`
  at which `f` is analytic and `f` is analytic at `update x i ζ`; **on the hyperplane**
  (`x i = ζ`) analyticity of `f` at `x` suffices.

### Layer 1: estimates on polydiscs

Over `ℂ`, values in a complex normed space (Banach where sums are formed).

- **1.1 Maximum modulus along a coordinate.** If `f` is analytic on the closed polydisc of radius
  `R` and `‖f‖ ≤ C` wherever the `i`-th coordinate has modulus `R`, then `‖f‖ ≤ C` on the polydisc.
- **1.2 Dividing by one-coordinate polynomials.** Let `g` be analytic on the closed polydisc of
  radius `R` and `f = (∏ ζ ∈ S, (z i - ζ) ^ m) • g` there, with `‖f‖ ≤ M` and the points of the
  finite set `S` in the disc of radius `r`, `0 ≤ r < R`. Then `‖g‖ ≤ M / (R - r) ^ (m · card S)`
  on the polydisc of radius `R` and `‖f‖ ≤ M (2r / (R - r)) ^ (m · card S)` on the polydisc of
  radius `r`. For a single power, `f = z i ^ m • g`, the sharper bounds are `‖g‖ ≤ M / R ^ m` and
  `‖f‖ ≤ M (r / R) ^ m` on the polydisc of radius `r ≤ R`.
- **1.3 Schwarz's lemma for a zero of order `T`** (one variable). If `g` is holomorphic and bounded
  by `M` on the open disc of radius `ρ` about `0` and `(T : ℕ∞) ≤ analyticOrderAt g 0`, then
  `‖g w‖ ≤ M (‖w‖ / ρ) ^ T` there.
- **1.4 Cauchy's inequalities.** For `HasFPowerSeriesOnBall F p 0 R` on any complex normed space,
  with values in a complex Banach space and `‖F‖ ≤ M` on the ball: every homogeneous term
  satisfies `‖p n (fun _ => y)‖ ≤ M` for `‖y‖ < R`; and on `ι → ℂ`, **every multi-index
  coefficient satisfies `‖coeff p α‖ ρ ^ (∑ i, α i) ≤ M` for `0 ≤ ρ < R`**. The multi-index form
  is proved by averaging the degree-`k` term against a character over the points `(ρ ω ^ j i)ᵢ` of
  the torus, with `ω` a primitive `(k + 1)`-th root of unity: the average is exact on polynomials,
  so no integral in several variables is needed.
- **1.5 Truncated Taylor interpolation** (Waldschmidt, Lemma 4.13). For
  `HasFPowerSeriesOnBall F p 0 R` on any complex normed space, with values in any complex Banach
  space and `‖F‖ ≤ M` on the ball, `‖F z - p.partialSum T z‖ ≤ (1 + T) M (‖z‖ / R) ^ T` for
  `‖z‖ < R`; and on `ι → ℂ`,
  `‖F z‖ ≤ (1 + T) M (r / R) ^ T + ∑_{k < T} ∑_{α ∈ univ.piAntidiag k} ‖coeff p α‖ r ^ k` for
  `‖z‖ ≤ r < R`. The constant is `1 + T`, not Waldschmidt's `1 + √T`: his bound on the Taylor
  polynomial uses Parseval's formula and so needs a Hilbert space of values, whereas Cauchy's
  inequality (1.4) holds in any Banach space, and `1 + T` is all Layer 4 uses.
- **1.6 Schwarz's lemma for Cartesian products** (Waldschmidt, Proposition 4.7). Let `n ≥ 1`, let
  `f` be analytic on the closed polydisc of radius `R` in `ℂⁿ` with `‖f‖ ≤ M` there, and let
  `E₁, …, Eₙ` be sets of `S` points in the disc of radius `r < R`. If `taylorCoeff f ξ κ = 0` for
  every `ξ ∈ E₁ × ⋯ × Eₙ` and every `κ` of order `< m` in each coordinate, then on the polydisc of
  radius `r`, `‖f‖ ≤ n (4r / (R - r)) ^ (mS) K ^ n M` with
  `K = 1 + ∑_{k < mS} (2 (R + r) / (R - r)) ^ k`, and for `5r ≤ R`,
  `‖f‖ ≤ n (5 · 3ⁿ r / R) ^ (mS) M`. The proof divides one coordinate at a time in Newton form by
  the points of `Eᵢ`, each repeated `m` times, writing `f` as a polynomial main term with constant
  coefficients plus one multiple of `∏_{ζ ∈ Eᵢ} (zᵢ - ζ) ^ m` for each coordinate. The vanishing
  kills the main term by root counting in one variable (a polynomial of degree `< m · card E` with a
  root of multiplicity `≥ m` at each point of `E` is zero) together with the injectivity of a tensor
  product of injective maps. This replaces Waldschmidt's ideal-theoretic Lemma 4.8.

### Layer 2: exponential polynomials

- **2.1 Taylor coefficients** (Waldschmidt, Lemma 4.9, in coefficient form). For `τ : ι → ℕ` and
  `w ξ : ι → ℂ`, `z ↦ (∏ v, z v ^ τ v) * exp (w · z)` has, at `ξ`, the Taylor coefficient
  `exp (w · ξ) ∏ v, ∑_{j ≤ σ v} ((τ v).choose j) ξ v ^ (τ v - j) w v ^ (σ v - j) / (σ v - j)!`
  of order `σ`, and its expansion about `ξ` converges absolutely on every polydisc. The same holds
  for finite linear combinations, which have power series on every polydisc about every point.
- **2.2 Growth.**
  `‖(∏ v, z v ^ τ v) * exp (w · z)‖ ≤ (1 + ‖z‖) ^ (∑ v, τ v) · exp ((∑ v, ‖w v‖) ‖z‖)`.
- **2.3 Linear independence.** In one variable: if `∑_{c ∈ T} Q c (ζ) exp (c ζ) = 0` for all
  `ζ ∈ ℂ`, with `Q c : ℂ[X]` and distinct `c`, then every `Q c = 0`. In several variables: if
  `∑_{w ∈ T} MvPolynomial.eval z (P w) · exp (w · z) = 0` for all `z : ι → ℂ`, with distinct
  `w`, then every `P w = 0`; and both statements with the frequencies given by a map injective on an
  arbitrary finite index set. The several-variable case restricts to a line on which the
  frequencies stay distinct and the polynomials stay nonzero, found as a nonvanishing point of a
  product of nonzero polynomials.

### Layer 3: arithmetic

Over a number field `K`.

- **3.1 Liouville's inequality.** A nonzero algebraic integer `α` satisfies
  `1 ≤ ‖σ α‖ · house α ^ ([K : ℚ] - 1)` at every embedding `σ`, since its field norm is a nonzero
  integer, by `NumberField.norm_norm_le_norm_mul_house_pow`. (`one_le_house_of_isIntegral` is the
  weaker `1 ≤ house α`.)
- **3.2 Sizes.** If `δ ^ a * α` and `δ ^ b * β` are algebraic integers of house at most `H` and
  `H'`, then `δ ^ (a + b) * (α β)`, `δ ^ a * (α + β)` (for `a = b`), `δ ^ (a k) * α ^ k` and
  `δ ^ c * α` for `c ≥ a` are algebraic integers with the houses `H H'`, `H + H'`, `H ^ k` and
  `δ ^ (c - a) H`; integers `x` have exponent `0` and house `|x|`; finite sums and products follow.
  **Liouville's inequality for a number of known size:** if `δ ≠ 0` and `δ ^ a * α` is a nonzero
  algebraic integer of house at most `H`, then `1 ≤ δ ^ a ‖σ α‖ H ^ ([K : ℚ] - 1)` at every embedding.
- **3.3 The field of the data.** Finitely many algebraic numbers lie in
  `IntermediateField.adjoin ℚ S`, a number field, with a common denominator `δ` from
  `exists_integral_multiples` and a common bound for the houses of `δ` times each of them.
- **3.4 Thue–Siegel's lemma** (Waldschmidt, Lemmas 4.11 and 4.12). Let `X ≥ 1` be an integer.
  For real forms: if `∑ᵢ |u i j| ≤ U` for every `j` and `ℓ ^ (card κ) < (X + 1) ^ (card ι)` with
  `ℓ ≥ 1`, there is a nonzero `ξ : ι → ℤ` with `|ξ i| ≤ X` and `|∑ᵢ u i j ξ i| ≤ U X / ℓ` for all
  `j`. For complex forms, with `ι` nonempty: if `∑ᵢ ‖u i j‖ ≤ exp U` for every `j` and
  `(√2 · X · exp (U + V) + 1) ^ (2 · card κ) ≤ (X + 1) ^ (card ι)`, there is a nonzero `ξ` with
  `|ξ i| ≤ X` and `‖∑ᵢ u i j ξ i‖ ≤ exp (-V)`. Both over arbitrary finite index types.

### Layer 4: the auxiliary function

- **4.1 Proposition 4.10** (Waldschmidt). Let `φ_λ` (`λ` in a finite type of cardinality `L`) have
  power series on the polydisc of radius `R` in `ℂⁿ`, `n ≥ 1`, with `‖φ_λ‖ ≤ M_λ` there and
  `∑ M_λ ≤ exp U`. Let `N, r > 0`, put `W = N + U + V` and assume `12 n² ≤ W`,
  `e ≤ R / r ≤ exp (W / 6)` and `(2W) ^ (n + 1) ≤ L N (log (R / r)) ^ n`. Then there are integers
  `p_λ`, not all zero, with `|p_λ| ≤ exp N` and `‖∑ p_λ φ_λ‖ ≤ exp (-V)` on the polydisc of radius
  `r`. The integers come from 3.4 applied to the Taylor coefficients of degree `< T` at the origin,
  scaled to the radius `r` with 1.4, and the conclusion from 1.5.

### Layer 5: the criterion of Schneider–Lang for `ℂ^{d₀} × (ℂˣ)^{d₁}`

- **5.1 Corollary 4.2** (Waldschmidt). Let `d₀ ≤ n < d₀ + d₁`, let `x₁, …, x_{d₁} ∈ ℂⁿ` be
  linearly independent over `ℚ` with algebraic coordinates, and let `y₁, …, yₙ` be a basis of `ℂⁿ`
  over `ℂ`. Then one of the numbers `y j h` (`h < d₀`) and `exp (xᵢ · yⱼ)` is transcendental. The
  proof is §4.6: with `F (z) = ∑_λ p_λ z ^ τ_λ exp ((∑ᵢ t_{λ,i} xᵢ) · z)` from 4.1, the numbers
  `σ! • taylorCoeff F (s · y) σ` at the points `s · y = ∑ⱼ sⱼ yⱼ`, `s ∈ [0, S₁)ⁿ`, lie in the
  number field of the data with explicit size (2.1, 3.2), and are small by 4.1 and Cauchy's
  inequality (1.4), so by Liouville's inequality (3.2) they vanish for `σ` of order `< S₀` in each
  coordinate; `F` is nonzero by 2.3; the first total degree `M₀ ≥ S₀` at which some
  Taylor coefficient at some `s · y` is nonzero is bounded above through 1.6 applied to
  `z ↦ F (∑ⱼ zⱼ yⱼ)` and below by Liouville, and a choice of parameters makes the bounds
  incompatible. The first nonvanishing order is measured by **total degree**: the change of
  variables `z ↦ ∑ⱼ zⱼ yⱼ` preserves vanishing in each total degree (0.4) but not in each
  coordinate, and total degree `< M₀` gives order `M₀ / n` in each coordinate, which is what 1.6
  needs. (Waldschmidt's step 5 measures the order in each coordinate, which the change of
  variables does not preserve.)
- **5.2 Corollary 4.3.** The case `d₀ = 0`: if `x₁, …, x_d ∈ ℂⁿ` have algebraic coordinates and are
  linearly independent over `ℚ` with `n + 1 ≤ d`, and `y₁, …, y_ℓ` span `ℂⁿ` over `ℂ`, then some
  `exp (xᵢ · yⱼ)` is transcendental.
- **5.3 Corollary 4.4.** The case `d₀ = 1`, `d₁ = n = d`: if `x₁, …, x_d ∈ ℂᵈ` have algebraic
  coordinates and are linearly independent over `ℚ`, and `y₁, …, y_d` is a basis of `ℂᵈ` with
  algebraic first coordinates, then some `exp (xᵢ · yⱼ)` is transcendental.
- **5.4 Hermite–Lindemann.** If `x ≠ 0` and `exp x` is algebraic, then `x` is transcendental;
  equivalently `exp β` is transcendental for algebraic `β ≠ 0`. Hence `e` and `π` are
  transcendental.
- **5.5 Gelfond–Schneider.** If `x ≠ 0` and `exp x` is algebraic, and `b` is algebraic and not
  rational, then `exp (b x)` is transcendental. With Mathlib's principal branch, in the name and
  form of Mathlib PR #42911: `GelfondSchneider.transcendental_cpow_of_isAlgebraic_of_irrational`,
  `a ^ b` is transcendental for algebraic `a, b` with `a ≠ 0 ∧ a ≠ 1` and `∀ i j : ℤ, b ≠ i / j`. Hence a quotient `y / x`
  of logarithms of algebraic numbers, `x ≠ 0`, is rational or transcendental; in particular so is
  `Complex.log a / Complex.log b` for algebraic `a, b` with `Complex.log b ≠ 0`.

### Layer 6: Baker's theorem

- **6.1 Theorem 4.5** (Waldschmidt). If `β₁, …, β_d` is a `ℚ`-basis of a number field `K ⊆ ℂ` of
  degree `d`, and `l₁, …, l_d` are logarithms of algebraic numbers with `∑ βᵢ lᵢ` algebraic, then
  every `lᵢ = 0`. The proof applies 5.2 or 5.3 to the conjugates of the `βᵢ` and needs the
  regularity of the matrix of embeddings of a basis; the step from the conclusion of the criterion
  to `lᵢ = 0` uses that regularity again, a step left implicit in Waldschmidt's §4.2.
- **6.2 Baker's theorem** (Waldschmidt, Theorem 1.6). If `l₁, …, lₙ` are logarithms of algebraic
  numbers, linearly independent over `ℚ`, and `β₀ + ∑ βᵢ lᵢ = 0` with algebraic `β₀, βᵢ`, then
  `β₀ = 0` and every `βᵢ = 0`; and the same for a family indexed by a finite set (`LinearIndepOn`).
- **6.3 Corollaries** (Baker, *Transcendental Number Theory*, Theorems 2.1–2.4). For logarithms
  `lᵢ` of algebraic numbers and algebraic `βᵢ`: `β₀ + ∑ βᵢ lᵢ ≠ 0` for algebraic `β₀ ≠ 0`, with no
  independence hypothesis; a nonzero `∑ βᵢ lᵢ` is transcendental; `exp (β₀ + ∑ βᵢ lᵢ)` is
  transcendental for algebraic `β₀ ≠ 0`; and `exp (∑ βᵢ lᵢ)` is transcendental when the `lᵢ` are
  nonzero and `1, β₁, …, βₙ` are linearly independent over `ℚ`. In Mathlib's principal branch,
  `α₁ ^ β₁ ⋯ αₙ ^ βₙ` is transcendental for algebraic `αᵢ ≠ 0, 1` under the same hypothesis on
  the `βᵢ`.

## Worked examples (acceptance criteria, keeping the theory honest)

- `Real.exp 1` and `Real.pi` are transcendental (5.4).
- `Real.exp Real.pi` is transcendental: it is `(-1) ^ (-I)` in the principal branch (5.5).
- `(2 : ℂ) ^ (Real.sqrt 2 : ℂ)` is transcendental (5.5).
- `Real.log 2` is transcendental (5.4), and `Real.log 2 / Real.log 3` is transcendental (5.5, with
  its irrationality from `2 ^ q ≠ 3 ^ p`).
- `Real.pi + Real.log 2` is transcendental (6.3): it is `(-I) · (π I) + 1 · log 2`, a nonzero
  linear form in the logarithms `π I` of `-1` and `log 2` of `2`.
