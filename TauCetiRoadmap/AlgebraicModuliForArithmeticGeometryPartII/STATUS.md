## Where this roadmap stands

None of the main theorems has landed. These are the numerical Néron–Severi theorem, Schröer's criterion and descent theorems, and the Nakai–Moishezon and Kleiman criteria. The Picard-scheme layer and the work on families over discrete valuation rings have not begun.

### Named results

- **Fibrewise detection of relative numerical classes.** Restriction to all fibres X_s [embeds Num(X/S) into ∏ₛ Num(X_s/κ(s))](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericalClassGroup.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.numericalFiberRestriction_injective). Hence Num(X/S) is torsion-free and [injects into N¹(X/S)_ℚ](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericalSpace.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.toRelRatNumericalClasses_injective) and into N¹(X/S)_ℝ.
- **Nondegeneracy of the rational degree pairing.** A class in N¹(X/S)_ℚ with zero degree on every integral curve in every fibre [is zero](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericalSpace.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.eq_zero_of_forall_relNumericalIntersectionNumberBaseChange_eq_zero).
- **Cartier divisors embed into Weil divisors on normal schemes.** On a Noetherian normal integral scheme, a Cartier divisor with zero Weil divisor [is zero](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/CartierDivisor/Factorial.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.CartierDivisor.toWeilDivisor_eq_zero_iff). The proof uses the theorem that a [Noetherian normal domain is the intersection of its height-one localizations](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/RingTheory/SerreCondition/Normal.html#TauCeti.mem_range_algebraMap_of_forall_height_eq_one). The rational degree of a ℚ-Cartier Weil divisor on a curve is therefore [well defined](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/CartierDivisor.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.CartierDivisor.qCartierIntersectionNumber_eq_div).
- **Linear equivalence implies numerical equivalence.** Every rational or real combination of principal Cartier divisors [is numerically trivial](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/CartierDivisor.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.CartierDivisor.principalSubmodule_le_numericallyTrivialSubmodule).
- **The numerical group of the projective line.** The degree [identifies Num(ℙ¹_k/k) with ℤ](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericalClassGroup.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.projectiveLine.numericalClassDegreeEquiv). This is the first example of the roadmap's checks.

### Notable definitions and infrastructure

- **Numerical triviality, absolute and relative.** [`NumericallyTrivial`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericallyTrivial.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.NumericallyTrivial) and [`RelNumericallyTrivial`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/NumericallyTrivial.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.RelNumericallyTrivial) cut out the subgroups Pic^τ(X/k) and Pic^τ(X/S). Num is defined as the quotient by them. Every later numerical statement in the roadmap is phrased through these quotients.
- **Nefness over a field.** [`IsNef`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/IntersectionNumber/Nef.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.LineBundleClass.IsNef) is nonnegativity on integral closed curves. It is closed under products, powers and restriction to closed subschemes, and depends only on the numerical class. This is the starting point for nef cones and Kleiman's criterion.
- **Contractions.** [`IsContraction`](https://taucetiproject.org/docs/TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/Morphisms/Contraction.html#TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.IsContraction) means a proper map with O_Y ≅ f_*O_X, tested on sections over every open. Contractions are surjective epimorphisms and are stable under composition and restriction. This is the object that Layers 3 and 4 descend.
