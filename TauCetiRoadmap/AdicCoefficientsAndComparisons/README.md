# Roadmap: adic coefficients and the comparison of schemes with v-sheaves

**Summary.** This roadmap builds regular adic coefficient categories on small v-stacks and compares scheme étale cohomology with the cohomology of their associated v-sheaves. It lifts the diamond six operations to adic coefficients, constructs both scheme avatars and their comparison functors, refines the shared scheme compactification package, and proves ECD Propositions 27.1–27.7 with their prime-to-characteristic coefficient hypotheses.

This roadmap builds the last two sections of Scholze's *Étale cohomology of diamonds* [ECD]: étale
cohomology of small v-stacks with adic coefficients such as `ℤ_ℓ`, and the comparison between the
étale cohomology of a scheme and that of the v-sheaf attached to it. It also builds the
scheme-theoretic tools that this comparison needs and that no other roadmap provides:
strict and finitely presented compactification refinements over quasi-compact quasi-separated
bases, asymmetric constructible Hom continuity, and extension of the direct image with proper
support to finite-type maps and left-completed unbounded and regular adic coefficients.

The headline results are the following.

- **Adic coefficients.** For a coefficient ring `Λ` complete for an ideal `I` generated by a
  regular sequence and containing an integer prime to `p`, the category `D_ét(Y, Λ)` of derived
  `I`-complete objects with étale reduction is the limit of the categories `D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ)`, carries
  a completed tensor product and the six operations, and satisfies the adic forms of ECD Theorems 1.8–1.10 and 1.13 (ECD §26).
- **Schemes as v-sheaves.** Every scheme `X` of characteristic `p` defines a small v-sheaf `X◇`, and
  every scheme locally of finite type over a complete discrete valuation ring `O` with perfect
  residue field defines a small v-sheaf over `Spd O`; the comparison functor `c_X*` goes from the
  étale category of `X` to that of `X◇` (ECD §27).
- **Comparison theorems.** In characteristic `p`, `c_X*` is fully faithful for every scheme and commutes with tensor
  products and pullback, its right adjoint `Rc_X*` satisfies the internal-Hom and direct-image
  identities obtained by adjunction, and `c_X*` commutes with proper support for separated
  finite-type maps of qcqs schemes (ECD 27.1–27.4). Over
  `O` it commutes with proper support (27.5), with direct images of constructible complexes (27.6),
  and it is fully faithful on constructible complexes (27.7).

These are used by the roadmaps that need to pass between schemes and v-sheaves: étale duality and
perverse sheaves (which imports 27.1–27.7), geometric Satake over the Fargues–Fontaine curve and
lisse coefficient categories on v-stacks (which use the adic coefficients and the
characteristic-`p` comparison), Hecke stacks and local shtukas, and the Fargues–Fontaine diamond.
The scheme-theoretic refinements also apply independently to classical analytic comparisons,
weights and purity, `p`-adic differential equations and K-theory of schemes.

The build has three strands, which meet in the comparison theorems.

```text
adic coefficients (L0):
  the adic étale category -> reconstruction from Λ/Iⁿ -> completed tensor and colimits
  -> reduction detects equivalences -> six operations -> adic main theorems
  -> rational coefficients from integral lattices

schemes as v-sheaves (L1):
  X◇ in characteristic p (plus ring = integral closure of 𝔽_p)
  -> the scheme adic category on X_proét -> X◇ over a complete DVR O
  -> the comparison functor c_X* -> the two residue-field conventions

schematic refinements over qcqs bases (L2):
  shared limits, descent and compactifications (§3)
  -> strict maps and Comp_fp -> limit descent and strict refinements
  -> Cartier and vector-bundle refinements -> asymmetric Hom continuity
  -> finite-type and left-completed unbounded discrete proper support
  -> regular-adic proper support, using the scheme adic category of L1

comparison theorems:
  L3  characteristic p: tensor and pullback, full faithfulness, internal Hom, direct images,
      proper support (27.1–27.4)
  L4  over O: the analytic test space, proper support (27.5)
  L5  smooth alteration pairs over finite field extensions, descent, normal-crossing boundaries
  L6  the punctured trait, semistable models, constructible direct images (27.6),
      constructible full faithfulness (27.7)
```

The first layers construct the coefficient and comparison objects; the last layers prove
the comparison theorems. The roadmap owns the adic coefficient categories, the scheme-to-v-sheaf functors, the
comparison functor, the strict compactification refinements and coefficient extensions over general bases, and the proofs of
27.1–27.7, including the steps that the source leaves to the reader (§7). The machinery it uses
— enhanced derived categories of sheaves and derived completion, small v-stacks and diamonds,
the étale cohomology of diamonds with its six operations, Huber's comparison theorems for
analytic adic spaces, purity and cycle classes for schemes, and de Jong's alterations — is a named
target of another roadmap, cited by layer.

Representative suggested forms supported by the pinned libraries appear in
[`Suggested.lean`](Suggested.lean); this README specifies every target, including constructions
whose carriers require the cited geometric and enhanced-category foundations.

## 2. Conventions

### 2.1 The prime `p` and the coefficients

`p` is a fixed prime, and the test objects are perfectoid spaces of characteristic `p`. A
*coefficient pair* `(Λ, I)` consists of a commutative ring `Λ` and an ideal `I ⊂ Λ` such that

1. `Λ` is `I`-adically complete and separated;
2. `I` is generated by a regular sequence (finite, by definition);
3. `I` contains some positive natural `n` coprime to `p`.

No noetherian hypothesis on `Λ` is made. Every `Λ/Iᵐ` is then killed by an integer prime to `p`
(a power of `n`), so the finite levels are in the range of the discrete theory. The ideal `I = 0`
(the empty sequence) is allowed and then says that `Λ` itself is killed by an integer prime to
`p`: this is the discrete case. The principal example is `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, `ℓ ≠ p`. The coefficient pair `(ℤ_p, (p))` is excluded.
The regular-sequence convention is Mathlib's: the quotient by the sequence is nonzero, so
`I` is proper and `Λ` is nontrivial. The zero coefficient ring has only zero complexes and is
handled by that separate degenerate identification.

In Layers L5–L6 (ECD 27.6 and 27.7) the coefficients are finite rings killed by an integer prime
to `p`; this is also the hypothesis of ECD Proposition 27.7, whose statement mentions only
finiteness (§7). Layer L4 (ECD 27.5) allows any discrete coefficient ring killed by an integer
prime to `p` and coefficient pairs as above.

### 2.2 Completeness, the unit and completed operations

Derived `I`-completeness of an object `A` means that the map `A → Comp_I A = Rlimₙ (A ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ)` is
an equivalence; it is not classical `I`-adic completeness of the complex or of its cohomology
sheaves (although `A` is derived complete exactly when each cohomology sheaf is). The tensor unit of
the adic categories is the completed constant object `Λ̂ = Comp_I Λ = Rlimₙ Λ/Iⁿ`; identifying it
with a discrete constant sheaf `Λ` would need an argument and is not used. Tensor products and
colimits of adic objects are completed after they are formed. For `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, the
telescope `Λ̂ →ℓ Λ̂ →ℓ ⋯` has ambient colimit `Λ̂[1/ℓ]`, the continuous coefficient sheaf;
its `I`-completion is zero. This continuous sheaf need not equal the discrete constant `ℚ_ℓ`.
Rational coefficients are obtained by the scalar localisation of
`L0/rational-constructible-coefficients`.

### 2.3 Enhancements

The categories of sheaves are presentable stable ∞-categories, as built by
`EnhancedDerivedSheaves`. Statements about limits of categories, descent, adjoints obtained from
presentability, and the coherence of operations are made at that level; the triangulated
homotopy category is what Definition 26.1 names. In particular the reconstruction
`D_ét(Y, Λ) ≃ limₙ D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ)` is a limit of enhanced categories, and it is false for the inverse
limit of homotopy categories.

### 2.4 The two scheme avatars

- *Characteristic `p`.* For any scheme `X` of characteristic `p`, the affine open `Spec R` has the
  adic avatar `Spa(R, R⁺)` with `R` discrete and `R⁺` the integral closure of `𝔽_p` in `R`; no
  finiteness condition on `X` is imposed.
- *Over `O`.* `O` is a complete discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field `k` of
  characteristic `p`, fraction field `K` and a fixed uniformiser `π`; it may have mixed or equal
  characteristic. `X` is locally of finite type over `O`, and `X◇(S)` consists of an untilt `S♯`
  over `Spa(O, O)` and a map `S♯ → X` of locally ringed spaces over `Spec O`. The result is a small
  v-sheaf over `Spd O` whose structure map is representable in locally spatial diamonds; it is not
  a diamond in general.
- The residue avatar of the second construction is `Spd(k, k)`, the first gives `Spd(k, k⁺)` for
  `Spec k`, and the canonical map goes `Spd(k, k) → Spd(k, k⁺)`. They agree when `k` is algebraic
  over `𝔽_p`.

### 2.5 The comparison morphism

`c_X` is the morphism of sites from the v-site of `X◇` to the étale site of `X` (discrete
coefficients) or to the pro-étale site of `X` (adic coefficients); `c_X*` goes from schemes to
v-sheaves and `Rc_X*` is its right adjoint. For unbounded discrete complexes the scheme side is the
left completion `D̂(X_ét, Λ)`; the ordinary `D(X_ét, Λ)` is used only where it is known to be left
complete. The adic scheme category lives in `D(X_proét, Λ)`.

### 2.6 Schemes and compactifications

A morphism is *finitely presented* if it is locally of finite presentation, quasi-compact and
quasi-separated. Finite type and finite presentation are kept apart: an ideal of a nonnoetherian
ring need not be finitely generated. A compactification of a separated finite-type `f : Y → X` over
a quasi-compact quasi-separated `X` is `(Ȳ, j, p)` with `j` a quasi-compact open immersion, `p`
proper and `p ∘ j = f`; density is not required. A morphism of compactifications `C' → C` is a map
of ambient schemes compatible with `j` and `p`; it is strict if `g⁻¹(j(Y)) = j'(Y)`; it points from
the finer to the coarser compactification. Cofiltered means nonempty, with common refinements of
pairs and equalisers of parallel pairs. Perfections in characteristic `p` are not of finite type;
statements for them are transported through the perfection, which changes neither the étale site
nor `X◇`.

### 2.7 Constructible complexes

`Dᵇ_c(X_ét, Λ)` consists of bounded complexes whose cohomology sheaves are locally constant with finitely generated Λ-module stalks
on the strata of a finite stratification by constructible locally closed subschemes. In L2 the
ring `Λ` is noetherian and no integer need be invertible on `X`; in L5–L6 `Λ` is finite and killed
by an integer prime to `p`.

### 2.8 Names

Lean names follow Mathlib: `Scheme`, `IsProper`, `IsOpenImmersion`, `LocallyOfFinitePresentation`,
`IsSchemeTheoreticallyDominant`, `IsCofiltered`, `ObjectProperty.FullSubcategory`. The adic
spectrum is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa`. Names introduced here live in a namespace
for this roadmap; the API names below (`AdicEtale.ofComplete`, `Compactification.Hom.strict_iff`, …)
are relative to it.

### 2.9 Light definitions and their basic API

`IsAdicCoefficientPair p I` bundles exactly §2.1's completeness, regular-sequence generation
and containment of some positive natural `n` coprime to `p`. Its projections are
`isAdicComplete`, `regular` and `primeToP`; prove independence of the generating regular sequence and stability under ring
isomorphisms carrying I to the chosen ideal. In the zero-ideal case characterize it by
nontriviality of Λ and `IsPrimeToPTorsion p Λ`. Source: [ECD] §26, pp. 161–162;
Mathlib `IsAdicComplete` and `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular`.

**Checks.**

- For ℓ≠p, (ℤ_ℓ,(ℓ)) satisfies all three conditions.
- For Λ=ℤ/ℓ and I=0, ℓ≠p, the empty regular sequence gives the discrete coefficient pair.
- For (ℤ_p,(p)) no integer prime to p lies in the ideal, so the pair is rejected.
- `not_isAdicCoefficientPair_int`: for distinct primes ℓ and p, `(ℤ, (ℓ))` is rejected.
  The sequence `[ℓ]` is regular and the ideal contains ℓ, but ℤ is not ℓ-adically complete.
- In a nonzero ring the unit ideal cannot be generated by a Mathlib regular sequence;
  the zero coefficient ring is instead covered by the separate zero-category convention.

`IsPrimeToPTorsion p Λ` means that some positive natural `n` coprime to `p` is zero in Λ.
Prove its transport under ring homomorphisms, passage to ideal quotients, and equivalence
with an integer annihilator coprime to p when p is prime. This is the discrete coefficient
domain, not an adic completeness condition. Source: [ECD] §14, Definition 14.13, and §26, p. 161.

**Checks.**

- ZMod ℓ satisfies it for distinct primes ℓ and p.
- ZMod p fails it: every annihilating integer is divisible by p.
- ℤ_ℓ fails it for every p, since its characteristic is zero.
- The zero ring satisfies it with annihilator 1, giving the zero coefficient category.

`IsCompleteDVRBase p O` bundles the maximal-ideal-adic topology on a domain DVR O,
maximal-ideal completeness, residue characteristic p and a perfect residue field. Its
projections are `isAdic`, `complete`, `charP_residue` and `perfect_residue`; prove compatibility
with a choice of uniformizer, uniqueness of the adic topology, and transport under a
topological ring isomorphism, with both the ring isomorphism and its inverse continuous.
The predicates used for the ring, topology and residue field
remain Mathlib's. Source: [ECD] §27, pp. 165–166.

**Checks.**

- O=ℤ_p with its usual topology has residue field 𝔽_p and satisfies the definition.
- O=k[[t]] with k perfect of characteristic p and its t-adic topology satisfies it in
  equal characteristic; no characteristic-zero hypothesis is built into the base.
- `not_isCompleteDVRBase_wrongChar`: ℤ_ℓ with ℓ ≠ p fails because its residue field has
  characteristic ℓ.
- `not_isCompleteDVRBase_imperfect`: `k[[t]]` with k imperfect of characteristic p fails
  the perfect-residue condition, even with its t-adic topology and completeness.
- A discrete topology on a nonfield DVR fails the topology condition: its zero ideal is
  open but contains no positive power of the nonzero maximal ideal.
- The identity from adically topologized ℤ_p to indiscretely topologized ℤ_p is a continuous
  ring isomorphism, but the target topology is not maximal-ideal-adic. Continuity of the
  forward map alone does not transport the base condition.

`charPPlus p R` is `(integralClosure (ZMod p) R).toSubring` for a ZMod p-algebra R.
The API consists of the native integral-closure membership criterion, functoriality under
algebra maps, and `charPPlus_eq_top` for a field algebraic over ZMod p. The set-level spectrum
inclusion is supplied by `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa_antitone`, rather than a second
spectrum lemma. Source: [ECD] §27, p. 163; Mathlib `integralClosure`.

**Checks.**

- For R=𝔽_p, the plus ring is all of R.
- For every algebraic field extension k/𝔽_p it is all of k, including infinite algebraic
  extensions; finiteness of k is not the criterion.
- An element transcendental over 𝔽_p is outside this plus ring, so the plus ring is proper
  in a field containing such an element.

`IsCharPScheme p` is the object property that p vanishes in Γ(X,𝒪_X), and `CharPScheme p`
is its full subcategory of Mathlib schemes. For a prime p this is the category of schemes
over 𝔽_p, including the empty scheme. `LFTScheme O` is Mathlib's
`CategoryTheory.MorphismProperty.Over @LocallyOfFiniteType ⊤ (Spec O)`. Prove the
object-membership characterizations, inherited full faithfulness, transport along isomorphisms,
and restriction to opens; base change of locally finite-type maps uses Mathlib's morphism-class
API. These source categories are light restrictions of existing categories. Source: [ECD]
§27, pp. 163 and 165–166; Stacks, Schemes over a base and Morphisms §29.15.

**Checks for characteristic-p schemes.**

- Spec 𝔽_p lies in `CharPScheme p`.
- Spec ℤ does not lie in it for a prime p.
- The empty scheme lies in it: its section ring is the zero ring, so the definition does
  not incorrectly impose exact positive characteristic on every section ring.

**Checks for locally finite-type schemes.**

- The identity of Spec O belongs to `LFTScheme O`.
- Spec O[T] belongs to it for every commutative O.
- Spec k[T₁,T₂,…] over a field k fails local finite type: at the origin its local ring
  has an infinite-dimensional cotangent space, excluding a finite set of local algebra generators.

`IsInvertibleTorsionOn X Λ` means there is a positive natural n with nΛ=0 and n a unit
in Γ(X,𝒪_X). Prove transport under scheme pullback, passage to coefficient quotients,
and equivalence with invertibility at all stalks for a fixed n. The positivity rules out
using the automatic identity 0Λ=0. Source: [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.4, p. 165;
EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8 for the supplied broader D⁺ torsion domain.

**Checks.**

- For a characteristic-p field k and primes ℓ≠p, ZMod ℓ satisfies the condition on Spec k.
- ZMod p does not satisfy it on Spec k: every positive annihilator is divisible by p and
  hence vanishes on the nonempty scheme.
- Characteristic-zero ℤ_ℓ is excluded on every base because it has no positive annihilator.
- On the empty scheme every finite-characteristic coefficient ring qualifies; all complexes
  on that scheme are zero, independently of the annihilator chosen.

The shared geometric contracts retain the following distinctions. Compactifications may have
extra proper components: for a point Y, the first inclusion into Y ⊔ Y is allowed, its fold to Y
is not strict, and the maps fixing the first component but differing on the second remain
distinct. For empty interior, every proper ambient is allowed. The affine line is not proper;
its projective-line compactification retains its affine chart under field extension. A proper
closed immersion Spec(A/J) → Spec A for J not finitely generated is not finitely presented.
Cofilteredness includes equalizing parallel arrows, beyond finding common refinements.
These are uses of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§G.1–G.3, not new compactification carriers.

For the limit contract, compatible sheaf systems are allowed to vary with the stage: for 𝔾_m
the colimit sheaf is 𝔾_m, while the individual inverse-image maps need not be isomorphisms.
The cohomology theorem includes H²(𝔾_m) and is not a global-sections assertion. Constructible
category continuity descends bounded complexes and their finite Postnikov diagrams; it makes
no finite-stage assertion for arbitrary unbounded complexes. Sources: Stacks Tags 01YX,
01ZM/01ZR, 01ZA/07RN, 0EUU/081F, 01ZJ, 0ATT/0ATU/0F41 and 09YQ; [Abe] §1.4,
pp. 4–5. Applications to Brauer groups in perfectoid towers are in [Ces] §4.10, pp. 9–11,
and §§5.3–5.4, pp. 12–13.

## 4. How to read the build

The layers use the exact contracts in §3. L0 restricts completion and reconstruction to
étale objects using DiamondEtaleCohomology and DiamondSixOperations. L1 constructs the scheme
avatars from DiamondsAndVStacks and PerfectoidSpaces, with scheme sites and enhancements.
L2 builds schematic geometry and discrete proper supports from the shared contracts; its
regular-adic extension also uses the scheme adic category of L1.
L3 combines L0–L2 with ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§8.2/11.1–11.2 and field-change invariance.
L4 uses the formal chart of AdicSpacesPartIII R2, relative analytification of AdicSpacesPartII R1,
and DiamondSixOperations S4–S5. L5 combines L4 with alterations, proper descent and
EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.9–0.10/F.6. L6 uses L4–L5 with the trait stalk and completion
contracts of ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.3/4.3–4.4.

L2's approximation, compactification, continuity and discrete proper-support targets use no
other layer of this roadmap and can be built in parallel with L0–L1. These schematic targets
are what the roadmaps listed in §1 import. Inside L2 strict morphisms and finite presentation
precede refinements, then asymmetric Hom continuity and discrete proper-support extensions.
Build `SchemeSupport.extendAdic`, `SchemeSupport.upperShriekAdic` and
`SchemeSupport.extendAdicAdj` after `L1/scheme-adic-category`. L3 and L4 can proceed in parallel
once L0–L2 are in place; L5 needs the proper-support comparison of L4, and L6 needs L4 and L5.

## 5. Layers

### Layer L0: Adic coefficients

*Milestone:* the adic étale category with its operations, and the adic forms of the main theorems.

This layer is ECD §26. Everything is built from the discrete theory at the finite levels
`Λ/Iⁿ`, each killed by an integer prime to `p`, and from derived completion on the replete v-topos.
The two points to keep in view are that completeness is a property of complexes, so tensor
products and colimits must be completed, and that the reconstruction from the finite levels is a
limit of enhanced categories.

#### The adic étale category (`L0/derived-I-complete-etale-category`)

Let `Y` be a small v-stack and `(Λ, I)` a coefficient pair as in §2.1. Define

```text
D_ét(Y, Λ) ⊂ D(Y_v, Λ)
```

as the full subcategory of those `A` that are derived `I`-complete and whose derived reduction
`A ⊗ᴸ_Λ Λ/I` lies in the discrete étale category `D_ét(Y, Λ/I)`. Morphisms are those of
`D(Y_v, Λ)`, at the enhanced level. Derived completeness is a property of the complex `A`, read
in the derived category of the replete v-topos of `Y`; it is not classical `I`-adic completeness of
the individual cohomology sheaves. No noetherian hypothesis on `Λ` is made. When `I = 0`, which the conventions
allow only for `Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p`, the category is the discrete one, through
the same inclusion.

*API.*

- `AdicEtale.ofComplete` (constructor): an object `A` of `D(Y_v, Λ)` with a proof that it is derived `I`-complete and a proof that `A ⊗ᴸ Λ/I` is étale gives an object of `D_ét(Y, Λ)`.
- `AdicEtale.forget` (coercion): the inclusion `D_ét(Y, Λ) → D(Y_v, Λ)`; it is fully faithful.
- `AdicEtale.mem_iff` (characterisation): an object of `D(Y_v, Λ)` lies in `D_ét(Y, Λ)` exactly when it is derived `I`-complete and its reduction modulo `I` is étale.
- `AdicEtale.reduce` (functoriality): for every `n ≥ 1` the reduction `A ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ` of an object of `D_ét(Y, Λ)` lies in `D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ)`.
- `AdicEtale.ext` (extensionality): two morphisms are equal if and only if their images in `D(Y_v, Λ)` are equal.
- `AdicEtale.discrete_zeroIdeal` (compatibility): for `I = 0` and `Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p`, the adic category is the discrete étale category of ECD 14.13, through the same inclusion into `D(Y_v, Λ)`.

**Checks.**

- `AdicEtale.point` (computation): at a geometric point `Y = Spd C`, with `Λ = ℤ_ℓ` and `I = (ℓ)`, the completed unit `Λ̂ = Rlimₙ ℤ/ℓⁿ` lies in the category and its reduction modulo `ℓⁿ` is the constant sheaf `ℤ/ℓⁿ`.
- `AdicEtale.torsion` (characterisation): the constant complex `ℤ/ℓ` lies in the category: derived `ℓ`-completeness does not mean torsion-freeness.
- `AdicEtale.reject_inverted` (non-example): at a geometric point, the discrete constant `ℚ_ℓ`, a nonzero object of `D(Y_v, ℤ_ℓ)`, is not derived `ℓ`-complete; its completion is zero.
- `AdicEtale.empty` (degenerate): for `Y` empty every object of the category is zero.

*Source:* [ECD] Definition 26.1, pp. 161–162, with the standing hypotheses at the start of §26.

*Needs:* EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E1.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.1; `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C0`; `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C2`; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.ObjectProperty.FullSubcategory`.

*Target names:* `adicProperty`, `AdicEtale`, `AdicEtale.forget`, `AdicEtale.ofComplete`, `AdicEtale.mem_iff`, `AdicEtale.reduce`, `AdicEtale.ext`, `AdicEtale.discrete_zeroIdeal`.

#### Reconstruction from the finite levels (`L0/adic-coefficient-limit`)

Under the hypotheses of §2.1, `D_ét(Y, Λ)` is a presentable stable ∞-category, and reduction
modulo the powers of `I` gives an equivalence

```text
D_ét(Y, Λ)  ≃  limₙ D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ),
```

where the limit is taken in presentable stable ∞-categories along the derived base-change
functors `− ⊗ᴸ_{Λ/Iⁿ⁺¹} Λ/Iⁿ : D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ⁺¹) → D_ét(Y, Λ/Iⁿ)`, with their
coherence data. The homotopy category of the left
side is the category of `L0/derived-I-complete-etale-category`. The limit is the limit of the enhancements; the inverse
limit of the homotopy categories is not equivalent to it. A tower whose `lim¹` does not vanish
shows that the reconstruction uses the derived limit and not the limit of cohomology: at a
geometric point with `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, put `N = ⊕_{k≥1} ℤ/ℓᵏ` and `Aₙ = N ⊗ᴸ ℤ/ℓⁿ`; the tower of
`H⁻¹(Aₙ) = N[ℓⁿ]` under multiplication by `ℓ` is not Mittag-Leffler, so `H⁰(Rlimₙ Aₙ)` differs
from `limₙ H⁰(Aₙ)`.

*Proof route.* Apply the reconstruction theorem for derived-complete sheaves on a replete topos, for an
ideal generated by a finite regular sequence and with no noetherian hypothesis, to the v-topos
of `Y`, which is replete. Restrict to the étale condition: if `A ⊗ᴸ Λ/I` is étale then so is
every `A ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ`, by the finite `I`-adic filtration of `Λ/Iⁿ`.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 26.2 and its proof, p. 162.

*Needs:* `L0/derived-I-complete-etale-category`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`CompatibleCoefficientSystems`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`coefficientSystemReconstruction`); `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C0`.

*Target names:* `adicCoefficientLimit`.

#### Completed tensor product and completed colimits (`L0/completed-tensor-and-colimits`)

On `D_ét(Y, Λ)` define the completed tensor product and the completed colimit by

```text
A ⊗̂ B = Comp_I (A ⊗ᴸ_Λ B),        colimᵃᵈⁱᶜ Aα = Comp_I (colim Aα),
```

with the colimit on the right taken in `D(Y_v, Λ)`. The unit is the completed constant object
`Λ̂ = Comp_I(Λ) = Rlimₙ Λ/Iⁿ`. Internal `RHom` is the right adjoint of `⊗̂`, and these data make
`D_ét(Y, Λ)` closed symmetric monoidal. The inclusion `D_ét(Y, Λ) ⊂ D(Y_v, Λ)` does not preserve
colimits in general: the ordinary coproduct of complete objects need not be complete, and its
completed value is a different object.

*Proof route.* Restrict the completed tensor product and completed colimits of derived-complete sheaves to
the étale condition, which is stable under them because it can be checked after reduction
modulo `I`. Internal `RHom` on `D_ét(Y, Λ)` is the right adjoint of `⊗̂` there; it exists by
presentability, since `⊗̂` preserves completed colimits in each variable. This constructs
the internal Hom intrinsically in the adic étale category. The inclusion into all of
`D(Y_v, Λ)` has no right adjoint in general: such an adjoint would force the inclusion to
preserve colimits, contrary to the ℓ-telescope Check.

*API.*

- `AdicEtale.tensor` (structure): the completed tensor product makes `D_ét(Y, Λ)` closed symmetric monoidal, with unit the completed constant object.
- `AdicEtale.colimit` (universal-property): the completion of the colimit formed in `D(Y_v, Λ)` is the colimit in `D_ét(Y, Λ)`: maps from it to a complete object are compatible families of maps from the terms (at the enhanced level).
- `AdicEtale.tensor_reduce` (compatibility): `(A ⊗̂ B) ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ ≅ (A ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ) ⊗ᴸ_{Λ/Iⁿ} (B ⊗ᴸ Λ/Iⁿ)`.
- `AdicEtale.internalHom` (universal-property): `Map(A ⊗̂ B, C) ≃ Map(A, RHom(B, C))`, naturally in all three variables.
- `AdicEtale.unit_tensor` (simp): `Comp_I(Λ) ⊗̂ A ≅ A`.

**Checks.**

- `AdicEtale.tensor_point` (computation): for `Λ = ℤ_ℓ` at a point, `ℤ/ℓ ⊗ᴸ_{ℤ_ℓ} ℤ/ℓ` has nonzero cohomology in degree `−1`, so the operation is not the underived tensor product.
- `AdicEtale.telescope` (non-example): for `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, the telescope `Λ̂ →ℓ Λ̂ →ℓ ⋯` has ambient colimit `Λ̂[1/ℓ]`, the continuous coefficient sheaf, and `I`-completion zero.
  On a nonempty geometric point the ambient colimit is nonzero, so the inclusion into `D(Y_v, Λ)` cannot have a right adjoint.
- For `Y = Spd C`, sections of `Λ̂[1/ℓ]` over `ℤ_ℓ̲ × Y` are continuous maps `ℤ_ℓ → ℚ_ℓ`; the discrete constant `ℚ_ℓ` has only locally constant maps. The identity map belongs only to the continuous sheaf.
- `AdicEtale.empty_colimit` (degenerate): the completed colimit of the empty diagram is zero.
- `AdicEtale.unit` (computation): `Λ̂ ⊗̂ Λ̂ ≅ Λ̂` at a point.

*Source:* [ECD] §26, the paragraph after Proposition 26.2, p. 162.

*Needs:* `L0/adic-coefficient-limit`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.1 (`derivedComplete_colimit`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.4 (`completedTensor_hom_adjunction`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.4 (`derivedComplete_internalHom`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`completedCoefficientRing`); EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4.

*Target names:* `AdicEtale.tensor`, `AdicEtale.colimit`, `AdicEtale.tensor_reduce`, `AdicEtale.internalHom`, `AdicEtale.unit_tensor`.

#### The six operations on adic coefficients (`L0/six-operations-for-adic-coefficients`)

Let `f : Y' → Y` be a map of small v-stacks.

- `f*` is restriction along `f`; it preserves derived completeness and reduction, so it maps
  `D_ét(Y, Λ)` to `D_ét(Y', Λ)`. Its right adjoint is `Rf_*`.
- If `f` is compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds and of locally finite
  `dim.trg`, then `Rf_! : D_ét(Y', Λ) → D_ét(Y, Λ)` is the functor that corresponds, under the
  equivalence of `L0/adic-coefficient-limit`, to the compatible family of finite-level functors
  `Rf_!` on `D_ét(−, Λ/Iⁿ)`; `Rf^!` is its right adjoint. For an open immersion `Rf_!` is
  extension by zero, and for a proper eligible map it is `Rf_*`. No `Rf_!` is attached to a map
  outside this class.
- `⊗̂` and `RHom` are those of `L0/completed-tensor-and-colimits`.

All six operations commute with derived reduction modulo `Iⁿ`, the result being the
corresponding operation on `D_ét(−, Λ/Iⁿ)`, by canonical identifications that are compatible
with the transition maps; the base-change transformations and projection formulas of the finite
levels pass to the limit with their geometric hypotheses unchanged. These compatibilities are
statements about the coherent diagrams, not only about objects.

*Proof route.* Construct `Rf_!` on each `D_ét(−, Λ/Iⁿ)` from the support pushforward for eligible maps of
small v-stacks, compatibly with the transition maps, and take the limit; obtain right adjoints
from presentability. Identify the finite-level right adjoints with scalar extension using that
`Λ/Iⁿ` is a perfect `Λ`-module.

*API.*

- `AdicSix.pullback` (functoriality): `f*` for every map of small v-stacks, with its coherent identifications for identities and composites.
- `AdicSix.pushAdjunction` (universal-property): `f* ⊣ Rf_*`.
- `AdicSix.support` (constructor): for an eligible `f`, the coherent `Rf_!`; it is extension by zero for open immersions and `Rf_*` for proper maps.
- `AdicSix.shriekAdjunction` (universal-property): `Rf_! ⊣ Rf^!`.
- `AdicSix.reduce` (compatibility): each of the six operations, reduced modulo `Iⁿ`, is the corresponding operation of `D_ét(−, Λ/Iⁿ)`. Use the named pullback and support scalar-change maps and their right-adjoint mates, all required to be invertible; coherent Λ-linearity and the finite-level identifications are hypotheses of this comparison, with perfect coefficient change from §3.
- `AdicSix.baseChange` (compatibility): the base-change transformations and projection formulas of the finite levels pass to the adic operations, with the same geometric hypotheses.

**Checks.**

- `AdicSix.identity` (degenerate): for the identity of `Y` the four functors are the identity and the units and counits are identities, up to the specified coherent identifications.
- `AdicSix.empty_open` (degenerate): extension by zero from the empty open is the zero functor.
- `AdicSix.open_boundary` (non-example): for an open `j : U → Y` and a geometric point outside `U`, the stalk of `j_!Λ̂` there is zero, while that of `Rj_*Λ̂` need not be.
- `AdicSix.proper` (computation): for the map from two geometric points to one, `Rf_!Λ̂ ≅ Rf_*Λ̂ ≅ Λ̂ ⊕ Λ̂`.

*Source:* [ECD] §26, the paragraph on the operations, and Remark 26.3, p. 162.

*Needs:* `L0/adic-coefficient-limit`; `L0/completed-tensor-and-colimits`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E4.2/E4.5 (`regularIdeal_quotients`, `perfectCoefficientChange`); `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C3`; `DiamondSixOperations:S0`; `DiamondSixOperations:S2`; `DiamondSixOperations:S3`.

*Target names:* `AdicSix.pullback`, `AdicSix.support`, `AdicSix.pushAdjunction`, `AdicSix.shriekAdjunction`, `AdicSix.reduce`, `AdicSix.baseChange`.

#### Adic forms of the main theorems (`L0/adic-forms-of-introduction-theorems`)

Let `(Λ, I)` be as in §2.1 and use `⊗̂` and the operations above. For a map `f : Y → X` of small
v-stacks:

1. *(Theorem 1.8.)* For A,B on X, `f*A ⊗̂ f*B ≅ f*(A ⊗̂ B)`.
   For A on X and B on Y, `Rf_* RHom(f*A, B) ≅ RHom(A, Rf_*B)`.
   If f is compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds and locally of finite
   dim.trg, then for A on Y and B on X,
   `Rf_!(A ⊗̂ f*B) ≅ Rf_!A ⊗̂ B` and
   `RHom(Rf_!A, B) ≅ Rf_* RHom(A, Rf^!B)`.
   For A,B both on X, the same eligibility condition gives
   `Rf^! RHom(A, B) ≅ RHom(f*A, Rf^!B)`.
2. *(Theorem 1.9.)* For a cartesian square with `g : X' → X`: `g* Rf_* A ≅ Rf'_* g'* A` for
   `f` quasi-compact and quasi-separated and `A` whose reduction modulo `I` is bounded below;
   `g* Rf_! A ≅ Rf'_! g'* A` when f is compactifiable, representable in locally spatial
   diamonds and locally of finite dim.trg; `Rg^! Rf_* A ≅ Rf'_* Rg'^! A` when g has
   those eligibility properties. Here g' is the pullback of g and f' the pullback of f.
3. *(Theorem 1.10.)* If moreover `Λ/I` is killed by a power of a prime `ℓ ≠ p` and f is separated, representable in
   locally spatial diamonds, locally of finite dim.trg and `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth, then `Rf^!A ≅ f*A ⊗̂ Rf^!Λ̂` with `Rf^!Λ̂` v-locally isomorphic to
   `Λ̂[d]` for some `d ∈ ℤ`, `f* RHom(A, B) ≅ RHom(f*A, f*B)`. For a cartesian square with horizontal map
   g:X'→X and vertical f as above, smooth pullback gives
   `f* Rg_* ≅ Rg'_* f'*` and `g'* Rf^! ≅ Rf'^! g*`. If g is eligible, also
   `f'* Rg^! ≅ Rg'^! f*`. These are the canonical exchange maps.
4. *(Theorem 1.13.)* Pullback is fully faithful on `D_ét(−, Λ)` in each of these three
   situations: X is over a discrete algebraically closed characteristic-p field k and the base
   changes to a discrete algebraically closed extension k′; X is over such a k and the base
   changes to Spa(C,C⁺), for a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean field C of
   characteristic p containing k and an open bounded valuation subring C⁺ containing k;
   or X is over Spa(C,C⁺) and the base changes to Spa(C′,C′⁺), for an extension of complete
   algebraically closed characteristic-p nonarchimedean fields with C⁺⊂C′⁺, where the
   induced map of adic spectra is surjective. These are ECD Theorem 19.5's three domains.

All maps are the canonical ones and the geometric hypotheses are those of the discrete
theorems. The adic analogues of ECD Theorems 1.11 and 1.12, which concern `𝔽_ℓ`-coefficients,
are not part of this target. Example: for `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)` and the proper map from two
geometric points to one, `Rf_!Λ̂ = Rf_*Λ̂ = Λ̂ ⊕ Λ̂`, computed level by level.

*Proof route.* Each assertion says that a canonical transformation between composites of the operations is
an equivalence (for Theorem 1.13, that a unit is). By EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`isIso_of_isIso_mod`, `perfectCoefficientChange`) it
suffices to check after reduction modulo `I`, where `Λ/I` is killed by an integer prime to `p`
and the discrete theorems apply. For 1.10, `Rf^!Λ̂ = Rlimₙ Rf^!(Λ/Iⁿ)` is invertible because each
`Rf^!(Λ/Iⁿ)` is v-locally `Λ/Iⁿ[d]` with `d` independent of `n`.

*Source:* [ECD] the last sentence of §26, p. 163, and Theorems 1.8–1.10 and 1.13, pp. 5–8. Theorem
1.8(iii) is used with `A` on the source and `B` on the target (§7).

#### Rational coefficients from integral lattices (`L0/rational-constructible-coefficients`)

For a prime `ℓ ≠ p` and a site on which an integral bounded constructible `ℓ`-adic category
`D_cons(X, ℤ_ℓ)` is available, define the rational lattice category `D_cons(X, ℤ_ℓ)[1/ℓ]` as its
scalar localisation: it has the same objects, morphisms `Hom(A, B) ⊗_{ℤ_ℓ} ℚ_ℓ`, and the
composition induced coherently from the enhancement. EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.5
(`VanishingCycles.rationalAdicRealization`) supplies the Noetherian scheme rational category
and its comparison with integral constructible models up to isogeny, for invertible ℓ. Extend
that comparison to topologically noetherian schemes in the setting of Bhatt–Scholze §6.8,
compatibly with the supplied functor on its domain. No equivalence with a category of all
rational sheaves on a general small v-stack is asserted: the
completed `ℓ`-telescope is zero (`L0/completed-tensor-and-colimits`), so rational coefficients
cannot be obtained by inverting `ℓ` with completed colimits, and an identification on general
v-stacks needs a separate lattice and constructibility theorem that this roadmap does not
claim.

In the light suggested forms, `RationalLattice.ellMul C ℓ` is precisely the family of
scalar-ℓ identity maps in an existing preadditive ℤ_ℓ-linear category C;
`RationalLattice C ℓ` is Mathlib's localization at this family and
`RationalLattice.localise` is its native functor Q. The Hom theorem identifies the image of f
with 1⊗f and the inverse scalar action with ℓ⁻¹⊗f, so it fixes the normalization of the
rational linear structure. The point, torsion, zero and isogeny Checks below apply to these
light specializations as well as to their enhanced geometric instances.

### Layer L1: Schemes as v-sheaves

*Milestone:* the functors `X ↦ X◇` and the comparison functor `c_X*`.

This layer is the opening of ECD §27. The characteristic-`p` construction starts from an
arbitrary scheme and its discrete affine pairs `(R, R⁺)` with `R⁺` the integral closure of `𝔽_p`.
The construction over `O` starts from a scheme locally of finite type over a complete discrete
valuation ring with perfect residue field and uses marked untilts over `Spa(O, O)`. The two give
residue avatars that are related by a canonical map and differ in general (they agree when
`k` is algebraic over `𝔽_p`). The scheme side of the adic comparison
lives on the pro-étale site, with the left-completed étale category as its reduction.

#### The v-sheaf of a scheme of characteristic `p` (`L1/char-p-scheme-diamond-and-comparison-functor`)

For a scheme `X` of characteristic `p`, define the small v-sheaf `X◇` on perfectoid spaces of
characteristic `p` by `X◇(S) = Hom(S, Xᵃᵈ)`, where the adic avatar `Xᵃᵈ` is glued from the
affine pieces `Spa(R, R⁺)`, `Spec R ⊂ X` open, with `R` discrete and `R⁺` the integral closure of
`𝔽_p` in `R`. For affinoid `S` and `X = Spec R` this is `Hom(R, 𝒪(S))`: the image of `R⁺` lies in
`𝒪⁺(S)` automatically, since `𝒪⁺(S)` is integrally closed in `𝒪(S)` and contains `𝔽_p`. The
plus ring is the integral closure of `𝔽_p` even when `R` is a field. No finiteness hypothesis on
`X` is made. The construction is functorial, open immersions go to open immersions, and it
factors through the perfection: `X_perf◇ ≅ X◇`. For `X` locally of finite type over `𝔽_p` and a
perfectoid field `K` of characteristic `p`, `X◇ ×_{Spd 𝔽_p} Spd K` is the diamond of the
analytification of `X_K`, naturally in `X`.

*Proof route.* In the affine case `S ↦ Hom(R, 𝒪(S))` is a v-sheaf because `𝒪` satisfies v-descent; glue
along scheme opens, which give open subfunctors. For smallness, the base change to `Spd K`
is the diamond of the analytification of `X_K` (for `X` not locally of finite type, use
infinite-dimensional balls), and `Spd K → *` is a v-surjection. Frobenius is an automorphism of
`𝒪(S)` for perfectoid `S` of characteristic `p`, hence the factorisation through perfection.

*API.*

- `SchemeDiamond.charP` (constructor): the functor `X ↦ X◇` from schemes of characteristic `p` to small v-sheaves.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_points` (characterisation): for a perfectoid `S`, maps `S → X◇` are the maps of adic spaces `S → Xᵃᵈ`, the plus rings being integral closures of `𝔽_p`; for `X = Spec R` and affinoid `S`, the ring maps `R → 𝒪(S)`.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_open` (compatibility): an open immersion of schemes gives an open immersion of v-sheaves.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_perfection` (equivalence): `X_perf◇ ≅ X◇`, naturally in `X`.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_functor` (functoriality): compatibility with identities and composites, and with gluing along opens.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_spa` (compatibility): the affine chart of `Spec R` is `Spa(R, R⁺)` for the discrete ring `R`, whose points are `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa R⁺`.
- `SchemeDiamond.charP_baseChangeField` (compatibility): for `X` locally of finite type over `𝔽_p` and a perfectoid field `K` of characteristic `p`, `X◇ ×_{Spd 𝔽_p} Spd K ≅ (X_K)ᵃⁿ◇`, naturally in `X`; this is the identification used in the proofs of ECD 27.2 and 27.4.

**Checks.**

- `SchemeDiamond.Fp` (computation): `(Spec 𝔽_p)◇ = Spd(𝔽_p, 𝔽_p)`.
- `SchemeDiamond.plusRing` (non-example): for a perfect field `k ⊃ 𝔽_p`, the chart of `Spec k` uses `k⁺`, the integral closure of `𝔽_p`, and not `k`; the two differ when `k` has an element transcendental over `𝔽_p`.
- `SchemeDiamond.empty` (degenerate): the empty scheme gives the empty v-sheaf.
- `SchemeDiamond.frobenius` (characterisation): the absolute Frobenius of `X` induces an isomorphism of `X◇`, also when `X` is not perfect.

*Source:* [ECD] §27, opening paragraph, p. 163.

#### The adic étale category of a scheme (`L1/scheme-adic-category`)

Construct `schemeEtaleToProEt`, the fully faithful embedding of the left-completed
étale category into the pro-étale derived category for every scheme X and ring Λ. Its essential
image is exactly the complexes with classical étale cohomology sheaves; its bounded-below
restriction is the usual fully faithful étale pullback. This is the unbounded embedding required
here beyond SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17's site and repleteness contract and the normalized
bounded comparison of EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.4.3. Prove compatibility with reduction,
shifts and Postnikov limits. Source: [BS] Propositions 5.2.6 and 5.3.2, pp. 37–38.
The zero complex, a nonzero classical constant at a geometric point, and a pro-étale sheaf at
that point with genuinely varying sections on profinite sets distinguish zero, inclusion and
failure of essential surjectivity onto all pro-étale sheaves.

For a scheme `X` and `(Λ, I)` as in §2.1, define `D_ét(X, Λ) ⊂ D(X_proét, Λ)` as the full
subcategory of derived `I`-complete objects `A` whose reduction `A ⊗ᴸ_Λ Λ/I` lies in the
essential image of the left-completed étale category `D̂(X_ét, Λ/I)`; equivalently, the
reduction has cohomology sheaves pulled back from `X_ét`. The ordinary `D(X_ét, Λ/I)` may replace
`D̂(X_ét, Λ/I)` only where `D(X_ét)` is known to be left complete (for instance under a finite
cohomological dimension hypothesis); in general the unbounded comparison needs the left
completion. Bounded-below étale complexes agree with their pro-étale pullbacks.

*Proof route.* Use derived completeness on the replete pro-étale topos and Bhatt–Scholze Proposition 5.3.2
(p. 38 of [BS]) for the unbounded left-completed essential image (Proposition 5.2.6, p. 37, covers only bounded-below
complexes); take the full subcategory.

*API.*

- `SchemeAdic.ofComplete` (constructor): a derived `I`-complete object of `D(X_proét, Λ)` whose reduction modulo `I` has classical étale cohomology sheaves gives an object.
- `SchemeAdic.forget` (coercion): the inclusion into `D(X_proét, Λ)`; it is fully faithful.
- `SchemeAdic.reduce` (functoriality): for `n ≥ 1` the reduction modulo `Iⁿ` lies in the image of `D̂(X_ét, Λ/Iⁿ)`.
- `SchemeAdic.boundedBelow` (compatibility): on bounded-below complexes, the pullback from `X_ét` to `X_proét` is fully faithful and agrees with the left-completed embedding (Bhatt–Scholze Proposition 5.2.6, p. 37).
- `SchemeAdic.leftComplete` (equivalence): if `D(X_ét, Λ/I)` is left complete, the condition may be stated with ordinary étale complexes.
- `SchemeAdic.ext` (extensionality): morphisms are equal if and only if their images in `D(X_proét, Λ)` are.
- `schemeAdicSymmetric` and `schemeAdicMonoidalClosed` (structure): for every scheme X and coefficient pair of §2.1, construct the symmetric closed monoidal structure on `SchemeAdic(X, Λ)`. Tensor and small colimits are the ambient pro-étale operations followed by derived I-completion; the unit is the completed constant object, and internal Hom is right adjoint to completed tensor. Prove that these constructions retain classical étale reductions and satisfy the tensor–Hom adjunction.
- `SchemeAdic.pullback` (functoriality): for every scheme map `f : Y → X` and the same coefficient pair, `f* : SchemeAdic(X, Λ) → SchemeAdic(Y, Λ)` is completed pro-étale pullback, preserving small completed colimits.
- `SchemeAdic.pushforward` and `SchemeAdic.pullbackAdj` (adjunction): construct `Rf_* : SchemeAdic(Y, Λ) → SchemeAdic(X, Λ)` as the right adjoint of this pullback, with its unit, counit and coherent composition. Presentability and the colimit assertion give the adjunction without properness or finite-type hypotheses on f.

**Checks.**

- `SchemeAdic.geometricPoint` (computation): for `X` the spectrum of an algebraically closed field and `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `ℓ ≠ p`, the completed constant lattice lies in the category and its reductions are the étale constants `ℤ/ℓⁿ`.
- `SchemeAdic.zeroIdeal` (compatibility): for `I = 0` and `Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p`, the category is the essential image of `D̂(X_ét, Λ)`, not all of `D(X_proét, Λ)`.
- `SchemeAdic.reject_inverted` (non-example): for `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, on a nonempty scheme the nonzero discrete constant `ℚ_ℓ` is not in the category.
- `SchemeAdic.empty` (degenerate): over the empty scheme every object is zero.
- For the identity of X the pullback–pushforward adjunction is the identity adjunction.
- On a geometric point with `Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, completed tensor with `Λ̂` fixes every object. The telescope `Λ̂ →ℓ Λ̂ →ℓ ⋯` has ambient colimit `Λ̂[1/ℓ]` and `I`-completion zero.

#### The v-sheaf of a scheme over a complete discrete valuation ring (`L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`)

Fix a complete discrete valuation ring `O` with perfect residue field `k` of characteristic `p`.
For `X` locally of finite type over `O`, let `X◇(S)` be the set of pairs of an untilt `S♯` of `S`
over `Spa(O, O)` and a map `S♯ → X` of locally ringed spaces over `Spec O`. Then `X◇` is a small
v-sheaf over `Spd O`, functorial in `X` and compatible with gluing along opens, and the structure
map `X◇ → Spd O` is representable in locally spatial diamonds: for a perfectoid `S → Spd O` with
untilt `S♯`, `X◇ ×_{Spd O} S ≅ (X ×_{Spec O} S♯)◇`, naturally in `X` and `S`. The v-sheaf `X◇`
itself is in general not a diamond: `(Spec O)◇ = Spd O`, and for `O = ℤ_p` this contains the
sub-v-sheaf `Spd(𝔽_p, 𝔽_p) = *` (the locus `p = 0`), which is not a diamond, while
sub-v-sheaves of diamonds are diamonds ([ECD] Proposition 11.10; [SW] Lecture 10, p. 74).
Indeed, a quasi-pro-étale presentation of `*` would yield a quasi-pro-étale geometric field
`Spa C → *`, but `Spa C × Spa C → Spa C` has points with residue field transcendental over C
and is therefore not pro-étale. For `Spec k` the construction gives `Spd(k, k)`, which
differs from the characteristic-`p` avatar `Spd(k, k⁺)` as soon as `k⁺ ≠ k`.

*Proof route.* Marked untilts over the pair `(O, O)` satisfy v-descent; for affine `X = Spec A` the second
datum is an `O`-algebra map `A → 𝒪(S♯)`, a v-sheaf condition. For an affinoid perfectoid
untilt `S♯`, PerfectoidSpaces P3.17a.2–P3.17a.4 supply complete stably sheafy charts.
AdicSpacesPartII R1.2 (`scheme-fibre-product-analytification`) then gives
`X ×_{Spec O} S♯`, its universal property and stably sheafy charts. Applying
DiamondsAndVStacks D6 (`gluing-and-the-diamond-functor`, `etale-site-comparison`) identifies
its locally spatial diamond with `X◇ ×_{Spd O} S`. These identifications prove the relative
representability assertion.

*API.*

- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR` (constructor): the functor from schemes locally of finite type over `O` to small v-sheaves over `Spd O` whose structure maps are representable in locally spatial diamonds.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_points` (characterisation): maps `S → X◇` are pairs of an untilt `S♯` over `Spa(O, O)` and a map `S♯ → X` of locally ringed spaces over `Spec O`.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_functor` (functoriality): compatibility with identities, composites and gluing along opens.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_closedFibre` (compatibility): the residue point `Spec k` goes to `Spd(k, k)`.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_generic` (compatibility): on the generic fibre the construction agrees with the diamond of the analytic generic fibre, where that is defined.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_empty` (simp): the empty scheme goes to the empty v-sheaf.
- `SchemeDiamond.overDVR_representable` (characterisation): for a perfectoid `S → Spd O` with untilt `S♯`, `X◇ ×_{Spd O} S ≅ (X ×_{Spec O} S♯)◇`, naturally in `X` and `S`.

**Checks.**

- `SchemeDiamond.residue` (computation): `Spec k` gives `Spd(k, k)`, with `k` itself as plus ring.
- `SchemeDiamond.base` (degenerate): `Spec O` gives `Spd(O, O)` with the identity as structure map.
- After an affinoid perfectoid untilt `Spa(R, R⁺)`, the affine line is covered by the sheafy relative balls with ring `R⟨T⟩` after rescaling T; no perfectoid base field is required for their sheafiness.
- The closed locus `T² = 0` has the same maps from perfectoid tests as `T = 0`; its diamond is defined by these equations, without asserting that the nonreduced quotient `R⟨T⟩/(T²)` is sousperfectoid.
- `SchemeDiamond.emptyDVR` (degenerate): the empty `O`-scheme gives the empty v-sheaf.
- `SchemeDiamond.nonagreement` (non-example): for a perfect field `k` with an element transcendental over `𝔽_p`, `k⁺ ≠ k`, so the two residue avatars may not be identified.
- `SchemeDiamond.notDiamond` (non-example): for `O = ℤ_p`, `(Spec O)◇ = Spd ℤ_p` is not a diamond ([SW] Lecture 10, p. 74): it contains the sub-v-sheaf `Spd(𝔽_p, 𝔽_p) = *` where `p = 0`, sub-v-sheaves of diamonds are diamonds (ECD Proposition 11.10), and `*` is not a diamond. A definition with values in diamonds would exclude `Spec O`.

*Source:* [ECD] §27, the mixed-characteristic paragraph, pp. 165–166 (read in the relative form, §7).

#### The comparison functor (`L1/comparison-pullback`)

For a scheme `X` of characteristic `p`, the morphism of sites `c_X : X◇_v → X_ét` gives, for
`Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p`, the derived pullback

```text
c_X* : D̂(X_ét, Λ) → D_ét(X◇, Λ),
```

and `c_X : X◇_v → X_proét` gives, for `(Λ, I)` as in §2.1, the completed pullback
`c_X* : D_ét(X, Λ) → D_ét(X◇, Λ)` from the scheme adic category of `L1/scheme-adic-category`.
The same construction applies to schemes locally of finite type over `O` with the v-sheaf of
`L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`. The morphism goes from the v-site to the scheme site,
so `c_X*` goes from schemes to v-sheaves. `c_X*` commutes with reduction modulo `Iⁿ`, sends the
unit to the unit, preserves completed colimits and so has a right adjoint `Rc_X*`, and at a
geometric point `Spd C → X◇` it is the geometric stalk of the scheme at the induced point
`Spec C → X` (for schemes over `O`, at the point `Spec C♯ → X` given by the untilt `C♯` attached
to the geometric point).

*Proof route.* Construct the morphisms of sites on étale and pro-étale objects, derive the pullback, and
complete it in the adic case. Check reduction and stalks, including the mixed-characteristic
convention for the residue field.

*API.*

- `SchemeComparison.pullback` (constructor): `c_X*` on the source category specified above (discrete or adic).
- `SchemeComparison.reduce` (compatibility): `c_X*` commutes with reduction modulo `Iⁿ`, by the canonical coherent identification.
- `SchemeComparison.stalk` (characterisation): at a geometric point `Spd C → X◇`, the stalk of `c_X* A` is the geometric stalk of `A` at the induced point `Spec C → X`.
- `SchemeComparison.unit` (simp): `c_X*` sends the unit to the unit.
- `SchemeComparison.colimit` (functoriality): `c_X*` preserves completed colimits, and so has the right adjoint `Rc_X*`.
- `SchemeComparison.pushforwardAdicDVR` and `SchemeComparison.adjAdicDVR` (adjunction): for every X locally of finite type over the complete adic DVR O of §2.4 and coefficient pair of §2.1, construct `Rc_X* : AdicEtale(X◇, Λ) → SchemeAdic(X, Λ)` as the right adjoint to the completed comparison pullback, with unit, counit and triangle identities. This is an adjunction on the full unbounded presentable adic categories, supplied by the colimit preservation above and EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.4/E3.2.

**Checks.**

- `SchemeComparison.point` (computation): at a geometric point, a finite constant coefficient complex pulls back to the same complex.
- `SchemeComparison.zero` (degenerate): `c_X* 0 = 0`.
- `SchemeComparison.openSupport` (characterisation): the pullback of `j_!A` for a scheme open `j` has zero stalks outside the corresponding open of `X◇`.
- `SchemeComparison.adic` (compatibility): the pullback of the `ℤ_ℓ`-lattice has reductions `ℤ/ℓⁿ`; the operation involves derived completion.

#### Comparison of the two residue-field conventions (`L1/plus-convention-comparison`)

Let `k` be a perfect field of characteristic `p` and `k⁺` the integral closure of `𝔽_p` in `k`.
The inclusion `k⁺ ⊂ k` gives a natural map `Spd(k, k) → Spd(k, k⁺)` from the residue avatar of
the mixed-characteristic construction to the characteristic-`p` avatar of `Spec k`; on adic
spectra it is the inclusion `Spa(k, k) ⊂ Spa(k, k⁺)` (enlarging the plus ring shrinks the
spectrum). If `k` is algebraic over `𝔽_p`, then `k⁺ = k` and the map is the identity. It is not
an isomorphism in general: when `k` contains an element transcendental over `𝔽_p` the plus rings
differ.

*Source:* [ECD] §27, the mixed-characteristic paragraph, pp. 165–166.

*Needs:* `L1/char-p-scheme-diamond-and-comparison-functor`; `L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa_antitone`.

*Target names:* `SchemeDiamond.isIso_plusConventionMap`, `SchemeDiamond.plusConvention_avatars`.

#### Examples

The two avatars of Spec k are Spd(k,k⁺) and Spd(k,k). They agree for algebraic k/𝔽_p; a transcendental element separates their plus rings. The empty scheme gives the initial v-sheaf and Spd ℤ_p is a small v-sheaf, with its relative diamond representability retained.

### Layer L2: Schematic geometry over quasi-compact quasi-separated bases

*Milestone:* strict finitely presented compactification refinements, asymmetric Hom continuity, and coefficient extensions of proper support over qcqs bases.

ECD 27.4 is stated for arbitrary quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes of characteristic `p`,
and its proof needs the direct image with proper support, compactifications and continuity
arguments in that generality. This layer uses the shared scheme contracts of §3 and builds the stated refinements. It is purely
schematic up to its last two targets, which extend the classical direct image with proper
support. Its schematic geometry and discrete proper-support targets are independent of L0–L1;
the regular-adic extension uses `L1/scheme-adic-category`. Finite type and finite presentation are kept
apart throughout, compactifications are not assumed dense, and cofilteredness includes the
equalisation of parallel maps.

The targets refine supplied compactification geometry by strictness and finite presentation,
establish asymmetric Hom continuity, and extend proper support to the stated coefficient domains.
The shared limits, Nagata and continuity contracts are fixed in §3.

#### Maps and strict maps of compactifications (`L2/compactification-morphism`)

Use EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.1's `Compactification.Hom` for a map
`g : C' → C`, with its two commuting triangles. Define only its strictness predicate here:
`g` is *strict* if `g⁻¹(j(Y)) = j'(Y)`, equivalently if the square formed by
`g`, the two open immersions and the identity of `Y` is cartesian. Morphisms need not be strict.
The direction is from the finer compactification to the coarser one.

*API.*

- `Compactification.Hom.strict_iff` (characterisation): `g` is strict if and only if the inverse image of the open of the target is the open of the source.
- `Compactification.Hom.strict_comp` (functoriality): identities and composites of strict morphisms are strict.
- `Compactification.Hom.strict_baseChange` (compatibility): base change preserves strictness ([Zav] Remark 2.1.3).

**Checks.**

- `Compactification.Hom.identity` (degenerate): every identity is strict.
- `Compactification.Hom.boundary` (non-example): the fold `Y ⊔ Y → Y` from the extra-component compactification is a morphism of compactifications but is not strict.
- `Compactification.Hom.empty` (degenerate): for empty `Y` every morphism is strict, both opens being empty.
- `Compactification.Hom.baseChange` (compatibility): for a strict morphism, the inverse image of the target open in its base change is exactly the base change of `Y`.

*Source:* [Zav] Definition 2.1.1 and Remark 2.1.3, p. 10.

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.1 (`Compactification`).

*Target names:* `Compactification.Hom.strict_iff`, `Compactification.Hom.strict_comp`, `Compactification.Hom.strict_baseChange`.

#### Finitely presented compactifications (`L2/fp-compactification`)

For `f : Y → X` separated and finitely presented, with `X` quasi-compact and quasi-separated, a
finitely presented compactification is a compactification whose proper map `p` is locally of
finite presentation; since `p` is proper it is then finitely presented. Finite type does not
replace finite presentation here: over a ring `A` with an ideal `J` that is not
finitely generated, the closed immersion `Spec(A/J) → Spec A` is proper but not of finite
presentation.

*API.*

- `FPCompactification.mk` (constructor): a compactification together with a proof that `p` is locally of finite presentation.
- `FPCompactification.forget` (coercion): the underlying compactification, with the same ambient scheme.
- `FPCompactification.baseChange` (functoriality): base change along any `X' → X` preserves finitely presented compactifications and their open factorisation.
- `FPCompactification.finitePresentation` (characterisation): `p` is quasi-compact, quasi-separated and locally of finite presentation.

**Checks.**

- `FPCompactification.proper` (degenerate): for `f` proper and finitely presented, the identity compactification is finitely presented.
- `FPCompactification.empty` (degenerate): the empty compactification of the empty scheme is finitely presented.
- `FPCompactification.basePoint` (computation): base change of `ℙ¹_k ⊃ 𝔸¹_k` along a field extension keeps the same open chart and a proper, locally finitely presented ambient map.
- `FPCompactification.rejectFiniteType` (non-example): over a ring `A` with a non-finitely-generated ideal `J`, the proper closed immersion `Spec(A/J) → Spec A`, viewed as a compactification of itself, is not finitely presented.

*Source:* [Zav] Definition 2.1.1, p. 10.

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.1 (`Compactification`); Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.LocallyOfFinitePresentation`; Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.AffineSpace`.

*Target names:* `fpProperty`, `FPCompactification`, `FPCompactification.mk`, `FPCompactification.forget`, `FPCompactification.finitePresentation`, `FPCompactification.baseChange`.

#### Strictness from a dense source (`L2/strict-from-dense`)

A morphism `g : C' → C` of compactifications whose source open `j'(Y)` is topologically dense in
`Ȳ'` is strict. Topological density suffices even over non-reduced schemes; schematic density is
a different, stronger condition, needed only for extending vector bundles.

*Proof route.* Over the open `g⁻¹(j(Y))`, `j'` is a section of the separated map `g⁻¹(j(Y)) → Y`, hence a
closed immersion; its image is open and dense, so it is all of `g⁻¹(j(Y))`.

*Source:* [BP] Lemma 2.1.4 and its proof, p. 7; [Stacks] Lemma 38.32.1 (Tag 0ATU), last assertion.

*Needs:* `L2/compactification-morphism`.

*Target names:* `strictFromDense`.

#### Finitely presented compactifications over a limit (`L2/fp-compactification-limit`)

Let `(Yᵢ)` be a directed system of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes with affine transition
maps, a least index `0` and limit `Y`, and let `X₀ → Y₀` be separated and finitely presented. The
base-change functor

```text
colimᵢ Comp_fp(X₀ ×_{Y₀} Yᵢ / Yᵢ)  →  Comp_fp(X₀ ×_{Y₀} Y / Y)
```

is an equivalence: objects and morphisms descend, and equalities of morphisms hold at a later
stage. It sends strict morphisms to strict morphisms, and a morphism that is strict at the limit
is strict after enlarging the index.

*Proof route.* Descend ambient schemes and maps (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.5 (`ClassicalCoefficients.finitePresentationDescent`)), then properness, the open
immersions and their triangles (SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.9 (`finite_presentation_limits`)). For strictness descend
the equality of the two quasi-compact opens `g⁻¹(j(Y))` and `j'(Y)`.

*Source:* [Zav] Lemma 2.1.4 and its proof, pp. 10–11.

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.5 (`ClassicalCoefficients.finitePresentationDescent`); SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.9 (`finite_presentation_limits`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§G.1–G.3 (compactification category); Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.exists_map_preimage_eq_map_preimage`.

*Target names:* `fpCompactificationLimit`.

#### Strict cofilteredness of finitely presented compactifications (`L2/fp-strict-cofiltered`)

Let `X` be quasi-compact and quasi-separated and `f : Y → X` separated and finitely presented.
Then `Comp_fp(f)` is nonempty, any two objects have a finitely presented common refinement by
strict morphisms, and any two parallel morphisms are equalised by a strict morphism from a
finitely presented compactification. No noetherian hypothesis on `X` is needed.

*Proof route.* For nonemptiness and common strict refinements, descend `f` and the finite
diagram to a noetherian approximation of `X` (`L2/fp-compactification-limit`), compactify and
take schematic closures there using EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3, then base change back.
Schematic closure, used for the dense common refinement, need not preserve finite presentation
over the original base.

Parallel arrows can be equalised directly. For `u,v : B ⇉ C` over `X`, form
`E = B ×_{C ×_X C} C` using `(u,v)` and the diagonal of `C/X`. Since `C → X` is proper and
finitely presented, its diagonal is a finitely presented closed immersion (Stacks Tag 0818).
Thus `E → B` is a finitely presented closed immersion and `E → X` is proper and finitely
presented. Both arrows restrict to the same interior `Y`, so `E ×_B Y ≅ Y` scheme-theoretically.
The resulting quasi-compact open `Y → E` makes `E` a finitely presented compactification,
and `E → B` is a strict refinement equalising `u,v`. No coherence assumption on `X` is used.

**Checks.**

- For `u = v`, `E = B` and the equalising refinement is the identity.
- For the compactifications `B = C = Y ⊔ Y` of a point `Y`, the identity and the map
  folding the second component onto the first have equalizer the first component. Its
  inclusion in `B` is strict and finitely presented.
- The same diagonal argument works over any non-Noetherian affine base: proper finitely
  presented `B,C` still give finitely presented `E`; a schematic dense replacement is a
  different construction and is not used to form this equalizer.

*Source:* [Zav] Corollary 2.1.5 and its proof, p. 11; [Stacks] Lemma 29.22.12 (Tag 0818).

*Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations §0.9 (`noetherian_approximation`); `L2/fp-compactification-limit`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3 (coherent refinements); Mathlib `CategoryTheory.IsCofiltered`.

*Target names:* `fpStrictCofiltered`, `fpCompactification_isCofiltered`, `fpCompactification_equalizer`.

#### Effective Cartier ideal sheaves

Use the pinned predicate `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.IsEffectiveCartier`:
every point has an affine neighborhood on which the ideal is generated by an element whose
multiplication map is injective. On a commutative ring this is precisely the non-zero-divisor

stability under products: on a common affine neighborhood the product equation is the product

The new geometric target here is `L2/cartier-boundary-cofinal`.

*Source:* Tau Ceti `a91d3aa`, `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EffectiveCartierDivisor/Basic.lean`,
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.IsEffectiveCartier`; Stacks §31.14,
Definition 31.14.1 and Lemma 31.14.2 (Tag 01WQ).

**Checks.**

- The unit ideal is generated by 1 on every affine open and defines the empty divisor.
- On a nonempty scheme the zero ideal fails: zero is not a non-zero-divisor in a nonzero
  affine neighborhood of a point.
- On Spec k[T], the ideal (T) qualifies, whereas its pullback to T=0 is the zero ideal on

- The ideal (ε) in k[ε]/(ε²) fails because ε is a zero-divisor, although its support is
  the same closed point as that of the ideal (0).

#### Compactifications with Cartier boundary (`L2/cartier-boundary-cofinal`)

Let `S` be a noetherian affine scheme and `f : Y → S` separated of finite type. The
compactifications whose boundary `Ȳ ∖ j(Y)` is the support of an effective Cartier divisor form
an initial full subcategory of `Comp(f)`, and the refinement maps into it may be taken strict.
The empty divisor is allowed, so a proper `f` with `Ȳ = Y` qualifies.

*Proof route.* Given `Ȳ`, choose a coherent ideal `𝓘` on `Ȳ` with zero locus `Ȳ ∖ j(Y)`, for example the
ideal of the reduced boundary, and blow up `Ȳ` (not `Y`) along `𝓘`. The blow-up is proper, an
isomorphism over `Y`, and the inverse image of `𝓘` is invertible, so the new boundary is an
effective Cartier divisor. The complement of an effective Cartier divisor is scheme-theoretically
dense, so the map to `Ȳ` is strict by `L2/strict-from-dense`. Initiality follows with
EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3 (coherent refinements).

*Source:* [BP] Lemma 2.1.5 and its proof, p. 7, with the blow-up taken on the ambient compactification (§7).

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3 (coherent refinements); `L2/strict-from-dense`; Tau Ceti roadmap `StableReduction`, Layer 4 (blow-ups).

*Target names:* `cartierBoundary_initial`.

#### Compactifications to which a vector bundle extends (`L2/vector-bundle-extension-cofinal`)

Let `S` be a noetherian affine scheme, `f : Y → S` separated of finite type and `F` a finite
locally free `𝒪_Y`-module, whose rank is locally constant and may differ between open and closed
pieces of `Y`. The compactifications `Ȳ` to which `F` extends as a finite locally free module form
an initial full subcategory of `Comp(f)`, and the refinement maps into it may be taken strict.

*Proof route.* Split `Y` into its finitely many open and closed pieces `Y_r` on which `F` has constant rank
`r`, and replace `Ȳ` by the disjoint union of the scheme-theoretic closures of the `Y_r`: this is
proper over `S`, strict over `Ȳ`, and each `Y_r` is scheme-theoretically dense in its closure.
Extend `F|Y_r` to a coherent module on its closure (Stacks Tag 0G41), and blow up the `r`-th
Fitting ideal: the blow-up is an isomorphism over `Y_r` and the strict transform is locally free of
rank `r` (Stacks Tag 0ESN, whose hypotheses — schematic density and constant rank — are arranged
by the first step). Conclude with cofilteredness. Example: rank one on one connected component and
rank two on another.

*Source:* [BP] Lemma 2.1.6 and its proof, p. 7, completed as in §7; [Stacks] Lemma 31.36.3 (Tag 0ESN) and Lemma 28.23.5 (Tag 0G41).

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3 (coherent refinements); `L2/strict-from-dense`; Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.image`; Mathlib `AlgebraicGeometry.IsSchemeTheoreticallyDominant`; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.4/0.23–0.23a; Tau Ceti roadmap `StableReduction`, Layer 4 (blow-ups); `AdicSpacesPartIII:R2/fitting-ideal-invertible-locally-free`.

*Target names:* `vectorBundleExtension_initial`.

#### Continuity of maps between constructible complexes (`L2/constructible-Hom-continuity`)

Let `(X_α)` be a small filtered system of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes with affine
transition maps and limit `X`, and `Λ` a noetherian ring. Let `F₀ ∈ D⁻_c(X_{α₀}, Λ)` be bounded
above with constructible cohomology and `G₀ ∈ D⁺(X_{α₀}, Λ)` bounded below, with pullbacks
`F_α, G_α` and `F, G`. Then

```text
colim_α Hom(F_α, G_α)  →  Hom(F, G)
```

is bijective, and the same holds after shifting `G`. The hypotheses are asymmetric — `F` bounded
above and constructible, `G` bounded below — and no integer is assumed invertible on the schemes.

*Proof route.* Truncate against the bounds and induct on the cohomological range of `F`. For a
constructible sheaf use an exact sequence `K → j_!Λ_U → F → 0` with `U` quasi-compact étale over
`X_{α₀}` and `K` constructible, and induct on the Ext degree; for `j_!Λ_U` use adjunction and
SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_cohomology_limits`).

*Source:* [Abe] §1.4, the proof of the continuity lemma, pp. 4–5 (the statement on maps is proved inside it).

*Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_cohomology_limits`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17.

*Target names:* `constructibleHomContinuity`.

#### Proper-support direct image over qcqs bases (`L2/scheme-support-extension`)

Extend EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.8's étale direct image with proper support from
separated finitely presented maps to separated finite-type morphisms `f : Y → X` of qcqs
schemes. On bounded-below complexes of modules over a ring killed by a positive integer
invertible on X, for any compactification `f = p ∘ j` put `Rf_! = Rp_* ∘ j_!`. The result is
independent of the compactification up to coherent isomorphism, and it
satisfies `R(g ∘ f)_! ≅ Rg_! Rf_!` with identity and associativity coherence, base change along
any cartesian square, and the projection formula `Rf_!(A ⊗ f*B) ≅ Rf_!A ⊗ B` for
bounded-below A,B with B of finite Tor amplitude. It agrees with
extension by zero for open immersions and with `Rf_*` for proper maps, and with the classical
construction on the full finitely presented qcqs overlap, with its exchange and composition
maps. Its regular-adic extension is constructed in
`L2/coefficient-and-unbounded-extension` after `L1/scheme-adic-category`, through the limit
over `Λ/Iⁿ`, and commutes with reduction. For the perfection of such a map in
characteristic `p` it is transported through perfection, which does not change the étale site.
No second direct image with proper support is introduced alongside the classical one.

*Proof route.* Use Nagata and dense common refinements in the general compactification
category (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§G.1–G.3), without a finite-presentation assumption.
The dense refinements are strict by `L2/strict-from-dense`. For a strict refinement
`g : C' → C`, properness and the cartesian interior square give the canonical map
`j_! → Rg_* j'_!`: by proper torsion base change (§F.2.1) it is an equivalence on the
interior and on every boundary stalk, where both sides vanish. This applies to proper maps
that are not finitely presented. For a general refinement use the proper/open comparison
of Stacks Tag 0F7G; equivalently, pass through a dense replacement, whose two composites
agree by the supplier's uniqueness theorem. The canonical comparisons satisfy composition
by pasting and the cocycle law by §G.3's comparison principle.

Construct composition, base change and projection by this same proper/open calculus. On
§0.8's finitely presented qcqs domain these are the supplied transformations, since they
use the same compactification carrier and the same proper exchange. The separate theorem
`L2/fp-strict-cofiltered` applies only to finitely presented interiors and is not needed
for this finite-type extension. No descent of a non-finitely-presented proper map to a
finite-presentation diagram is used.

*API.*

- `SchemeSupport.extend` (constructor): `Rf_!` on the bounded-below coefficient domain above for separated finite-type maps of qcqs schemes, restricting coherently to `ClassicalAlgebraic.properSupports` on every finitely presented map in that domain.
- `SchemeSupport.strictRefinement` (compatibility): for every strict morphism `g : C' → C` of compactifications of such an f, the canonical `j_! → Rg_* j'_!` is an equivalence, with identity, composition and base-change coherence; neither ambient proper map nor g is assumed finitely presented.
- `SchemeSupport.compactification` (characterisation): `Rf_! ≅ Rp_* j_!` for every compactification `f = p ∘ j`, compatibly with refinements.
- `SchemeSupport.open` (simp): for an open immersion `j`, `Rj_!` is extension by zero.
- `SchemeSupport.proper` (compatibility): for proper `f`, `Rf_! = Rf_*`.
- `SchemeSupport.compose` (functoriality): `R(g ∘ f)_! ≅ Rg_! Rf_!`, with identity and associativity coherence.
- `SchemeSupport.baseChange` (compatibility): for a cartesian square the canonical exchange `g* Rf_! → Rf'_! g'*` is an isomorphism, under the stated hypotheses on schemes and coefficients.
- `SchemeSupport.projection` (compatibility): `Rf_!(A ⊗ f*B) ≅ Rf_!A ⊗ B`, with the coefficient and Tor bounds above; its unbounded extension uses the bounds of `L2/coefficient-and-unbounded-extension`.
- `SchemeSupport.adic` (compatibility): for adic coefficients, reduction modulo `Iⁿ` identifies the adic `Rf_!` with the finite-level one.

**Checks.**

- `SchemeSupport.properPoint` (computation): for a finite scheme with two points over a geometric point, `Rf_! = Rf_*` and sends `Λ` to `Λ ⊕ Λ`.
- `SchemeSupport.openBoundary` (computation): for `𝔸¹ ∖ {0} ⊂ 𝔸¹`, extension by zero has zero stalk at `0`.
- `SchemeSupport.empty` (degenerate): the map from the empty scheme gives the zero functor.
- `SchemeSupport.refinement` (characterisation): for two compactifications related by a strict refinement, the two functors `Rp_* j_!` agree by the canonical proper base-change map, not by an arbitrary isomorphism.
- For `A = 𝔽_p[x₁,x₂,…]`, `J = (x₁,x₂,…)` and `ℓ ≠ p`, the proper closed immersion
  `f : Spec(A/J) → Spec A` has support `Rf_*` with `𝔽_ℓ` coefficients. The ideal J is not
  finitely generated, so f is not finitely presented and `Comp_fp(f)` is empty: a quasi-compact
  open followed by a finitely presented proper map would make f finitely presented.
- For a proper f, the dense identity compactification strictly refines its extra-component
  compactification `Y ⊔ Y` (interior the first component); the comparison discards the zero
  extension on the second component, including for the preceding non-finitely-presented f.
- Three dense refinements give the same comparison by the canonical cocycle law; for empty
  interior every support functor and every such comparison is the unique map between zeros.

*Source:* [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.4, p. 165; Stacks §63.9 (Tag 0F7H),
Lemma 63.8.6 (Tag 0F7G), and Lemma 59.91.12 (Tag 0DDE).

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§G.1–G.3 (general compactifications and coherent refinements); `L2/strict-from-dense`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.7–0.8/F.2.1 (`VanishingCycles.properBaseChange`)/F.3; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4/E3.4.

*Target names:* `SchemeSupport.extend`, `SchemeSupport.strictRefinement`, `SchemeSupport.compactification`, `SchemeSupport.«open»`, `SchemeSupport.compose`, `SchemeSupport.baseChange`, `SchemeSupport.projection`, `SchemeSupport.adic`, `SchemeSupport.proper`.

#### Larger coefficients and unbounded complexes for the qcqs support (`L2/coefficient-and-unbounded-extension`)

The direct image with proper support of `L2/scheme-support-extension` extends to coefficient

scalars; these are part of the target. It does not assume that every module over the

étale coefficient categories with `SchemeAdic`, retaining its classical étale reduction

### Layer L3: The comparison in characteristic `p`

*Milestone:* ECD Propositions 27.1–27.4.

With L0–L2 in place, the characteristic-`p` comparison is proved for every scheme, without
finiteness hypotheses for 27.1–27.3 and for separated finite-type maps of qcqs schemes in 27.4.
The analytic input is the change of algebraically closed base field (ECD Theorem 19.5) and
Huber's comparison theorems.

#### Compatibility with tensor products and pullback (`L3/commutation-and-adjoints-27-1-27-3`)

For a scheme `X` of characteristic `p`, `c_X*` is symmetric monoidal: for the derived tensor
product with discrete coefficients, and for the completed tensor product with adic
coefficients. For every morphism `f : Y → X` of schemes of characteristic `p` the canonical
transformation

```text
(f◇)* ∘ c_X*  ≅  c_Y* ∘ f*
```

is an equivalence. Pullback needs no eligibility condition on `f`. The construction over `O` of
`L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond` satisfies the same identities for schemes locally of
finite type over `O`.

*Proof route.* Compute both sides on the underlying morphisms of sites. For adic coefficients compare the
completed tensor products after reduction (EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`isIso_of_isIso_mod`, `perfectCoefficientChange`)). Check the
identity and composition coherences inherited from the morphisms of sites.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.1, p. 163, and its mixed-characteristic version, p. 166.

*Needs:* `L1/comparison-pullback`; `L0/completed-tensor-and-colimits`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`isIso_of_isIso_mod`, `perfectCoefficientChange`).

*Target names:* `SchemeComparison.pullbackMonoidal_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pullbackMonoidalAdic_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pullbackCommutes_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pullbackCommutesAdic_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pullbackMonoidal_DVR`, `SchemeComparison.pullbackCommutes_DVR`.

#### Full faithfulness in characteristic `p` (`L3/full-faithfulness-27-2`)

For every scheme `X` of characteristic `p`, with no finiteness or noetherian hypothesis, the
functor

```text
c_X* : D_ét(X, Λ) → D_ét(X◇, Λ)
```

is fully faithful, both for `Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p` (with the left-completed
`D̂(X_ét, Λ)` as source in the unbounded case) and for `(Λ, I)` as in §2.1. Its right adjoint is
`Rc_X*`, and the unit `A → Rc_X* c_X* A` is an isomorphism.

*Proof route.* Reduce modulo `I` and pass to bounded-below complexes by Postnikov limits. Étale descent,
perfection, closed embeddings and descent along finitely many coordinates reduce to constructible
complexes on perfected affine space; by EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.6 (`ClassicalCoefficients.constructibleDerivedDescent`) to affine space of
finite type. After reducing to `X` over `𝔽̄_p`, base change to `C`,
the completed algebraic closure of `𝔽̄_p((t))`: on the scheme side étale cohomology is invariant
under change of algebraically closed base field, and on the v-sheaf side pullback is fully
faithful (ECD Theorem 19.5). Exhaust affine space by closed balls of growing radius, use that restriction
of cohomology stabilises on finite-dimensional balls, and identify the canonical map on the
cohomology of affine space with the analytic comparison over `C`. The conclusion is that the
natural restriction maps are isomorphisms; equality of cardinalities would not suffice.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.2 and its proof, pp. 163–164.

*Needs:* `L1/comparison-pullback`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E4.5 (`isIso_of_isIso_mod`, `perfectCoefficientChange`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.6 (`ClassicalCoefficients.constructibleDerivedDescent`); ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.2 (radius stabilization); ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.1; `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C6`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E2.3; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §11.2 (affine-space exhaustion).

*Target names:* `comparisonUnit_isIso_charP`, `comparisonUnitAdic_isIso_charP`, `fullyFaithful_charP`, `fullyFaithfulAdic_charP`.

#### Internal Hom and the right adjoint (`L3/internal-Hom-right-adjoint`)

For `A ∈ D_ét(X, Λ)` and `B ∈ D_ét(X◇, Λ)` there is a natural isomorphism

```text
Rc_X* RHom(c_X* A, B)  ≅  RHom(A, Rc_X* B),
```

for discrete and for adic coefficients, and for both comparison constructions. `B` lives on the
v-sheaf side; `c_X*` is not claimed to commute with internal Hom.

*Proof route.* `Rc_X*` exists because `c_X*` preserves colimits (adjoint functor theorem for presentable
categories). The isomorphism is the mate of the monoidality of `L3/commutation-and-adjoints-27-1-27-3`
under the tensor–Hom adjunctions; check it on mapping objects and after reduction.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.3(i), p. 165.

*Needs:* `L3/commutation-and-adjoints-27-1-27-3`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E3.2.

*Target names:* `internalHomComparison_charP`, `internalHomComparisonAdic_charP`, `internalHomComparison_DVR`, `internalHomComparisonAdic_DVR`.

#### Direct images and the right adjoint (`L3/pushforward-right-adjoint`)

For a morphism `f : Y → X` in either comparison setting,

```text
Rf_* ∘ Rc_Y*  ≅  Rc_X* ∘ R(f◇)_*,
```

the right-adjoint mate of the isomorphism `(f◇)* c_X* ≅ c_Y* f*` of
`L3/commutation-and-adjoints-27-1-27-3`. This is not the direct-image comparison
`c_X* Rf_* ≅ R(f◇)_* c_Y*`, a different statement that this roadmap proves for proper `f`
(`L3/rf-shriek-comparison-27-4`, `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`) and for constructible
complexes over `O` (`L6/constructible-direct-image-comparison-27-6`); no constructibility is
needed here.

*Proof route.* Form the composite adjunctions `(f◇)* c_X* ⊣ Rc_X* R(f◇)_*` and `c_Y* f* ⊣ Rf_* Rc_Y*` and transfer
the isomorphism of left adjoints by `CategoryTheory.conjugateIsoEquiv`.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.3(ii), p. 165.

*Needs:* `L3/commutation-and-adjoints-27-1-27-3`; `L3/internal-Hom-right-adjoint`; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.conjugateIsoEquiv`.

*Target names:* `SchemeComparison.pushforwardRightAdjoint_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pushforwardRightAdjointAdic_charP`, `SchemeComparison.pushforwardRightAdjoint_DVR`, `SchemeComparison.pushforwardRightAdjointAdic_DVR`.

#### Proper support in characteristic `p` (`L3/rf-shriek-comparison-27-4`)

Let `f : Y → X` be a separated morphism of finite type between quasi-compact quasi-separated
schemes of characteristic `p`, or the perfection of such a morphism. Then `f◇` is compactifiable,
representable in locally spatial diamonds and of locally finite `dim.trg`, and the canonical map

```text
c_X* Rf_!  →  R(f◇)_! c_Y*
```

is an equivalence, for discrete coefficients killed by an integer prime to `p` and for adic
coefficients, with `Rf_!` the direct image with proper support of `L2/scheme-support-extension`.
Its right-adjoint mate is `Rf^! Rc_X* ≅ Rc_Y* R(f◇)^!`. The statement is in this direction; it does
not say that `c_Y* Rf^! ≅ R(f◇)^! c_X*`.

*Proof route.* Factor `f = p ∘ j` by EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.2 (`nagataCompactification`). Eligibility of `f◇`: open
immersions go to open immersions; for proper `f` check after base change to a perfectoid `S`,
where `f◇` becomes the diamond of the analytified proper map, with `dim.trg` bounded by the
relative dimension. For open immersions the comparison is extension by zero. For proper maps
reduce by Postnikov limits and colimits to constructible coefficients and finitely presented
models over bases of finite type (EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.6 (`ClassicalCoefficients.constructibleDerivedDescent`)), then use the change of
algebraically closed field (ECD Theorem 19.5), proper base change for schemes, and Huber's
comparison for proper maps. Pass to adic coefficients by reduction and to the right adjoints by
mates.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.4 and its proof, p. 165.

*Needs:* EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.2 (`nagataCompactification`); `L2/scheme-support-extension`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.6 (`ClassicalCoefficients.constructibleDerivedDescent`); `L3/full-faithfulness-27-2`; `L0/six-operations-for-adic-coefficients`; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §8.2; `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C6`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17; Mathlib `CategoryTheory.conjugateIsoEquiv`; `DiamondsAndVStacks:D6/etale-site-comparison`; `DiamondSixOperations:S0`; `L1/char-p-scheme-diamond-and-comparison-functor`; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§0.4/0.23–0.23a.

*Target names:* `SchemeDiamond.charPSupportData`, `properSupportComparison_charP`, `properSupportComparisonAdic_charP`, `properPushforwardComparison_charP`, `upperShriekComparison_charP`, `upperShriekComparisonAdic_charP`.

### Layer L4: Proper support over a complete discrete valuation ring

*Milestone:* ECD Proposition 27.5.

Over `O` the base `Spd O` is not analytic, so the comparison is detected on an analytic test
space whose diamond covers `Spd O` and is cohomologically smooth over it.

#### The analytic test space over `O` (`L4/analytic-test-space`)

Let `O` be as in §2.4, with uniformiser `π`. Give `O[[x]]` the `(π, x)`-adic topology and let
`T = {x ≠ 0} ⊂ Spa(O[[x]], O[[x]])`, with its structure map to `Spa(O, O)`. Then:

- `T` is an analytic adic space: on `T` the element `x` is a topologically nilpotent unit.
- `T◇ → Spd O` is surjective and `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth for every prime `ℓ ≠ p`.
- Hence pullback along `T◇ → Spd O` is conservative on `D_ét`, and stays so after any base
  change.
- For `X` locally of finite type over `O`, `(X ×_{Spec O} T)◇ ≅ X◇ ×_{Spd O} T◇`, naturally in
  `X`, where `X ×_{Spec O} T` is the relative analytification.

`Spa(O, O)` is not analytic, so the smoothness is checked after base change to perfectoid test
objects, not on `Spa(O, O)` itself. Both the special and the generic fibre are covered: over a perfectoid field in which `π = 0`,
choose a nonzero topologically nilpotent value of `x`; over one in which `π ≠ 0`, `π` is
topologically nilpotent and one again chooses a nonzero topologically nilpotent value of `x`. The point of `Spa(O[[x]], O[[x]])` with `x = 0` is not in `T`.

*Proof route.* `T` is the analytic locus `x ≠ 0` of the formal chart of `O[[x]]`; the relative analytification is
Huber's fibre product over `T`, whose diamond is the fibre product of diamonds. For a perfectoid
`S → Spd O` with untilt `S♯`, `T◇ ×_{Spd O} S` is the diamond of the punctured open unit disc over
`S♯`, a separated smooth analytic adic space over `S♯`, hence `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth over `S`
(ECD Proposition 24.4). Since `ℓ`-cohomological smoothness is tested after base change to strictly
totally disconnected perfectoid spaces (ECD Definition 23.8) and compactifiability is v-local on
the target (ECD Proposition 22.3(iii)), `T◇ → Spd O` is `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth. Pullback along
a surjective `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth map is universally conservative.

The light definition `DVRTestSpace.pointSet O` is the intersection of
`TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa (⊤ : Subring (PowerSeries O))` with the valuations whose
support omits x. Power series carry the coefficientwise topology; for a DVR with its adic
topology this is the (π,x)-adic topology. Prove its membership characterization, independence
of the uniformizer, and `DVRTestSpace.analytic` in its set-level form: every point has nonopen
support and lies in some rational chart `{v(πⁿ) ≤ v(x) ≠ 0}`. Identify the underlying space
of `DVRTestSpace.mk` with this locus as `DVRTestSpace.points`. The special-fibre,
generic-fibre and excluded-zero Checks below test this definition separately from the
construction of its adic structure.

*API.*

- `DVRTestSpace.mk` (constructor): the adic space `T` with its `(π, x)`-adic charts and its map to `Spa(O, O)`.
- `DVRTestSpace.analytic` (characterisation): on `T` the element `x` is a topologically nilpotent unit, so every point of `T` is analytic.
- `DVRTestSpace.diamondCover` (structure): `T◇ → Spd O` is surjective and `ℓ`-cohomologically smooth for every prime `ℓ ≠ p`.
- `DVRTestSpace.conservative` (universal-property): after any base change, pullback to the test space reflects isomorphisms in `D_ét`.
- `DVRTestSpace.fibreProduct` (compatibility): the diamond of the relative analytification of `X` over `T` is `X◇ ×_{Spd O} T◇`, naturally in `X`.

**Checks.**

- `DVRTestSpace.base` (degenerate): for `X = Spec O` the identification returns `T◇`.
- `DVRTestSpace.special` (computation): over a perfectoid field of characteristic `p` above the special fibre, a nonzero topologically nilpotent value of `x` gives a point of `T◇`.
- `DVRTestSpace.generic` (computation): over a perfectoid field above the generic fibre, values of `π` and `x` that are topologically nilpotent with `x ≠ 0` give a point of `T◇`.
- `DVRTestSpace.zeroExcluded` (non-example): the point with `x = 0` of `Spa(O[[x]], O[[x]])` is not in `T`.
- `DVRTestSpace.unbounded_continuous_point`: for `O = k[[t]]` with k perfect containing
  an element s transcendental over 𝔽_p, compose reduction modulo t with the rank-two valuation
  given first by x-adic order and then by a valuation of k extending the valuation at s = ∞
  on `𝔽_p(s)`, applied to the leading coefficient. It is continuous and has `v(x) ≠ 0`, but
  `v(s) > 1`; thus it lies in `Cont(O[[x]])` and not in T. Boundedness on the plus ring in
  `Spa(O[[x]], O[[x]])` is essential.
- With ℤ_q given the discrete topology, the x-adic valuation on ℤ_q[[x]] has `|qⁿ| = 1 > |x|` for every n, so it lies in no claimed rational chart. This topology fails `IsCompleteDVRBase`.
- With the indiscrete coefficient topology the ambient continuous valuation spectrum is empty, since 1 lies in the closure of zero; the existence Checks require the adic base topology.

*Source:* [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.5, p. 166, with the base change to perfectoid test objects explained in §7.

*Needs:* `L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`; Tau Ceti `TauCeti.ValuationSpectrum.spa`; `DiamondsAndVStacks:D6/gluing-and-the-diamond-functor`; `AdicSpacesPartII:R1/scheme-fibre-product-analytification`; `AdicSpacesPartIII:R2/noetherian-formal-scheme-as-adic-space`; `DiamondSixOperations:S4`; `DiamondSixOperations:S5`.

*Target names:* `DVRTestSpace.mk`, `DVRTestSpace.analytic`, `DVRTestSpace.diamondCover`, `DVRTestSpace.conservative`, `DVRTestSpace.fibreProduct`.

#### Proper support over `O` (`L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`)

Let `f : Y → X` be a separated morphism between schemes of finite type over `O` (global finite
type, not only locally). Then `f◇` is compactifiable, representable in locally spatial diamonds
and of locally finite `dim.trg`, and

`Rf^! Rc_X* ≅ Rc_Y* R(f◇)^!`. The compared map is the canonical base-change transformation. No full

*Proof route.* Factor `f` into an open immersion and a proper map; for the open immersion both sides are

coefficients by Postnikov limits and finite cohomological dimension, pull back to the test space
`T` of `L4/analytic-test-space`, and identify both sides there: Huber's proper comparison on the
scheme side, and the base change of ECD Proposition 17.6 with ECD Lemma 15.6 on the diamond side.
Since pullback to `T` is conservative, the canonical map is an equivalence; conclude for adic
coefficients by reduction and for the right adjoints by mates.

### Layer L5: Alterations and normal-crossing boundaries

*Milestone:* the descent and local calculations that the proof of ECD 27.6 asserts without detail.

This layer carries the geometric part of the proof of ECD 27.6: smooth alteration pairs over
finite field extensions, descent of the comparison along proper hypercovers, and the explicit
comparison of direct images along a normal-crossing boundary. The regular-model and semistable
alteration theorems are imported.

#### Smooth alteration pairs over finite field extensions (`L5/smooth-alteration-pairs`)

For any field F, integral separated finite-type F-scheme X and proper closed subset Z of X,
construct a finite extension `F'/F`, an alteration `φ : X₁ → X`, and an open immersion
`j : X₁ → X̄₁`, where X̄₁ is geometrically integral, smooth and projective over F'. The
F-structure on X₁ factors through its F'-structure. The reduced union of the complement of
X₁ with `j(φ⁻¹(Z))` is a strict normal-crossing divisor D whose nonempty intersections of
r distinct components are smooth of codimension r over F'. If F is perfect, retain the
generically étale choice of φ.

SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.19 supplies the regular projective alteration and its finite
extension data. The stronger smooth-pair conclusion is `smoothAlterationPair` here. Apply
that theorem over an algebraic closure of F and descend the finite-presentation diagram,
including its finitely many smooth boundary strata, to a finite extension F'/F, as in [dJ]
§4.5. This retains the common field over which both the ambient and all boundary strata are
smooth; regularity over an imperfect field alone does not imply these hypotheses.

**Checks.**

- A smooth projective geometrically integral X with a smooth strict normal-crossing boundary
  already satisfies the target with `F' = F` and the identity alteration.
- Over `F = 𝔽₂(s,t)`, the regular conic `x² + s y² + t z² = 0` with Z empty admits an
  identity regular alteration but is not smooth. Over `F' = F(√s,√t)` its reduced base
  change is a projective line mapping finitely and surjectively to the conic; this gives
  the smooth-pair alteration with empty boundary.
- For `X = 𝔸¹_F` in characteristic p and `Z = V(Tᵖ − a)` with `a ∉ Fᵖ`, X is smooth
  and Z is regular, but Z is not smooth over F. After `F' = F(a^(1/p))`, use
  `𝔸¹_{F'} ⊂ ℙ¹_{F'}`; the reduced inverse image of Z and the point at infinity are
  disjoint smooth boundary components.

*Source:* [dJ] Theorem 4.1, Remark 4.2 and §4.5, p. 66.

*Needs:* SchemeAndStackFoundations §4.19 (`alteration_theorem`), §§4.16–4.17 (alterations and strict normal crossings), and §0.9 (finite-presentation descent).

*Target names:* `smoothAlterationPair`.

#### Descent along proper hypercovers by alterations (`L5/alteration-hypercover-descent`)

Let `Λ` be a finite ring killed by an integer prime to `p`. For a scheme of finite type over a
field or over `O`, with the open subscheme whose direct image is being compared, build a proper
hypercover by applying the alteration theorems of de Jong recursively to the matching objects.
For uniformly bounded-below complexes of Λ-modules, pullback is an equivalence onto Cartesian
complexes with the same uniform lower bound on the hypercover (cohomological descent, at the
enhanced level), on both the scheme and v-sheaf sides. The descent is compatible with `c*`: the comparison map is an
equivalence on the base as soon as it is one on every term of the hypercover. The alterations
may have degree divisible by `ℓ`: nothing is divided by the degree and no trace splitting is
used. A proper surjection is not a covering of the étale site; the descent used is proper
cohomological descent. Example: the degree-`ℓ` map `ℙ¹ → ℙ¹`, `z ↦ z^ℓ`, over a field of
characteristic `≠ ℓ`, as the first stage of a proper hypercover, gives descent with `Λ = ℤ/ℓ` although
its trace splitting is unavailable.

*Proof route.* Work on affine opens of the base. In each matching object pass to its reduction
and integral components; field components use `smoothAlterationPair`, retaining the finite
extension over which the pair is smooth, and components dominating the trait use the
semistable alteration theorem after a finite extension. These reductions
meet the separated/integral hypotheses of SchemeAndStackFoundations §§4.19–4.20. On the scheme
side use §2.16 (`proper_hypercover_descent`), extending its sheaf-level comparison to uniformly
bounded-below finite-coefficient complexes by truncation and proper base change, and to
enhanced Cartesian descent using EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4/E3.4. On the v-sheaf side
a proper surjection is a v-cover, so EnhancedDerivedSheaves §E2.6 applies directly to this
v-hypercover. `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`
identifies the two augmentations, so the comparison is detected on the hypercover.

**Checks.**

- The identity proper hypercover induces the identity comparison on bounded-below complexes.
- The degree-ℓ map of ℙ¹ gives descent with ℤ/ℓ coefficients even though its trace cannot split the unit.
- Reducing a nonreduced matching object and taking the disjoint union of its integral components gives a proper surjection; no smoothness or integrality of the original object is assumed.

*Source:* [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.6, p. 166 (the reduction by alterations, whose descent step is not written there; §7); Stacks Tags 0DHM/0DHP.

*Needs:* `L5/smooth-alteration-pairs` (`smoothAlterationPair`, retaining the finite field extension and smooth boundary strata); SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.16 (`proper_hypercover_descent`) and §§4.16–4.20 (`alteration_theorem` for regular projective models and the semistable trait alteration); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3 (proper base change); SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17; `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4/E2.6/E3.4.

*Target names:* `alterationHypercoverDescent`, `alterationHypercover_detectsUnit`.

#### The comparison along a normal-crossing boundary (`L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison`)

Let `F` be a finite extension of the residue field `k` of `O` or of its fraction field `K`, let `X`
be smooth over `F`, `D ⊂ X` a strict normal-crossing divisor with smooth strata, and
`j : X ∖ D → X`. For `Λ` finite and killed by an integer prime to `p`, the canonical comparison map
for `Rj_*Λ` becomes an equivalence after pullback to the analytic test space of
`L4/analytic-test-space`. For q ≥ 0, at a geometric point x̄ where exactly r branches of D meet,

```text
(Rᵠ j_* Λ)ₓ̄  ≅  ⋀ᵠ (Λ(−1)^r),
```

and the corresponding geometric stalk on the analytic side has the same description. The
Kummer classes of the r branches generate these stalks; their identifications respect
generization, cup products and residues, and the comparison carries each generator to its
analytic counterpart. The number of branches is measured at x̄, so the formula varies across
the strictly henselian chart. In equal characteristic `K = k((t))` is imperfect: use
`smoothAlterationPair` over a finite extension `K₁` of `K`, retaining the K₁-structure and
smoothness of the boundary strata ([dJ] Remark 4.2 and §4.5). Purely inseparable extensions
change neither the étale sites nor the diamonds. Example: for one boundary component the degree-one generator and
its Tate twist match; for two components the cup-product generator and both residue maps match.

*Proof route.* Scheme side: purity `Ri^!Λ ≅ Λ(−c)[−2c]` on each smooth stratum, the Kummer class of a divisor,
cycle classes and the Gysin sequence give the exterior algebra by induction on the strata. Analytic
side: the same description for the analytification over `T`, from analytic purity and the
cohomology of the punctured disc. Then check that `c*` sends each algebraic Kummer class to the
analytic one, compatibly with cup products and residues, and conclude along the strata
filtration. Reduction of general constructible coefficients to constant ones uses finite covers
and `L5/alteration-hypercover-descent`.

**Checks.**

- On the strict henselization of 𝔸¹ at 0 with D={0} and Λ=𝔽_ℓ, the closed stalk of `R¹j_*Λ` is Λ(−1), while its restriction to the generic open is zero.
- At an intersection of two branches, their Kummer classes generate degree one and their exterior product generates degree two; the two residue maps have the exterior-algebra signs.
- Away from D there are zero branches: the degree-zero stalk is Λ and every positive-degree stalk vanishes, compatibly with generization from a boundary point.

*Source:* [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.6, pp. 166–167 (the local calculation is asserted there without detail; §7).

*Needs:* `L5/smooth-alteration-pairs` (`smoothAlterationPair`, smooth ambient and boundary strata over the retained finite extension); `L5/alteration-hypercover-descent`; `L4/analytic-test-space`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.10 (`ClassicalAlgebraic.smoothBoundaryPurity`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §F.6 (`VanishingCycles.smoothPairGysin`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §0.9 (`ClassicalAlgebraic.cycleClasses`); EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.7/0.10/F.6; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §10.4; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§8.2/11.1–11.2; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§4.16–4.20 (`alteration_theorem` for regular projective models and the semistable trait alteration).

*Target names:* `normalCrossingComparison`, `normalCrossingStalks`.

#### Examples

The degree-ℓ map of ℙ¹ supplies descent with ℤ/ℓ coefficients without dividing by ℓ. For one boundary branch, H¹ has the Kummer generator with twist −1; for two branches their product generates degree two, with its residue signs.

#### Dependencies

L4; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§2.16/4.16–4.20; EnhancedDerivedSheaves §§E1.3–E1.4/E2.6/E3.4; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§0.9–0.10/F.6; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§10.4/11.1.

### Layer L6: Constructible complexes over a complete discrete valuation ring

*Milestone:* ECD Propositions 27.6 and 27.7.

The punctured trait and strictly semistable models complete the local analysis; the two
propositions follow by dévissage. The coefficients are finite rings killed by an integer prime to
`p`.

#### The punctured trait (`L6/trait-open-comparison`)

Let `O` be as in §2.4 with fraction field `K`, `j : Spec K → Spec O`, `Λ` finite and killed by an
integer prime to `p`, and `M` a finite `Λ`-module with continuous `G_K`-action, viewed as an étale
sheaf on `Spec K`. Then the comparison map

```text
c_O* Rj_* M  →  Rj◇_* c_K* M
```

is an equivalence. At a geometric closed point `s̄` both stalks are canonically `RΓ(I_K, M)`, with
`I_K = Gal(K^sep/K^sh)` the inertia group and the residual action of `G_k`; at geometric generic
points both are `M`. These identifications commute with the comparison map, the transition maps
and the twists. The closed stalk is inertia cohomology, not `G_K`-cohomology.

*Proof route.* Scheme side: the stalk of `Rj_*M` at `s̄` is the cohomology of `Spec K^sh`, which is continuous
cohomology of `I_K` with its `G_k`-action. Analytic side: the nearby-cycle stalk formula for
constant coefficients identifies the stalk with the same Galois cohomology, compatibly with the
completion of `Spec O` along `s`. Diamond side: the étale site of `Spd K` is that of `Spa K`, and
v-cohomology agrees with étale cohomology on `D_ét`. Finally verify that the canonical comparison
acts as the identity on continuous cocycles.

*Source:* [ECD] proofs of Propositions 27.6 and 27.7, p. 167 (the punctured trait is said there to be a direct computation with Galois cohomology on both sides).

*Needs:* ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.3; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §4.4; `DiamondsAndVStacks:D6/etale-site-comparison`; `DiamondEtaleCohomology:C2`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17; `L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`.

*Target names:* `traitOpenComparison`, `traitClosedStalk`.

#### Strictly semistable models (`L6/semistable-boundary-induction`)

Let `X` be a proper strictly semistable model over `O`, after a finite extension of the trait (the
residue field stays perfect), with generic fibre `j : X_η → X`. For constant coefficients `Λ`,
finite and killed by an integer prime to `p`, the comparison map for `Rj_*Λ` is an equivalence.

*Proof route.* On smooth strata apply `L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison` and smooth pullback. Induct on the
dimension of the charts to show that the cone of the comparison is supported on finitely many
closed points of the special fibre. Proper pushforward is conservative on complexes supported on
finitely many closed points (after a finite extension of the residue field their stalks appear as
a finite direct sum; it is not conservative in general). By `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`
the pushforward to the trait reduces the claim to `L6/trait-open-comparison`.

*Source:* [ECD] proof of Proposition 27.6, p. 167 (the induction is sketched there; §7).

*Needs:* `L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison`; `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`; `L6/trait-open-comparison`; SchemeAndStackFoundations §§4.16–4.20; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§8.2/11.1–11.2.

*Target names:* `semistableComparison`.

#### Direct images of constructible complexes (`L6/constructible-direct-image-comparison-27-6`)

Let `f : Y → X` be a separated morphism between schemes of finite type over `O`, where `O` may
have mixed or equal characteristic, and `Λ` a finite ring killed by an integer prime to `p`. For
every `A ∈ Dᵇ_c(Y_ét, Λ)` the canonical map

```text
c_X* Rf_* A  →  R(f◇)_* c_Y* A
```

is an equivalence. Here constructible means bounded with finite locally constant cohomology on the
strata of a finite constructible stratification. Nothing is claimed for unbounded or for `ℓ`-adic
complexes beyond this.

*Proof route.* Factor `f` by Nagata; the proper part is `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`. For the open part,
dévissage and proper hypercovers reduce to constant coefficients on an open `U` lying in the
generic fibre (`π ≠ 0`) or in the special fibre (`π = 0`); the split is by the uniformiser, since in
equal characteristic an open of characteristic `p` can meet the generic fibre. Use the field
alterations and `L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison` for the special-fibre and horizontal cases,
and `L6/semistable-boundary-induction` with `L6/trait-open-comparison` for the generic-fibre
inclusion. Every step acts on the canonical map, including Galois actions and twists.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.6 and its proof, pp. 166–167, with the case split corrected as in §7.

*Needs:* `L4/proper-support-comparison-27-5`; `L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison`; `L6/semistable-boundary-induction`; `L6/trait-open-comparison`; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.2 (`nagataCompactification`).

*Target names:* `constructibleDirectImageComparison`.

#### Full faithfulness on constructible complexes over `O` (`L6/constructible-full-faithfulness-27-7`)

Let `X` be locally of finite type over `O` and `Λ` a finite ring killed by an integer prime to
`p`. For every `A ∈ Dᵇ_c(X_ét, Λ)` the unit `A → Rc_X* c_X* A` is an equivalence; so `c_X*` is fully
faithful on bounded constructible complexes. The prime-to-`p` hypothesis is part of the statement
(§7).

*Proof route.* For `X = Spec O`, dévissage along finite covers reduces to constant coefficients and to constants
supported on the closed fibre, where `L6/trait-open-comparison` and continuous Galois cohomology
apply. For separated affine `X` of finite type, use `L6/constructible-direct-image-comparison-27-6`
for the structure map to `Spec O`. Check the unit on a basis of such étale neighbourhoods and use
étale descent for `X` locally of finite type; no global finite-type hypothesis is introduced.

*Source:* [ECD] Proposition 27.7 and its proof, p. 167.

*Needs:* `L6/constructible-direct-image-comparison-27-6`; `L6/trait-open-comparison`; `L1/comparison-pullback`; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §1.3; EtaleCohomologyFoundations §§F.0.1/0.8/F.2.1/F.3; SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.17.

*Target names:* `constructibleUnit_isIso`, `constructible_fullyFaithful`.

#### Examples

At the closed point of a trait the direct-image stalk is inertia cohomology, whereas the geometric generic stalk is M. For O = k[[t]] the case split uses t, and the generic field k((t)) requires the stated finite-field-extension alteration. The comparison unit is invertible for bounded constructible finite prime-to-p coefficients.

#### Dependencies

L1–L2 and L4–L5; ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology §§1.3/4.3–4.4; DiamondsAndVStacks D6; DiamondEtaleCohomology C2; the scheme trait and alteration contracts in §3.

## 6. Required examples and regression tests

Each definition in §5 lists its Checks; these are the ones that separate the intended objects
from plausible wrong ones, and every implementation should check them.

- **Coefficients at a geometric point** (`Λ = ℤ_ℓ`, `I = (ℓ)`, `ℓ ≠ p`). The completed unit
  `Λ̂` lies in the adic category and reduces to `ℤ/ℓⁿ` (`AdicEtale.point`); the constant `ℤ/ℓ` lies
  in it, so completeness is not torsion-freeness (`AdicEtale.torsion`); the discrete constant `ℚ_ℓ` does not,
  and its completion is zero (`AdicEtale.reject_inverted`, `SchemeAdic.reject_inverted`);
  `ℤ/ℓ ⊗ᴸ ℤ/ℓ` has cohomology in degree `−1` (`AdicEtale.tensor_point`); the telescope
  `Λ̂ →ℓ Λ̂ →ℓ ⋯` has ambient colimit `Λ̂[1/ℓ]`, the continuous coefficient sheaf,
  and `I`-completion zero (`AdicEtale.telescope`);
  for two geometric points over one, `Rf_!Λ̂ = Rf_*Λ̂ = Λ̂ ⊕ Λ̂` (`AdicSix.proper`). The reconstruction
  uses derived limits: the `H⁻¹`-tower of `(⊕ₖ ℤ/ℓᵏ) ⊗ᴸ ℤ/ℓⁿ` has a nonzero `lim¹` (`L0/adic-coefficient-limit`).
- **Rational lattices.** The rank-one lattice has endomorphism ring `ℚ_ℓ`, `ℓ`-power torsion
  becomes zero, multiplication by `ℓ` becomes invertible (`RationalLattice.point`,
  `RationalLattice.torsion`, `RationalLattice.isogeny`).
- **Plus rings.** For a perfect field `k ⊃ 𝔽_p` containing an element transcendental over `𝔽_p`,
  the plus ring of the characteristic-`p` chart is not `k` (`SchemeDiamond.plusRing`), and the
  residue avatars `Spd(k, k)` and `Spd(k, k⁺)` differ (`SchemeDiamond.nonagreement`); `Spec 𝔽_p`
  goes to `Spd(𝔽_p, 𝔽_p)` (`SchemeDiamond.Fp`); Frobenius acts invertibly on `X◇` for non-perfect
  `X` (`SchemeDiamond.frobenius`); `Spd ℤ_p` is not a diamond (`SchemeDiamond.notDiamond`).
- **Compactifications.** Over a point, `Y ⊔ Y` with `j` the first summand is a compactification in
  which `Y` is not dense (`Compactification.extraComponent`); folding it onto `Y` is a morphism that
  is not strict (`Compactification.Hom.boundary`); two morphisms that agree on `Y` and differ on the
  extra component stay distinct (`Compactification.parallelMaps`); the affine line is not a
  compactification of itself (`Compactification.nonproper`); the closed immersion of
  `Spec(A/J)` for a non-finitely-generated `J` is proper but not finitely presented
  (`FPCompactification.rejectFiniteType`); the projective line compactifying the affine line keeps
  its chart after a field extension (`FPCompactification.basePoint`); compactifications of the
  empty scheme keep every proper ambient (`Compactification.category_empty`).
- **Limits and continuity.** Continuity of étale cohomology applies to `𝔾_m` in degree `2`, where the
  sheaf colimit is `𝔾_m` but the individual inverse images need not be
  (SchemeAndStackFoundations §2.15 (`etale_cohomology_limits`)); a bundle of rank one on one component and rank two on another
  extends after refinement (`L2/vector-bundle-extension-cofinal`). Quasi-compactness is essential
  for finite-stage Hom equality: over a field k the schemes `X_n = ⊔_{m≥n} Spec k` have affine
  tail inclusions and empty limit, so their constant maps 0 and 1 to `𝔸¹_k` agree at the limit
  but at no stage (the limit contract in §3).
- **Proper support over qcqs bases.** For the open immersion `𝔾_m ⊂ 𝔸¹` extension by zero has zero
  stalk at the origin (`SchemeSupport.openBoundary`); strict refinements give the same `Rp_* j_!` by
  the canonical map (`SchemeSupport.refinement`).
- **The test space.** The point with `x = 0` of `Spa(O[[x]], O[[x]])` is not in `T`
  (`DVRTestSpace.zeroExcluded`); `T◇` has points over the special and the generic fibre
  (`DVRTestSpace.special`, `DVRTestSpace.generic`).
- **Alterations and boundaries.** The degree-`ℓ` map `z ↦ z^ℓ` of `ℙ¹` gives descent with `ℤ/ℓ`
  coefficients without a trace splitting (`L5/alteration-hypercover-descent`); for one boundary
  component the Kummer class and its twist match, and for two components the cup product and both
  residues match (`L5/normal-crossing-local-comparison`); the closed stalk of the punctured trait is
  inertia cohomology, not `G_K`-cohomology (`L6/trait-open-comparison`).

## 7. Corrections to the sources

The following points in the sources are handled differently here. In each case the stated result
stands, with the hypothesis or argument indicated; none of them changes a target of another
roadmap.

1. **Theorem 1.8(iii) of [ECD]** (p. 5). For `f : Y → X` the projection formula
   `Rf_!(A ⊗ f*B) ≅ Rf_!A ⊗ B` is stated with `A` and `B` in the wrong categories: `A` lives on the
   source `Y` and `B` on the target `X`, as in ECD Proposition 22.23. `L0/adic-forms-of-introduction-theorems`
   uses this form.
2. **The mixed-characteristic functor of [ECD] §27** (p. 165). The functor `X ↦ X◇` over `O` is
   described as taking values in diamonds over `Spd O`. Already `(Spec ℤ_p)◇ = Spd ℤ_p` is not a
   diamond ([SW] Lecture 10, remark after Remark 10.1.4, p. 74): it contains the sub-v-sheaf
   `*` given by `p = 0`, which is not a diamond by the geometric-field product test in L1, while
   sub-v-sheaves of diamonds are diamonds (ECD Proposition 11.10). The correct statement, and the
   one that the proof of Proposition 27.5 uses, is that `X◇` is a small v-sheaf whose map to `Spd O`
   is representable in locally spatial diamonds (`L1/mixed-characteristic-scheme-diamond`).
3. **The proof of [ECD] Proposition 27.5** (p. 166). The cohomological smoothness of
   `T◇ → Spd O` for the test space `T = {x ≠ 0} ⊂ Spa O[[x]]` is attributed to ECD Proposition 24.4,
   which is about smooth maps of analytic adic spaces, while `Spa(O, O)` is not analytic. The
   argument goes through after base change to perfectoid test objects, where `T` becomes the
   punctured open unit disc, using ECD Definition 23.8 and Proposition 22.3(iii)
   (`L4/analytic-test-space`).
4. **The proof of [ECD] Proposition 27.6** (pp. 166–167). The reduction by de Jong's alterations,
   the explicit computation along a normal-crossing boundary, the induction for strictly
   semistable models and the case of the punctured trait are stated without proof. Layers L5 and
   L6 supply them. The descent along an alteration is proper cohomological descent along a
   hypercover; nothing is divided by the degree of the alteration, which may be divisible by `ℓ`.
5. **The case split in the proof of [ECD] Proposition 27.6** (p. 166). The dévissage is split
   into opens with `p ≠ 0` and opens of characteristic `p`. When `O` has equal characteristic,
   `O = k[[t]]`, every open has characteristic `p`, but an open meeting the generic fibre is not of finite type
   over `k` (its generic fibre lives over `K = k((t))`), so the alteration and normal-crossing
   reduction over `k` does not apply to it. The split is by the
   uniformiser instead: opens in the generic fibre (`π ≠ 0`) and opens in the special fibre
   (`π = 0`); over the imperfect field `K = k((t))` de Jong's Theorem 4.1 gives an alteration
   smooth over a finite extension of `K` (his Remark 4.2), and purely inseparable extensions change
   neither étale sites nor diamonds (`L6/constructible-direct-image-comparison-27-6`).
6. **[ECD] Proposition 27.7** (p. 167). The statement assumes only that `Λ` is finite, but the proof
   goes through Proposition 27.6, which assumes `Λ` killed by an integer prime to `p`; the
   `p`-torsion case is not covered by the argument. `L6/constructible-full-faithfulness-27-7` keeps
   the prime-to-`p` hypothesis.
7. **The remark after Definition 2.1.2 of [BP]** (p. 7). Cofilteredness of the category of
   compactifications is justified by the existence of common refinements only. Since a
   compactification need not be dense, two morphisms can differ on an extra boundary component, so
   parallel morphisms must also be equalised; the equaliser is the pullback of the closed diagonal
   ([Stacks] Lemma 38.32.1). See EtaleCohomologyFoundations §G.3 (coherent refinements).
8. **The proof of [BP] Lemma 2.1.5** (p. 7). In the notation of [BP], `X` is the open and `X̄` its
   compactification. The blow-up meant is the blow-up of the ambient
   compactification `X̄` along the ideal of its boundary; the printed text blows up `X`, on which that
   ideal is the unit ideal. See `L2/cartier-boundary-cofinal`.
9. **The proof of [BP] Lemma 2.1.6** (p. 7). A coherent extension of the bundle is fed into the
   Fitting-ideal blow-up of [Stacks] Lemma 31.36.3, which needs the open to be scheme-theoretically
   dense and the bundle to have constant rank. Neither is arranged, since compactifications need not
   be dense and the bundle need not have constant rank. Splitting `Y` into its constant-rank pieces
   and replacing the ambient by the disjoint union of their scheme-theoretic closures arranges both
   (`L2/vector-bundle-extension-cofinal`).

## 8. Suggested Lean forms

[`Suggested.lean`](Suggested.lean) records light real-bodied coefficient and plus-ring
definitions, the scalar localization of an existing linear category, scheme-carrier predicates,
and the valuation locus of the DVR test space. Its named theorems state the scalar-localization

(`RationalLattice.torsion`), the full plus ring for algebraic fields (`charPPlus_eq_top`), and
analyticity of the DVR test locus (`DVRTestSpace.analytic`) on existing library carriers.
These are suggested forms, not an exhaustive checklist; the README is definitive.

The enhanced categories `AdicEtale`, `SchemeAdic`, their six operations and coherent exchange
maps, the scheme v-sheaves and comparison functors, compactification refinements and their
initiality theorems, alteration descent, and ECD 27.1–27.7 are mathematical targets of the
corresponding layers above. Their intended domains, codomains and hypotheses are specified
there. A category constructed by a supplier remains that supplier's category; no opaque carrier
or data-valued placeholder is part of the suggested forms.

For each exchange theorem the target is invertibility of the specified canonical map.
`AdicSix.pullbackReduction` and `AdicSix.supportReduction` are the scalar-change maps, and
`AdicSix.reduce` includes their right-adjoint mates under the named adjunctions. The maps
`AdicSix.baseChangeMap`, `SchemeSupport.baseChangeMap`, the support exchanges of 27.4 and 27.5,
and the direct-image exchange of 27.6 are retained as named constructions with this requirement.
The identifications `Rf_! ≅ Rp_* j_!` commute with refinement. Tensor and internal-Hom
comparisons require enhanced naturality and coherence. The normal-crossing comparison matches
Kummer generators, cup products and residues as specified in L5.
