# p-adic measures, completed group algebras, and characteristic ideals

This roadmap develops the shared analytic and algebraic language of Iwasawa theory and p-adic L-functions: bounded measures on profinite spaces, their completed-group-algebra description, Mahler–Amice theory and its integral operator calculus, pseudo-measures and character evaluation, the structure of finitely generated Iwasawa modules and their characteristic ideals, determinants and specialization, and the coefficient-order algebra used in integral Brumer–Stark and Euler-system arguments.

This README is the definitive mathematical specification. [Suggested.lean](Suggested.lean) gives nonexhaustive prototype signatures and tests, not proofs of the targets. Every target below includes its stated API and supporting lemmas. Statements proved in the libraries are inputs, not a second implementation programme. New library declarations use the `TauCeti` prefix and the subject-appropriate namespaces beneath it; unprefixed names in the prototype describe the proposed API, not a competing library.

## Conventions and dependency order

The prime p includes 2 unless a target explicitly restricts it. Put Z = ℤ_p, U = Zˣ, A_n = (ℤ/p^nℤ)ˣ and red_n = `PadicInt.unitsToZModPow n`. Indices n and coefficient precisions r are independent and include 0. A_0 is the trivial group; ZMod(p^0) is the zero ring. Finite projection pushforward sums coefficients over fibres, without averaging.

D(X,R) is `AbstractMeasure X R R`. For compact X and a normed coefficient ring this is the continuous R-linear dual of C(X,R). Weak and strong topologies are selected explicitly and never identified. Norms of measures mean the operator norm of the field-valued continuous functional through `toCLMEquiv`; an integral measure's norm, when used, means the norm after its specified extension to the coefficient fraction field. Integral measure carriers receive no competing norm instance.

Right convolution on a monoid is (μ⋆ν)(f) = μ(x ↦ ν(y ↦ f(xy))). For the additive group of Z, use `Multiplicative Z` and its canonical homeomorphism to Z when applying the existing convolution ring instance. On U the convolution is multiplicative. Inclusion U → Z induces a linear measure map, not an algebra map between those two convolutions.

The Amice transform has coefficients A_μ,n = μ(x ↦ binom(x,n)). Write Y = 1+T, so δ_a transforms to Y^a. The Mahler derivation is ∂ = (1+T)d/dT. The operator φ is substitution by (1+T)^p−1; ψ is its measure-theoretic left inverse, with ψφ = 1 and φψ = restriction to pZ. Hence 1−φψ is restriction to U. Formal substitution at a zero-constant series and topological evaluation at a nonzero topologically nilpotent element are different APIs.

For module structure, O is a complete DVR with uniformizer ϖ and finite residue field k, and Λ = O[[T]]. These algebraic results do not require mixed characteristic. For character spaces and the mixed-characteristic growth formula, O is specifically the valuation ring of a finite extension K/ℚ_p, with its natural norm and embedding into C_p. Write #k = p^f and e = v_ϖ(p). Statements specialized to O = Z say so.

A pseudo-measure is an element λ of the total quotient ring Q(R), localized at the non-zero-divisors, such that ([g]−1)λ belongs to R for every g. R may have zero divisors. Evaluation uses an integral cleared numerator and a clearing factor whose character image is a unit in the receiving ring. It does not extend a character to the whole total quotient ring.

For finitely generated modules, pseudo-null means zero localization at all primes of height at most one. Over Λ this is equivalent to finite cardinality. A pseudo-isomorphism is a map with pseudo-null kernel and cokernel; existence of a reverse map is asserted for torsion modules, not for all finitely generated modules. Elementary divisors retain the multiset of pairs (prime, exponent), not just the sum of exponents.

Implement L0, the finite-coordinate and comparison foundations of L1, L2, and L3 first. L4's coefficient-ring and generic module theory then supplies its invariants. Character-space targets retain the identifiers L0a but are placed after their L1–L4 prerequisites. L5 and L6 consume this algebra. Within L2, Cartier extraction precedes residue ψ. Basic comparison theorems do not depend on their later Amice-coordinate compatibility statements.

Each named target includes its API, naturality/transport laws and tests. Supporting-lemma identifiers refer to the explicit mathematical signatures in the companion prototype; where a target below supplies additional generality or a stronger endpoint, that statement is required in addition to the prototype's specialization. A prototype's absence is not permission to assume an endpoint. Sectional source locators apply to their targets unless another locator is given. API identifiers are the named declarations; numeric `-api-N` identifiers used below are fixed aliases, not numbers to recompute when an API list changes.

## L0 — Continuous functions, clopen restriction and dual topologies

Standing assumptions: X and Y are compact spaces, s is a clopen subset of X, and R is a normed commutative ring. Strong operator-norm statements use a nontrivially normed field K. Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), Definitions 3.5, 3.7–3.8, Remark 3.31, Corollary 3.32 and Remark 3.33, pp. 119, 127–129. Inputs: continuous maps, their restriction API, `AbstractMeasure.map`, `toCLMEquiv`, weak and strong topologies.

<a id="L0-clopen-zero-extension"></a>**Clopen zero extension.** Construct z_s = `ContinuousMap.zeroExtendClopen s R : C(s,R) →L[R] C(X,R)`, equal to f on s and zero off s. Require `zeroExtendClopen_apply_mem`, `zeroExtendClopen_apply_not_mem`, `norm_zeroExtendClopen`, `restrict_zeroExtendClopen` and `zeroExtendClopen_restrict`: restriction is a left inverse, and extension after restriction multiplies by 1_s. The norm equality includes the empty subset. Linearity is inherited from the bundled map. On X = Fin 2, s = {0}, extending constant 7 gives 7 at 0 and 0 at 1; extension from the empty subset is zero. Supporting lemmas: `clopen-zero-extension-inside`, `clopen-zero-extension-outside`, `clopen-zero-extension-norm`, `clopen-zero-extension-restriction`, `clopen-zero-extension-projector`.

<a id="L0-clopen-restriction"></a>**Measure restriction.** Define r_s = `AbstractMeasure.restrictClopen s R : D(X,R) →ₗ[R] D(s,R)` by r_s(μ)(f) = μ(z_s f). Let j_s be the existing inclusion pushforward. Require `restrictClopen_apply`, `restrictClopen_map_subtype`, the inside/outside Dirac formulas and `restrictClopen_map_preimage`. In particular r_s j_s = id. Restriction of 2δ_0−3δ_1 to {0} is 2δ_0; δ_1 restricts to zero; restriction to the empty subset is zero. Supporting lemmas: `clopen-restriction-evaluation`, `clopen-restriction-section`, `clopen-projector-evaluation`.

<a id="L0-clopen-support-characterization"></a>**Support characterization.** There exists a unique ν on s with j_sν = μ exactly when μ annihilates every continuous function vanishing on s; then ν = r_sμ. Prove the complementary decomposition μ = j_s r_sμ + j_(sᶜ) r_(sᶜ)μ (`clopen-complement-decomposition`).

<a id="L0-clopen-decomposition-equivalence"></a>**Complementary decomposition.** Construct `clopenDecomposition s R : D(X,R) ≃ₗ[R] D(s,R) × D(sᶜ,R)`, with forward map the restrictions and inverse the sum of inclusion pushforwards. Require both evaluation formulas and both inverse identities. On Fin 2 the components of 2δ_0−3δ_1 are (2δ_0,−3δ_1); recombining unit atoms gives δ_0+δ_1; the empty-subset case is the identity after the canonical subtype transports. Extend this to every finite clopen partition, with refinement compatibility. Supporting lemma: `clopen-restriction-pushforward`.

<a id="L0-clopen-inclusion-weak-closed-embedding"></a>**Weak inclusion.** j_s is a closed embedding for weak topologies; its range is the annihilator subspace in the support characterization. Prove `pushforward-weak-continuous` and `clopen-restriction-weak-continuous` first.

<a id="L0-clopen-decomposition-weak-homeomorphism"></a>**Weak decomposition.** The complementary, and finite-partition, linear equivalences are homeomorphisms for weak/product topologies.

<a id="L0-clopen-inclusion-strong-closed-embedding"></a>**Strong inclusion.** For field coefficients j_s is an isometry on continuous duals and a strong closed embedding. Supporting lemmas: `pushforward-operator-norm-bound`, `clopen-restriction-operator-norm-bound`, `clopen-inclusion-operator-norm`.

<a id="L0-clopen-decomposition-strong-homeomorphism"></a>**Strong decomposition.** The complementary and finite-partition decompositions are strong homeomorphisms.

<a id="L0-measure-unit-ball-not-norm-compact"></a>**The infinite-domain unit ball is not norm compact.** For X infinite, compact and totally separated, and K a nontrivially normed ultrametric field, the operator-norm unit ball in C(X,K) →L[K] K is not compact. No completeness or properness of K is needed. Prove `dirac-weak-continuous`, `dirac-operator-norm` and `dirac-difference-operator-norm`: distinct Dirac measures have difference norm 1. Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), Example 3.10 and Remark 3.28(3), pp. 120, 125–126. Also retain `rational-test-integral-scaling`: on compact X every ℚ_p-valued test becomes Z-valued after multiplication by one power of p.

**Reduction and local constancy.** Retain `residue-map-locally-constant`, using `PadicInt.ker_toZMod`, `norm_lt_one_iff_dvd` and `maximalIdeal_eq_span_p`. Source: [RJW](#ref-rjw), Lemma 12.13, pp. 182–183, together with the existing reduction kernel.

## L1 — Finite coordinates and completed group algebras

Finite-coordinate constructions use a topological commutative ring R and finite discrete spaces A,B. Separation adds compactness, Hausdorffness and total disconnectedness of X and a normed R. Algebra projections require the quotient map to be a continuous monoid homomorphism. Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), §§3.2–3.3, Propositions 3.15–3.16, Definition 3.17, Remark 3.18 and Example 3.19, pp. 119–123.

### Finite coefficients

<a id="L1-finite-projection"></a>**Finite projection.** For q : C(X,A), define π_q : D(X,R) →ₗ[R] (A →₀ R) by π_q(μ)(a) = μ(1_(q⁻¹{a})). Require the coefficient formula, linearity, pairing with every A → R, Dirac formula, refinement by `Finsupp.mapDomain`, and total-mass formula. The map q need not be surjective. Tests: π_idδ_0 is the atom at 0; collapsing 2δ_0+3δ_1 to a point gives 5; an empty fibre has coefficient zero. Supporting lemmas: `finite-projection-coefficient`, `finite-projection-pairing`, `finite-projection-dirac`, `finite-projection-refinement`, `finite-projection-mass`, `finite-discrete-reconstruction`.

<a id="L1-finite-discrete-coefficient-bijection"></a>**Finite discrete reconstruction.** π_id is a linear bijection, with inverse c ↦ Σ_a c(a)δ_a. Require both round trips. Prove `finite-projection-weak-continuity` on coefficients.

<a id="L1-finite-projections-separate"></a>**Separation.** On compact Hausdorff totally disconnected X, equality of π_q for every continuous q : X → Fin n implies equality of measures. Derive this from density of locally constant test functions; no completeness of R is needed.

<a id="L1-joint-finite-projection"></a>**Joint coordinates.** For R = Z define π_(r,q) by reducing π_q modulo p^r, as an additive map. Require precision change, quotient refinement, Dirac and zero-precision formulas. Tests at p = 2: a unit atom remains 1 modulo 4; precision 0 gives zero; 2δ projects to zero modulo 2 but not modulo 4. Supporting lemmas: `joint-finite-coefficient`, `joint-finite-precision`, `joint-finite-refinement`, `joint-finite-dirac`.

<a id="L1-joint-finite-separation"></a>**Joint separation.** All joint coordinates determine an integral measure. Use finite-coordinate separation and separation of p-adic integers by their reductions.

### Convolution and algebra projections

<a id="L1-right-convolution-function"></a><a id="L1-convolution-product"></a><a id="L1-convolution-algebra"></a><a id="L1-dirac-hom"></a>**Existing convolution inputs.** Use Mathlib `convolveFunRight`, `mul_def`, `mul_apply`, `dirac_mul_dirac`, `one_def`, `one_apply`, the ring/algebra and commutative instances, and `diracHom`. These identifiers name consumed inputs, not new definitions. Extend the API by the coefficient algebra-map formula, functoriality under continuous monoid pushforward, total mass, and the finite Dirac expansion. Supporting identifiers: `convolution-evaluation`, `right-convolution-evaluation`, `commutative-convolution`, `convolution-pushforward`, `convolution-total-mass`, `finite-convolution-multiplicativity`.

<a id="L1-finite-projection-algebra-map"></a>**Finite algebra projection.** Bundle Π_q(μ) = `MonoidAlgebra.ofCoeff (π_q μ)` as an R-algebra map. Require `finiteProjectionAlgHom_apply`, coefficient and Dirac formulas. Tests distinguish atom locations, δ_x⋆δ_y ↦ [q(x)q(y)], and coefficient sum 5 under the trivial quotient.

<a id="L1-finite-discrete-algebra-equivalence"></a>**Finite measure algebra.** Π_id is an algebra equivalence D(A,R) ≃ₐ[R] R[A], inverse Σ_a c.coeff(a)δ_a. Require the inverse-on-single formula. Tests: δ_a ↦ [a]; the inverse of 2[a]+3[b] has mass 5 for a ≠ b; [a,r][b,s] corresponds to rsδ_(ab).

<a id="L1-joint-projection-ring-map"></a>**Finite-precision ring projection.** Bundle the coefficient reduction of Π_q as a unital ring map to (ZMod(p^r))[A], with application, Dirac, product and zero-precision formulas. Precision 0 is the unital map to the zero ring, not an exception.

### General profinite reconstruction and comparison

<a id="L1-profinite-coordinate-reconstruction"></a>**Reconstruction on a profinite group.** Let Γ be a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected topological group. From coefficients c_U ∈ Z[Γ/U] compatible under quotient pushforward for every open normal U, construct an integral measure by first integrating locally constant functions on finite quotients and then extending continuously. Require independence of quotient, the bound |I(f)| ≤ ‖f‖, recovery of every coefficient and uniqueness. Prove the weak topology is exactly the subspace topology on the compatible families in the product of finite-level coefficient spaces; the compatibility locus is closed. This construction has no countability hypothesis. The unit-tower construction below is its specialization, with its explicit finite-level API retained.

<a id="L1-measure-completed-algebra-equiv"></a>**Measures are the canonical completed group algebra.** Construct `toCompletedGroupAlgebra : D(Γ,Z) →ₐ[Z] TauCeti.completedGroupAlgebra Z Γ` with its U-projection exactly Π_(Γ→Γ/U). Prove its Dirac formula, injectivity, surjectivity, and weak closed-embedding/homeomorphism statements, and weak compactness of D(Γ,Z). Bundle the resulting algebra equivalence. Use the existing `proj`, `of`, `mk`, `lift` and `ext`; do not define another limit-ring carrier. Naturality in continuous group homomorphisms is required. Tests: δ_x⋆δ_y projects to [x̄ȳ], distinct points give distinct images, and δ_1 maps to 1. Prerequisites: finite projection, its multiplicativity, general profinite reconstruction and the existing completed algebra. The later coordinate/Amice comparison is not a prerequisite.

<a id="L1-completed-algebra-coefficients"></a>**Finite coefficient extension.** Let O be the integers of a finite extension of ℚ_p with its natural norm and finite free Z-module structure. Construct O ⊗_Z D(Γ,Z) ≃ₗ[O] D(Γ,O), sending a⊗δ_x to aδ_x and compatible with convolution, finite projections and pushforward. A finite free basis and its continuous coordinate functionals give the construction; prove basis independence. Identify O ⊗_Z Z[[Γ]] with the existing `completedGroupAlgebra O Γ`, not a new carrier. The finite-free topology is basis independent and the algebra is complete. Inverting p gives bounded K-valued measures; it does not give all of lim_U K[Γ/U]. Tests: O = Z recovers the identity; 1⊗δ_x maps to δ_x; distinct coefficients on δ_x remain distinct. General normed-algebra variants require a bounded continuous Z-action, completeness, ultrametricity and the stated finite-free topological comparison, not an arbitrary algebra structure with an unrelated norm.

Supporting identifiers: `unit-reduction-refinement`, `integer-reduction-norm`; the continuity, surjectivity and kernel identifiers refer to the library declarations, not restatements.

<a id="L1-local-field-integers-comparison"></a>**Existing local-field comparison.** Use `Padic.integerRing_eq_subring` and `integerRingEquiv`.

<a id="L1-unit-reduction-filtration"></a>**Filtration comparison.** Under U → ℚ_pˣ, ker red_n is the pullback of `TauCeti.unitFiltration ℚ_p n`, for every n ≥ 0.

<a id="L1-unit-reduction-cofinality"></a>**Cofinality.** Every open subgroup of U contains ker red_n for some n. Also prove `unit-reduction-separation`.

<a id="L1-unit-reduction-quotient"></a>**Modular-unit quotient.** Construct U/ker(red_n) ≃* A_n using `quotientKerEquivOfSurjective`. Require its representative, inverse-representative and equality-with-native-construction formulas. Tests: n = 0 is trivial; the class of 2 at p = 3,n = 2 maps to 2 modulo 9; −1 and 1 differ at p = 2,n = 2.

<a id="L1-unit-reduction-discrete-factorization"></a>**Discrete homomorphism descent.** Every continuous homomorphism U → A into a discrete group factors through some red_n. <a id="L1-unit-locally-constant-factor"></a>**Locally constant test descent.** Every locally constant U → A, for arbitrary A, factors through red_n. Require the converse, fixed-level uniqueness, refinement, continuous-discrete version, depth-zero criterion and density of these cylinder functions among continuous normed tests. Supporting lemmas: `unit-locally-constant-characterization`, `unit-test-factor-uniqueness`, `unit-test-factor-refinement`, `unit-continuous-discrete-factor`, `unit-test-zero-factor`, `unit-cylinder-density`. <a id="L1-unit-measure-separation"></a><a id="L1-unit-joint-measure-separation"></a><a id="L1-unit-diagonal-measure-separation"></a>**Unit-coordinate separation.** The coordinates at all red_n determine a normed-ring-valued measure. For integral measures all joint coordinates suffice, and already the diagonal coordinates (precision n, depth n) suffice. Neither shallow axis alone determines a measure.

<a id="L1-unit-coordinate-integral"></a>**Unit-coordinate integration.** For compatible c_n : A_n →₀ Z define `unitCoordinateIntegral` on locally constant tests by Σ_a g(a)c_n(a) for any f = g∘red_n. Require independence, linearity, the uniform bound and the factor formula. Supporting lemmas: `unit-coordinate-pairing-refinement`, `unit-coordinate-pairing-independent`, `unit-coordinate-integral-factor`, `unit-coordinate-integral-bound`. Tests: coefficients at 1 and 2 with masses 2 and 3 give mass 5; a constant test a gives a c_0(1); compatible single atoms give a f(u).

<a id="L1-unit-coordinate-measure"></a><a id="L1-unit-coordinate-existence-uniqueness"></a>**The measure of unit coordinates.** Construct `ofUnitCoordinates` as the unique continuous extension, agree with general profinite reconstruction, and require recovery, compatibility, reconstruction, mass, zero/add/scalar and Dirac laws. Supporting lemmas: `unit-coordinate-measure-locally-constant`, `unit-coordinate-measure-bound`, `unit-coordinate-recovery`, `unit-coordinate-compatibility`, `unit-coordinate-reconstruction`. Tests retain sums of two atoms, arbitrary scalar Dirac measures and colliding reductions without averaging.

<a id="L1-unit-coordinates-range"></a><a id="L1-unit-coordinates-weak-closed-embedding"></a><a id="L1-unit-measures-weak-compact"></a>**Weak unit-coordinate topology.** The image is exactly the compatible families, a closed subset of ∏_n(A_n → Z). The coordinate map is a weak closed embedding and the measure space is compact. Require continuity of the inverse on families (`unit-coordinate-inverse-continuous`).

<a id="L1-unit-joint-coordinates-weak-closed-embedding"></a><a id="L1-unit-joint-weak-convergence"></a>**Joint topology and convergence.** Joint coordinates give a weak closed embedding into the product of discrete coefficient rings. Along any filter, weak convergence is equivalent to eventual equality of each finite joint coordinate. Require `unit-joint-zero-basis`, `unit-diagonal-zero-basis`, `unit-measures-weak-topological-ring` and `unit-measures-weak-linear-topology`. <a id="L1-finite-quotient-tower"></a>**Finite quotient towers.** A tower consists of continuous surjections q_n : Γ → A_n to finite discrete groups, transition homomorphisms compatible with the q_n and kernels separating points. Prove transition composition and cofinality of the kernels for compact Γ. Construct `unitTower` and `padicTower` and compare their compatible-family descriptions with the canonical completed algebra. A constant identity tower on a finite group is a test; trivial quotients of a nontrivial group fail separation. Tower-indexed compactness is a specialization of the general comparison.

<a id="L1-character-integral-algebra-hom"></a>**Character integration.** For a continuous monoid homomorphism κ : G → R, evaluation μ ↦ μ(κ) is an R-algebra map. Require its application, Dirac, trivial-character/total-mass and composition-with-`diracHom` formulas. Tests over ℤ on C₂: the trivial character gives mass 2 on δ_1+δ_g; the sign character gives 0 on δ_1+δ_g and 2 on δ_1−δ_g; cδ_g evaluates to cκ(g). Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), §3.6, pp. 129–131.

## L2 — Mahler–Amice theory and integral operators

On integral power series B = Z[[T]] use the coefficientwise p-adic topology, not a sup-norm topology. For field coefficients the Amice image consists of bounded coefficient sequences, not all formal series. Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), Theorem 3.25 and §§3.5.1–3.5.5, pp. 124–129, with the additional locators below.

### Weighting, moments and formal calculus

<a id="L2-weight"></a>**Weighting.** Define `AbstractMeasure.weight g : D(X,R) →ₗ[R] D(X,R)` by (weight g μ)(f) = μ(gf). Require application, one, zero, multiplication, constants, Dirac, pushforward compatibility and iteration. Tests over ℤ₃: weighting δ_0 by x is zero although δ_0 ≠ 0; δ_1 is unchanged; δ_2 becomes 2δ_2. Supporting lemmas: `weight-evaluation`, `weight-multiplication`, `weight-pushforward`, `weight-iteration`.

<a id="L2-mahler-derivation"></a>**Mahler derivation.** Define ∂ = `(1+X) • PowerSeries.derivative` as a derivation on R[[T]], for any commutative ring R. Require application, constants, variable, Leibniz, coefficient-map and iterated coefficient-map laws, and coeff_n ∂F = (n+1)coeff_(n+1)F + n coeff_n F. Tests: ∂7 = 0, ∂T = 1+T, ∂(1+T)^2 = 2(1+T)^2. Supporting lemmas: `mahler-derivation-value`, `mahler-derivation-coefficients`, `mahler-derivation-map`, `mahler-derivation-iterate-map`, `mahler-recurrence`.

<a id="L2-amice-weight"></a><a id="L2-ordinary-moment"></a>**Moments.** Prove A(weight x μ) = ∂Aμ and its iterates, and μ(x^k) = constantCoeff(∂^k Aμ), including k = 0. Supporting lemma: `amice-iterate-weight`.

<a id="L2-ordinary-moment-exp"></a>**Exponential comparison.** Over a ℚ-algebra, formal substitution T ↦ exp(T)−1 conjugates ∂ to d/dT. Require `exp-conjugacy`, `exp-iterate`, `exp-coefficient`, and the integral-measure formula after mapping coefficients to ℚ_p: μ(x^k) = k! coeff_k(Aμ(exp(T)−1)). No rational algebra structure is imposed on Z.

<a id="L2-algebra-amice-weight"></a><a id="L2-algebra-ordinary-moment"></a><a id="L2-algebra-ordinary-moment-exp"></a>**Coefficient-algebra moments.** Extend the recurrence, weighting and moment formulas to a normed commutative Z-algebra R with continuous scalar action, using the image of the Mahler tests. ### Coefficient rings and the canonical coordinate

<a id="L4-weierstrass-adapter"></a>**Weierstrass and quotient adapters.** Use Mathlib's preparation theorem and uniqueness. <a id="L4-iwasawa-coordinate"></a>**The O-coordinate.** Let O be a commutative noetherian local ring, complete and separated for its maximal ideal, with residue characteristic p. ### Pseudo-nullity, reflexive hulls and elementary factors

<a id="L4-pseudo-null"></a>**Pseudo-nullity.** Define `IsPseudoNull A M` by finite generation and vanishing at primes of height ≤ 1. <a id="L4-pseudo-isomorphism"></a>**Pseudo-isomorphisms.** Define the predicate on a linear map by pseudo-null kernel and cokernel. <a id="L4-projective-dimension-and-resolution"></a>**Minimal resolutions.** For finite Λ-module M put H₀ = M/TM and H₁ = M[T]. Construct a minimal exact free resolution 0 → Λ^d₂ → Λ^d₁ → Λ^d₀ → M → 0, with entries of the differentials in 𝔪_Λ, where

d₀ = dim_k(H₀/ϖH₀),

Tests: M = Λ gives (1,0,0) over every O; M = k gives (1,2,1); over a ramified or unramified extension the free rank is still 1, not dim_𝔽p(O/pO). <a id="L4-delta-submodule"></a>**δ-submodule.** Over Z[[T]] define M_δ = ⋃_n ker ω_n, with membership, map, zero, equivalence and monotonicity laws. Prove it is finite over Z for finite M (`delta-finite-control`). Tests: zero and free modules have δ = 0; Λ/(T) has δ = M.

**Cyclotomic and exact-sequence inputs.** Define ω_n and ξ₀ = T, ξ_(n+1) = Σ_(i<p)(1+T)^(i p^n); thus ∏_(j≤n)ξ_j = ω_n and ω_(−1) = 1 is the empty product. These targets follow L1–L4 despite their retained L0a identifiers. O is the integers of a finite extension K/ℚ_p with a fixed valued embedding into C_p. <a id="L0a-character-space"></a>**Continuous unit characters.** Define W(A) = Hom_cts(U,Aˣ), on `ContinuousMonoidHom`, for complete nonarchimedean coefficient algebras with their continuous coefficient maps. <a id="L0a-principal-unit-disc"></a>**The principal disc.** For A = C_p and a chosen topological generator u of the principal units, evaluation at u identifies their continuous characters with {z : ‖z−1‖ < 1}. <a id="L0a-bounded-functions"></a>**Functions of measures.** For μ ∈ D(U,O), define F_μ(χ) by L2's O → C_p extension and integration. Require the bound |F_μ(χ)| ≤ ‖μ_K‖, convolution multiplicativity, Dirac evaluation, additivity, scalar compatibility and injectivity. On the disc of a torsion character η, form the η-weighted sum of the clopen coset restrictions, transport the principal-unit coordinate to Z and evaluate its Amice transform at t. Tests: δ_a gives χ(a); δ_1 gives 1; δ_a−δ_b has zero mass but is detected by x ↦ x; |χ(a)| = 1 gives the Dirac bound. <a id="L0a-meromorphic-functions"></a>**Functions of pseudo-measures in one variable.** For λ a pseudo-measure and χ ≠ 1, choose any g ∈ U with χ(g) ≠ 1 and set F_λ(χ) = F_(([g]−1)λ)(χ)/(χ(g)−1). For a fixed principal generator a, the expression with denominator χ(a)−1 is used only on its nonvanishing locus. At the nontrivial torsion characters it has removable singularities, proved by changing the clearing element. <a id="L0a-universal-character"></a>**Universal characters.** On the canonical Λ_O(Γ) for Γ ≅ Z, lift `completedGroupAlgebra.of` to Γ → Λ_O(Γ)ˣ. ## L5 — Graded determinants, specialization and compact exactness

### Finite-projective determinant foundation

<a id="L5-graded-line-foundation"></a>**Graded invertible modules.** Use the common invertible-module/finite-projective carrier shared with AlgebraicVectorBundles. A graded line is an invertible A-module with a locally constant parity on Spec A. Its tensor symmetry is multiplication by (−1)^(ab) on parity a,b. Construct tensor, unit, inverse, evaluation, base change and their coherence. For a finite projective module whose rank varies on Spec A, construct its determinant by its clopen rank loci, and give compatibility with the affine finite-locally-free determinant and exterior powers. No constant global rank is silently assumed. Tests: det(A) has odd parity; det(A²) has even parity; swapping the two summands of A⊕A induces −1 on the determinant over ℤ. Sources: [KM](#ref-km), I, Definition 1 and Theorem 1, pp. 23–25; [Stacks](#ref-stacks), determinant constructions and Tag 0FJI.

<a id="L5-determinant-line"></a>**Determinant of a perfect complex.** A strict perfect complex is a bounded cochain complex of finite projective A-modules, on `HomologicalComplex` over `ModuleCat A`. Define det_A(P) = ⊗_i det_A(P^i)^((-1)^i), with parity Σ_i rank(P^i) mod 2. Require functoriality on isomorphisms, canonical quasi-isomorphism invariance with composition, homotopy compatibility, degreewise-split short-exact multiplicativity with associativity and symmetry coherence, arbitrary coefficient base change, duality and shift. Acyclic perfect complexes have a canonical trivialization to the unit line. The inverse line has the same parity modulo two; shifting P negates its integer Euler rank and leaves this parity unchanged.

Pin the two-term convention: P = [A --a--> A] in degrees −1,0 has determinant Hom(det A,det A). Its canonical section is a; after inverting a the trivialization det(P) → A[1/a] sends 1 to a⁻¹. Require both directions of this trivialization explicitly. Tests: A[0] has underlying line A and odd parity; the two-term complex has even parity and the displayed rational trivialization; a finite projective rank-r module in degree 0 has top exterior power and parity r mod 2. Sources: [KM](#ref-km), I, Definition 1 and Theorem 1; [Stacks](#ref-stacks), [Tag 0FJI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0FJI).

<a id="L5-rational-trivialization"></a>**Characteristic lattice of the determinant.** For Λ = O[[T]] and perfect P with finitely generated torsion cohomology, tensoring with Q(Λ) is acyclic. Under its canonical trivialization det(P)⊗Q(Λ) → Q(Λ), prove

image(det(P) → Q(Λ)) = ∏_i char_Λ(H^i(P))^((-1)^(i+1))

as fractional ideals. Negative exponents use invertible fractional ideals over the UFD; do not identify them with integral ideals. The proof localizes at height-one DVRs, computes the two-term elementary complexes there and uses the determinant exactness and reflexive ideal comparison. Tests: Λ/(a) in degree 0 gives a⁻¹Λ, the shift gives aΛ, and finite Λ-modules give the unit fractional ideal. This is compatible with the pinned section/trivialization distinction. Sources: [KM](#ref-km), Theorem 1; [RJW](#ref-rjw), Definition 13.3 and Lemma 13.6; the local-length computation is an explicit intermediate target.

<a id="L5-specialization"></a>**Derived specialization and Tor.** For perfect P and A′ = Λ/(f), require the canonical base-change isomorphism det_(A′)(P⊗_Λ A′) ≃ det_Λ(P)⊗_Λ A′. The tensor complex of projectives computes the derived tensor, including when f = 0. For the following two-term universal-coefficient sequence assume f ≠ 0 (hence regular in Λ):

0 → H^i(P)/fH^i(P) → H^i(P⊗_Λ A′) → H^(i+1)(P)[f] → 0.

Construct the maps naturally in P, identify H^(i+1)(P)[f] with Tor₁^Λ(H^(i+1)(P),A′), and prove the sequence. If f is injective on all cohomology modules, the Tor terms vanish. Over an arbitrary quotient A′, individual cohomology and Tor modules need not be perfect; their ordinary module determinants are not used without that additional hypothesis. For an unconditional factorization, use perfect Λ-resolutions of H^i(P) and their derived specializations, together with the determinant filtration isomorphism. If the individual A′-modules are perfect, derive the corresponding alternating determinant formula as a separate corollary.

Tests: P = [Λ --T--> Λ] in degrees −1,0 and f = T gives H⁻¹ = H⁰ = O, with total even determinant parity; the naive quotient O alone has odd parity although its underlying line is also O. With f = T², both specialized cohomology modules are O of infinite projective dimension over Λ/(T²), while the specialized two-term complex remains perfect; require this rejection test using its periodic resolution. A finite Λ-module has unit characteristic ideal but can have nonzero M/TM, so finite error terms cannot simply be dropped under specialization. Sources: [KM](#ref-km), Definition 1(iii); [Stacks](#ref-stacks), Tag 0FJI; the universal-coefficient sequence follows from the explicit free resolution [Λ --f--> Λ] of A′ and is itself a target.

### Compact modules and Nakayama

<a id="L5-compact-limit-exact"></a>**Existing compact-limit exactness.** Use `TauCeti.exists_forall_map_succ_eq_and_forall_eq_of_surjective` and the directed compact T₂ lifting theorem for compatible levelwise surjections. On compact Hausdorff modules with continuous maps, the inverse limit of a levelwise short exact sequence is short exact. Supply the linearity and closed-embedding packaging if not already present, on the same module carriers; do not require transition surjectivity where the compact lifting theorem does not. Sources: [RJW](#ref-rjw), Proposition 13.13, p. 193, and the existing Tau Ceti inverse-system theorems.

<a id="L5-topological-nakayama"></a>**Topological Nakayama.** For the compact local ring Λ = O[[T]] with finite residue field, a compact Hausdorff topological Λ-module M with continuous scalar action is zero if M/𝔪M is zero, and is finitely generated if M/𝔪M is finite, generated by lifts of a residue-field basis. A continuous map of compact Hausdorff Λ-modules is surjective if its reduction modulo 𝔪 is surjective. Prove the closedness of the finite generated image, passage to its compact quotient, and the zero criterion; do not assume closedness of an arbitrary submodule. The more general profinite-local-ring version requires an open maximal ideal, finite residue field and a cofinal system of open ideals, with their actions on finite continuous module quotients made explicit. Tests: Λ is generated by one element; ∏_n k has infinite residue quotient and is not finitely generated; discrete ℚ_p/Z is the noncompact counterexample to the zero criterion. Uses: compact lifting, the maximal-ideal calculation and algebraic Nakayama on finite quotients.

## L6 — Coefficient orders, presentations and exact duality

The arithmetic setting is a finite abelian group G, O the integers of a finite extension K/ℚ_p containing its character values, and G = G_p×G′ with #G′ prime to p. The cited Brumer–Stark application uses odd p; generic ring, matrix and duality statements retain only their actual hypotheses. Characters are G →* Oˣ. Character orders are images in products of O, not those products themselves. Fitting ideals at every index are the existing `TauCeti.fittingIdeal`.

Sources: [DK](#ref-dk), §2.2, Lemmas 2.2–2.7, pp. 15–18; Lemma 3.9, pp. 25–26; §6.1, pp. 40–41; Appendix A.4 and B.2, pp. 86, 93ff. Existing transpose uniqueness and stable-category infrastructure are not rebuilt.

### Character orders

<a id="L6-character-evaluation"></a>**Evaluation.** Use `TauCeti.DiagonalizableGroup.point ψ` as ev_ψ : O[G] →ₐ[O] O; `charEval` is only an abbreviation. Require agreement on group elements and scalar atoms, trivial-character augmentation, row orthogonality, comparison with `MonoidAlgebra.lift` and the action on `Representation.ofLinearCharacter`. Construct `jointEval Ψ` by taking all ψ-coordinates. For finite G and a domain with enough roots, prove joint injectivity by column orthogonality (`joint-evaluation-injective`). Tests: ev_ψ(1±h) for ψ(h) = −1; the trivial-group bijection; a single character is not injective for nontrivial G and nonzero O.

<a id="L6-character-group-ring"></a>**Character group ring.** Define R_Ψ = range(jointEval Ψ) as an O-subalgebra of ∏_(ψ∈Ψ)O, with its canonical surjection α_Ψ. Require kernel and coordinate formulas, extensionality, coordinate evaluation and surjectivity, quotient universal property, quotient-kernel equivalence, restriction maps for inclusions Ψ ⊆ Ψ′, their identity/composition/surjectivity, and injectivity into two subfamilies whose union is Ψ. Require R_∅ = 0, R_{1} ≃ O and R_Ĝ ≃ O[G] under the appropriate injectivity hypotheses. No Gorenstein hypothesis is built into this definition.

Tests: when G = C_p and p is not a unit, the individual coordinate idempotents of O^Ĝ do not belong to R_Ĝ; for G = C_p×C_p and Ψ = {1,ψ₁,ψ₂} with the two coordinate primitive-root characters, R_Ψ is the triples congruent modulo ζ_p−1, and its reduction has a two-dimensional square-zero maximal ideal/socle, so it is not Gorenstein; the empty family is the zero ring. The non-Gorenstein conclusion uses the DVR/canonical-module criterion below, not an untyped predicate.

<a id="L6-character-group-ring-lattice"></a>**Lattice and regular elements.** Prove the scaled-idempotent formula #G·δ_ψ ∈ R_Ψ and #G·O^Ψ ⊆ R_Ψ. For a PID O, finite G and #G ≠ 0, R_Ψ is finite free of rank #Ψ. If O/(#G) is finite, its index in O^Ψ is finite. For x ∈ R_Ψ, regularity is equivalent to nonzero values in every coordinate, and to regularity in the product. Require the multiplication-induced lattice quotient comparison. Supporting lemmas: `character-group-ring-scaled-idempotent`, `character-group-ring-finite-index`, `character-group-ring-nonzerodivisor`.

<a id="L6-norm-element-kernel"></a>**Subgroup norms.** For I ≤ G, let N_I be the existing `subgroupCharSum` of the trivial character. The kernel for Ψ = {ψ | ψ|_I ≠ 1} is (N_I), yielding O[G]/(N_I) ≃ R_Ψ. The proof uses enough characters and their joint injectivity. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.2.

<a id="L6-component-character-group-ring"></a><a id="L6-component-group-ring-equiv"></a>**Prime-to-p components.** With χ a character of G′ and Ψ_χ = {ψ | ψ|_(G′) = χ}, show ker α_(Ψ_χ) = (1−e_χ). Construct the map π_χ : O[G] → O[G_p], g′g_p ↦ χ(g′)[g_p], and the induced component equivalence, with the scalar-atom formula, kernel, surjectivity, inverse, uniqueness, character-evaluation compatibility and trivial-factor cases. The twist is χ, not χ⁻¹. Require the component product decomposition and the norm quotient R_χ/(N_I) ≃ R_Ψ for Ψ = {ψ ∈ Ψ_χ | ψ|_I ≠ 1}. Supporting lemmas: `character-idempotent-evaluation`, `group-ring-component-decomposition`, `component-norm-quotient`. Tests include the sign component, both trivial factors and a non-real character distinguishing χ from χ⁻¹. Sources: [DK](#ref-dk), Corollary 2.3.

<a id="L6-character-group-ring-local"></a><a id="L6-character-group-ring-adic-complete"></a>**Units, locality and completeness.** For finite Ψ, x is a unit iff every coordinate is a unit, equivalently their product is a unit. For nonempty Ψ in one component, unit status is detected by any one coordinate and R_Ψ is local with residue field k. Prove the local evaluation map, the maximal-ideal formula, and 𝔪_Ψ^N ⊆ 𝔪_O R_Ψ ⊆ 𝔪_Ψ for some N ≥ 1. If O is complete noetherian local and R_Ψ finite over O, it is complete noetherian local for this topology. Empty Ψ is excluded from local-ring assertions. Supporting lemmas: `character-group-ring-unit-criterion`, `character-group-ring-unit-one-character`, `character-group-ring-maximal-ideal-power`, `character-group-ring-eval-local-hom`, `character-group-ring-residue-field`.

<a id="L6-character-group-ring-index"></a>**Principal quotient order.** For regular x ∈ R_Ψ, prove finiteness and #R_Ψ/(x) = #O/(∏_ψψ(x)). Require the finite-quotient instance for a DVR with finite residue field, deduced from its powers-of-uniformizer filtration. The more general version assumes an infinite Dedekind domain with finite nonzero principal quotients. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.5.

<a id="L6-sharp-involution"></a>**Sharp.** Use the Hopf antipode g ↦ g⁻¹ on O[G], with scalar-atom, involutivity and ψ(x^#) = ψ⁻¹(x) formulas. It induces R_Ψ ≃ₐ[O] R_(Ψ⁻¹), not generally an endomorphism of R_Ψ. Require kernel transport, quotient and coordinate formulas, inverse, functoriality with restriction, and transport of ideals and principal ideals. Tests: a primitive cubic character changes to its inverse; Ψ = {ψ} need not be inversion stable; trivial G gives the identity.

### Lattice and finite-module duality

<a id="L6-canonical-module"></a>**Canonical module of a coefficient order.** Let R be a nonzero commutative O-algebra finite free over the DVR O, including nonempty character orders. Use ω_R = Hom_O(R,O) with action (rφ)(s) = φ(rs). Construct the adjunction Hom_R(M,ω_R) ≃ Hom_O(M,O), with evaluation at 1 forward and inverse f ↦ (m ↦ (r ↦ f(rm))); require linearity, naturality and both round trips. For character orders embed ω_R as the trace-dual lattice in R⊗_O K using the nondegenerate trace pairing of the finite étale generic fibre, and compare with the existing trace-dual API. A Gorenstein order is characterized by ω_R being invertible over R (free of rank one in the local case). Reuse any existing compatible Gorenstein predicate and prove this criterion rather than introducing an unrelated ring condition. Prove O[G] is Gorenstein by the perfect coefficient-of-1 pairing and pass to its idempotent components. Arbitrary character quotients need not be Gorenstein. Require the socle-dimension criterion over a local order and the triple-order rejection example above. Sources: the trace-dual order theory of GlobalNumberFields; the displayed coinduction adjunction and DVR lattice duality supply the generic proof route.

<a id="L6-lattice-duality"></a>**Exact lattice duality.** On R-modules finite free over O, D_ω(M) = Hom_R(M,ω_R) is an exact contravariant duality, with its evaluation bidual isomorphism. Exactness refers to short exact sequences whose three terms are O-lattices; prove it by the preceding adjunction and O-splitting. If R is Gorenstein, prove the corresponding exactness and reflexivity for Hom_R(M,R), using the invertible canonical module; a chosen identification ω_R ≃ R must be explicit when comparing the two functors. Require scalar-isomorphism and sharp transport to R_(Ψ⁻¹). Tests: R = O gives ordinary finite-free duality; R = O[G] gives the coefficient pairing; no exactness on all torsion modules is inferred from the lattice theorem.

<a id="L6-finite-module-duality"></a>**Finite-module duality.** Use K/O as the quotient of the coefficient field by its integral O-submodule. On finite O-torsion R-modules put D_fin(M) = Hom_O(M,K/O), equivalently Hom_R(M,E_R) with E_R = Hom_O(R,K/O) and its coinduced action. Construct the equivalence by the same explicit adjunction. Prove exactness, natural evaluation biduality, preservation of cardinality and O-length, and functorial sharp/contragredient action: g acts on a dual functional by precomposition with g⁻¹ when the action is transported by sharp. These statements do not require R Gorenstein. The R-linear dual Hom_R(M,R) is not a substitute: for R = O and M = O/ϖ it is zero while D_fin(M) has order #k. Require this test, D_fin(0) = 0 and the exact dual of 0 → O/ϖ → O/ϖ² → O/ϖ → 0. Prove the coinduced injective resolution 0 → ω_R → Hom_O(R,K) → E_R → 0; for finite torsion M it yields Ext¹_R(M,ω_R) ≃ D_fin(M). A chosen Gorenstein trivialization gives the R-dualizing specialization. All Ext and quotient objects use existing module/homological carriers.

### Quadratic presentations and Fitting ideals

<a id="L6-quadratic-presentation"></a>**Quadratic presentation.** A quadratic presentation is an explicit right-exact R^n → R^n → M → 0, with the relation matrix, surjective generator map and equality of its relation image with the kernel. No injectivity of the relation matrix is included in the definition. Require comparison with `Module.Presentation`, transport across module equivalence, zero and cyclic presentations, direct sums, base change and the defining matrix/generator formulas. Its determinant is Fitt⁰(M)'s generator (`fitting-quadratic`). Require the regular-determinant criterion for injectivity of the square matrix and its relation to projective dimension where the ring hypotheses justify it. Tests: R/(a) with its 1×1 matrix; the zero module with empty or identity presentation; different square matrices with the same cokernel give the same Fitting ideal.

<a id="L6-quadratic-cardinality"></a>**Quadratic cardinality.** For a finite-index subring B of a finite product of PIDs, a quadratically presented B-module with Fitt⁰ = (x), x regular and B/(x) finite, is finite of order #B/(x). Include the source's possible finite-field factors; do not assume x is regular in the larger product without proving it or removing the appropriate finite ideal. Supporting lemmas: `pid-cokernel-cardinality`, `finite-index-cokernel-descent`, `cokernel-modulo-finite-ideal`, `finite-index-subring-nonzerodivisor`. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.4.

**Higher Fitting inputs and extensions.** All indices remain `TauCeti.fittingIdeal R M i`. Use the existing minors, chain, base-change and free-summand formulas. In the shifted formula Fitt^i(M⊕R^r), state the range i ≥ r or the convention for negative indices explicitly; when i < r the ideal is zero, not Fitt⁰(M) obtained by truncated natural subtraction. Retain the additional higher-Fitting API in the companion signatures on this carrier, including localization and rank/generator criteria with their hypotheses.

<a id="L6-quadratic-presentation-extension"></a>**Extensions of quadratic presentations.** For 0 → A → B → C → 0 with A,C quadratically presented, construct B's block presentation [[ψ_A,−X],[0,φ_C]] from chosen lifts of generators and their lifted relations. Require its size, exactness, defining generator formula and determinant det ψ_A det φ_C. Prove `extension-relation-matrix`. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.6.

<a id="L6-fitting-extension"></a>**Fitting multiplicativity with quadratic quotient.** If A is finitely presented and C quadratically presented in 0 → A → B → C → 0, prove B finitely presented and Fitt⁰(B) = Fitt⁰(A)Fitt⁰(C). Keep the explicit finiteness hypotheses over a nonnoetherian R. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.6.

<a id="L6-fitting-fibre-product"></a>**Common-quotient comparison.** For exact sequences with common quotient C and quadratically presented B,B′, with finitely presented kernels A,A′, prove Fitt⁰(A)Fitt⁰(B′) = Fitt⁰(A′)Fitt⁰(B), using the fibre product. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 2.7.

### Compound matrices and exterior-power cokernels

<a id="L6-compound-matrix"></a>**Compound matrices.** For finite linearly ordered row/column types define C_r(A) on `Set.powersetCard` by the increasing-order r×r minors. Identify it with the matrix of the existing exterior-power map in the existing exterior-power bases. Require identity, multiplication (Cauchy–Binet), transpose, degree-one and top-degree formulas and the comparison with `Matrix.pairMinor` at r = 2. Tests: C₁ of a 2×3 matrix recovers its entries; C₂ of a 2×2 matrix is its determinant; C₃ of a 2×3 matrix has no rows. These generic statements belong in the shared matrix/exterior-power API, not a character-order-specific carrier.

<a id="L6-higher-adjugate"></a>**Higher adjugates.** For A indexed by Fin m and 0 ≤ r ≤ m define adj_r(A)_(T,S) = (−1)^(ΣS+ΣT) det A[Sᶜ,Tᶜ]. Prove both C_r(A)adj_r(A) = det(A)I and the reverse product, using the generalized Laplace expansion with the increasing-order signs. Require the shuffle-sign formula, diagonal and off-diagonal Laplace formulas and adj₁ = adjugate, adj_m = (1). Tests include the 3×3 ordinary adjugate, top degree, and the identity matrix. Supporting lemmas: `complement-shuffle-sign`, `generalised-laplace-expansion`, `compound-image-determinant`. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), proof of Lemma 3.9.

<a id="L6-exterior-cokernel-annihilator"></a>**Exterior cokernel annihilation.** For N ⊆ M, N finitely generated and M finitely presented, and r ≥ 1, Fitt⁰(M/N) annihilates the cokernel of ⋀^rN → ⋀^rM. Prove finiteness of the presentation of M/N, use the existing exterior-power maps, and derive the result from compound matrices and the determinant-image lemma. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 3.9.

### Contragredient duals and transposed presentations

<a id="L6-contragredient-dual"></a>**Ring-linear contragredient dual.** For a ring isomorphism σ : S ≃ R, use Hom_R(M,R) with scalars restricted along σ, so (sφ)(m) = φ(σ(s)m). Require underlying-functional conversion in both directions, extensionality, the semilinear scalar formula, functorial precomposition, identity/composition, dual bases, products and finite-projective preservation. This is the presentation/lattice dual; finite-module duality is L6/finite-module-duality. Tests: σ = id gives the usual dual; for O[G] with σ = sharp, (g·id)(1) = g⁻¹; when g ≠ g⁻¹ it differs from the ordinary scalar action. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), §6.1, equation (80).

<a id="L6-presentation-transpose"></a>**Transpose of a specified presentation.** For P₁ →f P₀ → M → 0 with finite projective P_i, use the existing `AuslanderReitenTranspose f`, restricting scalars along σ to obtain the contragredient transpose over S. Require quotient map, kernel and surjectivity, universal property, equality with the underlying existing transpose, transport of isomorphic presentations, identity/zero relation summands and coefficient base change. For a square matrix (a_ij), the transpose presentation matrix is (σ⁻¹(a_ji)), with its size and entry formulas. Tests: identity has zero transpose; the presentation R² → R, (a,b) ↦ a of zero has transpose S, not zero; multiplication by a has transpose S/(σ⁻¹a). Minimal presentations and a new stable-category construction are not prerequisites.

<a id="L6-transpose-stable-equivalence"></a>**Existing stable uniqueness.** Use `TauCeti.AuslanderReitenTranspose.nonempty_linearEquiv_prod_dual` for two projective presentations and restrict scalars along σ. This is an input, not another uniqueness theorem to construct.

<a id="L6-transpose-fitting"></a>**Quadratic transpose Fitting ideal.** For the transpose of the specified square presentation, prove Fitt⁰_S(M^tr) = σ⁻¹(Fitt⁰_R(M)). The presentation is part of the input; adding a free zero-relation summand changes this Fitting ideal. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), Lemma 6.1.

<a id="L6-transpose-higher-fitting"></a>**Excess generators.** For an exact presentation with finite projective P₁,P₀ of constant ranks t,t+s, prove Fitt⁰_S(M^tr) = σ⁻¹(Fitt^s_R(M)). Prove the free case by minors (`transpose-higher-fitting-free`) and the projective case by localization and base change. Require finite presentation of both cokernels and use `Module.rankAtStalk` for constant rank; do not replace projective modules over ℤ[G] by free ones without justification. Source: [DK](#ref-dk), equation (171), Appendix B.2.
