# Roadmap: stating abundance for projective klt varieties

Build the reusable divisor, positivity, and singularity theory needed to state the
following conjecture in Lean:

> Let `k` be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, and let `X` be a
> normal integral projective variety over `k` with Kawamata log terminal (klt)
> singularities. If its canonical divisor `K_X` is nef, then `K_X` is semiample:
> there is an integer `m > 0` for which `m K_X` is Cartier and
> `O_X(m K_X)` is generated by global sections.

The endpoint is a fully defined proposition, together with the comparison theorems
that establish the meaning of its definitions. Proving the conjecture is outside
this roadmap. In particular, the final proposition is not introduced as an axiom
or as a theorem proved with `sorry`. The
[CFSGStatement roadmap](../CFSGStatement/README.md) provides another example of a
roadmap whose endpoint is a statement.

## Scope and conventions

- A variety is an integral separated scheme of finite type over its specified
  field. The final statement quantifies over algebraically closed fields of
  characteristic zero and projective varieties of every dimension, including zero.
  Projective means projective over `Spec k`, not merely proper.
- This is abundance for `(X, 0)`. There is no boundary divisor, relative base,
  positive-characteristic case, or assumption that `X` is smooth, terminal,
  factorial, or Q-factorial. In particular, klt must not conceal Q-factoriality.
- `K_X` is an integral Weil divisor, defined up to principal divisor. It need not
  be Cartier. Klt includes Q-Gorensteinness: some positive integral multiple of
  `K_X` is Cartier. A canonical line bundle on all of `X` is not assumed.
- Coefficients of Weil divisors are integers; rational Weil divisors have rational
  coefficients. A prime divisor is represented by its codimension-one generic
  point. Orders of nonzero rational functions are integers, positive for zeros.
- Fix the sign by `O_X(D)(U) = {s : div(s)|_U + D|_U >= 0}`, including the zero
  section separately. With compatible canonical divisors,
  `a(E, X) = coeff_E(K_Y - f^* K_X)`; log discrepancy is `1 + a(E, X)`.
  Klt means `a(E, X) > -1`, equivalently positive log discrepancy.
- Semiampleness requires one **positive** Cartier multiple with global generation.
  Effectivity or a nonzero section is insufficient. Nefness is nonnegativity on
  the closed cone of curves, with an equivalent degree-on-curves criterion.

The minimal model program, construction of minimal models, resolution of
singularities, log resolutions, Kodaira-type vanishing, the Mori cone theorem, finite
generation of canonical rings, and the equality of Kodaira and numerical
dimensions are outside scope. The all-divisors definition of klt below does not
require choosing a resolution. The canonical divisor is constructed from top
differentials on the smooth locus, without a general duality theory. Classical
ampleness theory, including Serre vanishing and the numerical criteria needed
to prove Kleiman's criterion in Layer 4D, is included.

## Layer 0 — normal varieties and the codimension-one interface

**Input:** Mathlib schemes, commutative algebra, function fields, smooth and
birational morphisms, and the shared codimension-one point API.

1. Supply the implications needed for a normal finite-type integral `k`-scheme:
   Noetherianity, a DVR stalk at each codimension-one point, finite dimension, and
   `dim X = trdeg_k k(X)`. Compare each DVR order with `Scheme.ord`.
2. Prove that a normal variety is regular in codimension one. Over a perfect
   field, construct the open smooth locus and prove that it equals the regular
   locus. Consequently every irreducible component of its complement, the
   singular locus, has codimension at least two in `X`. Identify the
   codimension-one points and function field after restriction to any open
   containing the generic point and every codimension-one point.
3. For a birational `k`-morphism between integral varieties, expose the induced
   `k`-algebra isomorphism of function fields and its identity and composition
   laws. Proper birational models of `X` consist of an actual normal integral
   variety `Y` and a proper birational `k`-morphism `Y -> X`; no smoothness or
   Q-Gorenstein condition is imposed on `Y`.

**Milestone:** codimension-one orders and rational forms can be transported across
these open restrictions and function-field isomorphisms. The identity is a
proper birational normal model whenever `X` is normal.

## Layer 1 — normal divisor theory and Q-Cartier divisors

**Input:** Layer 0 and JacobianChallenge's shared Cartier/Weil/line-bundle API.

1. Extend the existing principal-divisor, restriction, linear-equivalence, and
   coefficient APIs to normal varieties in arbitrary dimension. Construct rational
   coefficients compatibly with Mathlib cycles, with coefficient extensionality,
   integral inclusion, finite support, addition, and rational scalar multiplication.
2. **Unique extension across a complement of codimension at least two.** For an
   open immersion `j : U -> X` into a normal integral variety, assume every
   irreducible component of `X \ U` has codimension at least two in `X`. Prove
   that restriction is an isomorphism of groups of Weil divisors
   `Div(X) -> Div(U)`. Construct its inverse by sending
   `D = sum_i a_i [Z_i]` to `sum_i a_i [closure_X(Z_i)]`. Prove that these
   closures are prime divisors, that the sum has finite support, and that
   extension and restriction are mutual inverses: every Weil divisor on `U`
   has a unique Weil-divisor extension to `X`. Prove additivity, compatibility
   with successive such open restrictions, and the analogous result with
   rational coefficients. Under `k(U) = k(X)`, prove
   `extension(div_U(f)) = div_X(f)` for every nonzero rational function `f`.
   Deduce compatibility with linear equivalence and the induced isomorphism
   `Cl(X) -> Cl(U)`. This result concerns Weil divisors; it does not assert that
   the extension of a Cartier divisor remains Cartier or Q-Cartier on `X`.
3. Prove the affine normal-domain regularity criterion: a rational function is
   regular precisely when it belongs to every height-one localization. Use it to
   prove injectivity of Cartier-to-Weil and the equality of the Cartier `O(D)` and
   Weil `O(D)` constructions for Cartier divisors. Do not assert that every Weil
   divisor on a normal variety is Cartier.
4. For a rational Weil divisor `D`, define Q-Cartierness by the existence of
   `r : N`, `r > 0`, and a Cartier divisor `A` whose Weil divisor is `r D`.
   Prove its agreement with the local definition on these quasi-compact schemes,
   closure under addition and rational scaling, and invariance under Q-linear
   equivalence. All comparison maps retain the underlying Weil divisor.
5. Along a dominant morphism of normal varieties, construct Cartier pullback from
   local rational equations. Prove compatibility with the associated line bundle,
   principal divisors, addition, restriction, identity, and composition. Define
   `f^*D = (1/r) f^*A` for Q-Cartier `D`; prove independence of `(r,A)` and the
   same laws. This gives pullback for every proper birational model in Layer 0.

**Milestone:** Weil divisors extend uniquely across complements of codimension
at least two, compatibly with principal divisors and linear equivalence. The
Q-Cartier constructions are independent of the chosen Cartier index and respect
Q-linear equivalence. Pullback of line bundles exists along arbitrary morphisms;
pullback of rational Cartier equations is used only with its stated hypotheses.

## Layer 2 — canonical divisors from actual differentials

**Input:** Layers 0–1 and AlgebraicVectorBundles L0A–C.

1. Glue the affine modules of Kähler differentials to `Omega^1_(X/k)`, with
   localization and generic-fiber comparisons. On a smooth integral variety of
   dimension `n`, prove it is locally free of rank `n`. Form its top exterior
   power and identify its generic fiber with `wedge^n Omega_(k(X)/k)`.
2. For an invertible sheaf on a normal variety and a nonzero rational section,
   construct its divisor using local trivializations and codimension-one orders.
   Prove finite support, independence of trivialization, the formula
   `div(g s) = div(g) + div(s)`, and the associated-line-bundle isomorphism.
3. For normal `X`, let `U` be its smooth locus. Layer 0 proves that `X \ U` has
   codimension at least two. On `U`, form the canonical line bundle
   `omega_U = wedge^n Omega^1_(U/k)` and choose a nonzero rational section `eta`.
   Define `K_U(eta) = div_U(eta)` by item 2. Apply Layer 1's unique-extension
   theorem to define `K_X(eta)` as the unique Weil divisor restricting to
   `K_U(eta)`. Prove that using any smaller smooth open whose complement in `X`
   has codimension at least two gives the same divisor. Use compatibility with
   principal divisors to prove `K_X(g eta) = K_X(eta) + div_X(g)` for every
   nonzero rational function `g`. Thus the extension is unique for a fixed
   rational top form, and the canonical divisor class is independent of that
   choice. This construction gives a Weil divisor; its Q-Cartierness is the
   separate Q-Gorenstein condition.
4. Prove compatibility under `k`-isomorphisms and restriction. For a birational
   model `f : Y -> X`, use the induced function-field isomorphism to use the
   **same** rational form for `K_X` and `K_Y`. For smooth `X`, identify
   `O_X(K_X)` with the actual top exterior power of its cotangent sheaf.

**Milestone:** canonical divisors exist and any two are linearly equivalent.
Q-Gorensteinness is a concrete predicate independent of the top form. Dimension
zero gives canonical divisor zero. No formal placeholder for a canonical class
is part of this interface.

## Layer 3 — discrepancies and klt

**Input:** Layers 0–2. Assume `X` normal and `K_X` Q-Cartier.

For every proper birational normal model `f : Y -> X` and every prime divisor
`E` on `Y`, define

```math
a(E;Y/X)=\operatorname{coeff}_E\bigl(K_Y(\omega)-f^*K_X(\omega)\bigr).
```

1. Prove independence of the nonzero top form and of the Cartier index. If
   `r K_X` is Cartier, prove `r a(E;Y/X)` is integral. Thus the strict inequality
   `a > -1` has the uniform rational gap `a >= -1 + 1/r`.
2. Prove compatibility with isomorphisms of models over `X`, and with strict
   transforms at codimension-one points where a dominating model is an
   isomorphism. The defining quantifier ranges over **all** models and all their
   prime divisors; it is not a selected finite list. No quotient of models or
   assertion about existence of resolutions is needed to form this quantifier.
3. Define klt to mean Q-Gorensteinness and `a(E;Y/X) > -1` for every such `(Y,f,E)`.
   Prove invariance under `k`-isomorphisms. On the identity model discrepancies
   vanish. Prove that smooth varieties are klt: pullback of a local regular top
   form has nonnegative order at each codimension-one point of a normal model.
   The differential pullback and its generic nonvanishing are part of this proof.

**Milestone:** an intrinsic klt predicate with the all-divisors meaning, its
unfolding theorem, Q-Gorenstein consequence, and smooth examples. Checking only
divisors on `X`, or only the identity model, does not discharge this layer.

## Layer 4 — numerical spaces, the closed cone of curves, and nefness

**Input:** Layer 1, the invertible-sheaf pullback/tensor API, and the curve-degree
contracts specified above. Work over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero
field. Construct the numerical spaces for projective varieties without imposing
smoothness, klt, or Q-factoriality.

### Layer 4A — curve degrees and intersection with divisors

1. On an integral regular projective curve `C/k`, identify the existing
   Euler-characteristic degree of an invertible sheaf with the residue-weighted
   degree of any rational-section divisor. Supply the finiteness hypotheses in
   the comparison. Prove degree zero for the trivial bundle, additivity under
   tensor product, negation under dual, and multiplication under tensor powers.
2. Given an integral projective curve `C`, construct its finite normalization
   `nu : C^nu -> C`, using the existing normalization and regular-model theory;
   prove projectivity and smoothness of `C^nu` over the characteristic-zero
   field. Construct its morphism to this given `C`, not only an abstract regular
   model of its function field. Prove the degree formula for finite dominant
   maps between regular projective curves, including the positive field degree.
3. For a Cartier divisor `A` and an integral closed curve `C` in `X`, define
   `A . C = deg(nu^*(O_X(A)|_C))`. Extend the pairing bilinearly to finite
   real linear combinations of Cartier divisors and of integral curves. Prove
   that principal divisors pair to zero. For line bundles, use the same degree
   formula and prove compatibility with Cartier representatives.
4. For Q-Cartier `D` represented by `r D = A`, define
   `D . C = deg(nu^*(O_X(A)|_C))/r`. Prove independence of `(r,A)` and equivalence
   with the rational extension of the Cartier pairing. Include curves contained
   in the support of `D`: pull back the line bundle, not a rational equation
   that vanishes identically on the curve.

### Layer 4B — numerical equivalence and finite-dimensional duality

1. Define numerical equivalence of real 1-cycles by equality of intersection
   with every Cartier divisor, and numerical equivalence of real Cartier
   divisors by equality of intersection with every integral curve. Form the
   quotient real vector spaces `N_1(X)_R` and `N^1(X)_R`, respectively, and
   descend the intersection pairing. Relate these constructions to the integral
   and rational numerical groups and extension of scalars.
2. Prove the numerical Neron-Severi finiteness theorem: the group of Cartier
   divisors modulo numerical equivalence is finitely generated. This is a
   substantive foundation to implement, not an extra hypothesis on `X` or an
   opaque assumption. Deduce finite-dimensionality of both real spaces and prove
   that the pairing identifies each with the real linear dual of the other.
   The proof must cover the stated field generality, not only complex varieties.
3. Equip the spaces with their finite-dimensional real vector-space topologies.
   Prove independence of a basis or norm, continuity of the intersection
   functionals, and compatibility of the duality with these topologies.
4. For Q-Cartier `D`, construct `[D] = (1/r)[A]` in `N^1(X)_R` for `r D = A`
   and prove index independence. Preserve the map from actual divisors to
   numerical classes; semiampleness in Layer 5 still concerns actual line bundles
   and is not asserted to be invariant under numerical equivalence.

### Layer 4C — the closed cone of curves and its dual

1. Define `NE(X)` as all finite sums `sum_i a_i [C_i]` with real `a_i >= 0`
   and integral closed curves `C_i`. Define `NEbar(X)` as its topological closure
   in `N_1(X)_R`. Prove the cone and convexity laws and closedness of `NEbar(X)`.
   Do not assume that `NE(X)` itself is closed or that either cone is polyhedral.
2. Define the nef cone as the dual cone
   `Nef(X) = {alpha : N^1(X)_R | forall z in NEbar(X), alpha . z >= 0}`.
   Define a Cartier or Q-Cartier divisor to be nef when its numerical class
   belongs to this cone. Prove that the nef cone is closed and convex.
3. Prove equivalence with `D . C >= 0` for every integral closed curve, using
   linearity on `NE(X)` and continuity on its closure. Then prove equivalence
   with `deg(g^* L) >= 0` for all maps from smooth integral projective curves
   for the corresponding Cartier line bundle `L`. Include constant maps
   (degree zero), factorization of nonconstant maps through the normalization
   of their image, and the finite-map degree formula. These equivalences do
   not depend on Kleiman's ampleness criterion.
4. Prove invariance under numerical equivalence, hence Q-linear equivalence,
   closure under addition, and equivalence under positive rational scaling.
   In particular, if `r D = A` with `r > 0`, then `D` is nef exactly when
   `O_X(A)` is nef. Prove preservation of nef line bundles under pullback
   between projective varieties via the curve-testing characterization.

### Layer 4D — ampleness and Kleiman's criterion

**Input:** Layers 4A–C and the global-generation foundations in Layer 5, items
1–2, which can be implemented before this sublayer.

1. Use geometric ampleness, with its positive-power very-ample criterion,
   independently of the cones. Supply the projective cohomology finiteness,
   Serre vanishing, Hilbert-polynomial intersection numbers, and restriction and
   multiplicity formulas needed for the Nakai-Moishezon criterion. Prove that
   criterion, including its use on possibly singular subvarieties.
2. Prove the bridge from curve nonnegativity to nonnegative top intersections
   on subvarieties, and deduce that ample plus nef is ample for Q-Cartier
   divisors. These are proof targets, not assumptions or definitions of nefness.
3. Prove Kleiman's criterion for projective `X`: a Cartier or Q-Cartier divisor
   `D` is ample if and only if `D . z > 0` for every nonzero `z` in `NEbar(X)`.
   For the converse, use compactness of the unit slice to obtain
   `D - epsilon H` nef for some positive rational `epsilon` and ample `H`.
4. Define the real ample cone from positive real combinations of ample Cartier
   classes. Prove `Amp(X) = interior(Nef(X))` and
   `closure(Amp(X)) = Nef(X)`, including numerical invariance of ampleness.
   Keep the zero-dimensional case explicit. Positivity on individual curves
   alone must not be substituted for positivity on every nonzero class in the
   **closed** cone.

**Milestone:** nefness has interchangeable closed-cone and curve-testing
descriptions; ampleness has a separately proved Kleiman criterion. For a
Q-Gorenstein variety, `[K_X] = (1/r)[r K_X]` supplies the numerical class used
in the abundance hypothesis. No decomposition into extremal rays or Mori cone
theorem is required.

## Layer 5 — global generation and semiampleness

**Input:** Layer 1 and the sheaf operations for items 1–3; Layer 4C for the nef
comparison in item 4. Items 1–2 also supply Layer 4D and do not depend on it.

1. Define global generation by the surjectivity of the evaluation morphism from
   the free `O_X`-module on global sections to the sheaf. Prove the stalkwise
   criterion. For invertible sheaves, prove equivalence to: at every point, some
   global section has nonzero image in the one-dimensional residue-field fiber.
   On a quasi-compact scheme, prove that a finite family of sections suffices.
2. Prove invariance under isomorphism, global generation of the trivial bundle,
   preservation under arbitrary pullback, and preservation under tensor products
   and nonnegative tensor powers. Identify the criterion for `O_X(A)` with the
   absence of base points in the complete linear system of the Cartier divisor `A`.
3. Define a semiample line bundle by the existence of a positive tensor power
   that is globally generated. Define semiample Q-Cartier `D` by the existence
   of `m > 0` and a Cartier representative of `m D` whose associated line bundle
   is globally generated. Prove agreement of these definitions, invariance under
   Q-linear equivalence, and equivalence under positive rational scaling.
4. Prove that if `r D` is Cartier then `D` is semiample if and only if some
   positive tensor power of `O_X(r D)` is globally generated. In particular a
   witnessing multiple can be replaced by any positive integral multiple of it.
   Prove that semiample implies nef, using a nonzero section on a curve to
   identify its divisor as effective. Prove that Q-linearly trivial divisors
   are semiample.

**Milestone:** `Semiample K_X` unfolds to exactly the positive-Cartier-multiple
conclusion in the opening statement, with no choice of index affecting its truth.

## Layer 6 — the abundance proposition and its meaning

**Input:** Layers 0–5.

Define a universe-polymorphic, field-indexed proposition `AbundanceConjecture k`
for an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field. Its quantified data are
actual schemes over `Spec k`; its hypotheses are integrality, finite type,
projectivity, normality, the Layer 3 klt condition, and nefness of the Layer 2
canonical divisor. Its conclusion is Layer 5 semiampleness.

The mathematical expansion is:

```math
\forall X/k\ \text{normal integral projective and klt},\quad
K_X\ \text{nef}\ \Longrightarrow
\exists m\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\quad
mK_X\ \text{Cartier and }\mathcal O_X(mK_X)\ \text{globally generated}.
```

The exact Lean spelling follows the implemented public APIs. A file containing
uninterpreted parameters named `CanonicalDivisor`, `Klt`, `Nef`, or `Semiample`
does not state this conjecture. Definitions of predicates with `sorry` bodies
do not discharge any target. Suggested signatures, when added, must import the
actual dependencies and explicitly distinguish incomplete constructions from the
finished proposition.

Prove the unfolding theorem and independence from the chosen canonical divisor.
Using smooth-implies-klt, prove that `AbundanceConjecture k` implies the smooth
canonical-bundle version. Keep the conjecture as an explicit hypothesis in every
conditional application. Do not prove or assume the converse reduction here.

## Acceptance instances and completion

Each layer includes its characteristic equations, extensionality where relevant,
and isomorphism/restriction laws. The following concrete instances are targets:

- `Spec k`: normal, projective, smooth and klt, with canonical divisor zero and
  a globally generated trivial canonical bundle. This also tests dimension zero.
- The trivial line bundle on every projective variety is nef and globally
  generated. Its positive tensor powers and Q-linearly trivial divisor examples
  test the positive-multiple conventions.
- On `P^1_k`, compute `div(dt) = -2[infinity]`, identify
  `O(K) = O(-2)`, and compute degree `-2`. Its canonical divisor is not nef.
  Use the rational-curve calculations of AlgebraicCurves and prove the named
  comparison with Layer 2's top-form divisor. This tests the canonical sign and
  prevents a predicate that declares all divisors nef.
- For every smooth projective `X`, the klt predicate applies and the canonical
  Weil-divisor formulation agrees with the top-exterior-power formulation.
- For `P^1_k`, identify `N_1(X)_R` and `N^1(X)_R` with `R`, with the curve
  class and `O(1)` as paired generators. Compute `NEbar(X)` and the nef cone
  as the nonnegative ray and the ample cone as the positive ray. For `Spec k`,
  both numerical spaces are zero; verify the cone and ampleness statements in
  that topology, including the vacuous nonzero-class condition in Kleiman.
- The final statement, these comparisons, and their transitive definition/proof
  dependencies contain no `sorryAx`, invented mathematical axioms, or opaque
  stand-ins. Ordinary Lean classical axioms are allowed. A kernel-checked `Prop`
  definition is the endpoint; it is not a proof that the proposition is true.

The divisor/singularity chain is `0 -> 1 -> 2 -> 3`. The numerical chain is
`1 -> 4A -> 4B -> 4C -> 4D`. Layer 5 items 1–2 depend only on Layer 1 and the
sheaf suppliers and feed Layer 4D; Layer 5's semiample-to-nef comparison consumes
Layer 4C. Thus the ampleness and semiampleness comparisons have no circular
dependency. Layer 6 consumes the resulting interfaces. Layer 1's
normal Cartier-to-Weil injectivity and sheaf comparison are a first implementation
unit; they have a direct affine normal-domain proof and a reusable output.

Suggested library homes are extensions of `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/CartierDivisor/`,
`WeilDivisor/`, and `LineBundle/`, followed by `CanonicalDivisor/`, `Singularities/`,
`Positivity/`, and `Abundance/Statement.lean`. These are proposed locations, not
alternative mathematical interfaces.
