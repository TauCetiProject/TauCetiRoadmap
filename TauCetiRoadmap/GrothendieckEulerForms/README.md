# Roadmap: Grothendieck groups, Cartan maps, and Euler forms

Grothendieck groups turn direct-sum, exact-sequence, or triangle decompositions into additive
relations.  Euler pairings then turn homological information into integral forms, but only when the
homological groups are finite-dimensional and vanish outside a finite range.  This roadmap builds
the categorical infrastructure needed to make both constructions reusable and precise: Quillen
exact structures, split/exact/abelian/triangulated Grothendieck groups and their universal
properties, the Cartan map from projectives to modules, finite-resolution comparison, ordinary and
graded Ext-Euler pairings, and the two numerical quotients of a nonsymmetric form.

The development consumes Mathlib's category theory and the finite-dimensional-algebra targets in
[quiver representations](../RepresentationTheory/QuiverRepresentations/README.md).  It does not
copy that roadmap's path algebras, projective covers, simples, Cartan matrices, or quiver Ringel
form.  Instead it supplies the general categorical constructions in which those objects live and
proves the comparison theorems that turn them into lattices.

Suggested homes in Tau Ceti are `TauCeti/CategoryTheory/Exact/` for exact structures and exact
functors, `TauCeti/CategoryTheory/GrothendieckGroup/` for categorical Grothendieck groups,
`TauCeti/Algebra/Homology/EulerCharacteristic/` for the finite-support Euler machinery, and
`TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/GrothendieckGroup/` for module, Cartan, graded, and numerical
applications.  The exact file split is an implementation choice; the dependency order below is
not.

## Scope boundary

This roadmap ends with exact, abelian, and triangulated categories, finite resolutions, Ext, and
algebraic specializations of Laurent-polynomial-valued forms.  Everything is built in both the
ungraded and the internally graded setting; see **Graded throughout** below.  It does not
construct or use:

- DG or A-infinity algebras, categories, or modules;
- perfect derived categories, smoothness, properness, compact generation, Serre functors, or
  Calabi--Yau structures;
- Verdier quotients, derived localization, or singularity categories;
- Frobenius, stable, periodic, or curved categories;
- hopfological algebra or a categorical interpretation of specialization at a root of unity;
- zigzag or preprojective algebras, or the McKay correspondence, as subjects;
- a categorification of an ADE or rank-24 lattice;
- the Cartan determinant conjecture.

Bounded homotopy and derived categories appear only in the standard K₀ comparisons that follow
from the ordinary exact/triangulated infrastructure built here.  No perfect-category or
localization machinery is introduced to obtain them.

## Standing conventions

- **Smallness.**  A categorical Grothendieck group is formed only for an essentially small
  category.  Given `[CategoryTheory.EssentiallySmall.{w} C]`, use a small type such as
  `Shrink.{w} (Skeleton C)` for the generators.  An object of `C` is sent through the skeleton and
  shrink equivalences.  Relations may still be indexed by actual conflations or triangles in `C`;
  their generators lie in the chosen small universe.  The public construction lives in universe
  `w`, and its universal property proves independence of the skeleton/shrink choices and invariance
  under equivalence.  An implementation using `SmallModel C` is equivalent, but arbitrary model
  choices must not leak into the public API.
- **Exact structures are data.**  A Quillen exact structure on an additive category is an
  isomorphism-closed distinguished class of kernel-cokernel pairs satisfying E0/E0op, E1/E1op, and
  E2/E2op.  In Bühler's numbering E0/E1/E2 are stated for inflations (identities, composition, and
  pushout) and their dual axioms for deflations.  It is not Mathlib's predicate
  `ShortComplex.Exact`.  The split exact structure on an additive category and the canonical exact
  structure on an abelian category are different data even though both use the same underlying
  category.
- **Functor terminology.**  A functor preserving distinguished conflations is called a
  *conflation-exact functor* in this roadmap's API, for example
  `ExactStructure.IsConflationExact F`.  Do not reuse the ambiguous name `ExactFunctor`:
  Mathlib's `CategoryTheory.exactFunctor` and bundled `C ⥤ₑ D` concern preservation of finite limits
  and colimits.  Prove the precise comparison between these notions for additive functors between
  abelian categories with their canonical exact structures.
- **K₀ relations.**  Exact K₀ is generated by object classes with
  `[Y] = [X] + [Z]` for each conflation `X ↪ Y ↠ Z`.  Split K₀ uses biproduct relations.
  Triangulated K₀ uses the same equation for every distinguished triangle
  `X ⟶ Y ⟶ Z ⟶ X[1]`.
- **K-theory notation.**  For a finite-dimensional algebra `A`, `K₀(proj A)` means the split K₀ of
  finitely generated projective modules and `G₀(mod A)` means the abelian K₀ of the stated
  finite-dimensional/finite-length module category.  Dancso--Licata use the reverse letters in part
  of their exposition; translate their notation rather than adopting it.
- **Modules are left modules.**  This matches the quiver-representation roadmap and landed Tau
  Ceti.  If a cited source uses right modules, transpose its matrix formulas explicitly.  With
  simples `S_i`, projective covers `P_i`, and Cartan entries
  `C_{ij} = [P_j:S_i]`, columns record projectives in the simple basis.
- **General-field multiplicities.**  Write `D_i = End_A(S_i)`.  Exactness of
  `Hom_A(P_i,-)` gives
  `dim_k Hom_A(P_i,M) = dim_k D_i · [M:S_i]`.  Thus
  `dim_k Hom_A(P_i,S_j) = δ_{ij} dim_k D_i`, not `δ_{ij}` in general.  The integral
  projective/simple pairing uses the multiplicity coordinate `[M:S_i]`.  Raw `k`-dimension gives
  the perfect Kronecker pairing only when the simples are absolutely simple, for example over an
  algebraically closed splitting field.  Do not assert a canonical `D_i`-vector-space structure on
  every `Hom_A(P_i,M)` without separately constructing the correct sided action.
- **Cartan comparison.**  The Cartan map is
  `K₀(proj A) ⟶ G₀(mod A)`.  When the modules under consideration have finite projective
  resolutions, the resolution theorem makes it an isomorphism and sends a module back to the
  alternating class of any finite projective resolution.  In finite bases its matrix has determinant
  `±1`.  Determinant `+1` is not a target.
- **Euler finiteness.**  An Ext-Euler value
  `χ(X,Y) = ∑ n, (-1)^n dim_k Ext^n(X,Y)` exists only after proving separately that every term is
  finite-dimensional and that all sufficiently high terms vanish.  Mathlib's `finsum`-based Euler
  characteristic deliberately returns a value even without finite support; that totalized value is
  not used to hide divergence here.
- **Nonsymmetry.**  The raw Euler form is generally nonsymmetric.  Its left radical consists of
  classes pairing to zero against every second argument; its right radical consists of classes
  annihilated by every first argument.  They and their quotients remain distinct unless an explicit
  duality proves otherwise.
- **Graded throughout.**  The internal grading is not an afterthought added in Layer 6.  A graded
  exact category is an exact category with a chosen grading-shift autoequivalence `{1}` preserving
  conflations; every construction in Layers 1--5 has a graded counterpart over that datum, and the
  graded counterpart is a target of this roadmap, built alongside the ungraded one rather than
  after it.  Concretely: graded exact and abelian `K₀` with their shift action, graded finite and
  linear resolutions, the graded Cartan map and matrix, graded Ext with its internal degree, and
  the graded quiver comparison.  Layer 6 adds only the Laurent coefficient ring, the
  sesquilinearity that goes with it, and the specializations; it does not introduce the grading.
  Where a statement genuinely has no graded analogue, say so and say why.  The downstream
  roadmaps -- zigzag, hopfological, and DG/`A∞` -- consume the graded versions almost exclusively,
  so an ungraded-only layer is not a usable dependency.
- **Grading conventions.**  The internal shift is normalized by `[M{1}] = q[M]`.  With
  `qdim Hom(M,N) = ∑_j q⁻ʲ dim_k Hom(M,N{j})`, equivalently
  `∑_j q^j dim_k Hom(M{j},N)`, the q-Euler form is q-antilinear in its first
  argument and q-linear in its second.  The coefficient involution is Mathlib's
  `LaurentPolynomial.invert`, sending `q` to `q⁻¹`; do not introduce a private bar operation.
  Evaluation at `q = 1` and `q = -1` always defines an algebraic specialization.  Agreement at
  `q = 1` with an ungraded K-group or Euler form is a separate comparison theorem requiring an
  explicit forgetful functor and graded/ungraded Ext hypotheses; it is not a consequence of an
  internal shift alone.  Either specialization may destroy nondegeneracy.

## The build, in layers

### Layer 0: intrinsic exact structures and conflation-exact functors

Build exact-category data without assuming that the ambient additive category has arbitrary kernels
or cokernels.

- **Kernel-cokernel pairs.**  Package objects `X,Y,Z`, composable maps
  `X ⟶ Y ⟶ Z`, the zero composite, and the actual universal properties exhibiting the first map
  as a kernel of the second and the second as a cokernel of the first.  Bridge this package to
  Mathlib's `Limits.IsKernel`/`Limits.IsCokernel` when those declarations apply.  Isomorphisms of
  pairs include compatible isomorphisms at all three objects.
- **Exact structures.**  An `ExactStructure C` is an isomorphism-closed class of these pairs with
  the self-dual Bühler axioms E0/E0op, E1/E1op, and E2/E2op.  E2 constructs a pushout of every
  inflation along an arbitrary map and proves the resulting map is an inflation; E2op constructs
  the dual pullback of a deflation.  Use genuine universal squares, not named `Prop` fields.
  Develop the standard
  consequences: split sequences are conflations, direct sums of conflations are conflations,
  isomorphisms are both inflations and deflations, and the bicartesian characterization of
  pushouts/pullbacks along admissible maps.
- **Calculus and duality.**  Define inflations, deflations, admissible morphisms, maps and
  isomorphisms of conflations.  Construct the opposite exact structure and prove that every theorem
  with an E/op partner transports correctly.
- **Conflation-exact functors.**  Define additive functors preserving conflations, composition,
  identity, natural-isomorphism invariance, reflection of conflations, exact equivalences, and
  transfer of an exact structure along an equivalence.  Prove preservation of admissible
  pushouts/pullbacks.  For abelian categories with the canonical exact structures, compare this
  predicate with Mathlib's finite-limit/finite-colimit notion and state exactly which hypotheses
  make them equivalent.
- **The three fundamental constructions.**  Construct the split exact structure on every additive
  category; the canonical exact structure of all short exact sequences on every abelian category;
  and the induced structure on a replete full **additive** extension-closed subcategory.  Equivalently,
  make closure under a zero object and finite biproducts explicit.  Prove that the last inclusion is
  conflation-exact and reflects conflations.  In particular, do not collapse the split and canonical
  structures on an abelian category.

This layer follows Bühler, Definition 2.1, Remarks 2.2--2.8, Propositions 2.9 and 2.12, Section 5,
and Sections 13.1--13.3.  These are also the acceptance tests for the precise axiom packaging.

### Layer 1: the small presentation and universal additive invariants

Build the common engine for split, exact, and triangulated K₀.

- **Small generators.**  For an essentially small category `C`, form a free abelian group on
  `Shrink (Skeleton C)`.  Supply the class of every original object and prove it respects
  isomorphism.  Also give induction and quotient-lift principles whose statements quantify over
  objects of `C`, so users do not manipulate skeleton representatives.
- **Relations as generated subgroups.**  Package families of additive relations as additive
  subgroups of the free abelian group and quotient by them.  Prove the universal property: a
  function on objects, invariant under isomorphism and satisfying the selected relations, factors
  through a unique additive homomorphism.  Include extensionality by values on object classes.
- **Choice independence.**  Show that two skeleton/small-model presentations carry a canonical
  isomorphism characterized by the class maps.  Prove naturality and cocycle laws for these
  canonical isomorphisms; public theorems must not depend on definitional equality between choices.
- **Products and biadditivity.**  Prove compatibility with product categories and finite
  biproducts.  A functor in either variable satisfying the relevant relations yields the expected
  bilinear map on K₀.

The model is Weibel, *The K-book*, Chapter II, Section 1 for group completion, Section 5 for
symmetric-monoidal/split K₀, and Section 6.1--6.2 for presentations, set-theoretic care, and the
universal property.

### Layer 2: split, exact, abelian, and triangulated Grothendieck groups

Specialize Layer 1 while retaining a uniform public API.

- **Split K₀.**  Quotient by `[X ⊞ Y]-[X]-[Y]`.  Supply the class map, biproduct relation,
  induction, universal property for additive invariants, functoriality for additive functors,
  identity/composition/naturality, and equivalence invariance.  Relate the result to Mathlib's
  commutative-monoid group completion of the monoid of isomorphism classes.
- **Exact K₀.**  For an exact structure `E`, quotient by
  `[Y]-[X]-[Z]` for every `E`-conflation `X ↪ Y ↠ Z`.  Supply the same API with
  conflation-additive invariants and conflation-exact functors.  Exact equivalences induce additive
  equivalences.  The identity functor from the split exact structure to `E` induces the canonical
  comparison `splitK₀ C ⟶ exactK₀(C,E)`; prove its universal characterization and naturality.
- **Abelian K₀.**  Define `abelianK₀ C` as exact K₀ for the canonical abelian exact structure, not
  as a second presentation.  Give convenient lemmas in terms of `ShortComplex.ShortExact`, additive
  functoriality under the correct exactness hypothesis, and equivalence invariance.
- **Triangulated K₀.**  For an essentially small pretriangulated category, quotient by
  `[Y]-[X]-[Z]` for each `T : Triangle C` with `distTriang C T`.  Give the triangle-additive
  universal property, maps induced by triangulated functors, naturality, equivalence invariance,
  products, and compatibility with split triangles.  Prove `[0]=0`, `[X[1]]=-[X]`, and more
  generally `[X[n]]=(-1)^n[X]` with the correct integer-shift interpretation.
- **Bounded-complex comparisons.**  Prove the standard isomorphism from split K₀ of an additive
  category to triangulated K₀ of its bounded homotopy category, sending a bounded complex to the
  alternating sum of its terms.  For an abelian category, use Mathlib's existing derived-category
  infrastructure to prove that the class of a bounded complex is the alternating sum of its
  homology classes and obtain the bounded-derived-category comparison.  For a full exact
  subcategory of an abelian category, state every closure hypothesis needed for cycles, boundaries,
  and homology objects to remain in the subcategory, but keep its Euler-class comparison at the
  complex level.  A derived category of a general Quillen exact category would require
  exact-acyclic localization infrastructure and is outside this roadmap; do not smuggle that
  construction into this milestone.
- **Graded exact categories and graded K₀.**  Define a graded exact category as an exact category
  with a chosen conflation-exact autoequivalence `{1}`, together with graded conflation-exact
  functors and graded exact equivalences.  Build graded split, exact, abelian and triangulated K₀
  by the same Layer 1 engine, each carrying the induced `ℤ`-action `[M{n}]` of the shift, with the
  universal property for invariants equipped with a compatible invertible shift action.  Prove
  that forgetting the grading along a conflation-exact functor `U` with `{1} ⋙ U ≅ U` induces a
  map from graded to ungraded K₀, and record that this map is not an isomorphism in general -- the
  hypotheses that make it one are Layer 6's business.  The Laurent-module repackaging of the
  `ℤ`-action is Layer 6; the action itself is here.

Weibel, Chapter II, Sections 5--7 supplies split and exact K₀, Section 6.1.2 supplies the abelian
universal property, and Exercise II.9.15 supplies the triangulated presentation and bounded
comparisons.

### Layer 2A: arbitrary-ring finite projectives and change of scalars

This algebraic specialization applies to arbitrary associative unital rings A,B, including the zero ring. Generic categorical Layers 1–2 are constructed independently before it; their small presentations and splitK₀ universal properties have no finite-projective-module premise. The module, balanced tensor, retract and idempotent constructions below are independent of that generic group-completion engine. Only the final comparison consumes both these module constructions and the already constructed Layers 1–2. None of these algebraic steps assumes finite-dimensional algebra, an involution, topology, positivity or a C*-norm. The category of left modules follows the standing convention; a source right A-module is a native Module Aᵐᵒᵖ. Retain right-sided formulas explicitly in the comparison below, and derive the left-handed version by taking opposite rings.

**Balanced extension of scalars.** Consume AlgebraicKTheory’s associative balanced quotient through the explicit left-module/opposite-ring transport in KTheoryLowDegrees, and identify its right-module carrier M⊗_A B for f:A→+*B with the quotient of the free abelian group on pairs (m,b) by the additive relations in each variable and (ma,b)=(m,f(a)b). Define the right B action on generators by (m,b)c=(m,bc), prove it preserves every relation, and prove all module laws. Export the universal property for biadditive A-balanced maps to abelian groups and its right-B-linear specialization. Do not instantiate Mathlib TensorProduct over a noncommutative A: its base CommSemiring hypothesis is real. AlgebraicKTheory first forms the additive tensor over ℤ and then imposes the A-balancing relations. Prove its comparison with the free-abelian-on-pairs presentation by the additive-tensor and free-group universal properties; both maps preserve m⊗b, kill the displayed relations and are inverse by generator induction. The ℤ-base use satisfies native commutativity, whereas the balancing ring A remains arbitrary. Compare the arbitrary-ring characteristic-predicate interface specified by that balanced construction through the concrete balanced map flippedTmul:N→ₗ[A](M→+X), with domain-precomposition A action (a•h)(m)=h(ma). Prove explicitly that this linearity is exactly the balancing equation. Export lift, lift_comp and lift_uniq in that shape, generation/induction and the unique additive comparison between any two universal carriers. Use a local domain-action instance so it is not confused with an unrelated codomain module action. Construct the characteristic predicate and induced left/right bimodule actions for this quotient; an ordinary commutative-base TensorProduct example does not supply the arbitrary-ring quotient carrier. For N=B with left A action through f and right B multiplication, supply the actual commuting A/Bᵐᵒᵖ actions and identify the quotient right Module Bᵐᵒᵖ with the prototype IsTensorProduct.rightModule by uniqueness on generators. Preserve universes: a characteristic predicate quantifying target P only in the universe of X is a same-universe specialization. Prove the concrete quotient universal property for abelian groups P in arbitrary universes directly by the free-group quotient eliminator, and construct ULift comparisons, independence of lifts and the natural comparison at a common maximum universe. Instantiate that specialization without weakening the exported cross-universe universal property. A Tensor-Hom adjunction restricted to a commutative base does not supply this arbitrary-A carrier. Installed Mathlib `IsTensorProduct` in `Mathlib.RingTheory.IsTensorProduct` assumes `CommSemiring`; its bilinear lift is used only for the commutative specialization. The arbitrary-ring flipped characteristic predicate and its right-module action are explicit comparisons with the Part II balanced quotient, not an assertion that the installed commutative predicate has weaker hypotheses.

Construct maps induced by right-linear maps, their identity/composition equations, and the additive functor on module categories. Construct explicit natural right-module equivalences A⊗_A B≃B, M⊗_A A≃M and (M⊕N)⊗_A B≃(M⊗_A B)⊕(N⊗_A B). Their maps are a⊗b↦f(a)b, m⊗a↦ma and componentwise inclusion/projection; prove their inverses and naturality by induction on tensor generators. For A→B→C construct (M⊗_A B)⊗_B C≃M⊗_A C by (m⊗b)⊗c↦m⊗g(b)c, inverse m⊗c↦(m⊗1)⊗c. Prove unit, associativity and base-change coherence on generators, including zero modules and zero rings.

The domain-action and opposite-module presentations require explicit transports. For an actual Module Rᵐᵒᵖ M use the double-opposite ring map R→(Rᵐᵒᵖ)ᵈᵐᵃ and the displayed domain-action formula; for a commutative left-module presentation use the commutativity-dependent toOpposite map. Construct and name the ring/action identifications between MulOpposite and DomMulAct, prove their scalar equations and the same balanced universal property, rather than letting definitional equality or an instance diamond silently change the action. The base-change case uses genuine right multiplication on B and its Hom-space precomposition transport. Compare the common lifts/induced maps on generators, so the finite-projective API is natural under either carrier presentation.

**Additive-monoid targets and the historical quotient comparison.** The preceding free-abelian-group eliminator initially has abelian-group targets, whereas the characteristic predicate quantifies AddCommMonoid targets. Supply this missing adapter explicitly. For P an arbitrary AddCommMonoid and a balanced biadditive f:M×B→P, define its lift to native AddUnits P by value f(m,b), inverse f(−m,b), and prove the two inverse equations using additivity and f(0,b)=0. Equivalently, at each b use native AddMonoidHom.toHomUnits on f(·,b). Native AddUnits.coeHom is injective, so its value equations prove additivity in b and the balancing/linearity identities for the lifted map. Apply the free-group quotient universal property into the abelian group AddUnits P and compose AddUnits.coeHom to obtain the required tensor→+P. Prove uniqueness: every additive map from the tensor group to P lifts by toHomUnits; equality on tensor generators and injectivity of coeHom identify it with this lift. This works in every target universe, not just the prototype class universe.

Compare also the FreeAddMonoid(M×N) quotient by addConGen of zero, addition, right/left balancing and additive commutation relations, with its flipped lift. Construct the ring-specialized comparison with that universal-property shape. On this monoid quotient define negation by m tensor n↦(−m) tensor n, prove it preserves every defining relation and gives additive inverses by generator induction. Its resulting additive group has the same free-group universal property. The two quotient maps preserving generators are inverse by the respective induction principles, commute with the actual right-B action and the characteristic-predicate flipped lifts, and obey the ULift/naturality comparisons already required. No general non-unital or all-semiring formalization is required by the ring-specific finite-projective source.

**Finite free retracts.** Use native Module.Projective and Module.Finite over Aᵐᵒᵖ. A finitely generated projective M admits maps i:M→A^n, r:A^n→M with r i=id for some finite n: choose a finite generating surjection and split it by projectivity. Conversely any retract of a finite free module is finitely generated projective, by finite generation under images and projectivity under retracts. Construct M⊕ker(r)≃A^n using (m,k)↦i(m)+k and v↦(r(v),v−i(r(v))); prove the displayed inverse equations. The complement is finitely generated projective. No finite-complement assertion is made for a projective module without finite generation. Apply the tensor functor to the splitting equations and identify A^n⊗_A B≃B^n to prove change of scalars preserves finite projectives without a flatness hypothesis. Prove naturality and the coherent two-step comparison above on the full finite-projective subcategories.

**Idempotent matrices and actual ranges.** Consume Part II Layer 1.1’s explicit row-coordinate transport of AlgebraicKTheory A0’s idempotent presentations over the opposite ring and construct the explicit transpose/right-module comparison below. With column vectors in A^n and right scalar multiplication, an A-valued n×n matrix acts by left matrix multiplication. Prove its right linearity and the equivalence between matrices and endomorphisms of this native finite free module. For p²=p define P_p={v | pv=v}, equivalently range(p), as a right submodule. The inclusion and p supply the splitting; 1−p supplies the complementary range and P_p⊕P_(1−p)≃A^n. Conversely transport i r from a finite free retract to an idempotent matrix and construct M≃P_p, not merely an equality of K-classes.

For p∈M_n(A),q∈M_m(A), prove P_p≃P_q iff rectangular matrices x∈M_(m,n)(A), y∈M_(n,m)(A) satisfy yx=p, xy=q after normalization x=qxp,y=pyq. An isomorphism of ranges extends by projection/inclusion to such x,y. Conversely restrict normalized x,y to the ranges and check both composites. If only xy=q,yx=p are initially supplied, normalize to qxp and pyq and prove the same identities, explicitly using p²=p,q²=q. Include n=0,m=0,zero idempotents and the zero ring. This is algebraic equivalence, without self-adjointness or a partial isometry.

Prove equivalence is reflexive, symmetric and transitive by normalized rectangular products. Prove block direct sums preserve it, permutation matrices give commutativity/associativity/unitality, and common zero-block stabilization gives the same equivalence. For the last assertion the normalized x,y give the involution U=[[1−p,y],[x,1−q]], U²=1, conjugating diag(p,0_m) to diag(0_n,q); compose with the explicit block permutation. Do not assert cancellation or conjugacy without stabilization.

**Small monoid and split K₀ comparison.** Use AlgebraicKTheory A0’s idempotent monoid through the displayed Part II coordinate transport, and prove its identification with the explicit commutative quotient of the small sigma type of finite sizes and matrix carriers in the ring universe. Construct its equivalence with isomorphism classes of finite projective right modules from the range/retract comparisons, independently of a chosen presentation. Prove block sum corresponds to biproduct, unit and arbitrary finite sums, and scalar extension sends p to f.mapMatrix(p) with its actual range comparison. Consume Layer 1's small-model independence and Layer 2's splitK₀ universal property, native TauCeti.SplitK0 and Algebra.GrothendieckAddGroup to construct the natural group-completion isomorphism. Export classes [P], generation by differences, and the correct stable equality criterion: [P]−[Q]=[P']−[Q'] iff some finite projective T satisfies P⊕Q'⊕T≃P'⊕Q⊕T. The common T is essential in a noncancellative monoid. Prove functoriality under unital ring maps, identity/composition and opposite-ring translation. OperatorKTheory K1 consumes this algebraic comparison only after its separate Hilbert-module/polar comparison converts idempotents to projections over a unital C*-algebra; no such conversion is asserted here for an arbitrary ring.

**Construction checks.** Require the unit A, A=0, n=0, p=0/1, a noncentral idempotent in M₂(A), rectangular unequal-size range equivalences and a noncommutative matrix ring A=M₂(k). Check the balancing equation against ma⊗b=m⊗f(a)b and the right B action. A deliberately commutative-only tensor carrier must fail the noncommutative control. Withhold finite-complement output on an infinite free module; withhold projection/partial-isometry output absent *-structure and C*-hypotheses. Positive controls retain ordinary ring-map extension and matrix composition without flatness or topology. These are written construction targets; no new Lean declaration is claimed implemented by this text.

### Layer 3: finite resolutions and the resolution theorem

Develop finite resolutions as reusable categorical data before applying them to projectives.

- **Objects with finite resolutions.**  Given an object property `P` in an exact category, define
  finite `P`-resolutions as finite chains of conflations (equivalently, bounded exact augmented
  complexes) whose resolving terms satisfy `P`.  Relate the recursive, complex, and object-property
  presentations.  Provide length, truncation, transport under isomorphism, zero padding, and direct
  sums.  Match Mathlib's finite-resolution object-property API when available.  Do not claim
  functorial maps between arbitrary preselected `P`-resolutions without a lifting hypothesis.
- **Resolving subcategories.**  Package repleteness, zero and biproduct closure, extension closure,
  closure under kernels of admissible epimorphisms between objects in the ambient exact
  subcategory, and existence of finite `P`-resolutions for every ambient object.  Make the induced
  exact structure on `P` explicit.
- **Euler class of a resolution.**  Prove that the alternating K₀ class of a finite resolution is
  independent of its length, zero padding, and choice of resolution.  Prove additivity on
  conflations by Weibel's common-refinement and mapping-cone argument under the packaged resolving
  hypotheses, rather than appealing to the projective horseshoe lemma in this generality.
- **Resolution theorem.**  Under the resolving hypotheses, the inclusion induces an isomorphism on
  exact K₀.  Its inverse sends `[X]` to the alternating sum of any finite `P`-resolution.  Prove both
  inverse identities, naturality under compatible exact functors, and equivalence invariance.
- **Projective-resolution calculus.**  Separately, under enough projectives, construct comparison
  maps to a fixed projective resolution, prove uniqueness up to chain homotopy, and prove the
  projective horseshoe lemma.  Use these stronger statements for the later projective-module
  application; do not attribute them to arbitrary resolving objects.
- **Graded resolutions.**  Redo the whole layer for a graded exact category with its shift `{1}`:
  graded finite `P`-resolutions with their internal degrees recorded, graded resolving
  subcategories, the graded Euler class, the graded resolution theorem, and graded comparison and
  horseshoe.  Prove that forgetting the grading sends a graded resolution to an ungraded one and
  that the two Euler classes agree under the `q = 1` specialization of Layer 6.
- **Linear resolutions and the Koszul boundary.**  For a graded algebra generated in degree zero
  and one over its semisimple degree-zero part, define a **linear** graded projective resolution
  of a graded module: the `i`th term is generated in internal degree `i`.  Prove the elementary
  reformulations -- linearity of the resolution of the graded simples is equivalent to diagonal
  vanishing of the bigraded `Ext^{i,j}(S,S)` for `i ≠ j`. Prove that a linear resolution is
  minimal. Define the matrix-valued Betti Poincaré series using the multiplicities of graded
  projectives in that minimal resolution, retaining both homological and internal degrees.
  The alternating Euler identity with the graded Cartan matrix is coefficientwise, under
  local finiteness and the degreewise finiteness that makes each coefficient a finite sum.
  Under diagonal support it specializes to the one-variable inverse with `q ↦ -q`; a converse
  stated using Betti series must retain that support/comparison data. Do not infer Koszulity
  merely from a numerical Hilbert-series identity for a separately named quadratic dual.  This is the vocabulary in which Koszulity is stated: **this layer supplies the
  definition and its elementary equivalences, and the duality theorems which use them belong to
  the DG/`A∞` roadmap's relative-Koszul layer and to the zigzag roadmap.**  Fixing it here keeps
  one notion of linearity for both, rather than two.

The generic reference is Weibel, *The K-book*, Theorem II.7.6 and Lemma II.7.6.1: finite
`P`-dimension for every object, closure of the ambient exact subcategory under kernels of
surjections, and the common-refinement argument.  Bühler, Sections 11--12 supplies the stronger
comparison and horseshoe results specifically for projective resolutions in exact categories.  The
Tau Ceti statement records the extension-closed induced exact structures as well as the kernel and
finite-resolution hypotheses, rather than hiding them in ambient notation.

### Layer 4: finite-dimensional algebras and the Cartan map

Let `k` be a field and `A` a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra.  Work with left modules.  The precise
module subcategories are replete full subcategories: finite-dimensional modules (equivalently
finite-length/finitely generated modules in this regime) with the canonical abelian exact
structure, and finitely generated projectives with the induced split structure.

- **The two groups.**  Define `K₀(proj A)` and `G₀(mod A)` through Layers 2--3.  Prove Morita and
  algebra-equivalence invariance.  Under the explicit finite, basic/split, and Krull--Schmidt
  hypotheses supplied by the quiver roadmap, prove that indecomposable projective classes form a
  basis of `K₀(proj A)` and simple classes form a basis of `G₀(mod A)`.
- **The projective/simple pairing.**  Define the integral pairing by
  `⟨[P_i],[M]⟩mult = [M:S_i]` and extend bilinearly.  Prove it is a perfect pairing in the indexed
  bases.  Separately prove
  `dim_k Hom_A(P_i,M) = dim_k D_i · [M:S_i]`, where `D_i=End_A(S_i)`, and its diagonal
  specialization.  Under an explicit split/absolutely-simple hypothesis, identify the multiplicity
  pairing with raw Hom dimension.
- **The Cartan map and matrix.**  The inclusion of projectives into modules is
  conflation-exact from the split induced structure and gives
  `c_A : K₀(proj A) ⟶ G₀(mod A)`.  In the projective-domain/simple-codomain bases its `j`th
  column is the composition-factor vector of `P_j`, so its `(i,j)` entry is
  `[P_j:S_i]`.  Compare this with the quiver roadmap's Cartan matrix and with the corrected Hom
  formulas; prove all transpose statements forced by left-module conventions rather than relying
  on prose.
- **Finite projective resolutions.**  On the full subcategory of modules admitting finite
  finitely generated projective resolutions, apply Layer 3 to prove that `c_A` is an isomorphism.
  If all finite-dimensional modules have such resolutions--in particular, for a finite-dimensional
  algebra of finite global dimension--this is the full `K₀(proj A) ≃ G₀(mod A)` comparison.  Its
  inverse is the alternating projective-resolution class.
- **The graded Cartan map.**  For a graded finite-dimensional algebra with graded simples and
  graded projective covers, construct `K₀^gr(proj A) ⟶ G₀^gr(mod A)` as a map of modules over the
  shift action, and the graded Cartan matrix whose `(i,j)` entry is the graded multiplicity
  `∑_d q^d [P_j : S_i{d}]`.  Prove that forgetting the grading recovers the ungraded Cartan map,
  and that setting `q = 1` recovers the ungraded matrix.  When the simples have linear resolutions
  in the sense of Layer 3, relate the graded Cartan matrix to the inverse of the graded Poincaré
  matrix under `q ↦ -q`.
- **Unimodularity and its boundary.**  Under the hypotheses of the previous bullet -- so that
  `c_A` is an isomorphism -- and with finite projective/simple bases, deduce that the Cartan
  matrix is invertible over `ℤ` and has determinant `±1`.  Record no target asserting its sign is
  positive.  The finite-resolution hypothesis is exactly the boundary: `k[ε]/(ε²)` has Cartan
  matrix `[2]`, so a statement of unimodularity for every finite-dimensional algebra is false and
  must not be recorded.

Assem--Simson--Skowroński, Volume I, Chapter I, Sections 4--5 covers indecomposable projectives and
projective covers; Chapter III, Section 3, especially Definitions 3.4 and 3.7 and Propositions
3.8, 3.10, and 3.13, covers K₀, Cartan matrices, unimodularity, and Euler forms in its
algebraically-closed/right-module convention.  Peter Webb, *A Course in Finite Group Representation
Theory*, Chapter 7, Sections 7.3--7.4, especially Proposition 7.4.1 and Corollary 7.4.2, supplies
the arbitrary-field division-endomorphism correction.  Dancso--Licata, Sections 1.1 and 2.2,
motivates the projective/module comparison; its reversed `G₀`/`K₀` notation is translated to the
standard notation fixed above.

### Layer 5: finite-support Euler characteristics and Ext-Euler descent

The reusable finite-support theorem comes before the Ext specialization.

- **Finite graded families.**  For an additive invariant `a` from objects of an additive/exact
  category to an abelian group, define the signed sum of a finitely supported `ℤ`-graded family.
  The input includes an explicit finite support or bounds; it is not inferred from the value of
  `finsum`.  Prove reindexing, shift/sign, direct-sum, and finite-exact-sequence formulas.
- **Bounded complexes and Euler--Poincaré.**  Define the Euler characteristic of a bounded complex
  by its terms.  If homology is defined, all terms and homology objects lie in the invariant's
  domain, and the relevant cycles/boundaries satisfy the exact-structure closure conditions, prove
  equality with the alternating homology sum.  For vector spaces, record finite-dimensionality of
  every term and boundedness explicitly.  Connect this theorem to Mathlib's landed
  `HomologicalComplex.eulerChar`/`homologyEulerChar` API without inheriting its junk value for
  infinite support.
- **Ext-finite pairs.**  For object properties `P` and `Q` in a `k`-linear abelian category, define
  a pair to be Euler-admissible when (i) every `Ext^n(X,Y)` with `P X` and `Q Y` is
  finite-dimensional over `k`, and (ii) for each such pair there is a bound beyond which it
  vanishes.  Develop closure under isomorphism, extensions in either variable, finite sums, and
  passage to suitable full subcategories.  Keep Hom-finiteness, eventual Ext-vanishing, and a
  uniform finite-global-dimension bound as separate predicates and implications.
- **The Ext-Euler value.**  Define the finite sum under an Euler-admissible witness and prove
  independence of the chosen bound.  Prove isomorphism invariance.  Construct the long exact Ext
  sequences in both variables, cut them off using eventual vanishing, and apply the finite
  Euler--Poincaré theorem to prove additivity in both variables.
- **Descent.**  Descend the object-level value to a biadditive pairing on the exact/abelian
  Grothendieck groups of the two selected subcategories.  Give the universal characterization and
  naturality under functors carrying Ext and preserving the chosen finiteness data.
- **Projectives and resolutions.**  Prove projective evaluation
  `χ(P,N)=dim_k Hom(P,N)` and, under the split hypothesis, `χ(P_i,S_j)=δ_{ij}`; retain the
  `dim_k D_i` diagonal over a general field and the separate integral multiplicity pairing.  Under
  finite projective resolutions, compute `χ(M,N)` from the alternating Hom complex and identify its
  matrix with the inverse/transposed Cartan matrix in the pinned bases.
- **Graded Ext and graded descent.**  Redo the layer for a graded abelian category: bigraded
  `Ext^{n,j}`, Euler-admissibility with both the cohomological and the internal support finite,
  the graded long exact sequences in both variables, and descent to graded `K₀`.  Keep the two
  finiteness conditions separate, exactly as in the ungraded case.  The Laurent-polynomial
  packaging of the resulting bigraded numbers is Layer 6; the bigraded groups and their additivity
  are here.
- **Quiver comparison.**  For a finite acyclic quiver, prove that the categorical Ext-Euler form
  agrees with Tau Ceti's Ringel form on dimension vectors and with the landed projective evaluation.
  Its symmetrization agrees with `titsPolarForm`; the raw form remains nonsymmetric.  Do the graded
  version too: with the path-length grading on `kQ`, prove the corresponding statement for the
  bigraded Ext-Euler form, and prove that it specializes at `q = 1` to the ungraded one through the
  comparison hypotheses of Layer 6.  The graded statement, not the ungraded one, is what the
  zigzag roadmap's graded Cartan matrix `(1+q²)I + qA` is compared against.

Weibel, *An Introduction to Homological Algebra*, Sections 1.3 and 1.6 supplies long exact homology
sequences in abelian categories, Sections 2.4--2.7 supplies derived-functor/Ext long exact
sequences, and the finite Euler--Poincaré argument is the two bounded exact sequences through
cycles and boundaries.  Weibel, *The K-book*, Proposition II.6.6 and Proposition II.7.5 give the
corresponding K₀ Euler formulas and their closure hypotheses.  Assem--Simson--Skowroński,
III.3.13 gives the finite-global-dimension module formula.

### Layer 6: Laurent coefficients, q-Euler forms, and specialization

The graded categories, graded `K₀`, graded resolutions, the graded Cartan matrix and bigraded Ext
are already built in Layers 2--5.  This layer adds only the coefficient ring and what depends on
it.  Let `R = ℤ[q,q⁻¹]`, represented by `LaurentPolynomial ℤ`, with bar involution
`LaurentPolynomial.invert`.

- **Shift-module structure.**  For a graded exact category from Layer 2, construct the `R`-module
  structure on its graded K₀ and prove
  `[M{n}]=q^n[M]` for every `n : ℤ`, in particular `[M{1}]=q[M]`.  State the universal
  property for additive invariants equipped with a compatible invertible shift action.
- **Finite Laurent support.**  Define graded dimension only for graded vector spaces whose pieces
  are finite-dimensional and zero outside a finite set of internal degrees.  Define graded Ext
  admissibility with both cohomological finite support and finite Laurent support in every surviving
  degree.  For the target-shift pieces use
  `qdim Ext^n(M,N)=∑_j q⁻ʲ dim_k Ext^n_gr(M,N{j})`; equivalently reindex source shifts as
  `∑_j q^j dim_k Ext^n_gr(M{j},N)`.  Prove closure properties needed for exact sequences.
- **q-Euler form.**  Define
  `χ_q(M,N)=∑_n (-1)^n qdim Ext^n(M,N)`.  Package it using Mathlib's sesquilinear-map
  infrastructure.  Prove
  `χ_q(rM,N)=bar(r)χ_q(M,N)` and `χ_q(M,rN)=rχ_q(M,N)`, including the generating
  identities for shifts.  Prove additivity and descent to graded K₀.
- **Matrices and duality.**  Define graded Cartan and Gram matrices, with conjugate-transpose change
  of basis.  Relate projective, simple, and dual bases under only the explicitly constructed graded
  dualities; no Serre or DG-perfect duality is invoked.
- **Algebraic specialization.**  Define base change of the graded K-group and q-Euler form along
  evaluation at `q=1` and `q=-1`, and prove the latter's shift/sign formula.  Supply examples showing
  that either evaluation can turn a nondegenerate Laurent-polynomial matrix into a degenerate
  integer matrix.  These constructions mention no ungraded category.
- **Comparison after forgetting grading.**  State this only from explicit data: an ungraded exact
  category, a conflation-exact functor `U` from the graded category, and a natural isomorphism
  `{1} ⋙ U ≅ U`.  Prove that the induced K₀ map factors through the `q=1` base change.  Give
  sufficient hypotheses which make the factored map an isomorphism, and verify them in each claimed
  module-category example; shift compatibility by itself is not sufficient.  To identify Euler
  forms, separately require finite-support graded/ungraded comparison isomorphisms
  `⊕_j Ext^n_gr(M,N{j}) ≅ Ext^n_ungr(U M,U N)` for every surviving `n`, compatible with connecting
  maps.  Only under these hypotheses prove that evaluation at `q=1` is the ordinary Ext-Euler form.

Dancso--Licata, Section 2.2, especially Definition 2.3 and Proposition 2.7, is the model for graded
K₀ and the q-Euler form; Sections 1.2 and 3.1--3.2 fix q-antilinearity, Gram matrices, left/right
duals, and specialization warnings.  Tau Ceti adopts its sesquilinearity convention but not its
reversed ungraded K-group letters.

### Layer 7: left and right numerical Grothendieck groups

- **Radicals.**  For a biadditive pairing `b : L × R ⟶ ℤ`, define
  `rad_left(b)={x | ∀y, b(x,y)=0}` and
  `rad_right(b)={y | ∀x, b(x,y)=0}` as additive subgroups.  Give the Laurent/sesquilinear
  analogues as submodules stable under bar where applicable.
- **Numerical quotients.**  Define the left numerical group as `L/rad_left(b)` and the right
  numerical group as `R/rad_right(b)`.  Descend the pairing between these two quotients and prove
  that it has zero left and right radicals.  When pairing one quotient against an unquotiented
  side, state only the corresponding one-sided nondegeneracy.
- **Functoriality.**  A map on the left descends only after proving it sends the left radical into
  the target left radical; state and prove the dual condition on the right.  Pairing-preserving
  pairs of maps yield maps of both numerical quotients only after proving both radical
  inclusions. Explicitly, for `f:L→L'`, `g:R→R'` and
  `b'(f x,g y)=b(x,y)`, require `f(rad_left(b)) ⊆ rad_left(b')` and
  `g(rad_right(b)) ⊆ rad_right(b')`. Pairing preservation on the images alone does not imply
  these inclusions. Surjectivity of `g` suffices for the left inclusion, and surjectivity of
  `f` suffices for the right; prove these sufficient criteria separately. Pairing-preserving
  equivalences induce equivalences of the two respective quotients, with their inverse maps.
- **Euler specialization.**  Apply the construction to ordinary and q-Euler pairings.  Keep the
  left and right quotients as distinct public definitions.  Prove an identification only when an
  explicit duality already constructed in this roadmap supplies it.

Dancso--Licata, Definition 3.2 and the discussion following Definition 3.1 provide the left/right
warning for nonsymmetric q-pairings.  The quotient construction here is elementary group/module
theory and is stated through its universal property.

## Worked examples and acceptance criteria

These computations are part of the roadmap.  They fix signs, handedness, support hypotheses, and
matrix orientation.

### Finite-dimensional vector spaces

For the essentially small category of finite-dimensional `k`-vector spaces, prove that every short
exact sequence splits and that split K₀ and canonical exact/abelian K₀ are both `ℤ`.  The class of a
space maps to its dimension, the one-dimensional space maps to `1`, and dimension satisfies the
universal property of additive invariants.  Show that this calculation is independent of the
chosen skeleton of finite-dimensional spaces.

### The quiver `1 ⟶ 2`

Use left modules and the Ringel convention

`⟨x,y⟩ = x₁y₁ + x₂y₂ - x₁y₂`.

The simples have dimension vectors `S₁=(1,0)` and `S₂=(0,1)`.  The indecomposable projectives are
`P₁=(1,1)` and `P₂=(0,1)`.  With rows `(S₁,S₂)` and columns `(P₁,P₂)`, the Cartan matrix is

```text
C = [1 0]
    [1 1].
```

Thus the Cartan map sends `[P₁]` to `[S₁]+[S₂]` and `[P₂]` to `[S₂]`.  In the simple basis the raw
Euler matrix is

```text
E = [ 1 -1]
    [ 0  1],
```

so that `E = C⁻ᵀ` with these row/column conventions.  Its symmetrization is

```text
E + Eᵀ = [ 2 -1]
          [-1  2],
```

the type `A₂` Gram matrix.  Verify these identities from both finite projective resolutions and the
landed quiver Euler formula.  Any transpose or arrow reversal changes a displayed off-diagonal
entry and must fail this test.

### An oriented `E₈` quiver

Choose and record one acyclic orientation of the `E₈` Dynkin graph.  Compute the nonsymmetric Euler
matrix `I-A`, where `A_{ij}` counts arrows `i⟶j`, and prove that its symmetrization is the
positive-definite `E₈` Gram matrix `2I-(A+Aᵀ)`.  The example asserts no E₈ categorification: it is a
compatibility check between the general categorical pairing, Tau Ceti's quiver formula, and the
root-system Gram convention.

### The dual numbers

Let `A=k[ε]/(ε²)` and `S=A/(ε)`.  Its Cartan matrix is the one-by-one matrix `[2]`, because the
regular indecomposable projective has two copies of `S` as composition factors.  The periodic
minimal projective resolution

```text
⋯ ⟶ A --ε--> A --ε--> A ⟶ S ⟶ 0
```

gives `Ext_A^n(S,S) ≅ k` for every `n≥0`.  Each Ext group is finite-dimensional, but eventual
vanishing fails, so `χ(S,S)` is not defined.  Prove that the Euler-admissibility predicate rejects
this pair and that no `finsum` junk value is exposed as its Euler pairing.  This is the mandatory
test separating Hom-finiteness from convergence.

### A minimal graded check

Take a graded category with one simple `M` and its internal shifts, with a one-dimensional degree
zero endomorphism space and no higher Ext.  Verify `[M{1}]=q[M]`,
`χ_q(M{1},M)=q⁻¹χ_q(M,M)`, and `χ_q(M,M{1})=qχ_q(M,M)`.  At `q=1` both shifts have the same
algebraically specialized class; at `q=-1` a single shift changes sign.  Equip this example with its
explicit ungraded category and forgetful functor, verify the comparison hypotheses above directly,
and only then identify the `q=1` class and pairing with the ordinary ones.  Add a two-by-two Laurent
matrix whose determinant is nonzero before evaluation and zero at one of these values,
demonstrating that specialization need not preserve nondegeneracy.

## Ordering and cross-roadmap dependencies

Layer 0 is foundational.  Layer 1 supplies the small quotient engine.  Layer 2 instantiates it for
the four categorical K-groups.  Layer 3 supplies resolution classes and the resolution theorem.
Layer 4 consumes Layers 0--3 and the finite-dimensional-algebra objects from
[quiver representations](../RepresentationTheory/QuiverRepresentations/README.md).  Layer 5 first
builds finite-support Euler--Poincaré and then uses Layers 2--4 to descend Ext pairings.  Each of
Layers 2--5 is built in the graded setting as well as the ungraded one, per **Graded throughout**;
Layer 6 then adds only the Laurent coefficient ring, the sesquilinearity and the specializations.
Layer 7 consumes the pairings from Layers 5--6.

The graded work is not a second pass over a finished ungraded roadmap.  A graded layer is
completed with its ungraded counterpart, because the downstream roadmaps consume the graded
constructions and would otherwise have nothing to import.

The bounded-complex portions of Layers 2 and 5 may be developed together after the exact and K₀
interfaces exist.  The module-specific Layer 4 and the abstract triangulated part of Layer 2 can
also proceed independently at that point.  Every acceptance example is completed with the first
layer that makes it expressible; the dual-numbers rejection is required before Ext-Euler descent is
accepted.

The DG/A-infinity, stable/periodic/curved, hopfological, zigzag/preprojective, and McKay subjects
are separate roadmap areas that consume this one.  No declaration from those areas is a
prerequisite here.
